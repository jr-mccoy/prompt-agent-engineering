# Content Marketing Strategy for Solo Developers

**Objective:** Design a sustainable, time-efficient content marketing strategy that generates organic discovery for your Android app. This prompt produces a complete content plan including a calendar, SEO keyword targets, a repurposing framework, and time-boxed workflows -- all calibrated for a single person with no marketing background who can invest 2-4 hours per week.

**When to Use:** Use this after you have a working app (or solid beta) and need to build an organic discovery channel beyond the Play Store. Content marketing compounds over time, so starting earlier is better, but you need something real to write about. If you are pre-launch, focus your content on the problem your app solves rather than the app itself.

---

## Context Gathering

Provide the following information so the strategy can be tailored to your situation:

1. **App & Audience**
   - What does your app do in one sentence?
   - Who is the primary user? (age range, profession, interests)
   - What problem does it solve? What pain point triggers someone to search for a solution?

2. **Your Expertise**
   - What topics can you write about confidently? (e.g., Android development, the domain your app serves, productivity tips)
   - Do you have any existing online presence? (blog, Twitter/X, Reddit karma, dev.to, Medium)

3. **Competitive Landscape**
   - Name 2-3 competitor apps or alternative solutions
   - Do any competitors publish content? (blogs, YouTube, newsletters)

4. **Constraints**
   - How many hours per week can you realistically commit to content? (target: 2-4)
   - Do you prefer writing, recording video, or creating visual content?
   - Do you have any budget for tools? (target: $0, but good to know)

5. **Goals**
   - Primary goal: organic traffic, app installs, email list growth, authority building?
   - Timeline: when do you need to see measurable results?

## Instructions

### CRITICAL: Verification Requirements

Before finalizing any content strategy, verify the following:

1. **Keyword Validation** -- Every target keyword must have evidence of actual search volume. Use free tools (Google autocomplete, AnswerThePublic, Ubersuggest free tier) to confirm people actually search for these terms.
2. **Competitor Content Gap** -- At least 3 content topics must address gaps that competitors are NOT covering well. Do not simply replicate what exists.
3. **Time Budget Audit** -- The total weekly time commitment must not exceed the stated constraint. Add up estimated times for each activity and verify the total.
4. **Repurposing Feasibility** -- Each pillar piece of content must have at least 3 documented derivative formats. Verify that the derivatives are genuinely useful (not just the same text posted everywhere).
5. **Measurement Plan** -- Every content type must have a specific, trackable metric. "Brand awareness" is not a metric; "monthly organic sessions to blog from target keywords" is.
6. **Acceptable Null Result** -- If keyword research reveals no meaningful search volume for your niche, the strategy should pivot to community-driven content (Reddit, forums, Discord) rather than SEO-first content. Document this pivot clearly.

### False-Positive Prevention

- **DO NOT** recommend daily posting schedules. Solo developers cannot sustain this, and burnout kills content programs.
- **DO NOT** suggest "going viral" as a strategy. Viral content is unpredictable and unrepeatable.
- **DO NOT** recommend paid content distribution (sponsored posts, ads) in a zero-budget plan.
- **DO NOT** conflate vanity metrics (page views, social likes) with business metrics (installs, signups, retention).
- **DO NOT** suggest creating content about topics you have no expertise in just because they have search volume.
- **DO** focus on sustainable, compounding content that builds long-term organic traffic.
- **DO** prioritize content that directly addresses your target user's pain points.
- **DO** build content around your unique expertise as a developer -- this is your unfair advantage.
- **DO** set realistic expectations: content marketing typically takes 3-6 months to show meaningful results.

### Phase 1: Content Pillar Discovery

Content pillars are 3-5 broad topic categories that everything you publish falls under. They sit at the intersection of what you know, what your audience needs, and what search engines can rank.

**The Pillar Discovery Framework:**

| Dimension | Question | Example (Fitness Tracker App) |
|-----------|----------|-------------------------------|
| Your Expertise | What can you teach authentically? | Android development, step counting algorithms, health data privacy |
| User Pain Points | What problems does your audience Google? | "how to track workouts without a smartwatch", "free step counter app" |
| App Connection | How does each topic naturally lead to your app? | Every article about workout tracking can mention your app as a solution |
| Competition Gap | What are competitors NOT writing about? | Most fitness blogs ignore privacy; your app is privacy-first |

**Pillar Selection Rules:**
- Choose 3-5 pillars maximum. Fewer is better for a solo developer.
- At least 1 pillar must be a "Problem Pillar" -- content about the problem your app solves (not the app itself).
- At least 1 pillar must be a "Behind the Scenes Pillar" -- content about building the app (appeals to developer audiences and build-in-public followers).
- Each pillar must support at least 10 individual article ideas. If you cannot brainstorm 10, the pillar is too narrow.

**Pillar Documentation Template:**

```
Pillar: [Name]
Target Audience Segment: [Who specifically reads this?]
Search Intent: [Informational / Navigational / Transactional]
Sample Keywords (5-10): [keyword1, keyword2, ...]
Article Ideas (10+):
  1. [Title idea]
  2. [Title idea]
  ...
App Connection: [How does this pillar naturally lead readers to your app?]
```

### Phase 2: SEO Keyword Research (Zero Budget)

You do not need expensive tools. Here is a step-by-step process using only free resources:

**Step 1: Seed Keyword Generation**
- List 10-15 phrases your ideal user might type into Google when looking for a solution to the problem your app solves.
- Include long-tail variations (4+ word phrases). These are easier to rank for.

**Step 2: Validate with Free Tools**

| Tool | What It Tells You | How to Use |
|------|-------------------|------------|
| Google Autocomplete | Real search queries | Type your seed keyword, note suggestions |
| Google "People Also Ask" | Related questions | Search your keyword, expand PAA boxes |
| AnswerThePublic (free) | Question-based queries | Enter seed keyword, export visualization |
| Google Trends | Relative interest over time | Compare keywords, check seasonality |
| Ubersuggest (free tier) | Volume estimates, difficulty | 3 free searches/day, use wisely |
| Reddit/Forum search | Language your audience uses | Search your topic, note exact phrasing |

**Step 3: Keyword Prioritization Matrix**

Score each keyword on these dimensions (1-5 scale):

| Keyword | Search Volume | Competition | Relevance to App | Your Expertise | Total Score |
|---------|--------------|-------------|-------------------|----------------|-------------|
| Example | 3 | 4 (low comp = high score) | 5 | 4 | 16 |

**Prioritize keywords with Total Score >= 14.** This means high relevance and expertise even if volume is modest.

**Step 4: Content Type Mapping**

| Search Intent | Content Type | Example |
|---------------|-------------|---------|
| "How to..." | Tutorial/Guide | "How to track water intake without an app" |
| "Best..." | Listicle/Comparison | "Best free habit trackers for Android" |
| "What is..." | Explainer | "What is sleep hygiene and why it matters" |
| "[Problem]" | Problem-Solution | "Phone battery dies from fitness tracking" |
| "[Product] review" | Review/Alternative | "FitBit app alternatives for Android" |

### Phase 3: Content Calendar Design

A solo developer content calendar must be ruthlessly simple. Here is the recommended cadence:

**Weekly Time Budget (3 hours/week target):**

| Activity | Time | Frequency |
|----------|------|-----------|
| Write 1 pillar article (800-1200 words) | 90 min | Weekly |
| Repurpose into 3-4 derivative pieces | 30 min | Weekly |
| Schedule social posts for the week | 20 min | Weekly |
| Engage in 2-3 relevant communities | 20 min | 3x/week |
| Review metrics and adjust | 20 min | Monthly |

**Monthly Content Calendar Template:**

```
Week 1: [Pillar A] article + derivatives
  - Blog post: [Title]
  - Twitter/X thread: [Key takeaways]
  - Reddit comment opportunity: [Subreddit + context]
  - Dev.to cross-post: [Modified intro for developer audience]

Week 2: [Pillar B] article + derivatives
  ...

Week 3: [Pillar C] article + derivatives
  ...

Week 4: [Pillar A] article + derivatives (rotate back)
  ...

Monthly Bonus (if time allows):
  - 1 "build in public" update post
  - 1 guest post pitch to relevant blog
```

**Batching Strategy:**
Write articles in batches when inspiration strikes. A single 3-hour Sunday session can produce 2-3 articles that you then schedule across weeks. This is far more efficient than writing on-demand.

### Phase 4: The Repurposing Framework

Every pillar article should generate 4-6 derivative pieces. This is how you multiply your output without multiplying your time.

**The Content Waterfall:**

```
1 Blog Post (800-1200 words)
├── 1 Twitter/X Thread (5-8 tweets summarizing key points)
├── 1 Reddit Post or Comment (adapted for subreddit norms)
├── 1 Dev.to / Medium cross-post (with canonical URL back to your blog)
├── 1 LinkedIn post (if your audience is professional)
├── 3-5 Social media quotes (individual insights as standalone posts)
└── 1 Email newsletter segment (if you have a list)
```

**Platform Adaptation Rules:**

| Platform | Tone | Format | Key Rule |
|----------|------|--------|----------|
| Your Blog | Authoritative, detailed | Long-form, SEO-optimized | Canonical source, always publish here first |
| Twitter/X | Conversational, punchy | Thread (5-8 tweets), single insights | Lead with the most surprising finding |
| Reddit | Helpful, humble, non-promotional | Problem-solution, "I learned..." | NEVER directly promote. Add value first. Link only if asked or natural. |
| Dev.to | Technical, tutorial-style | Cross-post with `canonical_url` | Add developer-specific context |
| LinkedIn | Professional, insight-driven | Short-form (300 words max) | Focus on lessons learned, business insights |
| Medium | Polished, story-driven | Re-edited for Medium audience | Use Medium's built-in distribution |

**Critical Reddit Rules:**
- Read and follow each subreddit's rules before posting. Many ban self-promotion outright.
- Build karma in the community for 2-4 weeks before ever mentioning your app.
- The 90/10 rule: 90% of your Reddit activity should be genuinely helping others, 10% can reference your work.
- Best format: "I built [app] to solve [problem]. Here is what I learned." NOT "Check out my app!"

### Phase 5: SEO On-Page Basics

You do not need to be an SEO expert, but these fundamentals matter:

**Every Blog Post Must Have:**

1. **Title Tag** -- Include primary keyword, under 60 characters
2. **Meta Description** -- 150-160 characters, include keyword, include a call to action
3. **H1 Heading** -- Matches or closely mirrors the title tag
4. **H2/H3 Subheadings** -- Include secondary keywords naturally
5. **Internal Links** -- Link to 2-3 of your other articles
6. **External Links** -- Link to 1-2 authoritative sources (Google rewards this)
7. **Image Alt Text** -- Describe images with keywords where natural
8. **URL Slug** -- Short, keyword-rich (e.g., `/blog/track-water-intake-android`)

**Free Blog Hosting Options:**

| Platform | Pros | Cons | Best For |
|----------|------|------|----------|
| GitHub Pages + Hugo/Jekyll | Free, fast, developer-friendly | Requires setup | Developers comfortable with Git |
| Hashnode | Free, custom domain, SEO-friendly | Less customizable | Developers wanting quick setup |
| WordPress.com (free tier) | Easy, familiar | Limited customization, ads | Non-technical users |
| Notion + Super.so | Beautiful, easy | $12/mo for Super.so | Design-conscious developers |
| Dev.to | Free, built-in audience | Less SEO control | Reaching developer audience |

### Phase 6: Measuring What Matters

**Metrics That Actually Matter for Solo Developers:**

| Metric | Tool (Free) | Target (Month 3) | Target (Month 6) |
|--------|-------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Organic sessions/month | Google Analytics / Plausible | 200+ | 1,000+ |
| Email subscribers | Mailchimp / Buttondown | 50+ | 200+ |
| Keyword rankings (page 1) | Google Search Console | 3-5 keywords | 10-15 keywords |
| Blog-to-install conversion | UTM parameters + Play Console | Track trend | 2-5% CTR on app links |
| Content pieces published | Your calendar | 12 articles | 24 articles |

**Metrics to Ignore:**
- Social media follower count (vanity metric)
- Page views without conversion context
- Bounce rate (misleading for blogs -- people find answers and leave, which is fine)
- Domain authority scores (lagging indicator you cannot directly control)

**Setup Google Search Console (Free, Essential):**
1. Verify your domain
2. Submit your sitemap
3. Check "Performance" tab weekly for:
   - Which queries bring traffic
   - Click-through rates (improve titles for low CTR)
   - Average position (target keywords moving toward page 1)

## Customization Guide

1. **Video-First Creator:** Replace blog posts with YouTube videos. Use the same pillar/keyword framework but optimize for YouTube search (titles, descriptions, tags). Repurpose video transcripts into blog posts.
2. **Technical Audience (Developer Tools):** Lean heavily into Dev.to, Hacker News, and GitHub-based content. Replace Reddit community engagement with open-source contributions and GitHub Discussions.
3. **Non-English Market:** Adapt keyword research for target language. Use Google Trends with region filter. Consider that competition is often lower in non-English markets.
4. **Pre-Launch App:** Focus 100% on the "Problem Pillar" -- write about the problem your app will solve without mentioning the app. Build an email waitlist instead of driving to the Play Store.
5. **B2B Android App:** Replace consumer-focused platforms (Reddit, Twitter/X) with LinkedIn and industry-specific forums. Content should focus on ROI, case studies, and business outcomes.
