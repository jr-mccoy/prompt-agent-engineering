# Zero-Budget App Launch Marketing Plan

**Objective:** Create a complete launch marketing plan for a mobile app with $0 advertising budget, covering pre-launch buzz building (4-6 weeks before), launch day execution, and post-launch sustain strategies — all executable by a single developer with no marketing team and no marketing experience.

**When to Use:** Use this prompt when preparing to launch a new app, when relaunching after a major update, or when you've built something good but have no idea how to get people to discover it. This addresses the most common solo developer failure mode: "I built it, but nobody came." Every tactic here costs $0 in cash spend — the only investment is your time (estimated 5-10 hours/week during launch period).

**Important context:** This prompt teaches marketing fundamentals while providing actionable steps. If you have "no experience" in marketing, that's exactly who this is for. Marketing is a learnable skill, and for a solo developer, authenticity and expertise in your niche are your biggest advantages over well-funded competitors.

---

## Context Gathering

Before building the launch plan, understand your specific situation:

1. **App Details:**
   - "What is your app and what problem does it solve?"
   - "What makes it different from existing solutions?"
   - "Is it free, freemium, paid, or subscription?"
   - "What platform(s) (Android, iOS, both)?"

2. **Target Audience:**
   - "Who specifically needs this app? (Be as narrow as possible)"
   - "Where do these people hang out online (subreddits, Discord servers, forums, Twitter/X communities)?"
   - "What words do they use to describe the problem your app solves?"
   - "How do they currently solve the problem without your app?"

3. **Your Assets:**
   - "Do you have any existing audience (blog, Twitter, GitHub followers)?"
   - "Do you have beta testers who can provide testimonials?"
   - "Do you have a landing page or website?"
   - "Do you have any screenshots or demo videos ready?"

4. **Launch Timeline:**
   - "When is your target launch date?"
   - "How many weeks do you have for pre-launch preparation?"
   - "How many hours per week can you dedicate to marketing?"

5. **Constraints:**
   - "Are there any marketing channels you're uncomfortable with (social media, video, public speaking)?"
   - "Do you want to remain anonymous or build a personal brand?"

## Instructions

### CRITICAL: Honest Expectations

**Before building a launch plan, understand these realities:**

1. **Most apps don't go viral on launch day** — and that's OK. Sustainable growth beats a one-day spike.
2. **Your first launch will teach you more than it earns** — treat it as a learning experiment.
3. **Quality of audience beats quantity** — 100 engaged users who give feedback are more valuable than 10,000 downloads with 99% day-1 churn.
4. **Consistency beats intensity** — 2 hours/week for 6 months outperforms 80 hours in one week.
5. **Your unfair advantage is expertise** — you know your problem space deeply. That knowledge IS your marketing content.

### False-Positive Prevention

- ❌ Do NOT promise specific download numbers — results vary wildly by category and niche
- ❌ Do NOT recommend tactics that require skills the solo developer doesn't have without providing learning resources
- ❌ Do NOT suggest strategies that require ongoing time investment beyond what a solo developer can sustain
- ❌ Do NOT recommend paid advertising tactics disguised as "free" (e.g., "run a few test ads")
- ❌ Do NOT treat every social platform as equal — focus on where the target audience actually is
- ✅ DO provide realistic expectations and timelines for each tactic
- ✅ DO prioritize tactics by effort-to-impact ratio
- ✅ DO teach the "why" behind each tactic (so the developer can adapt)
- ✅ DO include measurement for each tactic (how to know if it's working)

### Phase 1: Pre-Launch Foundation (6-4 Weeks Before Launch)

Build the assets and presence you'll need for launch day.

#### 1.1 Define Your Launch Positioning (Day 1 — 2 hours)

**Answer these three questions. Write them down. Everything else flows from these:**

```markdown
1. WHO is this for?
   "[Specific person] who [specific situation]"
   Example: "Android users who track family grocery lists but hate that
   existing apps require everyone to create accounts"

2. WHAT does it do that's different?
   "Unlike [alternatives], [App Name] [key differentiator]"
   Example: "Unlike Todoist or AnyList, FamList lets family members
   join with just a QR code — no account required"

3. WHY should someone switch/start using it NOW?
   "[Trigger event or pain point] that makes this the right moment"
   Example: "If you've ever stood in a grocery store texting 'did you
   already buy milk?' — that's the moment FamList solves"
```

**These three answers become your marketing message.** Use them everywhere: app store description, landing page, social media bio, every post you write.

#### 1.2 Build a Simple Landing Page (Week 1 — 3 hours)

**Purpose:** Give people somewhere to go before the app is live, and capture email addresses.

**Free options:**
- GitHub Pages + a single HTML file
- Carrd.co (free tier)
- Notion public page (works but less professional)

**Your landing page needs exactly 5 things:**
1. **Headline:** Your "what does it do that's different" answer from 1.1
2. **Screenshot or demo GIF:** One hero image showing the app in action
3. **3 bullet points:** Key benefits (not features — what the user GETS)
4. **Email signup:** "Get notified when we launch" (use Buttondown, Mailchimp free tier, or Google Forms)
5. **Your social media link:** So people can follow for updates

**That's it.** Resist the urge to over-design. Get it live in one sitting.

#### 1.3 Set Up Your Presence Where Your Users Are (Week 1-2 — 2 hours)

**Pick exactly 2 platforms.** More than 2 is unsustainable for one person.

**Platform selection guide:**

| If your users are... | Primary platform | Secondary platform |
|---------------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Developers / tech-savvy | Twitter/X or Hacker News | Reddit (relevant subreddit) |
| General consumers | Instagram or TikTok | Reddit (relevant subreddit) |
| Professionals | LinkedIn | Twitter/X |
| Niche community (hobby, interest) | Reddit or Discord | Facebook Group |
| Parents / families | Facebook Groups | Instagram |
| Students | TikTok | Instagram |

**Set up your profile:**
- Professional photo or app icon as avatar
- Bio: your "who is this for" positioning from 1.1
- Link: your landing page
- Pin: a post introducing what you're building and why

#### 1.4 Start "Building in Public" Content (Weeks 2-4 — 2 hours/week)

**What to share (pick 2-3 per week):**

| Content type | Example | Why it works |
|-------------|---------|-------------|
| **Problem stories** | "I realized existing grocery list apps all require accounts when my mom couldn't figure out the signup..." | Relatable, shows you understand the user |
| **Progress updates** | "Just finished the QR code sharing feature. Here's a 15-second demo:" | Builds anticipation |
| **Decisions and reasoning** | "Should the app be free with ads, or paid at $2.99? Here's my thinking..." | Invites engagement |
| **Struggles and lessons** | "Spent 3 days debugging Firestore sync. Here's what I learned about offline-first architecture." | Authentic, attracts developers |
| **Behind the scenes** | "This is my desk at 11pm. The app is almost ready for beta. Who wants to try it?" | Creates personal connection |

**What NOT to share:**
- Security vulnerabilities or exploit details
- Exact tech stack vulnerabilities
- Revenue numbers (until you're comfortable — they're public on some platforms)
- Customer data or private conversations
- Anything that makes you uncomfortable

**Posting cadence:** 3 times per week. Not daily (unsustainable). Not weekly (forgettable).

#### 1.5 Build a Beta Tester Community (Weeks 2-4 — 2 hours total)

**Goal: 20-50 beta testers before launch.**

**Where to find them:**
- Your social media followers (from building in public)
- Reddit posts in relevant subreddits asking for beta testers
- Friends and family (but also get strangers for honest feedback)
- Niche online communities (Discord servers, forums)
- BetaList.com (free listing for beta apps)
- Twitter/X posts with relevant hashtags

**How to ask:**

```
I'm building [App Name] — [one sentence description].

Looking for 20 beta testers who [fit your target audience].

What you get:
- Early access before public launch
- Direct line to me for feature requests
- Your feedback shapes the app

What I ask:
- Use the app for 1 week
- Tell me 1 thing you like and 1 thing that frustrated you
- 5-minute feedback form

Interested? [Link to signup]
```

**Why beta testers matter beyond testing:**
- They become your first reviewers on launch day
- Their feedback creates testimonial quotes for your store listing
- They feel ownership and become word-of-mouth advocates

### Phase 2: Launch Week Preparation (1 Week Before — 4 hours)

#### 2.1 Prepare Launch Day Assets

**Create these assets ahead of time (not on launch day):**

- [ ] **App Store listing** — Fully optimized (see `android_play_store_optimization.md`)
- [ ] **Screenshots** — At least 4, with text overlays explaining benefits
- [ ] **App store description** — Keyword-optimized, benefit-focused
- [ ] **Demo video/GIF** — 30-60 seconds showing core value
- [ ] **Social media announcement post** — Written and ready to paste
- [ ] **Reddit post** — Written for each relevant subreddit (customize per community rules)
- [ ] **Hacker News "Show HN" post** — If applicable (tech/developer audience)
- [ ] **Product Hunt listing** — Drafted (if targeting tech audience)
- [ ] **Email to beta testers** — Ask them to download, review, and share on launch day
- [ ] **Press kit** — Simple page with app description, screenshots, your bio (use landing page)

#### 2.2 Pre-Schedule Launch Communications

**Email to beta testers (send 2 days before):**

```
Subject: [App Name] launches [Day]! You helped make this happen.

Hey [Name],

[App Name] goes live on [Day]. You were one of the first people
to use it, and your feedback directly shaped what we're shipping.

Here's how you can help on launch day:

1. Download the public version from [Play Store link]
2. Leave an honest review (even 2-3 sentences helps enormously)
3. Share with one person who'd find it useful

Thank you for being part of this. Seriously.

— [Your name]
```

#### 2.3 Identify Launch Communities

Map exactly where you'll post on launch day:

| Platform | Specific Location | Rules to Follow | Post Time |
|----------|------------------|-----------------|-----------|
| Reddit | r/[subreddit] | [Community rules summary] | [Time] |
| Twitter/X | Your feed + hashtags | [Relevant hashtags] | [Time] |
| Hacker News | Show HN | [Guidelines] | 8-10am ET |
| Product Hunt | New product | [Submission rules] | 12:01am PT |
| [Discord] | [Server name] | [Rules] | [Time] |
| [Forum] | [Section] | [Rules] | [Time] |

**Important:** Read each community's self-promotion rules. Getting banned for spam on launch day is worse than not posting at all. Most subreddits require you to be an active, contributing member — not just someone who shows up to promote.

### Phase 3: Launch Day Execution (Launch Day — 4-6 hours)

#### 3.1 Launch Day Timeline

```
Morning (8-10 AM your time):
□ Verify app is live and downloadable
□ Test the download-to-first-use flow yourself
□ Send beta tester email (if not sent day before)
□ Post on Twitter/X with demo GIF
□ Post on your primary platform

Mid-Morning (10 AM - 12 PM):
□ Post on Reddit (if rules allow)
□ Submit to Hacker News (Show HN) if applicable
□ Submit to Product Hunt if applicable
□ Update landing page with Play Store link

Afternoon (12-4 PM):
□ Respond to EVERY comment, question, and mention
□ Thank early reviewers publicly
□ Share early download milestone (if comfortable)
□ Post a "thank you" update on primary platform

Evening (4-8 PM):
□ Check crash reports (Crashlytics)
□ Respond to any new reviews
□ Post day-1 reflection (what surprised you, how it feels)
□ Email anyone who signed up on the landing page
```

#### 3.2 Community Post Templates

**Reddit template (adapt to each subreddit's norms):**

```
[Title]: I built [App Name] to solve [specific problem]. Here's what I learned.

Hey [subreddit],

I've been [part of this community / dealing with this problem] for [time],
and I got frustrated that [specific frustration].

So I built [App Name] — [one sentence description].

**What makes it different:**
- [Differentiator 1]
- [Differentiator 2]
- [Differentiator 3]

**What I learned building it:**
- [Interesting lesson 1]
- [Interesting lesson 2]

It's free [or pricing model] on [platform]. [Link]

I'm here to answer any questions. Would love honest feedback —
what would make this useful for you?
```

**The key:** Lead with value and story, not promotion. Show that you're a member of the community, not a marketer parachuting in.

**Twitter/X thread template:**

```
1/ I just launched [App Name] — [what it does in one sentence].

Here's the 6-month journey from idea to launch: 🧵

2/ The problem: [Relatable problem description]

3/ Existing solutions fell short because [specific gap]

4/ So I built [solution] that [key differentiator]

5/ [Demo GIF or screenshot]

Here's what it looks like in action.

6/ The hardest part wasn't the code — it was [surprising challenge]

7/ Launch day numbers: [whatever you're comfortable sharing]

8/ What's next: [upcoming features based on beta feedback]

Try it: [link]
Feedback welcome — reply or DM me anything.
```

#### 3.3 Launch Day Engagement Rules

**Do:**
- Respond to every comment within 1 hour
- Thank people who share or review
- Answer questions honestly (including "what can't it do yet")
- Share credit with beta testers
- Be authentic about your nervousness/excitement

**Don't:**
- Get defensive about criticism
- Argue with negative feedback (say "thanks, I'll look into that")
- Spam multiple communities with identical posts
- Ask people to "please download and rate 5 stars"
- Disappear after posting (the real value is in the conversations)

### Phase 4: Post-Launch Sustain (Weeks 1-8 After Launch)

#### 4.1 First Week After Launch

**Daily (30 minutes):**
- Check and respond to all Play Store reviews
- Check crash reports and fix critical issues
- Post one update on your primary social platform

**End of Week 1 review:**
- How many downloads?
- What's the day-1 / day-7 retention rate?
- What are the top 3 user complaints/requests?
- Which marketing channel drove the most downloads?

#### 4.2 Ongoing Marketing Rhythm (2-4 hours/week)

| Day | Activity | Time |
|-----|----------|------|
| Monday | Check metrics (downloads, reviews, retention) | 15 min |
| Tuesday | Create one piece of content (post, article, video) | 1 hour |
| Wednesday | Engage in communities (help people, answer questions) | 30 min |
| Thursday | Respond to reviews and support requests | 30 min |
| Friday | Plan next week's content topic | 15 min |

#### 4.3 Content Flywheel (Sustainable Content Strategy)

**The idea:** Your expertise in your app's problem space IS your content. You don't need to be a "content creator" — you need to share what you know.

**Content sources that never run dry:**

| Source | Content Angle | Example |
|--------|-------------|---------|
| User questions | "How to [solve common problem]" | Blog post or thread |
| Bug fixes | "Here's what caused [issue] and how I fixed it" | Technical post |
| Feature development | "Why I added [feature] and the tradeoffs" | Build-in-public update |
| Industry news | "What [news] means for [your niche]" | Commentary/opinion |
| Competitor analysis | "I tried [competitor] for a week. Here's what I learned." | Comparison |
| User stories | "How [user type] uses [App Name] for [use case]" | Case study |

**Content repurposing chain:**
1. Write a detailed blog post →
2. Extract 3-5 key points for a Twitter/X thread →
3. Turn each point into a standalone social post →
4. Compile monthly posts into a newsletter →
5. Reference in Reddit comments when relevant

**One piece of content becomes 5+ touchpoints.**

#### 4.4 Review Management

**Responding to negative reviews:**

```
Template for fixable issues:
"Thanks for the feedback! I've identified the [issue] and will have
a fix in the next update ([timeline]). I'll follow up when it's ready."

Template for feature requests:
"Great suggestion! This is on my roadmap. I'm a solo developer so
I prioritize by user impact — your feedback helps me decide what's next."

Template for unfair reviews:
"I'm sorry you had that experience. I'd love to understand what went
wrong — could you email me at [email]? I want to make this right."
```

**Never:** Be sarcastic, argue publicly, or ignore reviews. Every response is visible to potential users.

#### 4.5 SEO and Discoverability (Low-Effort, High-Reward)

**Simple SEO tactics that compound over time:**

1. **Write 1 blog post per month** targeting a keyword your users search for
   - Host on your landing page domain for SEO benefit
   - Example: "Best free grocery list apps 2026" (if that's your niche)

2. **Answer questions on StackOverflow, Reddit, Quora** related to your app's problem space
   - Don't link to your app in every answer (that's spam)
   - Include your app in your profile bio
   - Only mention your app when it's genuinely relevant

3. **Create a GitHub repository** with useful resources related to your app's domain
   - Example: Awesome list, open-source component, or tutorial
   - Links back to your app naturally

### Phase 5: Measure and Iterate

#### 5.1 Key Metrics to Track Weekly

| Metric | Source | What It Tells You | Action If Low |
|--------|--------|-------------------|---------------|
| Daily downloads | Play Console | Awareness | Improve ASO / content |
| Day-1 retention | Firebase Analytics | First impression quality | Improve onboarding |
| Day-7 retention | Firebase Analytics | Core value delivery | Improve core features |
| Store rating | Play Console | User satisfaction | Fix top complaints |
| Crash-free rate | Crashlytics | Technical quality | Fix crashes |
| Organic search impressions | Play Console | ASO effectiveness | Optimize keywords |
| Social media engagement | Platform analytics | Content resonance | Adjust content strategy |

#### 5.2 Monthly Retrospective (30 minutes)

```markdown
## Month [X] Marketing Review

**Downloads this month:** [Number]
**Growth vs last month:** [+/- %]
**Best performing channel:** [Channel]
**Best performing content:** [Post/article]
**User feedback themes:** [Top 3]

**What worked:**
- [Tactic that drove results]

**What didn't work:**
- [Tactic to stop or change]

**Next month's focus:**
- [1-2 specific actions based on what you learned]
```

## Customization Guide

- **For developer-focused apps:** Lean heavily into Hacker News, Twitter/X tech community, and GitHub presence. Write technical blog posts. Build in public is especially effective.
- **For consumer apps:** Lean into visual platforms (Instagram, TikTok). Focus on the problem story, not technical details. Find Facebook Groups and Reddit communities where your users discuss the problem.
- **For B2B/professional apps:** Lean into LinkedIn. Write case studies and how-to guides. Target industry-specific forums and communities.
- **For niche/hobby apps:** Find the 2-3 online communities where enthusiasts gather. Become a genuine, helpful member BEFORE launching. These communities can drive 80% of your initial growth.
- **For apps with a blog or website already:** Expand the SEO section. Content marketing is your biggest advantage for sustainable, compounding growth.
- **For introverts uncomfortable with social media:** Focus on written content (blog, Reddit, forums) over video/live content. Build in public through written updates rather than face-to-camera content. Your expertise speaks louder than your personality.
