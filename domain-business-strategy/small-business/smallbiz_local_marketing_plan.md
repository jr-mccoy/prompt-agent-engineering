# Local Marketing Plan

**Objective:** Give a local business owner a marketing plan they can run in a few
hours a week — the handful of things that bring nearby customers through the door,
in the order that pays back fastest, with a way to tell within weeks which ones work.

**When to Use:**
- Your customers come from within a short drive or walk and you have capacity for more.
- You have tried "posting on social media" and cannot tell if it did anything.
- A salesperson is selling you local ads and you want to know what to do first.
- You are opening a second location or moving.

**Not this prompt if:**
- You are launching an app, SaaS or online product to an internet audience —
  `domain-business-strategy/go-to-market/marketing_zero_budget_launch_plan.md`.
- You need a technical audit of your website's search performance —
  `domain-agentic-resources/skills/marketing/seo-audit/` (its Local Business section
  lists the common listing and schema issues).
- You need ad images — `domain-advertising/advertising_local_small_business.md`.

## Inputs

1. What you sell, average ticket, and how much more work you can take per week.
2. Service area: walk-in radius, delivery or travel zone.
3. Who your best current customers are and how they found you (ask, or check).
4. Current presence: business profile, website, listings, review count and rating,
   any customer email or SMS list (with permission to contact).
5. Hours per week you can give marketing, and a monthly dollar budget.
6. Neighbours: businesses that serve the same customers without competing.

## Method

1. **Set a capacity-based goal (DS-02).** `goal = extra jobs or visits per week you
   can actually serve × average ticket`. Marketing beyond capacity creates bad reviews.

2. **Install the measurement first.** A "how did you hear about us?" question at
   checkout or booking, tallied weekly, plus the business profile's own calls,
   direction requests and website clicks. Without this, week 12 is guesswork.

3. **Sequence by payback (DS-06).**
   1. **Foundations (weeks 1–2, free):** business profile complete — categories,
      hours, service area, photos, products or services; name, address and phone
      identical across listings; website shows the same.
   2. **Reviews (from week 2, free):** a standing request process — asked in person,
      followed by a short link — to every satisfied customer. Reply to every review
      (see `smallbiz_customer_review_response.md`).
   3. **Existing customers (week 3, free):** one useful message a month to the
      permission-based list; a simple referral ask.
   4. **Neighbourhood (weeks 4–8, low cost):** two partnerships with non-competing
      businesses; one local event or community-group presence, following each
      group's rules on promotion.
   5. **Paid local ads (week 9+, only if 1–4 are done):** small, geographically tight,
      tied to one offer, tracked with its own code or number.

4. **Constrain to the owner's time (CM-02).** Total weekly hours in the plan must not
   exceed hours available. Cut from the bottom of the sequence, never the top.

5. **Set a kill signal per tactic (DP-13).** For each: the metric, the threshold, and
   the date. Example: "partnership flyer — fewer than 3 'heard from Café X' by week 8 → stop."

6. **Check (QA-01).** Goal fits capacity; hours fit availability; every tactic has a
   kill signal; nothing paid starts before the free foundations are done.

## Output Format

```
# Local marketing plan — [business], [12 weeks from date]

## Goal
[+n per week] × [ticket] = [+$ per week] by week 12; capacity [n]

## Measurement
Tally question: … | Profile metrics: … | Review count baseline: …

## Plan
| Weeks | Tactic | Owner hrs/wk | $ | Metric | Kill signal |

## Weekly hours check
[plan hours] vs [available]

## Week-12 review questions
```

## Verification

- [ ] The goal does not exceed stated capacity.
- [ ] Measurement starts in week 1.
- [ ] Foundations precede reviews, reviews precede paid.
- [ ] Every tactic has a metric and a dated kill signal.
- [ ] Weekly hours fit what the owner stated.

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **Social media posting is not a plan.** Without a tally question it produces
   activity, not evidence.
2. **Do not buy ads before the profile is finished.** Paid traffic to an incomplete
   listing with twelve reviews wastes the budget.
3. **Never offer incentives for reviews, and never write or buy them.** Platforms
   prohibit it, some jurisdictions regulate it, and it damages trust. Ask every
   customer, not only happy ones you selected.
4. **Do not over-fill capacity.** Marketing that brings more work than you can do well
   turns into slow service and one-star reviews.
5. **Contact only people who agreed to hear from you.** Your customer list is for
   customers who opted in; no purchased lists.
6. **Do not judge a tactic in week two.** Set the kill date up front and wait for it.
7. **Dual failure:** a plan with fifteen tactics gets none done. Five, done every week,
   beats fifteen started.

## Example Output

```
# Local marketing plan — Harbor Street Bike Repair, 28 Sep – 20 Dec 2026

## Goal
+10 repairs/week × $85 average ticket = +$850/week by week 12.
Capacity: shop can take 14 more repairs/week (mechanic schedule).

## Measurement
Tally at checkout: "How did you hear about us?" (profile, friend, partner, event,
ad, walked by). Profile baseline: 38 calls/month, 61 direction requests/month.
Reviews: 23 at 4.6.

## Plan
| Wks | Tactic | Hrs/wk | $ | Metric | Kill signal |
| 1–2 | Complete profile: services, hours, 20 photos; fix old phone number on 2 listings | 3 | 0 | Profile calls | — (foundation) |
| 2–12 | Ask every customer at pickup; text review link | 0.5 | 0 | New reviews/week | < 2/week by wk 6 → change the ask |
| 3–12 | Monthly service-tip email to 410 opted-in customers + "bring a friend" line | 1 | 0 | "Friend" tallies | < 4 by wk 8 → drop referral line |
| 4–12 | Partnership: tune-up voucher at Ridgeway Coffee and the climbing gym | 0.5 | 60 printing | "Partner" tallies | < 3 by wk 8 → stop |
| 6 | Saturday free safety check at farmers' market | 5 (one-off) | 40 | "Event" tallies | < 5 in 4 wks after → don't repeat |
| 9–12 | Local search ad, 3 km radius, "winter tune-up $65" | 0.5 | 200/mo | Code WINTER | < 8 redemptions by wk 12 → stop |

## Weekly hours check
Typical week: 0.5 + 1 + 0.5 + 0.5 = 2.5 hrs (3 in weeks 1–2, 7.5 in week 6);
owner has 3 → week 6 needs the Saturday covered by the mechanic.
Spend: $60 + $40 + $200 × 1 month (weeks 9–12) = $300 total.

## Week-12 review questions
Which tally sources grew? Did reviews reach 40? Is +10/week reached, and is the
mechanic's schedule still under capacity?
```

## Techniques Used

- **DS-06 Prioritization Guidance:** tactics sequenced by payback.
- **CM-02 Constraint Specification:** owner hours and capacity bound the plan.
- **DS-02 Metric Specification:** a tally question and profile metrics per tactic.
- **DP-13 Kill Signal Definition:** each tactic has a threshold and a date.
- **QA-01 Self-Verification:** goal, hours and sequencing checked before starting.
