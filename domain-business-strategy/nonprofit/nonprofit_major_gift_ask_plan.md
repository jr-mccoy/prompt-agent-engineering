# Major Gift Ask Plan

**Objective:** Turn a relationship with one donor into a specific, respectful
solicitation plan — ready or not ready, how much, for what, asked by whom, in what
conversation — and a stewardship plan that begins the day they answer.

**When to Use:**
- A donor has signalled interest in a larger commitment and nobody has a plan.
- A campaign has a gift table and named prospects, and each needs an ask strategy.
- A board member has agreed to join an ask and needs to know their role.

**Not this prompt if:**
- You are the donor deciding how to give tax-efficiently —
  `domain-finance/tax-planning/finance_charitable_giving_tax_strategy.md`.
- You are closing a commercial deal with a buying committee —
  `domain-sales-customer/sales/sales_mutual_close_plan.md`. A gift is not a
  purchase; the donor gets no deliverable, and pressure tactics from sales are wrong
  here.
- You need the argument itself — `nonprofit_case_for_support.md` first.

## Inputs

1. **Relationship history:** gifts (dates, amounts, purposes), volunteer roles,
   events attended, conversations and what the donor said, in their words.
2. **Stated interests:** which programs they ask about, what they have said they care
   about.
3. **Public information only:** published philanthropy, public board roles. No
   inference of wealth from property, address or employer.
4. The case for support and the specific funding need this gift could meet.
5. Candidate askers and their relationship to the donor.

## Method

1. **Readiness check (RT-05).** Three questions, each answered with evidence:
   - **Inclination:** has the donor shown sustained interest in *this* work?
   - **Linkage:** is there a person they trust who can ask?
   - **Capacity signal:** what has the donor *said or done* that suggests the amount
     is plausible? Past giving pattern and the donor's own words count; speculation
     does not.

   Any unanswered question means the next step is a cultivation move, not an ask.
   Name the move and the date.

2. **Set amount and purpose.** A specific figure tied to a specific budget item,
   sized from the donor's giving history and stated intent. The purpose must be
   something the donor has expressed interest in, and a real line in the budget.
   Offer at most one alternative (a smaller amount or a multi-year pledge).

3. **Choose the asker and the room (CM-09).** Who opens, who makes the ask, who
   stays quiet. The asker should have given themselves. State the authority boundary:
   what the asker may agree to on the spot (payment schedule, recognition
   preference) and what they may not (restricting the gift to a new purpose, naming
   rights, anything touching a gift agreement).

4. **Script the conversation.** Opening that connects to the donor's interest;
   update on the work; the ask in one sentence with the amount; **then silence**;
   the listening plan for each likely response.

5. **Pre-empt objections (NE-23).** For each plausible response — "not now",
   "less", "let me talk to my spouse or adviser", "I'd rather fund X" — a respectful
   answer and a next step. "Let me talk to my adviser" is a good answer and gets a
   follow-up date, not a rebuttal.

6. **Pledge and paperwork.** Payment schedule, whether a gift agreement is needed,
   and what must go to qualified review: restrictions, naming, non-cash assets,
   anything the donor asks about deductibility.

7. **Stewardship (DS-06).** Within 48 hours a personal thank-you; within a
   month a report on the specific purpose; a named steward and a calendar of
   touchpoints for the pledge period.

8. **Check (QA-01).** Amount traceable to history, purpose to budget, every donor
   fact to a source.

## Output Format

```
# Major gift plan — [donor code], [date]

## Readiness
| Question | Evidence | Status |
Verdict: [Ready to ask | Cultivate first → move, date]

## The ask
Amount: [x] | Purpose: [budget line] | Alternative: [y] | Pledge: [schedule]

## People
Asker: … | Opens: … | Authority: may agree to … / may not agree to …

## Conversation plan
Opening / update / ask sentence / silence / responses

## Objections
| Donor says | We say | Next step |

## Paperwork and review flags
## Stewardship calendar
| When | What | Who |
```

## Verification

- [ ] Each readiness answer cites something the donor did or said.
- [ ] The amount is justified by giving history and stated intent, not by need alone.
- [ ] The purpose is a real budget line the donor has shown interest in.
- [ ] The asker's authority boundary is written.
- [ ] Every objection response ends in a next step, not an argument.
- [ ] No donor-side tax advice appears; questions are referred to the donor's adviser.

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **Need is not readiness.** The organisation needing $50,000 says nothing about
   whether this donor is ready to give it.
2. **Do not research what the donor has not shared.** Estimating wealth from home
   value, employer or ZIP code is intrusive and often wrong; the plan uses giving
   history, the donor's own words and public philanthropy.
3. **One amount, stated.** "Whatever you feel comfortable with" leaves the donor
   doing the organisation's work.
4. **Do not promise what the asker cannot grant.** Naming rights, new restrictions
   and non-cash gifts go through the organisation's gift acceptance process.
5. **Not a sales close.** No urgency tactics, no assumptive close. A "not now" is
   answered with gratitude and a date.
6. **No tax advice to donors.** Deductibility, appreciated stock, donor-advised funds
   and bequests are questions for the donor's own adviser; the organisation provides
   its receipt and gift acceptance policy.
7. **Dual failure:** never asking because the relationship feels too warm is also a
   failure — the donor told you they were thinking about it.

## Example Output

```
# Major gift plan — Donor A (tutor since 2021), 24 Sep 2026

## Readiness
| Question | Evidence | Status |
| Inclination | Gave $1,000/yr 2021–2024, $2,500 in 2025; tutors weekly | Yes |
| Linkage | Board chair recruited Donor A as a tutor; they meet monthly | Yes |
| Capacity signal | Told ED on 12 Jun: "I want to do something bigger for the waitlist" | Yes |
Verdict: Ready to ask.

## The ask
Amount: $21,850 — 20 learners' full program year (20 × $1,092.50), half of the
40-learner expansion gap of $43,700 in the Reading for Work case.
Alternative: $10,925 — 10 learners.
Pledge: two payments of $10,925 (Jan and Jul 2027), or one payment.

## People
Asker: board chair (has given this year). Opens: program director with learner
progress data. Authority: may agree to the payment schedule and anonymous
recognition; may not agree to naming or restricting to a new program.

## Conversation plan
Update: 64 waiting; 42 of last year's 72 learners gained a reading level.
Ask: "Would you consider $21,850 to fund twenty of the forty places that clear
the waitlist?" Then stop talking.

## Objections
| Donor says | We say | Next step |
| "That's more than I expected." | "Ten places at $10,925 would still change ten lives." | Confirm which, or a date to decide |
| "I need to talk to my adviser." | "Of course — may we send the gift acceptance policy?" | Follow-up 14 Oct |
| "I'd rather fund tutor training." | "Training is $10,000 [verify]; we can restrict to it." | Treasurer confirms line |

## Paperwork and review flags
- Pledge over $10,000 → gift agreement using the organisation's template; treasurer reviews.
- Any question on deductibility → refer to donor's own adviser.

## Stewardship calendar
| When | What | Who |
| Within 48h | Handwritten thanks | Board chair |
| Mar 2027 | Enrolment report for the 20 places | Program director |
| Oct 2027 | Year-end outcome report | ED |
```

## Techniques Used

- **RT-05 Evidence-Based Reasoning:** readiness rests on what the donor did and said.
- **NE-23 Objection Pre-emption:** each likely response has an answer and a next step.
- **DS-06 Prioritization Guidance:** the stewardship calendar sequences follow-up.
- **CM-09 Authority Boundary Specification:** what the asker may and may not agree to.
- **QA-01 Self-Verification:** amount and purpose traced to history and budget.
