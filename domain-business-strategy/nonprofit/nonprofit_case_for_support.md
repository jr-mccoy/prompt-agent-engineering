# Case for Support

**Objective:** Produce the organisation's master fundraising argument — a single
internal document, sourced line by line, from which every appeal letter, grant
narrative and major-gift conversation is cut — so that staff, board and volunteers
tell the same true story with the same numbers.

**When to Use:**
- Board members describe the organisation differently from each other and from staff.
- Every new appeal or proposal starts from a blank page.
- A campaign, expansion or new program needs a written rationale before asks begin.
- The existing "case" is a brochure with adjectives and no costs.

**Not this prompt if:**
- You need ad imagery or visual campaign prompts —
  `domain-advertising/advertising_nonprofit_cause_marketing.md`.
- You are positioning a product against commercial competitors —
  `domain-business-strategy/go-to-market/marketing_competitive_differentiation.md`.
  A nonprofit's "why us" is about capacity and evidence, not share of wallet.
- You need one specific letter or one specific proposal — cut it from this document
  with `nonprofit_donor_appeal_letter.md` or `nonprofit_foundation_grant_proposal.md`.

## Inputs

1. Mission statement and the specific programs in scope.
2. **Need evidence:** local or sector data, each with source and year.
3. **Results evidence:** what the organisation has measured, with dates — outputs and
   outcomes kept separate. A logic model if one exists.
4. The program and organisational budgets, and the funding gap the case addresses.
5. Stories the organisation holds **with written consent**, if any.
6. Who will read derived versions: individual donors, foundations, corporate partners,
   board members making asks.

## Method

1. **State the problem in the reader's terms (RP-02).** One paragraph, one or two
   sourced figures, and the local face of it. Name who is affected and what it costs
   them, not what it costs the organisation.

2. **Answer "why now" honestly (NE-27).** What happens if nothing changes — a
   waitlist that grows, a service that closes, a cohort that ages out. The cost of
   inaction must be a documented consequence, not an implied catastrophe.

3. **Answer "why us" with evidence (RT-05).** Track record, relationships, staff
   expertise, cost per result compared with plausible alternatives. Every claim is
   either sourced or marked `[verify]`. Superlatives ("the only", "the leading")
   require proof or are removed.

4. **State the plan and its cost.** What the organisation will do with new money,
   in units a donor can picture, and the total. The funding gap is arithmetic:
   cost minus committed funding.

5. **Translate gifts into what they buy (CM-02).** Gift levels map to real line
   items in the budget. A "$45 buys…" statement must be true at the budget's own
   unit cost. Mark any gift level that funds general operations as such — honestly
   — rather than dressing it up as a specific purchase.

6. **Write the invitation.** What you are asking the reader to join, not merely pay
   for. One paragraph.

7. **Cut the derivative extracts.** A 150-word version, a 50-word version, a
   one-line version, and the three facts every board member must be able to say.

8. **Cross-check (QA-01).** Every number in every extract matches the master; every
   story has a consent record; every gift level reconciles to the budget.

## Output Format

```
# Case for support — [organisation / campaign]

## The problem          [paragraph, sourced]
## Why now              [documented consequence of inaction]
## Why us               | Claim | Evidence | Source/date |
## The plan and its cost
   Cost: [total] − committed [x] = gap [y]
## What a gift does     | Gift | Buys | Budget line | Unit cost check |
## The invitation       [paragraph]

## Extracts
150 words: … / 50 words: … / One line: …
Three facts every board member can say: 1. … 2. … 3. …

## Evidence register
| Figure | Source | Date | Used in |
## Consent register (stories)
| Story | Consent on file | Restrictions |
```

## Verification

- [ ] Every figure appears in the evidence register with a source and date.
- [ ] Gift-level statements reconcile to budget unit costs.
- [ ] The funding gap equals cost minus committed funding.
- [ ] Outputs and outcomes are not conflated.
- [ ] No story appears without consent recorded.
- [ ] Extracts contain no figure absent from the master.

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **A mission statement is not a case.** If the draft could belong to any
   organisation in the sector, it has no "why us".
2. **Need is not the same as urgency.** A problem that has existed for thirty years
   needs a documented reason the moment is now; otherwise say so plainly and argue
   on effectiveness instead.
3. **Do not fabricate the donor math.** "$25 feeds a family for a week" must be the
   organisation's real cost. Inflated impact math is discovered by exactly the
   donors who matter most.
4. **Never write a composite beneficiary as if real.** If a story is a composite,
   label it; if no consented story exists, use data and the program's own words.
5. **Overhead shame is not a message.** Do not promise "100% goes to programs"
   unless it is true and the operating budget is funded elsewhere.
6. **Superlatives need proof.** "Only provider in the county" is checked or removed.
7. **Dual failure:** a case so hedged it inspires nobody fails too — sourced claims
   can still be stated with conviction.

## Example Output

```
# Case for support — Riverbend Literacy: Reading for Work campaign

## The problem
[need: share of Marion County adults below basic literacy, source, year]. For an
adult who cannot read a safety notice or a job application, the cost is work.

## Why now
Riverbend's waitlist was 64 adults on 1 Aug 2026 (intake log), up from 31 a year
earlier. At current capacity the median wait is 7 months; 1 in 4 waitlisted
adults did not respond when a place opened (2025 intake review).

## Why us
| Claim | Evidence | Source/date |
| Learners progress | 58% of 72 learners gained one reading level in 2025 | Program assessments, Jan 2026 |
| Volunteers stay | 41 tutors, median tenure 2.3 years | Volunteer roster, Aug 2026 |
| Cost-effective | $1,093 per learner per year | FY26 program budget |

## The plan and its cost
Grow from 80 to 120 learners a year: cost $131,100 − committed $87,400 = gap $43,700.

## What a gift does
| Gift | Buys | Budget line | Check |
| $45 | One learner's assessments for a year | Assessments $3,600 / 80 | ✓ |
| $1,093 | One learner's full program year | Program $87,400 / 80 | ✓ |
| $10,000 | Tutor training for the expansion year | Training line | [verify with finance] |
| Any amount | General operations that keep the doors open | Unrestricted | Stated plainly |

## The invitation
Join the neighbours who make it possible for an adult in Marion County to read the
paperwork of their own life.

## Extracts
One line: Riverbend teaches adults to read — $1,093 funds a learner for a year,
and 64 are waiting.
Three facts: 64 waiting; 58% gain a reading level; $1,093 a learner.

## Evidence register
| Figure | Source | Date | Used in |
| 64 waitlisted | Intake log | 1 Aug 2026 | Why now; one-liner |
| $1,093 | FY26 budget ($87,400 / 80) | Jul 2026 | Gifts; one-liner |

## Consent register
| Story | Consent | Restrictions |
| None supplied | — | Use data; request consented stories from program staff |
```

Arithmetic check: unit cost is $87,400 / 80 = $1,092.50, shown rounded as $1,093;
the expansion cost is 120 × $1,092.50 = $131,100, and the gap is $131,100 − $87,400
= $43,700 — assuming unit cost holds at the larger scale, which the finance lead
must confirm before the campaign launches.

## Techniques Used

- **RP-02 Audience-Specific Framing:** the problem is written in the reader's terms.
- **RT-05 Evidence-Based Reasoning:** the evidence register traces every claim.
- **NE-27 Cost of Inaction Framing:** "why now" rests on a documented consequence.
- **CM-02 Constraint Specification:** gift levels are constrained to real unit costs.
- **QA-01 Self-Verification:** extracts are checked against the master.
