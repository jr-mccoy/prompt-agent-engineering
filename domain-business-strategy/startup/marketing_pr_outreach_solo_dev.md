# PR and Media Outreach for Solo App Developers

**Objective:** Get legitimate press coverage for your app through targeted, personalized outreach to journalists, bloggers, and content creators -- without a PR agency, without a budget, and without feeling sleazy about self-promotion. One well-placed article can drive more downloads than months of social media.

**When to Use:** Use this when you have a launched app (or are within 2 weeks of launch) with a clear story to tell. PR outreach works best when you have a genuine narrative: a unique angle, impressive traction, a compelling personal story, or a product that fills a gap journalists have noticed. Do not attempt PR outreach if you have nothing genuinely newsworthy -- forced pitches waste everyone's time and burn bridges.

**Important context:** Journalists receive 50-200 pitches per day. Yours must stand out. The single biggest mistake solo developers make is sending mass, generic emails. This guide teaches you to send fewer, better pitches to carefully selected contacts. Quality over quantity is the only strategy that works without a PR team.

---

## Context Gathering

Before crafting your outreach strategy, provide:

1. **Your Story**
   - What is genuinely interesting or unusual about your app or your journey?
   - Do you have a compelling origin story? (Quit your job, built it for a family member, scratched your own itch)
   - Do you have surprising metrics? (Growth rate, user engagement, revenue from a niche)
   - Is there a trend your app connects to? (AI, privacy, sustainability, remote work)

2. **App Maturity**
   - Is this a launch announcement, a major update, or a milestone story?
   - What is your current download count and rating?
   - Do you have notable user testimonials or case studies?

3. **Target Publications**
   - What publications do your target users read?
   - Have you seen competitor apps covered in media? Where?
   - Are there niche blogs, newsletters, or YouTube channels in your app's category?

4. **Assets Available**
   - Do you have professional-quality screenshots?
   - Do you have a logo in multiple formats?
   - Do you have a headshot or founder photo?
   - Do you have a one-page fact sheet about the app?

5. **PR Experience**
   - Have you ever pitched media before? What happened?
   - Do you know any journalists personally?
   - Are you comfortable with interviews (written, audio, video)?

## Instructions

### CRITICAL: Verification Requirements

1. **Journalist Relevance Verification** -- Every journalist on the outreach list must have written about similar apps, similar topics, or similar developer stories in the last 6 months. Verify by checking their recent articles. Do not pitch a journalist who only covers enterprise software about your consumer app.
2. **Pitch Personalization Audit** -- Each pitch must reference at least one specific article the journalist wrote. Generic pitches ("Dear journalist, I have an exciting app") are immediately deleted. Verify personalization before sending.
3. **Press Kit Completeness** -- The press kit must include all required elements (screenshots, logo, description, fact sheet, founder bio) before any outreach begins. Sending a pitch without a press kit wastes the opportunity.
4. **Timing Validation** -- Outreach must be timed to Tuesday-Thursday, morning hours in the journalist's timezone. Verify timezone before scheduling sends.
5. **Follow-Up Discipline** -- Maximum one follow-up email per journalist, sent 5-7 days after initial pitch. More than one follow-up is harassment. Verify follow-up policy.
6. **Acceptable Null Result** -- If honest assessment shows the app has no genuinely newsworthy angle (no unique story, no impressive metrics, no trend connection), recommend delaying PR outreach. Instead, focus on building a story worth telling: reach a milestone, get a notable user, or create interesting data. Pitching without substance wastes both your time and the journalist's.

### False-Positive Prevention

- **DO NOT** send mass emails. Personalized outreach to 15-20 well-chosen journalists beats blasting 200 generic pitches.
- **DO NOT** use PR distribution services (PRNewswire, etc.) unless you have a genuinely major announcement. They are expensive and get ignored for small app launches.
- **DO NOT** follow up more than once. If a journalist does not respond to your pitch and one follow-up, they are not interested. Move on.
- **DO NOT** pitch embargoed news unless a journalist specifically agrees to the embargo. Solo developers rarely need embargoes.
- **DO NOT** lie about metrics, users, or traction. Journalists verify claims, and getting caught destroys your credibility permanently.
- **DO NOT** expect guaranteed coverage. Even excellent pitches have a 5-15% response rate. Plan for rejection as the default.
- **DO** lead with the story, not the product. Journalists cover stories, not product announcements.
- **DO** make their job easy. Provide everything they need (screenshots, quotes, data) in the first email.
- **DO** respect their time. Keep pitches under 200 words.
- **DO** build relationships before you need them. Follow journalists on social media, engage with their work, share their articles.

### Phase 1: Target Identification -- Finding the Right Journalists

Not all press is equal. An article in a niche blog read by your exact target audience is worth more than a mention on TechCrunch read by millions who will never use your app.

#### Publication Tiers for App Developers

| Tier | Publication Type | Examples | Difficulty | Impact | Best For |
|------|-----------------|---------|------------|--------|----------|
| **1 (Dream)** | Major tech publications | TechCrunch, The Verge, Ars Technica, Wired | Very hard | Very high but broad | Viral moments, credibility |
| **2 (Target)** | App-focused publications | Android Authority, Android Police, 9to5Google, XDA | Hard | High and targeted | Android-specific apps |
| **3 (Sweet Spot)** | Niche blogs and newsletters | Category-specific blogs, industry newsletters | Medium | Medium but very targeted | Niche apps, B2B tools |
| **4 (Accessible)** | YouTube reviewers and podcasts | App reviewers, indie dev podcasters | Easy-Medium | Medium, long-tail | All app types |
| **5 (Foundational)** | Indie dev communities | IndieHackers, Hacker News, Product Hunt | Easy | Low-Medium but community-driven | Developer tools, early traction |

**Strategy for solo developers:** Focus 70% of effort on Tier 3 (niche blogs) and Tier 4 (YouTube/podcast). These are most likely to respond, most targeted to your audience, and easiest to personalize pitches for.

#### How to Find Specific Journalists

**Step 1: Search for who covers your space**

```
Google searches:
- "[competitor app name] review"
- "best [your app category] apps [current year]"
- "[your app category] app launch"
- "indie developer [your category]"

Note: Write down every journalist name, publication, and article URL.
```

**Step 2: Build your outreach list**

| Journalist | Publication | Recent Article | Email | Twitter | Notes |
|-----------|-------------|----------------|-------|---------|-------|
| [Name] | [Pub] | [Article title + URL] | [Email] | [Handle] | [Relevance note] |

**Target: 15-20 journalists.** Not more. Quality outreach to 15 people takes 3-4 hours and yields better results than mass email to 200.

**Step 3: Find email addresses**

- Check the journalist's author page on the publication
- Check their Twitter/X bio
- Use Hunter.io (free tier: 25 searches/month)
- Use the publication's standard format (firstname@publication.com, firstname.lastname@publication.com)
- Check their personal blog or website

### Phase 2: Pitch Crafting -- The Personalized Email

Your pitch email must accomplish three things in under 200 words:
1. Show you know who they are and what they cover
2. Tell a compelling story (not describe a product)
3. Make it easy for them to say yes

#### Pitch Template (Adapt, Do Not Copy Verbatim)

```
Subject: [Specific angle] -- solo dev story for [Publication]

Hi [First Name],

I read your piece on [specific article title] and [specific
observation about the article -- shows you actually read it].

I'm a solo developer who just [launched / hit milestone / built
something relevant to their beat], and I thought it might be
a story worth sharing with [Publication] readers.

[THE HOOK -- one or two sentences that make this newsworthy]:
- "[App Name] hit [metric] in [timeframe] with $0 marketing budget"
- "I quit my job at [company] to build an app my [family member] needed"
- "There are 50 [category] apps, and they all ignore [underserved niche]"

I've put together a press kit with screenshots, data, and my
full story: [press kit link]

Would this be interesting for [Publication]? Happy to share
more detail or jump on a quick call.

Best,
[Your Name]
[App Name] -- [one line description]
[App link]
```

#### Pitch Angles That Work for Solo Developers

| Angle | Why Journalists Care | Example |
|-------|---------------------|---------|
| **David vs. Goliath** | People root for the underdog | "Solo developer competes with Google's [app category]" |
| **Origin story** | Human interest | "Engineer builds app after [personal experience]" |
| **Surprising metrics** | Data makes news | "[App] grew to 50K users in 3 months with zero marketing spend" |
| **Trend connection** | Ties into bigger narrative | "[App] rides the [AI/privacy/digital wellness] wave" |
| **Unique approach** | Technical or design novelty | "This app uses [unusual approach] to solve [common problem]" |
| **Community story** | Shows impact beyond downloads | "How a community of 500 [users] shaped this app's roadmap" |
| **Counter-narrative** | Challenges conventional wisdom | "Why this developer chose Android-only when everyone says 'iOS first'" |

**Important:** You need ONE strong angle, not all of them. Pick the most compelling and lead with it.

### Phase 3: Press Kit Creation

A press kit makes a journalist's job easy. Easy jobs get done. Hard jobs get skipped.

#### Press Kit Contents

Create a single webpage or shared folder containing:

| Item | Format | Description |
|------|--------|-------------|
| **App Description** | Text (250 words max) | What it does, who it's for, what makes it different |
| **Fact Sheet** | Text or PDF | Launch date, download count, rating, pricing, platforms, founder name |
| **Screenshots** | PNG, high-res (1080x1920 min) | 5-8 screenshots showing core features, with and without device frames |
| **App Icon** | PNG, SVG | High-res, multiple sizes (512x512, 1024x1024) |
| **Logo** | PNG, SVG | If different from app icon, include both |
| **Founder Photo** | JPG, high-res | Professional headshot (does not need to be studio-quality, but not a selfie) |
| **Founder Bio** | Text (100 words) | Who you are, relevant background, why you built this |
| **Demo Video** | MP4 or YouTube link | 30-60 second app walkthrough |
| **Press Release** | Text or PDF | Formal announcement (optional but professional) |
| **Contact Info** | Text | Email, phone (optional), social media, website |

#### Press Release Template

```markdown
FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE

# [Headline: Action Verb + App Name + Key Benefit]

**[City, Date]** -- [Your Name], a solo developer, today announced
[App Name], a [category] app that [key differentiator].

## The Problem
[2-3 sentences about the problem your app solves]

## The Solution
[2-3 sentences about how your app solves it differently]

## Key Features
- [Feature 1]: [Benefit]
- [Feature 2]: [Benefit]
- [Feature 3]: [Benefit]

## Availability
[App Name] is available now on [Google Play Store / platforms].
[Pricing information].

## About [Your Name]
[2-3 sentences about you and your background]

## Press Kit
[Link to press kit]

## Contact
[Your Name]
[Email]
[Phone -- optional]
[Website]
```

#### Hosting Your Press Kit

**Free options:**
- **Notion public page** -- Quick, easy, looks professional enough
- **Google Drive shared folder** -- Simple, universal access
- **GitHub Pages** -- Free, professional, developer-friendly
- **Your landing page** -- Add a /press page

### Phase 4: Outreach Execution

#### Timing Your Outreach

| Factor | Best Practice | Why |
|--------|-------------|-----|
| **Day of week** | Tuesday, Wednesday, or Thursday | Monday = inbox overload. Friday = checked out. |
| **Time of day** | 8-10 AM in journalist's timezone | Early morning, before inbox floods |
| **Relative to launch** | 1-2 weeks before launch (with embargo) OR launch day | Pre-launch for reviews. Launch day for news. |
| **Avoid** | Holidays, major tech events (CES, WWDC, I/O), breaking news days | Your pitch gets buried |

#### Sending Protocol

```
Week -2: Send pitches to Tier 3-4 (niche blogs, YouTube reviewers)
         These take longer to create content and are more accessible.

Week -1: Send pitches to Tier 2 (app-focused publications)
         They need a few days lead time for articles.

Launch Day: Send to Tier 1 (major tech publications) and Tier 5 (communities)
            Major pubs want fresh news. Communities want launch posts.

Week +1: Follow up (ONE follow-up only) with anyone who didn't respond.
```

#### Email Sending Checklist

Before hitting send on each pitch:

```markdown
- [ ] Subject line is specific and compelling (not "Check out my app")
- [ ] Opening line references their specific work
- [ ] Pitch is under 200 words
- [ ] Story angle is clear in the first two sentences
- [ ] Press kit link is included and working
- [ ] App store link is included
- [ ] Contact information is complete
- [ ] No typos (especially in their name and publication)
- [ ] Sent from a professional email address (not throwaway123@gmail.com)
- [ ] BCC, not CC, if you're emailing multiple people (but you should email individually)
```

### Phase 5: Follow-Up Etiquette

#### The One Follow-Up Rule

Send exactly ONE follow-up email, 5-7 business days after the initial pitch.

**Follow-up template:**

```
Subject: Re: [Original subject line]

Hi [First Name],

I wanted to follow up on my note from [day]. I know your inbox
is busy, so I'll keep this brief.

[One new piece of information -- a metric update, new feature,
or additional angle]:

"Since I last reached out, [App Name] has [new development]."

The press kit is here if it's useful: [link]

Either way, thanks for your time. I enjoy reading your work
on [Publication].

Best,
[Your Name]
```

#### If They Respond

**If they say yes:**
- Respond within 2 hours. Speed matters.
- Provide anything they ask for immediately.
- Be available for calls at their convenience.
- Do not try to control the narrative. Provide facts and let them write.
- After publication, share the article widely and thank them publicly.

**If they say no or not now:**
- Thank them graciously: "Thanks for letting me know. I'll reach out again if we hit a bigger milestone."
- Do not argue, negotiate, or guilt-trip.
- Keep them on your list for future announcements.

**If they never respond:**
- After one follow-up, stop. No response IS a response.
- They may cover you later. Journalists often save pitches for roundup articles.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-01 (Clear Objective Statement):** Objective frames PR as a targeted outreach activity with measurable outcomes, not vague "get press coverage."
- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** Five-phase workflow from target identification through follow-up mirrors the real PR campaign process.
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis):** Publication tier matrix evaluates difficulty, impact, and fit simultaneously. Pitch angle analysis covers multiple storytelling approaches.
- **CM-01 (Explicit Context Framing):** Context gathering captures story assets, publication landscape, and developer comfort level before strategy design.
- **CM-02 (Constraint Specification):** Follow-up limits, word count caps, and timing windows are explicitly constrained to prevent common mistakes.
- **QA-02 (Adversarial Thinking):** False-positive prevention specifically addresses mass emailing, misleading metrics, and unrealistic expectations.

## Customization Guide

1. **For developer tools / technical products:** Target developer-focused publications (The New Stack, Dev.to featured articles, Hacker News, Software Engineering Daily podcast). Lead with technical innovation rather than human interest.
2. **For consumer health/wellness apps:** Target health-focused publications and podcasts. Lead with user outcome stories (with permission). Emphasize privacy and evidence-based approaches.
3. **For B2B/enterprise apps:** Target industry-specific trade publications rather than general tech press. Lead with business outcomes (cost savings, efficiency gains) and customer case studies.
4. **For apps in crowded categories:** Your pitch angle must be sharply differentiated. "Another to-do list app" is not a story. "The to-do list app that works entirely offline for field workers" is a story.
5. **For developers who have been covered before:** Reference your previous coverage in new pitches. "You may remember our launch coverage in [Publication]. Since then, we've [milestone]." Previous coverage is strong social proof for new journalists.
6. **For international markets:** Target local-language tech publications in your primary markets. Adapt pitches to local media norms (some cultures prefer more formal outreach). Use Google Translate cautiously; consider hiring a native speaker for critical pitches.
