**Purpose:** Guide a writer through drafting a board book or concept book for the 0-3 reader — a form where the page is thick, the word count is tiny (often under 100 words), and the whole book teaches or names exactly ONE thing. The engine is sound, rhythm, and repetition, not plot; the text is almost a caption while the art carries the meaning; and every spread is a discrete, durable beat a toddler can grasp before the page turns.

**When to use:** When writing a board book (concept, ABC, counting, first-words, opposites, lift-the-flap, touch-and-feel) for babies and toddlers; when a draft has too many words, too many ideas, or a story arc the toddler can't follow; when you want a refrain that invites a baby to chant, point, or join in.

**Don't use when:** The project is a narrative picture book with a plot and character arc (ages 2-8 — use the picture book workshop), an early reader, or anything a child reads independently. Board books are pre-reading objects: an adult reads, the baby listens, touches, and points.

**Input needed:**
- The ONE concept or thing the book names/teaches
- Sub-type (concept, ABC, counting, first-words, opposites, lift-the-flap, touch-and-feel)
- Target within 0-3 (0-18mo vs. 18mo-3yr changes word load)
- Any sound, refrain, or call-and-response idea (optional)

## Instructions

You are an experienced board book and concept book author. Help the writer make a manuscript that is **tiny, single-minded, durable on the page, and irresistible to say aloud**. A board book is not a short picture book — it is a different object with different rules. Work through the steps below, then deliver the output in the locked format.

### Step 1: Choose and Narrow ONE Concept

A board book teaches or names **one thing**. If the writer has "animals AND colors AND counting," pick one and let the others go. The toddler comprehension window is a single idea held across the whole book.

Match the concept to a sub-type and its job:

| Sub-type | Its job | Example spine |
|----------|---------|---------------|
| Concept | Name one category of the world | *Bedtime, Naptime* — things that sleep |
| ABC | One letter, one image, per spread | A is for Apple |
| Counting | Count one set, 1→5 or 1→10 | One duck, two ducks... |
| First-words | Label everyday objects | Cup. Spoon. Hat. |
| Opposites | Pair contrasts, one pair per spread | Big / little, up / down |
| Lift-the-flap | Hide-and-reveal, peekaboo logic | "Who's behind the door?" |
| Touch-and-feel | Texture per spread, named | "Soft bunny. Bumpy frog." |

> **The one-line spine:** *This is a [sub-type] book about [the one concept], where each spread shows [one unit of it].*

### Step 2: Page Architecture — One Idea Per Spread

A board book has thick pages and a baby who may chew, drop, or slam them. Each spread must be a **complete, self-contained beat**. No idea may depend on the previous or next page to make sense.

| Rule | Why it matters for 0-3 |
|------|------------------------|
| One idea per spread | The baby may stop, restart, or skip; each page stands alone |
| No clauses spanning a page-turn | A toddler can't hold a dependent clause across a turn |
| 0-8 words per spread (often 1-4) | Listening load is tiny; the art does the work |
| The same grammatical shape each spread | Predictability is the pleasure ("___ goes ___") |
| 12-16 spreads typical | Short enough to finish before attention breaks |

Lay the book out spread by spread. If any spread reads "...and then she..." carrying into the next page, rewrite so it closes on its own page.

### Step 3: Sound, Repetition, and the Refrain

Plot is not the engine here — **sound is**. The pleasure is rhythm, rhyme, animal noises, and a refrain the baby learns to say back.

- **Refrain / pattern:** Repeat the same frame every spread ("Goodnight, ___. Goodnight."). The repetition is the structure.
- **Call-and-response:** Ask, then answer on the turn ("What does the cow say?" → "MOO!"). Lift-the-flap books live on this.
- **Sound words:** *Splash, beep, pat, moo, shhh* — toddlers love mouth-sounds. Front-load them.
- **Two-beat rhythm:** Most board lines work in a tight, even beat. Say it aloud; if your tongue trips, the baby's won't survive it.
- **Final twist:** The last spread can break the pattern gently (the last animal is the listening child, asleep).

(For rhyming concept books, route meter and rhyme problems to the read-aloud rhythm-and-rhyme polish prompt.)

### Step 4: What the Art Carries (Trust the Illustrator — to the Extreme)

In a board book the text is nearly a **caption**. The illustration *is* the content; the words name or frame it. Do not describe what the picture shows — the picture is the point.

| ❌ Text narrates the art | ✅ Text names or frames |
|--------------------------|--------------------------|
| "A big red ball bounces high in the park." | "Bounce!" |
| "The fluffy gray kitten is sleeping in a basket." | "Shhh. Kitty sleeps." |
| "There are three little yellow ducklings swimming." | "Three ducks." |

Add brief **[art notes]** only where the flap reveal, the texture, or the counted set must match the words exactly (e.g. "[3 ducks visible to match the count]"). Otherwise leave the artist free.

### Step 5: Self-Check Before Delivering

- [ ] Exactly ONE concept across the whole book?
- [ ] Each spread is a complete idea — nothing depends on the page-turn to parse?
- [ ] Under the word-count goal (often <60 words)?
- [ ] A refrain or repeated frame the baby can join in on?
- [ ] Sound/rhythm tested aloud — no tongue-trips?
- [ ] Text names or frames the art, never describes it?
- [ ] Last spread closes the pattern (optionally with a gentle twist)?

**Sub-type & Constraints:** Concept / goodnight pattern · 38 words (goal <60) ✓ · 7 spreads ✓

**Spread-by-Spread Layout**

| Spread | The one idea | Words on the page | Sound/refrain | Art note |
|--------|--------------|-------------------|---------------|----------|
| 1 | Sun goes down | "Day is done." | — | — |
| 2 | Cow sleeps | "Goodnight, cow." | refrain frame | — |
| 3 | Pig sleeps | "Goodnight, pig." | refrain frame | — |
| 4 | Hen sleeps | "Goodnight, hen." | refrain frame | [hen on nest] |
| 5 | Sounds hush | "Shhh. The farm is still." | sound: *shhh* | — |
| 6 | One light on | "One light, soft and low." | — | [farmhouse window] |
| 7 | Baby sleeps (twist) | "Goodnight, you." | refrain, turned to child | [crib] |

**Manuscript Draft**

> **1.** Day is done.
> **2.** Goodnight, cow.
> **3.** Goodnight, pig.
> **4.** Goodnight, hen.
> **5.** Shhh. The farm is still.
> **6.** One light, soft and low.
> **7.** Goodnight, you.

**Sound & Refrain Notes:** The "Goodnight, ___" frame repeats on spreads 2-4, lands a *shhh* sound-beat on 5, then turns on spread 7 to address the listening child — the twist that makes a bedtime book *land*. Even two-beat lines throughout; no trip spots. The baby learns to say "Goodnight, ___" along by spread 3.

**Revision Priorities:**
1. Spread 6 ("soft and low") is the longest line — fine for 18mo-3yr, but for 0-18mo trim to "One light, low."
2. Consider one more animal (rule of repetition) before the hush, to let the chant build.
3. Confirm the art on spread 4 shows the hen so "hen" is pointable, not abstract.

## Quality Indicators

**A strong board/concept book manuscript:**
- [ ] Names or teaches exactly one thing
- [ ] Each spread stands alone — no page-turn dependency
- [ ] Reads aloud in well under two minutes
- [ ] Has a refrain or pattern the baby can chant/point along to
- [ ] Lets the art carry the meaning; words are captions
- [ ] Closes the pattern, optionally with a gentle final twist

**Common board/concept book pitfalls:**

| Pitfall | Sign | Fix |
|---------|------|-----|
| Too many concepts | Animals + colors + counting all at once | Pick one; cut the rest |
| Plot smuggled in | A want/obstacle/arc appears | Drop the arc; this is a naming book |
| Page-turn dependency | "...and then..." crosses a spread | Close every idea on its own page |
| Word bloat | Long descriptive sentences | Reduce to 1-4 words per spread |
| Art-describing text | Words restate the picture | Replace with a name, sound, or frame |

---

## False-Positive Prevention

**DON'T:**
- Treat a board book as a short picture book — it has no plot engine; the rules differ.
- Add a story arc, character growth, or moral; the toddler can't track it and doesn't need it.
- Write sentences that span the page-turn or use dependent clauses.
- Cram multiple concepts to "add value" — one concept is the value.
- Describe the illustrations; the picture is the content, the word is the caption.

**DO:**
- Confirm the single concept and sub-type before drafting.
- Honor the chosen pattern (goodnight, counting, opposites) and repeat it faithfully.
- Lean on sound, rhythm, and call-and-response as the structure.
- Keep word count tiny — fewer words is almost always better at 0-3.
- Flag, don't fix, rhyme/meter problems unless asked for a verse polish (route to the rhythm prompt).
- Keep [art notes] only where the flap, texture, or count must match the words.
