# Children's Writing — Authoring for Young Readers

Craft-forward prompts for **writing kid-friendly material** — children's fiction and nonfiction — for aspiring and working authors. This domain is about *creating content for child audiences*, not teaching children (see `domain-education-teaching/`) or talking to your own child (see `domain-parenting/`).

The set covers the whole shelf: board/concept books, picture books, early readers, chapter books, middle-grade novels, and the upper-MG/young-teen crossover (fiction), plus narrative and expository/STEM nonfiction — backed by cross-cutting craft tools (openings, dialogue, character, revision, reading-level, read-aloud rhythm, hard topics), representation and illustrator-collaboration guidance, and an author-facing publishing/business track (query, synopsis, pitch).

**Expansion roadmap:** see [`EXPANSION_ROADMAP.md`](EXPANSION_ROADMAP.md) for a prioritized brainstorm of candidate future prompts (series bibles, self-publishing, wordless picture books, and more) — not yet built, just scoped.

## Core Conventions

Every prompt in this domain is built on a few load-bearing principles drawn from children's-book craft:

- **Child agency.** The child character drives the story and resolves the problem — not a parent, teacher, or rescuer.
- **No preaching.** Theme emerges from story; stated morals and "lessons" get cut. Entertainment is a legitimate goal.
- **Respect the reader.** "For kids" never means "dumbed down." Simplify language, not depth, voice, or emotional truth.
- **Trust the illustrator.** In illustrated forms, the text doesn't narrate what the picture will show.
- **Read aloud.** Picture books and early readers are heard as much as read; rhythm is a craft requirement.
- **Accuracy is non-negotiable in nonfiction.** No invented dialogue, dates, quotes, or scenes; inferences are flagged as inferences; every specific traces to a source. The AI must flag unverified claims rather than supply facts from memory.

Three conventions extend that rigor into the newer corners of the domain:

- **Representation humility.** The across-difference and inclusive-design prompts help an author do their homework — they are **not** a substitute for lived experience, own-voices authorship, or a paid sensitivity/authenticity reader. The AI surfaces risks and questions; it never certifies cultural accuracy or invents cultural fact.
- **Publishing anti-fabrication.** The business prompts never invent comp titles, agent or publisher names, sales/advance figures, market statistics, or agency-specific submission rules. Anything unverifiable is flagged **VERIFY** or left as a bracketed placeholder for the author to research.
- **Age boundary.** This domain runs from board books (0-3) through the upper-MG/young-teen crossover (11-14). Mature teen YA (explicit content, adult themes) is out of scope — see `domain-creative-writing/`.

## Age Categories & Word Counts (quick reference)

| Category | Age | Word count | Notes |
|----------|-----|-----------|-------|
| Board / concept book | 0-3 | ≤100 | One concept per book; durable page logic; thick pages |
| Picture book | 2-8 | ≤500-600 (fiction); ≤1,000 (NF) | ~32 pages standard; read-aloud |
| Early reader | 5-9 | ~100-2,500 (often ~1,200) | Leveled, controlled vocabulary, heavy spot art |
| Chapter book | 6-10 | 5,000-15,000 | 8-15 short chapters, occasional art |
| Middle grade | 8-12 | ~30,000-55,000 | Few/no illustrations; hope on the horizon |
| Upper-MG / young-teen crossover | 11-14 | ~40,000-60,000 | Rising interiority; short of mature teen YA |
| Young adult (mature) | 14+ | 50,000-80,000 | **Out of scope** → `domain-creative-writing/` |

*Guidelines, not laws — historical/fantasy run longer; many modern picture books run far shorter.*

## Anti-Patterns This Domain Guards Against

- **Didacticism** — turning a story into a lesson; stated morals.
- **Vocabulary-dumbing** — stripping voice and depth in the name of "simple."
- **Art-describing text** — prose that narrates what the illustration already shows.
- **Adult-rescue plots** — grown-ups solving the child's problem.
- **Forced rhyme** — bad meter and near-rhymes (a top rejection reason).
- **Nonfiction fabrication** — invented quotes, dates, scenes, or feelings-as-fact.
- **Toxic positivity** — pasting on false cheer instead of earned hope.
- **Stereotype & the single story** — flat, tokenized, or stereotyped representation of identities; the AI certifying accuracy it can't verify.
- **Publishing fabrication** — invented comp titles, agents, sales figures, or submission rules.
