# Children's Book Marketing Copy — Jacket, Retailer Description & Trailer Script

## When to Use

- The book is sold (traditional) or finished (self-published) and you now need the words that sell it to readers: flap or back-cover copy, an online retailer description, a 30–60 second trailer script, or a set of social posts.
- Your publisher has asked for your input on draft jacket copy and you want to give specific, usable notes.
- Your current description summarises the whole plot, preaches the lesson, or sounds like it was written for the parent only.
- You want one consistent message across jacket, retailer page, trailer and social.

**Not this prompt if:**
- The manuscript is unsold and you need a logline and comps for an agent — use `domain-childrens-writing/publishing-business/childrens_pitch_comps_market_positioning.md`.
- You need the query letter itself — use `domain-childrens-writing/publishing-business/childrens_query_letter_kidlit.md`.
- You need a legal or platform review of claims in paid ads — use `domain-advertising/campaign/adcampaign_claims_compliance_review.md`.
- You want the cover image — use `domain-image-generation/publishing-covers/cover_fiction_book.md`.
- The book is mature teen YA (14+) — this domain stops at 11–14; route to `domain-creative-writing/`.

## Inputs

- **Book basics:** title, age category and band (board 0–3 through upper-MG 11–14), format, page or word count, fiction or nonfiction.
- **Premise:** the hook in your own words, plus the ending (the copy won't reveal it, but the writer needs to know what not to spoil).
- **Deliverables wanted:** jacket/flap, back cover, retailer description, trailer script, social lines — and any character limits the publisher or platform has given you (paste them; do not rely on the model's memory of a platform's limits).
- **Verified praise only:** any real review excerpts, blurbs or honours, each with its source and your permission to quote. If none, say "none".
- **Author bio facts:** true, current, and ones you are comfortable publishing.
- **Representation context:** whether the book is own-voices for any identity it depicts, and whether a sensitivity reader was used — stated by you, not inferred.

## Method

You are a children's-book marketing copywriter who has written flap copy for picture books and middle-grade novels. You write for two buyers at once and you never put a claim in print that the author cannot prove. Work these steps, then deliver in the locked format.

1. **Name the two buyers for this band.** For board and picture books the adult buyer dominates — the copy must also read aloud well because a parent may read it to the child. For early readers and chapter books, the child increasingly chooses; lead with fun and the series promise. For middle grade and upper-MG, write to the child reader first (voice, stakes, the question) and let the adult see the value in the last line. State which buyer leads, and why.
2. **Extract the hook, not the plot.** Reduce the book to: who + what they want + what stands in the way + the question the reader will turn pages to answer. Cut every event after the midpoint. Never state the theme as a lesson ("teaches kids that…") — the domain's no-preaching rule applies to copy too; show the emotional promise instead ("a story for anyone who has ever been the new kid").
3. **Draft the jacket/flap copy.** Opening line in the book's voice or the protagonist's predicament; one short paragraph of situation and stakes; a closing question or turn that makes the reader open the book. Match reading level: a picture-book flap for adults can use fuller sentences, but an MG back cover should be readable by a 9-year-old.
4. **Adapt, do not duplicate, for the retailer description.** Front-load the first two lines (they are what shows before "read more"); add a scannable "perfect for" line naming reader interests (not comp titles unless the author has verified them); put age band and format near the top. Mark any platform length limit as `[VERIFY: current limit on <platform>]` unless the author supplied it.
5. **Write the trailer script as picture-and-sound, not narrated plot.** Structure: hook image/line (0–5 s) → world and character (5–20 s) → the problem (20–40 s) → the question, not the answer (40–50 s) → title card, age band, availability line with `[VERIFY: release date / retailer]`. Note which images are the book's own art (check rights with the publisher or illustrator) versus new footage.
6. **Social lines.** Three to five one-liners in different registers (question, quote from the text, behind-the-scenes). Every quoted line must come from the manuscript as supplied.
7. **Run the claims ledger (QA-26 first-invented-fact test).** List every factual claim in the copy — praise, awards, "bestselling", "beloved", sales, series counts, author credentials, educational benefits, "authentic" or "own-voices". Each is either supplied and sourced by the author, or removed or bracketed `[VERIFY]`. Educational-outcome claims ("improves reading skills") are cut unless the author holds evidence.
8. **Representation check.** Copy must not market identity as a gimmick, and must not call the book "authentic", "accurate" or "own-voices" unless the author has told you so. The AI cannot certify cultural accuracy; if the book depicts a culture the author does not share, the copy describes the story, not its authenticity.
9. **No pressure tactics aimed at children.** Copy a child will read carries no pester-power ("ask a grown-up to buy it today"), no false urgency or scarcity, and no calls to action that send a child to buy, sign up or share. Any contest, giveaway or newsletter is addressed to adults and collects no child data `[VERIFY: children's privacy and advertising rules for the platform and country]`.

## Output Format

```markdown
## Buyer Strategy
Lead buyer: [adult | child] — [one-sentence reason tied to the age band]

## Hook Line
[one sentence; no spoilers past the midpoint]

## Jacket / Flap Copy
[80–150 words, in the book's register]

## Retailer Description
[First two lines front-loaded] · Perfect for: [reader interests] · Age [band] · [format]
Length limit: [author-supplied | VERIFY]

## Trailer Script (30–60 s)
| Time | Visual | Audio / on-screen text | Asset rights |
|------|--------|------------------------|--------------|

## Social Lines
1. …

## Claims Ledger
| Claim | Where used | Source supplied? | Action (keep / VERIFY / cut) |

## Open Questions for the Author / Publisher
- …
```

## Verification

- [ ] Lead buyer is named and the copy's register matches it for this age band.
- [ ] No event after the midpoint and no ending appears in any deliverable.
- [ ] No sentence states the moral or promises a lesson.
- [ ] Every praise line, honour, sales or "bestselling" claim is author-sourced or removed.
- [ ] No comp title appears unless the author supplied and verified it.
- [ ] No "authentic / accurate / own-voices" language unless the author stated it.
- [ ] Every quoted text line is verbatim from the supplied manuscript.
- [ ] Platform limits, release dates and prices are supplied or bracketed `[VERIFY]`.
- [ ] The retailer description is adapted, not a copy of the flap.
- [ ] No copy aimed at children uses pester-power, urgency or scarcity, and no contest or sign-up collects child data.

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **Invented praise.** Models fill the "praise" slot with plausible review quotes or starred-review claims. Output `[PRAISE — author to supply with source]` instead; an empty slot is correct.
2. **"Bestselling" and "beloved" by default.** These are factual claims. Remove unless sourced, and never infer them from the author's enthusiasm.
3. **Spoiling the ending.** The model has the ending in its inputs and tends to use it for impact. Check each deliverable against the midpoint cut.
4. **Selling the lesson.** "Teaches children about kindness" sells to nobody under 12 and breaks the domain's no-preaching rule. Replace with the emotional promise.
5. **Writing only to the parent.** For MG and up, a flap that talks about "your child" loses the actual reader. Re-check the lead buyer.
6. **Certifying representation.** Do not describe the book as culturally accurate; describe the story.
7. **Pressure copy aimed at kids.** "Don't miss out!" and "ask your parents" lines read as ordinary marketing energy to a model but are manipulative for a child audience. Rewrite as the book's own question or promise.

## Example

**Input sketch:** Fictional MG fantasy (ages 8–12), *The Lantern Thief*, ~45,000 words. Twelve-year-old Pim steals the lantern that keeps the fog out of her island village to trade for her brother's cure; by the end she lights a new one. Deliverables: flap, retailer description, trailer. Praise: none. Author is not own-voices for the island culture, which is invented.

**Abbreviated output:**

- **Buyer Strategy:** Lead buyer: child — MG readers choose their own fantasy; the adult sees "brave sibling story" in the last line.
- **Hook Line:** Pim has one night to steal the only light that keeps the fog away — and one reason she can't say out loud.
- **Jacket / Flap Copy (opening):** "Everyone on Gull Island knows three rules: stay inside after dark, never whistle at the water, and never, ever touch the lantern. Pim is about to break all three…" (closes on: "What will the fog take, once the light is gone?")
- **Retailer Description:** first two lines = the hook line + "A fast, foggy island adventure for readers 8–12." Perfect for: readers who love heists, sibling loyalty, and spooky-but-not-scary settings. Length limit: VERIFY.
- **Trailer (excerpt):** 0–5 s — black screen, sound of waves, one line: "Never touch the lantern." Asset rights: cover art — VERIFY with publisher.
- **Claims Ledger:** "Praise" slot — none supplied → left empty. "Perfect for fans of…" — no verified comps → cut. "Authentic island folklore" — the island is invented and the author is not own-voices for any source tradition → cut; describe as "an original island world".
