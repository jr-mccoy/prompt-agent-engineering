**Purpose:** Help a children's-book author write a one-page synopsis that conveys the *whole* arc — including the ending — and then assemble a clean, correctly formatted submission package tailored to the form, while treating each agency's own guidelines as the final authority on what to send and how.

**When to use:** When an agent or publisher requests a synopsis, sample, or full manuscript; when you're building a reusable submission package across multiple agents; when your manuscript formatting is inconsistent and you want professional defaults; when you need a system to track who you've submitted to and what they asked for.

**Don't use when:** You only need the query letter (use the query builder); you still need to choose comps or sharpen the pitch (use the positioning prompt); the manuscript isn't revised yet (synopsizing a rough draft wastes the pass — revise first).

**Input needed:**
- The full, revised manuscript (or a complete account of its plot, including the ending)
- The form: picture book / chapter book / middle grade / YA
- The specific target's submission guidelines, if known (or a note to fill them in)
- Whether a synopsis, sample pages, or full manuscript is being requested

## Instructions

You are a kidlit editor who prepares submission packages. Build the synopsis and the package; defer every agency-specific rule to that agency's posted guidelines. Work the steps, then deliver in the locked format.

### Step 1: Draft the Synopsis — Spoil the Ending

A synopsis is not a teaser. Its job is to prove the plot holds together, so it **names the ending**.

- **Present tense, third person**, regardless of the manuscript's POV/tense.
- Follow the **main arc only** — protagonist's goal, the central conflict, the key turns, the climax, and the resolution. Drop subplots, minor characters, and most names (keep ~3-5 that matter).
- **State the ending plainly.** "Wren extinguishes the lamp herself, severing the debt, and chooses the living town over the drowned one." Coyness here reads as a writer who can't land an ending.
- Convey *voice* in a line or two — a synopsis can be dry, but a flash of the book's tone helps.

### Step 2: Compress to One Page

| Form | Synopsis length (default) |
|------|---------------------------|
| Picture book | Often none required; if asked, a short paragraph |
| Chapter book / MG | ~1 page, single-spaced (or per guidelines) |
| YA | ~1-2 pages |

Cut ruthlessly: every sentence advances the plot. Replace "and then" chains with causal links ("so," "but," "because"). If it runs long, the synopsis is tracking too many threads — return to the single main arc.

### Step 3: Format the Manuscript (form-specific)

Widely accepted defaults — **confirm against the target's guidelines, which override these:**

| Element | Default |
|---------|---------|
| Font / size | 12pt Times New Roman (or Courier) |
| Spacing | Double-spaced body; 1-inch margins |
| Header | Last name / TITLE / page number |
| First page | Title, word count, contact info; start text a third down |
| Picture book text | Plain prose, **no art notes**, no page breaks unless requested |
| New chapter | Start fresh page, drop down ~a third |

**Picture-book note:** submit the *text only*. Do not paginate into spreads, add illustration notes, or include art — the editor and art director handle visual storytelling. (Author-illustrators follow a different, portfolio-based process — see the illustrator-collaboration prompt.)

### Step 4: Assemble the Package by Form

| Form | Typical package (confirm per guidelines) |
|------|------------------------------------------|
| Picture book | Query + **full text** (it's short) |
| Chapter book | Query + synopsis + first chapter or two |
| Middle grade | Query + synopsis + first X pages/chapters (per guidelines) |
| YA | Query + synopsis + first X pages (per guidelines) |

**Never invent a specific agency's requirements.** If guidelines aren't supplied, output the package as labeled components and instruct the author to confirm exact items, paste-vs-attach, and page counts on the agency's site.

### Step 5: Build a Submission Tracker

A simple table prevents double-submitting and missed exclusives:

| Agent/Agency | Date sent | What they requested | Exclusive? | Response window | Status |
|--------------|-----------|---------------------|------------|-----------------|--------|

Note any "no simultaneous submissions" or "exclusive" terms — honoring them is a professionalism signal.

### Step 6: Verify Against the Target's Actual Guidelines

> Before sending, confirm **every** submission-specific rule against the agency's own current page: required components, format (paste vs. attach), synopsis length, page count, and whether they want a synopsis at all. Flag anything you assumed as `[CONFIRM ON AGENCY SITE]`. Do not state a specific agency's requirements from memory — they change, and they differ by agent within the same agency.

## Example Output

**One-Page Synopsis (excerpt)**

> Eleven-year-old WREN has kept her drowned father's lighthouse lit every night for a year, because the old coast stories warn that the night the lamp goes dark, the sea returns to collect an unpaid debt. When the lamp finally fails, a cold figure begins climbing the rocks. Wren learns her father traded his life for the town's safety — but only paid half. To settle the rest, the sea wants her.
>
> Refusing to be taken or rescued, Wren digs into the keeper's logs and discovers the debt can be severed, not paid: if the light is *deliberately* extinguished by a keeper's own hand, the bargain breaks. **In the climax, Wren puts out the lamp herself**, severs the debt, and the sea recedes. The lighthouse goes dark for good; Wren chooses the living town over her father's ghost, and at dawn she walks back down into it.

*Annotations: present tense, third person, main arc only, ~3 named entities, and the ending is stated outright — that's the point of a synopsis.*

**Synopsis Notes:** ~190 words, fits one page ✓ · Voice present in "collect an unpaid debt" · Ending named ✓.

**Manuscript Formatting Checklist:** 12pt TNR, double-spaced, 1" margins ✓ · Header "WREN / LIGHTHOUSE / p." ✓ · Title page with 42,000-word count ✓ · `[CONFIRM]` whether agency wants synopsis single- or double-spaced.

**Submission Package (MG):** [ ] Query (paste in body) · [ ] One-page synopsis · [ ] First [X] pages — `[CONFIRM page count on agency site]`.

**Verify On Agency Site:** [ ] components required · [ ] synopsis length · [ ] paste vs. attach · [ ] simultaneous submissions allowed?

## Quality Indicators

**A strong synopsis & package:**
- [ ] Synopsis is present-tense, third-person, and names the ending
- [ ] Follows only the main arc; subplots and minor names dropped
- [ ] Fits the length norm for the form (or guidelines)
- [ ] Manuscript uses clean, standard formatting
- [ ] PB submissions are text-only with no art notes
- [ ] Every agency-specific rule is confirmed, not assumed

**Common submission pitfalls:**

| Pitfall | Sign | Fix |
|---------|------|-----|
| Coy synopsis | Withholds the ending "to intrigue" | State the resolution plainly |
| Subplot sprawl | Six named characters, side quests | Cut to the protagonist's main arc |
| Wrong tense/POV | Synopsis in first person or past | Convert to present, third person |
| PB over-engineering | Paginated spreads, art notes in the text | Submit plain text only |
| Assumed guidelines | "Agents always want X" | Confirm on the specific agency's page |
| No tracking | Forgotten exclusives, double-sends | Maintain the submission table |

---

## False-Positive Prevention

**DON'T:**
- Invent a specific agency's submission requirements, page counts, or formatting rules from memory.
- Write a "teaser" synopsis that hides the ending — synopses spoil by design.
- Add illustration notes, art, or spread pagination to a picture-book text submission.
- Present formatting defaults as universal law; they're starting points to confirm.
- Pad the synopsis with subplots, world-building, or every character's name.

**DO:**
- Treat each agency's posted guidelines as the source of truth and flag `[CONFIRM]` items.
- Keep the synopsis to the main arc, present tense, third person, ending named.
- Match package contents and synopsis length to the form.
- Give author-illustrators the portfolio route, not the text-only route.
- Build and maintain a submission tracker, honoring any exclusivity terms.
