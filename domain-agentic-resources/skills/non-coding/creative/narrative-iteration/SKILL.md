# Narrative Iteration

Guide stories, scripts, and narrative content through structured revision cycles that systematically improve structure, character, dialogue, pacing, and voice.

## When to Use This Skill

- Revising a draft story, screenplay, essay, or narrative nonfiction piece
- Diagnosing why a narrative "isn't working" despite competent prose
- Preparing a manuscript for beta readers or editorial submission
- Iterating on marketing narratives, case studies, or brand stories
- Coaching writers through a revision process

## Core Concepts

### Story Structure Frameworks

**Three-Act Structure:**
- **Act 1 (Setup, ~25%):** Establish world, character, and inciting incident
- **Act 2 (Confrontation, ~50%):** Rising complications, midpoint shift, all-is-lost moment
- **Act 3 (Resolution, ~25%):** Climax, resolution, new equilibrium

**Save the Cat Beat Sheet (Blake Snyder):**
15 beats from Opening Image to Final Image. Useful for screenplays and commercial fiction.

**Kishōtenketsu (East Asian 4-act):**
Ki (Introduction) → Shō (Development) → Ten (Twist/contrast) → Ketsu (Reconciliation). No conflict requirement — tension comes through contrast, not opposition.

### The Revision Layer Model

Revision is multiple targeted passes, each focusing on a different layer:

| Pass | Focus | Key Questions |
|------|-------|---------------|
| 1. Structure | Arc, pacing, scene order | Does each scene earn its place? |
| 2. Character | Motivation, consistency, growth | Would this character actually do this? |
| 3. Tension | Stakes, conflict, suspense | Where does the reader want to stop reading? |
| 4. Dialogue | Voice, subtext, exposition | Does each character sound distinct? |
| 5. Prose | Sentence rhythm, word choice | Does the writing serve the story? |

## Workflow

### Phase 1: Diagnostic Read

Read the full draft without editing. Annotate only:
- ✓ Where you feel engaged
- ✗ Where attention drifts
- ? Where you're confused
- ! Where you feel strong emotion

**Output:** A heat map of narrative energy showing where the story works and fails.

### Phase 2: Structure Pass

1. List every scene with a one-line summary
2. For each scene identify: **Goal** (what the POV character wants), **Conflict** (what opposes them), **Outcome** (how it ends — usually a setback)
3. Check the arc: Does tension escalate? Is the midpoint a genuine shift?
4. Cut or combine scenes that don't advance plot OR reveal character
5. Apply the **"but/therefore" test** (Parker & Stone): If scenes connect with "and then" it's episodic (weak); "but... therefore..." has causal momentum (strong)

### Phase 3: Character Pass

For each major character:
1. State their **want** (conscious goal) and **need** (unconscious growth)
2. Track their emotional arc scene by scene
3. Verify consistency: Would they really do this given who they are?
4. Check for "convenience characterization" — acting out of character to serve plot
5. Ensure distinct voices (cover the name; can you tell who's speaking?)

### Phase 4: Tension and Stakes Pass

For each scene:
1. What's at risk if the character fails? (stakes)
2. What's the source of uncertainty? (tension)
3. Is the reader asking a question that pulls them forward? (suspense)
4. Are there scenes where nothing is at risk? → Flag for cutting or raising stakes

### Phase 5: Dialogue Pass

1. Read all dialogue aloud (or use text-to-speech)
2. Check for "on-the-nose" dialogue (characters saying exactly what they feel)
3. Look for subtext — the best dialogue says one thing and means another
4. Eliminate "As you know, Bob..." exposition dumps
5. Trim dialogue tags — let words and context identify the speaker

### Phase 6: Prose Pass (Line Edit)

1. Vary sentence length — short for impact, long for flow
2. Cut filler: just, very, really, quite, somewhat
3. Strengthen verbs — replace "was running" with "sprinted"
4. Check for unintentional repetition
5. Polish opening and closing paragraphs — these are your strongest writing

## Templates

### Scene Diagnostic Card

```markdown
## Scene: [Name/Number]
**POV Character:** [Name]
**Goal:** [What they want]
**Conflict:** [What opposes them]
**Outcome:** [Result — usually a setback]
**Emotional shift:** [Start → End state]
**Tension level:** Low / Medium / High
**Essential?** Yes / No — if no, justify keeping
```

### Character Arc Tracker

```markdown
| Scene | Want | Need | Emotional State | Growth Moment? |
|-------|------|------|-----------------|----------------|
| Ch 1  | Escape town | Accept roots | Restless | No |
| Ch 5  | Win contest | Prove worth | Determined | Midpoint |
| Ch 10 | Return home | Accept help | Humble | Climax |
```

## Best Practices

- **One pass, one focus**: Fixing structure AND prose simultaneously produces mediocre results on both.
- **Kill your darlings with a safety net**: Move cut scenes to a "deleted scenes" file.
- **Trust the heat map**: If your attention drifts, the reader's will too.
- **Dialogue is not conversation**: Real speech has filler; story dialogue is compressed and purposeful.
- **The opening promise must be kept**: Whatever the first chapter establishes, the ending must deliver.
- **Revise cold**: Wait 48+ hours between writing and first revision pass.
- **Read aloud**: Awkward rhythm and clunky dialogue become obvious when spoken.

## Common Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Why It Happens | How to Avoid |
|---------|----------------|--------------|
| Polishing prose in a broken structure | Line editing is satisfying; structural work is hard | Always do structure pass first |
| Saggy middle | Act 2 lacks a clear midpoint shift | Add a midpoint reversal that changes the game |
| Characters as plot puppets | Plot drives behavior instead of character | Start from character motivation, derive plot |
| Exposition dumps | Writer knows worldbuilding and wants to share | Reveal through action and conflict |
| Same voice for all characters | Writer's voice dominates | Give each character speech rules |
| Revision without diagnosis | Editing blindly | Always do the diagnostic read first |

## Quality Checklist

- [ ] Diagnostic heat map completed before any editing
- [ ] Every scene has a clear goal, conflict, and outcome
- [ ] Tension generally escalates from beginning to climax
- [ ] Each major character has a distinct want, need, and arc
- [ ] Characters have distinguishable voices in dialogue
- [ ] No "As you know, Bob" exposition in dialogue
- [ ] Opening establishes tone, stakes, and a question
- [ ] Closing delivers on the opening's promise
- [ ] Prose has varied sentence rhythm
- [ ] Draft read aloud at least once

## Examples

### Example: Revision Plan for a 60,000-word Novel

| Pass | Duration | Focus | Deliverable |
|------|----------|-------|-------------|
| Diagnostic | 2 days | Read without editing | Heat map + scene list |
| Structure | 3 days | Reorder/cut/add scenes | Revised outline |
| Character | 2 days | Arc tracking, motivation | Character sheets |
| Tension | 2 days | Stakes audit, midpoint | Revised Act 2 |
| Dialogue | 2 days | Voice, subtext | Dialogue notes |
| Prose | 3 days | Line-by-line editing | Clean manuscript |
| **Total** | **14 days** | | **Submission-ready** |
