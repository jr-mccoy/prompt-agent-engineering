# Interview Study Design

Plan rigorous qualitative interview studies from research question formulation through protocol design, sampling strategy, and analysis-ready data capture.

## When to Use This Skill

- Designing a qualitative research study involving interviews
- Creating interview guides for user research, market research, or academic inquiry
- Planning sampling strategy for purposive or theoretical sampling
- Preparing for IRB/ethics review of an interview study
- Setting up coding and thematic analysis workflows

## Core Concepts

### Research Question Types for Qualitative Inquiry

Qualitative research questions explore "how" and "why," not "how many":

| Good Qualitative RQ | Poor Qualitative RQ |
|---------------------|---------------------|
| "How do new managers experience their first year?" | "What percentage of managers succeed?" |
| "Why do patients leave the program?" | "How many patients complete treatment?" |
| "What role does trust play in remote teams?" | "Is remote work better than in-office?" |

### Interview Types

| Type | Structure | Best For |
|------|-----------|----------|
| **Structured** | Fixed questions, fixed order | Comparability across many participants |
| **Semi-structured** | Core questions + flexible follow-up | Most qualitative research (balance of rigor and depth) |
| **Unstructured** | Open-ended conversation, minimal guide | Exploratory research, life histories |

Semi-structured is the default for most studies.

### Sampling Strategies

| Strategy | How It Works | When to Use |
|----------|-------------|-------------|
| **Purposive** | Select participants who fit specific criteria | When you know what characteristics matter |
| **Maximum variation** | Select for diversity across key dimensions | When you want breadth of perspectives |
| **Snowball** | Participants refer others | Hard-to-reach populations |
| **Theoretical** | Select based on emerging theory (grounded theory) | When building theory iteratively |
| **Criterion** | All participants meet predefined criteria | When studying a specific experience |

### Saturation

Data collection continues until new interviews yield no new themes or insights. Typical saturation points:

- **Homogeneous sample:** 6-12 interviews
- **Moderate diversity:** 12-20 interviews
- **High diversity / complex topic:** 20-30+ interviews

## Workflow

### Phase 1: Define Research Questions

1. Start broad, then narrow to 1-3 focused qualitative RQs
2. Ensure RQs are open-ended (how, why, what role, what experience)
3. Verify the RQ is answerable through interviews (not surveys or observation)
4. Frame RQs around participant experience, not researcher hypotheses

### Phase 2: Design Sampling Strategy

1. Define inclusion/exclusion criteria
2. Choose sampling approach (purposive, maximum variation, snowball, etc.)
3. Estimate initial sample size (plan for saturation, not statistical power)
4. Create a participant recruitment plan
5. Plan for informed consent process

### Phase 3: Develop Interview Guide

Build a question hierarchy:

1. **Grand tour questions** (broad, opening): "Tell me about your experience with..."
2. **Mini-tour questions** (focused follow-up): "You mentioned X — can you walk me through that?"
3. **Probing questions** (depth): "What did that feel like?" / "Can you give me an example?"
4. **Structural questions** (framework): "How would you categorize the different types of...?"
5. **Contrast questions** (comparison): "How was that different from...?"

**Guide structure:**
1. Warm-up (rapport, easy questions)
2. Core questions (3-5 main topics)
3. Follow-up probes under each core question
4. Closing (anything else, thank you)

### Phase 4: Ethics and IRB Preparation

1. Draft informed consent document
2. Plan for data confidentiality (pseudonyms, secure storage)
3. Identify potential risks to participants
4. Plan for emotional distress protocols (if sensitive topics)
5. Submit IRB application (if required by institution)
6. Prepare participant information sheet

### Phase 5: Pilot and Refine

1. Conduct 1-2 pilot interviews
2. Assess question clarity, flow, and timing
3. Check if questions elicit rich, relevant data
4. Revise guide based on pilot findings
5. Note interviewer reflexivity (your own biases and reactions)

### Phase 6: Prepare for Analysis

Choose a coding methodology:

**Thematic Analysis (Braun & Clarke, 6 phases):**
1. Familiarize with data (read transcripts multiple times)
2. Generate initial codes (label meaningful segments)
3. Search for themes (group codes into candidate themes)
4. Review themes (check against data, refine)
5. Define and name themes (write theme descriptions)
6. Write up (select extracts, relate to RQ)

**Grounded Theory (Strauss & Corbin):**
- Open coding → Axial coding → Selective coding
- Theory emerges from data rather than being imposed

## Templates

### Interview Guide Template

```markdown
# Interview Guide: [Study Title]

**Duration:** ~[45-60] minutes
**Recording:** Audio [with consent]

## Introduction (5 min)
- Thank participant, explain purpose, confirm consent
- "There are no right or wrong answers — I'm interested in your experience."

## Warm-up (5 min)
1. "Can you tell me a little about your background with [topic]?"

## Core Questions

### Topic 1: [Theme]
2. "Tell me about your experience with [X]." (Grand tour)
   - Probe: "Can you give me a specific example?"
   - Probe: "How did that make you feel?"
   - Probe: "What happened next?"

### Topic 2: [Theme]
3. "[Core question]" (Grand tour)
   - Probe: "What was the biggest challenge?"
   - Probe: "How did you handle that?"

### Topic 3: [Theme]
4. "[Core question]" (Grand tour)
   - Probe: "How was that different from [contrast]?"

## Closing (5 min)
5. "Is there anything else you'd like to share that I haven't asked about?"
6. "What advice would you give someone in a similar situation?"
- Thank participant, explain next steps
```

### Trustworthiness Criteria (Lincoln & Guba)

| Criterion | Quantitative Equivalent | Strategies |
|-----------|------------------------|------------|
| **Credibility** | Internal validity | Member checking, triangulation, prolonged engagement |
| **Transferability** | External validity | Thick description, purposive sampling |
| **Dependability** | Reliability | Audit trail, peer debriefing |
| **Confirmability** | Objectivity | Reflexive journaling, triangulation |

## Best Practices

- **Ask "tell me about" not "do you think"**: Open prompts yield richer data than opinion questions.
- **Follow the participant's lead**: The best insights come from unexpected directions. Don't rigidly follow the guide.
- **Use silence**: After a participant answers, wait 3-5 seconds. They often continue with deeper insights.
- **Probe with "can you give me an example?"**: Moves from abstract to concrete.
- **Keep a reflexive journal**: Document your assumptions, reactions, and evolving understanding.
- **Record and transcribe**: Memory is unreliable. Audio recording (with consent) is essential.
- **Plan for member checking**: Share findings with participants for validation.

## Common Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Why It Happens | How to Avoid |
|---------|----------------|--------------|
| Leading questions | Researcher's hypothesis leaks into questions | Phrase as open, not suggestive |
| Insufficient probing | Discomfort with silence or follow-up | Practice probing; use "tell me more" |
| Premature saturation | Researcher fatigue, not true saturation | Track new codes per interview; stop when flat |
| Ignoring disconfirming data | Confirmation bias | Actively seek cases that challenge emerging themes |
| Poor reflexivity | Researcher unaware of own biases | Keep a reflexive journal throughout |
| Ethics shortcuts | Time pressure | Complete IRB and consent before any interviews |

## Quality Checklist

- [ ] Research questions are open-ended and qualitative in nature
- [ ] Sampling strategy is explicitly justified
- [ ] Interview guide has grand tour, probes, and closing
- [ ] Informed consent document prepared
- [ ] Pilot interviews conducted and guide refined
- [ ] Analysis methodology chosen and documented
- [ ] Data storage and confidentiality plan in place
- [ ] Trustworthiness strategies identified (member checking, audit trail, etc.)
- [ ] Reflexive journal started before data collection
- [ ] IRB approval obtained (if required)
