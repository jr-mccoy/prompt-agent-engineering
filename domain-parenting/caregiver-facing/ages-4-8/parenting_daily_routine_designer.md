# Daily Routine Designer — Morning, After-School, Bedtime

## Objective

Produce three house-specific daily routines for a 4–8 year old — a morning sequence (wake to leave), an after-school / dinner sequence (pickup to evening wind-down start), and a bedtime sequence (wind-down to asleep) — that (a) externalize executive function through visual schedules, (b) time-budget realistically, (c) eliminate the top 2–3 friction points in the parent's current routine, and (d) include explicit adaptations for ADHD, autism (ASD Level 1), strong-willed, and sensory-sensitive kids.

## When to Use

Use this prompt when a caregiver describes:

- Daily battles at specific times (mornings are chaos, after-school is a meltdown, bedtime takes 2 hours).
- Kids who "won't listen" during routine transitions.
- A recently changed schedule (new school, new sibling, DST, daycare to kindergarten jump).
- A parent returning to work or a caregiver-swap happening.
- ADHD or autism adaptations needed after a diagnosis or as a trial.
- A home where routines have never been formalized and it's now time.

**Do not use this prompt alone if:**

- The child is in crisis (recent trauma, grief, medical event) — stabilize first, design routines after.
- Parent is severely sleep-deprived — first sleep, then plan; a routine designed by an exhausted parent won't stick.
- The friction is actually a sensory or sleep problem dressed as a routine problem — see `parenting_sensory_at_home_toolkit.md`, and for sleep issues consult pediatrician.
- The issue is a co-parent alignment problem — address that first; two different routines in two houses are fine but require coordination.

## Inputs / Context to Gather

Before designing the routines, ask the caregiver for:

1. **Children's ages and profiles**
   - Age and temperament.
   - Any neurodivergence (ADHD, autism Level 1, anxiety, sensory sensitivity).
   - Reader / pre-reader (determines whether written, picture, or mixed schedule).

2. **Non-negotiable anchors**
   - Fixed times: bus leaves at 7:55, school day ends at 3:10, parent work starts at 9:00, sibling's bedtime at 8:30.
   - Medical / feeding requirements.

3. **Current routine walkthrough**
   - Minute-by-minute what happens now in each window.
   - Where the friction is (who gets stuck where).
   - What has been tried.

4. **Home constraints**
   - Solo vs. two-parent mornings.
   - Shared bathroom? bedrooms?
   - Access to outdoor space for movement breaks.
   - Screens in home / screens in bedroom.

5. **Realistic time budget**
   - What time the parent can actually be up.
   - How long drop-off / pickup takes.
   - Dinner prep reality (30 min? 10 min?).

6. **Child-voice opportunities**
   - For 6+, what does the child say is hard about mornings / evenings? (Ask them.)

## Constraints

### Must

- **Externalize into a visible artifact.** Every routine must produce a physical (picture/word) schedule the child can see, not just a parent's memory.
- **Limit steps.** No more than 6–8 visible steps per routine for this age. Split multi-part steps (breakfast = sit + eat + wipe = one "breakfast" icon, not three).
- **Budget with buffer.** Total routine time = sum of step times + 20–30% buffer. Parents chronically underestimate morning time.
- **Eliminate the top 2–3 current friction points.** Don't design a new ideal routine; design a repaired version of the current one.
- **Distinguish child-steps from parent-steps.** What does the child do? What does the parent do? Visual schedule is about the child's sequence, not the parent's.
- **Anchor each routine with a start cue and an end cue.** A predictable trigger (the breakfast timer rings = breakfast starts; "tuck-in song" = lights off).
- **Anchor each routine with a connection moment.** Brief — 30 sec to 2 min — where the parent and child connect on purpose (not in passing).
- **Age-tier.** 4–5 need shorter routines, more visual, more parent hands-on. 6–7 can do more independently. 8 can do most steps independently with check-in.
- **Include adaptation block** for ADHD / autism / strong-willed / sensory.
- **Include a 1–2 week trial plan** with adjustment points.
- **Include a universal safety block.**

### Must Not

- Must not prescribe one-size "gentle parenting" or any commercial brand.
- Must not ignore parent capacity (an aspirational 5:30 AM wake-up doesn't work for a parent who sleeps at 1 AM).
- Must not treat independence as the metric for success — connection and regulated transition is.
- Must not put a screen in the routine as a default babysitter; screens can be used intentionally but flag tradeoffs.
- Must not require elaborate props, apps, or subscriptions. Index cards and tape work.
- Must not make a routine so rigid it can't flex for a bad-morning day.

## Instructions

Follow this seven-step sequence.

### Step 1 — Audit the Current Routine

For each of the three windows (morning, after-school, bedtime), write down the current minute-by-minute sequence. For each step, mark: flows-fine (F), friction sometimes (S), friction always (A).

Example:
- 6:45 — Parent wakes (F)
- 7:00 — Wake child (S)
- 7:05 — Dress in room (A — child won't get dressed)
- 7:15 — Breakfast (F)
- 7:30 — Shoes and coat (A — every day a fight)
- 7:45 — Out the door (often late)

### Step 2 — Identify Top 2–3 Friction Points

Pick 2–3 "A" items that, if solved, would make the biggest impact. Don't solve all friction at once; most families can absorb 2–3 routine changes at a time.

### Step 3 — Design the Morning Sequence

Produce:

- **Start cue** (parent wake-up behavior, light, music, alarm).
- **6–8 step list**, each with estimated time (including buffer).
- **Visual schedule spec**: picture icons vs. words vs. photographs, where it lives (on fridge, on a door, on a clipboard the child carries, on a whiteboard).
- **Connection moment** built in (2-min snuggle before getting out of bed; a song during tooth-brushing together).
- **End cue** ("shoes on, backpack on, front door" chorus).
- **Parent job vs. child job** column.
- **Typical stuck points and pre-decided responses.**

**Sample morning (5yo) — visual icons:** Wake → Bathroom → Get dressed → Breakfast → Teeth → Shoes/coat → Out the door.

### Step 4 — Design the After-School Sequence

Produce:

- **Pickup landing** — most kids need 20–40 minutes of "no demands" after school to regulate. Name this explicitly. Snack + movement + downtime beat "how was your day?"
- **Post-regulation steps** — backpack unload routine, homework window if applicable, activity block.
- **Dinner approach** — predictable elements, kid-involvement if any.
- **Transition to evening wind-down cue.**

Many families mistake after-school meltdown for "behavior"; it is usually masking release from a long day of regulation. Build for that.

### Step 5 — Design the Bedtime Sequence

Produce:

- **Wind-down cue** — dimmer lights, screen off (ideally 1 hour before sleep), quieter voices.
- **Sequenced steps** — bath if relevant, PJs, teeth, toilet, story, hug/song, lights off.
- **Connection moment** — usually the biggest of the day: story, tuck-in, one-question check-in, song.
- **"One more" policy** — Pre-decide: how many extra-asks count as built-in (one drink, one hug, one question) vs. bedtime-stretching.
- **Sleep anchors** — same-place-same-time, darkness, cool room, white noise if helpful, consistent comfort object.
- **Parent exit plan** — how the parent actually leaves the room.

Age reminders: 4–5 yr olds need 10–13 hours of sleep/24; 6–12 yr olds need 9–12. A child routinely short on sleep will wreck every other routine.

### Step 6 — Adaptations Block

Append specific adjustments:

**ADHD**
- Working memory support: pictures, not words; visible checklist (not in a drawer).
- External time: visual timer counting down.
- Body-first for activation: 2-min movement break before any demand sequence.
- Reduce steps visible at once (one step highlighted; the rest visible but secondary).
- Link morning steps to already-automatic ones ("after teeth, we do shoes").
- See `parenting_adhd_executive_function_scaffold.md`.

**Autism / ASD Level 1**
- Identical sequence daily (predictability reduces load).
- Advance notice of any changes ("tomorrow is Saturday, no school").
- Visual schedule with photos of this child in each step (personalization).
- After-school recovery time is longer (40–60 min minimum).
- Sensory-light environment in the routine (no fluorescent lights while dressing).
- See `parenting_hfa_social_situation_decoder.md`, `parenting_sensory_at_home_toolkit.md`.

**Strong-willed**
- Control inside the sequence: child picks the order where possible ("teeth first or pajamas first?").
- Fewer parent-driven steps, more child-led.
- Avoid running commentary of instructions; point to the schedule and let it be the boss.
- Explicit "what's a choice today and what's not" column.
- See `parenting_strong_willed_power_struggle_defuser.md`.

**Sensory-sensitive**
- Morning: clothing laid out night before, tags cut, socks first.
- Low light, low sound until the child has regulated.
- Warning before any change in stimulation (lights on, shower on, vacuum on).
- See `parenting_sensory_at_home_toolkit.md`.

### Step 7 — Two-Week Trial Plan

Commit to:
- **Week 1:** Introduce the new routine with warmth and low-stakes. Expect friction. Use the visual schedule every day. Don't measure compliance; measure friction-count.
- **Check-in at Day 7:** What's working? What's not? One adjustment.
- **Week 2:** Hold adjusted version.
- **Check-in at Day 14:** Keep, tweak, or replace.

Routine change takes 2–4 weeks to settle in neurotypical kids, longer for ADHD/autism. Short-term pushback is not failure; it's adjustment.

## Output Format

Return a single document with these labeled sections, in order:

1. **Current Routine Audit** (F/S/A mapping per window)
2. **Top 2–3 Friction Points Targeted**
3. **Morning Sequence** (start cue / 6–8 steps / visual spec / connection / end cue / parent-vs-child column / stuck-point responses)
4. **After-School Sequence** (pickup landing / post-regulation / dinner / transition)
5. **Bedtime Sequence** (wind-down / sequenced steps / connection / one-more policy / sleep anchors / parent exit)
6. **Visual Schedule Spec** (format, location, materials, how to build)
7. **Adaptations** (ADHD / autism / strong-willed / sensory — only the ones that apply)
8. **Two-Week Trial Plan**
9. **Safety Block** (verbatim)
10. **Cross-References**

### Safety Block (include verbatim)

> **When to stop and get help now:**
> - If your child is consistently sleeping fewer than recommended hours (4–5 yr: 10–13; 6–8 yr: 9–12) for more than 2 weeks despite a solid routine → consult pediatrician (rule out medical sleep issues, anxiety, OSA).
> - If bedtime separation is triggering extreme panic for more than 2–3 weeks → talk to pediatrician about separation anxiety.
> - If your child is exhausted post-school every day for weeks (falling asleep at 4 pm, crying unprovoked) and a routine isn't fixing it → consider whether school masking / sensory load is the problem; route to `parenting_school_accommodation_conversation_prep.md` and pediatrician.
> - If you as the parent cannot get the child up despite routines, or you are yourself sleeping less than 5–6 hr most nights → Parent Warmline 1-855-4APARENT (1-855-427-2736) and pediatrician.
> - In medical emergency, 911.

## False-Positive Prevention

| Misfire | What it looks like | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Too many steps | 15-step morning | Consolidate; 6–8 visible max. |
| Aspirational time budget | 45-min morning when you have 30 | Subtract 20% from the fastest-possible time; plan to the realistic. |
| Designing for parent goals, not child sequence | Parent's to-do list | Child's sequence is the visual; parent's is separate. |
| No buffer | Every delay cascades | Build 20–30% buffer into the total. |
| Over-reliance on screens | "Morning show while eating" | One controlled use is fine; default screen is friction-source later. |
| No connection moment | Pure task-list routine | Kids move through lists better after 2-min connect. |
| Identical routine despite temperament mismatch | "Gentle wake-up" for a strong-willed kid | Match entry cue to temperament. |
| Ignoring sleep foundation | Designing bedtime that ends at 10pm for a 6yo | Fix the sleep total first. |
| Rigid routine with no flex-day plan | Bad morning = full meltdown | Add "if stuck, we skip X and keep Y" clause. |
| Ignoring after-school recovery need | No downtime between school and homework | 20–40 min pickup-landing is not optional. |
| Demanding independence too early | 4yo doing full routine alone | Parent presence is part of the routine for 4–5 yr olds. |
| Ignoring the need for advance notice in autistic kids | Changing schedule without warning | Warn the night before; show new schedule. |
| Solving a sensory problem with a routine | "If we just added a step…" | Route to sensory toolkit first. |
| Making visual schedule a punishment device | "You didn't follow the schedule" | Schedule is a tool, not a grading rubric. |
| Treating routine as a compliance test | Enforcing every minute | Routine is friction-reduction, not control. |
