**Purpose:** Help a parent build an addendum to a parenting plan for a child with disability, medical, ADHD, autism, or mental-health needs — covering medication continuity across homes, therapy and appointment coordination, IEP/504 alignment, shared documentation, consistency of routines and accommodations, who attends appointments, and emergency protocols. The output organizes the parent's own decisions into a calm, factual addendum a mediator, guardian ad litem (GAL), or court will take seriously, and that actually keeps the child's care consistent between two homes. It does not argue, accuse, or give medical or legal advice.

**When to use:** Attaching a special-needs section to a parenting plan; reducing care gaps when a child's medication, therapy, or accommodations differ between homes; coordinating IEP/504 participation; setting emergency and documentation protocols across households.

**When NOT to use:** You need a clinical decision (medication, diagnosis, treatment) → that is for the child's clinicians, not a document. You need to know whether a term is enforceable or how decision-making authority works legally → that is legal advice; ask an attorney. There is a safety or acute-mental-health concern → handle safety first (see Safety Block).

---

## Safety Block

Stop and use a different pathway if:
- A child is being abused, medically neglected, or is unsafe in either home → in the US, Childhelp National Child Abuse Hotline 1-800-422-4453; emergencies 911. Withholding medication or care can be a safety/CPS matter — document and route to professionals and your attorney.
- There is domestic violence, threats, stalking, or a protective/restraining order → National Domestic Violence Hotline 1-800-799-7233 (US). Care coordination around an unsafe parent goes through counsel/advocate.
- A child is talking about not wanting to be alive, or you are worried about their mental health → 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (US); pediatrician/psychiatrist same-day; for a child already in mental-health treatment, contact the treating clinician.

This prompt is educational support for organizing your own decisions. It is not a substitute for clinical, safety, or legal services.

## Scope Boundary — Read First

This builds a **parent-authored addendum**. It is **not legal advice, medical advice, a legal filing, or a substitute for an attorney, mediator, the child's clinicians, IEP/504 team, or your jurisdiction's custody and education law.** How medical decision-making authority is allocated, how IEP/504 consent and participation work, records-access rules, and terminology **vary by state and country and change over time.** Have an attorney or mediator review this, and have the child's clinicians/IEP team confirm the care details, before you rely on it or submit it. This prompt will **not** make clinical decisions, invent statutes, predict what a court will decide, or assert a term is "enforceable" or "standard." Where a legal or clinical concept appears, it is explained in plain language and flagged *confirm with counsel / the child's clinicians.*

## Core Principles

1. **Consistency across homes is the child's care.** For a child with special needs, two different routines, medication schedules, or accommodation approaches can undo treatment. The addendum's whole job is continuity.
2. **Medication continuity is non-negotiable for continuity of care.** Same medication, dosing, and timing in both homes; a clear plan for who fills, who supplies, and how it travels.
3. **Coordinate, don't compete, on appointments.** Therapy, specialists, and evaluations need a shared calendar, agreed attendance, and shared notes — not parallel, uncoordinated care.
4. **One IEP/504, two homes.** Both parents work from the same education plan and accommodations; align who participates in meetings and how information flows.
5. **Shared documentation prevents gaps.** A shared record of medications, providers, appointments, and accommodations means neither home is operating blind.
6. **Plan for the predictable emergencies.** Define what each parent does in a medical or mental-health crisis and how the other is notified.
7. **Neutral and child-centered; clinical details confirmed by clinicians.** The addendum records the care plan factually and routes the clinical truth to the providers. You decide and confirm; this organizes.

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Record medication continuity (same med/dose/timing, who supplies, how it travels) — as the clinicians have set it, flagged to confirm with clinicians.
- Define appointment coordination: shared calendar, attendance, shared notes.
- Align both homes to one IEP/504 and name participation/information flow.
- Specify shared documentation and emergency protocols.
- Flag legal concepts *confirm with counsel* and clinical concepts *confirm with the child's clinicians.*

**Must Not:**
- Give medical advice, recommend/change medication, dosing, or treatment.
- Characterize, accuse, or disparage the other parent.
- Invent legal/education standards or assert a term is "enforceable" or "standard."
- Predict court outcomes.
- Make the child responsible for managing their own medication handoff or carrying medical messages between parents.

## Instructions

### Stage 1 — Inventory the Child's Care
Restate the child's needs, medications, providers, education plan, and must-be-consistent routines in the parent's words. Flag clinical details to confirm with clinicians and legal/decision-making questions for counsel.

### Stage 2 — Build Medication Continuity
Record the medication regimen exactly as the clinicians set it (record, don't advise), plus: who fills prescriptions, how the supply is split/transferred, how dosing is logged across homes, and the rule that both homes follow the same schedule. Note allergies and any rescue medication.

### Stage 3 — Coordinate Appointments & Therapy
Set a shared calendar method; agree who schedules, who attends (one/both), and how session notes/recommendations are shared so both homes carry through the plan consistently.

### Stage 4 — Align IEP/504 and Accommodations
Name the single education plan, who attends IEP/504 meetings, how documents and teacher communication are shared, and which accommodations both homes maintain. *How consent and participation work — confirm with counsel and the school.*

### Stage 5 — Shared Documentation & Emergency Protocols
Define the shared record (medication list, provider directory, appointment log, accommodation summary) and emergency protocols (who does what in a medical/mental-health crisis, how the other parent is notified, key contacts). Assemble the addendum and tone-check.

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| Recommend a dose or medication change | Record the regimen as clinicians set it; flag confirm-with-clinicians |
| Let each home run a different routine | Specify the routines/accommodations both homes keep identical |
| "Mom skips his meds, so she shouldn't…" | Record the continuity rule neutrally; document facts elsewhere |
| Maintain two separate IEP approaches | Align both homes to one education plan |
| Claim a decision-making term is "enforceable" | Flag decision-making authority "confirm with counsel" |
| Predict "the court will give medical decisions to you" | Stay silent on outcomes; route to counsel |
| Make the child carry meds/notes between parents | Adults manage medication transfer and documentation |
| Treat the addendum as the clinical plan | The clinicians own care; this records and coordinates it |

## Adaptations

**By age band:**
- **0–3:** Early-intervention services, feeding/medical routines, frequent specialist visits; minimize disruption to therapy schedules.
- **4–8:** School entry and first IEP/504; medication timing around the school day; sensory and routine consistency at both homes.
- **9–12:** Growing self-management with support; homework/executive-function scaffolds consistent across homes; therapy continuity.
- **13–18:** Begin involving the teen in their own care planning (with support), transition-planning in the IEP; never offload medication management entirely onto the teen as a co-parenting workaround. *Health-care decision transitions — confirm with counsel/clinicians.*

**By situation/profile:**
- **High-conflict co-parent:** Maximize specificity and shared-record automation; minimize required real-time coordination; pair with `parenting_custody_communication_log_template.md`.
- **Long distance:** Plan telehealth continuity, provider coverage in each location, and medication supply across distance.
- **Acute mental-health needs:** Tighten the crisis protocol and notification; align with the treating clinician; see Safety Block.
- **Safety concerns / medical neglect:** Document factually and route to professionals/counsel; do not self-arrange care around an unsafe parent; see Safety Block.
