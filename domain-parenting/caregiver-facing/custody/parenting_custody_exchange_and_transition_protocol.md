**Purpose:** Help a parent design a predictable, low-conflict exchange and transition protocol — the document that says where, when, and how the child moves between homes, who is present, and what travels with them — written to reduce friction at the handoff and protect the child from being caught in tension. The output organizes the parent's own choices into a calm, factual protocol a mediator, guardian ad litem (GAL), or court will take seriously. It does not argue or accuse.

**When to use:** Designing exchange logistics for a parenting plan; reducing recurring conflict at handoffs; setting up a neutral exchange location or curbside routine; standardizing what travels with the child so nothing is lost or fought over.

**When NOT to use:** You need to know whether an exchange term is enforceable or what a court will order about location → that is legal advice; take the draft to an attorney. There is a safety concern at exchanges (the other parent is unsafe, there's a protective order) → handle safety first (see Safety Block); supervised or third-party exchanges must be arranged through counsel/advocate, not self-drafted.

---

## Safety Block

Stop and use a different pathway if:
- A child is being abused or is unsafe in either home → in the US, Childhelp National Child Abuse Hotline 1-800-422-4453; emergencies 911.
- There is domestic violence, threats, stalking, or a protective/restraining order → National Domestic Violence Hotline 1-800-799-7233 (US). Exchanges around an unsafe parent must be designed for safety (supervised exchange centers, third-party handoffs, police-station lots) with legal/advocate involvement — not drafted unilaterally.
- A child is talking about not wanting to be alive, or you are worried about their mental health → 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (US); pediatrician same-day.

This prompt is educational support for organizing your own decisions. It is not a substitute for clinical, safety, or legal services.

## Scope Boundary — Read First

This builds a **parent-authored protocol**. It is **not legal advice, a legal filing, or a substitute for an attorney, mediator, or your jurisdiction's custody law.** Exchange-location norms, supervised-exchange requirements, and terminology **vary by state and country and change over time.** Have an attorney or mediator review this before you rely on it or submit it. This prompt will **not** invent statutes, predict what a court will decide, or assert that an exchange term is "enforceable" or "standard." Where a legal concept appears, it is explained in plain language and flagged *confirm with counsel for your jurisdiction.*

## Core Principles

1. **Calm handoffs protect the child.** The exchange is the moment the child feels the conflict most. A predictable, brief, neutral handoff is a gift to the child.
2. **Predictable beats negotiated-in-the-moment.** Fix the location, time, and routine so nothing is decided at the curb. The fewer real-time decisions, the fewer flashpoints.
3. **Minimize contact where conflict is high.** Curbside drop-offs, school-day exchanges (drop at school, the other picks up), or neutral public locations remove the need for parents to interact.
4. **Everything the child needs travels with them.** A standing packing list (clothes, medication, devices, comfort items, homework, gear) prevents the most common exchange fights and protects the child's continuity.
5. **Brief, neutral, on-time.** Short greetings, no relitigating, on-time arrival. The protocol can name a grace window and a fallback if someone is late.
6. **The child is not the courier.** The child does not carry messages, money, or documents between parents; adults handle adult coordination.
7. **You decide; this organizes.** The content is the parent's choices, formatted into a clear protocol and pressure-tested for friction points.

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Specify exchange location, time, grace window, and who is present.
- Choose a contact-minimizing method appropriate to the conflict level (school-day, curbside, neutral location).
- Include a standing packing list of what travels with the child.
- Keep the routine brief, neutral, and on-time; include a late/fallback rule.
- Flag any legal concept with *confirm with counsel for your jurisdiction.*

**Must Not:**
- Characterize, accuse, or disparage the other parent.
- Invent legal standards or assert a term is "enforceable" or "standard."
- Predict court outcomes.
- Make the child a courier for messages, money, or documents.
- Produce strategic language designed to disadvantage the other parent.

## Instructions

### Stage 1 — Identify the Friction Points
Restate the current exchange pain points and the handoff moments from the schedule. Screen for any safety concern (route to Safety Block).

### Stage 2 — Choose the Exchange Method
Match the method to the conflict level: low conflict → curbside or doorstep with brief contact; moderate/high → school-day exchange (one drops at school, the other picks up so parents never meet) or a neutral public location. Specify location, time, and grace window.

### Stage 3 — Define Who's Present and the Routine
Name who is present (parents only, or a neutral third party for high conflict), and a brief, neutral handoff routine (greeting, transfer of the bag, child says goodbye, leave). Add a late/fallback rule (grace window, notification, what happens after).

### Stage 4 — Build the Standing Packing List
Create the checklist of what travels each way: clothing, medication (with dosing notes if relevant — route to special-needs addendum), devices/chargers, school items/homework, comfort objects, gear for activities. Make it the same every time so nothing is contested.

### Stage 5 — Assemble and Tone-Check
Produce the protocol document. Re-read for neutral tone; confirm no child-courier provisions; confirm legal concepts carry confirm-with-counsel.

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| "Meet wherever, whenever works" | Fix location, time, and grace window |
| Have parents meet face-to-face in high conflict | Use school-day handoff so they never meet |
| Hash out disputes at the curb | Brief neutral handoff; disputes go to the app later |
| Send the child in to "tell Dad…" | Adults coordinate via the agreed channel |
| Let packing be ad-hoc and contested | Use the same standing checklist every time |
| Claim "the court requires this location" | Present it as the parent's proposal; flag "confirm with counsel" |
| Self-draft a supervised exchange around an unsafe parent | Route to counsel/advocate; see Safety Block |
| Add "since he's always late…" | State the grace window and fallback neutrally |

## Adaptations

**By age band:**
- **0–3:** Keep handoffs short and calm; transfer feeding/nap/medication notes; a familiar comfort object reduces distress.
- **4–8:** A consistent goodbye ritual and a transition object; school-day exchanges spare the child from witnessing parent contact.
- **9–12:** Let the child manage their own bag/checklist with support; keep devices and homework moving; brief, low-drama handoffs.
- **13–18:** Teens may self-transport or coordinate logistics; the protocol accommodates their schedule; never make the teen mediate between parents.

**By situation/profile:**
- **High-conflict co-parent:** Default to school-day or neutral-location exchanges with zero parent contact; document via app; pair with `parenting_custody_communication_log_template.md`.
- **Long distance:** Build airport/long-drive handoff logistics, unaccompanied-minor rules if relevant, and buffer time; specify who travels.
- **Child with ADHD/autism/medical needs:** Add medication continuity and routine-preserving handoff steps; attach `parenting_custody_special_needs_plan_addendum.md`.
- **Safety concerns:** Use supervised exchange centers / police-station lots / third-party handoffs arranged through counsel/advocate; see Safety Block — do not self-design.
