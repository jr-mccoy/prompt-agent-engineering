**Purpose:** Give a parent a simple, transparent template and method for tracking shared child expenses — medical and dental, childcare, extracurriculars, school costs — and the reimbursements between parents, so the record is clean, dated, and receipt-backed. A transparent ledger supports cost-sharing conversations, reduces money disputes, and can be shared with a mediator, guardian ad litem (GAL), or court if needed. It does not calculate child support or argue who owes what under the law.

**When to use:** Setting up a shared-expense record from scratch; tracking who paid for what and what's owed; preparing a tidy expense summary before a mediation or review; keeping receipts and reimbursement requests organized over time.

**When NOT to use:** You need to know your support obligation, how expenses are legally allocated, or whether the other parent "must" pay a share → that is legal advice; take the ledger to an attorney. There is a safety situation → handle safety first (see Safety Block).

---

## Safety Block

Stop and use a different pathway if:
- A child is being abused or is unsafe in either home → in the US, Childhelp National Child Abuse Hotline 1-800-422-4453; emergencies 911.
- There is domestic violence, threats, stalking, or a protective/restraining order → National Domestic Violence Hotline 1-800-799-7233 (US). Keep financial records safely; route money disputes through counsel/advocate, not direct confrontation.
- A child is talking about not wanting to be alive, or you are worried about their mental health → 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (US); pediatrician same-day.

This prompt is educational support for organizing your own records. It is not a substitute for clinical, safety, or legal services.

## Scope Boundary — Read First

This builds a **parent-authored record**. It is **not legal advice, a child-support calculation, a legal filing, or a substitute for an attorney, mediator, or your jurisdiction's custody/support law.** How expenses are shared, what counts as a shareable expense, support formulas, and reimbursement requirements **vary by state and country and change over time.** Have an attorney or mediator review how to use this before you rely on it or submit it. This prompt will **not** calculate support, invent allocation percentages, assert what the other parent "owes," or predict what a court will decide. Where a legal concept appears, it is explained in plain language and flagged *confirm with counsel for your jurisdiction.*

---

## Core Principles

1. **Transparency is the point.** A record both parents could read without surprise is the goal — every line dated, categorized, and receipt-backed.
2. **Receipts, not recollections.** Each expense ties to a receipt, invoice, or statement; reimbursements tie to a dated payment record.
3. **Categories keep it clean.** Group expenses (medical/dental, childcare, school, extracurriculars, other) so totals are easy to read and discuss.
4. **Record what the plan defines as shared — and flag the rest.** Track the parent's own view of which expenses are shareable, but flag that the allocation/share is a plan/legal question (confirm with counsel).
5. **Neutral tone, even about money.** "Invoice $120; reimbursement requested 5/3; not yet received" — not "he never pays his share."
6. **Running balance, clearly shown.** Make "who has paid what / what is outstanding" obvious at a glance.
7. **You record; this organizes.** The content is the parent's actual expenses and payments; this structures them and screens out blame language.

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Record each expense with date, category, item, amount, who paid, and receipt reference.
- Track reimbursement requests and payments with dates and method.
- Show a running balance / outstanding summary.
- Keep tone neutral; describe money facts without blame.
- Flag allocation/share/support concepts with *confirm with counsel for your jurisdiction.*

**Must Not:**
- Characterize or accuse the other parent ("never pays").
- Calculate child support, invent allocation percentages, or assert what is "owed."
- Predict court outcomes or assert a parent "must" reimburse.
- Include non-child or retaliatory expenses.
- Use the child as a messenger about money.

## Instructions

### Stage 1 — Set Up Categories and the Sharing Basis
Confirm the expense categories and restate (as-is) what the plan says about sharing. Flag that the share percentage and what's shareable are plan/legal questions (confirm with counsel) — the ledger records facts, not the legal answer.

### Stage 2 — Log Each Expense Factually
For each expense: date, category, item, amount, who paid, receipt reference. Strip any blame language. Note where a receipt is missing so it can be obtained.

### Stage 3 — Track Reimbursements
Record each reimbursement request (date, amount, what it covers) and each payment (date, amount, method). Keep requests and payments linkable.

### Stage 4 — Produce the Running Summary
Build a category-totals view and an outstanding-balance summary, stated neutrally (amounts requested, amounts paid, amounts pending) — without asserting obligation.

### Stage 5 — Tone & Completeness Check
Re-read for blame language and remove it; confirm every line is receipt-backed or flagged as missing; confirm allocation concepts carry confirm-with-counsel.

## Output Format

```markdown
# Shared Child Expense Ledger — [Child's initials]
Maintained by [parent], period [start–end]. Status: [private record / for mediation / for attorney]
NOT A LEGAL FILING OR SUPPORT CALCULATION. A factual record subject to review by counsel/mediator.

## Sharing Basis (as the parent understands the plan — confirm with counsel)
[e.g., "Plan states uncovered medical split 50/50, reimbursement within 30 days." — confirm with counsel for your jurisdiction.]

## Expense Log
| Date | Category | Item | Amount | Paid by | Receipt | Shareable? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-04-03 | Medical | Pediatric copay | $35.00 | Parent A | yes (#A03) | per plan |
| 2026-04-10 | Activities | Soccer registration | $120.00 | Parent A | yes (#A10) | per plan |

## Reimbursement Log
| Date requested | Covers | Amount requested | Date paid | Amount paid | Method |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-04-15 | Mar–Apr medical | $77.50 | 2026-04-22 | $77.50 | App transfer |

## Running Summary
- Category totals: Medical $___, Childcare $___, School $___, Activities $___.
- Reimbursements requested: $___ | paid: $___ | pending: $___.
- [Allocation/obligation questions → confirm with counsel.]
```

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| "He owes me $400 and refuses to pay" | "Reimbursement requested 5/3: $400; pending as of [date]" |
| Calculate "your child support should be $X" | Record expenses only; flag support "confirm with counsel" |
| Decide an expense "must" be split 50/50 | Note the plan's stated basis; flag allocation "confirm with counsel" |
| Log an item with no receipt as fact | Note "receipt missing — to obtain" |
| Add non-child or retaliatory costs | Track only the child's shared expenses |
| Editorialize about spending habits | Keep amounts and dates; omit commentary |
| Send the child to collect money | Adults handle reimbursement directly |
| Treat the ledger as a court order | Treat it as a record; let counsel interpret obligation |

## Adaptations

**By age band:**
- **0–3:** Childcare, formula/diapers if shared, well-child visits, early-intervention costs.
- **4–8:** School supplies, after-school care, lessons, dental, first activities.
- **9–12:** Sports/activity fees, tutoring, school trips, devices for school.
- **13–18:** Larger activity/competition costs, test fees, driving-related and pre-college expenses; involve the teen in budgeting, not in collecting money.

**By situation/profile:**
- **High-conflict co-parent:** Use a shared-expense app that timestamps requests and approvals; keep the ledger meticulous; pair with `parenting_custody_communication_log_template.md`.
- **Long distance:** Track travel/visitation-related costs separately and note who bears them per the plan (confirm with counsel).
- **Child with ADHD/autism/medical needs:** Track therapy, evaluation, medication, and accommodation costs as their own category; pair with `parenting_custody_special_needs_plan_addendum.md`.
- **Safety concerns:** Keep financial records securely; route disputes through counsel/advocate; see Safety Block.
