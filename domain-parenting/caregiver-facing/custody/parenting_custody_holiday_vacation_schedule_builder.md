**Purpose:** Help a parent build the holiday, school-break, and summer rotation — the part of a parenting plan that says who has the child on the days the regular schedule doesn't cover — as a clean, specific rotation table that supersedes the regular residential schedule. The output organizes the parent's own choices into a calm, factual document a mediator, guardian ad litem (GAL), or court will take seriously. It does not argue, accuse, or invent legal standards.

**When to use:** Drafting the holiday/break section of a parenting plan; resolving recurring holiday disputes by writing them down once; planning a summer rotation or vacation-travel rules; mapping religious or cultural observances onto the calendar.

**When NOT to use:** You need to know what holiday split a court will order or whether a term is enforceable → that is legal advice; take the draft to an attorney or mediator. There is a safety concern about holiday contact → handle safety first (see Safety Block) and work through counsel/advocate.

---

## Safety Block

Stop and use a different pathway if:
- A child is being abused or is unsafe in either home → in the US, Childhelp National Child Abuse Hotline 1-800-422-4453; emergencies 911. Do not negotiate holiday time around an unsafe situation; document and route to CPS/law enforcement and your attorney.
- There is domestic violence, threats, stalking, or a protective/restraining order → National Domestic Violence Hotline 1-800-799-7233 (US). Holiday exchanges and travel must be designed for safety first, usually with legal/advocate involvement — not drafted unilaterally.
- A child is talking about not wanting to be alive, or you are worried about their mental health → 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (US); pediatrician same-day.

This prompt is educational support for organizing your own decisions. It is not a substitute for clinical, safety, or legal services.

## Scope Boundary — Read First

This builds a **parent-authored schedule**. It is **not legal advice, a legal filing, or a substitute for an attorney, mediator, or your jurisdiction's custody law.** Holiday-allocation norms, travel-consent requirements, and terminology **vary by state and country and change over time.** Have an attorney or mediator review this before you rely on it or submit it. This prompt will **not** invent statutes, predict what a court will decide, or assert that a rotation or term is "enforceable," "standard," or "what the court wants." Where a legal concept appears (e.g., out-of-state travel notice, passport consent), it is explained in plain language and flagged *confirm with counsel for your jurisdiction.*

## Core Principles

1. **The holiday schedule overrides the regular one.** State this explicitly so there's no ambiguity when a holiday falls during the "wrong" parent's regular time.
2. **Alternate the big ones; fix the personal ones.** Major holidays are commonly alternated year to year (even/odd); each parent commonly keeps their own birthday, Mother's/Father's Day. Splitting a single day (morning/afternoon) is an option but adds an exchange.
3. **Define holidays by exact dates and times.** "Christmas" is ambiguous; "Dec 24 noon to Dec 25 noon" is not. Tie each holiday to a clear start and end.
4. **Plan summer as blocks, not as chaos.** Summer is usually split into defined weeks/blocks chosen by a deadline, with a tiebreaker rule (e.g., even-year parent picks first).
5. **Honor what matters to this family.** Religious, cultural, and family-tradition days belong in the rotation if they matter to the child — named neutrally.
6. **Specific beats vague, and a tiebreaker beats a fight.** Where you can't fix the answer, fix the selection rule and deadline.
7. **You decide; this organizes.** The content is the parent's choices, formatted into a clean rotation table and pressure-tested for gaps and conflicts with the regular schedule.

## Constraints

**Must:**
- State that the holiday/break schedule supersedes the regular residential schedule.
- Define each holiday by exact start/end date and time, and how it rotates (alternating even/odd, fixed, or split).
- Build summer as defined blocks with a selection rule and deadline.
- Include a tiebreaker/selection rule wherever choice is involved.
- Flag travel-notice, consent, and passport concepts with *confirm with counsel for your jurisdiction.*

**Must Not:**
- Characterize, accuse, or disparage the other parent.
- Invent legal standards, statutes, travel-consent rules, or predict court outcomes.
- Assert that any rotation or term is "enforceable," "standard," or "what the court wants."
- Insert the child as a messenger or decision-maker.
- Produce strategic language designed to capture the most desirable days at the child's expense.

## Instructions

### Stage 1 — Inventory the Calendar
List every holiday, break, and special day that matters to this family, plus the school calendar's key dates. Flag any input that is a legal question (e.g., international travel consent) for counsel.

### Stage 2 — Choose an Allocation Method per Day
For each holiday/day, pick: **alternate by year** (even/odd), **fixed** (always one parent), or **split** (morning/afternoon, with one extra exchange). Note the trade-offs of splitting (more transitions) vs. alternating (less frequent but cleaner). Default personal days (each parent's birthday, Mother's/Father's Day) to that parent.

### Stage 3 — Build the Summer & Long-Break Rotation
Define summer/winter/spring breaks as blocks (e.g., two non-consecutive weeks each), with a **selection rule** (who picks first in even vs. odd years) and a **deadline** by which each parent must commit their choice. Address how summer interacts with the regular schedule (suspends it / runs alongside it).

### Stage 4 — Add Travel Provisions (literacy, not advice)
If travel is likely, draft a neutral travel-notice provision (itinerary, contact info, return date) and, for out-of-state/international, note passport/consent considerations — each flagged *confirm with counsel for your jurisdiction.* Use `parenting_custody_common_plan_provisions_explainer.md` for plain-language definitions.

### Stage 5 — Assemble the Rotation Table & Review
Produce a clean year-at-a-glance rotation table (even-year / odd-year columns), confirm each entry has dates and times, confirm the supersedes line is present, and confirm the tone is neutral and every legal concept is flagged.

## Output Format

```markdown
# Holiday, Break & Summer Schedule — [Child's initials]
Draft [version], [date]. Status: [private draft / for mediation / for attorney review]
NOT A LEGAL FILING. Subject to review by counsel/mediator and jurisdiction requirements.

**This schedule SUPERSEDES the regular residential schedule for the days it covers.**

## Holiday Rotation
| Holiday / Day | Start → End (dates & times) | Even years | Odd years | Method |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Thanksgiving | Wed 6pm → Sun 6pm | Parent A | Parent B | Alternate |
| Winter Break — 1st half | [dates] | Parent A | Parent B | Alternate |
| Winter Break — 2nd half | [dates] | Parent B | Parent A | Alternate |
| Child's birthday | [date/time] | [shared/split/alternate] | | |
| [Parent A] birthday | [date] | Parent A | Parent A | Fixed |
| Mother's Day / Father's Day | [date] | with that parent | with that parent | Fixed |
| [Religious/cultural observance] | [dates] | [allocation] | | |

## Summer & Long Breaks
- Summer: [number] weeks each, [consecutive/non-consecutive].
- Selection rule: [even-year parent picks first; odd-year parent picks first].
- Deadline: choices committed in writing by [date] each year.
- Interaction with regular schedule: [suspends / runs alongside].

## Travel Provisions
- Notice: [days] advance written itinerary with dates, locations, contacts.
- Out-of-state / international: [passport/consent note — confirm with counsel].

## Tiebreakers
[Selection rules and deadlines for any contested choice.]

---
## Open questions for your attorney/mediator
- [Jurisdiction-specific items — e.g., travel-consent or passport requirements.]
```

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| Write "Christmas with Mom" | Specify "Dec 24 noon → Dec 25 noon, alternating even/odd" |
| Leave summer as "to be worked out" | Define blocks, a selection rule, and a deadline |
| Claim "courts always split holidays this way" | Present it as the parent's proposal; flag norms "confirm with counsel" |
| Split every holiday into morning/afternoon | Reserve splitting for days that matter; alternate the rest |
| Omit the supersedes line | State holidays override the regular schedule |
| Add "since he ruined last Christmas…" | Keep the table factual; omit history |
| Assert a travel-consent rule from memory | Note the concept and flag "confirm with counsel" |
| Make the child choose where to spend a holiday | Adults allocate; the child is informed, not deciding |

## Adaptations

**By age band:**
- **0–3:** Keep holiday blocks short; avoid long separations from the primary attachment figure; favor a few meaningful days over many fragmented exchanges.
- **4–8:** Predictable, named days the child can anticipate; a travel object that comes along; keep splitting to a minimum.
- **9–12:** Account for the child's own events and friend plans during breaks; longer summer blocks reduce churn.
- **13–18:** Build flexibility for the teen's jobs, camps, and social commitments; name how reasonable teen preferences are weighed without making the teen the decider. *Whether a court weighs a teen's preference — confirm with counsel.*

**By situation/profile:**
- **High-conflict co-parent:** Maximize specificity; fix dates/times and selection deadlines so nothing is "worked out" in the moment; pair with `parenting_custody_communication_log_template.md`.
- **Long distance:** Bias toward longer break blocks and summer; build travel logistics and notice into the rotation.
- **Child with ADHD/autism/medical needs:** Preserve routine where possible; keep holiday transitions few and well-prepared; attach `parenting_custody_special_needs_plan_addendum.md`.
- **Safety concerns:** Do not self-draft holiday travel/exchange terms around an unsafe parent; see Safety Block and work through counsel/advocate.
