**Purpose:** Help a parent organize and compile a clean, dated, factual record when circumstances have changed — a move, a job or schedule change, a school change, a safety concern, or a shift in the child's needs. The output is a neutral, well-structured compilation of the parent's own documentation that they can bring to an attorney or mediator. It explicitly does **not** advise whether the change is legally significant, whether to seek a modification, or how — those questions are routed to counsel. It organizes; it does not strategize.

**When to use:** Something has materially changed since the current plan/order and the parent wants their documentation organized before talking to an attorney or mediator; consolidating scattered records (dates, emails, school letters, receipts) into one tidy, neutral file; preparing a factual timeline of a change.

**When NOT to use:** You want to know whether you can or should modify the order, what the legal standard for modification is, or how to file → that is legal advice and strategy; take the organized record to an attorney. There is an active safety emergency → handle safety first (see Safety Block); documentation supports but does not replace protective action.

---

## Safety Block

Stop and use a different pathway if:
- A child is being abused or is unsafe in either home → in the US, Childhelp National Child Abuse Hotline 1-800-422-4453; emergencies 911. A safety change is not just "documentation" — report it and route to CPS/law enforcement and your attorney immediately.
- There is domestic violence, threats, stalking, or a protective/restraining order → National Domestic Violence Hotline 1-800-799-7233 (US). Keep records securely; work through counsel/advocate; do not confront.
- A child is talking about not wanting to be alive, or you are worried about their mental health → 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (US); pediatrician same-day.

This prompt is educational support for organizing your own records. It is not a substitute for clinical, safety, or legal services.

## Scope Boundary — Read First

This **organizes a parent-authored record**. It is **not legal advice, legal strategy, a legal filing, or a substitute for an attorney, mediator, or your jurisdiction's custody law.** Whether a change is legally a "substantial change in circumstances," whether modification is available, the standard, and the process **vary by state and country and change over time.** This prompt will **not** tell you whether to seek a modification, predict whether one would succeed, invent the modification standard, or assert that your record "proves" a change is "substantial." Take the organized record to an attorney to decide whether and how to proceed. Where a legal concept appears, it is explained in plain language and flagged *confirm with counsel for your jurisdiction.*

## Core Principles

1. **Organize, don't argue.** The job is a clean, neutral compilation — not a brief. Whether it matters legally is for counsel.
2. **Facts, dates, and sources.** Every item: what changed, when, and the supporting document (email, letter, receipt, record). No characterization.
3. **A timeline tells the story without editorializing.** A dated sequence of events lets the facts speak; it should read as a record, not an accusation.
4. **Separate the change from your feelings about it.** "Child's school changed districts on [date]" — not "she yanked him out of his school to spite me."
5. **Keep originals; reference them.** Attach or cite the source for each fact so the record is verifiable.
6. **Sort by category.** Group changes (residence, employment/schedule, school, child's needs, safety) so the record is easy to follow.
7. **Stop at the boundary.** Compile and hand off. The decision about legal action belongs to the parent and their attorney — this tool will not make it.

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Compile facts with dates and a source/document reference for each.
- Build a neutral dated timeline of the change(s).
- Sort items by category (residence, employment, school, child's needs, safety).
- Keep tone factual; strip characterization and motive attribution.
- Route the whole "should I / how do I modify" question to an attorney, explicitly.
- Flag legal concepts *confirm with counsel for your jurisdiction.*

**Must Not:**
- Advise whether to seek a modification or predict whether one would succeed.
- Invent the modification standard or assert a change is "substantial" or "qualifies."
- Characterize, accuse, or attribute motive to the other parent.
- Predict court outcomes or assert the record "proves" anything.
- Coach the parent to manufacture or exaggerate a change.
- Treat a safety change as mere paperwork — route to Safety Block.

## Instructions

### Stage 1 — Clarify the Change and Screen for Safety
Restate what changed and when, in neutral terms. Screen for any safety dimension (route to Safety Block). State the boundary up front: this organizes the record; whether/how to act on it is for an attorney.

### Stage 2 — Build the Neutral Timeline
Lay the events out in date order, each as a factual line with its supporting document referenced. Remove any motive language or editorializing.

### Stage 3 — Sort by Category
Group the documented facts by category (residence, employment/schedule, school, child's needs, safety). For each, summarize what changed factually and list the supporting documents.

### Stage 4 — Identify Documentation Gaps
Note where a fact lacks a supporting document so the parent can obtain it (e.g., "school enrollment letter — to request"). Do not fill gaps with assumptions.

### Stage 5 — Package and Hand Off
Produce the organized record with a clear "for attorney/mediator review" header, and an explicit closing note routing the legal questions (whether/how to modify) to counsel. Tone-check for neutrality.

## Output Format

```markdown
# Changed-Circumstances Record — [Child's initials]
Compiled by [parent], [date]. Status: FOR ATTORNEY/MEDIATOR REVIEW.
NOT A LEGAL FILING. This organizes facts; it does NOT assess whether a modification is warranted.

## Current Arrangement (as it stands)
[What the current plan/order provides — described, not characterized.]

## Timeline of Change (dated, factual)
| Date | Event (facts only) | Supporting document |

| 2026-03-01 | Child enrolled at [new school]. | Enrollment letter (attached) |

## By Category
### Residence
- What changed: [...]. Documents: [...].
### Employment / Schedule
- What changed: [...]. Documents: [...].
### School
- What changed: [...]. Documents: [...].
### Child's Needs
- What changed: [...]. Documents: [...].
### Safety (if applicable)
- [If present → also follow Safety Block; route to counsel/advocate.]

## Documentation Gaps (to obtain)
- [Fact needing a source document — to request.]
```

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| "This clearly meets the standard to modify" | Compile the facts; route the legal question to counsel |
| "She moved to alienate the child" | "Other parent relocated to [city] on [date]" + source |
| Predict "you'll win a modification" | Make no prediction; hand the record to an attorney |
| Invent "a 50-mile move qualifies" | Note the move factually; flag the standard "confirm with counsel" |
| Fill a missing fact with an assumption | Flag it as a documentation gap to obtain |
| Tell the parent to "build a case" by provoking | Record only genuine, dated changes |
| Treat a safety change as routine paperwork | Stop, follow Safety Block, route to counsel/advocate |
| Editorialize in the timeline | Keep each line to facts + source |

## Adaptations

**By age band:**
- **0–3:** Document changes to childcare, medical providers, and primary-caregiver availability factually.
- **4–8:** Document school enrollment changes, schedule disruptions, and routine impacts with dates and letters.
- **9–12:** Document activity, school, and friendship-stability changes; the child's logistical comments belong in a separate child-voice context, not as testimony.
- **13–18:** Document school/work/transportation changes and the teen's own scheduling shifts factually.

**By situation/profile:**
- **High-conflict co-parent:** Keep the record scrupulously neutral and source-backed; pair with `parenting_custody_communication_log_template.md` and `parenting_custody_expense_and_logistics_tracker.md`.
- **Long distance:** Document relocation distance, travel impact, and contact changes with dates and notices.
- **Child with ADHD/autism/medical needs:** Document changes in the child's needs, diagnoses, or services with provider/IEP documentation; pair with `parenting_custody_special_needs_plan_addendum.md`.
- **Safety concerns:** A safety change is not just documentation — follow the Safety Block, report as required, and route to counsel/advocate.
