**Purpose:** Give a parent a template and method for keeping a factual, neutral, dated record of co-parenting communication, exchanges, missed or late pickups, and noteworthy incidents — organized so the record reads as a calm, contemporaneous log rather than an argument or accusation. A clean record supports memory, supports cost- and schedule-discussions, and can be shared with a mediator, guardian ad litem (GAL), or court if needed. It does not argue a case or characterize the other parent.

**When to use:** Maintaining an ongoing record of co-parenting interactions; documenting recurring late/missed exchanges or schedule deviations factually; capturing what happened during a specific incident while it's fresh; building a tidy record before a mediation or GAL meeting.

**When NOT to use:** You want advice on whether a pattern is "contempt," whether to file something, or what a log "proves" legally → that is legal advice; take the log to an attorney. There is an active safety situation → handle safety first (see Safety Block); documentation supports but does not replace calling for help.

---

## Safety Block

Stop and use a different pathway if:
- A child is being abused or is unsafe in either home → in the US, Childhelp National Child Abuse Hotline 1-800-422-4453; emergencies 911. Document factually AND report; a log is not a substitute for reporting.
- There is domestic violence, threats, stalking, or a protective/restraining order → National Domestic Violence Hotline 1-800-799-7233 (US). Keep records safely; share with your advocate/attorney; do not confront.
- A child is talking about not wanting to be alive, or you are worried about their mental health → 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (US); pediatrician same-day.

This prompt is educational support for organizing your own records. It is not a substitute for clinical, safety, or legal services.

## Scope Boundary — Read First

This builds a **parent-authored record**. It is **not legal advice, a legal filing, evidence rulings, or a substitute for an attorney, mediator, or your jurisdiction's custody law.** What records are admissible, how they are weighed, and recordkeeping requirements **vary by state and country and change over time.** Have an attorney or mediator review how to use a log before you rely on it or submit it. This prompt will **not** invent legal standards, tell you a log "proves contempt," predict what a court will decide, or assert that an entry is "admissible." Where a legal concept appears, it is explained in plain language and flagged *confirm with counsel for your jurisdiction.*

---

## Core Principles

1. **Facts only — who, what, when, where.** A record's power is its neutrality. The moment it editorializes ("he was deliberately cruel"), it reads as an argument and loses credibility.
2. **Contemporaneous beats reconstructed.** Log at the time or same day. Note when an entry was written if it's after the fact.
3. **Neutral language, no adjectives of blame.** "Pickup occurred at 6:42 pm; scheduled time was 6:00 pm" — not "he was late again, as usual."
4. **Quote, don't characterize.** For communications, record the exact words (or attach the message) rather than your interpretation of intent.
5. **Separate observation from feeling.** If you note an impact ("child was tearful at drop-off"), keep it observational, not diagnostic, and never attribute motive to the other parent.
6. **Consistency and completeness.** Log the routine and uneventful too, not only the problems — a record of only grievances looks selective.
7. **You record; this organizes.** The content is what actually happened; this structures it into a clean, dated, neutral format and screens out editorializing.

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Record facts only: date, time, who, what, where, and exact words where relevant.
- Strip every adjective of blame, motive attribution, and editorial.
- Note whether each entry is contemporaneous or written later.
- Keep impact observations observational and non-diagnostic.
- Flag any legal concept (admissibility, "contempt," "violation") with *confirm with counsel.*

**Must Not:**
- Characterize, accuse, diagnose, or attribute motive to the other parent.
- Invent legal standards or assert an entry "proves" anything or is "admissible."
- Predict court outcomes.
- Coach the parent to bait, provoke, or manufacture incidents for the record.
- Include the child's reports as ammunition or position the child as a witness.
- Add commentary, sarcasm, or history.

## Instructions

### Stage 1 — Confirm What to Log and the Method
Confirm the categories (communication / exchanges / incidents) and the capture method. Recommend logging the routine as well as the exceptions so the record is complete, not selective.

### Stage 2 — Neutralize the Raw Entries
Take the parent's raw account and rewrite each into a factual, neutral entry: date, time, location, who, what happened, exact words quoted where relevant. Remove adjectives of blame and any attribution of intent. Where the parent wrote an interpretation, convert it to an observation or drop it.

### Stage 3 — Tag Contemporaneity and Source
For each entry, note whether it was written at the time or later, and the source (app screenshot, text, direct observation). Encourage attaching the original message rather than paraphrasing.

### Stage 4 — Format the Running Log
Produce the entries in a consistent dated table. Keep impact notes (e.g., child's observable state) factual and rare; never diagnose or assign motive.

### Stage 5 — Selectivity & Tone Check
Re-read the log: is it complete (includes uneventful exchanges), neutral (no blame language), and free of legal conclusions? Flag any legal concept with confirm-with-counsel.

## Output Format

```markdown
# Co-Parenting Log — [Child's initials]
Maintained by [parent], started [date]. Status: [private record / for mediation / for attorney]
NOT A LEGAL FILING. A factual record subject to review by counsel/mediator.

## Method
[Where communications occur; how exchanges are documented; originals retained.]

## Running Log
| Date | Time | Type | What happened (facts only) | Exact words / source | Written |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-05-12 | 6:42 pm | Exchange | Pickup at [point]. Scheduled 6:00 pm. | Text 6:35: "running late" (attached) | Same day |
| 2026-05-14 | 7:10 pm | Communication | Asked re: dentist appt 5/20. | Email (attached) | Contemporaneous |
| 2026-05-18 | 9:00 am | Exchange | Exchange on time at school. | Direct observation | Same day |

## Notes on Use
- Originals (texts, emails, app logs) retained and attached where possible.
- Entries written later are marked as such.
- [Any legal concept — e.g., "whether this matters legally" — confirm with counsel.]
```

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| "He was late again, deliberately disrespecting me" | "Pickup 6:42 pm; scheduled 6:00 pm" |
| "She's an unfit parent — see entry 4" | Record the fact; draw no conclusion |
| Paraphrase a text as "basically threatened me" | Quote the exact words / attach the message |
| Log only the other parent's misses | Log all exchanges, including the uneventful ones |
| Add "this proves contempt" | Note the fact; flag legal meaning "confirm with counsel" |
| Diagnose the child ("clearly traumatized") | Note observable state ("tearful at drop-off") |
| Bait the other parent to create a record | Record only what genuinely happens |
| Treat the log as a legal verdict | Treat it as a neutral record; let counsel interpret |

## Adaptations

**By age band:**
- **0–3:** Note feeding/nap/medication handoff details factually (e.g., last feeding time) — useful and neutral.
- **4–8:** Note school items, comfort objects, and homework that traveled; keep child-state notes observational.
- **9–12:** Note activity logistics and device/homework continuity; record the child's logistical messages factually, never as testimony.
- **13–18:** Note the teen's own communications and schedule changes factually; do not enlist the teen as a witness.

**By situation/profile:**
- **High-conflict co-parent:** Move all communication to a parenting app so the record is automatic and timestamped; log scrupulously and completely; pair with `parenting_custody_exchange_and_transition_protocol.md`.
- **Long distance:** Log virtual-contact attempts and outcomes factually (time, duration, completed/missed).
- **Child with ADHD/autism/medical needs:** Log medication and therapy handoffs factually; pair with `parenting_custody_special_needs_plan_addendum.md`.
- **Safety concerns:** Keep records securely; share with an advocate/attorney; see Safety Block. Do not confront based on the log.
