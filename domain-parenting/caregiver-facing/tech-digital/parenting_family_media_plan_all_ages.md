# Family Media Plan for the Whole Household

**Objective:** Produce one written tech agreement for the whole household — the
protected times and places, each child's tier of rules, the adults' commitments,
overnight device storage, the readiness gate for new devices and accounts, and a
30-day trial with a review date.

**When to Use:**
- The family has children of different ages and each has different, ad-hoc rules
  that cause "not fair!" arguments.
- Screens are crowding out sleep, meals, homework, or time together — for adults as
  well as children.
- A new device has arrived (tablet, console, a child's phone) and the household
  rules need resetting.
- **Not this prompt if** the question is **whether a tween should get a first phone
  and which type** — use
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/ages-9-12/parenting_first_phone_decision_framework.md`
  (this plan's readiness gate sends you there). Under-3s only →
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/ages-0-3/parenting_screen_time_for_under_3.md`.
  A teen's social-media terms →
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/ages-13-18/parenting_teen_social_media_renegotiation.md`.
  Gaming fights and surprise in-game bills → `parenting_gaming_conflict_and_spending_reset.md`;
  AI chatbots → `parenting_ai_chatbot_companion_family_rules.md` (both in this folder).
  This prompt is the household-wide umbrella.

## Safety Block

- **A child is being contacted by an adult stranger, asked for images, or
  threatened with images** → stop and follow
  `domain-psy-ops/personal-defense/psyops_youth_online_manipulation_guide.md`: do not
  pay, do not delete the account, preserve usernames and messages (never save
  sexual images of a minor), report to NCMEC CyberTipline (US) and police.
- **Screens are tied to self-harm content, suicidal talk, or eating-disorder
  content** → 988 (US) for self-harm talk; pediatrician this week; tighten access
  while care starts.
- **A child cannot stop despite clear harm** (sleep, school, friendships falling
  away for months) → screen for problematic use with the pediatrician; see the
  gaming prompt for gaming disorder criteria.
- Outside the US, use your local reporting and crisis services.

## Inputs / Context

1. **Every household member:** ages, devices each uses, current rules.
2. **What goes wrong now:** sleep, mealtimes, homework, fights at switch-off, adults'
   phone use, content worries, online contact.
3. **Values:** what the family wants more of (sleep, outdoor time, reading, being
   together) — the plan protects these.
4. **Shared custody or other homes** where rules differ.
5. **Children's own views** (ask each child over 6).

## Method

1. **Set safe defaults first (DS-26).** Defaults that carry most of the benefit and
   are easy to keep:
   - All devices charge **outside bedrooms** overnight (one family charging shelf),
     adults included. Sleep is the best-evidenced harm.
   - No screens at **meals** and in the **first and last 30–60 minutes** of the day.
   - Screens in **shared spaces** for under-12s.
   - **Co-view and co-play** for young children.
   These follow the logic of the AAP Family Media Plan (healthychildren.org) and the
   2023 US Surgeon General's advisory on social media and youth mental health.
2. **Analyse risk on several dimensions, not minutes (RT-02).** For each child rate
   five areas: sleep displacement, content, contact (who can reach them), commerce
   (spending, ads, loot boxes), and crowding-out (homework, activity, family time).
   Set rules where the risk is, not a single time cap.
3. **Write age tiers.**
   - **Under 2:** video chat with family only; see the under-3 prompt.
   - **2–5:** about an hour a day of quality content, watched with an adult; no
     devices for calming every upset.
   - **6–9:** shared-space use, adult-approved apps and games, no open chat with
     strangers, no social media.
   - **10–12:** gradual autonomy; first-device decision through the readiness gate;
     contact limited to known people.
   - **13–17:** negotiated rules, more privacy, the sleep rule still holds; teen
     helps write their own section.
4. **Check for over-protection (DP-12).** Rules that are stricter than any risk
   justifies breed secrecy and do not teach self-control. For each rule ask: what
   harm does this prevent, and is there a less restrictive rule that would? Leave
   room for the child's real social life online.
5. **Commit the adults.** Phones away at meals and during homework help; no
   scrolling during bedtime routine; adults use the charging shelf too. Children
   copy behaviour more than rules.
6. **Set the readiness gate.** A new device, game with online chat, social account,
   or AI app needs: the child can explain the rules, has shown responsible use at the
   current tier for ~3 months, and a parent has set the controls. Send phone and
   social decisions to the dedicated prompts.
7. **Run a 30-day trial (DP-11).** Pick 3–4 rules, write them on one page, sign, and
   track one or two signals (bedtime actually reached, mealtime fights). Review at
   day 30; keep, loosen, or tighten one thing at a time.

## Output Format

```
# Family Media Plan — [family], trial [start] → review [date]

## What we're protecting (values)
## Household defaults (everyone, adults included)
## Charging shelf location and time
## Risk map per child: sleep / content / contact / commerce / crowding-out
## [Child, age] tier rules (one block per child)
## Adult commitments
## Readiness gate for new devices, games, accounts, AI apps
## Two-home notes (if relevant)
## Signals we'll watch; review questions at day 30
## Signatures
```

## Verification

- [ ] Overnight charging outside bedrooms applies to everyone.
- [ ] Each child's rules target their highest-rated risks, not a flat minute cap.
- [ ] Each rule has a harm it prevents (over-protection check done).
- [ ] Adults have written commitments.
- [ ] Phone and social decisions route to the dedicated prompts via the gate.
- [ ] Trial has a fixed review date and 1–2 signals.

## False-Positive Prevention

| Misfire | What it looks like | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Minutes as the only metric | "Two hours max for everyone" | Rate sleep, content, contact, commerce, crowding-out. |
| Same rules for 5 and 15 | Teen has the toddler's rules | Tiers by age and track record. |
| Rules for kids only | Parent on phone at dinner | Adult commitments in writing. |
| All screen use is harmful | Banning video calls with grandparents | Connection and creation are not the same as scrolling. |
| Over-tightening after one incident | Total ban for a month | Fix the specific risk; keep the relationship. |
| Plan as permanent law | Never revisited | 30-day trial, then reviews each term. |

## Adaptations

- **Two homes:** agree the sleep and contact rules across homes if possible; accept
  differences elsewhere and say so to the children.
- **Neurodivergent child:** screens may be a regulation tool; plan transitions with
  timers and a next activity rather than removing the tool.
- **Child with a disability using AAC or assistive tech:** that device is never
  confiscated as a consequence.
- **Shift-working parents:** set the defaults by household routine, not clock time.
- **Low-device households:** a shared family device needs a booking rule.

## Example Output

```
# Family Media Plan — the Okafor-Lindqvists, trial 6 Oct → review 5 Nov

## Protecting
Sleep, Sunday family dinner, the kids' football and drawing.

## Defaults (everyone)
All phones, tablets, and the Switch charge on the hall shelf by 21:00
(Ella 22:00). No devices at meals. First 30 min after waking screen-free.

## Risk map
Noah (4): crowding-out HIGH (tablet for every car trip); content MED.
Freya (10): sleep HIGH (YouTube in bed); contact MED (Roblox chat).
Ella (15): sleep HIGH; content MED; contact MED.

## Noah, 4
Tablet: 3 approved apps, weekends 2 x 30 min, with an adult. Car trips
under 30 min: songs and I-spy.

## Freya, 10
Devices in living room only. Roblox: friends-only chat, no Robux without
asking. YouTube Kids profile. Phone: not yet — readiness gate in spring.

## Ella, 15 (Ella wrote this section)
Phone off the shelf 07:00–22:00. Homework phone in the hall until done.
Location sharing on, both ways (Mum shares too).

## Adults
Mum and Dad: phones on the shelf during dinner and bedtime stories; no
work email after 20:00 unless on call.

## Readiness gate
New app, game with chat, account, or AI app: explain the rules, 3 months
of following current ones, parent sets controls with the child watching.

## Signals
Freya asleep by 21:15 on school nights (target 4 of 5). Dinner fights per
week (now ~3).

## Review 5 Nov
What worked? What one rule changes? Ella proposes her own changes.
```

## Techniques Used

- **DS-26 Safe Defaults Pattern** — overnight charging outside bedrooms, no-screen meals, and shared-space use as the starting set.
- **DP-12 Over-Protection Diagnosis** — each rule is checked for the harm it prevents and a less restrictive option.
- **DP-11 Safe Experiment Design** — a 30-day trial with one or two signals and a single-change review.
- **RT-02 Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework** — sleep, content, contact, commerce, and crowding-out rated per child.
