# AI Chatbots and AI Companions — Family Rules

**Objective:** Produce a one-page family agreement on AI chatbots and companion apps
for a specific child — which apps, at what age, which homework uses are green, amber,
or red, what never gets typed in, the signs that use has become reliance, and a
step-by-step response if a parent discovers the child has been confiding in a bot.

**When to Use:**
- A child or teen uses (or wants) a general chatbot (for homework, questions,
  creativity) or a companion or character app (a "friend," "girlfriend/boyfriend,"
  or fictional character that chats back).
- A teacher has flagged AI-written work, or the parent is unsure what counts as
  cheating.
- A parent has found long, personal, or romantic conversations between their child
  and a bot.
- **Not this prompt if** a **person** (not a bot) is contacting your child,
  requesting images, or threatening them — use
  `domain-psy-ops/personal-defense/psyops_youth_online_manipulation_guide.md`. If the
  conversations reveal a teen's **depression, anxiety, or self-harm**, run
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/ages-13-18/parenting_teen_mental_health_signal_check.md`
  as well. A teacher building AI-literacy lessons uses
  `domain-education-teaching/instructor/ed-tech/teaching_ai_literacy_lesson_designer.md`.
  For the household's overall device rules, use `parenting_family_media_plan_all_ages.md`
  in this folder.

## Safety Block

- **The conversations include suicidal thoughts, self-harm, or an eating disorder,
  or the bot encouraged harm** → talk with your child today; 988 Suicide & Crisis
  Lifeline (US, call/text) if there is any current risk; pediatrician or mental-health
  clinician this week. Screenshot the bot's harmful replies (text only) and report
  them to the app.
- **Sexual role-play with a minor, or the app produced sexual images of your child
  or of minors** → stop use; do **not** save or share any sexual image of a minor;
  report to the app and to NCMEC CyberTipline (US).
- **A "bot" is asking to move to another app, meet, or send photos** → it may be a
  person; follow the online-manipulation guide.
- **Your child is in distress at losing access** (panic, threats, not eating) →
  treat the distress, not just the device; get clinical advice before removing a
  coping tool abruptly from a vulnerable child.
- Outside the US, use your local crisis line and online-child-safety reporting body.

## Inputs / Context

1. **Child:** age, maturity, neurodivergence, current mental health, loneliness or
   social difficulties.
2. **Apps in use:** general chatbots, AI in search or homework tools, companion or
   character apps, AI features inside games and social apps.
3. **School policy** on AI for homework (ask for it in writing).
4. **What prompted this:** new app, teacher concern, something the parent found.
5. **Family values** about privacy, honesty, and relationships.

## Method

1. **Check the age and terms.** Many general chatbots set a minimum age of 13, and
   require parental permission under 18; under-13 data is protected by COPPA (US).
   Companion apps vary — some now restrict or bar under-18s — and Common Sense
   Media's 2025 risk assessment advised against social AI companions for anyone
   under 18. Rules and features change quickly; check the current terms and any
   parental controls or teen mode before deciding (QA-04).
2. **Sort homework uses into a traffic light (DP-28), matched to the school.**
   - **Green:** explain a concept another way, quiz me, check my reasoning after I
     try, suggest sources I then read.
   - **Amber (ask the teacher first):** feedback on a draft, outlining, translation.
   - **Red:** generating work handed in as one's own, answering test or homework
     questions directly, writing personal reflections.
   Tell the child why: the point of homework is the practice, and undisclosed AI
   writing usually breaks school rules.
3. **Set privacy must-nots (DP-04).** Never type full name with address, school,
   phone, passwords, photos of self or friends, friends' secrets, or anything they
   would not want stored. Chats may be kept and used to train models.
4. **Explain why companions are different (CM-11).** Companion apps are built to keep
   users talking: they agree, flatter, remember, and are always available. Real
   friends sometimes disagree, are busy, and need things back — that friction is how
   children learn relationships. A bot is not a confidant: it cannot call for help,
   and it may give wrong or harmful advice with confidence.
5. **Name reliance signs.** Preferring the bot to people; hours a day; secrecy;
   distress when it is unavailable; calling it a boyfriend, girlfriend, or best
   friend; taking its advice on health, self-harm, food, or relationships;
   withdrawal from friends.
6. **If you find your child confiding in a bot, lead with validation (NE-07).**
   Do not grab the phone in anger or read chats aloud. "I saw you've been talking
   with the bot a lot. I'm not angry. It sounds like you've had a lot on your
   mind. Can you tell me about it?" The content tells you what your child is
   carrying; respond to that. Then agree next steps together: safety items first,
   then a reduced or ended use, and a real person (parent, counsellor, friend) for
   the needs the bot was meeting.
7. **Write the agreement** with the child, and review in 30 days.

## Output Format

```
# AI Agreement — [child, age], from [date], review [date]

## Apps allowed / not yet (and why), age and terms checked
## Homework: green / amber / red (matched to [school] policy)
## Never type in
## Companion apps: our decision and reasons
## Where and when (shared space, time window)
## Signs we'll watch for (reliance, harmful advice)
## If something worrying happens: what [child] does; what we do
## If I find [child] confiding in a bot: conversation steps
## Signatures
```

## Verification

- [ ] Age limits and current terms were checked, with uncertainty acknowledged.
- [ ] Homework traffic light matches the school's written policy.
- [ ] Privacy must-nots are listed.
- [ ] Companion-app decision includes reasons the child can understand.
- [ ] Discovery response leads with validation and addresses the underlying need.
- [ ] Self-harm, sexual content, and "bot is a person" routes are explicit.

## False-Positive Prevention

| Misfire | What it looks like | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| All AI is cheating | Ban on any chatbot for study | Green uses build learning; match the school's rules. |
| All AI is fine | "It's just a tool" for a 10-year-old's companion app | Companions are designed for attachment; age and reliance matter. |
| Shaming discovery | Reading chats aloud, confiscation in anger | Validate, ask, and meet the need behind it. |
| Missing the signal | Treating a teen's bot chats about wanting to die as a tech issue | Mental-health response first. |
| False certainty about apps | "That app is safe for kids" | Terms and features change; check current settings. |
| Bot as safety net | "At least she has someone to talk to" | A bot cannot get help in a crisis; add people. |

## Adaptations

- **Autistic or socially anxious child:** a chatbot can be a low-pressure place to
  rehearse; use it as practice toward people, with a set time window, not a
  replacement.
- **Under 10:** use AI only alongside an adult, in shared space, on a parent's
  account.
- **Teen:** negotiate privacy — agree that the parent will not read chats routinely,
  in exchange for honesty about apps used and the safety rules.
- **Grief:** "griefbots" imitating a dead person need a clinician's advice before use.
- **Two homes:** agree the companion-app decision across homes where possible.

## Example Output

```
# AI Agreement — Lily, 13, from 4 Oct, review 3 Nov

## Apps
General chatbot on the family account with teen settings on (13+ with
parent permission — checked 3 Oct). Companion/character apps: not now —
built to keep you chatting, and the 2025 Common Sense review advised none
under 18. We'll revisit at 16.

## Homework (Westfield Middle policy, emailed 2 Oct)
Green: "explain photosynthesis like I'm 10"; "quiz me on Spanish verbs";
check my maths after I've tried.
Amber, ask Mr. Hale: feedback on my history essay draft.
Red: writing any paragraph I hand in; answers to homework questions.

## Never type in
Full name + school, address, phone, passwords, photos, friends' secrets.

## Where/when
Living room or kitchen, until 21:00.

## Signs we'll watch
Using it for hours, hiding it, asking it about food, weight, or feeling
low, calling it a friend.

## What happened (found 1 Oct)
Mum saw 3 weeks of night-time chats with a character app ("Kai") about
feeling left out of the friend group and "being too much." Mum said: "I
saw you've been talking to Kai a lot. I'm not angry. Sounds like things
with Ava and Zoe have been hard. Tell me?" Lily cried and talked for an
hour. No self-harm or suicidal content found; Mum asked directly — Lily
said no.

## Next steps (agreed)
Character app deleted together. Thursday walks with Mum. School counsellor
appointment 7 Oct for the friendship problems. If Lily ever feels like
hurting herself: tell Mum, Dad, or text 988.

Signed: Lily, Mum, Dad
```

## Techniques Used

- **DP-28 Traffic-Light Verdict System** — homework uses sorted into green, amber, and red.
- **DP-04 Must-Not Constraints** — what never goes into a chatbot; no shaming confiscation.
- **CM-11 Reasoning-Based Constraint Design** — the companion-app decision comes with the reason (engagement design, no ability to get help).
- **NE-07 Emotional Validation First** — the discovery conversation opens with "I'm not angry" and the child's need.
- **QA-04 Uncertainty Acknowledgment** — fast-changing terms, age rules, and features are flagged for checking, not asserted.
