# Tech & Digital Prompts (Caregiver-Facing)

Household-level and incident-level planning for children's technology: one family media plan covering every child and the adults, family rules for AI chatbots and AI companion apps, and a reset for gaming that has gone wrong (switch-off fights or an unauthorised in-game spend). Each prompt sets rules where the actual risk is — sleep, content, contact, commerce, crowding-out — rather than by minutes alone, and routes online exploitation and mental-health signals out of the tech frame.

## Scope

**In scope:**
- A whole-household tech agreement (toddler to teen, adults included) with a readiness gate for new devices, games, accounts, and AI apps.
- AI chatbots and companion/character apps: age and terms checks, homework traffic light, privacy, emotional reliance, and what to do if a child is confiding in a bot.
- Gaming conflict and in-game spending: refund requests, purchase locks, proportionate repayment, the driver behind the play, match-based stopping, and the ICD-11 gaming-disorder screen.

## Safety notes

Every prompt carries a Safety Block. A person contacting a child for images, meetings, or money → do not pay, preserve usernames and message text (never save sexual images of a minor), report to NCMEC CyberTipline and police. Self-harm, suicidal, or eating-disorder content in a child's chats or feeds → 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline and a clinician. Violence at switch-off → safety first, then assessment. App terms, age limits, and parental-control features change often — the prompts say to check current settings rather than asserting them. Outside the US, use local equivalents.
