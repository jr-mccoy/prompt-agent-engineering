# Gaming Conflict and In-Game Spending Reset

**Objective:** Produce a reset plan for one child's gaming after something has gone
wrong: the money steps if there was an unauthorised spend, locks so it cannot happen
again, a fair repayment, what gaming is doing for this child, a stopping routine that
works with the game's design, and a clear screen for when it is more than a habit.

**When to Use:**
- A bank or card statement shows in-game purchases the parent did not approve
  (skins, currency, loot boxes, battle passes).
- Every switch-off ends in shouting, slammed controllers, or negotiation.
- Gaming is pushing out sleep, homework, sport, or friends, or a teen games through
  the night.
- **Not this prompt if** you are setting up gaming rules **proactively** for a 9–12
  year-old — use
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/ages-9-12/parenting_video_game_agreement_designer.md`.
  A clinician treating behavioural addiction uses
  `domain-psychology/specialty-clinical/psychology_behavioral_addiction_protocol.md`.
  For general consequence design, use
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/cross-age/parenting_natural_consequence_designer.md`.
  For whole-household device rules, use `parenting_family_media_plan_all_ages.md` in
  this folder.

## Safety Block

- **Violence toward people at switch-off** (hitting, threats, using objects) → safety
  first: step back, keep siblings away, call 911 (US) if anyone is in danger; then
  pediatrician or child mental-health assessment — this is not a rules problem.
- **Self-harm talk or suicidal statements** when gaming is restricted → 988 (US)
  now; do not remove a vulnerable teen's main coping outlet abruptly without a plan
  and clinical advice.
- **An adult in the game is asking for photos, moving chat elsewhere, or offering
  currency in exchange for anything** → stop, preserve usernames, follow
  `domain-psy-ops/personal-defense/psyops_youth_online_manipulation_guide.md`.
- **The spending is large and the child is selling items, using others' cards, or
  borrowing to gamble on skins** → treat as possible gambling harm; talk with the
  pediatrician; card fraud involving someone else's card needs the card issuer.
- Outside the US, use your local emergency, crisis, and child-safety services.

## Inputs / Context

1. **Child:** age, neurodivergence (ADHD and autism change both the pull of games
   and the cost of transitions), mood, friendships, school.
2. **Games and platforms:** titles, console/PC/phone, online or offline, voice chat,
   how spending works in each game.
3. **The incident:** amount, dates, payment method, whether the child understood it
   was real money.
4. **Current pattern:** hours on school days and weekends, bedtime, what happens at
   switch-off.
5. **Household rules now** and what has been tried.

## Method

1. **Contain the money first.** Within days, not weeks:
   - **Refund requests:** Apple via reportaproblem.apple.com; Google Play through the
     order history (fastest within 48 hours, then via the developer); Xbox,
     PlayStation, Nintendo, Steam, and Epic each have their own refund pages and
     limits. State the facts: "My 9-year-old made these purchases without
     permission." Refunds are discretionary and not guaranteed. A card chargeback is
     a last resort and may get the account banned.
   - **Lock it:** remove saved cards; require approval for every purchase (Apple
     "Ask to Buy," Google Family Link purchase approval); set console monthly spending
     limits (Xbox, PlayStation, Nintendo parental controls).
2. **Calculate a proportionate repayment (NE-11).** The aim is learning, not
   punishment. `Repayment = amount × share the child is responsible for`, capped at
   what can be paid within ~4–8 weeks from allowance or jobs. A 7-year-old who did
   not understand real money: small token share; a 15-year-old who used a saved card
   knowingly: most of it. Show the maths to the child.
3. **Explain the design.** Loot boxes, limited-time offers, and premium currencies
   hide real prices and use chance-based rewards; some countries (for example
   Belgium) treat paid loot boxes as gambling. Children are not "weak" for falling
   for systems designed by adults.
4. **Identify the dominant driver (DP-06).** What is gaming doing for this child?
   Social connection (friends are online), competence (good at it when school is
   hard), escape (from anxiety, low mood, bullying, family conflict), or
   regulation (ADHD stimulation). The driver decides the plan: a social gamer needs
   friends-time protected; an escaping gamer needs the thing they are escaping
   addressed.
5. **Find the root cause of switch-off fights (RT-09).** Usually: stopping
   mid-match (losing progress or letting the team down), no warning, no next
   activity, or a parent who negotiates. Fix: stop by **matches or natural save
   points**, not minutes; 10- and 2-minute warnings; a fixed next activity
   (snack, shower); and the same response every time.
6. **Gate the concern level (DP-05).** WHO's ICD-11 gaming disorder (6C51) requires
   impaired control, increasing priority over other interests and daily
   activities, and continuing despite harm, with significant impairment usually over
   at least 12 months. Most children who game a lot do not meet this. Use three
   levels: **habit** (rules and routine), **concern** (impairment in one area >1
   month — pediatrician conversation), **clinical** (the ICD-11 pattern, or gaming
   to escape depression or anxiety — child mental-health assessment).
7. **Write the reset agreement** with a 4-week review.

## Output Format

```
# Gaming Reset — [child, age], [date]

## Money: what happened, refund requests filed (where, date, outcome)
## Purchase locks now in place
## Repayment: amount × share = total; schedule
## What gaming does for [child] (dominant driver) and what that means
## Switch-off fights: root cause and new stopping routine
## Concern level: habit / concern / clinical — evidence
## Agreement: when, where, how long (by matches/save points), voice chat, spending
## Review in 4 weeks: signals
```

## Verification

- [ ] Refund requests named per platform, with "not guaranteed" stated.
- [ ] Purchase approval and spending limits are set, saved cards removed.
- [ ] Repayment is proportionate to age and knowledge, with the maths shown.
- [ ] A dominant driver is named with evidence.
- [ ] Stopping uses matches or save points with warnings and a next activity.
- [ ] Concern level uses ICD-11 features and impairment, not hours alone.

## False-Positive Prevention

| Misfire | What it looks like | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Hours = addiction | "Four hours on Saturday — he's addicted" | ICD-11 needs impaired control and impairment, typically over 12 months. |
| Missing escape gaming | Tightening rules for a teen gaming to avoid bullying | Address the driver; rules alone push it underground. |
| Child as fraudster | Treating a 7-year-old's tap-to-buy as theft | Young children often don't grasp in-game money is real. |
| Minute timers mid-match | "Time's up" in the middle of a ranked game | Stop at match ends and save points. |
| Total ban after the bill | Console confiscated indefinitely | Fix the locks, repay, reset rules; keep a path back. |
| Assuming refunds | "The store will refund it" | Discretionary; file fast, lock anyway. |

## Adaptations

- **ADHD:** transitions are harder; use visual timers, a physical "last match" token,
  and a movement activity next.
- **Autistic child:** warn about the stopping rule well in advance; special-interest
  games may be key to wellbeing — protect time for them.
- **Teen:** negotiate the plan; protect sleep (devices out of the bedroom) as the
  non-negotiable; let them manage their own time within it.
- **Two homes:** share the purchase locks and the stopping routine with the other
  household.

## Example Output

```
# Gaming Reset — Mateo, 11, 3 Oct

## Money
Card statement 1 Oct: 14 purchases of V-Bucks over 9 days = $186.85, on
Mum's card saved on the Switch. Mateo knew it was "real money but not that
much." Refund requests: Nintendo support filed 2 Oct (pending); Epic
refund request in the Fortnite account 2 Oct (pending).

## Locks
Card removed from the Switch eShop; Nintendo parental controls: spending
$0 without PIN. Mum's phone: Family Link purchase approval for the tablet.

## Repayment
$186.85 × 50% (knew it was money, didn't know the total) = $93.43.
Allowance $10/week + extra jobs ($5 car wash) → ~6 weeks. Shown to Mateo
on paper. Any refund reduces his share by half the refund.

## Driver
Social: his three closest friends play together on weekday evenings
since they moved to different middle schools. Spending was to match
their skins.

## Switch-off fights
Root cause: told to stop mid-match, letting his squad down. New routine:
"two more matches" at 19:30, 10-min and 2-min warnings, controller on
the shelf at match end, then snack and shower. Same response each time.

## Concern level: habit
Homework done, football 2×/week, sleep fine. No ICD-11 pattern.

## Agreement
School days 18:30–20:00 by matches; Saturday afternoon with friends.
Voice chat friends-only. No purchases; $10 birthday gift cards OK, spent
with a parent.

## Review 2 Nov
Signals: switch-off fights per week (now ~5), homework done before play.
```

## Techniques Used

- **DP-06 Dominant Driver Identification** — social, competence, escape, or regulation decides the plan.
- **RT-09 Root Cause Explanation Pattern** — switch-off fights traced to mid-match stopping and missing transitions.
- **NE-11 Embedded Calculation Formulas** — proportionate repayment, amount × share, scheduled against allowance.
- **DP-05 Stakes-Based Gate Policy** — habit / concern / clinical levels anchored to ICD-11 features.
