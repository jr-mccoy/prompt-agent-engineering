**Purpose:** Help a parent design a durable, low-contact strategy for raising children with a co-parent who is chronically combative, unpredictable, or draining — when ordinary cooperation has repeatedly failed. The output is a practical operating posture (parallel parenting), a set of disengagement scripts, a documentation routine, and a plan for keeping the children out of the crossfire. It organizes the parent's own choices; it does not diagnose the other parent or aim to "beat" them.

**When to use:** Every attempt at cooperative co-parenting collapses into conflict; the other parent reliably escalates, baits, or rewrites events; you are exhausted by the back-and-forth; you need a way to keep functioning across two homes without needing the other parent to change. Works alongside an existing parenting plan or custody order.

**When NOT to use:** There is active abuse, threats, stalking, or a protective-order situation — that is a safety matter, not a "high-conflict" communication problem (see Safety Block and `parenting_coparenting_with_unsafe_or_absent_parent.md`). You need to know whether to modify an order or what your order means → that is legal advice; take it to an attorney or mediator.

## Safety Block

Stop and use a different pathway if:
- The other parent threatens to harm you or the children, stalks you, shows up uninvited, or escalates intimidation → National Domestic Violence Hotline 1-800-799-7233 (US). High-conflict strategy is not a substitute for a safety plan; if there is immediate danger, 911. See `parenting_coparenting_with_unsafe_or_absent_parent.md`.
- You learn a child is being harmed or is unsafe in the other home → Childhelp National Child Abuse Hotline 1-800-422-4453; emergencies 911. Document and route to CPS/your attorney — do not negotiate.
- A child shows signs of mental-health crisis, talks about not wanting to be alive, or is being used by the other parent in ways that are harming them → 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (US); pediatrician same-day.
- The "conflict" includes coercive control, financial control, or monitoring of your movements → that is abuse, not a difficult personality; consult an advocate before changing your approach.

A high-conflict personality and an abusive, dangerous one are not the same thing, and the strategies differ. When in doubt, treat safety as the higher priority and consult an advocate or attorney.

## Core Principles

1. **You cannot make the other parent cooperate — you can only change your side.** Parallel parenting works precisely because it removes the need for the other parent's goodwill. Stop waiting for them to change.
2. **Reduce the surface area for conflict.** Fewer real-time coordination points, fewer open-ended discussions, fewer chances to be baited. Channel everything through structure: the plan, the app, the calendar.
3. **Disengage from bait, not from your kids.** "Gray rock" and non-response apply to provocations — not to genuine logistics or to your children, who need you fully present.
4. **Document for clarity, not for ammunition.** Keep a factual record because memory fades and details matter, not to build a case to humiliate the other parent. The two motives produce very different records.
5. **The children are not a battlefield, a courier, or a witness.** Your job is to make their two homes feel like two safe places, not two sides of a war.
6. **Sustainability beats victory.** This is a years-long relationship. Design something you can maintain calmly, not a posture that requires you to be furious to keep up.

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Distinguish **parallel parenting** (low contact, independent operation) from **cooperative co-parenting** (shared coordination) and recommend the right one for this situation.
- Provide concrete disengagement and gray-rock scripts the parent can use verbatim.
- Build a documentation routine that is factual, dated, and child-focused.
- Center every recommendation on protecting the children from the conflict.
- Account for the parent's own capacity and sustainability.

**Must Not:**
- Badmouth, disparage, or characterize the other parent to or near the kids.
- Use the child as a messenger, spy, witness, or confidant about the conflict.
- Coach the parent to bait, provoke, "win," score points, or build a record designed to disadvantage the other parent.
- Diagnose or label the other parent (e.g., "narcissist," "borderline," formal "parental alienation").
- Recommend self-help workarounds to court orders or safety situations.

## Instructions

### Stage 1 — Validate, Then Reframe
Acknowledge how draining this is — chronic high conflict is genuinely depleting. Then reframe the goal: not to fix or out-argue the other parent, but to build a structure that works regardless of their behavior.

### Stage 2 — Choose the Parenting Mode
Assess whether **cooperative** co-parenting is realistic or whether **parallel** parenting fits better:
- **Cooperative** (frequent coordination, flexibility, shared decisions in the moment) requires two reasonably cooperative adults.
- **Parallel** (each parent runs their own home independently; contact is minimal, written, and logistics-only; almost nothing is negotiated in real time) fits when cooperation reliably fails. Recommend parallel where the inputs show repeated escalation.

### Stage 3 — Reduce the Surface Area
Design the low-contact architecture:
- **One channel** (a parenting app or email), one topic per message, BIFF format (route to `parenting_coparenting_message_composer_biff.md`).
- **The plan does the talking:** specific exchange times/places, clear decision domains, minimal discretion. Vagueness creates fights.
- **Async over real-time:** shared calendar instead of live negotiation; written confirmations instead of phone calls.

### Stage 4 — Gray-Rock and Disengagement Scripts
Provide ready-made responses for common bait:
- **The provocation that needs no answer:** silence, or a single neutral acknowledgment ("Noted, thanks").
- **The repeated argument:** state the position once, then a holding line ("I've shared my position; I'm not able to keep discussing this").
- **The emotional hook:** name nothing about their character; respond only to any genuine logistic buried inside.
Make clear gray-rock means *boring and brief*, not cruel or stonewalling on real logistics.

### Stage 5 — Documentation Discipline
Set up a factual log: date, what happened, who/what/when, kept neutrally. Note its purpose is the parent's own clarity and, if ever needed, a record an attorney can use — never a weapon, and never something the kids see. Pair with a communication log if one exists.

### Stage 6 — Protect the Kids
Specify the firewall: no venting to or in front of the kids; no using them to relay messages or gather information; reassure them that both homes are safe; keep your home calm and predictable. Add a script for when the child reports something inflammatory from the other home (verify directly, briefly, neutrally).

### Stage 7 — Sustainability and Escalation Path
Name the parent's own supports (therapy, a co-parenting coach, friends who aren't the kids), the capacity check, and the escalation path for genuine disputes: direct attempt → app/written → mediation/parenting coordinator → counsel. Court is a last resort, not a first move.

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| Wait for the other parent to "finally get it" | Build a structure that works without their cooperation |
| Confuse a difficult personality with abuse | Run the Safety Block; treat danger as danger |
| Gray-rock genuine logistics and your kids | Stay boring on bait, fully present for real matters |
| Log events to humiliate or "expose" them | Log facts for your own clarity and possible counsel use |
| Vent about the other parent near the kids | Keep the firewall; your home stays calm |
| Send the child to gather intel from the other home | Verify directly, briefly, neutrally |
| Match every provocation with a rebuttal | Single neutral acknowledgment or no response |
| Call them a "narcissist" in writing | Describe behaviors, label nothing |
| Litigate every disagreement | Reserve court for genuine, last-resort disputes |
| Run on fury to keep up | Build something sustainable; protect your own capacity |

## Adaptations

**By age band:**
- **0–3:** Conflict at exchanges is most damaging here; use neutral exchange points or third-party/curbside handoffs; keep handoffs silent and brief.
- **4–8:** Kids start to sense and absorb tension; protect them from loyalty binds; keep both homes' routines stable and never ask them to report on the other home.
- **9–12:** Kids may be actively recruited or messaged through; firmly keep them out of adult logistics; name (gently) that it's not their job to fix things between you.
- **13–18:** Teens often have their own read on each parent; resist pulling them onto your "side"; let them have a relationship with both parents on their own terms while you stay disengaged from the other parent.

**By profile:**
- **High-conflict co-parent (core case):** Maximize specificity in the plan; minimize discretion; default to parallel parenting; one channel only.
- **Child with ADHD/autism:** Routine and medication continuity matter extra; document handoffs of medical info factually so the child isn't caught between systems.
- **Anxious child:** Predictability is the treatment; keep your home's calm non-negotiable; shield the child from app notifications and overheard calls.
- **Unsafe/absent-parent context:** Stop here and use `parenting_coparenting_with_unsafe_or_absent_parent.md`; coordinate documentation and contact with an advocate/attorney.
