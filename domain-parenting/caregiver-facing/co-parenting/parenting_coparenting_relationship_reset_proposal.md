**Purpose:** Help a parent who wants to improve a strained co-parenting relationship draft the reset — a message or conversation that takes honest ownership of their own part, points forward at the kids rather than backward at grievances, and proposes a few concrete new working agreements. The output is the reset message/script plus the working agreements to offer, designed to lower defenses and create a workable path, not to assign blame.

**When to use:** Things have been rough and you genuinely want a better working relationship for the kids' sake; you've both been stuck in a pattern you want to break; a calmer season makes a reset realistic; you're ready to own your part without expecting a full apology in return.

**When NOT to use:** The other parent is abusive, coercive, or dangerous — a "reset" can be exploited; protect yourself and the kids first (see Safety Block and `parenting_coparenting_with_unsafe_or_absent_parent.md`). You want the reset to extract an admission or "win" the relationship — that defeats the purpose. The situation needs a legal change, not a relationship reset → that's for counsel.

---

## Safety Block

Stop and use a different pathway if:
- The other parent is abusive, threatening, or coercively controlling — a "reset" invites manipulation or false hope and can be unsafe → National Domestic Violence Hotline 1-800-799-7233 (US); consult an advocate before reopening the relationship. Parallel parenting, not reconciliation, may be the right posture (`parenting_coparenting_high_conflict_response_strategy.md`).
- A reset is being driven by pressure, guilt, or a desire to reunite romantically rather than to co-parent better → separate the two; this is about the working relationship, not the marriage.
- A child is unsafe in the other home → safety first; Childhelp 1-800-422-4453; emergencies 911; this is not the moment for a relationship reset.
- A child is in mental-health crisis → 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (US); pediatrician same-day.

A reset is appropriate between two non-dangerous adults who can keep agreements. If the other parent can't or won't, structure (parallel parenting) protects the kids better than goodwill.

---

## Core Principles

1. **Own your part first, genuinely.** A reset that opens with the other parent's failures is just another accusation. Lead with a sincere, specific acknowledgment of your own contribution.
2. **Face forward, toward the kids.** The reason to reset is the children's wellbeing, not to resolve who was right. Keep the message pointed at the future.
3. **No score-settling disguised as honesty.** "I'm sorry I yelled, but you…" is not ownership. Drop the "but."
4. **Propose concrete agreements, not vibes.** "Let's be nicer" fails. "One topic per message, 24-hour response on non-urgent items, no discussing adult issues at exchanges" can work.
5. **Invite, don't demand.** Offer the reset; the other parent may not be ready. A non-defensive invitation that asks for nothing in return is most likely to land.
6. **A reset doesn't erase boundaries.** Improving the relationship doesn't mean dropping the structure that protects the kids; the new agreements *are* the structure.

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Lead with sincere, specific ownership of the parent's own part — no "but."
- Point the reset forward, at the kids, not backward at grievances.
- Propose 3–6 concrete working agreements.
- Frame it as an invitation that asks for nothing in return.
- Preserve the structure/boundaries that protect the kids.

**Must Not:**
- Badmouth or characterize the other parent to or near the kids.
- Smuggle blame, scorekeeping, or a demand for apology into the reset.
- Coach the parent to manipulate, "win," or extract concessions.
- Use the child as a messenger or as leverage for the reset.
- Diagnose or label the other parent.

## Instructions

### Stage 1 — Check Readiness and Motive
Confirm this is about a better working relationship for the kids (not reuniting romantically, not guilt, not extracting an admission), and that the other parent is safe to re-engage with. If not, redirect to parallel parenting or safety resources.

### Stage 2 — Find Your Genuine Part
Help the parent name a specific, real piece of their own contribution to the pattern — something true they can own without performance. Pair with `parenting_coparenting_self_audit.md` if needed.

### Stage 3 — Draft the Ownership Opening
Write a sincere opening that owns that part with no "but," no comparison, no implied "your turn." Keep it brief and real.

### Stage 4 — Face Forward
Write the forward-looking core: this is about making the kids' lives calmer; here's what you'd like to try together going forward.

### Stage 5 — Propose Concrete Agreements
Translate the hopes into 3–6 specific, testable agreements (communication channel and cadence, exchange conduct, decision process, keeping adult issues away from the kids). Route any that need separate detail to `parenting_coparenting_shared_decision_framework.md` or `parenting_coparenting_consistency_across_homes.md`.

### Stage 6 — Close as an Invitation
End with a low-pressure invitation that asks for nothing back: "No pressure to respond right away — I just wanted to offer this for the kids." Provide both a conversation version and a message version.

## Output Format

```markdown
# Reset Proposal — for [Children's initials]' sake

## Ownership opening (no "but")
> [Sincere, specific acknowledgment of my own part.]

## Facing forward (the why)
> [This is about making things calmer for the kids; here's what I'd like to try.]

## Working agreements I'm proposing
1. Communication: [channel + cadence, e.g., app, one topic, 24h on non-urgent]
2. Exchanges: [conduct, e.g., brief and friendly, no adult issues in front of kids]
3. Decisions: [process — route to decision framework if needed]
4. Around the kids: [no venting, no messages through them]
5–6. [as relevant]

## Invitation close (asks for nothing back)
> [Low-pressure offer for the kids' benefit.]
```

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| "I'm sorry, but you started it" | Own your part cleanly; drop the "but" |
| Use the reset to extract an apology | Offer ownership freely; expect nothing back |
| Propose vague "let's be nicer" | Offer concrete, testable agreements |
| Reopen with a dangerous/abusive ex | Run the Safety Block; consider parallel parenting |
| Confuse co-parenting reset with reuniting | Keep it about the working relationship |
| Send the proposal through the child | Address the co-parent directly |
| Drop boundaries to seem conciliatory | Keep the protective structure; agreements are structure |
| List old grievances as "context" | Face forward; the past isn't the proposal |
| Demand an immediate yes | Invite low-pressure; let them think |
| Label them or rehash their faults | Speak only to your part and the future |

## Adaptations

**By age band:**
- **0–3:** Resets here focus on handoff calm and reliable logistics; the agreements are concrete (notice, supplies, schedule) — the toddler benefits from steadier exchanges.
- **4–8:** Kids feel the parents' tension acutely; a successful reset visibly lowers the temperature; agreements about not arguing at exchanges matter most.
- **9–12:** Kids notice improvement and relief; involve them only in noticing the calm, never in negotiating the reset.
- **13–18:** Teens have seen the pattern; a genuine reset models repair for them; don't enlist them as witnesses or beneficiaries of "see, I tried."

**By profile:**
- **High-conflict co-parent:** A reset may not stick; keep agreements minimal and structural; be ready to fall back to parallel parenting if it's exploited; pair with `parenting_coparenting_high_conflict_response_strategy.md`.
- **Child with ADHD/autism:** Center agreements on the routine and medical continuity the child needs; a calmer co-parenting relationship directly stabilizes the child.
- **Anxious child:** A visible reset reassures; keep your home calm regardless of whether the reset takes.
- **Unsafe/absent-parent context:** Do not propose a reset to a dangerous parent; see Safety Block and `parenting_coparenting_with_unsafe_or_absent_parent.md`.
