**Purpose:** Give a parent a concrete, rehearsable plan for the moment a conflict ignites in front of the children — at a handoff, a school event, a doctor's office — so they can disengage, shield the child from the conflict, and reset, even when the other parent is provoking them. The output is a short in-the-moment script, a physical-exit plan, and a child-repair routine for afterward.

**When to use:** Exchanges keep turning tense in front of the kids; the other parent baits you where the children can hear; you've reacted in front of the kids before and want a plan so it doesn't happen again; you want to rehearse a calm response before the next handoff.

**When NOT to use:** The flare-ups involve threats, intimidation, or physical danger — that is a safety situation needing an advocate and possibly law enforcement, not a de-escalation script (see Safety Block). You're trying to "win" the exchange or document the other parent's behavior in the moment — that's not the goal here.

---

## Safety Block

Stop and use a different pathway if:
- An exchange involves threats, physical aggression, stalking, or you fear for your safety → National Domestic Violence Hotline 1-800-799-7233 (US); call 911 if there is immediate danger; arrange supervised or third-party/public-place exchanges and consult an advocate/attorney. Do not try to "de-escalate" your way through a threat.
- A child is grabbed, frightened, or used as a shield during a conflict, or witnesses violence → protect the child first; emergencies 911; Childhelp National Child Abuse Hotline 1-800-422-4453; document and route to counsel.
- A child shows trauma symptoms or crisis after exchanges → 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (US); pediatrician/therapist.

A de-escalation script is for verbal flare-ups between two non-dangerous adults. If exchanges aren't safe, the answer is a safety plan and a different exchange structure, not a better comeback.

## Core Principles

1. **The child's experience is the only scoreboard.** In that moment, "winning" is the child not seeing a fight. Being right is irrelevant; protecting the child is everything.
2. **You only control your half.** You can't stop the other parent from provoking; you can refuse to provide the second half of an argument.
3. **Disengage, don't engage.** A flare-up needs two participants. Decline the role: brief, neutral, then exit.
4. **Move the child's body and attention.** Physically and mentally redirect the child out of the conflict zone — into the car, toward a task, with a calm reassurance.
5. **Pre-plan beats improvise.** You will not invent calm under provocation; you rehearse it. Have your line and your exit ready before the handoff.
6. **Repair with the child afterward — without blame.** Name that things felt tense, reassure the child it's not their fault, don't narrate the other parent's villainy.

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Provide a short, rehearsable in-the-moment script (a disengage line + an exit).
- Include a physical plan to move the child out of the conflict.
- Provide a calm, non-blaming child-repair routine for afterward.
- Account for the other parent provoking in front of the child.
- Keep the focus on protecting the child, not winning the moment.

**Must Not:**
- Coach the parent to bait back, "win," or document/perform for an audience in the moment.
- Badmouth or characterize the other parent to or near the child during or after.
- Use the child as a buffer, messenger, or witness in the conflict.
- Diagnose or label the other parent.
- Suggest engaging through a genuine safety threat instead of exiting/calling for help.

## Instructions

### Stage 1 — Validate and Set the Goal
Acknowledge how hard it is to stay calm when provoked in front of your kids. Set the single goal: the child does not experience a fight. Everything else waits.

### Stage 2 — Build the In-the-Moment Script
Provide a 2-part response:
- **Disengage line** (neutral, brief, ends the exchange): e.g., "I'm not going to discuss this now. Let's get [child] settled." / "I hear you. I'll follow up by message."
- **Exit:** move toward the car/door; end the interaction. No last word, no rebuttal.

### Stage 3 — Move the Child
Plan the physical redirect: a calm cue to the child ("Let's go, buddy — grab your bag"), getting them into the car or away from the adults, a reassuring tone. The child's body and attention leave the conflict zone first.

### Stage 4 — When They Provoke in Front of the Child
Provide responses for being baited: name nothing, defend nothing, give the disengage line, exit. The non-response is the skill. If there's a genuine logistic, "I'll message you about that," then leave.

### Stage 5 — Change the Setting if You Can
Where flare-ups recur, propose structural fixes: neutral/public exchange points, curbside handoffs, exchanges through school (child goes from one parent's care to school to the other), or a third party — so the moment has less fuel.

### Stage 6 — Repair With the Child Afterward
Give a non-blaming repair script: acknowledge the tension ("That felt a little tense back there"), reassure ("You didn't do anything wrong, and you're safe"), no villain narrative about the other parent, return to normal. Optionally name a feeling word and move on.

## Output Format

```markdown
# De-Escalation Plan — exchanges/events with [Children's initials]

## My disengage script (rehearse this)
- Line 1 (decline the argument): "[neutral phrase]"
- Line 2 (if a real logistic): "I'll message you about that."
- Exit: [move to car/door; no last word]

## Moving the child
- Cue to child: "[calm redirect]"
- Plan: [get child to car/away first, reassuring tone]

## If they provoke in front of the child
- Response: [name nothing, defend nothing, disengage line, exit]

## Setting changes to reduce flare-ups
- [neutral/public point / curbside / school exchange / third party]

## Repairing with the child afterward
- Acknowledge: "That felt a little tense."
- Reassure: "You didn't do anything wrong; you're safe."
- No villain story; return to normal.
```

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| Get the last word in front of the kids | Disengage line, then exit — no rebuttal |
| Argue the facts at the handoff | "I'll message you about that," then leave |
| Use the child as a buffer or witness | Move the child out first; protect them |
| Narrate the other parent as the villain afterward | "That felt tense; you're safe; not your fault" |
| Stay and "defend yourself" when baited | Non-response is the skill; exit |
| Film or perform for an audience mid-conflict | Protect the child, not your case |
| Keep using a charged exchange spot | Switch to neutral/public/school/curbside |
| Quiz the child about what they heard | Reassure and return to normal |
| Match the other parent's volume | Lower your voice; brevity de-escalates |
| Call them names where the child can hear | Say nothing about their character |

## Adaptations

**By age band:**
- **0–3:** Babies/toddlers absorb tone and tension; keep handoffs silent and gentle; a calm voice and quick exit matter most; consider curbside handoffs.
- **4–8:** Kids personalize conflict ("is it my fault?"); the repair script's reassurance is essential; keep exchanges brief and predictable.
- **9–12:** Kids notice and remember; they may try to mediate — gently relieve them of that job; debrief lightly and non-blamingly.
- **13–18:** Teens may take sides or get angry at the provoking parent; don't recruit them; model the disengagement you want them to learn; let them have feelings without feeding them ammunition.

**By profile:**
- **High-conflict co-parent:** Assume provocation; pre-plan the single line and exit; default to structural fixes (neutral/school exchanges); pair with `parenting_coparenting_high_conflict_response_strategy.md`.
- **Child with ADHD/autism:** Transitions are already hard; a tense exchange compounds dysregulation; keep exchanges quiet, predictable, and short; bring a transition object.
- **Anxious child:** Conflict at exchanges spikes anxiety; over-prepare the calm routine; strong, repeated reassurance afterward.
- **Unsafe/absent-parent context:** If exchanges aren't safe, this script is not enough — see Safety Block and `parenting_coparenting_with_unsafe_or_absent_parent.md`; arrange supervised/third-party exchanges through counsel.
