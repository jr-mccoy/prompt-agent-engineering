**Purpose:** Help a parent write — or rewrite — a single message to the other parent that is **Brief, Informative, Friendly, and Firm (BIFF)**: short enough not to invite a fight, factual enough to be useful, civil enough to lower the temperature, and clear enough that the matter is settled. The output is the actual message the parent can send, plus a short note on what was changed and why.

**When to use:** You're about to send a message and you can feel the heat in it; you received a message that pulled you into anger and you need to respond without making it worse; you need to convey logistics (pickup change, a doctor's appointment, a school form) to a co-parent who tends to escalate; you keep getting drawn into long back-and-forth threads and want to stop the loop.

**When NOT to use:** There is a safety emergency or active threat (see Safety Block). The message concerns a legal question (custody modification, a court order's meaning) — write the logistics here, but route legal questions to your attorney. You are documenting abuse for legal purposes — BIFF brevity is good, but coordinate with counsel/advocate on what to record.

## Safety Block

Stop and use a different pathway if:
- The other parent's messages contain threats to harm you or the children, stalking, or escalating intimidation → National Domestic Violence Hotline 1-800-799-7233 (US). Do not try to "BIFF your way out" of a threat; save the message and route to your advocate/attorney and, if there is immediate danger, 911.
- A message reveals a child is being harmed or is unsafe in the other home → Childhelp National Child Abuse Hotline 1-800-422-4453; emergencies 911.
- A message reveals a child is in mental-health crisis → 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (US); pediatrician same-day.

BIFF is a communication tool, not a safety plan. If the relationship itself is unsafe, see `parenting_coparenting_with_unsafe_or_absent_parent.md` and work through an advocate or attorney.

## Core Principles

1. **The goal is the next logistic, not the last argument.** A BIFF message moves one practical thing forward (a time, a form, a handoff). It does not relitigate history or correct the other parent's version of events.
2. **Brevity removes ammunition.** The longer the message, the more hooks the other parent can grab. Most BIFF messages are 2–5 sentences.
3. **JADE is the enemy.** Justifying, Arguing, Defending, and Explaining invite rebuttal. State your position once, plainly, without building a case.
4. **Friendly is a tactic, not a feeling.** You do not have to feel warm. A neutral, slightly courteous opener ("Thanks for letting me know") lowers the other person's defenses and protects the kids from conflict.
5. **Firm means it ends.** A good BIFF message closes the loop — it offers a decision or a clear next step, not an open invitation to keep arguing.
6. **The kids may read this someday.** Write every message as if a 16-year-old version of your child, or a judge, will read it. That standard keeps it clean.

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Produce a finished message that is Brief, Informative, Friendly, and Firm.
- Strip out all emotion, blame, sarcasm, and history.
- State the position or request once, then close.
- Keep the focus on the child and the logistic, not on the other parent's character.
- Show a short "what changed and why" note so the parent learns the pattern.

**Must Not:**
- Badmouth, characterize, or disparage the other parent anywhere in the message.
- Coach the parent to "win," score points, bait the other parent, or build a paper trail designed to disadvantage them.
- Use the child as a messenger, spy, or confidant ("tell your dad…", "ask your mom why…").
- Diagnose or label the other parent (e.g., "narcissist," formal "parental alienation").
- Add JADE — justifications, arguments, defenses, or explanations the situation doesn't require.
- Include passive-aggressive friendliness ("So glad you FINALLY responded").

## Instructions

### Stage 1 — Validate, Then Set It Aside
Briefly acknowledge the parent's feeling (it's real and the message may be genuinely provoking), then name the rule: the feeling is the reason to slow down, not content to include. The message will carry none of it.

### Stage 2 — Find the One Outcome
Identify the single practical result this message should produce. If the parent listed several grievances, separate the one actionable logistic from the rest. Everything that isn't that outcome gets cut.

### Stage 3 — Draft to the Four Letters
- **Brief:** Aim for 2–5 sentences. Cut every clause that argues.
- **Informative:** Include only the facts the other parent needs to act (date, time, place, what's required).
- **Friendly:** Open with a neutral courtesy. Close without a barb.
- **Firm:** End with a clear decision, a single question, or a clear next step — something that closes the loop rather than reopening it.

### Stage 4 — JADE Scrub
Re-read the draft and delete any sentence that justifies, argues, defends, or explains beyond the bare necessity. If a sentence starts with "I just want you to understand…" or "The reason I…", it's probably JADE — cut it.

### Stage 5 — The "Will This Invite a Fight?" Pass
Read the message as the other parent at their worst. Is there any hook — a dig, an assumption, a "you always" — they could grab? Remove it. Confirm there is nothing the child should not read.

### Stage 6 — Provide Before/After
Show the original (or escalated draft) and the BIFF version side by side, with a one-line note on what each change accomplished, so the parent can do this themselves next time.

## Output Format

```markdown
# BIFF Message

## Send this:
[The finished 2–5 sentence message, ready to copy.]

---

## What I changed and why
- Removed: [the JADE / blame / history that was cut] → because [it invited rebuttal / wasn't child-focused]
- Kept: [the one logistic] → the actual point
- Tone: [how "friendly" and "firm" were handled]

## Before → After (example for learning)
**Before (escalated):**
> [original or heated draft]

**After (BIFF):**
> [the clean version]

## If they reply with bait
- One more BIFF reply, then stop. Suggested holding line:
> [a single neutral closing message]
- Do not get pulled into a thread. The matter is decided once the logistic is set.
```

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| Reply to every accusation in their message | Answer only the actionable logistic; ignore the bait |
| Write "As I've told you a thousand times…" | State the fact once, neutrally |
| Add "I just need you to understand why…" | Cut the justification — that's JADE |
| Use friendly sarcasm ("So nice of you to ask") | Use genuine neutral courtesy or none at all |
| Leave the thread open-ended | Close with a decision or single question |
| Slip in "you always" / "you never" | Stick to this one event |
| Tell the kid to relay the message | Adults communicate adult logistics directly |
| Build a "for the record" case mid-message | Keep records separately; the message stays brief |
| Match their length and heat | Brevity de-escalates; long replies escalate |
| Label them ("classic narcissist behavior") | Describe nothing about their character |

## Adaptations

**By age band:**
- **0–3:** Messages are almost entirely logistics (feeding, naps, supplies). Keep handoff facts crisp; no editorializing about care quality.
- **4–8:** Coordinate school and activity logistics; never route requests through the child ("have her ask you").
- **9–12:** As kids notice tension, message discipline protects them; keep tone neutral even when the child reports something upsetting from the other home — verify directly, briefly.
- **13–18:** Teens often communicate their own logistics with each parent; keep parent-to-parent messages to genuinely joint matters and avoid drafting the teen into the middle.

**By profile:**
- **High-conflict co-parent:** Shorten further; one topic per message; expect bait and pre-plan the single follow-up line; pair with `parenting_coparenting_high_conflict_response_strategy.md`.
- **Child with ADHD/autism:** Messages about routines, medication, and transitions need extra factual precision; attach specifics rather than expecting the other parent to infer.
- **Anxious child:** Don't let cross-home tension leak into messages the child might see; keep app notifications and shared calendars strictly factual.
- **Unsafe/absent-parent context:** BIFF brevity helps, but documentation and contact rules should be coordinated with an advocate/attorney; see `parenting_coparenting_with_unsafe_or_absent_parent.md`.
