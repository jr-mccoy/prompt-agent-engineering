# Supporting a Struggling Reader at Home

**Objective:** Produce a home reading plan for one child that is short enough to keep up,
targeted at the part of reading that is actually breaking down, aligned with what the
school is teaching, and protective of the child's confidence — plus a clear threshold
for when the caregiver should push for a formal evaluation.

**When to Use:**
- A child aged 5–12 is reading below grade level, avoids books, or guesses at words.
- The school has flagged reading on a screener, or placed the child in a reading
  intervention group, and the caregiver asks "what should we do at home?"
- The child has been diagnosed with dyslexia (or a specific learning disability in
  reading) and the family wants home practice that fits the school's program.
- Bedtime reading has turned into a nightly fight.
- **Not this prompt if** you are the **teacher or interventionist** designing
  instruction — use `domain-education-teaching/instructor/student-support/teaching_dyslexia_structured_literacy_plan.md`.
  If you need to prepare for a school meeting or request an evaluation, use
  `parenting_learning_concern_school_meeting.md`; for the written request itself, use
  `domain-written-advocacy/institutions-and-records/advocacy_school_written_request.md`.
  Homework fights across subjects → `parenting_homework_battles_plan_5_8.md`.
  This prompt is the caregiver's home practice.

## Safety Block

- **Hearing or vision not checked in the last year**, frequent ear infections, or speech
  that is hard to understand → pediatrician for hearing and vision screening, and
  speech-language evaluation if speech or language is a concern.
- **Loss of reading or speaking skills the child once had** → pediatrician promptly;
  regression is not a reading-instruction problem.
- **Child calls themselves stupid daily, refuses school, or says they want to die** →
  pause practice; see `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/mental-health-behavior/` and
  the pediatrician. Self-worth outranks reading minutes.
- **Paid programs promising to cure dyslexia** through vision therapy, coloured overlays,
  or brain-training exercises → US pediatric and ophthalmology bodies state these are
  not treatments for dyslexia; spend the money on evidence-based tutoring instead.

**This plan supports reading at home; it does not diagnose dyslexia** and does not
replace structured literacy instruction from a trained teacher.

## Inputs / Context

1. **Child:** age, grade, language(s) at home, speech history, attention, mood about
   reading, family history of reading difficulty.
2. **School information:** screener or benchmark scores (e.g., DIBELS, Acadience, i-Ready),
   intervention group and program name, any IEP/504 or diagnosis.
3. **What reading looks like now:** a 1-minute sample — does the child sound out,
   guess from pictures or first letters, read slowly but accurately, or read fluently
   but not understand?
4. **Listening comprehension:** can they follow and retell a story read to them?
5. **Time available** on weekdays and who will do it.
6. **What has been tried** (levelled readers, apps, tutoring, reading logs).

## Method

1. **Locate the breakdown (DS-01, RT-02).** The Simple View of Reading says reading
   comprehension = decoding × language comprehension; either one near zero sinks the
   product. Sort the child:
   - **Decoding weak, listening strong** (classic dyslexia pattern): slow, inaccurate
     word reading; understands stories read aloud well.
   - **Fluency weak:** accurate but laboured; meaning lost to effort.
   - **Language comprehension weak:** reads words fine, cannot say what the passage
     meant; also struggles with stories read aloud → discuss speech-language input.
   - **Both weak** → evaluation sooner.
2. **Run two channels, kept separate.** *Practice channel* (10–15 minutes, child reads,
   at the child's decoding level). *Enjoyment channel* (adult reads aloud or audiobook at
   the child's thinking level, no demand to read). Children with dyslexia who keep the
   enjoyment channel keep building vocabulary and knowledge.
3. **Practice channel content, matched to the breakdown.**
   - Decoding: decodable books that use only the letter patterns the child has been
     taught (ask the school for its scope and sequence); quick sound–letter review;
     "say each sound, then blend"; never "guess from the picture."
   - Fluency: repeated reading — the same short passage 3 times across the week, timing
     words correct per minute if the child enjoys it; paired reading (adult and child
     read together, child taps to read solo).
   - Comprehension: before/during/after questions, retell in three parts, explain new
     words.
4. **Correct gently and specifically.** Wait 3–5 seconds; point to the tricky part;
   prompt "What sound does *sh* make?"; model and reread the sentence. Praise effort and
   strategy ("You blended that whole word").
5. **Align with school.** Ask the teacher what pattern the child is working on and use
   the same language and cues. Home practice should consolidate, not introduce, new
   patterns.
6. **Protect access and dignity.** Audiobooks and text-to-speech for content the child
   cannot yet decode; these do not "stop them learning to read." Tell the child the
   truth: "Your brain learns reading a different way; we're going to teach it that way."
7. **Track for six weeks (DP-11).** Note accuracy on a decodable passage and, for grade 1+,
   words correct per minute every two weeks; keep the child's mood rating too.
8. **Evaluation gate (DP-05).** If the child is in intervention and the gap is not
   closing over 6–8 weeks, or two or more warning signs below are present, ask the school
   for data and a meeting, and request a full evaluation in writing.

## Output Format

```
# Home Reading Plan — [child, age, grade]   Start: [date]

## What's breaking down (and the evidence)
Decoding: [..]   Fluency: [..]   Language comprehension: [..]
## Practice channel (10–15 min, days/week, who)
Warm-up | main activity | reread | praise line
## Enjoyment channel (read-aloud / audiobook list)
## How we correct (scripts)
## Alignment with school (pattern of the week, cue words, program)
## Six-week tracker
| Date | Passage | Accuracy % | WCPM | Mood (1–5) |
## Evaluation threshold and next step
```

## Verification

- [ ] Breakdown is classified with evidence from a sample and listening comprehension.
- [ ] Practice is 10–15 minutes and pitched at the decoding level, not the grade level.
- [ ] Enjoyment channel is separate and demand-free.
- [ ] No picture-guessing or "skip it" cues in the correction scripts.
- [ ] Home practice uses the school's patterns and language.
- [ ] Evaluation threshold and route are explicit.

## False-Positive Prevention

| Misfire | What it looks like | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Waiting for a "late bloomer" | "Boys read later" while the gap widens | Early, explicit help works best; act on warning signs. |
| Reading reversals as dyslexia | 6-year-old writes b/d backwards | Reversals are common to about age 7; weak sound–letter links matter more. |
| Calling a language problem dyslexia | Decodes fine, understands little | That is a comprehension profile; consider speech-language input. |
| Levelled books for a weak decoder | Child memorises patterns and guesses | Use decodables for practice; levelled books for shared reading. |
| More minutes as the fix | 45-minute nightly drills, tears | Short daily sessions beat long weekly ones; protect motivation. |
| Treating audiobooks as cheating | Banning audio to "force" reading | Listening builds the vocabulary that comprehension needs. |

**Warning signs (ask for an evaluation):** family history of dyslexia; trouble with
rhyming or hearing the sounds in words at 5; still not reading simple three-letter words
by the middle of grade 1; very slow, effortful reading by grade 2–3 despite
intervention; spelling far worse than other work; strong verbal ability that does not
match reading.

## Adaptations

- **Multilingual child:** reading difficulty shows up in all languages; do not wait for
  English fluency before asking about it. Read aloud in the strongest home language.
- **ADHD:** 5-minute bursts, movement between activities, tracker visible.
- **Older child (9–12):** guard dignity — age-appropriate decodables (hi-lo books),
  audiobooks for class novels, keyboard and speech-to-text for writing.
- **Two homes:** share one page with the pattern of the week and the correction script.
- **Caregiver with own reading difficulty:** audiobooks and the school's practice
  materials carry more of the load; your encouragement still counts.

## Example Output

```
# Home Reading Plan — Theo, 8, grade 3   Start: 7 Oct

## What's breaking down
Decoding: weak — 1-minute sample, 41 words, 78% accurate; guesses from
first letter ("house" for "horse"). Fluency: slow because of decoding.
Language comprehension: strong — retold a grade-4 chapter read aloud
with details. Pattern fits a decoding problem. School screener (Sept):
well below benchmark; in a reading group 4x/week. Dad had trouble
reading at school.

## Practice channel — Mon–Thu, 15 min, Mum
3 min: sound cards for this week's pattern (vowel teams ai/ay).
8 min: decodable book from the school's series at the same pattern.
4 min: reread yesterday's page; Theo tracks with a finger.
Praise: "You checked every sound in that word."

## Enjoyment channel
Dad reads "The Wild Robot" aloud at bedtime; audiobook of class novel
on the school run.

## How we correct
Wait 4 seconds. Point under the vowel team. "What do these two letters
say together?" Theo blends, rereads the sentence. Never "look at the
picture."

## Alignment
Teacher email 4 Oct: group uses an Orton-Gillingham-based program; this
week ai/ay. We use the same "vowel team" term and tap-out cue.

## Tracker
| 7 Oct  | decodable p.4  | 78% | 41 | 2 |
| 21 Oct | decodable p.9  | 86% | 48 | 3 |

## Evaluation threshold
Theo has three warning signs (family history; below benchmark in grade
3 despite group; decoding far below listening). → Ask the school on
22 Oct for his intervention progress data and request a full
evaluation in writing; prepare with
parenting_learning_concern_school_meeting.md.
```

## Techniques Used

- **DS-01 Framework Application** — the Simple View of Reading sorts the child's difficulty before any activity is chosen.
- **RT-02 Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework** — decoding, fluency, and language comprehension assessed separately with evidence.
- **DP-11 Safe Experiment Design** — a six-week tracked routine with simple measures and mood.
- **DP-05 Stakes-Based Gate Policy** — a non-closing gap or multiple warning signs trigger an evaluation request.
