# Academics & Skills Prompts (Caregiver-Facing)

Helping a child who is struggling with schoolwork — nightly homework fights, learning to read, and the school meeting where a caregiver asks what is going on and whether the child should be evaluated. Each prompt gives the caregiver a short home routine or a meeting plan, and a clear threshold for when a struggle at home is really a learning difficulty the school should assess.

## Scope

**In scope:**
- Homework battles with 5–8-year-olds: load check against the teacher's intended time, dominant driver, time cap and stop-and-note rule, real choices, learning-issue warning signs.
- Home reading support for a struggling reader (5–12), including a child in school intervention or with a dyslexia diagnosis: decoding vs. fluency vs. comprehension, decodable practice plus a separate read-aloud channel.
- Preparing for and following up a school meeting about learning concerns, including asking for an evaluation and the same-day written summary.

## Safety notes

None of these prompts diagnoses a learning disability; they organize evidence and route to qualified evaluators. Check hearing and vision before assuming a learning or behaviour problem; loss of skills the child once had goes to the pediatrician promptly. Avoid paid "cures" for dyslexia (vision therapy, coloured overlays, brain-training) — US pediatric and ophthalmology bodies state they are not treatments. Any adult losing control during homework, or a child saying they want to die → stop and use `../mental-health-behavior/parenting_child_suicide_talk_response_6_12.md` (988 in the US). Education rights and timelines differ by country, state, and district; legal-literacy points are flagged *verify locally* and free help is available from parent advocacy services (in the US, each state's Parent Training and Information Center).
