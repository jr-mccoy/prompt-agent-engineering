# School Meeting About Learning Concerns

**Objective:** Walk into a school meeting about a child's learning with organized evidence,
a clear ask, and prepared answers to the usual delays — and walk out with a written record
of what was agreed, who will do it, and by when.

**When to Use:**
- A child is behind in reading, writing, spelling, or math, and the caregiver has
  requested (or been offered) a meeting with the teacher, student support team, or
  intervention team.
- The child has been in a school intervention for weeks without visible progress, or the
  caregiver suspects dyslexia, dyscalculia, or dysgraphia and wants an evaluation.
- A previous meeting ended with "let's wait and see" and nothing happened.
- **Not this prompt if** the child's **grades are fine** but behaviour, regulation, or
  social friction is the problem — use
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/ages-4-8/parenting_school_accommodation_conversation_prep.md`.
  If you only need to **write the request letter**, use
  `domain-written-advocacy/institutions-and-records/advocacy_school_written_request.md`.
  For the home reading routine, use `parenting_struggling_reader_home_support.md`.
  This prompt covers the meeting itself and its follow-up.

## Safety Block

- **The child is being bullied, harmed, or says they want to die** → that is the first
  agenda item or a separate urgent contact today, not a learning meeting; see
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/mental-health-behavior/` and the school's
  safeguarding lead.
- **Formal dispute, exclusion, or legal deadline** → contact your area's free parent
  advocacy service (in the US, every state has a federally funded Parent Training and
  Information Center) or an education attorney.
- **Loss of skills, seizures, or sudden change in speech or behaviour** → pediatrician
  first; these are medical questions.

**Legal-literacy layer — verify locally.** Rights, timelines, and names of processes differ
by country, state, and district and change over time. US example: under IDEA, a parent may
request an evaluation at any time; once the parent gives written consent, the evaluation
must be completed within 60 days or the state's own timeline; if the school refuses, it must
give Prior Written Notice explaining why; and federal guidance (OSEP Memo 11-07) says an
RTI/MTSS process cannot be used to delay or deny an evaluation. England example: a parent can
ask the local authority directly for an EHC needs assessment. **This prompt does not tell you
whether your child qualifies or what the school must provide.**

## Inputs / Context

1. **Child:** age, grade, subjects of concern, strengths, history (speech delay, ear
   infections, family history of learning difficulty), any diagnosis.
2. **School data you have:** report cards, screener/benchmark scores, intervention
   group and how long, progress-monitoring graphs if shared.
3. **Home evidence:** dated work samples, homework time logs, reading samples, the
   child's own words about school.
4. **Meeting details:** who invited whom, who will attend, length, stated purpose.
5. **What you want:** more or different intervention with a review date, a full
   evaluation, or both.
6. **Constraints:** language access (interpreter), work schedule, co-parent involvement.

## Method

1. **Build a one-page evidence pack (RT-05).** Three to five dated observations, each
   tied to evidence: "Homework reading log took 45–60 minutes nightly for 4 weeks
   (log attached; teacher's estimate 15 minutes)." Add two work samples and any scores.
   Lead with one line of strengths.
2. **Request data before the meeting.** Ask in writing for screener scores, the
   intervention name, frequency, group size, start date, and progress-monitoring data.
   No data is itself information.
3. **Decide the ask in advance.**
   - **Ask A — intervention plan:** named intervention, minutes per week, how progress is
     measured, review meeting date within 6–8 weeks, and what result would trigger an
     evaluation.
   - **Ask B — evaluation:** a full evaluation covering all areas of suspected need
     (cognitive, academic, language, and attention if relevant), with consent forms sent
     home.
   - Ask B is reasonable when intervention has run 6–8+ weeks without closing the gap,
     several warning signs are present, or the gap is large.
4. **Prepare for the common responses (NE-23).**
   - "Let's wait and see / she's young" → "What would we see in six weeks that tells us
     more? Can we set that date and criterion now?"
   - "He needs to finish RTI first" → "I understand intervention continues. I am also
     requesting an evaluation, which I understand can run in parallel. Please respond
     in writing."
   - "She's not far enough behind" → "Can you show me the data and the benchmark?"
   - "We don't diagnose dyslexia" → "I'm asking you to evaluate her reading, including
     phonological processing and decoding, and to describe her needs."
5. **Run the meeting.** Bring a second adult if possible; take notes; ask each person to
   state their role. Ask: what does the data show; what is being done; how will we know
   it is working; what is the next step and date.
6. **Listen for decisions, not reassurance (DS-40).** At the end, read back: "So the
   actions are … by …; is that right?"
7. **Send the written summary the same day (NE-20).** Short, neutral, dated, addressed
   to the meeting chair: what was discussed, actions with owners and dates, your
   evaluation request restated if made. It is the record if memories differ.
8. **Calendar the follow-up.** Review date, consent-form deadline, and a check-in if
   nothing arrives within 10 school days.
9. **Hold uncertainty honestly (QA-04).** The meeting is to get the child assessed and
   supported, not to prove a diagnosis.

## Output Format

```
# Learning-Concern Meeting Pack — [child, grade]   Meeting: [date, attendees]

## Strengths (one line)
## Concerns with evidence (3–5 dated items)
## Data requested before the meeting (and received?)
## Our ask: [A intervention plan | B evaluation | both]
## Questions to ask
## If they say… / we respond…
## Read-back of actions at the end
## Same-day follow-up email (draft)
## Calendar: review date, consent deadline, 10-school-day check
```

## Verification

- [ ] Every concern is dated and tied to evidence.
- [ ] Data request precedes the meeting.
- [ ] The ask is decided in advance with a review date or evaluation request.
- [ ] At least three common delaying responses have prepared answers.
- [ ] The follow-up email lists owners and dates and restates any request.
- [ ] Legal-literacy points are marked "verify locally."

## False-Positive Prevention

| Misfire | What it looks like | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Arriving with a diagnosis | "She has dyslexia and needs X" | Describe evidence; ask for evaluation of the need. |
| Accepting vague reassurance | "We'll keep an eye on it" | Ask for named action, measure, and date. |
| Treating the meeting as adversarial | Opening with legal threats | Start collaborative; escalate only if the record shows delay. |
| Verbal-only agreements | No written trace | Same-day summary email every time. |
| Over-reading one bad test | Panic over one low score | Look for a pattern across sources and time. |
| Assuming the law is the same everywhere | Quoting a US timeline in another country | Verify with the local authority or advocacy service. |

## Adaptations

- **Multilingual family:** request an interpreter; ask how the school separates
  language learning from a learning difficulty.
- **Two homes:** agree in advance who speaks for which point.
- **Private evaluation already done:** share it, ask the school to consider it in
  writing; it does not automatically replace the school's own process.
- **Older child attends:** prepare them to say what is hard and what helps.

## Example Output

```
# Learning-Concern Meeting Pack — Priya, grade 2
# Meeting 14 Nov: class teacher, reading specialist, assistant principal

## Strengths: curious, strong vocabulary, loves science, kind.

## Concerns with evidence
1. Fall screener (Sept): well below benchmark in nonsense-word fluency.
2. Reading group 4x/week since 2 Oct; teacher says "slow progress."
3. Home reading log 45–60 min nightly for 4 weeks (log attached;
   teacher estimate 15 min).
4. Spelling test samples (3 attached): 2/10, 3/10, 2/10.
5. Mother and older brother have dyslexia diagnoses.

## Data requested 7 Nov: screener scores, intervention name, group size,
progress-monitoring graph. Received 12 Nov: screener only.

## Our ask: B — full evaluation, plus intervention continues with data
shared every 4 weeks.

## Questions
What does her progress-monitoring show since October? What is her
growth compared with the goal line? Who will evaluate, and what areas?

## If they say… / we respond…
"Let's give the group until spring." → "Intervention can continue. I am
also requesting an evaluation today and will put it in writing."
"She's bright, she'll catch up." → "Her verbal strength and her reading
gap together are exactly why I'm asking."

## Read-back
Graph by 21 Nov (specialist); consent form by 18 Nov (AP).

## Follow-up email (sent 14 Nov, 4:30 pm)
"Thank you for meeting today. To confirm: (1) I requested a full
evaluation of Priya's reading and spelling, including phonological
processing; (2) Ms. Ruiz will send the consent form by 18 Nov;
(3) Mr. Lee will share her progress-monitoring graph by 21 Nov;
(4) the reading group continues 4x/week. Please let me know if I have
recorded anything incorrectly."

## Calendar
18 Nov consent due; 2 Dec check-in if not received; school to confirm
the evaluation timeline in writing.
```

## Techniques Used

- **RT-05 Evidence-Based Reasoning** — every concern is dated and tied to a sample, log, or score.
- **NE-23 Objection Pre-emption** — prepared answers to "wait and see," "finish RTI first," and "not far enough behind."
- **DS-40 Follow-Up Action Extraction** — actions with owners and dates are read back and written up.
- **NE-20 Third-Party Handoff Package** — the same-day summary email stands on its own as the record.
- **QA-04 Uncertainty Acknowledgment** — the goal is assessment and support, not proving a diagnosis; legal points flagged to verify.
