**Purpose:** Help a caregiver decide when to seek professional help, what kind to seek, and how to navigate the help-seeking process — including when the first provider was dismissive, when self-referral is needed, and when the clinical-routing decision is age-specific.

**When to use:** A specific concern surfaced and caregiver isn't sure if it warrants professional help; pediatrician was dismissive about a real concern; deciding which kind of clinician; multiple concerns coexist and routing is unclear; cross-age sibling situation; insurance / cost barriers requiring strategy.

**When NOT to use:** Acute crisis (suicidality, abuse, medical emergency, psychosis) → emergency response (988 in US, ED, mobile crisis); return to this prompt for follow-on care planning.

---

## Clinical-Signal Triggers

These are always same-day or urgent:

- Suicidal ideation with plan, intent, or means → 988 / mobile crisis / ED.
- Self-harm with severity → urgent care / ED / mental-health response.
- Suspected abuse (physical, sexual, emotional, neglect) → CPS / law enforcement; the disclosure is the priority.
- First-episode psychosis indicators (hallucinations, paranoid thinking, marked functional decline) → urgent psychiatric eval; coordinated specialty care programs.
- Eating-disorder + vital instability → adolescent medicine ED.
- Substance overdose → 911; naloxone if opioid suspected.
- Acute medical (severe pain, syncope, bleeding, head injury, severe respiratory, severe dehydration) → ED.
- Postpartum psychosis (in birthing parent) → ED; this is a psychiatric emergency.

---

## Core Principles

1. **Earlier intervention generally beats later.** Across most developmental, mental-health, and learning concerns, earlier evaluation and treatment predict better outcomes. Watchful waiting is sometimes appropriate; prolonged watchful waiting often costs.
2. **Caregiver concern is data.** "I'm worried" is a clinical signal even when the caregiver can't articulate why. Caregivers know their kids in ways providers don't.
3. **The first dismissive provider is not the final word.** Pediatricians and other primary care providers are not specialists in every developmental and mental-health area. A "let's wait and see" can be appropriate or it can miss something. Seek second opinions.
4. **Routing matters as much as recognition.** The right specialist makes a major difference. Generic therapy for OCD or eating disorder is often not enough; specific evidence-based modalities matter.
5. **Self-referral is often available.** In many systems, you can directly refer to a therapist, OT, SLP, audiologist, or developmental psychologist without a pediatrician gate.
6. **Multiple concerns often co-occur.** ADHD + anxiety, autism + GI, learning disability + depression. Coordinated care matters.

## Instructions

### Stage 1 — Concern-to-Specialist Matching

**Speech / language delay or disorder:**
- Speech-Language Pathologist (SLP).
- Self-referral often available.
- Early Intervention (US, 0–3) and school-based services (3+) free in many cases.
- For autism / pragmatic-language: ND-affirming SLP.

**Motor delay / coordination:**
- Pediatric Physical Therapist (PT) for gross motor.
- Occupational Therapist (OT) for fine motor, sensory processing, daily-living.
- Self-referral often available.
- Early Intervention (0–3) and school-based services.

**Sensory processing / regulation:**
- OT trained in sensory integration.
- ND-affirming.

**Feeding (beyond infant):**
- Pediatric feeding therapist (often SLP, OT, or RD trained in feeding).
- For ARFID: specialized.

**Hearing concerns:**
- Pediatric audiologist.
- Self-referral often available.
- Repeat hearing screens at any age if concern.

**Vision concerns:**
- Pediatric ophthalmologist or developmental optometrist.

**Behavior / mental-health (general):**
- Therapist (LMHC, LCSW, LMFT, psychologist).
- Specific to presentation:
  - Depression / anxiety: CBT-trained.
  - OCD: ERP-trained.
  - PTSD: TF-CBT or EMDR.
  - Adolescent NSSI / emotion dysregulation: DBT-A trained.
  - Eating disorder: FBT (anorexia-spectrum) or CBT-E (bulimia / BED).
  - Autism: ND-affirming clinician.
  - ADHD: behavioral parent training, EF coaching.
  - Substance: adolescent SUD specialist; MET/CBT.
  - Trauma: trauma-informed clinician.
- Self-referral usually available.

**Medication evaluation:**
- Pediatric / adolescent psychiatrist.
- Pediatrician sometimes initiates for mild-moderate.
- Limited access; consider integrated primary-care psychiatry, telehealth, university-based clinics.

**Developmental evaluation (autism, global developmental concerns):**
- Developmental-behavioral pediatrician.
- Developmental psychologist.
- Multidisciplinary autism evaluation team (AAP, neurologist, OT, SLP, psychologist).
- Long waits common; start the referral early.

**ADHD evaluation:**
- Pediatrician (often initiates).
- Pediatric psychologist or developmental-behavioral pediatrician for complex.
- Adolescent psychiatry for medication consideration.

**Learning disability evaluation:**
- School psychologist (free school evaluation under IDEA in US).
- Independent psychoeducational evaluation (private, often more thorough).
- Neuropsychologist for complex.

**Neurology:**
- Headaches with neurological signs, seizure-like events, regression, motor concerns, tics escalating, suspected genetic syndrome, suspected mitochondrial.
- Pediatric neurologist via referral or self-refer in some systems.

**Adolescent medicine:**
- Eating disorders, complex puberty, transitions of care, somatic-heavy presentations, sexual / reproductive health, gender-affirming care.

**Allergy / immunology:**
- Severe / persistent allergies, suspected immunodeficiency.

**Endocrinology:**
- Precocious / delayed puberty, growth concerns, diabetes, thyroid.

**Gastroenterology:**
- Severe / persistent GI, eosinophilic esophagitis, IBD signs.

**Cardiology:**
- Murmur with concern, chest pain on exertion, syncope, family history.

### Stage 2 — When the First Provider Is Dismissive

Pattern: caregiver brings concern; provider says "let's wait and see" / "all kids do that" / "you're being anxious."

When this is appropriate:
- Vague concern, no specific signs, kid functioning well.
- Specific behavior within developmental window.
- Provider has done a real assessment, not a one-minute response.

When this is dismissal:
- Specific signs across domains.
- Concern persists over months.
- Provider didn't ask follow-up questions.
- Caregiver intuition ("something is off") sustained.
- Family / friends / teachers also noticing.

How to push past:

```
"I hear you saying [their position]. I want to be specific:
[list specific signs, time course, function impact].

I'd like:
- A referral to [specialist] for evaluation.
- Or, if you don't think a referral is needed, can you put in
  the chart what specific signs would change your recommendation?

If we're going to wait and see, I'd like a follow-up window —
say, [3 months] — and what we should be looking for."
```

If still dismissed:
- Second opinion (different pediatrician, often within same practice).
- Direct self-referral to the specialist where allowed.
- Insurance company may require referral for some specialists; navigate that.
- Independent evaluation (out-of-pocket if needed).

### Stage 3 — Self-Referral Pathways

In most US systems, you can directly contact:

- **Therapist:** find via Psychology Today, Open Path Collective, insurance directory, school recommendations, community mental-health center.
- **SLP / OT / PT:** private practices accept self-referral; some require pediatrician script for insurance.
- **Audiologist:** self-referral typical.
- **Educational evaluator (private psychoeducational):** self-pay; some insurance.
- **Developmental psychologist:** often self-referral for evaluation.
- **Adolescent SUD assessment:** self-referral typical.
- **Crisis services (988, mobile crisis):** self-referral.
- **Pediatric psychiatry:** harder; often referral required, long waits.

For school-based services (US):

- **Early Intervention (birth–3):** parent referral; free; coordinator assigned.
- **IDEA Part B (3–21):** request evaluation in writing to school district; specific timeline triggers.
- **504 plan (chronic medical, mental-health, learning):** request via school administration; medical documentation often required.

### Stage 4 — Insurance and Cost Strategy

Honest about barriers:

**With insurance:**
- Use insurance directory but verify directly with provider (directories are often outdated).
- Ask about coverage before first visit.
- In-network vs. out-of-network differences large.
- Some specialists don't take any insurance; out-of-pocket only.
- Mental-health parity laws exist; your insurance is required to cover mental-health at parity; sometimes have to push.

**Without or with limited insurance:**
- Community mental-health centers.
- Federally Qualified Health Centers (FQHC) — sliding scale.
- University training clinics — graduate trainees, supervised, low-cost.
- Open Path Collective — therapists offering reduced-cost slots.
- Project HEAL (eating disorders).
- NAMI (mental health) — free family support.
- School-based services where available.
- Telehealth — can expand access.

**For evaluations:**
- School evaluations (IDEA) — free.
- Pediatric residency / training programs — sometimes free or low-cost.
- Insurance-covered evals through pediatrician referral.

### Stage 5 — Coordinating Multiple Concerns

When concerns coexist (e.g., ADHD + anxiety + LD; autism + GI + sleep):

- Identify primary clinician who coordinates.
- Pediatrician often holds the medical-coordination role.
- Care manager via insurance sometimes available.
- Caregiver may end up coordinating; build a one-page "current status" document.

Avoid:
- Specialist silos where each handles only their piece.
- Conflicting recommendations without conversation.
- Repeat questioning of kid across providers (trauma-informed coordination).

### Stage 6 — When to Push for Earlier Evaluation

Do not wait when:

- Sustained signs across domains for >6–12 weeks (depending on age).
- Kid showing functional impairment.
- Family functioning significantly affected.
- Provider waitlist is months — start now.
- Pediatrician suggests waiting but you're seeing what they're not seeing.
- New regression, especially in young child.

Time matters in:
- Autism evaluation (earlier intervention = better outcomes).
- Eating disorders.
- First-episode psychosis.
- Substance escalation.
- LD evaluation (earlier accommodation = less academic damage).
- Mental-health treatment generally.

### Stage 7 — Geographic Access (Rural / Underserved)

For rural / underserved areas:

- Telehealth has dramatically expanded access; many specialists offer.
- Regional university medical centers may have outreach clinics.
- Project ECHO and similar programs train local pediatricians in specialty care.
- State developmental disability councils, autism centers.
- Travel for evaluation; local follow-up.

### Stage 8 — When to Stop Looking for Professional Help

If you've engaged appropriate specialists and:

- Treatment is in place and working.
- Watchful waiting is now appropriate.
- Kid is functioning.
- Family is supported.

It's appropriate to stop adding providers and let the work happen.

If treatment isn't working:

- Re-evaluate fit (clinician fit, modality fit).
- Consider second opinion.
- Consider intensifying (IOP, PHP, residential where appropriate).
- Consider whether driver was correctly identified.

### Stage 9 — Caregiver-Side Support

The work of getting a kid help is exhausting. Caregivers need:

- Their own support (therapist, parent group, friend).
- NAMI Family-to-Family for mental-health.
- F.E.A.S.T. for eating disorders.
- Autism-parent groups (online and local).
- Other condition-specific groups.
- Specialist referral can include "is there a parent-coaching component?"

### Stage 10 — Adaptations Across Profiles

- **Newborn / infant:** pediatrician-centered; Early Intervention key entry point.
- **Toddler / preschool:** Early Intervention until 3; school district 3+; developmental pediatrics for autism / global concerns.
- **School-age:** school evaluation as one path; community providers as another.
- **Tween:** mental-health evaluation often emerging; school counselor as bridge.
- **Teen:** confidential adolescent-medicine; teen-friendly providers; teen voice in choosing.
- **ND profile:** ND-affirming providers; specific expertise required.
- **LGBTQ+:** affirming providers; specific health concerns; gender-affirming care via adolescent medicine and pediatric endocrinology.
- **Trauma history:** trauma-informed providers; coordination of care matters.
- **Chronic illness:** medical home; care coordination.
- **First-gen / immigrant:** language-matched providers where possible; cultural broker; navigate system together.
- **Foster / kinship / adopted:** trauma-informed; coordinate with caseworker; adoption-competent if relevant.
- **Custody / split household:** consent and information-sharing complications; address upfront.

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| Default to "see your pediatrician" universally | Specific specialist matters |
| Treat watchful waiting as default for clear signals | Earlier often better |
| Treat dismissive provider as final word | Push-back, second opinion, self-refer |
| Skip self-referral pathways | Often available |
| Pretend cost isn't a barrier | Honest strategy |
| Treat single-modality as enough for complex | Coordination matters |
| Send kid to generic therapist for OCD / ED | Specific evidence-based modality required |
| Route teen to provider without teen voice | Teen-fit matters for engagement |
| Skip ND-affirming clinician for ND kid | Affirming care is treatment-quality issue |
| Treat school evaluation as second-best | Free, often thorough; one legitimate path |
| Skip caregiver-side support | Sustainable only with caregiver support |
| Coordinate via specialist silos | Primary clinician role matters |
| Default to inpatient when outpatient fits | Match level of care to acuity |
| Wait for pediatrician script when self-referral allowed | Time-sensitive |
| Forget telehealth for rural / underserved | Major expansion of access |
| Push past kid's resistance for non-acute eval | Engagement matters; pace |
