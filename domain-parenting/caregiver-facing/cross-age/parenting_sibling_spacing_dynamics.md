**Purpose:** Help a caregiver understand the developmental dynamics specific to their sibling configuration, conduct functional analysis of recurring conflicts, and design interventions that match the actual driver rather than treating "sibling fighting" as a single problem.

**When to use:** Persistent sibling conflict; new sibling expected or arrived; specific age-gap dynamic causing friction; blended-family sibling integration; older sibling parentification concern; younger sibling in big sibling's shadow; twin / multiple dynamics; half-sibling navigation.

**When NOT to use:** Acute physical safety concern (one sibling injuring another with intent and severity) — separate, then assess for intervention beyond this prompt. Sibling sexual abuse — specialized clinical and legal pathway.

---

## Clinical-Signal Triggers

- Pattern of one sibling intentionally injuring another with severity → mental-health evaluation of perpetrating sibling.
- Sibling sexual abuse / inappropriate sexualized behavior → specialized clinical evaluation; possible mandatory reporting; safety planning to keep the targeted sibling safe.
- Targeted sibling showing trauma signs → mental-health evaluation.
- One sibling consistently parentified to the point of role-loss → therapeutic support.

---

## Core Principles

1. **Sibling dynamics are driven by developmental stage, not just personality.** What looks like "she's just mean to her brother" is often "she's 5 and he's 3 and the gap has specific predictable friction."
2. **Each sibling has their own relationship with the family.** They are not the same kid in the same house. Birth order, age at parental life events, parental capacity at their stage — all differ.
3. **Conflict has function.** Attention, status, autonomy, fairness, identity — sibling conflict is rarely random.
4. **Comparison kills sibling relationship.** Praising one for what the other lacks; using one as the standard.
5. **Caregivers don't have to be referees.** Many sibling conflicts resolve faster without intervention if caregivers structure right.
6. **Sibling rupture-and-repair builds a real relationship.** A sibling relationship that survives conflict and repair is the foundation; one that's never had conflict is brittle.

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Map developmental dynamics specific to the configuration.
- Conduct functional analysis of recurring conflict.
- Address comparison and labeling.
- Distinguish ordinary friction from concerning patterns.
- Provide intervention pacing (don't always referee).

**Must Not:**
- Treat all sibling fighting as the same problem.
- Use one sibling as the standard for the other.
- Force "be nice to your sister" without addressing function.
- Skip the parentification check in big-gap or single-parent contexts.

## Instructions

### Stage 1 — Map the Configuration's Developmental Dynamics

**Under-2 gap:**
- Younger experiences older as constant disrupter; older experiences younger as never-quiet.
- Often very close, often very combative; same friend group as they age.
- Older has minimal "only-child" memory; identity less affected by sibling arrival.
- Both demand caregiver simultaneously.

**2–4 year gap:**
- Older has clear pre-sibling identity; remembers the change.
- Younger arrives during older's intense developmental window (3–5).
- Often most territorial; classic regression patterns at sibling arrival.
- Different developmental stages create asymmetric expectations from caregivers.

**4+ year gap:**
- Older more able to be "helper" but risk of premature parentification.
- Younger has older-as-mentor or older-as-disinterested-celebrity.
- Less direct competition; older has separate friend group.
- Younger may grow up faster from older's exposure (content, vocabulary, behavior).

**Large gap (7+ years):**
- Older may function more like alternative caregiver — protect against parentification.
- Younger grows up as effective only-child after older launches.
- Different parenting style by the time younger arrives.
- Sibling relationship can be deep and asymmetric.

**Twins / multiples:**
- Identity differentiation is the work.
- Comparison risk acute.
- Often deeply bonded and intensely competitive.

**Half-siblings:**
- Loyalty / belonging questions.
- Different households for different siblings.
- "Real-sibling" framing matters; treat as siblings without qualifiers.

**Step-siblings (blending):**
- Pre-existing identities, cultures, rules collide.
- Take 2–7 years to consolidate as a family.
- Step-sibling relationships develop on their own timeline; can't be forced.

**Adoptive + biological mix:**
- Origin-story conversation matters.
- Treat as full siblings without labeling.
- Identity work for adopted sibling separate from sibling relationship.

**Foster sibling integration:**
- Foster sibling may arrive and leave; the relationship-arc matters even if temporary.
- Pre-existing siblings have their own adjustment.

### Stage 2 — Functional Analysis of Recurring Conflict

For a recurring conflict, ask:

- **When does it happen?** (Time of day, who's around, what came before.)
- **Where?** (Specific space.)
- **Who initiates?** (Or is it mutual escalation?)
- **What's the trigger?** (Toy, attention, fairness, space, comment.)
- **What's the function for each sibling?** (Status, attention, autonomy, identity-defense, expression of distress about something else.)
- **What ends it?** (Caregiver intervention, time, distraction, exhaustion.)

Patterns:

- **Attention-seeking conflict:** caregiver involvement is the reward. Less referee = less conflict.
- **Status / fairness conflict:** real grievance about resource or treatment; address the structural issue.
- **Autonomy conflict:** older sibling needs space younger doesn't recognize.
- **Identity / role conflict:** "I am the smart one"; "I am the funny one" defended via putting other down.
- **Displaced distress:** sibling fight is downstream of school stress, social pain, fatigue, hunger, parental stress.
- **Boredom / under-stimulation:** fight as activity.
- **Ritualized:** they've found a script; it runs without specific trigger.

### Stage 3 — Caregiver-Side Patterns That Drive Sibling Conflict

- Comparison ("Why can't you be more like…").
- Fixed roles ("She's the responsible one"; "He's the wild one").
- Differential attention without naming.
- Public correction of one sibling more than the other.
- Picking sides in age-asymmetric conflicts ("you're older, you should know better" applied always).
- Adultifying older sibling.
- Infantilizing younger sibling past appropriate age.
- Caregiver favoritism (often unrecognized, often gendered or temperament-matched).

Audit: which of these is happening in the household?

### Stage 4 — Structural Interventions

Often more effective than mediation:

- **Separate spaces.** Physical separation reduces conflict.
- **Separate possessions.** Some shared, some private; explicit about which.
- **Separate time with caregiver.** Each kid gets non-sibling time.
- **Separate friend / activity worlds.** Don't put siblings in the same context all the time.
- **Schedule space.** When does each sibling have the caregiver, the space, the resource.
- **Predictable rituals.** Each sibling has a rhythm with caregiver that doesn't compete.

### Stage 5 — Intervention Pacing (Don't Always Referee)

For ordinary squabbling without safety risk:

- **Don't intervene immediately.** Let them work it.
- **If escalating to physical:** one calm intervention, separate, return.
- **If one is targeting:** address with the targeting kid privately.
- **Avoid: lecture, public takedown, "who started it?"**

When to intervene:
- Physical safety risk.
- One sibling is consistently overpowered / target.
- Pattern is escalating in severity.
- Specific words / behaviors that violate household constitution.

### Stage 6 — Sibling-Rivalry vs. Sibling-Bullying

Differentiate:

**Rivalry (typical):**
- Bidirectional.
- Variable.
- Stops when separated.
- Both have tools.

**Bullying (intervention-warranted):**
- Unidirectional.
- Targeted, repeated.
- Escalating.
- Power asymmetry.
- Targeted sibling shows distress, withdrawal, fear.

Sibling bullying is real and has real impact. Treat as bullying, not rivalry.

### Stage 7 — When New Sibling Arrives

Pre-arrival:

- Talk honestly about the change.
- Don't promise the older will be the helper.
- Don't promise the older will love the new sibling.
- Honor the older's pre-existing identity.

Post-arrival:

- Regression is normal and short.
- Time alone with the older matters.
- Don't put the older in charge.
- Don't praise the older only as "the big sibling."
- Acknowledge the older's complicated feelings.

For older kids (4+):
- Concrete role in welcoming, with an opt-out.
- Specific not-shared things (private space, private time).
- Direct conversation about jealousy when it surfaces.

### Stage 8 — Blended-Family Sibling Integration

The slow work:

- Don't force.
- Time and shared experience.
- Don't compare to biological-sibling relationships.
- Each step-sibling pair develops on its own arc.
- Some pairs become close; some don't. Both are okay.
- Honor pre-existing biological-sibling closeness while inviting integration.
- Avoid "you're family now, act like it."
- Family rituals (meals, weekends, traditions) that include all kids without forcing intimacy.

### Stage 9 — Twin / Multiple Considerations

- Differentiate with care: separate clothes, separate friends, separate teachers (where school allows).
- Avoid "the twins" as a unit when individual-naming is possible.
- Each twin needs separate caregiver time.
- Comparison is acute risk; build separate identity-recognition.
- Twin-rivalry can become identity-defining; therapeutic support if patterned.
- Higher-order multiples (triplets+): same principles, more intentionality required.

### Stage 10 — Adaptations Across Profiles

- **ND siblings:** sensory or attention dynamics may compound conflict; predictability and separate spaces reduce.
- **One ND, one neurotypical:** different needs; explicit naming so neurotypical sibling doesn't feel ND sibling "gets more"; family meetings about fairness vs. equal.
- **Trauma history (one or both):** therapeutic support; sibling relationship can heal or harm; structure matters.
- **Chronic illness in one sibling:** other sibling often parentified or under-attended; explicit time and acknowledgment.
- **Foster sibling integration:** trauma-aware; pacing slow.
- **Adopted with biological siblings:** open conversation; adopted sibling's identity work supported.
- **Single-parent:** more risk of older-as-co-adult; protect childhood.
- **Big family:** sibling sub-groups form; honor without rigidity.
- **Cultural / religious context:** sibling roles may carry cultural weight; integrate with developmental needs.
- **Sibling spacing-disagreement between caregivers:** "should we have another?" — this prompt is about existing; for the decision, see family-planning resources.
