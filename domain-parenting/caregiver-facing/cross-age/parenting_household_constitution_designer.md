**Purpose:** Help a family articulate a household constitution — the explicit values, shared agreements, and rules-with-reasons — that orients the family across the years and gives kids something to test against.

**When to use:** Designing the household constitution for the first time; revisiting after major change (move, new sibling, divorce, blending, foster placement); resolving a recurring conflict that needs structural framing; teen pushing on rules and asking "why"; preparing for a developmental transition (school start, middle school, high school).

**When NOT to use:** Acute family crisis (handle the crisis first; constitution work is medium-term).

---

## Core Principles

1. **Articulating values doesn't create them — it clarifies them.** The family already lives by values, often implicitly. Writing them down makes them coachable, testable, and adjustable.
2. **Rules-with-reasons travel further than rules-without.** A rule whose reason kids understand survives the moment of testing.
3. **The constitution distinguishes values, rules, and preferences.** Not everything is equally weighted.
4. **It's a living document.** Reviewed at least yearly; rewritten as the family changes.
5. **Kids participate in age-appropriate ways.** They don't draft it from scratch, but they help with the version they will live under.
6. **The household constitution is for inside the household.** It does not require everyone outside to live by it.

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Distinguish values, rules, and preferences.
- Include rules-with-reasons.
- Adapt to family structure.
- Provide kid-participation framework.
- Include review cadence.

**Must Not:**
- Default to a single template.
- Mandate values; articulate the family's actual values.
- Treat the constitution as enforcement document.
- Skip the dialogue about disagreement among caregivers before drafting.

## Instructions

### Stage 1 — Caregiver Pre-Work

Before drafting with kids:

- Caregivers align on what's actually held as values.
- Where caregivers disagree: name the disagreement; decide which goes in, which is held privately, which gets caregiver-discussion-space.
- Audit own family-of-origin: what was modeled, what to keep, what to drop.
- Audit cultural / religious / community values: what's central, what's preference.

### Stage 2 — The Three Tiers

**Tier 1: Values** (the why).
- 3–7 values that orient the family.
- Examples: "We treat each other gently." "We tell the truth." "We care for our community." "We make space for hard feelings." "We try things and accept failure." "We don't make others smaller to feel bigger."

**Tier 2: Rules** (the must / must-not).
- 5–15 explicit rules tied to values.
- Examples: "No hitting." "We use kind words." "We tell each other where we are." "Phones charge in the kitchen overnight." "We come to dinner most nights."

**Tier 3: Preferences** (the if-possible).
- Soft preferences that aren't rules but are family style.
- Examples: "We like to read together at night." "We try not to fight in the car."

### Stage 3 — Articulating Values

For each candidate value, ask:

- Is this actually how we live, or how we wish we lived?
- Is this a value or a preference?
- Would this still hold under stress?
- Can we name what it looks like in action?

A useful test: pick a recent family conflict. What value would have helped? Add that.

### Stage 4 — Articulating Rules

For each rule, articulate:

- The rule itself.
- The why (tied to a value).
- The age range (some rules are age-specific).
- The exception conditions (if any).
- The renegotiation cadence.

Example:
```
Rule: We don't have phones at the dinner table.
Why: We're trying to actually be with each other.
Ages: Everyone, including caregivers.
Exception: Pre-arranged check-in (work emergency, sick relative).
Renegotiation: Yearly review; one-off requests at family meeting.
```

### Stage 5 — Categories Worth Covering

- **Physical safety:** what we don't do to each other's bodies.
- **Emotional safety:** what we don't do to each other's feelings; how we handle anger.
- **Honesty:** what counts as honest; what counts as deceptive; what we do when honesty is hard.
- **Repair:** how we apologize, how we reconnect after rupture.
- **Shared resources:** household, food, money, space, time.
- **Tech and screens:** norms across ages.
- **Outside-the-household:** how we talk about our family with others; what's private.
- **Help-seeking:** what we do when we need help.
- **Mistakes:** what happens when we mess up.
- **Disagreement:** how we handle differences in values, religion, politics, identity.

### Stage 6 — Kid Participation

**Ages 4–8:**
- They contribute by naming what feels good and what feels bad in the family.
- They participate in agreeing to specific kid-relevant rules.
- They can decorate / illustrate the constitution.
- They don't draft; they ratify.

**Ages 9–12:**
- They can co-author specific sections (their bedroom, screen time, chores).
- They can argue for changes at family meeting.
- They sign.

**Ages 13–18:**
- They negotiate.
- They can challenge values.
- They can disagree with rules and propose changes.
- The constitution becomes more transparent about what's caregiver-decided and what's negotiable.
- They are honest co-authors.

### Stage 7 — Format

A useful format:

```
[FAMILY NAME] HOUSEHOLD CONSTITUTION
Version [X], [date]
Reviewed every [interval]; next review [date].

OUR VALUES
[3–7 values, each with one-sentence elaboration.]

OUR RULES
[Categorized by domain; each with the rule, the why, the age,
exceptions, renegotiation.]

OUR PREFERENCES
[Soft preferences as family style.]

WHEN WE MESS UP
[How we repair; how we hold each other accountable.]

WHEN WE DISAGREE
[How conflicts get processed.]

REVIEW
[Cadence + how to add an item.]

SIGNED
[Each family member signs; younger kids may print or stamp.]
```

Can be a one-pager or a small printed document. Posted somewhere visible.

### Stage 8 — Review Cadence

- **Yearly review** at minimum.
- **Trigger reviews** at major changes: new sibling, move, divorce, blending, kid age-band transition.
- **Mini-review** at recurring conflicts: "is the constitution still working on this?"
- Add and remove without ceremony; the document is alive.

### Stage 9 — Adapting for Family Structure

**Single-parent:**
- Constitution can be drafted with kids; the absence of co-parent disagreement simplifies.
- Includes how the family handles the absent parent (visits, contact, references).

**Blended family:**
- Critical document. Two histories merging.
- Co-authored by both adults explicitly.
- Address how step-relationships are named, treated, navigated.
- May need separate norms for biological-parent-and-kid relationships and step-parent-and-kid.
- Cross-household coordination is usually separate, not in the household constitution.

**Co-parenting across households:**
- Each household has its own constitution.
- Some shared minimums in shared parenting plan, separate document.
- Kids navigating two constitutions need explicit help: "This is how we do it here. They may do it differently there. Both can work."

**Multigenerational:**
- Grandparent / aunt / uncle as constitution co-author.
- Cultural / generational norms.

**Foster / kinship:**
- Constitution is part of new-family orientation.
- Holds steady when other things shift.
- Includes specific items relevant to fostering (e.g., privacy, what we share with biological family, how transitions are handled).

**Adoptive family:**
- Constitution can name adoption-related values explicitly (open conversation, adoption isn't shameful, ongoing identity work).

**LGBTQ+-headed:**
- Same fundamentals; may include explicit values about identity-affirmation.

**Religious family:**
- Religious values explicit if central.
- Distinguish religious-required from family-preference.

**Cultural-translation family:**
- Articulate which culture(s) the family operates in; how language works; when each set of cultural norms applies.

### Stage 10 — Adaptations Across Profiles

- **Autistic kids:** explicit, written, predictable; literal language; review cadence honored.
- **ADHD kids:** brief, visual, accessible; review during meeting.
- **Anxious kids:** clarity is calming; consistent application.
- **Trauma history:** safety-and-predictability values surface; how-we-handle-conflict matters extra.
- **Big age range:** layered constitution (kid-section + teen-section + everyone-section).
- **Recent life-disruption:** the constitution is part of stabilizing.

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| Default to a single template regardless of family | Articulate the family's actual values |
| Mandate values not lived | Articulate, don't aspire-only |
| Treat constitution as enforcement document | Orientation, not punishment |
| Skip caregiver alignment first | Pre-work matters |
| Skip rules-with-reasons | Why travels further than what |
| Make the constitution all-rules-no-values | Value layer matters |
| Make it all-values-no-rules | Operationalization matters too |
| Require kids to draft from scratch | Age-appropriate participation |
| Skip review cadence | Living document |
| Use it to embarrass kids | Constitution is for orientation, not weaponization |
| Treat as universal across two households | Each household has its own; not transferable |
| Add new rules without removing old ones | Audit; remove stale |
| Use during conflict to corner the kid | Use outside conflict to set the frame |
| Skip cultural / religious explicit articulation when relevant | Central values are central |
| Treat kids' ratification as performative | They co-own when they help author |
