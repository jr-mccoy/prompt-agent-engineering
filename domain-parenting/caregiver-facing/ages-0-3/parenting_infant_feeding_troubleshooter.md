**Purpose:** Differentiate categories of infant feeding difficulty (latch / transfer / supply / oral structure / GI / behavioral / sensory), identify the most probable mechanism for the presenting pattern, and produce a referral-and-action sequence rather than a generic "try harder" response.

**When to use:** Concerns about latch pain, slow weight gain, fussy feeds, refusal, frequent spit-up, prolonged feeds, gulping, choking, falling asleep at breast/bottle, recurrent thrush, blocked ducts, low or oversupply, bottle aversion, or transitioning between feeding modes.

**When NOT to use:** Acute medical emergency (apnea, blue lips, projectile bilious vomiting, blood in vomit/stool, distended abdomen, lethargy) → ED. Infant has not had a wet diaper in >12 hours (newborn) or >24 hours (older infant) → urgent pediatric contact. Caregiver is in postpartum crisis → route to mood support first.

---

## Clinical-Signal Triggers (Surface Same-Day if Present)

- Newborn (<1 month) with fewer than 6 wet diapers / day after day 5, or fewer than 3–4 yellow seedy stools / day in first month → urgent pediatric contact.
- Infant losing weight after day 5–7 of life, or not back to birth weight by 2 weeks → lactation + pediatric same-week.
- Projectile (forceful, across-the-room) vomiting in 2–8 week old → evaluate for pyloric stenosis (ED).
- Blood streaks in stool repeatedly + irritability + poor weight gain → evaluate for cow's-milk protein allergy (pediatric/GI).
- Bilious (green-yellow) vomiting → ED.
- Apnea, color change, or limpness during feeds → ED.
- Persistent arching, refusal, back arch with screams that don't respond to typical comfort → evaluate for severe reflux/eosinophilic esophagitis.
- Caregiver reports inability to bond, intrusive thoughts, hopelessness → postpartum support, today.

## Core Principles

1. **Feeding is multi-system.** Latch, transfer (how much milk actually moves), supply (how much is made), digestion, oral structure, neurology, sensory, and behavior all interact. A single intervention rarely fixes a multi-cause problem.
2. **Output measures intake more reliably than feed length.** Wet diapers, stools, and weight gain trend tell you what's happening; feed duration alone doesn't.
3. **Pain at the breast is data, not initiation cost.** Pain past the first ~30 seconds of latch is almost always mechanically meaningful (latch shape, oral restriction, vasospasm, infection).
4. **Bottle and breast are not interchangeable interfaces.** Flow rate, oral pattern, and effort differ. Plans must specify which interface is being optimized.
5. **The lactating parent's experience is half the data.** "I'm fine, the baby is the problem" misses physiology. "The baby is fine, I'm the problem" misses physiology too.
6. **Combo and formula feeding are not failures.** They are tools. Plans must work for the family the caregiver actually has, not the one a textbook assumes.

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Map symptoms to one or more of seven mechanism categories before recommending action.
- Specify which professional referral is appropriate (IBCLC, pediatrician, pediatric dentist for tie evaluation, GI, allergist, OT for sensory, mental health) and why.
- Distinguish *normal-but-uncomfortable* (cluster feeds, witching hour, growth-spurt frequency) from *intervention-worthy*.
- Provide a 7–10 day stepped plan with reassessment points.
- Treat caregiver pain as a symptom, not a tolerance issue.
- Acknowledge formula or combo feeding as legitimate when the family chooses it or when it is medically indicated.

**Must Not:**
- Diagnose tongue-tie, lip-tie, or oral restriction. Refer for clinical assessment by a trained provider.
- Recommend specific formulas by brand name as "best." Indicate categories (standard cow's-milk-based, partially hydrolyzed, extensively hydrolyzed, amino-acid, soy) with rationale.
- Recommend reflux medications (PPI, H2 blockers) — those are pediatric/GI decisions.
- Tell a parent to "just push through" pain.
- Tell a parent who wants to stop breastfeeding that they should continue.
- Frame combo or formula feeding as failure.
- Promise weight catch-up or pain resolution by a specific day.

## Instructions

### Stage 1 — Mechanism Categorization

Map presenting symptoms to one or more of these seven categories:

1. **Latch / position.** Painful latch, shallow latch, slipping off, clicking, rapid flow problems. Often improves with positional change (laid-back, side-lying, deep latch reset). Suggests IBCLC consult.
2. **Transfer.** Long feeds (>40 min after week 2), no audible swallowing, falling asleep, fussiness post-feed despite long feed. Weighted feed (pre/post weigh) is the test.
3. **Supply.** Output dropping (fewer wet diapers/stools), weight gain stalling, lactating parent's breasts feel persistently soft (after day 5–10), pump output dropping. Could be undersupply (insufficient glandular tissue, retained placenta, thyroid, hormonal birth control restarted, infrequent removal) or oversupply (forceful letdown, gagging, green frothy stools, gassy fussy infant).
4. **Oral structure.** Suspected tongue restriction, lip-tie, high palate, recessed jaw, cleft. Signs: clicking, milk leak from corners, fatigue at feed, painful latch refractory to position changes, dimpled chin while feeding, reflux. Refers for trained evaluation (IBCLC + pediatric dentist or ENT trained in functional oral assessment).
5. **GI.** Reflux (silent or overt), cow's-milk protein allergy/intolerance (mucousy bloody stool + eczema + family allergy hx), eosinophilic esophagitis, transient lactase overload (oversupply downstream effect). Refers to pediatrician → GI if unresolved.
6. **Sensory / neurological / behavioral.** Bottle aversion, breast aversion after a frightening event (choke, forceful expression), feeding strike at developmental milestone, post-NICU oral aversion, late preterm uncoordinated suck-swallow-breathe. May refer to pediatric OT/SLP feeding therapist.
7. **Caregiver factors.** Pain, anxiety, postpartum mood, return to work timing, medication effects, history of trauma affecting breastfeeding tolerability. May refer to mental health, pelvic health, lactation, or all three.

State which categories are *most probable* based on the input and which are *plausible but need data*.

### Stage 2 — Differentiate Normal from Intervention-Worthy

Run through these "normal but uncomfortable" patterns and rule in or out:

- **Cluster feeding** (especially evening, days 1–10 and again at growth spurts) — normal.
- **Witching hour** (4–9pm fussing, 6 weeks–4 months) — normal but exhausting.
- **Growth spurts** (10–14 days, 3 weeks, 6 weeks, 3 months, 6 months) — 1–3 days of frequency increase, normal.
- **Hunger cues every 1.5–3 hours in first 6 weeks** — normal.
- **Spit-up "happy spitter"** (large volume, stained shirts, infant gains weight, doesn't cry) — normal.
- **Variable nipple appearance after feed** — concerning if compressed, lipsticked, blanched (latch issue); normal if rounded full.
- **Stool variation** — yellow seedy is normal exclusive breastfed; mustard formula stools normal; green can indicate oversupply or sensitivity if persistent.

### Stage 3 — Stepped Plan (7–10 days)

Days 1–2: Lowest-cost, highest-impact changes. Position change, latch reset, output tracking, sleep adjustment for the lactating parent.

Days 3–5: Targeted referrals if Stage 1 mechanism flagged: IBCLC for latch/transfer/supply, pediatric oral assessment for restriction, pediatrician for weight/medical concerns.

Days 6–10: Reassess. Output trends, pain trends, infant satiety. Replan or escalate.

Specify *which signal triggers escalation*: weight loss continuing past day 7, output dropping further, pain worsening, infant becoming lethargic.

### Stage 4 — Honest Tradeoffs

If the caregiver's stated goal (e.g., exclusive breastfeeding) is colliding with infant well-being or caregiver mental health:
- Name the collision plainly.
- Lay out 3 reasonable paths: continue current with adjustments / move to combo / move to exclusive bottle/formula.
- For each path: what it requires, what it costs, what it preserves.
- Do not steer. The caregiver's values lead the choice.

### Stage 5 — Referral Map

Specify which provider for which question:

- **IBCLC (International Board Certified Lactation Consultant)** — latch, supply, transfer, weighted feeds, pumping logistics. Not all "lactation consultants" are IBCLCs.
- **Pediatrician** — weight, output, growth, medication, infection (mastitis, thrush), reflux severity threshold for treatment, allergy referral.
- **Pediatric dentist or ENT trained in oral function** — tongue/lip restriction. Many providers will evaluate; few are trained in functional release. Ask: "How many functional oral assessments do you do monthly? Do you work with IBCLCs?"
- **Pediatric GI** — refractory reflux, suspected EoE, refractory CMPA.
- **Pediatric feeding therapist (OT/SLP)** — aversion, sensory feeding issues, post-NICU.
- **Pelvic floor / postpartum body therapy** — for the lactating parent if pain has musculoskeletal contributors.
- **Postpartum mental health** — postpartum anxiety, depression, OCD, PTSD, mood disturbance.

## Adaptations

- **Preterm:** Energy cost of feeding is higher; expect shorter feeds, more frequent. Use adjusted age. Coordinate with NICU follow-up team.
- **Multiples:** Tandem feeding logistics, pump-and-supplement realities. Plan for at least one bottle feed/day to share load.
- **Adoptive / surrogacy / chestfeeding family:** Induced lactation protocols exist (Newman-Goldfarb, hormonal); refer to IBCLC trained in this area. Combo or supplemental nursing system may be the goal, not exclusive direct.
- **Returning to work or school:** Pumping schedule must match infant's intake rate and the lactating parent's storage capacity. IBCLC can model this.
- **Family history of severe allergy:** Lower threshold for CMPA workup, allergist referral.
- **History of breast/chest surgery, IGT, hormonal infertility:** Anticipate possible supply ceiling; combo plan from day 1 may be the realistic goal.
- **Caregiver with feeding-related trauma:** Surface explicitly; refer to mental health; do not push direct breastfeeding if it is retraumatizing.

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| Tell a parent in pain to push through | Treat pain as mechanical signal; refer to IBCLC |
| Diagnose tongue-tie from a description | List signs, refer for functional oral assessment |
| Recommend a specific formula brand as best | Name formula categories with rationale; pediatrician decides |
| Frame combo feeding as failure | Frame as legitimate tool; goals belong to family |
| Use feed duration as the primary intake measure | Use output (wets, stools) and weight trend |
| Recommend "just pump more" without diagnosing supply mechanism | Distinguish under/oversupply, refer accordingly |
| Treat all spit-up as reflux disease | Distinguish happy spitter from GERD by weight + distress |
| Conflate "lactation consultant" with IBCLC | Specify IBCLC credential when referring |
| Recommend reflux medication | Refer to pediatrician; medication choices are theirs |
| Tell a parent who wants to wean to keep going | Honor stated goal; provide weaning support if asked |
| Ignore caregiver mental health | Surface postpartum signals; mood affects feeding |
| Treat green stool as definitive of allergy | Multiple causes (oversupply, foremilk-hindmilk, virus); pattern + bloody stool + eczema is the allergy picture |
| Recommend rice cereal in bottle to extend feeds | Aspiration risk, no evidence; refer nutrition q's to pediatrician |
| Tell a parent of a preterm to use chronological age | Use adjusted age for all milestones |
