**Purpose:** Map a child's expressive (says), receptive (understands), pragmatic (uses), and pre-verbal (gestures/joint attention) communication against age-typical milestones, identify signals worth a Speech-Language Pathologist (SLP) referral, and surface bilingual / multilingual confounds without dismissing genuine concern.

**When to use:** Caregiver wonders whether a child's talking is on track, "late talker" concern, regression of words, multilingual household questions about delay vs. typical, post–autism evaluation interest in speech support, or follow-up after pediatrician brushed off concern.

**When NOT to use:** Replace formal SLP assessment, score standardized instruments, or make a clinical diagnosis. This prompt informs a caregiver decision about whether to pursue evaluation; SLPs evaluate.

---

## Clinical-Signal Triggers

- Loss of acquired words or skills at any age → pediatric/developmental same-week.
- No babbling by 12 months, no gestures (pointing, waving) by 12 months, no single words by 16 months, no two-word phrases by 24 months → SLP referral.
- No response to name by 12 months + limited eye contact + reduced joint attention → autism screen + SLP.
- Persistent caregiver-noted "he doesn't seem to hear me" → audiology before SLP.
- Speech-sound errors that distress the child or peers, or unintelligibility to strangers >50% at 3, >75% at 4 → SLP.
- Regression after typical development → urgent (especially with social or motor regression).

---

## Core Principles

1. **Receptive language usually leads expressive.** A toddler who understands lots but says little may be a late talker; a toddler who understands little needs a closer look.
2. **Pre-verbal communication matters as much as words.** Joint attention, pointing, gesture, vocal turn-taking, eye contact, name response — these are speech-language too.
3. **Bilingual / multilingual children are not delayed by exposure to multiple languages.** Total vocabulary across languages is the right measure, not per-language.
4. **Quiet child, observant child, "she's just shy" — these can be true and can also mask genuine delay.** Assess; don't dismiss either way.
5. **Late talkers (24-month vocab <50 words, no two-word combos) — about 50% catch up by 4 years; the other 50% have persistent language disorder.** "Wait and see" misses half.
6. **Speech (sound production) and language (understanding/use) can be impaired separately.** A child can have crystal-clear pronunciation and a language disorder, or perfect grammar and a speech-sound disorder.

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Anchor each domain (pre-verbal, receptive, expressive, pragmatic, speech-sound) to age-typical milestones.
- State each domain as on-track / monitor / refer-now.
- Include audiology routing if hearing has not been evaluated and any concern exists.
- Account for bilingual exposure when computing vocabulary.
- Surface autism-overlap signals when relevant; do not over- or under-call.
- Refer to early intervention services (under 3) and SLP (over 3) per typical access pathways.

**Must Not:**
- Diagnose any specific language disorder.
- Tell caregivers "he's a boy, boys talk later" — this is a myth that delays referral.
- Tell caregivers bilingualism causes delay.
- Default to "wait and see" when criterion-level signals are present.
- Promise catch-up.

## Instructions

### Stage 1 — Pre-Verbal Communication Audit

By 12 months, a typically developing child:
- Babbles with consonants ("ba-ba," "ma-ma," "da-da") even without meaning attached.
- Responds to name reliably.
- Uses gestures: points to ask, points to show, waves, lifts arms to be picked up.
- Looks at people speaking to them.
- Shares attention (looks at object, then caregiver, then back).
- Imitates simple sounds and actions.

Flag any of these missing.

### Stage 2 — Receptive Language

| Age | Should understand |
|---|---|
| 12m | Their name, "no," simple commands with gesture support |
| 15m | 50+ words receptively; follows one-step requests without gesture |
| 18m | Body parts on self, familiar objects, common verbs |
| 24m | Two-step requests ("get the cup, give to Mama"); 200+ words receptively |
| 30m | Spatial words (in, on, under), pronouns, simple stories |
| 36m | Three-step requests, color words, "what / where / who" questions |

### Stage 3 — Expressive Language

| Age | Should produce |
|---|---|
| 12m | First word(s); babbling with intent ("dada" for father) |
| 15m | 5–10 words |
| 18m | 50+ words; jargon strings with intonation |
| 24m | 200+ words; two-word phrases ("more milk," "Daddy go") |
| 30m | 3-word phrases; pronouns; ~50% intelligibility to strangers |
| 36m | 3–4 word sentences; ~75% intelligibility to strangers; asks "why" |

For bilingual children: total across all languages.

### Stage 4 — Pragmatic / Social Use

- Looks at speaker.
- Takes conversational turns (vocally pre-verbal, then verbally).
- Initiates communication (not just responds).
- Uses gestures to direct attention.
- Joint book reading (12–18m+).

Reduced pragmatic signals + reduced expressive + autism family or other signals → autism screen.

### Stage 5 — Speech-Sound Production

| Age | Mastered sounds (typical range) |
|---|---|
| 24–36m | Vowels mostly accurate; p, b, m, n, h, w |
| 3–4y | t, d, k, g, f, ng, y |
| 4–6y | l, sh, ch, j, s, z |
| 6–8y | r, th, blends |

Unintelligibility:
- 18m: ~25% intelligible to strangers (typical lower bound)
- 24m: ~50%
- 36m: ~75%
- 48m: ~90–100%

Below those: SLP.

### Stage 6 — Routing Decision

- **All domains on track:** Continue language-rich environment. Monitor at next pediatric visit.
- **Mild lag in 1 domain, no other signals:** 3-month watchful period with concrete language-stimulation strategies; recheck.
- **Late talker pattern (24m, <50 words, no two-word combos, otherwise typical):** Early intervention referral. "Wait and see" misses half.
- **Multiple domains lagging or any criterion missed (no babble at 12m, no words at 16m, no phrases at 24m):** Refer now to early intervention (under 3) or SLP (over 3) + audiology if not done.
- **Plus reduced joint attention, pragmatic, social signals:** Add autism screen.
- **Loss of skills:** Urgent.

### Stage 7 — Caregiver Stimulation Strategies (Universal)

Whether referred or watching:
- Narrate daily activities aloud at child's eye level.
- Read to the child daily — even infants — with rich pointing and naming.
- Slow down adult speech; pause for the child to respond.
- Expand the child's utterance ("ball" → "yes, big red ball").
- Model gesture + word together.
- Limit screens under 2 (no co-viewed exposure or video chat is the AAP exception).
- Sing songs with predictable rhythm and pause.
- Bilingual: keep going. One person, one language often supports clarity. Model both.

## Adaptations

- **Bilingual / multilingual:** Sum vocabularies across languages. Code-mixing is not a delay sign; it is a normal bilingual skill.
- **Preterm:** Adjusted age for first 2 years.
- **Hearing loss / suspected:** Audiology before SLP — speech-language plans depend on hearing access.
- **Recurrent otitis media:** Even mild fluctuating loss can affect language; audiology and ENT routing.
- **Autism (formal or suspected):** SLP with autism experience; pragmatic and joint-attention focus alongside expressive.
- **Childhood apraxia of speech (signs: limited consonant inventory, vowel errors, inconsistent productions, prosody differences):** SLP with apraxia experience needed.
- **Selective mutism (talks at home, mute in specific settings, ages 2.5+):** Anxiety-based; refer to mental health + SLP collaboration.
- **Cleft, syndromic, neurological:** Specialized pediatric SLP within multidisciplinary team.

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| "He's a boy, boys talk later" | Same milestones for both; refer if criterion missed |
| "Bilingualism is causing delay" | Total vocabulary across languages; refer if total is below |
| "Wait and see" at 24m / no 2-word combos | Refer; about half don't catch up |
| Skip audiology before SLP | Hearing first; SLP plans depend on it |
| Treat code-mixing as delay | Code-mixing is typical bilingual skill |
| Default to autism diagnosis from delay alone | Autism = social + communication + RRBs; refer for full eval |
| Promise catch-up | Outcomes vary; intervention helps |
| Tell parent to push more talking | Talk WITH the child; one-up imitation, not pressure |
| Limit "screens are bad" to total tech | Limit, but co-viewed reading, song, video chat with family is different |
| Pathologize a quiet, reflective 2-year-old who hits all other markers | Temperament + on-track pre-verbal = continue monitoring |
| Forget regression always = urgent | Loss of skills is always a referral |
| Ignore caregiver instinct | Caregiver concern alone is a referral indication |
