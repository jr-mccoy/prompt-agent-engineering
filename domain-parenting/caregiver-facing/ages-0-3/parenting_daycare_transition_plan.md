**Purpose:** Build a plan for transitioning a child under 3 into childcare — picking a setting, ramping the start, communicating with the provider, surviving the first illness wave, and tracking real adjustment.

**When to use:** First daycare placement, switching providers, return-to-work timing decisions, kinship/grandparent care launch, in-home nanny start, mid-year provider change due to need or fit problem.

**When NOT to use:** Suspected provider safety concern (immediate consult licensing/CPS, do not delay), severe medical needs requiring specialized placement (pediatric coordination), foster placement transitions (use foster-specific resource).

---

## Clinical-Signal Triggers

- Bruises/marks unexplained, child fearful of provider, regression with no other cause → safety evaluation, do not return without investigation.
- Provider not following written health/safety plan despite documented requests → escalate to director, then licensing.
- Sustained adjustment failure (>6 weeks) with multiple indicators (sleep, eating, mood) failing to recover → reevaluate fit, not just child.
- Caregiver crisis around return-to-work → support pathway in parallel.

---

## Core Principles

1. **Adjustment averages 2–6 weeks** for under-3s in a well-matched setting.
2. **Provider quality > setting type.** A skilled in-home caregiver can outperform a poorly-staffed center; a high-quality center can outperform an isolated home setting.
3. **Ratios and staff continuity matter more than amenities.** State-recommended infant ratios are 1:3 or 1:4; toddler 1:4 to 1:6. Staff turnover predicts attachment disruption.
4. **The first 6–12 months in group care = constant illness.** This is not a sign of failure or wrong placement.
5. **The child's experience and the family's logistics are both legitimate.** "Best fit" sits at the intersection.
6. **Caregiver guilt about return-to-work is normal and not predictive of a wrong choice.**

**Must:**
- Provide setting-fit checklist before ramp.
- Recommend staggered start when feasible.
- Address illness wave honestly.
- Provide written communication protocol with provider.
- Address sleep, feeding, and separation as separate workstreams.
- Surface adjustment markers and tripwires.

**Must Not:**
- Recommend full-day-from-day-1 ramp without naming the cost.
- Pathologize illness wave as fit failure.
- Default to mother-only framing of caregiver guilt.
- Pretend caregiver and child experiences are the same — both matter.
- Recommend leaving unresolved safety concerns unaddressed.

## Instructions

### Stage 1 — Setting Fit Checklist

Before placement, walk through:

**Safety & Licensing**
- License and inspection records (state portal).
- Background checks on all adults present.
- Up-to-date CPR/first aid certifications.
- Safe sleep practices for under-1s observed (ABC: alone, back, crib).
- Mandated-reporter training for staff.

**Ratios & Continuity**
- Infant ratio 1:3 or 1:4 maximum.
- Toddler ratio 1:4 to 1:6 maximum.
- Primary caregiver assigned (1–2 staff who attend to your child).
- Staff retention: ask "How long have classroom teachers been here?"

**Daily Practices**
- Daily report (sleep, eat, diapers, mood) standardized.
- Outdoor time daily.
- Loose parts / responsive play (less screen, less worksheet).
- Photo/feed sharing protocol with consent.

**Health & Illness**
- Sick policy clear (fever, vomiting, diarrhea exclusion criteria).
- Medication administration protocol.
- Hand-washing protocol observed.

**Inclusion**
- Comfortable with chestfeeding/bottle-feeding requests.
- Multilingual provider or supportive of multilingual exposure.
- Allergy/medical plan handled.
- Open to caregiver visits/check-ins.

**Cultural Fit**
- Food, holidays, family configuration treated respectfully.
- Discipline/redirection approach aligns with caregiver values.

### Stage 2 — Staggered Ramp (Standard)

If schedule allows:

- **Week before start:** 1–2 visits with caregiver present (60–90 min). Child explores; caregiver chats with provider. Child meets primary caregiver.
- **Day 1–2:** 2-hour drop-off. Pick up before nap.
- **Day 3–4:** Half-day with one nap.
- **Day 5–7:** Half-day stretching.
- **Week 2:** Full schedule.

If schedule doesn't allow ramp (work pressure):
- Compress to 2-day mini-ramp on a weekend or PTO.
- Tell provider where you are on the curve so they know your child is at the start, not stalled.

### Stage 3 — Communication Protocol with Provider

Before start, send a one-page written profile:

- **Name + age + adjusted age (if preterm).**
- **Feeding:** mode, schedule, bottle prep instructions, allergies.
- **Sleep:** typical schedule, soothing preferences, what works at home, what doesn't.
- **Comfort:** transition object, words/songs that help.
- **Communication:** signs known, words used, what crying means at this age.
- **Triggers:** known sensory, transition, social.
- **Caregiver contact:** primary, backup, when to call vs. when to text vs. when daily report suffices.
- **Cultural:** language(s) spoken at home, holidays, food practices.
- **Special needs:** medical, developmental, allergy plan.

Schedule a 15-min weekly check-in for the first month.

### Stage 4 — Sleep at Care

- Naps may shorten or shift in the first 2–4 weeks. Normal.
- Provide familiar sleep cues if allowed (sleep sack, lovey at age-appropriate point, sound machine if center permits).
- Adjust home schedule to compensate (earlier bedtime, weekend recovery).
- If naps don't recover by week 4, request a check-in conversation.

### Stage 5 — Feeding at Care

- Bottle-feeding caregiver: deliver pumped or formula amounts that match infant's actual demand. Provider may report the bottle is gone in 5 minutes — coach paced bottle feeding.
- Older toddler: family staples that are familiar; expect appetite shifts in adjustment.
- Allergy plan written, posted, signed; epinephrine if prescribed; staff training documented.
- Caregiver feedback loop: weekly review of what was eaten / declined.

### Stage 6 — Separation Protocol

See `parenting_separation_anxiety_protocol_0_3.md`. Keep goodbye script consistent across both caregivers if applicable.

### Stage 7 — Illness Expectations

First 6–12 months in group care: 8–12+ illnesses common. URIs, ear infections, GI bugs, hand-foot-mouth, RSV, conjunctivitis. Each typically 5–10 days.

- Stock the medicine cabinet (pediatrician-recommended).
- Identify backup care for sick days (a parent stays home, or a network).
- Know the sick exclusion policy by heart.
- Don't dose with fever reducers to send to daycare (worsens contagion + child experience).
- Track for unusual patterns: recurrent ear infections may need ENT; recurrent severe wheeze may need pulmonology.

### Stage 8 — Adjustment Markers (4–6 weeks)

Track:
- Goodbye crying length (target: declining).
- Eating at care (target: returning to baseline).
- Sleeping at care (target: returning to ~80% of home naps).
- Mood at pickup (target: dysregulation tail shorter).
- Mood at home (target: returning to baseline within 1–2 hours of pickup).
- Caregiver experience (target: less dread, more confidence).

### Stage 9 — Tripwires

- 6+ weeks of consistent attendance, no improvement on adjustment markers → conversation with provider, reevaluate fit if no fixable issue.
- Safety concern → pause and investigate.
- Provider non-responsiveness to written requests → escalate.
- Child showing fear specifically of one staff member → investigate.

## Adaptations

- **Returning to work after parental leave:** Pumping logistics, milk handling protocol, work pumping schedule, freezer stash plan.
- **Solo caregiver:** Backup care plan is essential; no second adult to absorb sick days.
- **Multiples (twins/triplets):** Setting capacity for sibling group; same-classroom or split decision.
- **Preterm graduate:** Adjusted age for milestones; immune fragility may suggest delayed group-care start; pediatric coordination.
- **Allergy / medical needs:** Written plan, signed protocols, staff training, epinephrine training.
- **Multilingual:** Provider that speaks the home language preserves language exposure during peak hours.
- **Kinship grandparent care:** Same plan applies — the relationship doesn't substitute for routine, communication, and ramp.
- **Foster placement:** Trauma-informed; longer ramp; specialized provider if available.
- **Autistic / sensory profile:** Setting density, noise, transitions, predictability all matter; visit at busy times to assess.
- **Cultural eating practices:** Include menu match in fit; many providers will accommodate.

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| Recommend full-day day 1 by default | Stagger; compress only when needed and name the cost |
| Pathologize illness wave as fit failure | First 6–12 months = many illnesses; expected |
| Assume mother is the only caregiver feeling guilt | Use "caregiver"; address co-parents |
| Say "they all cry at first, ignore it" | Cry is data; track length and trend |
| Skip the setting-fit checklist | Fit is more predictive than approach |
| Default to "ratios don't matter that much" | Ratios are the strongest predictor of attention quality |
| Tell parents to medicate fevers to send to daycare | Worsens contagion and child outcome |
| Confuse a kinship caregiver with no plan needed | Same plan applies; relationship doesn't replace structure |
| Promise no illness | Illness is a feature; plan for it |
| Tell parents the child must love it by week 2 | 2–6 weeks adjustment; some children take longer |
| Ignore safety concerns to preserve placement | Investigate; do not delay |
| Treat written protocols as overkill | Written = remembered; ad hoc = forgotten |
