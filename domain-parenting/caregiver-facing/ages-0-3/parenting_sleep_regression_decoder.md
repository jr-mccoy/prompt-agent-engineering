**Purpose:** Differentiate a developmental sleep regression from environmental causes, illness, schedule mismatches, or feeding-related disruption — and respond with a plan matched to the actual cause rather than defaulting to "let them cry it out."

**When to use:** A previously-sleeping child has started waking, fighting bedtime, taking shorter naps, or refusing the crib. Caregiver suspects regression. Caregiver is exhausted and reacting.

**When NOT to use:** Sleep concerns in a never-good-sleeper (use newborn/infant sleep decoder); medical sleep disorder symptoms (apnea, persistent wakings >2 hr with distress, severe gasping) — pediatric.

---

## Clinical-Signal Triggers

- Apnea pause >20 seconds, gasping, color change, sweating that soaks sheet → pediatric/cardiac/sleep referral.
- New onset of severe head-banging during waking — observe trajectory; pediatric if persists or self-injury.
- Disruption coincident with illness (fever, ear pain, congestion, diarrhea) — treat illness first; sleep usually returns.
- Sleep disruption + persistent crying not responsive to typical comforts >3+ days, plus reduced eating/output → pediatric same-day.

---

## Core Principles

1. **Real developmental regressions exist** at ~4 months (sleep architecture matures), 8–9 months (object permanence + separation anxiety + motor leaps), 12 months (transitions + walking + nap drop), 18 months (separation surge + autonomy), and 24 months (cognitive leap + nap drop pressure).
2. **Many "regressions" are actually schedule mismatches.** Awake windows that worked at 5 months don't work at 7. Naps need adjustment regularly.
3. **The 4-month regression is permanent.** Sleep architecture matured; doesn't undo. Approach changes here are one-way.
4. **Other regressions are transient.** 2–6 weeks typically.
5. **Caregiver burnout during regression is the second crisis.** Plans must acknowledge it.

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Match the age to the most likely developmental window.
- Audit awake windows and total sleep against age expectations.
- Differentiate transient regression from permanent maturation.
- Provide a 1–4 week plan keyed to the cause.
- Address caregiver burnout explicitly.

**Must Not:**
- Default to extinction sleep training as the answer to a developmental regression.
- Push night-feed cuts in regressions for under-6-month olds.
- Promise full resolution by a specific date.
- Pathologize a regression as a "sleep training failure."

## Instructions

### Stage 1 — Match the Age to the Window

| Age | Most likely cause | What's happening |
|---|---|---|
| 3.5–4.5m | 4-month regression | Sleep cycles mature; brief awakenings every 45–60 min where one sleep stage transitions to next; child fully wakes through transitions |
| 5–6m | Schedule + nap consolidation | Naps consolidating from 4 to 3; awake windows lengthening |
| 8–10m | 8–9-month regression | Object permanence, separation anxiety, motor leaps (crawling, pulling up); cognitive disruption |
| 11–13m | 12-month nap drop (sometimes) + walking | Many parents drop second nap too early; usually 14–18m is right window |
| 17–20m | 18-month regression | Separation anxiety surge + autonomy explosion + language leap |
| 22–26m | 2-year regression | Cognitive leap; fears (dark, monsters); nap drop pressure (most kids 3–5y for full drop) |

State which window the child is in, and what's biologically happening.

### Stage 2 — Rule In/Out Other Causes

- **Illness** (fever, ear infection, congestion, GI bug): treat illness; sleep returns within 5–10 days post-illness.
- **Teething:** real for 2–4 days max; longer is something else.
- **Schedule mismatch:** check awake windows and total sleep.
- **Environmental:** room temperature, light leaks, noise, recent change.
- **Sleep crutch becoming non-functional:** rocking that worked at 4m doesn't work at 8m; intervention is revising the sleep onset, not extinction.
- **Big change** (travel, daycare start, new sibling, parent absence): expect 2–6 weeks transient.
- **Skill burst** (rolling, crawling, walking, talking): often 2–4 weeks of practice in crib at night.

### Stage 3 — Awake-Window Audit

| Age | Awake window | Naps | Total day sleep |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4m | 75–120 min | 4 → 3 | 14h |
| 6m | 2–3 hr | 3 | 13.5h |
| 9m | 2.5–3.5 hr | 2 | 13h |
| 12m | 3–4 hr | 2 → 1 | 12.5–13h |
| 15m | 3.5–4.5 hr | 2 → 1 | 12.5h |
| 18m | 4–5.5 hr | 1 | 12.5h |
| 24m | 5–6 hr | 1 | 12h |

If awake windows are off-target, fix that first; many "regressions" resolve in 5–7 days with schedule correction.

### Stage 4 — Plan by Cause

**4-month regression:**
- Permanent maturation, not a phase to wait out as-is.
- If currently rocking/feeding to sleep: hold steady for 2–3 weeks if you wish, but expect it to keep happening.
- Adjust to whatever sleep onset routine you can sustain long-term.
- Some families introduce more independent sleep onset here; some do not. Both are fine if the family is functioning.

**8–9-month regression:**
- Transient; 2–4 weeks typical.
- Object permanence: brief returns to reassure ("I'm right here"), then leave.
- Separation anxiety: predictable bedtime ritual.
- Motor practice in crib at night: skill will consolidate; settle within 1–2 weeks usually.

**12–13-month false regression:**
- Often a too-early nap drop. Try keeping 2 naps for another 4–8 weeks if possible.

**18-month regression:**
- Predictable ritual, transition object, brief responsive returns.
- Autonomy: offer choice ("which book?" "which pajamas?").
- 2–4 weeks typical resolution.

**2-year regression:**
- Predictability + reassurance; address specific fears (night light, monster spray, lovey).
- Don't drop nap unless child showing readiness over 4+ weeks (resists, sleeps OK at night without nap, early waking when nap held).

### Stage 5 — Caregiver Burnout

Regressions deplete caregivers. Plan for it:
- Two-caregiver split: alternate nights or split each night.
- Solo caregiver: nap-when-baby-naps; ask for one rescue night/week.
- Coffee + sunlight in the morning (zeitgeber for both child and caregiver).
- Avoid making major sleep approach changes when sleep-deprived; you'll regret most of them.

### Stage 6 — Tripwires

- Resolution not started in 4–6 weeks → reevaluate.
- Adjacent regressions in mood, eating, milestones → broader developmental check.
- Caregiver functional decline → support pathway.

---

## Adaptations

- **Preterm:** Adjusted age for window timing.
- **Multiples:** One regressing first, then the other; or both simultaneously. Synchronize where possible; one caregiver per child during peak.
- **Cosleeping family:** Regressions often less disruptive structurally; verify Safe Sleep Seven still met.
- **Recent return-to-work:** Daycare adjustment + regression overlap is common; double the patience window.
- **New sibling:** Regression in the older child common; predictable return of attention helps.
- **Autistic toddler:** Visual supports for night routine; predictable script.
- **Sensory profile:** Recheck environmental factors (sound, light, temperature) more carefully.

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| Default to extinction for any regression | Match plan to cause; many regressions resolve with schedule fix |
| Recommend night-feed cuts in 4-month regression for under-6m | Caloric demand still age-appropriate |
| Treat 4-month regression as transient | Permanent architecture maturation |
| Drop second nap at 12 months | Most kids hold 2 naps until 14–18m |
| Drop nap at 2y because of one bad week | 4+ weeks of consistent readiness signs needed |
| Make a major sleep approach change while sleep-deprived | Defer big decisions; stabilize caregiver first |
| Promise resolution by specific day | Provide 2–4 week window with replan tripwires |
| Treat teething as a 3-week explanation | 2–4 days max for teething disruption |
| Confuse a separation-anxiety regression with sleep training failure | Different cause, different plan |
| Tell a working parent the daycare adjustment isn't related | It often overlaps regression; double the window |
| Pathologize 8-month regression as a sleep problem | Cognitive/motor leap; transient |
| Ignore environmental factors | Light, sound, temp, room change can be the whole story |
