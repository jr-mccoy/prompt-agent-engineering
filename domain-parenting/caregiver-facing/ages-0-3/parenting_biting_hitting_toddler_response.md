**Purpose:** Decode why a 12–36-month-old is biting, hitting, kicking, or pushing — almost always a communication or regulation function — and produce an in-the-moment response, replacement-skill plan, environmental modifications, and daycare-coordinated 4-week protocol.

**When to use:** Recurring biting/hitting at home, daycare reports, sibling aggression, post-new-baby aggression, autistic or speech-delayed toddler with limited communication, daycare threatening dismissal.

**When NOT to use:** Aggression that draws blood from a victim repeatedly with apparent absence of distress and seems calculated → more thorough developmental + mental health workup; aggression in trauma context → trauma-informed pathway.

---

## Clinical-Signal Triggers

- Biting/hitting that is severe, frequent, escalating, and unresponsive to consistent intervention over 4–6 weeks → developmental and behavioral assessment.
- Self-injurious aggression (severe head-banging, self-biting that breaks skin) → pediatric same-week.
- Aggression with regression in language or social skills → urgent developmental assessment.
- Caregiver-targeted aggression with calculated planning in a 4+ year old → mental health.
- Caregiver responses including hitting/biting back, yelling close to face, physical punishment → caregiver support.

---

## Core Principles

1. **Toddler aggression is almost always communicative.** "I want," "I'm overwhelmed," "I need space," "I can't get the words out," "you took it."
2. **Frustration > theory of mind.** A 2-year-old does not yet fully understand that biting hurts. The "see, that hurts" lesson lands at 3–4y reliably, not earlier.
3. **Biting peaks at 18–30 months.** Hitting and pushing peak at similar window. Most resolves by 3–3.5y.
4. **Co-occurrence with speech delay is common.** Less language → more physical communication. Address both.
5. **Caregiver match-back (biting back, hitting back) does not teach.** It models, terrifies, and increases aggression.
6. **Dispassionate, predictable, consistent response > emotional, escalating response.**

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Run a function analysis (ABC: Antecedent → Behavior → Consequence).
- Provide an in-the-moment script.
- Provide a 4-week reduction protocol.
- Provide replacement-skill teaching plan.
- Address daycare consistency.
- Address caregiver flooding/anger.

**Must Not:**
- Recommend biting back, hitting back, or any retaliatory modeling.
- Recommend punishment for under-3s as primary intervention.
- Recommend removing the child from daycare without alternatives unless safety threshold reached.
- Pathologize 18-month-old biting in a developmentally typical curve.
- Promise full extinction in a specific window.

## Instructions

### Stage 1 — ABC Function Analysis

For 3–5 recent incidents:

| Antecedent (10–60 seconds before) | Behavior | Consequence (immediate response + outcome) |
|---|---|---|
| Tired, peer took toy | Bit peer's arm | Peer cried, caregiver picked up child, removed from area |
| Hungry, told to wait | Hit caregiver | Caregiver yelled, child taken to crib, stayed alone |
| Daycare end of day, sibling birth this week | Hit sibling | Caregiver scooped sibling, child cried |

Most likely function:
- **Communication** (no/I want/that's mine/move): teach signs, words, choice cards.
- **Regulation overflow** (overstimulated, tired, hungry): prevent + co-regulate.
- **Sensory seeking** (seeking deep input, oral input): provide alternatives.
- **Escape** (being asked to do something): adjust demands, transition warnings.
- **Attention** (caregiver was on phone, sibling getting attention): preventive connection.

### Stage 2 — In-the-Moment Script

When biting/hitting happens:

1. **Stop the contact safely.** Move yourself, the bitten/hit person, or the child. "I won't let you bite. Teeth are not for biting."
2. **Tend to the victim first** for 30–60 sec. The victim getting attention reduces attention-seeking aggression and teaches a relational moral.
3. **Brief words to the aggressor — short, unemotional.** "Biting hurts. Teeth are for food."
4. **Offer the replacement.** "If you're mad, you can stomp / squeeze / say 'stop' / sign 'help'."
5. **Stay near.** Do not banish to crib/room as primary response (under 2.5–3y).
6. **Do not yell into face, do not lecture, do not bite back.**
7. **Move on.** Brief intervention; do not extend.

### Stage 3 — Replacement Skills (Teach Daily, Outside Conflict)

Teach these in calm moments, with practice, over 4 weeks:

- **For "no/that's mine"**: sign for "stop," "no," "mine," word for "move."
- **For "I want": **sign for "more," "help," picture cards.
- **For frustration**: stomp, push wall, blow out candles, big breath, run to caregiver.
- **For overload**: hand on chest signal, withdrawal to a quiet corner, "all done" sign.

Roleplay these. Reinforce when used naturally.

### Stage 4 — Antecedent Modifications (Where Most Change Comes From)

Map the top 2–3 antecedents and modify:

- Hungry → snack-and-water station before triggering windows.
- Tired → earlier nap, earlier bedtime.
- Peer over-proximity → reduce setting density, parallel-play setup.
- Sibling reaction → 10–15 min/day exclusive caregiver-toddler time.
- New sibling jealousy → predictable returns to attention; older child not chronically deferred to baby.
- Daycare end-of-day → caregiver pickup script with 5-min decompression before demands.

### Stage 5 — 4-Week Reduction Protocol

**Week 1:** Antecedent tracking, in-the-moment script consistent, victim-first attention.
**Week 2:** Two new replacement skills taught daily; primary antecedent modified.
**Week 3:** Daycare/home consistency check; same script in both settings.
**Week 4:** Reduction expectation: most incidents drop 30–60% with consistent intervention; complete resolution typically 2–4 months.

### Stage 6 — Daycare Coordination

- Send a one-page plan: antecedents, replacement signals, in-the-moment words, what *not* to do (no time-out chair for under-2.5–3, no biting back).
- Ask daycare to log incidents in same ABC format.
- Avoid public shaming of the child at pickup ("She bit again today" said in front of toddler).
- If daycare is dismissing, request a meeting with director, classroom teacher, and you to align approach. Many cases stabilize within 4 weeks of joint plan.

### Stage 7 — Caregiver Capacity

If you are flooding/yelling/wanting to bite back:
- Step away when safe; hand off if a co-caregiver is present.
- Brief reset (60–90 seconds breath).
- See `parenting_parent_coregulation_reset.md`.
- Repair if you've yelled or grabbed too hard.

### Stage 8 — When to Escalate

- 4 weeks of consistent intervention, no reduction, severity increasing → developmental check + behavior support.
- Targeted aggression with planning in a 4+y child → mental health.
- Caregiver-targeted with anger pattern in older child → mental health.

## Adaptations

- **Speech-delayed toddler:** Maximize signs, picture cards, low-tech AAC; SLP referral.
- **Autistic toddler:** Sensory regulation often resolves more aggression than communication-only intervention; visual schedule + sensory plan.
- **Sensory seeker:** Proprioceptive input (heavy work, chewy tubes for oral seekers) provides what aggression was meeting.
- **New sibling jealousy:** Exclusive time with displaced child; do not narrate every moment in terms of the baby.
- **Daycare dismissal threat:** One-page plan, specific timeline, parent–director meeting. Most stabilize within 4 weeks of joint plan.
- **Solo caregiver, no respite:** Reduce ambition; one antecedent fix at a time; ask for one rescue session per week.
- **Trauma context (recent move, loss, frightening event):** Trauma-informed; aggression often regulates after stability returns.

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| Bite back to "show how it feels" | Models aggression; child too young to draw the moral |
| Recommend a 5-minute time-out chair for an 18-month-old | Stay near, brief words, replacement; under-3 cannot self-regulate alone |
| Pathologize biting at 18m as a behavior disorder | Within typical curve; assess function and reduce |
| Lecture for 2 minutes after each incident | Brief words; long lectures reinforce attention function |
| Punish without teaching replacement | Replacement skill is the change agent |
| Yell into the face of a biter | Increases dysregulation |
| Skip the victim attention | Victim-first changes the social contingency |
| Tell daycare to manage alone | Joint plan, same script, both settings |
| Confuse autistic regulation overflow with peer aggression | Sensory regulation is often the lever |
| Leave caregiver flooding unaddressed | Caregiver regulation predicts response quality |
| Promise full extinction in 1 week | 30–60% reduction in 4 weeks is realistic; full resolution in months |
| Recommend public shaming at pickup | Toddler learns the parent + teacher are unsafe; aggression often increases |
