**Purpose:** Replace calendar-driven toilet training with readiness-driven training; choose a method that fits the child and family; build a realistic 4–8 week plan with explicit troubleshooting for the three biggest derailers (constipation, refusal, regression).

**When to use:** Considering whether to start, child or daycare deadline pressure, sibling has trained, second-time-around with a different child, training stalling or regressing, transition between caregivers/settings, autism or sensory child being trained.

**When NOT to use:** Encopresis (chronic withholding with overflow soiling) → pediatrician/GI; medical urinary issues (UTI signs, painful urination, daytime urinary frequency); developmental concerns affecting body awareness — coordinate with pediatric care.

---

## Clinical-Signal Triggers

- Pain on urination, blood, fever → UTI workup, today.
- Withholding stool >2 days repeatedly + hard painful stools + soiling between → constipation/encopresis evaluation.
- No interest, no signs by 4y, especially with other developmental concerns → developmental check.
- Sudden regression with new pain, fever, behavior change → medical workup.
- Trauma history (medical, abuse, frightening event) tied to toileting → trauma-informed approach + mental health support.

---

## Core Principles

1. **Readiness is a constellation, not a date.** Most children show readiness 22–30 months; some not until 36+. Pushing earlier rarely speeds completion.
2. **Constipation is the #1 derailer.** A child who has been hurt by a hard stool will hold; held stools become harder; cycle. Treat constipation first or in parallel.
3. **Power struggles end training.** Toileting is one of three things a child fully controls (eating, sleeping, eliminating). Coercion creates retention.
4. **Daytime first; nighttime is later (and partly genetic).** Nighttime dryness depends on bladder capacity and overnight ADH production, often not until 5–7+.
5. **Setbacks are normal, not failure.** Illness, travel, stress, new sibling commonly cause regression. Plans must include regression handling.
6. **Boy/girl timing differences are small and overstated.** Individual variation dwarfs average sex differences.

## Instructions

### Stage 1 — Readiness Audit

A child is generally ready when ≥6 of the 10 signs are present and bowel pattern is stable.

- 0–4 signs: not yet. Recheck monthly.
- 5–6 signs: borderline. Build skills (interest visits to toilet, pulling pants, language for body) for 4–8 weeks; recheck.
- 7–10 signs: ready. Choose approach.

### Stage 2 — Method Selection (Tradeoffs)

| Approach | Description | Best for | Tradeoffs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Parent-led / scheduled (gradual)** | Caregiver offers toilet at intervals (after waking, post-meal, before sleep). 4–8 weeks typical. | Most families; child uncertain about full readiness | Slow, predictable, less intense; requires consistency |
| **Child-led** | Wait until child requests it / leads. Often 30–42 months. | Strong-willed children, families with no time pressure | Latest start; least power struggle |
| **Intensive 3-day (Oh Crap, similar)** | Switch to underwear/naked, intensive 3-day caregiver presence, structured. | Families with concentrated availability and a ready child | High intensity; works best with high readiness; can backfire if pushed too early |
| **Reward-led (chart, candy)** | Tangible rewards for production. | Some children respond | Risk of internal motivation crowd-out; can become a power-struggle currency |
| **Elimination communication–continuation** | Family who has cued from infancy; final transition often easier | Specific cultural/family practice | Requires earlier groundwork |

### Stage 3 — 4–8 Week Plan (Parent-Led, Adjust for Method)

**Week 1 — Underwear during waking hours, diaper for nap/night.**
- Caregiver-prompted toilet visits every 60–90 min, after waking, after meals.
- Praise specific, calm: "You used the toilet. Your body did the work."
- Accidents: "Your body is learning. Let's clean up. Underwear on, try again."
- Track on simple chart.

**Week 2 — Extend intervals to 90–120 min; respond more to child cues.**
- Begin "I need to go" practice — caregiver pauses, child decides.
- Travel-friendly: small folding seat for outings.

**Weeks 3–4 — Child initiates increasingly.**
- Daycare consistency: same approach, same words, same response to accidents.
- Bowel movements often lag bladder by 2–6 weeks.

**Weeks 5–6 — Daytime largely independent.**
- Drop scheduled prompts in favor of cue-based.
- Drop Pull-Ups during waking hours if used as transition.

**Weeks 7–8 — Settle daytime; consider nap.**
- Many children drop nap diaper around the same time daytime stabilizes.
- Nighttime: separate. Most children dry consistently overnight 4–7y; not before 4 unless natural.

### Stage 4 — Constipation Plan (Often the Root Cause of Stalling)

If the child has hard stools, withholds, or has had a painful experience:

- Pediatrician evaluation; many recommend MiraLAX (PEG 3350) per pediatric dosing for soft daily stools for several weeks.
- High-water diet, fiber-rich foods, prune juice or pear juice if pediatrician approves.
- Toilet sit time after meals (gastrocolic reflex aids).
- Footstool to allow squat-position bowel mechanics.
- Reassurance: "Soft poop is the goal. Soft poop doesn't hurt."

Toilet training a constipated child without addressing constipation first will fail.

### Stage 5 — Refusal Response

If the child refuses to use the toilet:

- Stop. Pause for 1–4 weeks. Return to diapers without shame.
- Frame: "Your body isn't ready yet. We'll try again later."
- Avoid: bribes, threats, rewards escalation, daycare dropoff conflict.
- Investigate: constipation? Recent change? Frightening flush? Falling in? Public-toilet phobia? Sensory aversion?
- Restart later with adjusted approach.

### Stage 6 — Regression Response

Common triggers: new sibling, move, illness, daycare change, parent stress, developmental burst (verbal explosion), sleep disruption.

- Don't restart from scratch.
- Frame: "Your body is just remembering. Let's slow down together."
- Reduce demands elsewhere; consolidate the routine.
- 2–6 weeks of recovery is typical.

### Stage 7 — Nighttime

- Daytime trained ≠ nighttime ready.
- Many children continue nighttime diaper for months to years.
- 5–7y is typical for most children to be reliably dry.
- Nighttime wetting in 6+ year old child = enuresis; pediatrician.
- Limit fluids 1–2 hours pre-bed; final pee at lights out; bladder maturation does the rest.
- Genetic — caregiver who wet the bed past 6 should expect similar in their child.

## Adaptations

- **Autism / ASD Level 1:** Visual schedule (use, flush, hands), social story, predictable script, sensory check (noisy flush, cold seat, paper texture). Allow longer ramp. Special interests can motivate.
- **ADHD:** Body-awareness training; scheduled prompts longer; transition warnings before potty visits.
- **Sensory aversive:** Toilet inserts, music, dim lights, weighted lap pad. Identify which sensory feature is hard and modify.
- **Speech delay:** Picture cards, sign language for "potty," "wet," "all done."
- **Hypotonia / motor delay:** Footstool essential; longer skill build; OT consult if persistent.
- **Strong-willed:** Maximally child-led timing. Avoid public power struggle. Privacy + autonomy.
- **Twins / multiples:** Stagger if possible; avoid comparison talk.
- **Daycare / split households:** Written plan shared; same words, same response, same intervals.
- **Trauma context:** Trauma-informed approach; coordinate with mental health if relevant.

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| Push training at 18m by calendar | Use readiness signs |
| Train through constipation | Treat constipation first or in parallel |
| Punish or shame accidents | Frame as practice; clean up matter-of-factly |
| Withhold attention as consequence | Stay neutral and warm |
| Conflate daytime and nighttime | Separate processes; nighttime often years later |
| Restart from scratch after regression | Resume and slow; treat as recovery |
| Promise 3 days = trained | 3-day method works for some; full process is weeks |
| Use big rewards as primary motivator | Internal + low-key acknowledgment; rewards can backfire |
| Force a child onto the toilet | Coercion guarantees retention |
| Tell parent it's a power struggle to win | Reframe — this is a battle no parent wins |
| Pathologize an autistic 4-year-old not yet trained | Adapt approach; many autistic children train 3–6+ |
| Skip footstool and squat physiology | Mechanical advantage matters |
| Diagnose enuresis at 4 | Most children dry by 5–7; eval at 6+ |
