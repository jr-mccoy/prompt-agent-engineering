**Purpose:** Build a realistic, values-aware screen-time plan for a child under 3, distinguishing types of screen exposure with different evidence profiles, addressing the most common situations where rigid rules fail (work-from-home, solo caregiver, multi-child households, travel, illness), and producing concrete defaults rather than guilt.

**When to use:** Caregiver wondering about screen use; child currently has more screens than caregiver wants; pediatrician or family member raised concerns; specific event approaching (long flight, illness recovery, sibling birth).

**When NOT to use:** Suspected screen-related sensory or sleep dysregulation needing pediatric eval; suspected speech/language impact (use speech check prompt).

---

## Clinical-Signal Triggers

- Speech/language delay with high passive-screen exposure → reduce, refer.
- Sleep dysregulation with evening-screen use → eliminate within 1 hour of bed.
- Significant tantrum at screen-off → address as transition + dependence pattern; stepdown.
- Caregiver exhaustion making screen-as-only-coping → caregiver support.

---

## Core Principles

1. **AAP recommends no media (other than video chat) under 18 months.** From 18–24 months, only high-quality co-viewed content. From 2–5 years, ≤1 hour/day of co-viewed high-quality content.
2. **Not all screen time is equivalent.** Video chat with grandparents is high-relational. Passive background TV is the lowest-quality. Co-viewed children's content sits in the middle.
3. **The strongest negative associations** are with passive background TV (associated with reduced parent-child talk) and solo high-stimulation device use (associated with attention regulation issues).
4. **Real-world caregiver constraints are real.** Solo caregiver making dinner, illness, multi-child household, travel — sometimes a screen is the right answer.
5. **The strongest positive intervention is co-viewing and co-talking** ("Look! That's a tractor. We have a tractor book!"), turning passive viewing into shared language exposure.
6. **Guilt-driven rules collapse.** Workable rules survive.

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Differentiate screen types by evidence profile.
- Acknowledge real-world caregiver constraints.
- Provide a default plan + a stretched-capacity plan.
- Address pre-sleep window separately.
- Surface co-viewing as the strongest positive lever.
- Avoid pure prohibition without alternatives.

**Must Not:**
- Pretend zero screens is realistic for every family.
- Default to shame language.
- Recommend educational content as a substitute for caregiver interaction.
- Dismiss video chat with family as "screen time" in the negative sense.
- Treat all screen exposure as harmful equally.

## Instructions

### Stage 1 — Categorize Current Use

| Type | Evidence profile | Default position |
|---|---|---|
| Video chat with familiar people | Relational; AAP exception | OK at any age |
| Caregiver-co-viewed content | Mixed; quality + interaction matter | OK from 18m, ≤1 hr/day |
| Solo viewing of children's content | Modest negative associations under 2; mixed 2+ | Avoid under 18m; minimal 18–24m; <1 hr 2–5y |
| Background passive TV | Reduces caregiver-child talk; associated with attention/language outcomes | Eliminate as a pattern |
| Solo high-stimulation device games | Attention regulation concerns | Avoid under 3 |

### Stage 2 — Default Plan (Adjusted for Constraints)

**Under 18 months:**
- Yes: video chat with family; brief educational app for joint attention practice if desired.
- No: solo content, background TV, device handover.
- If caregiver needs to make dinner / shower / handle a sibling crisis: 5–15 min of co-viewed content is not catastrophic.

**18–24 months:**
- Yes: video chat; co-viewed children's content; one 20–30 min episode of high-quality content with caregiver narration.
- Limit: solo screen use.
- No: background TV running while child plays.

**24–36 months:**
- ≤1 hour/day of high-quality, mostly co-viewed content.
- Avoid 1 hour pre-sleep.
- Background TV: turn off when no one is watching.
- Devices: borrowed not handed-over (caregiver controls).

### Stage 3 — Stretched-Capacity Plan (When Default Doesn't Fit)

**Solo caregiver / WFH / postpartum:**
- 30–60 min/day of co-viewed content during work-required windows is a reasonable tradeoff.
- Prefer same content series (predictability, recall, language exposure) over algorithm-driven feeds.
- Compensate with non-screen connection time.

**Travel / long flight:**
- Suspend the rule for the trip. Resume at home.
- Mix screens with non-screen activities (stickers, books, snack rituals).

**Illness:**
- Sick days have higher screen exposure; that's fine.
- Resume default when well.

**Multi-child:**
- Older child's screen ≠ younger child's screen. Try to keep younger out of older child's content (background exposure).

### Stage 4 — Pre-Sleep Window

- 1 hour pre-sleep: screen-free.
- Blue-light/melatonin effects are debated; the larger issue is content arousal and routine displacement.
- Books, calming music, simple play.

### Stage 5 — Content Quality

If using:
- Slow-paced, narrative-driven, repetitive (helps language uptake).
- Songs, simple stories.
- Programs designed with developmental input (Daniel Tiger, Bluey, Sesame Street, Mister Rogers, Tumble Leaf).
- Avoid: high-stimulation, fast-cut, ad-driven, algorithm autoplay (YouTube Kids without controls), unboxing channels.

### Stage 6 — Caregiver Co-Viewing Strategies

When viewing together:
- Narrate and label.
- Ask simple questions ("Where's the dog?").
- Connect to real life ("We have apples too!").
- Pause and discuss.
- Mute and narrate yourself.

This converts passive screen time into shared language exposure.

### Stage 7 — Stepdown if Currently Over Default

- Track current daily exposure for 3–5 days (honest).
- Set a 30-min/day reduction target per week.
- Substitute: outdoor time, music, pretend play, water table, sensory bin, rotating toy bins.
- Manage the transition tantrum: predictable warning, replacement activity, brief comfort.
- 4 weeks to typical stable plan.

### Stage 8 — Self-Compassion

- Caregiver who runs Sesame Street while doing dishes is not failing.
- Caregiver who hands a phone to a 4-month-old to watch shorts is making a different choice. Both can shift.
- Many of the most rigid screen rules collapse under postpartum reality. A workable rule is one you can keep.

---

## Adaptations

- **Speech-delayed toddler:** Reduce passive screen, increase co-viewed and live language; not a primary intervention but a reduction of competing input.
- **Autistic toddler:** Many find specific shows highly regulating; can be a sensory regulator. Work with strengths; protect transitions and pre-sleep.
- **Sensory-overloaded child:** High-stim content makes it worse; slow-paced helps.
- **Solo caregiver:** Default plan flexes; survival is the floor.
- **Bilingual household:** Use screen content in the less-dominant language for exposure boost.
- **Travel-heavy family:** Set "travel rules" and "home rules" explicitly; reduce reentry friction.
- **Postpartum / older sibling:** Older sibling's screen use becomes infant's exposure; manage proximity.

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| Insist on zero screens for every family | Acknowledge constraints; provide stretched-capacity plan |
| Treat all screen time as equivalent | Differentiate video chat / co-viewed / solo / background |
| Recommend educational app as substitute for caregiver interaction | Live talk and book reading > any app for language |
| Frame video chat with grandparents as harmful | AAP exception; relational |
| Default to shame | Workable rule survives; rigid rule collapses |
| Recommend high-stim algorithm-driven content | Slow-paced, narrative, repetitive content for language |
| Ignore pre-sleep window | Eliminate within 1 hour of bed |
| Recommend headphones for an infant | Audio exposure protection still matters |
| Treat solo caregiver constraints as moral failure | Survival floor is real |
| Forget background TV reduces caregiver-child talk | Turn off when no one is watching |
| Recommend reward charts for screen reduction in toddlers | Substitute activities + transition warnings, not reward systems |
| Promise speech recovery from screen reduction alone | Reduction is one variable; live talk is the active ingredient |
