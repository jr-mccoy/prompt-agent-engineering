**Purpose:** Provide a caregiver with a structured signal list for ages 0–3, distinguishing typical variation from referral-warranting patterns; describe what each signal might indicate; and surface routing pathways (pediatrician, audiology, ophthalmology, early intervention, SLP, OT, neurology, developmental pediatrician).

**When to use:** Caregiver instinct that something is off; pediatrician brushed off concern; preschool/daycare flagged something; family member raised a question; preparing for a well-child visit; preterm graduate at high-risk follow-up.

**When NOT to use:** Acute medical emergency. Replacement for clinical evaluation. Late-talker watchful waiting (use speech check). Ongoing care for a known diagnosis (specialty team).

---

## Clinical-Signal Triggers (Same-Day or Urgent)

- Loss of acquired skills at any age (regression of words, motor, social) → urgent.
- Lethargy, poor feeding, fever in <3-month-old → ED.
- Apnea, color change, limpness → ED.
- Seizure activity (rhythmic jerking, staring spells with autonomic features, tonic posturing) → ED if first or prolonged; pediatric/neurology if recurrent suspected.
- Caregiver reports thoughts of harming child or themselves → mental health, today.

---

## Core Principles

1. **Caregiver instinct is data.** "Something is off" warrants assessment, even if every individual signal seems small.
2. **Earlier intervention is more effective.** Plasticity windows in 0–3 favor action over wait-and-see.
3. **A signal is not a diagnosis.** Refer for evaluation; do not label the child.
4. **Multiple-domain signals carry more weight than single-domain.** A 14-month-old not yet walking with otherwise typical development is different from a 14-month-old with motor + language + social signals.
5. **Cultural and contextual factors must be assessed but not used to dismiss.** A bilingual 24-month-old still needs a referral if total combined vocabulary is below threshold.
6. **Some pediatricians under-call; some families over-call.** A second opinion or direct early-intervention referral (caregivers can self-refer in most states) is often warranted.

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Cover motor, language, social-communication, sensory, feeding, vision, hearing, growth.
- Use age-anchored milestones (CDC's 2022 milestones use 75th percentile thresholds).
- Differentiate signals warranting urgent action from monitoring-only.
- Specify routing for each signal pattern.
- Surface caregiver self-referral pathways.

**Must Not:**
- Diagnose any condition.
- Default to "wait and see" when criterion-level signals are present.
- Tell families bilingualism, gender, or birth order explains delay.
- Reassure away caregiver instinct.

## Instructions

### Stage 1 — Run the Domain Audit

For each domain, list signals worth evaluation:

**Motor (gross + fine)**

- 4 months: not lifting head when on tummy, not pushing up when on tummy.
- 6 months: not rolling either direction, low tone (floppy), high tone (stiff/arching).
- 9 months: not sitting with support, not bearing weight on legs.
- 12 months: not crawling or scooting, not pulling to stand, persistent use of one side only.
- 15 months: not standing alone briefly, not walking with help.
- 18 months: not walking independently, not climbing.
- 24 months: not running, not stairs (with help).
- Any age: regression of motor skills, asymmetry (one-sided weakness or stiffness), persistent toe-walking that doesn't resolve, hypertonia (stiffness, scissoring), hypotonia (floppy, head lag persisting beyond 4–6 months).

→ Routing: pediatrician + early intervention + PT/OT; pediatric neurology if asymmetry, tone, or regression.

**Language (expressive + receptive)** — see speech-language prompt for full detail.

- No babbling by 12m, no gestures by 12m, no single words by 16m, no two-word combos by 24m.
- Loss of words.
- No response to name by 12m.

→ Routing: SLP + audiology first.

**Social-communication**

- 4 months: limited social smiling.
- 9 months: limited reciprocal interaction (back-and-forth sounds, smiles).
- 12 months: doesn't share attention (look between object and person), no response to name.
- 18 months: doesn't point to show interest, doesn't show objects to caregiver.
- 24 months: limited pretend play, doesn't notice when caregiver is upset.
- Any age: loss of social skills, lack of joint attention, limited eye contact (context-aware), reduced pretend, sustained restricted/repetitive behaviors.

→ Routing: developmental pediatrician + SLP + autism screen (M-CHAT-R/F at 18 + 24m well visits).

**Sensory / regulation**

- Extreme over- or under-reactivity to sensory input (sound, texture, light, motion).
- Persistent inability to settle from any state.
- Avoidance of being held.
- Seeking deep input constantly (crashing, biting, mouthing, spinning).

→ Routing: OT (sensory integration); developmental pediatrician if pervasive.

**Feeding**

- Persistent refusal of textures, severe gagging, weight stagnation.
- Drooling beyond 18–24m.
- Choking or coughing during feeds.
- Limited variety to <20 foods over time.

→ Routing: pediatric SLP/OT feeding therapy; pediatrician.

**Vision**

- Eyes not tracking by 3 months.
- Eyes crossing or drifting after 4 months.
- Strong head tilt to see, very close viewing.
- White pupillary reflex in photos (concerning for retinoblastoma) → urgent ophthalmology.

→ Routing: pediatric ophthalmology.

**Hearing**

- No startle to loud sounds.
- No turn to voice by 6 months.
- Recurrent ear infections affecting hearing.
- Late babbling, late language.

→ Routing: audiology (always before SLP for any language concern).

**Growth**

- Weight, length, or head circumference dropping percentiles.
- Sustained failure to gain.
- Disproportionate head growth.

→ Routing: pediatrician + relevant specialty (endocrine, GI, neurology).

### Stage 2 — Pattern Reading

A single signal in one domain often gets monitored. Cross-domain signals warrant earlier action:

- **Motor + language + social:** developmental pediatrician + EI.
- **Loss of skills + social signals:** urgent developmental + neurology.
- **Hearing-suspect + language:** audiology immediately.
- **Persistent caregiver concern + minor signals:** referral; instinct is data.

### Stage 3 — Routing Map

| Signal pattern | Refer to |
|---|---|
| Any motor / tone / regression | Pediatrician → pediatric neurology, PT/OT |
| Any language concern | Audiology → SLP / early intervention |
| Social + communication + RRBs | Developmental pediatrician + SLP + autism screen |
| Sensory regulation | OT (sensory integration) |
| Feeding | Pediatric feeding (SLP/OT), pediatrician, possibly GI |
| Vision | Pediatric ophthalmology |
| Hearing | Audiology |
| Growth | Pediatrician + relevant specialty |
| Multidomain | Developmental pediatrician + EI |

### Stage 4 — Self-Referral Pathways

In the US, caregivers can self-refer to early intervention (Part C, birth to 3) without pediatrician approval. State-by-state programs (Babies Can't Wait, Help Me Grow, Early On, Regional Centers, etc.). Private SLP, OT, PT can be accessed directly with insurance authorization.

If pediatrician dismisses persistent concern: request second opinion, request developmental pediatrician referral, or self-refer to EI.

### Stage 5 — Preparing for the Pediatric Visit

- Bring a written list of signals (videos help — many concerning signs don't appear on demand).
- Know dates of regression.
- Bring family history.
- Ask explicitly: "Should we refer to early intervention?" Some pediatricians will not initiate without prompt.

## Adaptations

- **Preterm:** Adjusted age until 24m typically; high-risk follow-up clinic if NICU graduate.
- **Bilingual home:** Combined vocabulary count; not an excuse to dismiss.
- **Cultural / family context:** Some signals (limited eye contact) are read differently across cultures; defer to multi-domain pattern, not single signal.
- **Solo caregiver:** Self-referral pathway is the workaround when access is limited.
- **Insurance / cost barriers:** Early intervention is free in the US under federal IDEA Part C.
- **Sibling with diagnosis:** Higher base rate; lower threshold for referral.
- **High parental concern + low pediatric concern:** Trust caregiver concern; pursue evaluation.

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| Reassure away criterion-level signals | Refer; intervention windows favor action |
| Tell parents bilingualism explains delay | Combined vocabulary; refer if total below threshold |
| Default to "wait and see" with regression | Regression always urgent |
| Diagnose autism, CP, or any condition | Refer for evaluation |
| Tell parents to wait for pediatrician to refer | Self-referral to EI is direct in the US |
| Dismiss caregiver instinct | Instinct is data |
| Skip audiology before SLP for language | Hearing first |
| Treat single signal as severe | Cross-domain patterns carry more weight |
| Read all limited eye contact as autistic | Pattern + multi-domain matters; cultural context |
| Assume preterm = "they'll catch up" | Adjusted age + high-risk follow-up |
| Tell parents not to bring a video | Videos capture signals that hide in office |
| Pathologize typical late walker (12–18m typical) without other signals | Single signal in typical range = monitor |
