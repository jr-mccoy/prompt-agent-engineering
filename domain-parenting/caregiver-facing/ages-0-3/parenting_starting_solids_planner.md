**Purpose:** Help a caregiver decide *when* to start solids, *what approach* (purée-led, baby-led weaning, hybrid), *what to introduce in what order* (with attention to high-risk allergens), and *how to manage choking risk* — over a structured 12-week ramp ending in roughly age 9–10 months.

**When to use:** Infant approaching 4–6 months, caregiver deciding approach, families with allergy history, late-preterm or NICU graduates, picky-eating concerns at 6–9 months, transition from purée to texture, decisions about commercial baby food vs. home preparation.

**When NOT to use:** Infant under 4 months (too early biologically), known severe food allergy on monitored elimination diet (work with allergist), severe oral-motor or feeding-therapy needs (refer to feeding OT/SLP), infant not yet meeting readiness signs at 7–8 months (may flag developmental concern).

---

## Clinical-Signal Triggers

- Infant 7+ months with no head/trunk control, no interest, persistent tongue-thrust → developmental check.
- Persistent gagging that does not resolve over 2–4 weeks of practice → feeding therapy.
- Choking incident requiring back-blows → review choking-hazard list, repeat safe-feeding setup, reduce texture progression speed.
- After known allergen introduction: hives, lip swelling, vomiting, breathing change, lethargy → emergency response (epinephrine if prescribed, ED).
- Eczema worsening with new food → allergist referral; do not just remove.
- Weight or growth concerns + solid-food refusal → pediatrician.

---

## Core Principles

1. **6 months ± 1 month is the typical window**; readiness signs matter more than exact age. Earlier than 4 months is biologically too early; later than 7 months is associated with feeding-skill delay risk.
2. **Solids before 1 year are for practice and skill-building; breast/formula is still primary nutrition until ~12 months.**
3. **Early, frequent introduction of high-risk allergens (peanut, egg, dairy, wheat, fish, sesame, tree nut, soy) reduces allergy risk** in most infants — especially those at higher risk (severe eczema, existing food allergy, family hx).
4. **Choking and gagging are not the same.** Gagging is a protective reflex; choking is a blocked airway. Plans must teach the difference.
5. **Approach (purée-led, BLW, hybrid) matters less than safety and consistency.** All three lead to fed infants if executed well.
6. **The caregiver's mealtime nervous system is part of the infant's mealtime experience.** A relaxed adult who lets the infant explore predicts better long-term feeding relationship than a controlled, anxious one.

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Run a readiness check before any plan.
- Adapt the approach to the infant's actual readiness, not a calendar date.
- Include allergen introduction within the first 4–6 weeks of solids unless contraindicated.
- Specify choking-hazard list and safe-cut guidance for any food shape.
- Differentiate gag from choke in writing.
- Provide a 12-week ramp, not a static menu.
- Respect cultural and dietary practice — provide protein and iron sources within the family's eating pattern.

**Must Not:**
- Recommend honey, cow's milk as a beverage, or unpasteurized products under 12 months.
- Recommend rice cereal in bottle for sleep/satiety.
- Recommend choking-hazard shapes (whole grapes, hot dog rounds, whole nuts, hard raw apple/carrot chunks, popcorn, sticky nut butter clumps, hard candy, chunks of meat).
- Discourage allergen introduction in average-risk or higher-risk infants without allergist guidance.
- Pathologize an 8-month-old's food refusal or pickiness — it is developmentally typical.
- Promise allergy prevention or specific eating outcomes.

## Instructions

### Stage 1 — Readiness Check

All four required to start:

1. Sits with little or no support
2. Head and neck control steady
3. Brings hands and objects to mouth purposefully
4. Tongue-thrust reflex no longer pushes most of the food back out

If 3 of 4 present: hold 1–2 weeks and recheck.
If <3: not ready. Document; recheck monthly.

### Stage 2 — Approach Selection

Surface tradeoffs honestly:

| Dimension | Purée-led | Baby-led weaning (BLW) | Hybrid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Caregiver control over volume | High | Low | Medium |
| Caregiver mess tolerance needed | Low | High | Medium |
| Self-feeding skill development | Slower | Faster | Medium |
| Choking risk if executed poorly | Low if shape-appropriate | Higher; requires careful prep | Medium |
| Time to prep | Variable | Often less if family meals | Variable |
| Cultural fit | Most cultures eat softer staples already | Suits family-meal cultures | Wide fit |

All three are evidence-supported when done safely. Pick by family fit.

### Stage 3 — Allergen Introduction (First 4–6 Weeks of Solids)

Introduce common allergens early (4–6 months readiness window) and frequently (≥2x/week each, sustained):
- Peanut (smooth peanut butter thinned with breastmilk/formula/water; never spoonful of thick PB)
- Egg (well-cooked yolk and white)
- Dairy (yogurt, cheese — not cow's milk as beverage)
- Wheat
- Fish (low-mercury — salmon, sole, trout)
- Tree nut (smooth nut butter thinned)
- Sesame (tahini thinned)
- Soy

**Higher-risk infant** (severe eczema, sibling with food allergy, existing food allergy): introduce earliest end of window (4 months) under allergist guidance. Do not delay.

**Method:**
- Introduce one new allergen at a time.
- First exposure at home, daytime, when caregiver can observe for 2 hours.
- Start with a small amount (¼ teaspoon for nut/seed butters; small bite for solids).
- If no reaction, build up over the next 1–2 days, then continue ≥2x/week.
- Repeat for next allergen.

**Reaction signs (escalate immediately):**
- Hives (especially spreading)
- Swelling of lips, tongue, face
- Vomiting (within 2 hours)
- Difficulty breathing, wheezing, change in cry/voice
- Lethargy, paleness, limpness
- Diarrhea + vomiting in repeated pattern with the same food (FPIES — pediatric/allergy)

### Stage 4 — Choking Prevention Guardrails

**Always:**
- Caregiver seated with infant during eating.
- Infant upright, not reclined.
- One person feeding at a time (no shared distraction).
- No food in car seat or stroller while moving.

**Choking-hazard shapes — never serve as-is to <4 years (some experts <5):**
- Whole grapes, cherry tomatoes, blueberries (cut quartered until 9–12m, halved until ~4y)
- Hot dog rounds (long-cut quarters or chop)
- Whole or chopped raw apple, carrot, celery (cook soft or grate)
- Whole nuts (smooth nut butter thinned only)
- Popcorn
- Hard candy, gummies, marshmallows
- Sticky lumps of nut butter (always thin)
- Chunks of meat (shred or mince)
- Cheese cubes (soft strips or melted)
- Round coin slices of any food

**Safe shapes:**
- Long strips (length of adult thumb)
- Soft-cooked vegetables in spear shape
- Crushed-soft fruit
- Yogurt, scrambled egg, oats, mashed banana

### Stage 5 — Gag vs. Choke

- **Gag** — coughing, sputtering, red face, eye watering, sticking tongue out, possibly vomiting. The infant is moving food. **Do not intervene** with hands in mouth. Stay calm; encourage cough.
- **Choke** — silence, blue lips/face, no breath sound, panicked stillness. Airway is blocked. **Intervene immediately** with infant choking response (5 back blows + 5 chest thrusts; CPR if becomes unresponsive). Call emergency.

The infant gag reflex is forward (~mid-tongue) for the first 9 months — it triggers easily and is protective.

### Stage 6 — 12-Week Ramp

**Weeks 1–2:** Single foods, observe tolerance. 1 meal/day. Iron-rich first foods (iron-fortified cereal, pureed/mashed meat, lentils, tofu).

**Weeks 3–4:** Add second meal. Begin allergen introduction (1 new allergen every 2–3 days, then keep in rotation).

**Weeks 5–6:** Add third meal. Texture progression: smooth → mashed lumpy → soft pieces. Self-feeding practice with finger foods.

**Weeks 7–8:** Continue all 8 high-risk allergens in regular rotation. Increase variety; add herbs and gentle spices (most cuisines do; bland-only is a Western convention).

**Weeks 9–12:** Family meals (modified for choking shapes and salt/sugar limits). Open cup practice. Self-feeding leading.

### Stage 7 — Special Considerations

- **Iron:** breastfed infants need iron-rich solids by 6 months (iron stores from birth deplete). Heme iron (meat, fish) and pair plant iron with vitamin C. Vegan/vegetarian families: prioritize iron-fortified foods and pediatric iron status check at 9–12 months.
- **Salt:** keep family-meal portions low-salt; under 12 months <1g/day.
- **Sugar:** no added sugar before 2y per dietary guidance; small amounts in mixed foods unavoidable and not a crisis.
- **Ultra-processed foods:** mostly skip under 1y; food preference patterns set early.
- **Water:** small amounts with meals from 6 months; not a primary beverage until after 12m.
- **Cow's milk as beverage:** wait until 12 months. Yogurt and cheese are fine sooner.

## Adaptations

- **Severe eczema or existing food allergy:** Coordinate with allergist before allergen introduction; in-office introduction may be considered.
- **Preterm:** Use adjusted age for readiness; readiness signs are the gate, not date.
- **Down syndrome, hypotonia, oral-motor concerns:** Refer to pediatric feeding therapist; texture progression is slower; readiness assessment more nuanced.
- **Cleft lip/palate:** Surgical and feeding team coordinate; standard plan does not apply.
- **Vegan/vegetarian:** Plan iron, B12, omega-3, zinc, calcium, protein within plant pattern; consider pediatric dietitian consult.
- **Cultural staple foods:** Build the plan around family staples (rice + dal, congee, ugali, pap, atole, tortilla, naan + curry); these are appropriate first foods in their respective cuisines.
- **Daycare or shared care:** Communicate plan to all caregivers; allergen tracking form for daycare.

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| Recommend starting at 4 months by date alone | Anchor to readiness signs |
| Tell families to delay allergens | Introduce early and sustain ≥2x/week |
| Confuse gag with choke | Teach the distinction; do not intervene during gag |
| Recommend whole grapes, hot dog rounds, popcorn | List hazard shapes and safe-cut equivalents |
| Recommend cow's milk as a beverage <12m | Yogurt and cheese yes; cow's milk beverage at 12m |
| Default to bland Western food list | Build the plan around family staples |
| Tell vegan families plant patterns are inadequate | Plan nutrient priorities; consider pediatric dietitian |
| Pathologize 8-month-old "picky eating" | Frame as developmental practice; consistency over coercion |
| Promise BLW prevents picky eating | No approach guarantees outcome; safety + relationship matter |
| Recommend rice cereal in bottle | Aspiration risk; no evidence; refer nutrition q's |
| Use commercial brand recommendations | Categories and rationale; family chooses |
| Frame mess as a problem | Mess is part of skill development |
