**Purpose:** Help a caregiver decide where to route a symptom or concern: emergency room, urgent care, same-day pediatric call, next-business-day, well-visit mention, or home monitoring with specific watch criteria.

**When to use:** Acute symptom (fever, vomiting, rash, breathing change), behavior change, sleep change, feeding change, developmental concern, post-injury question, post-vaccination reaction question, mental health–adjacent concern.

**When NOT to use:** Replacement for acute medical judgment when the caregiver senses something serious — caregiver instinct trumps any flowchart. Mental health crisis (route to mental health crisis lines / ED).

---

## Clinical-Signal Triggers (ED or 911 Now)

- Difficulty breathing: retractions, nasal flaring, gasping, blue/grey lips, breathing rate >60/min sustained, apnea pause >20 sec.
- Severe lethargy: cannot be woken normally, limp, eyes glazed.
- Seizure activity, especially first or >5 min.
- Severe head injury (loss of consciousness, vomiting, severe drowsiness, asymmetric pupils, fall onto hard surface from height).
- Significant burn (large area, face/hands/genitals, deep).
- Choking that doesn't clear with back blows / chest thrusts.
- Anaphylaxis (face/lip swelling, breathing change, repeated vomiting + hives).
- Serious bleeding that doesn't stop with pressure.
- Severe dehydration (no wet diapers >12 hrs newborn / >24 hrs older infant, sunken fontanelle, dry mouth, lethargy).
- Fever in infant <8 weeks old of any height.
- Suspected ingestion of toxic substance (call Poison Control: US 1-800-222-1222).
- Anyone who has hurt the child or is threatening to.

---

## Core Principles

1. **Caregiver instinct is the most sensitive signal.** "Something is really wrong" warrants ED; do not wait for a flowchart match.
2. **Younger = lower threshold.** A 4-week-old with fever is an ED visit; a 4-year-old with fever is often home monitoring.
3. **Trajectory matters more than snapshot.** Worsening = sooner; stable or improving = often home.
4. **Behavior change is data.** A child who looks sick (lethargic, not eating, not playing, very different from baseline) is sick, even if vital signs are not in dangerous ranges yet.
5. **Most symptoms in toddlers are viral and self-limited.** Most don't need an office visit; some need monitoring; a few need urgent care.

## Instructions

### Stage 1 — Acute Symptom Triage

**Fever:**

| Age | Threshold | Routing |
|---|---|---|
| 0–8 weeks | 100.4°F (38°C) rectal | ED |
| 2–6 months | 100.4°F | Same-day pediatric call |
| 6–24 months | 102.2°F + lasting >24 hrs, or any fever + look sick / not drinking / lethargic | Same-day |
| 6–24 months | <102°F, looking well, drinking, peeing | Home monitoring |
| 2–3 years | Fever + looks sick, refusing fluids, lethargic | Same-day |
| Any age | Fever + stiff neck, seizure, severe headache, breathing change, persistent vomiting, dehydration | ED |
| Any age | Fever lasting >5 days continuously | Same-day |

**Breathing:**
- Retractions (skin pulling in around ribs/neck), nasal flaring, grunting, head bobbing, blue/grey color → ED.
- Persistent cough, no other concerning signs → next-business-day.
- Whooping cough sound, especially in unvaccinated → same-day.

**GI:**
- Projectile vomiting in 2–8 week old → ED (pyloric stenosis screen).
- Bilious (green-yellow) vomiting → ED.
- Bloody diarrhea → same-day.
- Dehydration signs (no wet diaper >8 hrs in toddler, dry lips, sunken eyes, lethargy) → same-day or ED.
- Repeated vomiting + lethargy → same-day or ED.
- Constipation: hard painful stools, withholding pattern → next-business-day.

**Rash:**
- Petechiae (small red/purple non-blanching dots) + fever → ED (rule out meningococcal).
- Severe blistering, peeling, or rash with breathing/swallowing change → ED.
- Localized rash, no fever, comfortable child → home-monitor or next-business-day.

**Head injury:**
- Loss of consciousness, vomiting >1x, severe drowsiness, fall onto hard surface from height, asymmetric pupils, fluid from ears/nose, seizure, age <12 months with significant fall → ED.
- Mild bump, brief cry, returns to normal → home-monitor.

**Allergic reaction:**
- Face/lip swelling, breathing change, throat tightness, repeated vomiting + hives, drop in alertness → ED, epinephrine if prescribed.
- Localized hives only, comfortable, normal breathing → home + next-business-day.

**Urinary:**
- Pain on urination, blood in urine, fever + suspected UTI → same-day.
- Toilet-trained child with new daytime wetting → next-business-day.

**Mental health / behavior:**
- Caregiver thoughts of harming child or self → mental health, today.
- Sudden behavior change without explanation, regression, withdrawal → next-business-day developmental visit.

### Stage 2 — Trajectory Check

For any non-emergent symptom:
- **Worsening:** route up the chain (home → next-day → same-day → ED).
- **Stable:** continue current routing.
- **Improving:** often home with watch criteria.

### Stage 3 — Watch Criteria (Home Monitoring)

When home is the answer, set explicit watch criteria with thresholds:
- "Call same-day if temperature persists >24 hrs."
- "Go to ED if breathing changes."
- "Call same-day if not drinking by [time]."
- "Wake to check at [time]."

Watch criteria + a time horizon = a real plan. "Just watch them" without criteria = anxiety.

### Stage 4 — Specific Common Scenarios

- **Common cold:** congestion, mild cough, low-grade fever — home + comfort + monitor for breathing change.
- **Stomach bug:** small frequent fluids (Pedialyte), monitor wet diapers, call if dehydrated or worsening.
- **Ear pulling:** common in toddlers; not always ear infection. Same-day if + fever, fussiness, sleep disruption beyond a couple nights.
- **Teething:** drooling, gum chewing, mild irritability for 2–4 days. Fever should not exceed 100.4°F; higher fever = something else.
- **Post-vaccination:** low fever, fussiness for 24–48 hrs typical. High fever, severe pain, persistent inconsolable crying >3 hrs → call.
- **Constipation:** hard stools, withholding — increase fluids/fiber; pediatrician if persistent or causing pain.

### Stage 5 — How to Call

When calling pediatric office:
- Have age, weight, temperature, symptom timeline, current behavior ready.
- State concern clearly: "I'm concerned about [X], onset [time], current state [Y]."
- Ask explicitly: "Should we be seen today, or is this monitor-at-home?"
- If you cannot reach the office: nurse line, urgent care, ED — by escalation depending on symptoms.

### Stage 6 — Documentation

Track for the next visit:
- Symptom timeline.
- Temperatures.
- Wet/dirty diapers.
- Eating, drinking, sleeping.
- What helped, what didn't.

This is the data the pediatrician needs.

## Adaptations

- **Newborn (<8 weeks):** Lower threshold for any concerning sign. Fever is always ED. Breathing changes are always evaluated.
- **Preterm:** Use adjusted age for first-year decision-making; immune fragility means earlier escalation.
- **Chronic condition / immunocompromised:** Discussed thresholds with care team in advance; lower threshold for fever or any new symptom.
- **Solo caregiver:** Pre-identified backup for ED transport if needed.
- **Distance to care:** Lower threshold for departure if travel time long.
- **Recent vaccination:** Expected vs. unexpected reactions distinguished.
- **Daycare exposure:** Often viral; specific outbreak reports help context.

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| Apply adult thresholds to a newborn | <8 weeks fever = ED, full stop |
| Tell parents teething caused 102°F fever | Teething doesn't cause real fever; reroute |
| Default to "wait it out" for breathing changes | Breathing changes always evaluated |
| Override caregiver instinct | Instinct is a primary signal |
| Recommend medication doses | Defer to pediatrician |
| Promise non-emergency for non-classic symptoms | Atypical presentations exist |
| Treat all rashes as benign | Petechiae + fever → ED |
| Skip the watch criteria | Without thresholds it's not a plan |
| Diagnose | Route, don't label |
| Forget chronic-condition baselines | Care team has set their own thresholds |
| Reassure projectile vomiting in 4-week-old | ED for pyloric screen |
| Treat repeated head injury vomiting as digestion | ED |
