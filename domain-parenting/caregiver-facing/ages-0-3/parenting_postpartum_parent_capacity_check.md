**Purpose:** Help a caregiver in the first postpartum year — birthing, non-birthing, or adoptive — self-assess capacity, distinguish typical adjustment from clinically significant patterns, identify the specific postpartum mood/anxiety/OCD/PTSD/psychosis presentations, and route to appropriate support.

**When to use:** Self-check, partner-noticed concern, six-week postpartum visit prep, return-to-work decision point, breastfeeding decision conflict, sleep crisis, sibling-with-newborn capacity drain, adoption-related adjustment, foster-placement adjustment.

**When NOT to use:** Replace clinical evaluation. Acute psychosis (hallucinations, delusions, thoughts of harming infant or self) → ED today.

---

## Clinical-Signal Triggers (Same-Day or ED)

- Thoughts of harming the infant or self that feel hard to dismiss → ED, today.
- Hallucinations or delusional thinking → ED today (postpartum psychosis is a medical emergency).
- Severe depression with inability to care for infant or self → today.
- Suicidal ideation → 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, ED, or warmline; not later.
- Caregiver who has not slept >2 hrs at a stretch in 7+ days, and is decompensating → urgent.
- Intrusive images of harm with conviction (psychosis) vs. intrusive images of harm with horror (PP-OCD) — both warrant care, urgency differs; psychosis is an emergency.

---

## Core Principles

1. **The postpartum year is not just for birthing parents.** Non-birthing parents, adoptive parents, foster parents experience adjustment, mood disorders, and identity shifts.
2. **Postpartum mood and anxiety disorders (PMADs) affect 1 in 5 birthing parents and ~1 in 10 partners.** Underdiagnosis is common.
3. **PP-OCD is distinct from psychosis.** Intrusive harm-thoughts with horror, hypervigilance, avoidance = PP-OCD (treatable). Hallucinations, conviction, paranoia = psychosis (emergency).
4. **Birth trauma can produce PTSD.** Replay, avoidance, hyperarousal, numbing — even with a "healthy outcome," the experience can be traumatic.
5. **Sleep deprivation alone produces emotional symptoms that look like depression but are different.** Both can co-exist; both warrant attention.
6. **Cultural framing matters.** Some cultures normalize postpartum struggle; others stigmatize it. Effective support meets people where they are.

## Instructions

### Stage 1 — Differentiate Baby Blues from PMADs

**Baby blues:**
- Onset within first 1–2 weeks postpartum.
- Mood lability (crying easily, irritability, joy then sadness).
- Resolves by ~2 weeks postpartum.
- Affects ~80% of birthing parents.
- Self-limited.

**Postpartum mood / anxiety disorder (PMAD):**
- Onset any time in first year (sometimes during pregnancy).
- Symptoms persistent (>2 weeks).
- Functional impairment.
- Multiple presentations (see Stage 2).

If symptoms persist past 2 weeks postpartum, intensify, or impair function: not blues.

### Stage 2 — Identify the Presentation

**Postpartum depression (PPD):**
- Persistent sadness, hopelessness, worthlessness, anhedonia.
- Sleep/appetite changes (beyond newborn-driven).
- Difficulty bonding or excessive worry about bonding.
- Suicidal thoughts in severe cases.

**Postpartum anxiety (PPA):**
- Constant worry, racing thoughts, physical anxiety symptoms (heart pounding, restlessness, GI).
- Hypervigilance about infant safety.
- Often co-occurs with PPD.

**Postpartum panic disorder:**
- Sudden panic attacks with physical symptoms.
- Fear of more attacks.

**Postpartum OCD (PP-OCD):**
- Intrusive thoughts/images of harm coming to baby.
- **Crucial distinguishing feature:** the caregiver experiences these thoughts with horror, distress, and a desire to avoid the situation.
- Compulsions: checking on baby repeatedly, avoiding being alone with baby, hand-washing, hyper-monitoring.
- The thoughts are ego-dystonic (foreign, unwanted).
- Treatable; common; and not the same as psychosis.

**Postpartum PTSD:**
- Birth or placement-related trauma.
- Flashbacks, intrusive images of birth, avoidance of birth-related cues, hyperarousal, dissociation.

**Postpartum psychosis:**
- Hallucinations, delusions (often baby-related: "the baby is the devil," "I'm being commanded to hurt my baby and I might"), paranoia.
- Severe insomnia.
- Mood lability swinging high and low.
- **Medical emergency.** Risk of suicide and infanticide.
- ED, today.

**Postpartum rage:**
- Intense anger, often disproportionate, often shame-laden.
- Often accompanies PPA, PPD, or sleep deprivation.

**Postpartum bipolar episodes:**
- New onset of bipolar disorder commonly occurs in the postpartum.
- Mood elevation + decreased need for sleep + high energy → not just adjustment.

### Stage 3 — Non-Birthing & Adoptive Caregivers

- Non-birthing parents experience PPD/PPA at rates ~1 in 10. Often missed.
- Adoptive caregivers experience similar adjustment, attachment, and mood patterns. Hormonal contributions differ; the experience does not always.
- Foster placement adjustment: similar plus trauma-informed considerations.
- All caregivers in the household get screened.

### Stage 4 — Sleep Deprivation Confound

- Severe sleep deprivation causes mood, anxiety, cognitive symptoms that overlap with PMADs.
- Both can co-exist.
- 4–6 hours of consecutive sleep at least 2x/week is roughly the threshold for sustainable function.
- Plan: shift sharing, formula or pumped backup for one feed, rescue night, postpartum doula.

### Stage 5 — Routing

- **OB / midwife:** First-line for screening and referral; can prescribe SSRIs.
- **Primary care:** Same.
- **Perinatal mental health specialist:** Therapist or psychiatrist with specialization (Postpartum Support International — PSI — provider directory).
- **Postpartum Support International:** 1-800-944-4773 (helpline, not crisis); HelpLine, text, chat.
- **Crisis:** 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline; Maternal Mental Health Hotline 1-833-TLC-MAMA (1-833-852-6262).
- **ED:** for psychosis, suicidality with plan/intent, severe self-care collapse.
- **Lactation + medication coordinated through provider.**

### Stage 6 — Self-Care Floor (Not Sufficient, But Necessary)

- Sleep: aim for one 4-hour stretch; protect ruthlessly.
- Daylight: 5–15 min outside daily.
- Hydration + protein.
- One adult conversation per day not about the baby.
- Brief outside the house if able.
- Discharge tasks not done by you (anything anyone else can do).

These are floor; not treatment.

### Stage 7 — Partner / Support Coordination

- Partners are often unsure how to help. Concrete asks land best.
- Examples: "Can you take the 4 a.m. feed?" "Can you handle the laundry?" "Can you sit with me without solving?"
- If partner is dismissive of struggle: name the pattern; consider couples support.

### Stage 8 — Cultural Considerations

- Some communities normalize postpartum suffering ("That's just being a mother"); name the conflict.
- Some communities stigmatize mental health treatment; offer culturally aligned providers, group support, faith-aligned resources.
- Bilingual / first-language providers improve treatment access.
- Postpartum doulas, lactation, peer-support groups can be entry points where therapy isn't acceptable.

## Adaptations

- **Birthing parent:** Hormonal contribution real; obstetric care continuity matters.
- **Non-birthing parent:** Validate. Often shame-laden ("I shouldn't be struggling"). Same routing.
- **Adoptive parent:** Adjustment is real; bonding is also a process, not an instant. Adoption-competent support.
- **Foster parent:** Trauma-informed support; specialized programs.
- **NICU graduate parent:** PTSD specifically common after NICU.
- **Loss in pregnancy or NICU period:** Perinatal loss specialist; PSI has loss support.
- **History of pre-existing mental health condition:** Higher base rate; lower threshold.
- **Bipolar history:** Postpartum is highest risk window for episode; coordinate psychiatric care from pregnancy on.
- **Solo caregiver:** Increased risk; aggressive support recruitment.
- **Twins / multiples:** Multiplicative load; multiples-specific groups.

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| Reassure away symptoms past 2 weeks | If persistent, not blues; route |
| Conflate PP-OCD with psychosis | OCD = horror at thoughts; psychosis = conviction; very different urgency |
| Skip non-birthing parents | ~1 in 10 prevalence; same routing |
| Forget adoptive parents | Adjustment + attachment process; equally real |
| Tell parent to stop breastfeeding to take medication | Many SSRIs and other meds compatible with breastfeeding; consult perinatal psychiatry |
| Default to "all new parents struggle, you're fine" | If functional impairment + persistent: not "fine" |
| Confuse sleep deprivation with PPD | Both can be present; treat both |
| Promise quick recovery | PMAD recovery is months with treatment |
| Dismiss intrusive thoughts | PP-OCD is treatable and common; psychosis is an emergency |
| Forget rage as a presentation | Often the loudest signal |
| Skip cultural framing | Treatment uptake depends on it |
| Pathologize cultural normalization, but don't accommodate | Both: validate culture + name the pattern |
