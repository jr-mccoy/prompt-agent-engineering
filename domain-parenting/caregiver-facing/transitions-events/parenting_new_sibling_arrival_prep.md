# New Sibling Arrival Prep

**Objective:** Produce a dated preparation plan that takes an older child from "a
baby is coming" through the first 12 weeks at home — when to tell them, what to say
at their developmental level, which other changes to move, who cares for them on the
day, how to protect connection, and what regression is normal versus a reason to call
someone.

**When to Use:**
- A pregnancy, surrogacy, adoption, or foster/kinship placement will add a child to
  the household within the next 1–9 months.
- The older child is 1–12 and the caregiver wants to reduce jealousy and regression.
- A baby has just arrived and the older child is regressing, clinging, or acting out.
- **Not this prompt if** the siblings already live together and are fighting — use
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/ages-4-8/parenting_sibling_conflict_coach.md`. If
  you want long-run patterns by age gap (rivalry vs. bullying, birth-order dynamics),
  use `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/cross-age/parenting_sibling_spacing_dynamics.md`.
  This prompt covers only the **arrival window**, roughly 3 months before to 3 months
  after.

## Safety Block

- **Never leave a child under ~5 alone with a newborn**, even briefly. Toddlers
  "help" by lifting, feeding, covering, or climbing into the bassinet. Baby sleeps in
  a separate, bare, flat sleep space (no pillows or toys the older child adds).
- **Persistent intent to hurt the baby** (repeated hitting the baby's head, attempts
  to smother or drop it after redirection, statements like "I want the baby dead"
  with matching behavior over weeks) → pediatrician and child therapist referral.
- **A parent with signs of postpartum depression, anxiety, OCD, or psychosis** →
  see `parenting_postpartum_parent_capacity_check.md`; Postpartum Support
  International HelpLine 1-800-944-4773; National Maternal Mental Health Hotline
  1-833-852-6262 (US). Thoughts of harming self or baby → 988 or 911 now.
- Outside the US, use your local emergency number and maternal/child health line.

## Inputs / Context

1. **Older child(ren):** age in years/months, temperament, any neurodivergence,
   current milestones in progress (toilet training, bed change, weaning, new school).
2. **Arrival:** due date or placement date; birth plan; expected hospital stay; any
   NICU risk; adoption/foster specifics (age of incoming child).
3. **Care plan for the day:** who could look after the older child, how far away,
   availability at 3 a.m.
4. **Household changes already planned:** room moves, crib-to-bed, daycare start,
   house move, parental leave dates.
5. **Family structure:** two-parent, single-parent, two homes, blended, multigenerational.
6. **Parent capacity:** pregnancy complications, prior postpartum difficulty, support.

## Method

1. **Read the older child's developmental level (DS-01).**
   - **Under 2:** cannot hold a future event; prepare through books, dolls, and
     routine-protection rather than explanation. Tell them in the last 4–6 weeks.
   - **2–3:** time is concrete. Tie the arrival to a marker ("when it gets cold",
     "after your birthday"). Tell in the second half of pregnancy, not at 8 weeks.
   - **4–6:** can follow a longer wait; ask many literal questions. Tell once the
     pregnancy is shared with others (they will overhear anyway).
   - **7–12:** can be told when adults are told; wants a real role and real
     information, including possible loss.
2. **Write the announcement in 3–5 sentences** at that level: what is happening,
   when, what the baby will actually be like (cries, sleeps, can't play for months),
   and what stays the same ("I will still read to you every night").
3. **Move other big changes out of the window (DP-04).** Any change the child will
   associate with displacement — leaving the crib, changing rooms, starting daycare,
   toilet-training push, weaning — happens at least 6–8 weeks before the due date or
   waits until 8–12 weeks after. If the crib is needed, the move happens early and is
   framed as the older child's upgrade, not the baby's takeover.
4. **Build the birth-day care plan.** Primary caregiver, backup, and a 3 a.m. option.
   Practice: the older child spends at least one rehearsal afternoon or night with
   the primary. Pack an older-child bag. Script the goodbye ("Grandma will take you
   to school; I will call you at dinner").
5. **Plan the first meeting.** The older child meets the baby when the parent's arms
   are free (baby in the bassinet, not held). Optional: a small gift "from" the baby —
   harmless, not necessary. Visitors greet the older child first.
6. **Protect connection with Special Time.** 10–15 minutes daily, child-led, named
   ("Our time"), with the baby cared for by someone else or asleep. Start it before
   the birth so it is not "what I got because of the baby."
7. **Offer real, optional jobs.** Fetching a diaper, choosing a song. Never required,
   never a replacement for being a child (no parentification of 9–12s).
8. **Pre-load regression and feelings (NE-07).** Tell the parent what to expect:
   toileting accidents, baby talk, wanting a bottle, night waking, clinginess,
   aggression toward the parent more than the baby. Validate the feeling, limit the
   action: "You're mad the baby takes my time. You can be mad. I won't let you push."
9. **Set the 12-week watch list** with thresholds (Verification below).

## Output Format

```
# New Sibling Plan — [older child, age] → arrival [date]

## Announcement (when + script)
## Changes to move (what → new date, reason)
## Birth-day care plan (primary / backup / 3 a.m. / rehearsal date)
## First meeting plan
## Special Time (when, how long, who covers the baby)
## Optional helper jobs
## Expected regression + scripts
## Newborn safety rules (household)
## 12-week watch list (normal / call pediatrician)
```

## Verification

- [ ] Telling time matches the older child's age band.
- [ ] Every competing change has a date ≥6–8 weeks before or ≥8–12 weeks after.
- [ ] Birth-day plan names a backup and a night option.
- [ ] Special Time is daily, protected, and starts before arrival.
- [ ] Newborn-supervision rule is explicit.
- [ ] Watch list separates expected regression (weeks) from concern (persisting
      beyond ~3 months, worsening, or dangerous).

## False-Positive Prevention

| Misfire | What it looks like | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Treating regression as defiance | Punishing toileting accidents | Expected in the window; quietly support, no shame. |
| Over-preparing a toddler | Talking about the baby daily from 12 weeks | Short, late, concrete preparation for under-3s. |
| Promising a playmate | "You'll have someone to play with!" | Describe a real newborn: cries, sleeps, can't play yet. |
| Forced love | "Say you love your sister" | Feelings are allowed; actions are limited. |
| Crib handoff right before birth | Older child moved out week 38 | Move early, or after 8–12 weeks. |
| Reading aggression toward parent as a danger to baby | Child hits mom, not the baby | Usually displaced protest; supervise, connect, limit. |
| Ignoring the parent's state | Plan assumes a rested parent | Check capacity; route PPD/PPA concerns. |

## Adaptations

- **Autistic or anxious child:** social story with photos of the hospital and who
  will pick them up; visual calendar counting down; noise plan (ear defenders for
  crying).
- **Adoption / foster placement:** the incoming child may be older; prepare both
  children for shared space, possessions, and the newcomer's own grief.
- **Two homes:** both households use the same announcement words; the non-birth
  household gets the regression list too.
- **NICU or loss risk:** tell 7–12s that babies sometimes need the hospital longer;
  prepare a plan if the baby does not come home on schedule.
- **Single parent:** the backup plan is the plan — line up two people, not one.

## Example Output

```
# New Sibling Plan — Maya, 2y 9m → due 14 Feb

## Announcement
Tell in early December, tied to "after Christmas, when it's still cold."
"There is a baby growing in Mama's tummy. The baby will come out after
Christmas, when it's still cold. Babies cry and sleep a lot and can't play
yet. You will still have bath and books with Mama every night."

## Changes to move
- Crib → toddler bed: move to 20 Dec (8 weeks before due date), framed as
  "Maya's big-girl bed." Crib stored until mid-March.
- Toilet training: paused; restart late April if she shows readiness.
- Daycare start (planned Feb): moved to 1 Dec so it is settled first.

## Birth-day care plan
Primary: Grandma Lin (15 min away), rehearsal sleepover 10 Jan.
Backup: neighbor Ana (daytime). 3 a.m.: Grandma by phone → drives over.
Bag: blanket "Bun", 2 outfits, photo book of Mama and Dad.

## First meeting
Baby in bassinet; Mama's arms free to hug Maya first. Optional gift (a toy
stroller for her doll) — fine but not needed.

## Special Time
"Maya Time" 15 min after dinner with Dad holding the baby; started 1 Jan.

## Expected regression
Accidents, baby talk, wanting the bottle, night waking. Script: "You want
to be little like the baby sometimes. I can carry you like a baby for a
minute. Then you can show me your big-girl jump."

## Newborn safety
Maya is never alone in a room with the baby. Bassinet in parents' room.

## 12-week watch list
Normal: regression and clinginess for 4–8 weeks, easing by 12.
Call pediatrician: regression worsening after 3 months; repeated attempts to
hurt the baby despite supervision; stops eating or sleeping for >1 week.
```

## Techniques Used

- **ST-02 Structured Sequential Instructions** — a dated sequence from announcement to week 12.
- **NE-07 Emotional Validation First** — name the older child's jealousy before limiting behavior.
- **DS-01 Framework Application** — developmental time-concept bands set when and how to tell.
- **DP-04 Must-Not Constraints** — no competing transitions inside the arrival window, no forced affection, no unsupervised contact.
