# Moving House — The Child's Side

**Objective:** Produce a child-centred move plan: a losses inventory per child, a
telling script and timeline, the choices the child genuinely gets, goodbye and
arrival rituals, a first-90-days routine and friendship rebuild, and a dated check-in
that separates normal adjustment from needing help.

**When to Use:**
- The family is moving home — across town, to a new city, or abroad — with children.
- A child is angry, sad, or withdrawn about a planned or recent move.
- A military, diplomatic, or job-driven family moves repeatedly.
- **Not this prompt if** you need the **logistics** — boxes, utilities, change of
  address, movers — use `domain-productivity/home-life/home_moving_checklist.md`. If
  the adult's own adjustment is the problem, use
  `domain-personal-development/prompts/life-transitions/lifetransition_relocation_adjustment.md`.
  If the move is between two parents' homes after separation, use
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/divorce/parenting_divorce_two_homes_transition_support.md`;
  if a custody order may restrict relocation, see
  `domain-legal/custody/legal_relocation_move_away_analysis.md` and counsel. For the
  school change itself, pair with `parenting_new_school_transition_plan.md`.

## Safety Block

- A move that is an **escape from domestic violence** needs a safety plan first:
  National Domestic Violence Hotline 1-800-799-7233 (US). Do not share the new
  address with the child's other contacts until it is safe.
- A child who talks about not wanting to live, hurting themselves, or running away
  → 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (US) and pediatrician same-day; 911 if in danger.
- Moves driven by eviction, homelessness, or disaster: under the US McKinney-Vento
  Act, children without fixed housing can stay in their school of origin with
  transport; ask the district's McKinney-Vento liaison.
- Outside the US, use your local emergency and family-support numbers.

## Inputs / Context

1. **Each child:** age, temperament, friendships, activities, attachments (pets,
   grandparents, a best friend), neurodivergence, history of past moves.
2. **The move:** distance, reason, date, whether the child can visit beforehand,
   whether the decision is final.
3. **School timing:** mid-year or between years; grade (entering middle or high
   school; senior year).
4. **What stays:** pets, furniture, extended family nearby, activities that exist in
   both places.
5. **Adult state:** is the move wanted, forced, or grief-laden for the parents?

## Method

1. **Inventory losses per child (RT-02).** Score each 0–2 on five dimensions:
   peers, place (room, house, neighbourhood), school, activities/identity (team,
   club, role), and people (grandparents, a parent left behind). The highest-scoring
   dimension sets the plan's focus. Adolescents usually score highest on peers;
   under-5s on routine and place.
2. **Choose telling time.** Under 5: 2–4 weeks before, with a picture countdown.
   6–12: once the decision is firm (often 1–3 months). Teens: as early as possible
   and before it is final where the decision can still absorb their input. Never
   let a child hear it from someone else first.
3. **Tell honestly (NE-07).** Reason in one sentence, what changes, what stays,
   room for the child's feeling: "You're allowed to hate this. I'll still listen."
   Do not sell the move or promise it will be better.
4. **Give real choices, not false ones (DP-04).** Paint colour, room layout, which
   activity to sign up for first, how to say goodbye. Never ask "do you want to
   move?" if the answer cannot change the decision.
5. **Weigh move timing.** Between school years is easiest for routines; for a
   socially anxious child a mid-year start can mean a class that notices the new
   child. For teens, a move in the final year of high school carries the highest
   cost — consider whether the teen can finish with a relative or how to keep
   graduation pathways intact.
6. **Goodbye rituals.** A last visit to favourite places; photo book of the old
   house; address/contact list for friends; a goodbye party; for young children,
   "goodbye house" walk-through. Plan the first remote contact with the best friend
   for week 1–2.
7. **Arrival rituals.** Unpack the child's room first; set up bed, lights, and the
   comfort object on night one. Keep bedtime, meals, and weekend rituals identical
   in the first two weeks even amid boxes.
8. **Rebuild peers fast.** Within 2–4 weeks: one structured activity with the same
   kids weekly (team, scouts, faith youth group, library club); invite one
   classmate over; locate a neighbourhood park routine.
9. **Set check-ins at weeks 2, 6, and 12** with the thresholds below.

## Output Format

```
# Move Plan — [family] [from → to], move date [..]

## Losses inventory (per child, 0–2 per dimension, focus area)
## Telling plan (when, who, script)
## Real choices offered
## Timing notes (school year, teen considerations)
## Goodbye rituals (dated)
## Arrival plan (night one, first two weeks)
## Peer rebuild (activity, first invite, by when)
## Check-ins (week 2 / 6 / 12) + when to get help
```

## Verification

- [ ] Each child has a scored losses inventory and a named focus dimension.
- [ ] Telling time fits each child's age; no child hears it secondhand.
- [ ] Choices offered are ones the adult will honour.
- [ ] Goodbye and arrival rituals have dates.
- [ ] At least one weekly peer activity is identified with a start date.
- [ ] Help thresholds are specific (duration plus function).

## False-Positive Prevention

| Misfire | What it looks like | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Selling the move | "You'll love it, there's a pool!" | Validate loss first; benefits land later, if at all. |
| Expecting quick adjustment | Concern at week 2 | Sadness and irritability for 6–8 weeks are common. |
| Missing the teen's real loss | Plan focuses on the bedroom | Peers and identity roles carry most weight for teens. |
| Treating anger as disrespect | Consequences for "I hate you for this" | Name the feeling; limit only harmful behavior. |
| Cutting old ties to help them settle | No calls to old friends | Old ties buffer; keep them while building new ones. |
| Ignoring parent grief | Parent's sadness leaks unexplained | Name it briefly: "I'm sad too, and I'm okay." |

## Adaptations

- **Autistic child:** photos/video tour of the new house and route; keep identical
  bedding and layout; schedule the move day's sensory load.
- **Military/repeat movers:** maintain a "portable home" kit (same rituals,
  same objects); Military OneSource and school liaison officers; Military Interstate
  Children's Compact covers records, placement, and graduation across US states.
- **International move:** language support; third-culture identity; time-zone plan
  for grandparents.
- **Separated parents:** a move away from the other parent is a custody matter
  first — confirm with counsel before telling the child anything final.

## Example Output

```
# Move Plan — Okafor family, Columbus → Denver, move date 28 June

## Losses inventory
Tobi (14): peers 2, place 1, school 2, activities 2 (club soccer captain),
people 1 → focus: peers + soccer identity.
Ada (6): peers 1, place 2, school 1, activities 0, people 2 (grandma 10 min
away) → focus: place + grandma.

## Telling plan
Tobi told 2 March, before the offer was signed, with real input on area and
school. Ada told mid-May with a countdown calendar.
Ada script: "We're going to live in a new house in Denver after school
ends. Grandma will video call every Sunday and visit at Thanksgiving. You
can be sad and still come with us."

## Real choices
Tobi: picks between two school-zone neighbourhoods; chooses his club tryout.
Ada: paint colour; which stuffed animals ride in the car.

## Goodbye rituals
Tobi: team goodbye dinner 21 June; group chat stays. Ada: "goodbye house"
walk 27 June; photo book with grandma.

## Arrival plan
Beds and Ada's nightlight up first night. Sunday pancakes continue.

## Peer rebuild
Tobi: club soccer tryout 12 July; summer conditioning 3x/week.
Ada: library story time + neighbourhood park daily at 5 p.m.

## Check-ins
Week 2: expect tears, irritability, "I want to go home."
Week 6: Tobi should have at least one peer contact outside practice.
Week 12 — get help (pediatrician or school counsellor) if: still isolated
with no peer contact; grades fall a full letter; sleep or appetite changed
for >2 weeks; refuses school; any talk of not wanting to live (988 now).
```

## Techniques Used

- **ST-02 Structured Sequential Instructions** — telling → goodbye → arrival → rebuild → check-ins.
- **RT-02 Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework** — five-dimension losses inventory per child.
- **NE-07 Emotional Validation First** — the child's loss is named before any upside.
- **DP-04 Must-Not Constraints** — no false choices, no selling, no severing old ties.
