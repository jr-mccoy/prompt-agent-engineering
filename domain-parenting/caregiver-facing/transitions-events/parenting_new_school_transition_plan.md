# New School Transition Plan

**Objective:** Produce a dated plan for one child entering one new school — the
dominant risk named, preparation steps from four weeks before to eight weeks after,
the supports that must transfer, the information the school needs, and check-in
thresholds.

**When to Use:**
- A child is starting kindergarten, moving from elementary to middle school or middle
  to high school, or changing schools because of a move, a school choice, or an
  expulsion/placement change.
- A child with an IEP, 504 plan, health plan, or allergy action plan is changing
  schools.
- A child is anxious in the weeks before a school start.
- **Not this prompt if** the child is under 3 and starting **childcare** — use
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/ages-0-3/parenting_daycare_transition_plan.md`
  (a separation-and-ramp problem, not a school one). If the child is already
  enrolled and refusing to go, use
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/ages-9-12/parenting_school_refusal_decoder_tween.md`.
  For a first accommodation meeting rather than a transfer, use
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/ages-4-8/parenting_school_accommodation_conversation_prep.md`.

## Safety Block

- **Bullying with threats, assault, sexual harassment, or a weapon** → report to the
  school in writing the same day; police if a crime; see
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/ages-9-12/parenting_bullying_target_response_protocol.md`.
- **Talk of self-harm or not wanting to live** → 988 (US) and pediatrician same-day;
  911 if in danger.
- **Medical plans** (anaphylaxis, diabetes, seizures) must be in place, with
  medication on site and staff trained, **before day one**. Do not start without
  them.
- Outside the US, use local emergency numbers and the school's safeguarding lead.

## Inputs / Context

1. **Child:** age, grade, temperament, strengths, prior transitions, friends
   moving with them (if any).
2. **Transition type:** K entry, level change, mid-year change, cross-district,
   cross-state, or international.
3. **Supports:** IEP, 504, health/allergy plan, gifted, English-learner services,
   counselling, behaviour plan.
4. **Logistics:** bus vs. drop-off, schedule, lunch system, lockers, class changes.
5. **What worries the child, in their words; what worries the parent.**

## Method

1. **Find the dominant risk (DP-06, RT-02).** Score 0–2 on four dimensions:
   **logistics** (route, timetable, locker, lunch), **academics** (curriculum gap,
   new expectations, homework load), **social entry** (no known peers, cliques
   formed, prior bullying), **support continuity** (IEP/504/health plan, services).
   Name the one that drives the plan; it gets the most steps.
2. **Know the transition's typical shape.** Kindergarten: separation, stamina,
   toileting, following group instructions. Middle school: multiple teachers, lockers,
   puberty, and a well-documented dip in grades and motivation in the first year.
   High school: credits, social sorting, more independence. Mid-year: friendships
   already formed — social entry usually dominates.
3. **Secure support continuity first.** Request records transfer in writing. In the
   US under IDEA, a new school in the same state must provide services comparable to
   the existing IEP until it adopts it or writes a new one; from another state,
   comparable services until it evaluates (if needed) and writes a new IEP. Bring a
   copy of the current IEP/504 to enrolment. Ask for a transition meeting before day
   one.
4. **Run the T−4 weeks timeline.** Visit the building; walk the route; practise the
   locker combination and timetable; meet the teacher or counsellor; read a book or
   look at the school website together; shift sleep to school schedule 1–2 weeks out.
5. **Engineer social entry.** Ask the school for a buddy; find one club or team that
   meets in the first two weeks; arrange one meet-up with a future classmate; for
   kindergarten, a playdate with a known classmate.
6. **Send the school a one-page profile.** Strengths, what helps when upset, health
   needs, family situation in a sentence, preferred contact.
7. **Script day one and the first week.** Goodbye ritual; pickup plan; what to do if
   lost or overwhelmed (name the adult to find). Keep after-school calm: snack, quiet,
   no interrogation — "Tell me one good thing" at dinner.
8. **Set check-ins at weeks 2, 4, and 8 (DP-04).** Do not judge the transition
   before week 4; do not ignore a pattern past week 8.

## Output Format

```
# School Transition Plan — [child, age] → [school, grade], start [date]

## Dominant risk (scores: logistics / academics / social / supports)
## Supports that must transfer (status, owner, deadline)
## T−4 weeks to day one (dated steps)
## Social entry plan
## One-page profile for the school
## Day one and week one script
## Check-ins (week 2 / 4 / 8): normal vs. act
```

## Verification

- [ ] Dominant risk named with scores.
- [ ] Every existing support has a transfer step, owner, and deadline.
- [ ] Medical plans confirmed before day one.
- [ ] At least one social-entry action is scheduled in weeks 1–2.
- [ ] Check-in thresholds are specific.

## False-Positive Prevention

| Misfire | What it looks like | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Panicking at week-one tears | Requesting a transfer after 5 days | Adjustment takes 4–8 weeks. |
| Missing the middle-school dip | Treating a one-grade drop as failure | A first-year dip is common; act if it persists or deepens. |
| Assuming services transfer automatically | IEP not implemented for 6 weeks | Bring the IEP; request comparable services in writing. |
| Over-scheduling social fixes | Five clubs at once | One weekly group with the same kids beats many. |
| Interrogating after school | "Who did you play with? Why not?" | Short, warm, low-pressure check-ins. |
| Labelling refusal as anxiety only | Ignoring bullying | Ask specifically about peers and safety. |

## Adaptations

- **Autistic child / sensory needs:** photo book of rooms and staff; quiet-space
  plan; earlier visit when the building is empty; written timetable.
- **ADHD:** colour-coded binders, a single homework planner, a locker check routine.
- **English learner:** confirm language services and an interpreter for meetings.
- **Gifted / 2e:** ask how enrichment and accommodations will both continue.
- **Foster care:** in the US, ESSA requires a school-of-origin decision based on the
  child's best interests and a foster-care point of contact in each district.

## Example Output

```
# School Transition Plan — Priya, 11 → Lincoln Middle, Grade 6, start 26 Aug

## Dominant risk
Logistics 2 (7 teachers, lockers, bus), academics 1, social 1 (3 friends
going), supports 2 (504 for ADHD: extended time, preferential seating).
Driver: support continuity + logistics.

## Supports
504 copy sent to counsellor 5 Aug; meeting with 504 coordinator 19 Aug
(parent). Owner: Dad. Teachers to receive accommodations by day one.

## T−4 weeks
29 Jul: sleep shift begins (bed 9:15 → 8:45).
12 Aug: open-house visit; walk schedule with map; practise locker 10 times.
19 Aug: 504 meeting; ask for a planner check by homeroom teacher.
22 Aug: bus route dry run.

## Social entry
Robotics club starts week 2; meet-up with Ana and Jules 24 Aug.

## Profile
Curious, funny, loves building. Loses track of materials; helped by a
checklist taped inside the locker. Contact: Dad by text.

## Day one
Bus. If lost, find the office (map in binder). After school: snack, 30 min
free time, "one good thing" at dinner.

## Check-ins
Week 2: tired and overwhelmed is normal.
Week 4: missing assignments in >2 classes → email teachers + 504 review.
Week 8 act if: dread or stomach-aches most school mornings, avoiding the
bus, no peer to sit with at lunch, or a drop of more than one grade in two
subjects → counsellor meeting; consider evaluation.
```

## Techniques Used

- **ST-02 Structured Sequential Instructions** — T−4 weeks through week 8.
- **RT-02 Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework** — four risk dimensions scored.
- **DP-06 Dominant Driver Identification** — the highest-risk dimension shapes the plan.
- **DP-04 Must-Not Constraints** — no judgment before week 4, no start without medical plans.
