# Solo Parenting by Choice or Circumstance

**Objective:** Produce a solo-parent household plan: where the load is, what breaks
first if the parent is sick or away, the named people and services who catch it, the
paperwork that protects the child, and the words the child will use to understand and
explain their own family.

**When to Use:**
- One adult is the only parent in the child's day-to-day life — by choice (donor
  conception, solo adoption, solo surrogacy) or by circumstance (a parent died, left,
  is uninvolved, or was never known).
- The parent has no plan for what happens if they get the flu, need surgery, or are
  in a car accident.
- A child is starting to ask "Why don't I have a dad/mum?" or is being asked by
  classmates.
- **Not this prompt if** there **is** another parent with contact and the issue is
  working with them — use `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/co-parenting/` (for an
  unsafe or absent one,
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/co-parenting/parenting_coparenting_with_unsafe_or_absent_parent.md`).
  If the main problem is that you are depleted, start with
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/parent-capacity/parenting_parent_overload_load_redesign.md`.
  For a shared family calendar, use
  `domain-productivity/home-life/home_family_schedule_coordinator.md`. This prompt is
  structural planning for a one-parent household.

## Safety Block

- **You have thoughts of harming yourself or that the children would be better off
  without you** → 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (US) now; you do not need to be at a
  breaking point to call. Immediate danger → 911.
- **You are afraid you might hurt your child** → National Parent Helpline
  1-855-427-2736 (US) or Childhelp 1-800-422-4453 for parent support; put the child
  somewhere safe and step away.
- **Sudden incapacity with no plan** (you are hospitalised, children alone) → this is
  the reason for the emergency card below; make it this week.
- **Leaving an abusive partner is why you are solo** → National Domestic Violence
  Hotline 1-800-799-7233, text START to 88788; safety plan before any contact
  arrangements.
- Outside the US, use your local crisis, parenting, and emergency lines.

## Inputs / Context

1. **How you came to be solo** and how long; any other parent, donor, or birth
   family information.
2. **Children:** ages, needs, school or childcare hours.
3. **Work:** hours, flexibility, travel, sick leave.
4. **Money:** income stability, childcare costs, benefits.
5. **People:** family nearby or not, friends, neighbours, other parents,
   community or faith groups, paid help.
6. **Paperwork in place:** will, named guardian, medical consent for others, life
   insurance.
7. **What the child knows and asks.**

## Method

1. **Find the single primary constraint (DP-09).** Of time, money, energy, and
   backup, one usually binds hardest. Name it; design around it first. Many solo
   parents can cope with any day; what fails is the day they are not available.
2. **Trace the cascade (RT-07).** Walk through: "If I wake up with a 39 °C fever on a
   school day, what happens at 7:00, 8:30, 15:00, 18:00?" Then for a 3-day hospital
   stay and a 2-week work trip. Every gap becomes a named person or service.
3. **Predict failure modes (DP-07).** Common ones: the one helper moves away; the
   backup lives 40 minutes away; the school will not release the child to someone
   not on the form; the parent never asks because asking feels like failing.
4. **Build a three-deep backup chain.** For each scenario: first call, second call,
   paid option. Put names on the school and childcare pick-up lists **now**. Give
   one person a key and the emergency card.
5. **Make specific asks.** "Could you do Tuesday pick-up once a month?" works;
   "Let me know if you ever need anything" does not get used. Trade where you can
   (cook a double batch, take their kids on a Saturday) so asks feel reciprocal.
6. **Route the paperwork to a lawyer.** A will naming a guardian, a standby or
   temporary guardian designation if your jurisdiction has one, medical
   authorisation for named adults, life insurance. Requirements vary — this prompt
   lists what to ask about, not what to sign.
7. **Explain the family, by age (CM-11).** Children who learn their origins early
   integrate them more easily; secrets learned later hurt trust.
   - **2–4:** a simple, positive story: "Some families have a mum and a dad, some
     have two mums, some have one mum. Our family is you and me, and Grandma."
   - **Donor conception:** start the story before 4 — "I wanted you so much. A
     kind man called a donor gave a tiny seed, and doctors helped me grow you."
   - **Death or departure:** honest and age-matched; route to grief or
     absent-parent prompts where needed.
   - **5–10:** give the child a sentence for classmates: "I have one mum. That's my
     family." Practise it.
   - **11+:** fuller facts; donor registries or birth-family contact as their choice
     with your support.
8. **Protect one recovery block a week** for the parent; schedule it like work.

## Output Format

```
# Solo-Parent Household Plan — [parent], [children, ages]

## Binding constraint and why
## Cascade walk-throughs: sick day / hospital stay / work trip
## Backup chain (first / second / paid) per scenario
## Pick-up lists and keys updated (where, who, done?)
## Emergency card (who to call, child info, where documents are)
## Support network asks (who, what, how often, what I offer back)
## Paperwork to raise with a lawyer
## How we explain our family: [child]'s words now; next stage
## My weekly recovery block
```

## Verification

- [ ] The binding constraint is named and the plan addresses it first.
- [ ] Each of the three scenarios has a first, second, and paid backup.
- [ ] Backups are on the school/childcare release lists.
- [ ] Asks are specific and time-bound.
- [ ] Legal items are flagged for a lawyer, not decided here.
- [ ] Family explanation is truthful, age-matched, and gives the child a sentence to use.

## False-Positive Prevention

| Misfire | What it looks like | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Deficit framing | "Your child will miss out without two parents" | Child outcomes track stability, money, and warmth, not parent count. |
| Generic self-care | "Take a bubble bath" | The binding problem is backup and load, not relaxation tips. |
| Vague support | "Lean on your village" | Named people, named days, on the release form. |
| Over-explaining to a 3-year-old | Donor biology lecture | A short, warm story, repeated and expanded. |
| Hiding origins | "We'll tell her when she's older" | Early, simple telling; secrets surface. |
| Legal advice | "Write a will naming X" as a final answer | List questions for a lawyer. |

## Adaptations

- **Solo by choice:** donor-conceived peer communities and books help the child see
  families like theirs; know what donor information you hold and how the child can
  access it later.
- **Widowed parent:** grief and capacity first; children's grief route via
  transitions-events.
- **Low income:** check childcare subsidies, tax credits, and school breakfast and
  after-school programmes; 211 (US) lists local help.
- **No family nearby:** build from other single parents, neighbours, faith or
  community groups; one reliable paid sitter is part of the chain.
- **Neurodivergent child:** backups must be briefed on routines and regulation needs.

## Example Output

```
# Solo-Parent Household Plan — Priya, with Arjun (4), solo by choice (donor)

## Binding constraint
Backup. Work is flexible (hybrid nurse educator), money is tight but steady;
nobody can collect Arjun if I am sick. Parents live 3 hours away.

## Cascade: I wake with a fever on a nursery day
07:00 I can't drive → 08:00 nursery drop missed → 18:00 dinner/bath.
Gap: no one local. Fix below.
Hospital 3 days: nobody to stay overnight → Mum drives up (3 h); first
night needs a local adult.
Work trip: none needed this year.

## Backup chain
Sick day: 1) Leah (neighbour, also solo mum, 2 doors down) 2) Sam
(nursery friend's dad) 3) Care.com sitter Rosa (met twice, $22/h).
Hospital: Leah night 1 → Mum from day 2.

## Lists and keys
Leah and Sam added to nursery collection list 5 Oct. Leah has a key.

## Emergency card (fridge + my wallet + Leah's phone)
Arjun's allergies (none), pediatrician, nursery, Leah, Mum, where the will
is.

## Asks (and trade)
Leah: backup sick day and first night in an emergency; I take her
daughter every other Saturday morning.
Sam: Thursday pick-up if I'm stuck at work, twice a month max.

## Paperwork for lawyer (appt 18 Oct)
Will naming my sister Kavya as guardian; medical authorisation for Leah
and Mum; is a standby guardian designation available in our state? Life
insurance amount.

## Explaining our family
Arjun now: "Some families have a mummy and daddy. Ours has Mummy and
Arjun. A kind helper called a donor gave a tiny seed so I could have you."
Classmate sentence practised in the car: "I don't have a daddy. I have a
mummy." Next stage (5–6): books with donor-conceived families.

## Recovery block
Sunday 2–4 pm while Arjun is at Leah's (swap with her Saturday).
```

## Techniques Used

- **DP-09 Single Primary Constraint Identification** — find the one constraint (usually backup) that binds hardest.
- **RT-07 Cascade Effect Analysis** — hour-by-hour walk-throughs expose where a one-parent day fails.
- **DP-07 Failure Mode Prediction** — anticipates the helper who moves, the release-list gap, the unasked ask.
- **CM-11 Reasoning-Based Constraint Design** — early truthful telling, with the reason (secrets surface and cost trust).
