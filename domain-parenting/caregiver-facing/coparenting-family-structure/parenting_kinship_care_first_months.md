# Kinship Care, the First Months

**Objective:** Produce a first-90-days plan for a relative caregiver who has
unexpectedly become a child's parent — what to tell the child and in what words, what
the child calls the caregiver, how to handle "I want my mum" and loyalty binds, how to
run contact with the parent safely, what the caregiver needs for themselves, and which
outside supports and legal-status questions to raise now.

**When to Use:**
- A grandparent, aunt, uncle, cousin, adult sibling, or close family friend
  ("fictive kin") is now the day-to-day carer because the parent cannot be —
  addiction, prison or jail, death, serious mental or physical illness, deployment,
  deportation, or a child-protection removal.
- The arrangement started suddenly (a phone call at 2 a.m., a caseworker at the door)
  and nobody planned for it.
- The child is asking where the parent is, when they go home, or is acting out
  around visits and calls.
- **Not this prompt if** you need the **legal** analysis of a relative's standing to
  seek custody or visitation — use
  `domain-legal/custody/legal_third_party_custody_visitation_analysis.md`. If you are
  the child's **other parent** dealing with an unsafe or absent co-parent, use
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/co-parenting/parenting_coparenting_with_unsafe_or_absent_parent.md`.
  If you are a licensed foster carer for a child who is **not** your relative, use
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/coparenting-family-structure/parenting_foster_placement_first_weeks.md`.
  This prompt is the relative-caregiver's family plan; it is not legal advice.

## Safety Block

- **The parent arrives intoxicated, threatening, or tries to take the child against
  a court or agency order** → do not hand the child over; call 911 (US) or your
  emergency number; tell the caseworker. Keep a copy of any order where you can
  reach it.
- **The child discloses abuse or neglect** in the parent's care → believe, do not
  interrogate, write down their words, report to child protective services
  (Childhelp 1-800-422-4453 can route you).
- **The child talks about wanting to die, self-harms, or uses substances** → 988
  (US) today; pediatrician same day. Overdose risk in the household → keep naloxone.
- **You cannot safely continue** (your own health, money, or safety) → tell the
  agency or a kinship navigator now; a planned change is far less harmful than a
  sudden collapse.
- Outside the US, use your local emergency, child-protection, and crisis lines.

## Inputs / Context

1. **Child:** age, temperament, neurodivergence, health and school needs, siblings
   (together or split).
2. **Why now:** the parent's situation, what the child witnessed, how the move
   happened, expected length (days, months, unknown, permanent).
3. **Arrangement type:** informal family arrangement, voluntary placement, or
   agency/court-ordered kinship placement — and what paperwork you hold.
4. **Contact:** any order or agency plan for visits and calls; the parent's current
   reliability and safety.
5. **Caregiver:** age, health, work, housing, money, other dependants, and your
   relationship with the parent (often your own child — with grief and anger).
6. **Family politics:** other relatives' views; the other parent's role.

## Method

1. **Lead with the child's experience (NE-07).** Removal from a parent is a loss even
   when the parent was unsafe. Expect grief, anger at you, idealising the parent,
   regression, sleep problems, hoarding food, or "honeymoon" good behaviour that ends
   at weeks 3–8. Name feelings before rules.
2. **Give an honest, age-matched account, with reasons (CM-11).** Children fill
   silence with self-blame ("Mum left because I was bad"). Truthful and short beats
   detailed or cleaned-up:
   **3–6:** "Mum is sick and can't take care of kids right now. You'll live with Nana
   while doctors help her. It's not because of anything you did." **7–11:** name it
   plainly ("Dad is in jail because he broke a law, for about two years").
   **12+:** fuller facts, addiction as illness *and* choices; agree what they tell
   friends. Never promise a return date you do not control.
3. **Settle the role and the name.** "Grandma" can stay "Grandma" — the child does
   not have to call you Mum. Say what changes (you sign school forms, you set
   bedtime) and what does not (the parent is still their parent).
4. **Map positions in the loyalty bind (MP-10).** Lay out what the child, the parent,
   the caregiver, and other relatives each want and fear. Common binds: the child
   feels disloyal for liking it here; the parent says "Grandma stole you"; the
   caregiver's anger at their own adult child leaks. Rules: no criticism of the parent
   in the child's hearing; the child may love both; adult conflict goes to adult
   channels.
5. **Make contact predictable and safe.** Use the order or agency plan; otherwise a
   written arrangement: fixed day/time, supervision if needed, sober-only rule, a
   no-show script (tell the child plainly; do not cover), calm before and after.
   Prison or jail: check visiting and video rules; letters and photos count.
6. **Stabilise the basics in week 1–4.** School enrolment (kinship caregivers can
   usually enrol with a caregiver affidavit or agency letter — check your state),
   medical consent authority, insurance or Medicaid, a primary-care visit, the
   child's comfort objects, a predictable daily routine.
7. **Look after the caregiver's capacity (DP-05).** Many kinship carers are older,
   working, or grieving the parent. Set a weekly respite slot, name one person to
   call, and screen yourself: sleeping, eating, drinking, coping? Gates below say
   when to escalate.
8. **Route the legal-status and money questions (DS-33).** Options and names vary by
   state and country — informal care, power of attorney, temporary guardianship,
   licensed kinship foster care (often with payments), subsidised guardianship,
   adoption. Ask a kinship navigator or legal aid which fits; do not sign away or
   accept anything on the spot. Benefits to check: child-only TANF grant (US),
   kinship foster payments, SNAP, Medicaid/CHIP, tax credits.
9. **Review at 2, 6, and 12 weeks** against the get-help thresholds.

## Output Format

```
# Kinship Care Plan — [child, age] with [caregiver relationship], from [date]

## What [child] has been told (exact words) and what we say next
## What [child] calls me; what changes and what stays the same
## Loyalty-bind map: child / parent / me / others — and our rules
## Contact plan: when, how, supervision, no-show script, before/after routine
## Weeks 1–4 basics: school, medical consent, insurance, routine, comfort items
## My capacity: respite, my one person, my warning signs
## Legal-status and money questions to take to [navigator / legal aid]
## Check-ins at 2 / 6 / 12 weeks — normal vs. get help
## Support routes
```

## Verification

- [ ] The child's account is truthful, age-matched, and includes "not your fault."
- [ ] No return date is promised that the caregiver does not control.
- [ ] Contact plan has a sobriety/safety rule and a no-show script.
- [ ] No criticism of the parent in the child's hearing is a written rule.
- [ ] Legal-status options are flagged as jurisdiction-specific and routed, not decided.
- [ ] Caregiver capacity has a respite slot and escalation gates.

## False-Positive Prevention

| Misfire | What it looks like | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Reading the honeymoon as "settled" | "He's fine, no problems at all" in week 2 | Expect testing at weeks 3–8; plan for it. |
| Reading grief as ingratitude | "After all I've done, she screams she hates me" | Anger at the safe adult is common; it is grief. |
| Protective lying | "Daddy's working far away" for prison | Truth in small pieces; lies are found out. |
| Cutting contact by default | Stopping calls because the child cries after | Distress after contact is normal; stop only for safety or harm. |
| Treating it as legal advice | "You should file for guardianship" | Route options to a navigator or lawyer. |
| Ignoring the carer | Plan is all child, no respite | Carer collapse is the biggest risk to the placement. |

## Adaptations

- **Grandparent:** plan for your own health appointments and a back-up carer.
- **Adult sibling as carer:** role confusion is sharper ("you're not my mum").
- **Siblings split across relatives:** schedule sibling contact as firmly as parent contact.
- **Neurodivergent child:** visual schedule of the new home and contact days.
- **Indigenous child (US):** the Indian Child Welfare Act gives the tribe a role.
- **Teen:** involve them in contact and naming decisions — discuss, do not decree.

## Example Output

```
# Kinship Care Plan — Jayden, 8, with Grandma Denise (paternal), from 14 Sep

## What Jayden has been told
Said 15 Sep: "Your mum is sick with an addiction — that's when someone's
body and brain get hooked on drugs and it's very hard to stop. She's in a
treatment place getting help. You'll live with me while she does. This is
not because of anything you did." Next: answer "when do I go home?" with
"I don't know yet. The judge and Ms. Patel will decide, and I'll tell you
when I know. You'll stay with me until then."

## Name and role
Still "Grandma." I sign school things and set bedtime (8:00). Mum is still
his mum and gets his drawings.

## Loyalty-bind map
Jayden: wants mum, likes my dog, feels bad liking it here.
Mum: ashamed; told him on the phone "Grandma took you."
Me: angry at my son's ex for the drugs, and at my son for leaving.
Rules: no adult talk about Mum when Jayden can hear. I told Ms. Patel about
the "took you" comment; she will raise it with Mum.

## Contact
Agency plan: supervised video call Wednesdays 6:00, visit at the family
centre every other Saturday 10:00. Before: snack, "you can tell Mum
anything about school." After: walk the dog, no homework that evening.
No-show script: "Mum didn't come today. I don't know why. It's not about
you. Do you want to draw her a picture or play cards with me?"

## Weeks 1–4
Enrolled at Lincoln Elementary 18 Sep (agency placement letter). Medicaid
transferred; pediatric visit 26 Sep. Blue blanket; routine chart on fridge.

## My capacity
Respite: my sister, Thursday 4–8 pm. My person: Pastor Ray. My warning
signs: second glass of wine most nights, not sleeping. Al-Anon Tuesdays.

## Questions for kinship navigator (appt 2 Oct)
Am I better as licensed kinship foster care (payments) or temporary
guardian? Child-only TANF? Who signs for his asthma medicine at school?

## Check-ins at 2 / 6 / 12 weeks — get help if:
- Talks about wanting to die or hurts himself → 988 / pediatrician today.
- Nightmares, startling, or avoidance of anything about home for >4 weeks
  → trauma-informed child therapist (ask for TF-CBT).
- School functioning drops for >4 weeks, or aggression at every visit.
Routes: state kinship navigator; GrandFamilies.org (Generations United);
211; Sesame Street in Communities materials on parental addiction.
```

## Techniques Used

- **NE-07 Emotional Validation First** — the child's grief and anger are named before any rule.
- **CM-11 Reasoning-Based Constraint Design** — each disclosure rule carries its reason (silence → self-blame; lies → lost trust).
- **MP-10 Position Mapping** — child, parent, caregiver, and relatives' wants and fears mapped to defuse loyalty binds.
- **DS-33 Jurisdiction-Adaptive Output** — legal-status and benefit options flagged as varying by state and country.
- **DP-05 Stakes-Based Gate Policy** — 2/6/12-week gates and safety triggers escalate to specialist or agency help.
