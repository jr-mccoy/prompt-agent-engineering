# LGBTQ+-Parented Family

**Objective:** Produce a family plan for an LGBTQ+-parented household: the child's
origin and family story at their current age and the next stage, a school briefing,
the child's own answers to questions, the parents' responses to hostile adults, and
when to escalate harassment or legal-parentage gaps.

**When to Use:**
- The family has two mums, two dads, a trans or nonbinary parent, a parent who came
  out after the child was born, or any LGBTQ+ parent, and the child is old enough to
  be asked about it (usually from about 3–4, when "Who's your daddy?" starts).
- The child is starting nursery or school, or a new school with unfamiliar staff.
- A relative, another parent, or a coach has said something hostile.
- **Not this prompt if** the child is the one exploring their own **gender or
  sexual identity** — that is a different conversation, not covered here (clinicians
  use `domain-psychology/populations/lgbtq-affirmative/`). If your child is
  being **bullied repeatedly**, run
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/ages-9-12/parenting_bullying_target_response_protocol.md`
  alongside this. A clinician formulating minority stress uses
  `domain-psychology/populations/lgbtq-affirmative/psychology_minority_stress_formulation.md`.
  If the issue is being a one-parent household, use
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/coparenting-family-structure/parenting_solo_parent_load_and_support.md`.

## Safety Block

- **Threats, vandalism, or physical harassment** directed at the family → document
  (dates, photos, screenshots of messages), report to police, and in the US ask
  whether it is recorded as a hate incident; tell the school if it involves pupils
  or parents there.
- **Your child is being bullied for their family and shows distress** (school
  avoidance, sleep change, self-blame) → school meeting this week; persistent
  distress or any self-harm talk → pediatrician, and 988 (US) for self-harm talk.
  The Trevor Project serves LGBTQ+ youth, including children of LGBTQ+ parents who
  are themselves questioning.
- **Legal parentage is incomplete** (only one parent is a legal parent; a
  non-biological parent has no adoption or court judgment) → see a family-law
  attorney. Marriage and birth certificates may not protect parentage everywhere;
  many attorneys recommend a confirmatory or second-parent adoption or parentage
  judgment. This prompt is not legal advice.
- Outside the US, use local police, school, and LGBTQ+ legal organisations.

## Inputs / Context

1. **Family structure** and the words each parent wants the child to use (Mama,
   Mum, Papa, Baba, a name, a nonbinary parent term like "Maddy" or "Zaza").
2. **How the child came into the family:** donor (known or ID-release or anonymous),
   surrogacy, adoption, foster care, a prior relationship, a parent's transition.
3. **Child's age,** what they know, what they have been asked.
4. **School:** type, location, local policy climate (some jurisdictions restrict
   classroom discussion of families — check), staff known.
5. **Extended family and community:** supportive, mixed, hostile.

## Method

1. **Stage the story (ST-44).** Each stage adds a layer to the same true story.
   - **2–4:** "You have two mamas. Lots of families look different. We love you." A
     picture book with families like theirs; family photos in the house.
   - **4–6:** origin basics: "To make a baby you need an egg and a sperm. We had
     eggs, and a kind man called a donor gave sperm. Doctors helped, and you grew in
     Mama Jo's tummy." Or the adoption or surrogacy equivalent.
   - **7–10:** how the family is formed, the words "gay," "lesbian," "trans," and
     what discrimination is; that some people disagree and are wrong to be unkind.
   - **11+:** donor or birth-family access, laws and history, the child's right to
     decide whom to tell.
2. **Give the child their own answers.** Practise short, true lines: "I have two
   dads." "I don't have a mum — I have two dads." "My donor isn't my dad; he helped
   make me." "That's private." The child never has to explain more than they want.
3. **Pre-empt the predictable objections (NE-23).** List the questions the child,
   classmates, school, and relatives will ask and prepare one calm answer each:
   "Which one is the real mum?" ("Both. I'm her real mum and so is Mama Jo.")
   "Isn't this confusing for kids?" ("Children understand their own families very
   well. Studies over decades find children of same-sex parents do as well as
   others.")
4. **Brief the school early.** Before the first week: one email or meeting covering
   the family, the names the child uses, both parents' contact details on every
   form ("Parent 1 / Parent 2" not "Mother / Father"), Mother's/Father's Day
   (make a card for a parent or another person, not skip it), family-tree and
   baby-photo assignments, and a request that staff respond to teasing the same way
   as any other.
5. **Hold must-not lines with hostile adults (DP-04).** Do not argue in front of
   the child; do not ask the child to defend the family; do not leave a child with
   someone who disparages their parents. One sentence and exit: "We won't discuss
   our family that way in front of our son." Repeat hostility → contact is supervised
   or ends.
6. **Adapt to place (DS-33).** Legal parentage, school policy, and safety vary by
   country and state. Name what applies locally and route it.
7. **Connect the child to families like theirs.** Peer groups and family events
   (Family Equality, COLAGE for people with LGBTQ+ parents) reduce the "only one"
   feeling.

## Output Format

```
# Our Family Plan — [parents], [child, age]

## The words we use (parent names, family words)
## [Child]'s story now (script) and the next layer at [age]
## [Child]'s ready answers (practised)
## Questions we expect and our one-line answers
## School briefing (email text) + forms, days, assignments
## Lines we hold with hostile adults
## Local legal and policy notes to check
## Community connection
## When to escalate
```

## Verification

- [ ] The story is true, age-matched, and has a next stage.
- [ ] The child has short, practised answers including "That's private."
- [ ] School briefing covers forms, names, parent days, and family-tree tasks.
- [ ] Hostile-adult responses keep the child out of the argument.
- [ ] Legal-parentage gaps are routed to a family-law attorney.
- [ ] Harassment has a documentation and reporting route.

## False-Positive Prevention

| Misfire | What it looks like | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Waiting to explain | "We'll tell her about the donor at 10" | Early, layered telling; the story grows with her. |
| Child as spokesperson | Child expected to educate the class | Parents and school carry that; the child may say less. |
| Reading every question as hostility | Anger at a 5-year-old asking "where's your mum?" | Children's curiosity is not prejudice; answer simply. |
| Skipping parent days | Child sits out Father's Day | Adapt: card for a parent, grandparent, or donor. |
| Debate in front of the child | Arguing with Grandad at dinner | One line, exit, discuss later between adults. |
| Assuming legal safety | "We're married, so we're both legal parents everywhere" | Check parentage with a family-law attorney. |

## Adaptations

- **Trans or nonbinary parent:** let the child use the parent's chosen name or
  parent word; if the parent transitioned after the child's birth, expect name
  slips and grief alongside acceptance; keep both true ("I'm still your parent").
- **Known donor:** agree the donor's role and title before birth and revisit.
- **Adoption or foster care:** combine with adoption-story work; birth parents are
  part of the story.
- **Rural or hostile area:** prioritise online and travel-to community; check school
  policy in writing.
- **Faith community:** some congregations are affirming; find one or keep the
  child out of rejecting settings.

## Example Output

```
# Our Family Plan — Jo and Kerry, with Remy (5), donor-conceived

## Words
Jo = "Mama," Kerry = "Mumma." Donor = "our donor, David" (ID-release).

## Remy's story now
"To make a baby you need an egg and a sperm. Mama and Mumma had eggs but
no sperm. A kind man named David gave some, and the doctor helped. You grew
in Mumma's tummy. Mama was there when you were born."
Next layer at 7–8: what "lesbian" means; that some people are unkind about
families like ours and they are wrong.

## Remy's ready answers (practised at bath time)
"I have a Mama and a Mumma." "I don't have a daddy. I have a donor." "That's
private."

## Expected questions
Classmate: "Who's your real mum?" → Remy: "Both."
Teacher: "Which of you is mum for the forms?" → "Both — please list us as
Parent 1 and Parent 2."
Grandad: "Doesn't she need a man in her life?" → "Remy has her uncles and
her swim coach. We won't discuss it in front of her."

## School email (sent 28 Aug to Ms. Okafor, cc. office)
"Remy has two mums, Jo (Mama) and Kerry (Mumma). Please list both of us on
all forms and contact lists. For Father's Day, Remy will make a card for
Uncle Ben. For family-tree work she can include her donor or not as she
chooses. If teasing happens, we'd like it handled as you would any other —
please let us know."

## Hostile adults
Parent at swim club said "unnatural" in Remy's hearing → Kerry: "Not in
front of my daughter." Walked away; emailed the club about its
inclusion policy.

## Local check
We live in a state where both of us are on the birth certificate; our
lawyer still advised a confirmatory adoption — completed 2023.

## Community
Family Equality summer family week; local rainbow families picnic monthly.

## Escalate if
Repeated teasing, Remy refusing school, or saying "I wish I had a dad"
with distress for >2 weeks → school meeting; pediatrician if it persists.
Threats → photos, police, school.
```

## Techniques Used

- **ST-44 Progressive Complexity Scaffolding** — one true story told in layers that grow with the child.
- **NE-23 Objection Pre-emption** — predicted questions from child, peers, school, and relatives answered in advance.
- **DP-04 Must-Not Constraints** — no debating in front of the child, no child-as-spokesperson, no care by disparaging adults.
- **DS-33 Jurisdiction-Adaptive Output** — legal parentage and school policy vary by place and are routed locally.
