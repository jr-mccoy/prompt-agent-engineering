# Foster Placement, the First Weeks

**Objective:** Produce a 30-day plan for one foster child's arrival and first weeks:
what to do on night one, how to build felt safety, how to respond to big behaviour
from a trauma-informed stance, how to handle family-time visits, what to tell the
caseworker and when, and the thresholds that mean the child or the placement needs
more help.

**When to Use:**
- A licensed (or provisionally approved) foster carer is about to receive, or has just
  received, a child who is not a relative.
- The child's behaviour in the first weeks is confusing — very compliant, very
  controlling, hoarding food, not sleeping, raging after visits.
- The carer needs a structure for visits and for reporting to the caseworker.
- **Not this prompt if** you are a **relative** who has taken in a grandchild, niece,
  or nephew — use
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/coparenting-family-structure/parenting_kinship_care_first_months.md`.
  To prepare **children already in your home** for a new placement, use
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/transitions-events/parenting_new_sibling_arrival_prep.md`.
  For body-safety teaching and disclosure response, use
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/safety-risk/parenting_body_safety_consent_lessons.md`.
  This prompt sits inside your agency's rules; where they differ, the agency wins.

## Safety Block

- **Disclosure of abuse** (past or current, including in your home or at a visit) →
  believe, do not interrogate, write the exact words, report the same day to the
  caseworker **and** the child-abuse hotline your agency names. Foster carers are
  mandated reporters in most jurisdictions.
- **Child talks about dying, self-harms, or runs away** → 988 (US) for self-harm
  talk; for a missing child, call police and the agency on-call line immediately —
  do not wait.
- **Medication, allergy, or health information is missing** → call the agency
  on-call line before giving any medicine; arrange the initial health screen within
  the agency's deadline (often days, with a full exam within about 30 days).
- **Sexualised behaviour toward other children in the home** → separate sleeping,
  line-of-sight supervision, report to the caseworker the same day for a safety plan.
- **You feel unable to keep yourself, your children, or the child safe** → call the
  agency now; ask for respite and support before a disruption becomes an emergency
  move.
- Outside the US, use your local child-protection and emergency services.

## Inputs / Context

1. **Child:** age, known history (as shared), number of prior placements, health,
   medications, school, neurodivergence, language, culture, faith, hair and skin care.
2. **Placement:** emergency or planned, expected length, permanency goal
   (reunification is the usual first goal), siblings placed elsewhere.
3. **Family-time plan:** who, how often, supervised or not, where.
4. **Your household:** other children, pets, routines, your support and respite.
5. **Agency rules:** discipline policy, photos and social-media rules, travel,
   haircuts, medical consent, reporting timelines.

## Method

1. **Arrival night: low demand, high predictability.** Show the child their bed,
   the bathroom, where food is, and how to find you at night. Offer a choice of two
   snacks. Do not do the house-rules talk tonight. Ask: "What helps you sleep?" Keep a
   light on if wanted. Let them keep all belongings — a bin bag of clothes is still
   theirs.
2. **Apply Regulate → Relate → Reason (DS-01).** Bruce Perry's sequence: a
   dysregulated brain cannot reason. First calm the body (lower voice, get low,
   water, movement), then connect ("You're safe. I'm staying."), then talk. TBRI
   (Trust-Based Relational Intervention) adds: meet physical needs first (hunger,
   thirst, sleep), give choices and "re-dos," and keep structure firm but warm.
3. **Build felt safety with trust deposits (DP-18).** Name small, repeatable deposits:
   keep every small promise, food always available (a snack basket the child can
   access stops hoarding over weeks), the same bedtime routine, telling the child
   what happens next each day. Count them; trust is built in reps, not speeches.
4. **Hold the must-nots (DP-04).** No physical punishment (prohibited for foster
   carers in nearly all agencies), no withholding food or family time as a
   consequence, no threats of moving them ("if you keep this up you'll have to
   leave"), no criticism of the birth family, no photos on social media unless the
   agency allows, no cutting the child's hair without agency permission.
5. **Prepare and decompress around family time.** Before: tell the child exactly
   when, where, and who; pack a photo or drawing. After: expect dysregulation for
   24–72 hours (sleep, bedwetting, aggression); plan a quiet evening; do not ask for
   a report on the parent. Record facts only (time, who came, child's state).
   Behaviour after visits is not evidence visits should stop — that is the
   caseworker's and court's call.
6. **Support the birth parent connection.** Speak about the parent with respect;
   with agency agreement, share school photos or a "shared parenting" notebook.
   Reunification is more likely when carers and parents work together.
7. **Write the caseworker update (NE-20).** Weekly in the first month: health,
   sleep, eating, school, behaviour patterns, visit observations (facts), needs, and
   questions. Keep a daily log; it protects the child and you.
8. **Gate escalation (DP-05)** at days 7, 14, and 30 using the thresholds below.

## Output Format

```
# First 30 Days — [child, age], placed [date], goal [reunification / other]

## Arrival night plan
## Felt-safety basics (food, sleep, belongings, daily preview)
## Big-behaviour response: regulate → relate → reason (scripts)
## Trust deposits we will make daily
## Must-nots (agency and ours)
## Family-time: before / during / after; what we log
## Birth-family connection plan
## Caseworker update template + schedule
## Day 7 / 14 / 30 check: normal vs. get help
## Support routes and respite
```

## Verification

- [ ] Arrival night has no rules lecture and includes food, sleep, and night-time access.
- [ ] Behaviour responses start with regulation, not consequences.
- [ ] Must-not list includes no physical punishment, no food or visit withholding, no threats of moving.
- [ ] Post-visit dysregulation is planned for and not used to argue against visits.
- [ ] Caseworker update is factual and scheduled.
- [ ] Disclosure and missing-child steps are explicit.

## False-Positive Prevention

| Misfire | What it looks like | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Honeymoon read as "no trauma" | Perfectly compliant 9-year-old in week 1 | Compliance can be survival; expect testing later. |
| Hoarding treated as stealing | Consequences for food under the bed | Food security first; a snack basket. |
| Visit distress read as harm | "Visits are bad for her" after one hard evening | Normal; log facts; the court decides contact. |
| Charts and points from day 1 | Reward chart on arrival | Relationship before contingencies. |
| Taking rejection personally | "He hates me" | Rejection of a new carer is loyalty and fear. |
| Over-asking about the past | Questions about what happened at home | Let the child lead; therapists take history. |

## Adaptations

- **Under 5:** routines and sensory comfort carry most of the work; keep the smell
  of home (unwashed blanket) if the child wants it.
- **Teen:** more say in room, food, and phone rules; independent-living planning
  starts early; respect their relationship with birth family and friends.
- **Different race, culture, or language:** learn hair and skin care, food, and
  faith practices; find community connections; ask the caseworker for an interpreter.
- **LGBTQ+ youth:** affirm name and pronouns as the youth wishes; agencies
  increasingly require affirming placements.
- **Indigenous child (US):** the Indian Child Welfare Act applies; support tribal and
  cultural connection.
- **Neurodivergent child:** visual schedules, sensory space, fewer transitions.

## Example Output

```
# First 30 Days — Mia, 9, placed 2 Oct (emergency), goal: reunification

## Arrival night
Arrived 8:40 pm with caseworker Ana. Showed room, bathroom, snack basket
on the low shelf ("this is yours — you don't have to ask"). Toast or
yoghurt? (toast). Night-light on. "I'm in the room next door. Knock any
time." No rules talk.

## Felt safety
Breakfast 7:15 every day. Each morning: "Today: school, then home, then
pasta." Her purple backpack stays in her room; nobody unpacks it but her.

## Big behaviour (screaming, slamming doors)
Regulate: lower my voice, sit on the floor, offer water or the trampoline.
Relate: "You're safe. I'm not going anywhere." Reason (later, calm): "Want
a re-do? Ask me again in your regular voice." No time-out alone.

## Trust deposits
Pick-up exactly 3:15. Snack basket always full. Bedtime story 8:00.

## Must-nots
No physical punishment. No taking away visits or food. Never "you'll have
to move." Nothing bad about her mum. No photos online. No haircut until
Ana agrees.

## Family time — Fridays 4–5 pm, supervised, family centre
Before: "Friday after school you'll see Mum for an hour at the centre."
After: pizza night, bath, early bed. Log facts only.
Day 3 after first visit: bedwetting, hit the wall — expected.

## Birth-family connection
With Ana's okay: school photo and a note to Mum in the shared notebook.

## Caseworker update (email Ana every Monday)
Health / sleep / eating / school / behaviour patterns / visit facts /
what Mia needs / my questions. First update 9 Oct.

## Day 7 / 14 / 30 — get help if:
- Disclosure or self-harm talk → steps in the Safety Block, same day.
- Nightmares, flashbacks, or rage most days beyond 30 days → ask Ana for a
  trauma assessment (TF-CBT or TBRI-informed therapist).
- I am losing patience daily or my own kids are unsafe → ask for respite
  and a placement-support meeting, before a crisis move.
Support: agency on-call line; foster-carer support group (2nd Tues);
respite carer approved for one weekend a month.
```

## Techniques Used

- **DS-01 Framework Application** — Perry's Regulate-Relate-Reason and TBRI principles structure the behaviour response.
- **DP-04 Must-Not Constraints** — no physical punishment, food or visit withholding, threats of moving, or criticism of the birth family.
- **DP-18 Trust Deposits Definition** — small, countable, repeatable acts that build felt safety.
- **NE-20 Third-Party Handoff Package** — weekly factual caseworker update template.
- **DP-05 Stakes-Based Gate Policy** — day 7/14/30 gates escalate to trauma assessment or placement support.
