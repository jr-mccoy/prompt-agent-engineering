# Mental Health & Behavior Prompts (Caregiver-Facing)

The caregiver's side of a child's emotional and behavioural difficulties — a young child who refuses school, lying in the early years, aggression toward siblings or parents, and the moment a school-age child talks about wanting to die. Each prompt is safety-first: it opens with the situations that need emergency or same-day help, gives the caregiver a concrete plan for home, and names the thresholds for bringing in a pediatrician or child mental-health clinician. **No prompt here diagnoses or treats a mental-health condition.**

## Scope

**In scope:**
- School refusal and drop-off distress in 5–8-year-olds: medical and safety check, four-function model, morning and handoff plan, reducing accommodations, graded return.
- Lying in 3–8-year-olds: fantasy vs. lie, lying as a developmental milestone, trap-free questions, making honesty pay.
- Aggression toward siblings or parents (5–17): written family safety plan, sibling protection, securing firearms/knives/medications, call thresholds, evaluation.
- First response when a 6–12-year-old talks about dying, suicide, or self-harm: asking directly, crisis lines, means safety, supervision, same-day assessment.

## Safety notes

Emergency routes used across this folder: **911** (US) for injury, attempt, weapon, or when a child cannot be kept safe; **988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline** (US, call/text; can connect to mobile crisis) for any talk of suicide; **Poison Control 1-800-222-1222** (US) for possible ingestion; **Childhelp 1-800-422-4453** (US) for abuse concerns. Outside the US: Canada 9-8-8 and Kids Help Phone 1-800-668-6868; UK Samaritans 116 123 and Childline 0800 1111; Australia Kids Helpline 1800 55 1800 and Lifeline 13 11 14; elsewhere findahelpline.com. Every suicidal statement — including one made in anger by a young child — gets a direct question and a same-day professional assessment. Firearms are moved out of the home where possible; all medications are locked. Bullying, abuse, or a retracted disclosure is treated as a safety issue, never as "lying" or "anxiety."
