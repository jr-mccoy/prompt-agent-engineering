# Child or Teen Aggression Toward Siblings or Parents

**Objective:** Make the home safer this week with a written family safety plan, give the
caregiver de-escalation steps that work in the moment, protect siblings, set clear
thresholds for calling for help, and start the path to an evaluation that looks for what
is driving the aggression.

**When to Use:**
- A child aged 5–17 hits, kicks, bites, chokes, or throws objects at a parent or sibling,
  or breaks things during outbursts.
- Siblings are afraid of the child, or a parent feels afraid in their own home.
- A teen is now bigger than the parent and outbursts have become dangerous.
- The caregiver is hiding injuries or the situation from others.
- **Not this prompt if** this is ordinary 4–8 sibling squabbling (fairness fights,
  occasional shoves) — use `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/ages-4-8/parenting_sibling_conflict_coach.md`.
  Toddler biting and hitting → `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/ages-0-3/parenting_biting_hitting_toddler_response.md`.
  Aggression toward peers at school → `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/ages-9-12/parenting_bullying_perpetrator_response_protocol.md`.
  An **adult** in the home is the one being violent → domestic-violence pathway (US National
  DV Hotline 1-800-799-7233) and `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/co-parenting/parenting_coparenting_with_unsafe_or_absent_parent.md`.

## Safety Block — Call Now If

- **Someone is injured, a weapon is involved, or you cannot stop the attack** → 911 (or
  your local emergency number). Ask for an officer with crisis-intervention training and
  say it is a mental-health crisis involving a child.
- **The child threatens to kill someone or themselves, or aggression comes with talk of
  dying** → 988 (US, call/text; can connect to a mobile crisis team) or the emergency
  department. Treat suicide risk and violence risk together.
- **Firearms in the home** → remove them from the home now (relative, gun shop, or law
  enforcement storage where available); if that is impossible, lock unloaded with
  ammunition locked separately, and the child must not know the codes or key location.
- **A sibling is being repeatedly hurt, or there is sexual behaviour between siblings** →
  the sibling's protection comes first; contact the pediatrician and child protection
  for advice (US: Childhelp 1-800-422-4453).
- **Sudden new aggression, confusion, or aggression after a medication change, head injury,
  or substance use** → urgent medical review.
- Outside the US: local emergency number; find crisis lines at findahelpline.com.

**This plan keeps people safer and organizes help; it does not diagnose the child.**

## Inputs / Context

1. **Child:** age, size, diagnoses or suspected conditions (ADHD, autism, anxiety, mood,
   trauma, learning or language difficulty), medications, sleep, substances (teen).
2. **Incidents:** last 3–5 — trigger, warning signs, what happened, injuries, how it ended.
3. **Who is at risk:** siblings' ages, other adults, pets.
4. **Home:** firearms, knives, medications, car keys, locks, a room that locks.
5. **Help already in place:** pediatrician, therapist, school counsellor, waitlists.
6. **Caregiver capacity:** own safety, health, support network, work.

## Method

1. **Secure the home first (DP-04).** Must-nots: no firearms accessible to the child;
   no knives or sharp tools left out; no stockpiled medications (lock all, including
   over-the-counter painkillers); no adult physically restraining the child unless trained
   and as a last resort to prevent serious injury; no sibling left alone with the child
   while incidents are frequent.
2. **Write the time-based response (AG-19).**
   - **Early warning (minutes before):** list the child's signs (pacing, swearing,
     clenched fists, "leave me alone"). Response: lower demands, fewer words, offer space,
     move siblings away calmly.
   - **During:** do not argue, lecture, or block exits; keep distance and a clear path to
     a door; siblings go to the safe room with the code word; call for help at the
     thresholds.
   - **After (the next hour):** no lecture; check injuries; let everyone calm down.
   - **Next day:** repair conversation and a short review of what happened before.
3. **Protect siblings.** A lockable safe room (or a neighbour's house), a code word, a
   phone with emergency numbers, and permission to leave and call. Tell siblings plainly
   that it is not their job to calm the child and not their fault. Watch them for fear,
   sleep problems, and withdrawal.
4. **Find the drivers (DP-06).** Common drivers include: demands beyond the child's skills
   (frustration, language, transitions); ADHD impulsivity; autistic overload; anxiety
   (aggression when cornered); mood problems (irritability, depression); trauma; sleep loss;
   hunger; screens or gaming ending; substances in teens; family stress. Note what happened
   in the 30 minutes before each incident.
5. **Reduce predictable triggers.** Warnings before transitions; solve recurring problems
   together when calm (Collaborative & Proactive Solutions approach); protected sleep;
   food before hard tasks.
6. **Repair, not shame.** The child helps fix what was broken and repairs with the person
   hurt, when calm; consequences are tied to safety (e.g., gaming time-limited if
   incidents follow gaming), never physical punishment.
7. **Get the evaluation going (DP-05).** Pediatrician appointment this week with the
   incident log; ask for referral to child psychiatry or psychology. Ask about parent-led
   programs with evidence for aggression (Parent Management Training, PCIT for younger
   children, Non-Violent Resistance for child-to-parent violence). Tell the school
   counsellor if aggression also occurs there.
8. **Tell someone.** Hidden family violence escalates; choose one or two trusted adults
   and the child's doctor.

## Output Format

```
# Family Safety Plan — [child, age]   Written: [date]   Review: [date]

## Home secured (checklist with done dates)
## Warning signs for [child]
## Our response: early warning / during / after / next day
## Sibling plan: safe room, code word, phone, what we tell them
## When we call: 911 / 988 or mobile crisis / pediatrician same-day
## Drivers we see (from incident log)
## Trigger reductions
## Repair after incidents
## Evaluation and support: appointments, referrals, school contact
## Trusted adults who know
## Incident log: | Date | Before (30 min) | Warning signs | What happened | Injury | Response | Ended how |
```

## Verification

- [ ] Firearms, knives, and medications are addressed with done dates.
- [ ] Response is written for early warning, during, after, and next day.
- [ ] Siblings have a safe room, code word, and permission to call.
- [ ] Call thresholds for 911, 988/mobile crisis, and same-day doctor are explicit.
- [ ] Drivers are drawn from logged incidents, not assumed.
- [ ] An evaluation appointment is booked or requested this week.

## False-Positive Prevention

| Misfire | What it looks like | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| "Just sibling rivalry" | One child always the target, siblings scared | Fear and injury mark aggression, not rivalry. |
| Blaming the child's character | "He's just a violent kid" | Look for drivers; most aggression has a pattern. |
| Matching force with force | Grabbing, shouting, spanking | Escalates and models violence; keep distance. |
| Siblings as peacemakers | Older sister calms him down nightly | Not a child's job; it harms her. |
| Waiting for the waitlist | No plan until the therapist appointment | Safety plan and pediatrician now. |
| Avoiding 911 at any cost | Serious injury, no call | Safety first; ask for crisis-trained responders. |

## Adaptations

- **Autistic child:** reduce sensory load, use visual warnings, and plan escape spaces;
  restraint and crowding make meltdowns worse.
- **Teen bigger than the parent:** focus on exits, a second adult, and calling for help
  early; NVR-style "we will not accept violence, and we will not fight back" statements.
- **Two homes:** both homes get the same safety plan; share the incident log.
- **Adopted, foster, or kinship child with trauma:** trauma-informed assessment; involve
  the caseworker or post-adoption service.
- **Single caregiver:** line up a neighbour or relative who can come within minutes.

## Example Output

```
# Family Safety Plan — Liam, 11   Written 4 Oct   Review 18 Oct

## Home secured
Dad's rifle to Uncle Rob's safe 4 Oct. Kitchen knives in a locked
drawer 4 Oct. All medicines in a lockbox 5 Oct.

## Warning signs
Stomping, "you always ruin everything," door slamming, red face.

## Response
Early: stop the request, "I'll give you space," take Ella (7) to her
room. During: no blocking his door; stay 2 metres away, near the
front door; Ella goes to Mum's room, locks it, code word "pancakes."
After: check injuries, no talk for an hour. Next day: 10-minute repair
talk after school snack.

## Sibling plan
Ella knows: "It's not your job to calm Liam. It's not your fault. You
can always go to Mum's room and call Grandma or 911."

## When we call
Injury or weapon → 911 (say "mental-health crisis, 11-year-old").
Threats to kill or self-harm talk → 988 or ER. Any incident with
injury → pediatrician same day.

## Drivers seen (6 incidents, 3 weeks)
5 of 6 within 20 minutes of gaming ending; 4 of 6 on nights with
<8 hours sleep. Irritable most days since summer.

## Trigger reductions
Gaming ends with a 10-minute warning and a snack; no gaming on
school nights for 2 weeks; lights-out 9 pm.

## Evaluation
Pediatrician 7 Oct with log; asking about mood and ADHD evaluation and
referral to child psychiatry. School counsellor told 6 Oct.

## Trusted adults
Grandma, Uncle Rob, Dr. Patel.
```

## Techniques Used

- **AG-19 Time-Critical Response Protocol** — actions written for early warning, during, the next hour, and the next day.
- **DP-05 Stakes-Based Gate Policy** — explicit thresholds for 911, 988/mobile crisis, and same-day medical care.
- **DP-06 Dominant Driver Identification** — the incident log identifies the drivers that the plan targets.
- **DP-04 Must-Not Constraints** — firearms, knives, medications, restraint, and unsupervised sibling time set as hard rules.
