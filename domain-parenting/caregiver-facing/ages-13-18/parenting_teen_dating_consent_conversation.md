**Purpose:** Give a caregiver the language to talk with a teen about relationships, consent, and sexual safety in a way that is accurate, affirming across identities, and useful enough to land before the teen needs it.

**When to use:** First romantic interest; first relationship; sex conversation pre-loading; concern about a current relationship; suspected coercive dynamic; after a breakup; nudes / sexting pre-loading; sextortion threat; online-dating concern; LGBTQ+ identity context.

**When NOT to use:** Acute dating-violence with safety risk; suspected statutory abuse by an adult; trafficking / exploitation indicators; sextortion in active extortion phase — escalate to the safety-action steps in this prompt and to the relevant authorities.

---

## Clinical-Signal Triggers

- **Active sextortion / extortion in progress** → do not pay; preserve evidence; report to FBI's IC3 (ic3.gov) and/or NCMEC CyberTipline (CyberTipline.org); contact platform; the teen is the victim, not the perpetrator. Reassure the teen.
- **Dating-violence injury or escalating threat** → safety planning; consider domestic-violence hotline (US 1-800-799-7233 or text START to 88788); restraining order pathway via local court.
- **Suspected adult predator (significant age gap, grooming pattern, off-platform contact)** → report to NCMEC CyberTipline and law enforcement.
- **Sexual assault disclosure** → RAINN (1-800-656-HOPE in US) and SANE-trained ED for forensic exam if within evidence window; the disclosure is the priority, not the investigation.
- **Pregnancy concern + safety risk in the relationship** → confidential adolescent-medicine or family-planning visit.

---

## Core Principles

1. **The conversation lands better before than after.** Pre-loaded sex, consent, and relationship conversations sit in the teen's repertoire when they need them. Reactive ones land into shame.
2. **Consent is enthusiastic, ongoing, specific, and revocable.** Silence is not consent. Past consent does not equal current consent. Consent to one act does not consent to another. Consent under intoxication is not valid. Consent under coercion is not valid.
3. **Healthy relationships have specific markers.** Mutuality, autonomy, comfort with disagreement, ability to be alone, sustained outside friendships, comfortable repair. Coercive control has specific markers too: monitoring, isolating, jealousy, pressure, escalating after attempts to end.
4. **Across orientations.** The conversations apply to straight, queer, trans, nonbinary teens. Orientation-specific risk and resource pieces matter (queer-affirming healthcare, dating-app safety in queer teen contexts, conversion-attempt risk in unaffirming families).
5. **Nudes are normalized in the youth context.** Pretending they aren't increases risk. Pre-load: legal exposure, sextortion vector, what to do if it happens, the fact that the teen who sends is not the criminal.
6. **The relationship with you is the safety net.** A teen who knows they can come to you without losing your relationship is a teen who comes to you when something is wrong.

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Use accurate, age-appropriate language.
- Be affirming across orientations and gender identities.
- Provide consent language that goes beyond "no means no."
- Pre-load sextortion and nudes specifically.
- Provide safety-planning language for dating violence.
- Differentiate adolescent relationship learning from coercive-control patterns.

**Must Not:**
- Use abstinence-only framing (evidence shows it underperforms on outcomes).
- Frame female-typical bodies as gatekeepers and male-typical as pursuers.
- Conflate orientation with risk.
- Frame the teen who sent a nude as the wrongdoer.
- Skip safer-sex conversation because of discomfort.
- Promise that no relationship will end badly.

## Instructions

### Stage 1 — The Pre-Loaded Relationship Conversation

```
"I want to talk about relationships before you're in one — or before
the next one, if you've started. Not interrogation. Information you
get from me before anyone else.

A healthy relationship looks like:
- You're more yourself with this person, not less.
- You can disagree and the relationship survives it.
- You still have your friends and your interests.
- You can spend time alone without panic from them or you.
- They know how to apologize. You know how to apologize.
- Sex, if and when it happens, is something both people are
  enthusiastic about, sober, and free to stop.
- They are happy when you do well, not threatened.

A controlling relationship looks like:
- They want to know where you are constantly.
- They want your password / location / notifications.
- They get jealous when you talk to anyone else.
- They isolate you from friends or family.
- They punish disagreement with silence, sulking, or anger.
- They threaten to hurt themselves if you leave.
- They escalate after you try to end it.

It can start great and slide into the second list. Trust your gut.
You're allowed to leave. I'm not your judge.

If you're in something that's gone weird, you can tell me. I won't
forbid you from your friends or your phone the moment you tell me.
I'll listen first."
```

### Stage 2 — The Consent Conversation

```
"Consent isn't 'no means no.' Consent is yes — clear, enthusiastic,
sober, ongoing, and freely given.

What that means in practice:
- Silence is not yes. Frozen is not yes. Drunk is not yes.
- Yes to one thing isn't yes to the next thing.
- Yes yesterday isn't yes today.
- 'I guess' isn't yes.
- A yes you can't actually say no to isn't yes.
- You can stop at any point. So can they. Without explanation.

This applies to whatever orientation, whatever pairing, whatever
relationship. Including — and especially — long-term relationships
where people sometimes stop checking in.

It's also yours to give and yours to revoke. Nobody is owed your
body, ever. Not because they paid for dinner. Not because you've
done it before. Not because they're upset.

If you've ever felt frozen and went along with it, that's something
worth talking about — with me, with a friend, or with a therapist.
That's not on you. Going along under pressure isn't consent."
```

### Stage 3 — The Sex Conversation (Beyond Consent)

```
"When and if you have sex, here's what I want you to have in your
head:

- Sober. Both of you.
- Talked about beforehand. What you're doing, what you're not doing,
  what you'd like to know about the other person's experience and
  status.
- Protected against pregnancy if relevant — condoms, IUD, pill,
  shot, emergency contraception (Plan B, ella) is a thing for after
  if a barrier failed or wasn't used.
- Protected against STIs — condoms work for many but not all.
  Regular STI testing if you're sexually active. PrEP exists for
  HIV prevention, and adolescent-medicine clinicians can prescribe.
- The first time isn't supposed to be perfect. It often isn't. Don't
  let media set the bar.

You can ask me anything. If you'd rather not, I can find you an
adolescent-medicine clinician or a confidential resource. Planned
Parenthood and many adolescent-medicine practices offer
confidential visits."
```

For LGBTQ+ teens, add orientation-specific framing:

```
"For [queer / lesbian / gay / bi / trans] sex specifically — same
principles, plus:

- Not all penetrative sex is equally risky for STIs or pregnancy;
  but many STIs transmit through any sexual contact.
- For trans / nonbinary teens on hormones — fertility may not be
  what you assume. Talk to your provider.
- Affirming clinicians exist. If yours isn't one, we find you one."
```

### Stage 4 — The Nudes and Sexting Pre-Load

```
"Nudes get sent. I'm not pretending they don't. So I want you to
know what I know:

- Anything you send can be screenshotted. There is no platform that
  prevents this in any reliable way.
- Once it leaves your phone, it can move anywhere. Forever.
- Sending a nude of yourself when you are under 18 is, legally, a
  felony in most US jurisdictions — even of yourself, even to a
  romantic partner. The law is not built for the reality, but it is
  the law. Teens have been charged.
- Receiving and possessing a nude of an under-18 person is also a
  felony.
- People you trust today are not always the same people in six
  months. After a breakup, things move.

Sextortion — someone threatening to release a nude unless you pay
or send more — is happening to a lot of teens. Often it's an
adult pretending to be another teen. They escalate fast. The
playbook:

  1. Don't pay. Paying does not stop it.
  2. Don't send more. Sending more does not stop it.
  3. Stop responding to the extortionist.
  4. Keep evidence — screenshots of the threats, usernames.
  5. Tell me. The very first thing — before anything else.
  6. Report: NCMEC CyberTipline (CyberTipline.org), Take It Down
     (takeitdown.ncmec.org for under-18 nudes that have been or
     might be shared), and the platform.
  7. The FBI handles the criminal side via IC3 (ic3.gov).
  8. You are the victim. You are not in trouble with me.

If this is happening: tell me right now. We act fast. You don't
get judged. We just act."
```

### Stage 5 — Online Dating and Apps

For 16+ teens (and even earlier, given platform reality):

```
"Online dating apps are part of how people meet now. Here's what
to know:

- Anyone can be anyone online. Pictures don't prove identity.
- Adults pretend to be teens. Often.
- A significant age gap online with a teen is grooming. Not
  romantic. If they're 22 and you're 16, that's not a relationship,
  that's a crime in most places.
- Real meeting: public place, told someone where, told what time,
  shared location with a friend or me, planned exit, plan-B if
  things feel off.
- Don't move off the platform fast — moving to private chat, video,
  Telegram, then to nudes — that's the grooming arc.
- Trust the gut. If it feels off, it's off. Leaving rude is fine.

For queer teens specifically — apps may be one of the few places
to meet. Same rules. Plus: predator targeting in some queer apps
is real."
```

### Stage 6 — When the Current Relationship Concerns You

```
[Approach calmly, not in front of the partner.]

"I want to check in on something. I'm not telling you what to do.

I've noticed [specific, behavioral]:
- They text constantly when you're not together.
- You've stopped seeing your friends.
- You seem to walk on eggshells about [topic].
- You stopped doing [thing you loved].
- You apologize a lot when we talk about them.

I might be wrong. I could be reading it wrong. I want to ask you,
because you're the one who knows.

How are things, actually? What's good? What's hard?

[Listen without prosecuting.]

If anything you said feels off to you too, I want you to know:
- You're allowed to leave a relationship. Even if you love them.
- I won't forbid you from seeing them — that doesn't work and
  often makes it worse.
- I will help if you need help. Always."
```

### Stage 7 — Breakup Support

Adolescent breakups can be functionally devastating. The brain treats peer-bond loss as significant.

```
"This is real. The fact that adults sometimes minimize it doesn't
make it less real. First-relationship endings hit hard because
this was the first time you felt that way about someone.

A few things:
- The intensity of feeling now does not predict how you'll feel in
  three months.
- You can be sad and functional. Eating, sleeping, moving, seeing
  one friend a day, doing one thing.
- Avoid the post-breakup drinking-or-using pattern; it doesn't
  shorten anything.
- Don't read the texts you wrote and didn't send. Don't read
  their old messages on a loop. (You will. Do less of it.)
- Their social-media presence is going to look great and that's
  the algorithm, not reality.
- Block and mute is okay. It's not childish. It's protective.
- If you're stuck more than a few weeks — sleep, energy, function
  — that may not be just the breakup. We can talk to someone.

I'm here. I won't tell you it'll be fine. But you'll be okay."
```

### Stage 8 — Safety Planning for Dating Violence

If escalating coercion or violence:

- **Don't try to break it up alone with the teen who's still attached.** That can accelerate the controlling partner.
- **Safety plan:** Phone access, escape route, code word, who to call, where to go.
- **Document:** Save threatening messages, take photos of any injury (with date stamp).
- **Resources:**
  - National Domestic Violence Hotline: 1-800-799-7233 or text START to 88788.
  - love is respect (loveisrespect.org): teen-specific.
  - Local domestic-violence shelter / advocate.
- **Restraining order pathway:** family court; teen-specific protective orders exist in many jurisdictions.
- **School:** Title IX coordinator if school context involved.
- **Therapeutic support** for teen — break-the-pattern work; this often requires real time.

### Stage 9 — Adaptations

- **LGBTQ+ teens:** affirming clinicians; chosen-family adults; queer-specific resources (Trevor Project for crisis); recognize that queer dating may happen earlier or later; don't assume orientation.
- **Trans / nonbinary teens:** affirming healthcare; pronouns; bodies-and-relationships conversations adapted; recognize misgendering during sexual contexts as a form of violation.
- **Asexual teens:** romantic without sexual is a real configuration; do not pathologize.
- **Autistic teens:** explicit social rules — coercion patterns can be harder to read; explicit naming of red flags; rehearsed scripts for declining.
- **ADHD teens:** impulsivity in romantic and sexual contexts; pre-loaded scripts more important.
- **Trauma history:** consent and safety conversations land in earlier wounds; trauma-informed approach.
- **Disability:** consent for disabled teens often skipped by surrounding adults; privacy and information matter.
- **Religious / cultural context:** family values matter; the teen needs information that keeps them safe even within cultural framing.
- **Foster / kinship / group home:** elevated risk for trafficking and grooming; structural supports needed.

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| Use abstinence-only framing | Evidence-based; safer-sex, consent, and contraception inclusive |
| Frame female bodies as gatekeepers, male as pursuers | Consent applies to all parties, all orientations |
| Conflate orientation with risk | Orientation isn't risk; specific contexts may be |
| Treat the teen who sent the nude as the wrongdoer | Sextortion victim is the teen; never the criminal |
| Tell teen to pay sextortionist | Paying does not stop it; report and preserve evidence |
| Forbid the partner as the first move | Forbidding intensifies; influence + safety planning |
| Promise no relationship ends badly | Some do; resilience comes from how the support lands |
| Skip the "freeze isn't consent" piece | Many teens go along under pressure; naming this matters |
| Skip nudes conversation because of discomfort | Reality of teen tech; pre-loading is protection |
| Treat 22-year-old + 16-year-old as romantic | This is grooming; in most jurisdictions, criminal |
| Skip the LGBTQ+ adaptation when relevant | Affirming framing is protective |
| Confront violently controlling partner alone with teen | Can accelerate violence; safety plan first |
| Treat first breakup as "puppy love" | Felt-intensity is real; respect it |
| Give all the info in one mega-conversation | Many small conversations land better than one big one |
| Skip the "you can come to me" message | This is the safety net |
