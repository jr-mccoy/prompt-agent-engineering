**Purpose:** Help a caregiver decode why a teen is disengaging from school — performance drop, attendance drop, refusal, dropout signals — by mapping the actual driver and designing a re-engagement or pathway-shift plan.

**When to use:** Grades dropping; absences accumulating; refusal to attend; sleeping all day; "I'm not going back"; midyear or end-of-year disengagement; senior slump; transfer / dropout discussion; alternative-pathway question; school-environment safety concern; suspected unmasked learning disability.

**When NOT to use:** Acute crisis (suicidality, self-harm in progress, abuse) — handle the crisis first using dedicated prompts; return for the engagement plan after.

---

## Clinical-Signal Triggers

- Disengagement + suicidal ideation → mental-health response.
- Disengagement + self-harm or substance escalation → integrated evaluation.
- Disengagement + abuse / safety risk at home or school → safety pathway.
- Disengagement + sudden personality change + social withdrawal → first-episode psychosis consideration.

---

## Core Principles

1. **Disengagement is a symptom, not a diagnosis.** The work is identifying the driver. Multiple drivers usually compound.
2. **Adolescent school disengagement is not laziness.** Treating it as laziness fails to address the driver and damages the relationship.
3. **The longer disengagement persists, the harder it is to re-engage in the same setting.** Speed matters once the pattern is clear.
4. **There are legitimate non-traditional pathways.** Alternative high schools, online schools, dual enrollment, GED + community college, gap year, apprenticeship — each is a real path for some teens.
5. **The relationship is the lever for re-engagement.** Coercion (grounding, threats, removing privileges) without addressing driver typically deepens the pattern.
6. **School systems sometimes harm teens.** Bullying, discrimination, sensory environment, identity-erasure, inflexible scheduling — these are real causes, not excuses.

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Map drivers across categories (academic, mental-health, social, identity, environment, family, sleep, substance, learning, neurodivergence).
- Differentiate disengagement from school refusal (anxiety-driven non-attendance).
- Surface alternative-pathway options without defaulting to them.
- Address the school-environment piece honestly (some schools are unsafe for some teens).
- Provide re-engagement structure proportional to driver.

**Must Not:**
- Default to consequences as the primary intervention.
- Treat alternative pathways as failure.
- Ignore identity-environment safety issues.
- Frame the teen as the only thing that needs to change.
- Skip the learning-disability / unmasked-ND consideration in adolescence.

## Instructions

### Stage 1 — Pattern Differentiation

**Disengagement (motivational / identity-based):**
- Going through motions; minimal effort; surface-level compliance.
- "Why does this matter?" energy.
- Often academic mismatch, identity-direction unclear, or boredom.
- Function preserved in non-school domains.

**School refusal (anxiety-driven):**
- Distress in anticipation of school; somatic complaints (stomachache, headache); morning meltdowns.
- Function intact at home, declines at school threshold.
- Avoidance pattern reinforces over time.

**Depression-driven:**
- Loss of interest across domains, not just school.
- Sleep, appetite, energy, mood signs.
- Function declining everywhere.

**Learning-disability-unmasked:**
- Demands increase in middle / high school.
- Was holding it together until now.
- Specific subject pattern (math, written language, executive function).
- Often paired with shame.

**Identity-environment-driven:**
- LGBTQ+ teen in unaffirming school.
- BIPOC teen in racist environment.
- Bullying that adults haven't addressed.
- Sexual harassment, assault history at school.
- Going-to-school is unsafe in a specific, identifiable way.

**Substance-driven:**
- Use during school day; use disrupting sleep and morning routine; use as primary daily activity.

**Family-driven:**
- Acute family stress (parental illness, divorce, death, financial collapse).
- Parentification (teen managing siblings, work).
- Home environment unsafe.

**Sleep-driven:**
- Chronic sleep deprivation; circadian phase delay; school start time mismatch.
- Often co-occurs with other drivers.

**Boredom / mismatch:**
- Material is far below or far above level.
- Specific learning style or interest unaccommodated.
- Was engaged once; lost it.

### Stage 2 — Direct Conversation With the Teen

```
"I want to talk about school. Not to lecture you. Not to ground
you. To understand.

[Pause for engagement.]

Walk me through a typical school day. From when you wake up. What's
the worst part? What's the part you'd skip first? What classes are
okay, what classes are bad?

[Listen without interrupting.]

What would have to change for school to be more bearable?

[Listen.]

What do you think about when you think about not going / not
finishing?

[Listen.]

Some of what you said tells me [reflective, specific].

I'm not going to assume the answer. We're going to look at this
together — with the school, maybe with someone outside school, and
with you driving some of the decisions."
```

### Stage 3 — Driver Mapping Worksheet

Score 0–3 (none / some / significant / dominant) across:

- Academic mismatch (under or over level)
- Anxiety / school refusal pattern
- Depression
- ADHD or executive function
- Learning disability
- Autism / sensory environment
- Bullying / harassment
- Identity environment (LGBTQ+, race, religion)
- Romantic / friend social
- Substance use
- Sleep
- Family stress / parentification
- Trauma re-emergence
- Loss of meaning / "what's this for"
- Physical health (chronic illness, pain, GI, migraines)

The drivers scored 2–3 are your work.

### Stage 4 — School-Side Levers

Engage with school proportional to driver:

**For academic mismatch:**
- Course-level review (move up, move down, change track).
- Dual enrollment with community college for under-stimulated teens.
- Tutoring / specific subject support.
- Schedule rebuild.

**For learning disability:**
- Educational evaluation (school or independent).
- IEP (Individualized Education Program) under IDEA if eligible.
- 504 plan for accommodations.
- Specific reading / math interventions.
- Executive-function coaching.

**For anxiety / school refusal:**
- Counselor involvement.
- Modified attendance during ramp.
- Safe-spaces (counselor's office, library, sensory room).
- Therapy outside school (CBT for anxiety, exposure-based gradual return).
- 504 plan with anxiety-specific accommodations.

**For depression:**
- Mental-health treatment outside school.
- Counselor coordination.
- Modified workload during treatment.
- 504 plan.

**For bullying / harassment:**
- Title IX coordinator if sexual harassment / assault.
- Anti-bullying policy reporting.
- School-counselor escalation.
- Documentation.
- Schedule changes.
- Transfer if school cannot make safe.

**For identity environment:**
- GSA (Gender-Sexuality Alliance) presence.
- Policies on names, pronouns, bathrooms.
- Affirming counselor or staff.
- If school cannot make safe — transfer is legitimate.

**For ADHD / 2e:**
- Evaluation if not done.
- Medication conversation if untreated.
- 504 / IEP.
- Specific accommodations (extra time, separate setting, frequent breaks, organizer support).

### Stage 5 — Alternative-Pathway Decision

Some teens won't re-engage with the current school regardless of supports. That's information.

**Alternative options to evaluate:**

- **School transfer** within district.
- **Alternative high school / continuation school** within district (often smaller, more flexible).
- **Online / hybrid public school.**
- **Charter school** with different model.
- **Independent / homeschool.**
- **Dual enrollment** at community college (some teens disengage in high school but engage with adult classroom).
- **Early college** programs.
- **GED + community college** (legal in most states at 16; often a viable pathway for teens who will not complete traditional high school).
- **Apprenticeship / trade pathway** (varies by state; can begin at 16 in many).
- **Gap year** with structured plan.
- **Recovery-supportive school** if substance is core driver.
- **Therapeutic boarding** for severe combined cases (controversial; vet rigorously; many programs harm rather than help; only with specialist guidance).

### Stage 6 — Re-Engagement Plan

Match the plan to driver:

```
[Plan structure]

Driver(s) identified:
Goal: [re-engage in current setting / transition to alternative
       setting / GED+ pathway / pause-and-stabilize]

Immediate interventions (this week):
- [Mental-health appointment if relevant]
- [School meeting if relevant]
- [Sleep restoration steps if relevant]
- [Modified attendance if needed]

Within 30 days:
- [Evaluation if relevant]
- [Schedule / accommodation changes]
- [Therapy started]
- [School plan defined]

Within 90 days:
- [Re-engagement assessment]
- [Pathway-shift decision if not re-engaging]

Pause-and-stabilize criteria:
- If [X] persists, we shift the plan to [alternative pathway].
```

### Stage 7 — When School Is the Wrong Container

For some teens, the current school is genuinely the problem. Signs:

- Teen functions in other contexts.
- Identifiable environment issue (bullying, identity-unsafety, learning mismatch).
- School has been told and not adequately responded.
- Teen is showing trauma signs from school environment.

In these cases, the work is not getting the teen "back to school." It's finding a container that fits.

### Stage 8 — Avoiding the Punishment Spiral

Common spiral:
- Teen disengages.
- Caregiver punishes (grounding, removing phone, removing car).
- Teen disengages further.
- Caregiver escalates.
- Relationship breaks.
- Teen drops out.

Break the spiral by separating:
- Driver work (therapy, evaluation, school engagement).
- Operational expectations (what is the teen actually expected to do).
- Relationship (kept warm and intact).

Consequences for non-engagement may have a place once driver is being addressed; they are rarely the lever that moves things.

### Stage 9 — Adaptations

- **ADHD:** unmasked in middle / high school as demands rise; medication, executive-function coaching.
- **Autistic:** sensory environment, social complexity, identity-based exclusion; ND-affirming clinician + school plan.
- **2e (gifted + ND):** intellectual engagement gap is huge; dual enrollment, specialized programs.
- **Trauma history:** school as trauma trigger; trauma-informed approach.
- **LGBTQ+:** affirming environment matters; transfer is legitimate if current school is unsafe.
- **BIPOC teens:** racism in school environment is real; specialist support, transfer where needed.
- **Chronic illness or disability:** medical 504 plan; flexible attendance.
- **First-gen / immigrant family:** school-system navigation may need cultural-broker help; advocacy.
- **Lower-SES family:** access to therapy, evaluation, alternative schools is a real barrier; school-based supports may be primary access point.
- **Custody / split household:** consistent plan across households; school informed of both households.
- **Twin / sibling effects:** if siblings are engaged, the contrast can intensify; protect each kid's pathway separately.

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| Treat disengagement as laziness | Driver work; multiple drivers usually compound |
| Default to consequences as primary intervention | Address driver; consequences alone deepen pattern |
| Treat alternative pathways as failure | Some teens succeed in non-traditional containers |
| Ignore identity-environment safety | LGBTQ+, BIPOC unsafety is real driver, not excuse |
| Skip learning-disability consideration in adolescence | Often unmasked when demands increase |
| Frame teen as the only thing that needs to change | Sometimes the school is wrong container |
| Push the teen back into the same school without addressing driver | Same setting + same driver = same outcome |
| Confuse school refusal with disengagement | Refusal is anxiety-driven; different intervention |
| Skip mental-health evaluation | Depression / anxiety masquerade as motivation |
| Assume substance isn't part of it | High overlap; consider |
| Treat senior slump as universal "phase" | Sometimes signal of depression / ED / substance |
| Default to therapeutic boarding | Many programs harm; rigorous vetting required |
| Forget sleep / circadian piece | Often compound driver; addressable |
| Overweight school's narrative | Teen and caregiver have their own data |
| Wait for failure before alternative-pathway conversation | Earlier conversation = better options |
| Assume GED is "less than" | Legal, viable, often-overlooked legitimate path |
