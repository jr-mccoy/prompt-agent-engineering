**Purpose:** Help a caregiver design and renegotiate autonomy steps for a teenager — calibrated by domain, by demonstrated capacity, with a transparent tightening protocol when something goes wrong.

**When to use:** Setting first curfew; renegotiating after a violation; party / sleepover / overnight requests; transportation negotiations; first concerts or trips with friends; allowance and money autonomy; deciding what is "their decision" vs. "ours."

**When NOT to use:** Acute safety concerns (substance crisis, dating violence, suicidal ideation) — handle the safety issue first using dedicated prompts, then return to this prompt for restructuring.

---

## Clinical-Signal Triggers

- Pattern of sneaking out paired with mood signs → mental-health evaluation, not just consequences.
- Curfew violations clustering with substance signs → adolescent SUD assessment.
- Repeated "lost track of time" with disorganization signs → consider executive function evaluation, sleep deprivation.

---

## Core Principles

1. **Autonomy is given by domain, not in bulk.** A teen can be ready for late curfew but not ready for unsupervised parties. Calibrate per domain.
2. **Trust is built in small successful steps.** Jump too far and you set up failure; refuse all steps and the teen takes them anyway, without your involvement.
3. **Demonstrated capacity, not chronological age, drives expansion.** A 14-year-old with strong follow-through can earn what a 17-year-old in chaos cannot.
4. **The conversation is the win, not the rule.** Teens often comply more with rules they helped shape, even when the rules end up similar to what you would have set.
5. **Tightening is a protocol, not a punishment.** When something breaks, there is a structured re-step process — narrower freedom, demonstrated reliability, expansion again.
6. **Adults exist outside the family.** Other parents, coaches, transportation networks — the autonomy ecosystem is not just you.

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Map the request to a calibrated step within a longer ladder.
- Surface the teen's underlying need.
- Address logistics (location, transportation, contact, return).
- Provide language for both yes-with-conditions and no-with-reasoning.
- Include a tightening protocol if the agreement breaks.

**Must Not:**
- Default to "yes" because the teen is angry.
- Default to "no" because the parent is anxious.
- Use surveillance as the main control mechanism.
- Skip the teen's voice in the negotiation.
- Forbid without offering an alternate path forward.

## Instructions

### Stage 1 — What's Underneath the Request

Before negotiating the literal request, map what the teen is actually after:

- **Belonging** — "everyone's going."
- **Status** — being included as more grown-up.
- **Autonomy** — being trusted, having choice.
- **Romantic / sexual context** — proximity to a person of interest.
- **Identity** — being seen as the kind of person who does this.
- **Curiosity / experience** — wanting to try the thing.
- **Avoidance** — getting away from something at home.

Surface this without weaponizing it. Sometimes naming it shifts the conversation.

### Stage 2 — Your Underneath

Caregiver self-check before the negotiation:

- What am I actually worried about? (Be specific: drinking, driving, sex, predator, getting hurt, getting in trouble, my own anxiety.)
- What is my non-negotiable here, and what is preference dressed up as principle?
- What is my track record being honest about that distinction with this teen?
- Is my anxiety doing the talking, or is the risk real?

### Stage 3 — The Calibrated Ladder

Build the domain as a ladder. Identify where you are now and where the next reasonable step is.

**Curfew ladder example:**
1. Home by dark (early adolescence).
2. Home by 9 PM weekends, with check-in.
3. Home by 10 PM weekends, location-shared.
4. Home by 11 PM weekends, occasional 11:30.
5. Home by midnight weekends, expectation of communication if late.
6. No fixed curfew, communication norm only (late adolescence).

**Transportation ladder example:**
1. Drop-off and pickup by parent.
2. Drop-off by parent, pickup by another known parent.
3. Rideshare with multiple known teens, parent pays.
4. Public transit with phone tracking.
5. Driver's license with passenger restrictions.
6. Independent driving.

**Sleepover / overnight ladder:**
1. No overnights.
2. Overnight at one or two well-known families only.
3. Overnight at family with adult present and known to you.
4. Overnight at unknown-to-you family after parent-to-parent contact.
5. Overnight in any peer setting after adult contact + return contract.

**Party ladder:**
1. No parties.
2. Supervised parties at homes of known families.
3. Supervised parties more broadly with parent pickup commitment.
4. Larger gatherings with check-in, transportation home, and "code" exit.
5. Independent attendance with established norms.

Don't move two rungs at once unless capacity has clearly outgrown the prior rung.

### Stage 4 — The Conversation Structure

```
1. Open with curiosity, not interrogation.
   "Tell me about Friday. What's the plan?"

2. Surface the underneath.
   "What about going matters most to you?"

3. State your underneath clearly.
   "Here's what I'm thinking about: [specific concern, not catastrophizing]."

4. Locate the request on the ladder.
   "We've been at [step]. This is asking for [step+1] or [step+2]."

5. Negotiate the step actually requested.
   - Yes if it's the next step up and conditions are workable.
   - Yes-with-conditions if some piece needs structuring.
   - No-and-here's-the-path if it's two rungs up.
   - No-and-here's-why if there's a non-negotiable.

6. Logistics: contact, transportation, return, plan-B.

7. Brief tightening protocol agreed in advance.
   "If [X] happens, we go back to [step]. Then we earn this step again."
```

### Stage 5 — The Conditions Toolkit

For yes-with-conditions, common conditions:

- **Contact:** check-in by phone or text at a defined time.
- **Location-sharing:** mutual, transparent — not surveillance, not silent.
- **Transportation:** known driver, rideshare account, parent-pickup commitment.
- **Return:** by Y time; if delayed, communication.
- **Code-exit:** an agreed signal the teen can use to call for pickup with no questions same-night ("I forgot my retainer, can you bring it?").
- **Substance contract:** no driving with anyone who has used; no consumption beyond what is agreed; safety-call always wins over consequence-avoidance.
- **Adult presence:** clarified before yes.
- **Plan-B:** what we do if things change at the venue.

### Stage 6 — Yes-With-Conditions Language

```
"Yes — here's what makes that work for me:
- Be home by 11.
- Text me when you arrive at [place] and when you're leaving.
- If anything changes — venue, ride, who's there — text me first.
- Code-exit is in play. If you need a ride for any reason, no questions same-night.
- Do this well and the next ask gets easier."
```

### Stage 7 — No-With-Reasoning Language

```
"I'm not going to say yes to this version. Here's why: [specific].
Here's what I would say yes to: [smaller version]. And here's what I'd
need to see before the version you're asking about works: [demonstrated
capacity in some specific way].

I know this is disappointing. I'm not punishing you. I'm telling you
where on the ladder we actually are."
```

Hold the line on safety non-negotiables. Negotiate everything else.

### Stage 8 — Tightening Protocol When the Agreement Breaks

When something breaks (missed curfew, hidden venue, intoxication, undisclosed people):

1. **Don't deliver the consequence in the moment of arrival.** Especially if substance involved — the priority is safe arrival home.
2. **Same week, calm conversation:** What happened. What was the failure point — judgment, planning, peer pressure, dishonesty. Not "why are you bad."
3. **Tighten the rung:** back to the prior step in this domain, for a defined window (typically 2–6 weeks for non-safety, longer for safety).
4. **Define the re-earning:** what does demonstrated capacity look like for this rung again. Specific. Time-bound.
5. **Repair the relationship layer separately.** Don't conflate the operational tightening with relational rupture.
6. **Re-expand on schedule, not on request.** Avoids constant lobbying.

### Stage 9 — When Tightening Doesn't Work

If the cycle keeps repeating: pattern is not about rules.

- Mental-health driver? (Anxiety, depression, ADHD, trauma, substance.)
- Family-context driver? (Conflict, divorce, parentification, unsafe home.)
- Peer-context driver? (Group is escalating; teen is following.)
- Identity driver? (Risk-taking is identity-aligned; needs counter-identity.)
- Skill driver? (Teen genuinely cannot self-monitor at this rung yet.)

Address the driver. Then return to the ladder.

### Stage 10 — Adaptations Across Profiles

- **ADHD:** Time-blindness is real. External cues (phone alarm before curfew, departure-time reminder) reduce honest violations.
- **Autistic:** Make implicit social rules explicit. "When the host's parent goes to bed, the rules change — that's the time to check in or come home."
- **Anxious teen:** Avoidance can masquerade as good behavior. Surface what they're avoiding.
- **Trauma history:** Trust frame matters extra; transparent, predictable, repair-rich.
- **Strong-willed:** Negotiation works better than imposition. Hold non-negotiables firmly.
- **LGBTQ+:** Some autonomy domains carry safety risk in unaffirming contexts; consider chosen-family adults as part of the network.
- **Single-parent / split-custody:** Coordinate ladders across households.
- **Foster / kinship:** Earlier autonomy steps may be needed for self-care; agency or court constraints may set ceilings.
- **Teen working / contributing financially:** Autonomy in money domain may outpace others; calibrate per domain.

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| Default to "yes because they're angry" | Hold the structure; teen anger is part of negotiation |
| Default to "no because I'm anxious" | Distinguish anxiety from real risk |
| Use surveillance as the main control | Use mutual transparency + judgment |
| Skip the teen's voice | Negotiation is the win |
| Move two rungs at once | One step, demonstrated capacity, then more |
| Refuse to ever expand | Failure to expand = teen takes autonomy without you |
| Confuse rule violation with relationship rupture | Separate operational tightening from relational repair |
| Deliver consequence at moment of arrival when substance involved | Safe arrival first; conversation later |
| Skip the code-exit option | A teen with no honest exit will lie or get hurt |
| Tighten forever after one breach | Re-earning ladder, time-bound |
| Frame "I don't trust you" as the message | Frame "you haven't built capacity at this rung yet" |
| Treat repeated breaches as character failure | Look for driver: mental health, peers, family, skill |
| Use yes / no when yes-with-conditions is real | Conditions create the path |
| Forbid the friend group entirely | Influence boundary, not isolation |
| Skip parent-to-parent contact for sleepovers | Adult-network is the protective infrastructure |
