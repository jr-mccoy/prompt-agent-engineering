**Purpose:** Help a caregiver have the post-secondary pathway conversation in a way that holds the teen's voice as the center, surfaces real financial and structural constraints, and treats non-college pathways as legitimate rather than fallback.

**When to use:** Sophomore / junior / senior year of high school; teen pushing back on college expectation; teen pushing toward expensive school caregiver can't afford; gap-year discussion; trade-school or apprenticeship interest; military consideration; undecided teen; community-college path; first-gen college family; neurodivergent teen with college fit questions; family pressure / disagreement about pathway.

**When NOT to use:** Acute mental-health crisis (handle that first; pathway can wait). Acute family conflict requiring repair before substantive conversation (use repair prompt first).

---

## Clinical-Signal Triggers

- Teen showing depression / anxiety triggered by pathway pressure → mental-health response.
- Family pressure escalating to relationship rupture → repair pathway, family therapy consideration.
- Teen with major executive-function or mental-health concern considering large-residential college → pre-college planning that includes treatment continuity.

---

## Core Principles

1. **Post-secondary pathway is the teen's life.** The caregiver's job is to inform, scaffold, and honor — not to choose.
2. **There are at least eight legitimate pathways:** 4-year residential college, 4-year commuter / urban college, community college, dual community-college-to-4-year transfer, trade / apprenticeship, military, work + part-time school, gap year with structure. Each fits different teens.
3. **College is not the safe default.** A bad-fit college experience that ends in debt and dropout is worse than a thoughtful trade or community-college start.
4. **Money is part of the conversation.** Pretending it isn't sets up worse decisions. Teens deserve honest financial information.
5. **Neurodivergent and 2e teens often need a different route.** Not because they're less capable, but because the fit considerations are different.
6. **The pathway is not permanent.** People change pathways. The choice at 18 isn't the choice forever.
7. **First-gen college families navigate translation.** The system is opaque to people who haven't navigated it. The teen often becomes the translator. Caregivers can support without pretending to know the system.

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Treat the teen as the decision-maker.
- Honor multiple legitimate pathways without ranking by default.
- Include financial reality.
- Address ND / 2e fit considerations specifically.
- Surface first-gen-college dynamics where relevant.
- Differentiate caregiver pressure from caregiver support.

**Must Not:**
- Default to 4-year college as the safe answer.
- Treat trade / military / work as fallback.
- Hide financial reality from the teen.
- Project the caregiver's regrets or wishes onto the teen's pathway.
- Frame gap year as "lost time."
- Skip the mental-health-fit considerations.

## Instructions

### Stage 1 — Surface the Caregiver-Side Pressure (Quietly)

Before the conversation with the teen, audit yourself:

- What do I want for my teen and why?
- What of that is about them, and what is about me — my pride, my regrets, my fears, my class, my community standing?
- What did my parents want for me at this age, and how did that land?
- Where am I projecting my own pathway (or wished pathway) onto theirs?
- What financial guilt am I carrying that's leaking into the conversation?
- What community pressure am I absorbing that's coming out as ultimatum?

The conversation works better when this is named to yourself first.

### Stage 2 — Map the Teen's Actual Position

```
"I want to talk about what comes after high school. Not to push
you toward an answer. To understand where you are.

What are you thinking right now — even if it's confused, even
if it changes by the day, even if you have no idea?

What do you imagine yourself doing at 22? Even if it's vague.

What kind of life do you want by 30? Money, location, work,
relationships, what you spend your time on?

What's appealing about [pathway]? What's the hesitation?

What do you wish someone would tell you?"
```

Listen long. Don't redirect.

### Stage 3 — Map the Eight Pathways

**1. Four-year residential college:**
- Best fit for: academically engaged, ready for full independence, social-residential life is a draw, has financial support or aid.
- Watch out for: high cost; loneliness for some; mental-health risk in transition; not all majors require it.
- Cost reality: $30K–$80K+/year sticker; aid varies dramatically.

**2. Four-year commuter or urban college:**
- Best fit for: academically engaged, prefers / needs to live at home or in city, lower cost path, family / care responsibilities.
- Watch out for: less residential community; commuter loneliness; transit time.
- Cost reality: lower than residential.

**3. Community college (2-year):**
- Best fit for: cost-conscious, undecided major, exploring before committing, getting transferable credits, working alongside school, ND teens benefiting from smaller setting.
- Watch out for: cultural assumption that "college = 4-year" can make teens feel less-than; structure of transferring requires planning.
- Cost reality: dramatically cheaper; many states have free community college.

**4. Community-college-to-4-year transfer:**
- Best fit for: most of community college fits; later transfer to 4-year for specific major / credential.
- Watch out for: course articulation matters; some transfers lose credits; advising critical.
- Cost reality: significant savings; often best financial path to bachelor's degree.

**5. Trade / apprenticeship:**
- Best fit for: hands-on learner; immediate income; specific field draw (electrician, plumber, welder, machinist, HVAC, carpentry, automotive, IT, healthcare-allied like surgical tech / paramedic, etc.); many can later add management or business.
- Watch out for: physical demands of some trades; long-term career planning; gendered cultural pushback.
- Cost reality: many apprenticeships pay during training; trade school typically cheaper than 4-year.
- Wage reality: many skilled trades earn at or above bachelor's-degree median.

**6. Military:**
- Best fit for: structure-thrives, specific career interest the military offers, GI-Bill college funding, identity-fit.
- Watch out for: this is a multi-year commitment with significant constraints; mental-health ramifications; physical risk; career fit varies by branch and MOS / rate / job; recruiter promises are not contracts.
- Decision considerations: ASVAB results, MEPS, branch differences, contract-reading.

**7. Work + part-time school:**
- Best fit for: financial necessity, specific work interest, building track record, undecided.
- Watch out for: full-time-work-while-studying takes longer; burnout risk; tuition reimbursement from employer can be powerful path.

**8. Gap year (structured):**
- Best fit for: burned-out, undecided, needs maturation time, wants real-world experience, mental-health recovery period, specific opportunity (work, travel, service program).
- Watch out for: unstructured gap year often becomes drift; structure matters.
- Specific structures: AmeriCorps / City Year / NCCC; employment with savings goal; structured travel (work-study, language); paid internship.
- Cost reality: gap-year programs vary; AmeriCorps pays a stipend + education award; employment-based can save toward subsequent education.

### Stage 4 — The Financial Reality Conversation

Honest, not punishing:

```
"I want to tell you what's real on the money side. Not to limit
your options — to inform them.

[Specific numbers:]
- We can pay [X] per year out of current income / savings.
- We can / can't take on more debt as parents.
- You can / cannot expect [specific] from grandparents.
- Federal student loans you could take on top: max ~$5,500
  freshman year, increasing through college, capped around
  $31K for undergrad as dependent student.
- Pell Grant available for families under [income threshold].
- State-specific aid [if applicable].

What this means in practice:
- [Specific school] sticker is [X]. Their typical net price for
  our income is [Y, from net price calculator on their site].
  After aid, we'd need [Z] from us / loans / your work.
- [Specific community college path] would cost about [X] total
  including the transfer to 4-year, with you graduating with
  [Y] in debt.
- [Trade school] is [X], with apprenticeship paying you [Y]
  during training.

This isn't to scare you. It's so you decide with real numbers."
```

Resources:
- **FAFSA** (Free Application for Federal Student Aid) — completed senior year of HS; required for federal aid; required by most colleges for institutional aid.
- **CSS Profile** — used by many private colleges for institutional aid.
- **Net Price Calculator** — every college has one on their site; gives more accurate cost than sticker price.
- **Pell Grant** for lower-income families.
- **State scholarship programs** vary widely.

### Stage 5 — Mental-Health and Fit Considerations

Mental-health factors in pathway choice:

- Teen with stable mental-health treatment in place → continuity matters in any transition.
- Teen with active anxiety / depression → smaller, closer-to-home, lower-stakes settings often better than large residential away.
- Teen with substance-use history → recovery-supportive college programs exist; sober housing.
- Teen with eating-disorder history → transition is high-risk window; treatment continuity essential.
- Teen with chronic illness → disability services on campus; medical-care accessibility.
- Teen with executive-function challenges → smaller, more-structured settings often better than large unstructured residential.

The right college is the one the teen can actually be at, not the most prestigious one they got into.

### Stage 6 — ND / 2e Specific Considerations

- **ADHD:** disability services offices, executive-function coaching, smaller class sizes, structured residential life. Some colleges have specific ADHD programs.
- **Autistic:** sensory environment, dorm setup, social structure, autistic-affirming services. Some colleges have specific autism-support programs.
- **Dyslexia / specific LD:** disability services, alternative-format texts, extended testing time, note-taking support.
- **2e (gifted + ND):** intellectual fit + support match; some honors colleges fit; some traditional colleges don't accommodate well.
- **PDA profile:** highly self-directed pathways often fit better; rigid college structures can backfire.
- **Documentation:** disability services require documentation from K–12; obtain before transition.

### Stage 7 — First-Gen College Considerations

If the teen would be the first in family to attend college:

- Acknowledge openly: "I haven't navigated this; we'll figure it out together."
- Use school college counselor more heavily.
- College access programs: TRIO, Upward Bound, QuestBridge, Posse, KIPP Through College, similar.
- First-gen orientation programs at many colleges.
- Awareness: family expectation can collide with college reality (dorm life, summer-at-school, expensive textbooks, social-class differences).
- Translation work in both directions: family ↔ college.

### Stage 8 — When Family Pressure Is the Issue

If caregiver / family is pushing in a direction the teen resists:

- Step back from advocacy.
- Articulate your worry honestly without ultimatum.
- Distinguish "I want this for you" from "I will withdraw support if you don't."
- Recognize that pathway is the teen's life; coercion poisons the relationship and often the pathway too.
- If extended family pressure is part of it, hold the teen's pathway as your job to protect from that pressure.
- Consider: if the teen pursues against your wishes, what is your stance? Define before the moment of decision.

### Stage 9 — When Teen Is Undecided

For genuinely undecided teens at 17–18:

- Undecided is not a failure; it's a real position.
- Pathways that preserve flexibility: community college, gap year with structure, work + part-time school.
- 4-year college as undecided major works for some; not for others (cost without direction is risky).
- "Take a year, get a job, see what you want to do" — for some teens, this is the answer.
- Pressure to choose by 18 is artificial; many teens decide between 19 and 22.

### Stage 10 — Adaptations

- **First-gen / immigrant family:** translation in both directions; college-access programs; cultural-broker support.
- **LGBTQ+ teens:** affirming campus environment matters; LGBTQ+ campus index resources; safety-state considerations.
- **Trans / nonbinary teens:** healthcare access, dorm policies, name / pronoun policies, state-political climate.
- **Religious family / teen:** religious-college fit if relevant; family-religion negotiation if teen is moving differently.
- **Teen with parent dependency / care responsibility:** pathway often constrained by family role; honor this; community college / commuter often the path; future-planning matters.
- **Lower-SES family:** financial-aid focus; community-college pathway; trade pathway; FAFSA / state-aid attention.
- **Higher-SES with college-pressure family:** pressure-audit; teen voice; trade pathway often resisted by family.
- **Custody / split household:** financial-aid forms require custodial parent income; coordinate.
- **Foster / kinship / adopted teens:** specific aid programs (ETV, Foster Youth Tuition Waivers in some states); transition supports.
- **Teens with serious mental-health history:** treatment continuity; closer-to-home often better in early transition.
- **Athlete pathway:** NCAA / NAIA / NJCAA differences; recruiting timeline; injury risk; identity-after-sport planning.
- **Military-family teens:** military pathway often familiar; alternative pathways may be less explored.

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| Default to 4-year college as safe answer | Multiple legitimate pathways; fit matters |
| Treat trade / military / work as fallback | Equal-status pathways for fit teens |
| Hide financial reality | Teen deserves honest numbers |
| Project caregiver regrets / wishes | Audit and contain |
| Push for "best" school regardless of fit | Fit > prestige; affordable + fit > expensive + fit |
| Treat undecided as failure | Real position; pathway-flexibility is a strategy |
| Treat gap year as lost time | Structured gap year is investment |
| Skip mental-health continuity in transitions | Transitions are high-risk windows |
| Frame ND teen's college path as same as neurotypical | Fit considerations differ |
| Skip FAFSA / net-price-calculator in financial conversation | These are concrete tools |
| Pretend you know the system as first-gen family | Use school counselors, access programs |
| Use ultimatum ("we won't support you if") | Coercion poisons pathway and relationship |
| Frame community college as failure | Often best financial path to bachelor's |
| Skip apprenticeship-pays-you piece in trade conversation | Differentiates from college-debt model |
| Treat decision at 18 as permanent | Pathway shifts are normal |
| Override teen voice with extended family pressure | Caregiver protects teen's pathway from extended pressure |
