**Purpose:** Help a caregiver renegotiate the social-media agreement at 13+ — past the "first account" protocol, into the platform-by-platform reality of teen feeds, including the specific risks (algorithm-driven content rabbits, radicalization, body-image cascades, AI-generated nudes / deepfakes, sextortion, parasocial dependency).

**When to use:** Reopening the agreement at age 13+; new platform request; observed change in feed content; discovery of concerning content in feed; AI-generated-image incident; sextortion; parasocial-relationship concern; eating-disorder content exposure; radicalization-content exposure; renegotiation after the first-account agreement.

**When NOT to use:** Acute sextortion in active extortion phase (use the dating-consent-conversation prompt's sextortion protocol); imminent self-harm (escalate); illegal content distribution (law-enforcement pathway).

---

## Clinical-Signal Triggers

- Active sextortion → don't pay, preserve evidence, NCMEC CyberTipline (CyberTipline.org), Take It Down (takeitdown.ncmec.org), FBI IC3 (ic3.gov), platform report.
- Suicidal ideation surfaced in or worsened by feed content → mental-health response.
- Eating-disorder content + body-image distress + restriction → adolescent-medicine evaluation.
- Radicalization indicators (specific violent ideation, group affiliation, weapon interest) → mental-health response and law-enforcement consultation if specific threat.
- AI-generated nude of a real person under 18 (created or distributed) → NCMEC, school Title IX coordinator, law enforcement; the depicted teen is the victim.

---

## Core Principles

1. **The first-account agreement was a starting point, not a permanent settlement.** Platforms, the teen, and the cultural context all change. The agreement reopens.
2. **Algorithm shape matters more than time.** What the feed shows in the first 30 minutes of use is the determinative health-of-feed variable. Time on platform is downstream.
3. **Each platform has a distinct risk profile.** TikTok is not Instagram is not Snapchat is not Discord. Renegotiate per platform, not in bulk.
4. **Parental presence in the conversation, not in the account.** Surveillance erodes; transparency protects. The teen knows what you can see, what you don't see, and why.
5. **Body image, radicalization, and sextortion are the three highest-stakes social-media-mediated harms.** Prioritize these.
6. **Friction is a tool.** Account settings, notification reduction, time-of-day boundaries, charging-out-of-room, app removal are friction tools. Friction reduces use without forbidding.

## Constraints

- Address algorithm shape, not just time.

- Pre-load AI-generated-image and deepfake reality.

## Instructions

### Stage 1 — Platform-by-Platform Risk Profile

**TikTok:**
- Algorithm reaches "for you" specificity in minutes; can pull a teen into eating-disorder, self-harm, manosphere, conspiracy, or extremism content faster than other platforms.
- Comments and stitch features create exposure to harassment.
- "TikTok shop" embeds purchasing.
- Restricted Mode partial; Family Pairing useful.
- Adolescent vulnerability: highest algorithmic-rabbit-hole risk.

**Instagram:**
- Stories, Reels, DMs.
- Body-image and beauty-filter exposure is heavy.
- DM-based grooming and sextortion vector.
- Public vs. private account choice critical.
- Teen Accounts settings (private by default for under-18; restricted contact; sensitive-content reduction): turn on.
- Adolescent vulnerability: body-image, comparison, DM risk.

**Snapchat:**
- Disappearing-message default normalizes secrecy.
- Snap Map exposes location to friends; turn off or limit.
- Snapchat AI is a chatbot; teens may treat as confidant.
- "My Eyes Only" hides content even from logged-in user view.
- Adolescent vulnerability: nudes / sexting are highest-prevalence here; sextortion vector.

**Discord:**
- Server-based; can be community or stranger.
- Voice chat in private servers with strangers is a primary grooming vector.
- "Friends" requests from outside teen's known network.
- Server moderation varies wildly.
- Adolescent vulnerability: stranger contact, voice chat with adults posing as teens.

**Reddit:**
- Subreddit-based; ranges from supportive to actively harmful.
- Pseudonymous; comparison and identity-formation around interests.
- Some subreddits are recovery-supportive; others are actively pro-ED, pro-self-harm, pro-violence.
- Adolescent vulnerability: ideology-formation, harmful subreddit membership.

**X (Twitter):**
- Algorithm reduces moderation; political and adversarial content surfaces fast.
- Explicit content reduced friction.
- Adult content easily visible to logged-in users.
- Adolescent vulnerability: radicalization, harassment exposure, explicit content.

**YouTube:**
- Algorithm pulls toward longer engagement; rabbit-hole effects real.
- Comments section toxicity.
- "Watch later" / kids-app distinction.
- Adolescent vulnerability: radicalization (especially manosphere / political), explicit content, parasocial relationships with creators.

**Twitch:**
- Live streaming; chat exposure; donation / subscription monetization.
- Stranger-adult host with teen audience contexts.
- Adolescent vulnerability: parasocial intensity, financial spending, late-night use.

**BeReal / similar:**
- Lower-stakes generally; comparison and time-of-day disruption main concerns.

### Stage 2 — Algorithm-Shape Conversation

```
"I want us to look at your feed together. Not to police it. To
think about what it's making you feel.

Show me — five minutes of TikTok / Instagram / etc. What's
coming up?

[Watch with them. No commentary at first.]

What does that make you feel? Energized? Numb? Bad about your
body? Bad about yourself? Connected? Angry?

Algorithms learn what keeps you watching. They optimize for
engagement, which is often outrage, comparison, or fear. Your
feed isn't accidental; it's a sculpture of what kept you
watching.

Here's what we can do without deleting:
- Use 'not interested' on content that pulls you toward feeling
  worse.
- Follow accounts that you do feel better after seeing.
- Reset the algorithm if it's gotten ugly: clear watch history,
  retrain by following intentionally for a week.
- Turn off recommendations / Explore where the platform allows.
- Use 'sensitive content' filters at the highest setting."
```

### Stage 3 — Body-Image Specific

If feed is body-image-heavy or teen is showing body-image distress:

```
"I'm noticing your feed has a lot of [fitness / beauty /
fashion] content. I'm not saying you can't be interested in
that. I am saying that the kind of body content algorithms push
isn't a balanced view — it's an extreme.

What I want you to know:
- The bodies in your feed are heavily edited, lit, posed,
  filtered, sometimes surgically altered.
- 'What I eat in a day' content is often disordered eating
  packaged as wellness.
- Fitness content can pull toward exercise compulsion.
- Filters distort your face on the screen so much that you
  start seeing your real face as wrong.

If your feed is making you feel bad about your body — and you
notice that — that's the signal to retrain it, mute, unfollow,
or step away.

If you're already restricting, hiding food, or working out to
compensate — that's something to talk about. Not a moral
failing. A signal."
```

(Cross to eating-disorder signal-response prompt if patterns warrant.)

### Stage 4 — Radicalization-Vector Specific

The manosphere, white-nationalist content, eating-disorder communities, conspiracy networks, and extremism content all reach teens through algorithmic pull. This is not paranoia; it is documented across major platforms.

```
"I want to talk about something that's not always comfortable.

There's content out there — a lot of it — that targets teen boys
specifically. It starts with 'how to be a man' or workout content
and moves into hatred of women, hatred of queer people, conspiracy
content, and sometimes worse. The algorithm pulls fast once you
engage.

Same thing exists for teen girls — pro-ana communities, extreme
beauty content, conspiracy spirals.

Same for any teen — political extremism, white-nationalist
content, terror-adjacent forums.

What I want you to know:
- These communities use specific words, memes, framings. Hearing
  them at school doesn't make you in them.
- The pull is by design. The platform wants you watching.
- If you ever notice your feed has gotten weirder — angrier,
  more 'us vs. them,' more contemptuous of whole groups — that's
  the signal.
- I'm not going to confiscate your phone the moment you tell me
  you've seen this content. I want to know so we can talk about
  it.
- If you find yourself believing things that scare you, or
  having thoughts of hurting people, that's something to talk
  about with me or a therapist."
```

### Stage 5 — AI-Generated Image / Deepfake Pre-Load

```
"I want to pre-load something that's now real:

AI image generators can create realistic nudes of real people
from a clothed photo. Including teens. Including classmates.

This is happening at high schools across the country. Targeting
girls primarily, but anyone can be targeted.

What I want you to know:

If someone targets you:
1. You are the victim. The image is not real. The harm is.
2. Tell me right away. Don't manage it alone.
3. We document — screenshots, who, where, when.
4. We report to the school (Title IX coordinator).
5. We report to NCMEC: Take It Down (takeitdown.ncmec.org)
   removes child sexual abuse material from major platforms.
6. We consider law enforcement; this is a crime in many
   jurisdictions.

If you're tempted to make one of someone:
- That's a felony. Federal and state. People your age are
  being prosecuted.
- It's also catastrophically harmful to the person depicted.
  Real harm, even if the image isn't 'real.'
- Don't. Not as a joke, not as revenge, not because someone
  said no, not 'just to have it.'
- If a friend is doing it, you tell me or another adult. The
  reporting can be anonymous; the harm to the targeted person
  is not.

If you're shown one of a classmate:
- Don't share, save, or distribute. Possessing or distributing
  is also a crime.
- Tell me. We figure out the right next move with the school
  and other parents."
```

### Stage 6 — Sextortion Pre-Load and Response

(Cross-references the dating-consent prompt for full sextortion protocol; the social-media-renegotiation surfaces the platform-specific patterns.)

```
"Sextortion patterns specific to social media:

- The contact almost always starts on Instagram, Snapchat,
  TikTok, Discord, or a gaming chat.
- The contactor often poses as a peer-aged teen (often female-
  presenting to male-targeted teens).
- The conversation moves fast: friendly, intimate, photo
  exchange or asked-for nude.
- The threat: 'I have your nude; pay or I send it to your
  followers / family / school.'

If it happens:
1. Don't pay.
2. Don't send more.
3. Stop responding.
4. Screenshot everything (threats, profile, usernames).
5. Tell me first. You are not in trouble. You are the victim.
6. Report: Take It Down (takeitdown.ncmec.org), NCMEC
   CyberTipline (CyberTipline.org), platform report, FBI
   IC3 (ic3.gov).
7. Block the account.

The teen who is sextorted is the victim. Not the criminal.
Pre-loading this means you can act fast if it happens, and you
know I won't shame you."
```

### Stage 7 — Parasocial Dynamics

If the teen is in deep with creators / streamers / online community:

```
"I want to ask about [creator / streamer / online community].
You spend a lot of time there. What is it giving you?

This is real for a lot of teens — the online figure feels like
a friend or a guide. The thing to watch for:

- The figure doesn't actually know you exist.
- The 'community' often feels closer than offline life because
  it's curated and emotional.
- Money: subscriptions, donations, merchandise can add up
  quickly.
- When the figure does something off, the loyalty conflict can
  feel intense.
- Time invested often comes from sleep, school, or offline
  relationships.

I'm not saying disengage. I am saying — keep one foot offline.
The relationships that actually shape you are with people who
know you back."
```

### Stage 8 — The Renegotiation Conversation Structure

```
1. Open with respect, not surveillance language.
   "I want to talk about how things are going on [platform].
    Not to take it away. To check in."

2. Surface what they've noticed.
   "What's working? What's not? What's annoying? What's
    happened that bothered you?"

3. Surface what you've noticed.
   "I've noticed [specific, behavioral, non-shaming]."

4. Make the algorithm visible.
   [Stage 2 conversation.]

5. Renegotiate the agreement per platform.
   - Time of day: charging out of room? school days off-platform?
   - Time totals: app limits in OS, not surveillance?
   - Account settings: visit and adjust together.
   - Specific friction tools: notifications off, certain platforms
     removed, certain hours blocked.

6. Pre-load high-stakes:
   - AI-image / deepfake conversation.
   - Sextortion conversation.
   - Body-image / radicalization conversation as relevant.

7. Document what you decided.

8. Schedule next renegotiation (3 months default)."
```

### Stage 9 — Friction Toolkit

- **Charging out of bedroom** (single most evidence-supported sleep / use intervention).
- **App limits** (OS-level, not surveillance app).
- **Notifications off** for non-essential.
- **DND / focus modes** during school and sleep.
- **Greyscale / monochrome display** during school hours.
- **Specific apps removed**, not all apps.
- **Browser version only** instead of native app (reduces engagement loops).
- **Account settings**: private by default, restrict DMs to followers, no account suggestions, sensitive-content filter at highest.
- **Two-factor authentication** to reduce account takeover (which sextortionists exploit).
- **Periodic "feed reset"**: clear watch history, retrain.

### Stage 10 — Adaptations

- **Anxious teens:** comparison effects intensified; pro-ED and self-harm content vector elevated; reduce body-comparison-heavy feeds.
- **Depressed teens:** parasocial relationships can feel like the main support; risk of isolating further; address connection broadly.
- **Autistic teens:** parasocial relationships and special-interest communities can be supportive when matched; algorithm-driven extremism (e.g., parts of manosphere targeting socially-isolated boys) is a documented pull.
- **ADHD teens:** time loss is steep; OS-level app limits useful; charging out of room critical.
- **LGBTQ+ teens:** online community can be life-saving in unaffirming offline contexts; do not strip without alternatives; affirming content protective.
- **Trans / nonbinary teens:** specific targeting in some communities; safety-by-account-settings.
- **Teen with eating disorder history:** explicit feed audit; specific blocking; adolescent-medicine and ED-specialist engagement.
- **Teen with prior trauma:** content triggers vary; teen voice in setting limits.
- **Single-parent household:** coordinate with other adult support; time-of-day boundaries when caregiver isn't present.
- **Custody / split household:** unify on minimums (charging out of room overnight; AI-image conversation; sextortion protocol) even if other rules diverge.

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| Default to "delete it all" | Friction beats forbidding for most contexts |
| Treat platforms as equivalent | Per-platform risk profile |
| Surveil account contents | Transparency + algorithm conversation |
| Skip the algorithm-shape conversation | Algorithm shape > time on platform |
| Pretend AI-image risk isn't here yet | It's already in high schools |
| Treat sextortion victim as wrongdoer | The teen is the victim; never the criminal |
| Strip queer teen's online community without alternative | Online queer community can be life-saving |
| Treat radicalization concern as paranoia | Documented; algorithm-driven |
| Treat body-image content as "just media" | Algorithmic intensity is qualitatively different |
| Skip the parasocial piece | Time and money sink; emotional investment is real |
| Frame charging-out-of-room as punishment | Single most evidence-supported intervention |
| Use notification of every app event | Notifications are the engagement engine; turn off |
| Treat one renegotiation as the final word | Schedule recurring; teen and platforms change |
| Frame the renegotiation as "I'm taking your phone" | Renegotiate the agreement, not strip it |
| Skip teen voice in the renegotiation | Compliance and safety both depend on teen buy-in |
