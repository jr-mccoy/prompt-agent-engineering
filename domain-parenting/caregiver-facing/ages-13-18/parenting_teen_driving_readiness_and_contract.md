**Purpose:** Help a caregiver assess whether a teen is actually ready to drive (beyond reaching the legal age), design a family driving contract built on graduated-licensing evidence, decide whether to use telematics, and have a clear after-crash protocol ready before it's needed.

**When to use:** Approaching learner's permit; considering whether to delay licensure; designing the contract before licensing; renegotiating after a violation or near-miss; ND-teen driving readiness; after a crash.

**When NOT to use:** Acute crash with injury (call 911, follow medical and police direction first; this prompt covers the post-stabilization conversation).

---

## Clinical-Signal Triggers

- Multiple near-misses in a short window → consider step-back; consider attention, vision, sleep, substance, or mental-health driver.
- Sleep deprivation + driving → drowsy driving has impairment comparable to alcohol; address sleep before extending driving.
- Substance use any → driving privileges paused until substance plan is in place.
- Suspected ADHD with driving difficulty → consider evaluation; ADHD treatment improves driving outcomes in adolescents.

---

## Core Principles

1. **Crash risk is highest in the first months and years of independent driving.** This is biological (frontal lobe maturation, attention, impulse control under arousal) and experiential (low miles in low-stakes contexts).
2. **Graduated Driver Licensing (GDL) reduces crashes.** All US states have some form. Caregivers can extend GDL beyond what the state requires — and should.
3. **Passengers, night, distraction, and alcohol or cannabis are the four pillars of teen-driver risk.** Pillars compound.
4. **Readiness is a skill assessment, not an age.** Some 16-year-olds are ready; some 18-year-olds are not. Delaying licensure is a legitimate choice.
5. **Telematics is a tool, not a strategy.** It can surface patterns. Used as surveillance, it erodes trust. Used transparently, with a feedback loop, it can support skill-building.
6. **Practice quantity and variety predict skill.** 50 supervised hours is the minimum in most state GDLs. 100+ across conditions (rain, night, highway, parking, complex urban) is closer to where competence lives.

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Distinguish readiness from age.
- Build the contract from GDL evidence.
- Address the four pillars (passengers, night, distraction, substance).
- Include sleep, weather, and emotional-state pieces.
- Provide telematics decision framework.
- Include after-crash response protocol.

**Must Not:**
- Default to "if the state allows, ready."
- Skip distraction (phone, music, eating, multiple-passenger conversation).
- Treat one crash or violation as deterministic.
- Use the contract as gotcha; use it as scaffolding.

## Instructions

### Stage 1 — Readiness Assessment

Score the teen across:

**Cognitive readiness:**
- Sustained attention in academic and other contexts.
- Capacity to inhibit impulse under arousal.
- Capacity to learn from mistakes (vs. dismissing or rationalizing).
- Reasonable risk perception in non-driving contexts.

**Emotional readiness:**
- Recovers from frustration without aggression.
- Doesn't drive (in simulator / with you) angry or panicked.
- Asks questions, accepts coaching.

**Behavioral readiness:**
- Follows rules at home, school, work.
- Sleep-adequate most nights.
- Not in active substance pattern.
- Not in active mental-health crisis.

**Practice readiness:**
- Hours: minimum state requirement, ideally more.
- Conditions: night, rain, highway, urban, parking, fog, snow if relevant.
- Variety: different routes, different traffic densities.
- Mistake recovery: has handled minor mistakes (curb hit, lane drift) under your supervision and improved.

**If readiness is low across multiple domains: delay licensure.** This is a normal, non-shameful choice. Many families wait until 17 or 18.

### Stage 2 — The Four Pillars of Risk

**Pillar 1: Passengers.**
- Each additional teen passenger multiplies crash risk for the new driver.
- GDL state laws typically restrict for first 6–12 months; family contract can extend.
- Recommend: zero non-family passengers for first 6 months; one for next 6; calibrated thereafter.

**Pillar 2: Night driving.**
- Nighttime crash risk is several-fold higher.
- Visibility, fatigue, and alcohol-on-road context all combine.
- Recommend: no driving 10 PM – 5 AM for first 6–12 months unless to/from a defined destination with parent communication.

**Pillar 3: Distraction.**
- Phone use is the leading distraction; even hands-free degrades attention.
- Music, eating, conversation with passengers all degrade.
- Recommend: phone in glovebox or DND-while-driving mode; music set before driving; no eating; passenger silence in difficult conditions.

**Pillar 4: Impairment.**
- Alcohol, cannabis, prescription stimulants, even some antihistamines.
- Drowsy driving comparable to alcohol impairment at sleep <6 hours.
- Recommend: zero use; zero riding with anyone who has used; sleep-minimum self-check.

### Stage 3 — The Family Driving Contract Template

```
DRIVING CONTRACT — [Teen name], effective [date], review [date].

Permission and Trust:
We are giving you the privilege of driving. This is real
responsibility. The terms below are because driving in your first
year is the highest crash-risk year of your life. We're not
underestimating you; we're working from data.

Hard rules (any violation = pause):
1. Zero alcohol, zero cannabis, zero prescription misuse before
   or during driving.
2. Zero riding with anyone who has used. Use the safety-call
   commitment instead.
3. Phone: out of reach or in DND mode while engine is on.
4. Seat belts: every person, every time, before the engine starts.
5. Speed limit: posted speed is the ceiling.

First 6 months (post-license):
6. No non-family passengers under 21 unless approved.
7. No driving 10 PM – 5 AM.
8. Destination communication: where, when, returning when.
9. New routes: pre-driven with a parent.

Months 6–12:
10. One non-family teen passenger maximum.
11. Night driving permitted with notification and route.
12. Highway / weather: case-by-case.

Months 12+:
13. Calibrated by demonstrated capacity.

Sleep, weather, and emotional state:
14. Less than 6 hours sleep: don't drive.
15. Severe weather (heavy rain, snow, ice, fog): don't drive
    unless required; call for ride if required.
16. Driving angry, panicking, crying, or after a fight: don't
    drive.

Maintenance and money:
17. Gas, insurance, maintenance — agreement: [specific].
18. Tickets, accidents, deductibles — agreement: [specific].

If something goes wrong:
19. Crash, regardless of fault: stop, check on others, call
    parent first or simultaneously with 911. Do not leave the
    scene. Do not admit fault. Exchange information.
20. Stopped by police: hands visible, calm, polite, comply,
    follow up with parent.
21. Substance situation while you're driving or about to drive:
    safety-call wins. We come.

Telematics:
22. We use [app / not]. Purpose: [as agreed]. Review cadence:
    [as agreed].

Renegotiation:
23. This contract gets reviewed every [interval]. As you build
    track record, terms expand. As problems happen, terms
    contract. Both directions are real.

Signed:
Teen: ___________
Caregiver: ___________
Date: ___________
```

### Stage 4 — The Telematics Decision

Telematics options (insurance app, dedicated device, phone app):
- Tracks: location, speed, hard braking, rapid acceleration, phone-use-while-driving.
- Some options: real-time alerts, history review.

Decision framework:
- **Use it** if: high-risk driver, recovery after incident, insurance-driven cost reduction, or teen requested as accountability scaffold.
- **Skip it** if: relationship is built on trust + the teen's track record supports, and surveillance would erode more than it adds.
- **If using:** transparent — teen knows it exists, what it tracks, what triggers a conversation. Not gotcha.
- **Review cadence:** weekly for first month, then less frequent. Conversation focused on patterns, not single events (unless dangerous).

Telematics-feedback conversation:
```
"Three hard brakes on Friday. What was happening? Was traffic worse,
were you tired, was someone else doing something? Walk me through
it. I'm not getting on you; I want to understand."
```

### Stage 5 — Practice Plan

Build the supervised hours with variety. A useful sequence:

1. **Empty parking lot:** start, stop, turn, accelerate, brake.
2. **Quiet residential:** stop signs, parking, three-point turn.
3. **Moderate traffic, daylight:** signaled intersections, lane changes.
4. **Highway entry, daylight:** merging, lane choice.
5. **Highway sustained:** passing, exiting.
6. **Dense urban:** complex intersections, pedestrians, cyclists.
7. **Night driving:** progressive — quiet first, then traffic.
8. **Weather:** rain, then if relevant snow / fog.
9. **Long-distance:** sustained driving with stops.
10. **Mistake recovery:** intentional simulated mistakes with debrief.

After each session: brief, specific debrief. What went well; what to work on; what's next.

### Stage 6 — After-Crash Response Protocol

Pre-loaded:
```
"If you're in a crash, ever, for any reason:

1. If you can: turn off the car, hazards on, get to safety.
2. Check on yourself first. Then anyone else in the car. Then
   anyone in any other vehicle.
3. Call 911 if anyone is hurt or there is significant damage. Many
   states require it.
4. Call me. We can be on the phone with you while you talk to
   police if you want.
5. Don't admit fault. Don't say 'I'm sorry, it was my fault.' Even
   if you think it was. Police and insurance figure that out.
6. Exchange information: license, insurance, plate, contact.
7. Take photos. Vehicles, damage, scene from multiple angles,
   street signs.
8. Get witness contact info if available.
9. Stay until released by police.

After:
- I'm not going to scream at you. We will figure out the next
  steps together.
- We may pause driving for a defined window while we figure out
  what happened.
- A crash isn't a moral failure. It's a data point that we use."
```

If injury or trauma: arrange medical and psychological follow-up. Post-crash anxiety / driving phobia is real; therapeutic support helps.

### Stage 7 — Adaptations Across Profiles

- **ADHD:** elevated crash risk; ADHD medication during driving hours improves outcomes (research base); telematics may provide useful external scaffolding; consider extended supervised practice.
- **Autistic:** route-familiar, clear-rules driving often strong; complex unpredictable contexts harder; explicit pre-driving conversation about novel scenarios.
- **Anxious:** driving phobia possible; graduated exposure with supportive teacher; therapy if persistent.
- **Trauma history:** crash can re-trigger; pre-loaded support plan.
- **Vision / hearing / motor:** medical clearance and adaptive equipment as needed; occupational-therapist driving evaluations exist.
- **Working teen using car for job:** scope-of-use clarity; insurance ramifications.
- **Custody / split household:** consistent contract across households; insurance coordination.
- **Single-parent:** the safety-call commitment matters extra; identify backup adults.
- **Rural context:** earlier and more driving necessary; may need more practice variety in farm-equipment, livestock-on-road, long-distance fatigue.
- **Urban context:** later licensure may be feasible; transit and rideshare reduce stakes of delay.

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| Default to "state-legal = ready" | Readiness is skill-based; delay is legitimate |
| Skip the four pillars | Passengers, night, distraction, substance compound risk |
| Treat one crash as the end of trust | Pause + debrief + structured reentry |
| Use telematics as gotcha | Transparent, feedback-loop tool, not surveillance |
| Skip drowsy-driving conversation | Sleep <6 hrs is impairment-equivalent |
| Treat ADHD teen as the same as neurotypical for driving | Elevated risk; medication and scaffolding matter |
| Skip "don't admit fault" line | Real legal exposure; teach it pre-loaded |
| Frame the contract as punishment | Scaffolding; expanding both ways with track record |
| Practice only in easy conditions | Variety builds skill; novel context is where crashes happen |
| Allow first-month night driving for social purposes | Night-driving risk is several-fold; calibrate |
| Allow first-month multi-passenger trips | Passenger count compounds risk |
| Trust phone-DND voluntary compliance only | Out-of-reach is more reliable for first months |
| Skip the safety-call commitment | The single most life-saving line |
| Treat crash recovery as just car-and-money | Psychological follow-up matters |
| Use rural / suburban / urban templates without adaptation | Context shapes both readiness and contract |
