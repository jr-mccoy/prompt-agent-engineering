# Twice-Exceptional (2e) Child

**Objective:** Help the caregiver see the whole child — advanced abilities and real
difficulties at the same time — get an evaluation that can find both, ask the school for
both kinds of support without trading one for the other, and run a home life that protects
the child's self-worth from the gap between what they can think and what they can produce.

**When to Use:**
- A child reads far above grade level but cannot get ideas onto paper, or is a maths whiz
  who loses every assignment.
- Teachers say "so bright, just needs to try harder," or the child is in gifted services
  and failing, or in support services and bored.
- An evaluation showed a big spread of scores and the caregiver is unsure what it means.
- The child says "I'm stupid" despite obvious ability, or melts down over small mistakes.
- **Not this prompt if** a 4–8-year-old's grades are fine and the issue is behavioural or
  social friction for one meeting — use
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/ages-4-8/parenting_school_accommodation_conversation_prep.md`.
  For a meeting focused on an academic gap and an evaluation request, use
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/academics-skills/parenting_learning_concern_school_meeting.md`.
  For praise language and perfectionism at home in general, use
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/cross-age/parenting_praise_encouragement_calibrator.md`.
  This prompt is the overall 2e picture and plan.

## Safety Block

- **Talk of wanting to die, hopelessness, or self-harm** → 988 (US) and
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/mental-health-behavior/parenting_child_suicide_talk_response_6_12.md`;
  for teens, `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/ages-13-18/parenting_teen_mental_health_signal_check.md`.
  Bright children who feel like failures are at real risk of depression.
- **School refusal, daily stomachaches, or shutdowns** → see
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/mental-health-behavior/parenting_school_refusal_young_child_5_8.md`
  or the tween/teen equivalents, and the pediatrician.
- **Burnout signs** (exhaustion, loss of interest in favourite subjects, sleeping much
  more, withdrawal) → reduce demands and see the pediatrician or the child's clinician.

**This plan does not diagnose giftedness or any disability, and the caregiver should not
interpret test scores alone.** A licensed psychologist interprets cognitive and
achievement testing.

## Inputs / Context

1. **Child:** age, grade, areas of strength (with examples), areas of difficulty (with
   examples), any diagnosis or prior testing.
2. **School picture:** gifted program status, IEP/504, grades by subject, teacher
   comments, homework time.
3. **Evaluation data** if any (share the report summary; do not ask the model to diagnose).
4. **Emotional picture:** frustration, perfectionism, intensity, anxiety, friendships,
   self-talk.
5. **Home:** interests, routines, siblings, what has been tried.

## Method

1. **Map strengths and challenges on separate axes (RT-02).** List verbal reasoning,
   visual-spatial, maths reasoning, creativity, and knowledge separately from reading,
   writing, spelling, processing speed, working memory, attention, organisation, and
   social/sensory needs. Each gets examples. The aim is a profile, not an average.
2. **Look through the three masking lenses (DP-27).** 2e research describes children who
   are:
   - **Identified as gifted, difficulty hidden** — keep up through effort until the work
     gets harder (often grades 4–7), then "suddenly" struggle.
   - **Identified with a difficulty, gift hidden** — support services focus on deficits;
     ability goes unseen and unchallenged.
   - **Neither identified** — ability and difficulty cancel out to "average," and the child
     is seen as unmotivated.
   Decide which lens fits; it determines what to ask for.
3. **Prepare for the evaluation (DS-01).** Ask for a comprehensive evaluation (cognitive,
   achievement, language, attention, social-emotional as relevant). A large gap between
   index scores (e.g., strong verbal reasoning, much lower processing speed or working
   memory) is common in 2e children and can make a single overall IQ score misleading —
   ask the psychologist how they interpret the spread. Ask whether they have experience
   with twice-exceptional children.
4. **Ask the school for both kinds of support (NE-23).** Both together: advanced or
   accelerated work in the strength area and accommodations or instruction for the
   difficulty (e.g., maths acceleration plus speech-to-text and extended time). Prepare
   for the objections:
   - "He can't be gifted if he's failing writing" → "His reasoning is advanced; his
     writing output is the barrier. He needs challenge and access."
   - "She doesn't qualify for support because her grades are passing" → "She is passing
     through hours of extra effort at home; here is the time log."
   - "Gifted services are for students without IEPs" → ask for the policy in writing;
     many districts allow both.
5. **Build the home around strengths.** Daily time for the interest area; let the child
   learn hard content through their strong channel (audiobooks, discussion, building).
   Strength-based work protects motivation and identity.
6. **Separate worth from performance.** Praise effort, strategy, and persistence over
   "you're so smart"; talk openly about the brain profile ("Your thinking is fast; your
   handwriting is slow. Both are true."); share stories of successful people with
   learning differences.
7. **Address perfectionism and intensity.** Model mistakes; set "good-enough" criteria
   before tasks; teach the child to break big ideas into small steps; expect strong
   emotional reactions and give recovery time rather than lectures.
8. **Grow self-advocacy.** By 9–10, the child can name one strength, one challenge, and
   one helpful accommodation and say it to a teacher.

## Output Format

```
# 2e Profile and Plan — [child, age, grade]   Date: [..]

## Strengths (with examples)
## Challenges (with examples)
## Masking pattern that fits (and why)
## Evaluation: status, areas to request, questions for the psychologist
## School asks — challenge side / support side (both)
## Objections and responses
## Home: strength time, access tools, how we talk about the profile
## Perfectionism and intensity plan
## Self-advocacy script (child's words)
## Warning signs and routes (mood, refusal, burnout)
```

## Verification

- [ ] Strengths and challenges are listed separately with examples.
- [ ] One masking pattern is named with reasons.
- [ ] Score interpretation is left to the psychologist.
- [ ] School asks include both challenge and support.
- [ ] Home plan includes strength time and worth-not-performance language.
- [ ] Mental-health and burnout warning signs are routed.

## False-Positive Prevention

| Misfire | What it looks like | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Averaging the child | "Average overall, so no need" | 2e profiles hide in the average; look at the spread. |
| "Lazy" or "not trying" | Bright child punished for missing work | Check for an output, attention, or organisation difficulty. |
| Giftedness used to deny support | "Too smart for an IEP" | Ability and disability can co-exist. |
| Support used to deny challenge | Only remediation, no enrichment | Bored 2e children disengage; offer both. |
| Calling every bright, quirky child 2e | Label without evidence | Ask for evaluation; describe, don't diagnose. |
| Parent interpreting raw scores | "His IQ means he's not ADHD" | Psychologist interprets the profile. |

## Adaptations

- **ADHD + gifted:** external structure for organisation; interest-driven tasks for
  sustained attention; check sleep.
- **Autistic + gifted:** deep interests as gateways; social and sensory supports alongside
  academic challenge; avoid over-scheduling.
- **Dyslexia + gifted:** audiobooks and discussion for high-level content; structured
  literacy for decoding (see `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/academics-skills/parenting_struggling_reader_home_support.md`).
- **Under-represented groups:** gifted identification misses many children from
  low-income, multilingual, and minority backgrounds; ask about universal screening and
  non-verbal measures.
- **Teen:** involve them in the plan; post-school choices can lean on strengths.

## Example Output

```
# 2e Profile and Plan — Isla, 10, grade 5   Date: 3 Oct

## Strengths
Reads adult science books; maths reasoning two grades ahead.

## Challenges
Written work: 3 sentences in 30 minutes; messy, slow handwriting;
loses homework; meltdowns over small errors; 2 hours of homework a
night for work classmates finish in 30 minutes.

## Masking pattern
"Identified as gifted, difficulty hidden": in the gifted pull-out since
grade 2; struggles appeared in grade 4 as writing demands rose.

## Evaluation
School psychologist evaluation requested 1 Oct (cognitive, achievement,
writing, attention). Ask: How do you read the gap between her verbal
and processing-speed scores? Have you evaluated 2e students before?

## School asks
Challenge: keep gifted pull-out; maths extension group.
Support: speech-to-text for long writing; graphic organisers; extended
time; homework reduced to the core.

## Objections and responses
"She's in gifted, so she's fine." → "Her reasoning is advanced; her
writing output takes four times longer than peers — time log attached."

## Home: Friday "deep dive" hour on space; dictation app for drafts.
How we talk: "Your ideas are fast; your hand is slow. We'll find
tools so your ideas get out."

## Perfectionism: "good enough" checklist before writing; Mum shares one
mistake a day; 10-minute reset after a meltdown, no lecture.

## Self-advocacy: "I think fast but write slow. Can I type or say it?"

## Warning signs
"I'm stupid" daily, stomachaches on school days, or loss of interest in
space → pediatrician; any talk of not wanting to be alive → 988 and the
6–12 suicide-talk prompt.
```

## Techniques Used

- **RT-02 Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework** — strengths and challenges mapped on separate axes rather than averaged.
- **DP-27 Multi-Lens Problem Diagnostic** — three masking lenses test which 2e pattern explains the picture.
- **DS-01 Framework Application** — 2e research on masking and score-spread interpretation shapes the evaluation questions.
- **NE-23 Objection Pre-emption** — prepared answers to the common school responses that deny one side of support.
