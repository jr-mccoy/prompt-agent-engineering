# Neurodivergence Prompts (Caregiver-Facing)

Supporting children whose brains and bodies work differently, beyond the ADHD, autism, sensory, and strong-willed prompts already in `../ages-4-8/`: tics and Tourette syndrome, twice-exceptional (gifted plus a learning or attention difference) children, and developmental coordination disorder (dyspraxia). Each prompt is neurodiversity-affirming — the aim is access, skills the child wants, and self-worth, not making a child look more typical — and each names the medical signs that need a doctor before any home plan.

## Scope

**In scope:**
- Tics and Tourette syndrome (4–17): how tics behave, look-alikes, responses that make tics worse, a one-page school sheet, classmate explanation with the child's consent, co-occurring ADHD/OCD/anxiety, medical triggers.
- Twice-exceptional children (6–16): separate strength and challenge maps, the three masking patterns, evaluation questions, asking school for challenge and support together, perfectionism and self-worth at home.
- Coordination difficulties / DCD (4–14): medical red flags, assessment, task-oriented practice with backward chaining, home adaptations, activity choice, school handwriting/keyboarding/PE supports.

## Safety notes

Loss of skills a child once had, new weakness, one-sided changes, staring spells or loss of awareness, or headaches with vomiting go to the pediatrician promptly (same day where stated) — these are not tics or coordination difficulty. Painful or injuring tics, sudden explosive onset of tics with OCD or food restriction, and tics after a medication change go to the doctor soon; possible functional tic-like behaviours (sudden complex onset, often after tic videos online) are managed differently and need a clinician. Bright children who feel like failures are at real risk of depression: any talk of wanting to die → 988 (US) and `../mental-health-behavior/parenting_child_suicide_talk_response_6_12.md`. Caregivers do not interpret test scores or diagnose; a licensed clinician does.
