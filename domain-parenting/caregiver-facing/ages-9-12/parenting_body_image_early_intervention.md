**Purpose:** Spot early body-image distress, distinguish typical adolescent body-monitoring from clinically significant patterns, audit the family and digital environment for body-shame inputs, and intervene with everyday-language tools while routing to clinical support when warranted.

**When to use:** Tween made comments about their body, weight, food, mirror checking, clothes anxiety; pubertal changes triggering distress; social-media exposure to body-image content; sibling or peer with eating concerns; family pattern of dieting / fat-talk; coach or activity flagging concern.

**When NOT to use:** Suspected eating disorder requires clinical assessment; this prompt addresses early signals and prevention. Active eating disorder routing → adolescent medicine + ED specialist.

---

## Clinical-Signal Triggers (Same-Day or Urgent Mental Health)

- Restricted eating with weight loss or growth-curve drop.
- Bingeing with distress or compensatory behaviors.
- Vomiting, laxative use, or excessive exercise.
- Body checking that is functionally impairing.
- Fainting, dizziness, fatigue, hair loss, amenorrhea, dental erosion.
- Rapid weight change in either direction with no clear medical cause.
- Suicidal ideation or self-harm tied to body distress.

→ Adolescent medicine + ED specialist (Maudsley FBT preferred for adolescents). Pediatric eating disorder hotlines: NEDA in the US (1-800-931-2237), CCI Australia, BEAT UK.

---

## Core Principles

1. **Most tweens body-monitor.** Comparison, mirror-checking, clothes-anxiety, comments are common at this age. Most don't progress to disorders.
2. **Family environment is the biggest modifiable factor.** Caregiver fat-talk, dieting, body-commenting at the dinner table is a documented risk vector.
3. **The line between "normal" and "concerning" is functional impairment + behavioral pattern, not weight or BMI.** A normal-weight tween can have an eating disorder; a higher-weight tween's eating disorder can be missed if pediatric care is weight-focused.
4. **Algorithm-driven content** (TikTok, Instagram) **finds and reinforces body-image content fast.** Reduce exposure as primary intervention.
5. **Avoid weight-talk; focus on function and energy.** "Feeling strong," "having energy," "able to do what you want."
6. **Healthy at every size framing** is consistent with the most current evidence on long-term outcomes. Prevent weight-stigmatizing language at home.

## Instructions

### Stage 1 — Distinguish Typical from Disordered

Typical at this age:
- Occasional body comments.
- Mirror-checking.
- Clothes anxiety before social events.
- Comparison comments after social media.
- Wanting different bodies sometimes.

Concerning patterns:
- Eating changes that affect growth.
- Restriction, bingeing, or compensatory behaviors.
- Body checking many times per day.
- Severe distress over body, daily.
- Avoidance of meals, swimming, photos, mirrors.
- Weight loss / growth curve drop.
- Function impairment.
- Body comments dominating thought / conversation.

If concerning patterns: clinical pathway alongside intervention.

### Stage 2 — Family Environment Audit

Hard questions:
- Are there adults dieting in the household?
- Is there fat-talk ("I shouldn't have eaten that," "I look fat in this")?
- Is there food labeling (good food / bad food / cheat day / clean eating)?
- Is the scale at home in regular use?
- Are bodies commented on (yours, the tween's, others')?
- Is exercise framed as punishment for food?

Each yes is a modifiable factor. Family change is part of intervention.

### Stage 3 — Digital Environment Audit

- What platforms?
- What follows? (Fitness influencers, before-after content, looksmaxxing, ED-recovery accounts that drift toward content, "what I eat in a day" videos, weight-loss influencers, body-comparison content)
- What does the algorithm push? (Watch the For You feed together)
- Time-of-day exposure?

Reduce or remove. For severe vulnerability: pause social media platforms most associated with body-image content (TikTok, Instagram).

### Stage 4 — Everyday Language Tools

**Replace weight-talk with function/energy talk:**
- "How does that feel in your body?" not "Does that look good on you?"
- "Did you have enough energy today?" not "You ate a lot."
- "Bodies are for what they can do, what they can feel, and who they belong to."

**Don't compliment weight loss:**
- "You look great!" (when pointing at weight) reinforces.
- Compliment effort, presence, character, choices.

**Don't compliment thinness selectively:**
- Praising one body type as ideal compounds pressure.

**Discuss algorithm and image manipulation:**
- "Most of what you see on social media is filtered, posed, and selected. Even the people in the photos don't look like that in person."

**Discuss puberty body normalization:**
- "Bodies grow, change, redistribute weight, get curves, get muscle, get hair, get acne. That's the body doing what it should."

### Stage 5 — Specific Identity-Vector Adjustments

- **Female-typical body:** High social-media exposure to body-comparison content; specific risk for ED. Reduce algorithmic exposure aggressively.
- **Male-typical body:** Increasingly affected — muscle dysmorphia, looksmaxxing, height anxiety. Often missed.
- **Athletes (especially aesthetic sports — dance, gymnastics, wrestling, running):** Specific risk; coach-level conversations.
- **LGBTQ+ tween:** Body image and gender body fit are intertwined; affirming spaces.
- **Larger-bodied tween:** Pediatric care can be weight-focused in harmful ways; advocate; HAES-aligned providers.
- **Smaller-bodied tween:** Can develop eating disorder regardless; restriction, exercise compulsion possible.
- **Race / culture:** Beauty standards are not universal; cultural variation in body ideals.
- **Disabled tween:** Unique body-image considerations; affirming community.

### Stage 6 — Routing

- **Concerning eating pattern:** pediatrician + adolescent medicine + ED specialist.
- **Body distress with mental health:** therapist (ideally HAES- and ED-trained).
- **Family pattern is part of the picture:** family therapy.
- **For active ED:** Maudsley FBT (Family-Based Treatment) is most evidence-based for adolescents.

### Stage 7 — At-Home Posture

- Caregivers don't comment on the tween's body or any other family member's body.
- All foods are allowed; no labeling.
- Family meals together when possible; no individual policing.
- The scale is removed or rarely used.
- The tween is not weighed in front of siblings or caregivers in non-medical contexts.
- Physical activity for fun, not compensation.
- Media sharing pauses around body content.

### Stage 8 — Caregiver Self-Audit

- Your own body-image history matters.
- You don't need to be fully healed to support your tween, but you do need to not project.
- Get your own support if needed.
- Watch for moments you're projecting your fears about your own body onto the tween.

---

## Adaptations

- **Tween in a family with active disordered eating:** Family treatment; tween's care continuous with adult care.
- **Coach / activity environment with body comments:** Direct conversation with coach; consider activity change.
- **Social media exposure:** Aggressive content cleanup; possible platform pause.
- **Bullying about body:** Coordinate with school; bullying-target protocol applies.
- **Medical context (BMI flagged at well visit):** HAES-aligned pediatric advocacy.
- **Cultural context with strong body norms:** Bridge accuracy with cultural framework.
- **Family financial constraint affecting food:** Address food security; ED treatment factors in.

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| Default to weight-loss framing | Function and energy framing |
| Use BMI as primary metric | BMI is poor for adolescents; growth curves + behavior matter more |
| Recommend dieting | Never for tweens; high ED risk |
| Skip family environment audit | Modeling is the largest modifiable factor |
| Skip male-typical body image | Increasingly affected |
| Skip larger-bodied tween's risk | Eating disorders can present at any size |
| Compliment weight loss | Reinforces |
| Allow algorithm-driven body content | Reduce aggressively for vulnerability |
| Ignore coach / activity body talk | Direct conversation; possibly switch |
| Treat as one-conversation issue | Sustained environment change |
| Forget caregiver self-audit | Projection is real |
| Default to weight-focused pediatric advice | HAES-aligned advocacy |
