**Purpose:** Design a usable video-game agreement matched to the games the tween actually plays — accounting for genre risk profile, voice-chat exposure, monetization (loot boxes, V-Bucks, skins), reaction patterns at end-of-session, and signs of disordered use. Replace blunt "screen time" caps with rules that match the actual harm vectors.

**When to use:** Setting up gaming for the first time, renegotiating after issues, family conflict around gaming, transition to multiplayer with voice chat, post-incident (toxic chat, big purchase, sustained meltdown).

**When NOT to use:** Suspected gaming disorder requiring clinical support; trauma-related gaming use; severe family conflict that gaming is a flashpoint of more than the source.

---

## Clinical-Signal Triggers

- Inability to stop with consistent escalation despite caregiver intervention; loss of interest in everything else; functional impairment in school, sleep, peers, family — gaming disorder screen.
- Severe meltdown at every session end → regulation work, not just rule-tightening.
- Online victimization (toxic chat, grooming) → safety response, not just agreement update.
- Sustained sleep disruption from gaming → strict pre-sleep cutoff + reassessment.

---

## Core Principles

1. **Total time is a poor proxy for harm.** A tween playing a calm strategy game alone for 90 minutes is different from a tween in voice chat with strangers in a high-aggression shooter for 45 minutes.
2. **Game design is engineered for retention.** Daily login bonuses, season passes, loot boxes, social pressure features. The game is fighting for attention by design.
3. **End-of-session dysregulation is real.** Some games (especially competitive online) leave kids amped or upset; transition planning matters.
4. **Voice chat is the highest-variability vector.** With friends only, low risk; with strangers in random matchmaking, higher risk.
5. **Loot boxes and microtransactions involve gambling-like mechanics.** A separate decision from time and content.
6. **Caregiver familiarity with the actual game is the highest-leverage caregiving move.** Watch them play; let them teach you.

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Match rules to specific games, not generic screen time.
- Address voice chat as a separate dimension.
- Address monetization as a separate dimension.
- Plan transitions / end-of-session.
- Address sleep and school protection.
- Surface gaming disorder signals.

**Must Not:**
- Default to "no games."
- Treat all games as equivalent.
- Default to time-only caps.
- Ignore caregiver familiarity gap.
- Pathologize friend-multiplayer.

## Instructions

### Stage 1 — Game Audit

For each game played:

| Game | Genre | Online? | Voice chat? | Monetization | End-of-session pattern |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Minecraft (servers) | Sandbox | Variable | Variable | Skins | Calm typically |
| Roblox | Platform / many genres | Yes | Yes | Robux | Variable; some are gambling-mechanic adjacent |
| Fortnite | Battle royale | Yes | Yes | V-Bucks, battle pass | Often amped/upset |
| Valorant / CS / Call of Duty | Shooter | Yes | Yes | Skins | Often amped/upset |
| Genshin / Honkai | Gacha RPG | Online | No voice | Gacha (gambling-like) | Calm typically |
| FIFA / NBA / Madden | Sports | Local + online | Yes | Ultimate Team packs (gambling-like) | Variable |
| Single-player titles | Various | No | No | None or DLC | Calm typically |
| Mobile loot games | Various | Often | No | High monetization | Variable |

State which games are high-stakes (harm vectors) and which are low-stakes.

### Stage 2 — Time Architecture

Replace "X hours per day" with:

- **Sessions, not hours.** "Two sessions on a school day, three on a weekend day, defined by [game]'s natural break points."
- **Pre-sleep cutoff.** Hard cutoff 60–90 min before bed (especially for competitive games).
- **Homework and chores first.** "Game time follows responsibilities."
- **One non-screen activity per day.**
- **Daily reset.** Yesterday's leftover game time doesn't carry.

### Stage 3 — Voice Chat Decision

Tier 1: voice chat off. Cleanest for first 6–12 months of online play.

Tier 2: voice chat with whitelisted friends only. Real-life friends, party-only, no random matchmaking voice.

Tier 3: voice chat in friend party + game audio with strangers (text or voice). Higher risk.

Tier 4: open voice chat with strangers. Highest risk; only with established trust track record.

Configure platform-level: PlayStation parental controls, Xbox Family, Nintendo Parental Controls, Discord settings.

### Stage 4 — Monetization Rules

- Loot boxes / gacha / pack pulls: not from caregiver's money. Use a fixed allowance budget the tween manages.
- V-Bucks / Robux gifts: gift on birthday/holidays only, fixed amount.
- Battle passes: caregiver buys one if it makes sense; not a constant flow.
- Subscriptions (Game Pass, etc.): household decision.
- "Free-to-play" games: monitor for psychological pressure (FOMO timers, exclusive limited skins).

Teach the gambling pattern: "This loot box gives you a 1% chance of [X]. That means you'll spend $50 to maybe get the thing. Want to do that?"

### Stage 5 — End-of-Session Transition

Pre-load:
- Verbal warning at session end approach (10 min, 5 min, end of round).
- Don't pull mid-match. Most games have natural end points.
- Brief decompression (snack, water, body movement) before next demand.
- For amped/upset patterns: planned transition activity.

For meltdown at session end (especially ADHD or autism + game with hyperfocus):
- Pre-game agreement on the end-of-session ritual.
- Repair conversation later, not during the meltdown.
- See `parenting_meltdown_response_script.md` for in-the-moment.

### Stage 6 — Friend Dynamics

- Friend gaming can strengthen real friendships.
- Friend gaming can also displace face-to-face time.
- Calibrate: "Online with [friend] is fine; in-person time still happens X/week."
- Cyber-bullying inside games: address as bullying, with platform reporting.

### Stage 7 — Gaming Disorder Signals

ICD-11 includes gaming disorder. Signals (sustained 12+ months for diagnosis):
- Impaired control (can't stop).
- Increasing priority over other interests.
- Continuation despite negative consequences.
- Functional impairment.

Earlier-warning patterns:
- Severe escalation at session end despite consistent intervention.
- Gaming through illness / mealtime / sleep without resistance.
- Loss of all non-gaming interests.
- Hiding gaming.
- Mood entirely tied to game performance.

If pattern persists 4–6 weeks of consistent intervention: clinical support.

### Stage 8 — Caregiver Familiarity

- Watch a session.
- Let the tween teach you the game.
- Ask: "What's the goal here? Why do you like this character?"
- This is the cheapest harm-prevention move: knowing what they're actually playing.

## Adaptations

- **ADHD tween:** Hyperfocus + slot-machine reward design = high engagement risk; firm session structure; transition support.
- **Autistic tween:** Often a special-interest, regulating activity; protect this; calibrate social demands during gaming.
- **Anxious tween:** Competitive online can amplify anxiety; consider co-op or single-player.
- **Sensory profile:** Headphone discipline; volume protection.
- **Sibling dynamics:** Shared screen / shared games as connection vs. conflict source.
- **Solo caregiver, gaming as "babysitter":** Honest naming; harm-reduce within constraints.
- **Custody schedule:** Same agreement in both houses where possible; gaming in one house only is usually a conflict.

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| Use total hours as the only metric | Match rules to specific games' harm vectors |
| Treat all games as equivalent | Differentiate by genre, online, voice, monetization |
| Default to "no games" | Honor friend dynamics + interest-based engagement |
| Pull mid-match | Use natural end points |
| Skip voice chat decision | Voice chat is the highest-variability vector |
| Allow caregiver-funded loot boxes | Tween manages a fixed budget; teach the math |
| Skip pre-sleep cutoff | Sleep protection central |
| Confuse hyperfocus with addiction | ADHD + autistic kids regulate via games; pattern + impairment matters |
| Skip the end-of-session ritual | Transition is half the conflict |
| Forget caregiver familiarity | Watch a session; learn the game |
| Pathologize peer gaming | Friend gaming strengthens real friendships |
| Tighten in anger after one bad day | Tighten by pre-agreed criteria |
