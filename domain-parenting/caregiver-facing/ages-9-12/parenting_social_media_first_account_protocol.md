**Purpose:** Help caregivers make a defensible first-account decision and execute the protocol if yes — picking platform, account configuration, follower scope, monitoring approach, and a 90-day review cycle. Address the highest-risk vectors directly: algorithm exposure to harmful content, social comparison and body image, unmoderated DMs, AI image generation/deepfakes, and influencer-driven mental-health misinformation.

**When to use:** Tween wants an account, peer pressure, school project requiring an account, post-phone-grant decision, sibling already has accounts, parents reconsidering after seeing concerning posts.

**When NOT to use:** Existing concerning patterns (suicidal ideation, eating disorder, sustained body-image distress, online victimization) — those need clinical support and platform restriction, not a "first account" framing.

---

## Clinical-Signal Triggers

- Existing depression, anxiety, eating disorder, self-harm, body-image distress → delay social media; coordinate clinical support.
- Online victimization or grooming risk → no new account; existing accounts paused; safety planning.
- Significant ADHD, autism, or rejection-sensitive dysphoria → tighter calibration.
- Family history of disordered eating + algorithm-driven content → significant risk vector; delay.

---

## Core Principles

1. **Platforms are not equivalent.** TikTok and Instagram have stronger algorithm-driven harm associations than YouTube comments or BeReal-style apps. Choose accordingly.
2. **The algorithm is the product.** Not the social graph. Social media risk is largely about the recommendation engine, not what friends post.
3. **Account configuration matters as much as platform choice.** Private account, no DMs from non-followers, age-honest, no link-in-bio for an 11-year-old changes the threat surface.
4. **Mental health literature is increasingly clear on harm associations**, especially for adolescent female-typical users. Delay is the most consistent recommendation when feasible.
5. **Monitoring without trust collapses the relationship.** Disclosed monitoring + open conversation works; secret monitoring is found and breeds deception.
6. **Caregiver modeling is part of the deal.** A caregiver who scrolls TikTok at the dinner table cannot effectively police a tween's TikTok use.

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Differentiate platforms by risk profile.
- Surface algorithm-driven harm vectors.
- Provide explicit account configuration steps.
- Plan disclosed monitoring approach.
- Address body image / mental health risk.
- Include 90-day review.

**Must Not:**
- Treat social media as inevitable.
- Recommend lying about age (most platforms officially 13+, but lying erodes both safety configs and trust).
- Default to "all teens have it."
- Promise harm-free use.
- Reduce to surveillance without trust.

## Instructions

### Stage 1 — Platform Risk Profile

| Platform | Algorithm intensity | DM exposure | Body image risk | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TikTok | Very high | High (open DMs by default) | High (looksmaxxing, ED content, looks compare) | Algorithm pushes content far past initial query |
| Instagram | High | High | Very high (filtered images, comparison) | Reels mimics TikTok intensity |
| Snapchat | Medium-high | Very high (Snap Maps, location, expiring content) | High | Disappearing-message dynamic; harassment vectors |
| YouTube | High (for shorts) / lower (for subscribed channels) | Medium | Medium | Comment moderation varies |
| Discord | Low (no algorithm in private servers) | Server-dependent | Low if servers managed | Predator and grooming risk in public servers |
| BeReal | Low algorithm | Low | Lower (real photos, friend-only) | Less commercial, smaller user base |
| Roblox/Fortnite social | Algorithmic-light | Voice + chat in games | Low | In-game risk vectors different |

Pick platform by use case, not just "what their friends use."

### Stage 2 — Decision: Allow or Delay?

Recommend delay or no if:
- Mental health concerns currently active.
- Body image / eating concerns or strong family history.
- Recent peer-conflict or bullying not resolved.
- Tween profile has rejection sensitivity, ADHD with notification dependence, or sustained anxiety.
- Tween is under 13 (platform terms; legal frame).

If allowed:
- Pick the lowest-algorithm platform that meets the actual social need.
- Treat it as a 90-day pilot.

### Stage 3 — Account Configuration (If Yes)

For TikTok / Instagram / Snapchat (highest risk):

- **Age honest** at signup.
- **Private account.**
- **DMs locked to followers only**, ideally none for first 90 days.
- **No location tagging.**
- **Comments restricted to followers, friends, or off.**
- **Mentions/tagging restricted.**
- **Activity and last-seen status hidden.**
- **Notifications: most off.**
- **Family Center or pairing tools enabled** where available.
- **Following list reviewed together at start; revisited monthly.**
- **No follow-back of strangers, no follow-back of celebrities/influencers in first 90 days** (algorithm seeded by initial follows).

For YouTube:
- YouTube Kids if age-eligible; otherwise restricted mode.
- Subscriptions reviewed.
- Comments off in early phase.

For Discord:
- Closed servers only (no public).
- DMs limited to friends.
- Voice channels managed.

### Stage 4 — Monitoring Approach (Disclosed)

- "I check the account weekly. That's the deal. Not because I don't trust you. Because the platform is designed to push content even you don't want."
- Use Family Center, Bark, or similar third-party where available.
- Tween can show caregiver content directly without shame.
- Tween can come to caregiver about something weird without consequence.

### Stage 5 — Conversations to Have Before First Login

- "What does the algorithm do?"
- "What if a stranger DMs you?"
- "What if someone posts something mean about you, or your friend?"
- "What if you see content that makes you feel bad about your body / your face / your life?"
- "What if you accidentally see something violent or sexual?"
- "What if a friend asks for an embarrassing photo?"
- "What if someone makes a fake image of you using AI?"

For each: scripted response. Pre-loading these is more effective than addressing them mid-incident.

### Stage 6 — 90-Day Pilot

Days 0–30: tightest configuration. Weekly co-review (look at the For You / feed together). Note flags.

Days 31–60: assess. Body image, mood, sleep, focus, grades, friendships — better, same, worse?

Days 61–90: review and decide — continue, tighten, expand, or pause.

If at any point: increased irritability, mood drop, sleep change, social withdrawal, body-image concerns, school decline → pause and reassess.

### Stage 7 — Specific High-Risk Vectors

- **Body image / ED content:** algorithm finds these fast; mute/block aggressively; coordinate with care if vulnerable.
- **Looksmaxxing / mewing / red-pill / manosphere:** male-typical adolescent rabbit hole; aware caregiving + value-based conversations.
- **Self-harm / suicide content:** report to platform, save evidence, coordinate care.
- **AI deepfake / non-consensual image generation:** legal in many places now; teach the topic; "if a fake image of you is made, come to me, no shame, we will handle it."
- **Influencer mental-health diagnoses:** TikTok in particular pushes self-diagnosis content; treat as conversation starter, not source of clinical truth.
- **Sextortion:** specific scam pattern; pre-load the response: "if this happens, come to me, no shame, no consequence; we will report and stop it."

### Stage 8 — Caregiver Modeling

- Family phone-down meals.
- Caregiver Screen Time visible.
- Caregiver doesn't post photos of the tween without consent.

## Adaptations

- **LGBTQ+ tween:** Online community can be a real lifeline; weigh both risk and benefit; pick lower-algorithm platforms with affirming spaces.
- **Identity-isolated tween (only [X] in school/town):** Online community can be a real lifeline; same calibration.
- **Body-image / ED vulnerability:** Delay or no, especially TikTok/Instagram.
- **ADHD:** Notifications mostly off; specific app focus; algorithm-driven scroll is hardest for this profile.
- **Autistic tween:** Special-interest accounts often beneficial; social platforms variably supportive.
- **Anxious tween:** Algorithm-driven comparison is high-risk; lower-algorithm platforms preferred.
- **Family conflict:** Don't add social-media decision into existing tension; resolve relational issues first.

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| Default to allow because peers have it | Decision per tween, per platform |
| Treat all platforms as equivalent | Risk profiles differ substantially |
| Allow algorithm-heavy platform first | Lower-algorithm platforms first if at all |
| Skip configuration | Configuration is half the risk reduction |
| Lie about tween's age to enable account | Erodes safety configs + trust |
| Surveil secretly | Disclosed monitoring; trust is the deal |
| Promise harm-free | Plan for issues; pre-load responses |
| Skip body-image / ED check | Major vector; delay if vulnerable |
| Treat self-diagnosis content as authoritative | Conversation starter, not clinical truth |
| Forget AI-deepfake reality | Pre-load the script; legal in many places |
| Forget caregiver modeling | Inconsistency collapses the rule |
| Add account decision to active conflict | Resolve relational issues first |
