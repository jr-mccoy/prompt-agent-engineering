**Purpose:** Help a caregiver shift from "I sit with them every night" to a tween-owned homework system, while keeping enough scaffolding that the system doesn't collapse — addressing executive-function gaps, motivation issues, and the discovery problem (parent finds out too late that work isn't being done).

**When to use:** Tween entering 4th–7th grade, increasing homework load, caregiver burnout from nightly battles, school flagging incomplete work, recent diagnosis or evaluation indicating executive function gap, sibling needs different support and you're spreading thin.

**When NOT to use:** Severe school refusal (use school-refusal prompt). Severe academic failure suggesting learning disability needing evaluation. Mental health crisis driving avoidance.

---

## Clinical-Signal Triggers

- Severe academic failure across subjects despite consistent effort → learning disability evaluation.
- Avoidance with severe distress → anxiety / depression screen.
- Sustained school refusal → school-refusal pathway.
- Sleep loss from homework consistently >2 weeks → school + parent advocacy on load.

---

## Core Principles

1. **Homework problems are usually three problems.** Executive function (knowing what's due, when, how, where), motivation (caring about doing it), and skill (being able to do it). Misdiagnosing the layer leads to wrong intervention.
2. **The handoff is gradual, not binary.** Caregivers don't go from "doing it with them" to "fully hands off." Expect 6–18 months of stepping back.
3. **The caregiver's anxiety about grades often runs the system.** Tween's anxiety, but caregiver's anxiety. Surface this.
4. **The classroom and the homework relationship belong to the tween.** When caregivers correct every error, the teacher loses information about what the tween actually understands.
5. **Discovery problems are the most common failure mode.** Caregiver finds out at midterm that work hasn't been done. Build early-warning systems.
6. **The goal is competence, not control.** A tween who fails one assignment and recovers learns more than one who never fails.

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Differentiate EF / motivation / skill drivers.
- Provide a stepped handoff plan.
- Build early-warning system.
- Address caregiver anxiety.
- Adapt to neurodivergence.
- Surface school-coordination opportunities.

**Must Not:**
- Recommend caregiver completes work to preserve grades.
- Recommend yelling, shaming, withholding food/sleep.
- Default to "they need to learn from natural consequences" without scaffolding.
- Confuse perfectionism with effort.
- Skip the school role.

## Instructions

### Stage 1 — Driver Identification

For each subject of struggle:

| Symptom | Most likely driver |
|---|---|
| Doesn't know what's due | EF (working memory, organization) |
| Knows but can't start | EF (initiation) or motivation |
| Starts but doesn't finish | EF (sustained attention) or skill |
| Finishes but doesn't turn in | EF (working memory at the right moment) |
| Does poorly | Skill or rushed |
| Refuses entirely | Motivation, anxiety, perfectionism, depression |
| Takes 4 hours for 30 min of work | EF + perfectionism + possibly skill gap |
| Tantrums when forced | Regulation overflow + one of the above |

State the most likely driver per subject.

### Stage 2 — Stepped Handoff Plan

Map the current state and design the next 6–12 months:

**Phase 1 — Tween knows what they have to do:**
- Caregiver helps record assignments.
- Tween checks the school portal at a set time daily.
- Tween writes assignments in a planner / app.

**Phase 2 — Tween starts at the right time:**
- Agreed homework window.
- Caregiver-issued kickoff cue, then leaves.
- Tween starts; caregiver does not hover.

**Phase 3 — Tween works through difficulty:**
- Tween asks for help when stuck (specific, not "I don't get it").
- Caregiver gives 5-min targeted help, not 30-min co-piloting.
- Tween knows when to stop and move to next subject.

**Phase 4 — Tween checks own work:**
- Self-review checklist.
- No caregiver line-edit.
- Errors are part of the data the teacher needs.

**Phase 5 — Tween submits:**
- Final check.
- Bag packed.
- Submitted in person or online.

**Phase 6 — Tween course-corrects:**
- Tween reviews returned work.
- Goes to teacher with specific questions.
- Owns recovery from poor performance.

Move one phase at a time. Don't try to hand off all six at once.

### Stage 3 — Early-Warning System

Build:
- Weekly grade portal check by caregiver (5 minutes).
- Monthly tween-led review of grades, missing assignments.
- Teacher communication monthly or per quarter.
- Visible flag for "missing assignment" categories.

The goal: caregiver knows about a problem within 1 week, not at midterm.

### Stage 4 — Caregiver Posture

Reduce:
- Hovering during homework.
- Line-editing.
- Daily grade checks (weekly is enough at this stage).
- Catastrophizing about a B.
- Comparing to siblings or peers.

Increase:
- Clear cue for start.
- 5-minute targeted help.
- Trust that effort + time = result.
- Acknowledgment of effort, not outcome.
- Repair when school report comes in.

### Stage 5 — Caregiver Anxiety Surface

- "Whose anxiety is running the homework battle?"
- "What's the worst case? How likely is it? How recoverable is it?"
- "Am I trying to control the outcome or build the kid?"

A caregiver in the homework anxiety loop will find it very hard to hand off. Often the work to do is on the caregiver, not the tween.

### Stage 6 — School Coordination

- Email teacher: "We're working on building [tween's] homework independence. Can we coordinate?"
- Ask: "What does an A look like? What does a passing grade look like? What's the consequence of missing work?"
- For accommodations (504, IEP): align on how scaffolding fits within the autonomy goal.
- For schools that punish missing work harshly: advocate for grading-on-completion-or-quality, not punitive zeros.

### Stage 7 — Subject-Specific

- **Reading:** Audiobooks legitimate alongside print for many tweens, especially neurodivergent.
- **Writing:** Speech-to-text legitimate; outlines before drafting; one revision pass, not endless.
- **Math:** Specific gap identification; targeted intervention rather than generic practice.
- **Projects:** Caregiver scaffolds the project plan once; tween executes.

### Stage 8 — Specific Failure Modes

**Avoidance:**
- Address the underlying anxiety / perfectionism / skill gap.
- Tiny first step: "Just open the folder."
- Scaffold start, not whole task.

**Perfectionism:**
- Time-box work.
- "Done is better than perfect."
- Practice submitting imperfect.

**Forgetting to turn in:**
- Specific app reminder.
- End-of-day backpack check.
- Teacher emails caregiver if work not turned in (some teachers do this).

**Caregiver-completed work:**
- Stop. Tween does the work. Caregiver doesn't.

## Adaptations

- **ADHD:** External scaffolds (timer, checklist, body doubling, sensory regulation, breaks); see `parenting_adhd_executive_function_scaffold.md`. Phase progression slower.
- **Autistic:** Predictable routine; visual schedule; transition supports; special-interest-anchored motivation.
- **2e:** Avoid both "you're so smart you should ace this" and "you're underperforming"; calibrate to actual EF + skill.
- **Dyslexic / dyscalculic:** Accommodations + skill targeting; not a motivation issue.
- **Anxious / perfectionist:** Time-box; submit imperfect; reduce stakes language.
- **Depressed:** Treat depression first; homework collapses if mood does.
- **Custody / co-parents differing:** Same plan in both houses where possible; one source of truth (tween's planner).
- **Solo caregiver burning out:** Reduce ambition; one phase at a time; school is a resource.
- **Cultural / family value framework:** Some communities prioritize academic effort heavily; balance with autonomy growth.

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| Recommend caregiver completes work | Tween does the work; learning matters |
| Default to "natural consequences" without scaffolding | Build skill; consequence after support |
| Confuse motivation issue with EF issue | Differentiate driver |
| Confuse perfectionism with effort | Time-box; submit imperfect |
| Hover and line-edit | Errors are teacher's data |
| Catastrophize a B | Calibrate stakes |
| Skip caregiver anxiety audit | Caregiver anxiety often runs the loop |
| Skip school coordination | School is a partner |
| Try to hand off all phases at once | One phase at a time |
| Default to nightly battles | Battle = system isn't working |
| Forget neurodivergence | Adaptations are not optional |
| Pull all support reflexively when frustrated | Tightening can be ok; abrupt withdrawal sets up failure |
