**Purpose:** Build a tween's money + chores + allowance system that distinguishes household-membership chores from earned-income work, sets up real budgeting categories, and creates the conditions for the tween to learn from financial decisions (including small failures) before stakes get higher.

**When to use:** First allowance setup, chore disagreements, sibling fairness conflicts, recent inheritance / gift money, tween wants to buy something expensive, tween is asking about babysitting / lawn-mowing / paid work, money lost / spent badly, family financial stress affecting the system.

**When NOT to use:** Family in active financial crisis (different conversation entirely); existing severe spending pattern indicating compulsive behavior (mental health pathway); use of money as control / abuse vector → relational repair first.

---

## Clinical-Signal Triggers

- Stealing money repeatedly → function analysis + mental health.
- Hoarding patterns with distress → mental health.
- Compulsive spending tied to mood → mental health.
- Caregiver using money as leverage in concerning ways → family / relational pathway.

---

## Core Principles

1. **Two distinct categories: household membership and earned income.** Conflating them ("you didn't make your bed so no allowance") undermines both.
2. **Allowance is for learning to manage money.** It serves practice, not behavior management.
3. **Chores are for being a member of the household.** Not for pay. The household is shared.
4. **Earned income is for going beyond.** Babysitting younger sibs, walking neighbor's dog, lawn work, dog washing, washing the family car — these are paid work.
5. **Real money learning requires real consequences.** A tween who never runs out and never makes a bad purchase doesn't learn.
6. **Save / spend / give categories preempt debates** about every transaction.

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Separate chores-for-membership from earned-income.
- Build save/spend/give categorization.
- Teach decision-making, not control purchases.
- Address sibling fairness without identical math.
- Allow real failures.
- Honor cultural / family value framing.

**Must Not:**
- Tie allowance to behavior or chore compliance.
- Default to "allowance is a reward."
- Default to controlling every purchase.
- Pretend this is the only money education that matters (school, family conversations matter too).
- Skip charitable giving framing.

## Instructions

### Stage 1 — Two-Category Distinction

**Household-membership chores:**
- Things every member does because the household is shared.
- Made bed, room reasonably tidy, dishes after meals, laundry pickup, pet care if family pet, taking out trash.
- Not paid. Not optional. Not bargained.

**Earned-income work:**
- Beyond-membership tasks.
- Mowing lawn, washing car, organizing garage, scrubbing baseboards, babysitting younger sibling, dog walking, helping with a sibling event.
- Paid at agreed rates.
- Tween can choose to do or not.

If a tween "doesn't want to" make their bed, that's a household membership conversation, not a money conversation.

### Stage 2 — Allowance Setup

Allowance = consistent recurring amount for managing money.

- **Amount:** Age-rough = $0.50–$2/year of age per week is a common starting point (so $5–$24/week for a 9–12-year-old, family-financial-context dependent). Pick what your family can sustain.
- **Frequency:** Weekly or biweekly. Match school cycle if helpful.
- **Method:** Cash, prepaid debit (Greenlight, Step, GoHenry), banked.
- **Adjustment:** Annual; tied to age and demonstrated competence with current amount.

### Stage 3 — Save / Spend / Give Categorization

Default split: 50% spend, 30% save, 20% give. Adjust per family values.

- **Spend (50%):** Tween's; for in-the-moment purchases. Tween can spend on what they want within family boundaries (no weapons, no inappropriate content). Tween experiences running out.
- **Save (30%):** For larger goals (specific item, future trip, college, future car). Tween picks goal. Caregiver may match contributions for specific savings goals.
- **Give (20%):** Charitable. Tween picks recipient. Family modeled giving.

If family follows religious tithing tradition, often this fits the give category.

### Stage 4 — Earned-Income Rates and System

Set rates per task:
- Mowing lawn (size-dependent): $5–$25
- Washing family car: $5–$10
- Babysitting younger sibling for date-night: $5–$10/hour
- Organizing project (garage section, basement): hourly or flat
- Dog walking for neighbor: market rate

Tween invoices (informal): "I did X, you owe me Y."
Caregiver pays promptly. Late payment is a bad lesson.

### Stage 5 — Big Purchase Practice

When tween asks for a big purchase:

- "What's the cost?"
- "How much do you have?"
- "How many weeks of allowance / earned work?"
- "Is it worth it to you?"

Let the tween decide. Even if it's a bad decision (an item that won't last). Real consequences are the teacher.

For very expensive items: parent-tween match arrangements ("you save half, we contribute half") are reasonable but not required.

### Stage 6 — Sibling Fairness

Avoid:
- Same dollar amount regardless of age (a 5-year-old and a 12-year-old shouldn't get the same).
- Public-facing comparison.
- Tying one sibling's amount to another's behavior.

Use:
- Age-tiered rates ("$1/year of age" makes the math automatic).
- Different earning opportunities per age.
- Each sibling's system is between them and the caregiver.

### Stage 7 — Specific Common Issues

**Tween blows it all in one day:**
- Don't bail out. The cycle is the lesson.
- Optional: short loan with repayment terms (interest framing, even nominal, teaches).
- Plan for next allowance differently.

**Tween hoards and won't spend:**
- Honor it; it's their money.
- Family modeling matters; if the savings is anxious, address.

**Tween wants to buy something the family doesn't allow:**
- Family boundaries hold (no weapons, age-inappropriate content).
- Otherwise: tween's money, tween's choice within reason.

**Tween steals from caregiver / sibling:**
- Address as theft, not allowance issue.
- Restitution + repair conversation.
- Function analysis (impulse, peer pressure, rebellion, mental health).

**Tween wants more / different:**
- Annual review.
- Earned income is the answer to "I want more."
- Don't capitulate just because tween asked.

**Sibling jealousy / fairness debate:**
- Each sibling's system is theirs.
- "Fair doesn't mean identical."

### Stage 8 — Real-World Money Education

Outside the system:
- Bring tween to grocery shop; talk about prices.
- Talk about caregiver's own money decisions (within age-appropriate disclosure).
- Talk about the family budget at age-appropriate level (rent / mortgage exists; food costs; savings exists).
- Avoid hiding money realities entirely (creates anxiety) and avoid burdening tween (creates parentification).

### Stage 9 — Tax / Legal Light Education

For older tweens:
- Earned-income from neighbors/family: not taxable up to thresholds, but worth conversation if substantial.
- W-2 / paycheck framing for future part-time work (often 14+).
- Banking: open a savings account; make the first deposit a ritual.

## Adaptations

- **ADHD tween (impulsive spending):** Cash visible (envelopes) often helps; prepaid debit with caregiver-visible app may help.
- **Anxious tween (avoid spending):** Honor; don't push.
- **Perfectionist tween:** Avoid making the system another evaluation arena.
- **Autistic tween (hyperspecific interest):** Saving for specific item often works very well; tween often a natural saver.
- **Family financial constraint:** Allowance amounts adjusted; earned-income emphasized; values-based framing.
- **Cultural context (religious tithing, family savings expectation):** Embed the value framework explicitly.
- **Custody / split households:** Consistent system in both houses ideally; allowance from one house is fine.
- **Solo caregiver:** Simplify the system; don't over-engineer.
- **Stepfamily:** Each tween has their own system; don't equalize across siblings of different families artificially.

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| Tie allowance to chore compliance | Separate; allowance is for money learning |
| Use allowance as behavior management | Different tools for behavior |
| Bail out tween who blew it all | Cycle is the lesson |
| Equalize sibling amounts identically | Age-tiered; "fair ≠ identical" |
| Control every purchase | Tween's money, tween's decisions within boundaries |
| Skip charitable giving | Build it in |
| Skip earned-income category | Going beyond is paid; membership is not |
| Hide all money realities from tween | Age-appropriate transparency |
| Burden tween with adult financial stress | Don't parentify |
| Default to prepaid debit without cash experience | Both modalities have value |
| Treat all spending decisions as evaluable by adults | Tween's value system can differ |
| Pretend tax / banking is too complex | Basic concepts at this age land |
