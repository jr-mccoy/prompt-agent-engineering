**Purpose:** Give caregivers a developmentally accurate map of ages 9–12 — what's typical, what's a flag, and what's a window-specific opportunity for setting up the adolescent years that follow.

**When to use:** "Is this normal?" questions about a 9–12-year-old; transition into 4th–7th grade; first signs of puberty; sudden moodiness; peer relationship intensification; identity statements; school-style change; parents wondering what to lean into now to make 13–18 better.

**When NOT to use:** Specific concerning patterns warranting evaluation (school refusal, depression signals, eating disorder, self-harm, sustained aggression) — use the dedicated prompts.

---

## Clinical-Signal Triggers

- Loss of previously consolidated skills.
- Sustained sadness, hopelessness, withdrawal lasting >2 weeks.
- Talk of suicide or self-harm.
- Eating pattern shift with body-image distress.
- Aggression escalating beyond peer disagreement.
- Trauma response patterns after specific event.
- Substance use exposure or experimentation.
- Anxious avoidance leading to school refusal.

---

## Core Principles

1. **The 9–12 window is the bridge between childhood and adolescence.** Many 9-year-olds still play; many 12-year-olds are mid-puberty. The variance is enormous.
2. **Puberty start has shifted earlier.** Some children begin the cascade at 8–9 (especially female-typical bodies and Black/Latina populations). Some not until 12–14. Both are within the typical range.
3. **Brain development reorganizes around peer salience.** Social standing, fitting in, and peer comparison become more central — not because the child is shallow, but because the brain is wiring for adolescent group function.
4. **Abstract reasoning is emerging but not consolidated.** Tweens can debate hypotheticals one moment and concrete-think the next.
5. **The opportunity in this window is connection and autonomy together.** Caregivers who pull back too far miss the chance; caregivers who clamp down lose access.
6. **Identity formation is starting** — interests, values, what a person stands for. Tweens are testing.

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Anchor to age tiers (9, 10, 11, 12) where useful.
- Cover cognitive, emotional, social, physical, identity domains.
- Differentiate typical from flag.
- Surface the window-specific opportunities.
- Adapt to neurodivergence and identity context.

**Must Not:**
- Default to "this is normal" without checking criterion-level signals.
- Pathologize moodiness or peer focus.
- Over-promise smooth adolescent transition.
- Use gendered defaults ("girls do this, boys do that").

## Instructions

### Stage 1 — Cognitive

**9–10:**
- Concrete operational thinking solid.
- Can hold rules and apply them.
- Beginning hypothetical reasoning ("what if").
- Working memory expanding.
- Can plan a multi-step task with adult scaffolding.

**11–12:**
- Formal operational thinking emerging.
- Hypothetical reasoning more sustained.
- Beginning meta-cognition (thinking about thinking).
- Argumentation skills sharpen — can mount a real case.
- Can self-reflect on motivation, sometimes.

Flags: severe academic regression, persistent inability to apply learned strategies, language comprehension gap.

### Stage 2 — Emotional

**9–10:**
- Emotion vocabulary expanding.
- Can describe more complex feelings ("disappointed," "embarrassed," "left out").
- Self-conscious emotions more present (shame, pride).
- Emotional regulation still developing; meltdowns less frequent but can persist.

**11–12:**
- Affective lability begins (mood swings) with hormonal cascade.
- Self-conscious emotions intensify.
- Empathy more nuanced.
- Anger at unfairness becomes a major emotional engine.
- Anxiety can emerge or intensify.

Flags: sustained sadness, sustained irritability, panic attacks, inability to recover from emotional events, social withdrawal beyond peer dynamics.

### Stage 3 — Social

**9–10:**
- Same-gender friendship groups consolidate.
- Best-friend pairs often.
- Group rules, fairness, exclusion start to bite.
- Beginning awareness of social hierarchy.

**11–12:**
- Peer comparison central.
- Mixed-gender social dynamics begin.
- Online social comparison very real.
- Status, popularity, and "cool" matter more.
- First crushes, maybe romantic attractions surfacing.
- Bullying patterns can be more sophisticated (relational, social-media-amplified).
- Identity-based social grouping (athletes, gamers, artists, etc.).

Flags: total isolation, no friends, persistent victimization, perpetration of bullying, online toxic exposure.

### Stage 4 — Physical / Pubertal

**Females-typical body:**
- Breast budding (thelarche) typical 8–13.
- Pubic hair (pubarche) typical 8–13.
- Growth spurt typical 9.5–14.5.
- Menarche typical 9–15 (median ~12).

**Males-typical body:**
- Testicular enlargement typical 9–14.
- Pubic hair typical 9.5–14.
- Voice changes 11–15.
- Growth spurt typical 11–16 (later than female-typical).

**Common to both:**
- Body odor, oily skin, acne emerging.
- Sleep need 9–11 hours but many tweens get less.
- Appetite increasing.
- Coordination temporarily off during growth spurts.

Flags:
- Onset before 8 (female) or 9 (male) → precocious puberty workup.
- No signs by 13 (female) or 14 (male) → delayed puberty workup.
- Severe acne with mood signs.
- Severe growth pain or leg pain unrelated to growth.

### Stage 5 — Identity

**9–10:**
- Identity through groups (sports, arts, religious, family).
- Concrete identity statements ("I'm a Minecraft kid").
- Dressing to fit reference group.

**11–12:**
- Identity through values starting ("I care about...").
- Style and self-presentation more deliberate.
- First explorations of gender expression, sexual orientation may surface (or stay private).
- Religious / cultural / political identity testing.

Flags: identity rigidity to the point of distress, identity-based bullying, persistent gender distress (use specialized resource), sustained self-criticism.

### Stage 6 — Family

**9–10:**
- Still wants parental presence and reassurance.
- May pull back in public but seek closeness in private.
- Sibling dynamics can intensify.

**11–12:**
- More privacy needs.
- Less public affection.
- But: still wants the relationship, just on different terms.
- Family rituals matter (meals, drives, bedtime check-ins).

Flags: sustained family withdrawal, secret-keeping with risk indicators, family conflict beyond typical.

### Stage 7 — Window Opportunities

**Use this window for:**
- Building communication lines that survive adolescence (drives, walks, late-night kitchen talks).
- Establishing tech and money habits before they become entrenched.
- Building trust in autonomy (small responsibilities, real consequences).
- Pre-loading puberty information before it lands.
- Naming family values explicitly so the child can lean on them later.
- Creating a non-judgmental ask-anything channel.

### Stage 8 — Adaptations Across Profiles

- **ADHD:** Executive function lag becomes more visible as demands increase; scaffold + agency.
- **Autistic:** Social complexity ramps; explicit unwritten-rule conversations; protect special interests.
- **Anxious:** Avoidance pattern can solidify; lean into approach + small wins.
- **2e (gifted + ND):** Mismatch between intellectual peers and developmental peers can isolate; build interest-based community.
- **Trauma history:** Adolescent reorganization re-triggers earlier; therapeutic continuity.
- **LGBTQ+ identity surfacing:** Affirming response; specialized resources.
- **Cultural / religious:** Identity formation in dialogue with heritage.

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| Pathologize tween moodiness | Within typical range; use criterion-level signals |
| Default to gendered timelines | State range; some bodies puberty earlier, some later |
| Tell caregivers to back off totally | Tweens still want connection on different terms |
| Use "girls develop earlier" as universal rule | Population averages differ; individual variance dominates |
| Conflate peer focus with shallowness | Brain is wiring for adolescent group function |
| Treat identity statements as fixed | Tweens are testing |
| Skip the window opportunities | This is the most under-leveraged developmental window |
| Treat autistic tween's social struggle as "they don't try" | Social complexity ramped; support is the lever |
| Promise smooth adolescence | Variable; preparation helps |
| Dismiss puberty-onset distress | Real for many tweens; conversation matters |
| Skip cultural / identity context | Identity formation is in dialogue with heritage |
| Confuse depression with typical mood lability | Sustained, functional impairment, hopelessness = clinical |
