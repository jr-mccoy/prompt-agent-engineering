# Safety & Risk Prompts (Caregiver-Facing)

Prevention-first safety planning for children at home and in other people's homes: body safety and consent, readiness to stay home alone, the questions to ask before playdates and sleepovers, and stage-matched childproofing. Each prompt ranks hazards by severity so the quiet, deadly risks (unsafe sleep, water, firearms, tip-overs, button batteries, medications) are handled before the frequent, minor ones.

## Scope

**In scope:**
- Age-tiered body-safety and consent teaching (2–12), grooming warning signs for adults, and the disclosure response.
- Home-alone and sibling-care readiness, including local legal-age guidance (which varies — always confirm locally).
- Safety questions for other homes: firearm storage, water, supervision, medications/edibles, rides, exit plans.
- Childproofing your own home by developmental stage.

## Safety notes

If a child discloses abuse: stay calm, believe them, **do not interrogate** or ask leading questions, write down their exact words, and report — local child protective services or police; Childhelp National Child Abuse Hotline 1-800-422-4453 (US). A Child Advocacy Center conducts the forensic interview. Recent contact (within about 72 hours) or injury → emergency department or CAC for a medical exam. Online exploitation → NCMEC CyberTipline. Poison exposure → Poison Help 1-800-222-1222 (US). Button-battery or magnet ingestion → emergency department immediately. Outside the US, use your local child-protection service, poison centre, and emergency number. None of these prompts gives legal advice.
