# Body Safety and Consent Lessons

**Objective:** Produce a body-safety plan for one child: the concepts to teach at
their age, the exact words, short repeated practice, the trusted-adult list, the
household rules adults follow, the grooming behaviours adults watch for, and a
disclosure response the caregiver can follow under stress.

**When to Use:**
- A caregiver wants to teach body safety to a child aged 2–12 before anything has
  happened.
- A child is starting daycare, school, sports, sleepovers, or camp.
- A caregiver is uneasy about an adult's behaviour toward their child.
- A child has said something that might be a disclosure (go straight to the Safety
  Block).
- **Not this prompt if** you need puberty, periods, or reproduction conversations —
  use `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/ages-9-12/parenting_puberty_prep_conversation_scripts.md`.
  For a single scripted hard conversation with a 4–8-year-old across many topics, use
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/ages-4-8/parenting_hard_topics_age_appropriate_scripts.md`.
  **Online grooming and sextortion** are covered by
  `domain-psy-ops/personal-defense/psyops_youth_online_manipulation_guide.md`; teen
  dating and consent by
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/ages-13-18/parenting_teen_dating_consent_conversation.md`.

## Safety Block — If a Child Discloses

1. **Stay calm and believe them.** Your face is the child's evidence of whether
   telling was safe. "Thank you for telling me. It's not your fault. I'm going to
   help."
2. **Do not interrogate.** Do not ask leading questions ("Did Uncle Ray touch your
   private parts?"), repeat questioning, or ask them to show you. Let them say what
   they say. Specialist forensic interviewers at a **Child Advocacy Center (CAC)**
   will talk with the child; repeated adult questioning can contaminate the account.
3. **Write down the child's exact words**, the date, time, and what prompted the
   disclosure, as soon as you can.
4. **Report.** In the US: local child protective services or police; Childhelp
   National Child Abuse Hotline **1-800-422-4453** helps you reach the right agency.
   Many states make every adult a mandated reporter; professionals (teachers,
   clinicians, coaches) always are. Outside the US, contact your local child-protection
   service or police.
5. **Protect the child now.** No contact with the person named. Do not confront them
   in front of the child or warn them before reporting.
6. **If contact was recent (within about 72 hours) or there is injury, pain,
   bleeding, or discharge** → emergency department or CAC for a medical exam; do not
   bathe the child or wash clothing beforehand if possible.
7. **Online exploitation** → NCMEC CyberTipline (report.cybertip.org). Danger now →
   911.
8. **Get support for the child and yourself:** CAC-linked therapist (TF-CBT is
   the evidence-based treatment); RAINN 1-800-656-4673 for caregivers.

## Inputs / Context

1. **Child:** age, verbal level, neurodivergence, disability (children with
   disabilities face roughly three times the risk of abuse).
2. **Settings:** childcare, school, sports, faith groups, babysitters, relatives,
   overnight stays.
3. **Family language and values** about bodies; words already used.
4. **Any specific concern** about an adult or older child.

## Method

1. **Teach by age tier (ST-02).**
   - **2–4:** correct names (penis, vulva, bottom, breasts); "your body belongs to
     you"; the touching rule — no one touches your private parts except to keep you
     clean or healthy, and then a grown-up you trust is there; "tell me, even if
     someone said not to."
   - **5–8:** add secrets vs. surprises (surprises are fun and get told soon; secrets
     about touching or bodies always get told); "no" and "stop" to unwanted touch,
     including tickling; three to five named trusted adults; tricks — gifts, special
     attention, "this is our special game."
   - **9–12:** add consent in friendships, pressure from older kids, photos and
     messages (link to online guide), and how grooming works — trust building,
     isolation, gradual boundary-pushing.
2. **Explain why each rule exists (CM-11).** Correct names let a child report
   clearly and signal to offenders that the child has been taught; most children who
   are abused are harmed by someone they know (about 9 in 10), so "stranger danger"
   alone fails.
3. **Live body autonomy at home (DP-04).** No forced hugs or kisses (offer a wave or
   high-five); stop tickling when the child says stop; knock on closed doors from
   about age 5–6.
4. **Practise little and often.** 2-minute "what-if" games at bath time or in the
   car, revisited monthly: "What if a big kid at camp says let's play a secret
   game?"
5. **Set adult-side household rules.** Prefer settings where adults are observable
   and interruptible; know the youth organisation's two-adult or no one-on-one
   policy; ask babysitters and activities about background checks and training.
6. **Know grooming behaviours (DP-05).** An adult or older youth who seeks
   one-on-one time, gives special gifts or privileges, keeps secrets with the child,
   tests boundaries (wrestling, tickling, bathroom intrusions), offers to babysit for
   free repeatedly, contacts the child privately online, or undermines the child's
   trust in parents. One sign is a reason to watch and limit access; a pattern is a
   reason to act and to consult a child-protection line.
7. **Know normal versus concerning child sexual behaviour.** Curiosity, touching
   own genitals, "playing doctor" between same-age children — common. Sexual
   knowledge beyond age, coercion, large age gaps, repeated behaviour after redirection,
   or distress → pediatrician or child-protection consultation.
8. **Rehearse the disclosure response** with the other caregivers so everyone
   responds the same way.

## Output Format

```
# Body Safety Plan — [child, age]

## What to teach now (concepts + exact words)
## Practice games (monthly)
## Trusted adults (3–5, child-chosen with you)
## Household body-autonomy rules
## Adult-side safeguards (settings, organisations, sitters)
## Grooming behaviours to watch + action rule
## Normal vs. concerning child sexual behaviour
## If my child discloses (one-page card)
```

## Verification

- [ ] Correct anatomical names used.
- [ ] Touching rule, secrets vs. surprises, and trusted adults are age-matched.
- [ ] Rules carry reasons; nothing is framed as fear of strangers only.
- [ ] Disclosure card includes "do not interrogate," exact-words record, report route, 72-hour medical step.
- [ ] Online risks are routed to the online-manipulation guide, not omitted.

## False-Positive Prevention

| Misfire | What it looks like | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Stranger-danger framing | "Never talk to strangers" | Most abuse is by known people; teach rules about touch and secrets. |
| Fear-based delivery | Scary stories | Calm, matter-of-fact, empowerment frame. |
| Treating curiosity as abuse | Panic at a 4-year-old touching themselves | Normal; redirect privately. |
| Investigating yourself | Repeated questions, confronting the adult | Report; let a CAC interview. |
| Making the child responsible | "If you'd said no it wouldn't happen" | Prevention is adults' job; it is never the child's fault. |
| One talk, done | Single conversation at age 5 | Repeat briefly and often through 12. |

## Adaptations

- **Non-speaking / AAC users:** add body-part and "stop" symbols to the device;
  teach and record a "tell" sign.
- **Intellectual disability:** concrete rules, visual supports, more repetition;
  plan for intimate-care routines with consent language.
- **Faith or modesty norms:** keep correct names and the touching rule; adapt
  framing to the family's language about privacy.
- **Abuse in family history:** caregiver's own trauma may surface; get support.

## Example Output

```
# Body Safety Plan — Sam, 6

## Teach now
"Your body belongs to you. Your private parts — penis and bottom — are
covered by your swimsuit. Nobody touches them except to help you stay
clean or healthy, like the doctor with Mum there. If anybody does, or asks
you to touch theirs, or shows you pictures of private parts, tell me or
another safe grown-up. Even if they say it's a secret. You won't be in
trouble."
Secrets vs. surprises: "Surprises are fun and get told soon, like a present.
Secrets about bodies always get told."

## Practice
Car game, first Saturday monthly. "What if your coach says, 'Don't tell
Mum I gave you candy'?" (Answer: tell.)

## Trusted adults
Mum, Dad, Grandma Pat, Ms. Ortiz (teacher), Aunt Lou.

## Household rules
Hugs are offered, not required. Tickling stops at "stop."

## Adult-side
Soccer club: confirmed two-adult rule and background checks.

## Grooming watch
One sign → watch and limit one-on-one time. Pattern → call Childhelp for
advice; no unsupervised contact.

## If Sam discloses
Calm. "Thank you for telling me. It's not your fault." No questions beyond
"Is there anything else you want to tell me?" Write his words. Call CPS or
police / Childhelp 1-800-422-4453. Recent contact → ER or CAC, no bath.
```

## Techniques Used

- **CM-11 Reasoning-Based Constraint Design** — each rule is paired with why it protects.
- **DP-04 Must-Not Constraints** — no forced affection, no interrogation, no confronting.
- **DP-05 Stakes-Based Gate Policy** — single sign vs. pattern vs. disclosure trigger different actions.
- **ST-02 Structured Sequential Instructions** — age tiers and a step-by-step disclosure card.
- **NE-07 Emotional Validation First** — "thank you for telling me" before any action.
