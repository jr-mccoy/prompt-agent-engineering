# Other Homes Safety Questions

**Objective:** Produce a short, polite, complete question set for a specific visit
to another home, the script to ask it, a yes / adjust / not-this-time decision rule,
and an exit plan the child knows.

**When to Use:**
- Before a first playdate, a sleepover, a birthday party at a home, or a regular
  after-school arrangement at a friend's or relative's house.
- A new babysitter, grandparent, or co-parent's partner will care for the child in
  their home.
- A caregiver feels awkward asking about guns or supervision and wants words.
- **Not this prompt if** you are auditing **your own** home — use
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/safety-risk/parenting_home_childproofing_by_stage.md`.
  If the concern is a teen's friend group or party culture rather than a specific
  house, use
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/ages-13-18/parenting_teen_friend_group_concerns.md`.
  Online contact and grooming by strangers are covered by
  `domain-psy-ops/personal-defense/psyops_youth_online_manipulation_guide.md`.

## Safety Block

- **Firearms:** about 4.6 million US children live in a home with at least one
  loaded, unlocked gun (2018 national survey), and firearms are now the leading cause
  of death for US children and teens. If a firearm is present and the host will not
  confirm it is stored unloaded and locked with ammunition locked separately, host
  at your home instead.
- **Water:** drowning is the leading cause of death for children aged 1–4 in the
  US, and it is silent and fast. No swimming without a named, sober adult "water
  watcher" who is not on their phone; four-sided pool fencing for young children.
- **If your child tells you something unsafe happened at another home** (touching,
  being shown a gun, alcohol given) → stay calm, thank them, follow the disclosure
  steps in `parenting_body_safety_consent_lessons.md`; Childhelp 1-800-422-4453 (US).
- Poison exposure → Poison Help 1-800-222-1222 (US); emergency → 911.

## Inputs / Context

1. **Event:** playdate, sleepover, party, regular care; duration; overnight?
2. **Child:** age, swimming ability, allergies/medical needs, any history of
   wandering or impulsivity, phone or not.
3. **Host:** how well you know them; who lives in or visits the home (older teens,
   adult relatives).
4. **Activities planned:** pool, trampoline, outings, driving, movies, gaming.
5. **Your non-negotiables** vs. preferences (some families allow PG-13 films; firearm
   storage is not a preference).

## Method

1. **Frame the ask as routine (CM-11).** People answer more openly when the question
   is "something I ask everyone," because it signals no judgment of them. Ask by
   phone or in person, not mid-drop-off with the children listening.
2. **Ask the core set every time (ST-02):**
   - **Firearms:** "Is there a gun in the home? How is it stored?" Acceptable:
     unloaded, locked in a safe or with a lock, ammo locked separately, key/code
     not reachable by kids.
   - **Supervision:** "Who'll be home? Will an adult be there the whole time?"
     Older siblings in charge and adults leaving are different events.
   - **Water:** pool, hot tub, pond, lake — fenced? Who watches?
   - **Medications and substances:** locked away, including cannabis edibles,
     vapes, and alcohol at parties.
   - **Allergies / medical:** do they know the plan; is the epinephrine going with
     the child; will they read labels?
   - **Driving:** "Will they be in the car? Who drives? Is there a booster/car seat?"
3. **Add situation-specific questions.** Sleepover: who sleeps where; other adults
   or older teens overnight; bedtime screens. Dogs: size, temperament, separated from
   kids? Trampoline: one child at a time, netting (AAP discourages home trampoline
   use). Screens: film ratings, online gaming with strangers.
4. **Apply the decision rule (DP-05).**
   - **Yes:** clear, specific answers.
   - **Adjust:** daytime instead of overnight; your home instead; you stay; no
     swimming; you drive.
   - **Not this time:** firearm storage unconfirmed; no adult present; evasive or
     irritated answers on the core set.
5. **Give the child an exit plan.** A code phrase in a call or text ("Did I leave my
   green sweatshirt?") means "come get me, no questions now." Promise pick-up without
   punishment. For teens, an "X-plan": text "X," parent calls with a pretext.
6. **Reciprocate.** Offer the same information about your home; it normalises the
   questions.

## Output Format

```
# Visit Safety Questions — [child] at [host], [event, date]

## How I'll ask (opening line)
## Core questions + acceptable answers
## Situation-specific questions
## Decision: yes / adjust (how) / not this time (why)
## Child's exit plan (code phrase, pick-up promise)
## What I'll share about our home
```

## Verification

- [ ] Firearm question asked with storage specifics, not "any guns?" alone.
- [ ] Supervision names a specific adult for the whole period.
- [ ] Water and medications/edibles covered where relevant.
- [ ] Decision rule applied; an unconfirmed firearm or no adult → not this time.
- [ ] Exit plan rehearsed with the child.

## False-Positive Prevention

| Misfire | What it looks like | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Not asking to avoid awkwardness | Assuming "nice family" = safe | Ask everyone; storage is not visible from the porch. |
| Interrogating the host | Ten-minute grilling at the door | Short phone ask, framed as routine. |
| Accepting "it's hidden" | Gun "on the top shelf" | Hidden is not locked; children find hidden guns. |
| Treating preferences as safety | Refusing over a PG film | Separate non-negotiables from preferences. |
| Ending friendships over one answer | Cutting off the family | Adjust the format (your house, daytime). |
| Punishing the exit call | Lecture on pickup | The plan works only if using it is safe. |

## Adaptations

- **Grandparents and relatives:** the same questions apply; older adults' medications
  (opioids, heart pills in daily organisers) are a common poisoning source.
- **Co-parent's home:** use neutral, child-focused wording; if storage or supervision
  is a genuine concern, see co-parenting and custody prompts rather than negotiating
  at handover.
- **Rural / hunting households:** firearms are normal; storage is still the question.
- **Child with autism or wandering risk:** ask about exterior door locks and water
  nearby; elopement and drowning risk are linked.

## Example Output

```
# Visit Safety Questions — Ella (8) sleepover at the Novaks', Sat 14 Sept

## Opening (phone, Wednesday)
"Ella's so excited. I ask every family the same few safety questions before
a sleepover — is now okay?"

## Core questions → answers
Guns: "Yes, a handgun." Storage: "In a lockbox in our closet, unloaded;
ammo in the garage cabinet, locked. Only we have the code." → acceptable.
Supervision: both parents home all night; 16-year-old son also home.
Water: hot tub, covered and locked. → ask: no hot tub at night.
Meds/edibles: "We have edibles — in a locked box in the kitchen." → acceptable.
Allergy: Ella's tree-nut allergy; auto-injector goes in her bag; Mrs Novak
will check pizza and dessert labels.
Driving: no outings planned.

## Situation-specific
Sleeping: girls in the den; son's room is upstairs. Film: PG.

## Decision
Yes, with adjustment: no hot tub; text at bedtime.

## Exit plan
Code: "Did I leave my green sweatshirt?" → Dad picks up, no questions until
morning. Practised Thursday.

## Our home
Shared that we have no firearms or pool, and our dog is crated during visits.
```

## Techniques Used

- **ST-02 Structured Sequential Instructions** — a fixed core set then situation-specific questions.
- **CM-11 Reasoning-Based Constraint Design** — each question carries why it matters, so the caregiver can explain and adapt.
- **DP-05 Stakes-Based Gate Policy** — yes / adjust / not-this-time gates keyed to severity.
- **IT-24 Template-Based Educational Scaffolding** — a reusable question template with fill-ins and acceptable-answer guides.
