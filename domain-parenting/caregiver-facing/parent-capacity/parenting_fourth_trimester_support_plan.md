# Fourth-Trimester Support Plan

**Objective:** Produce a written 12-week plan for the household after a birth or
adoption — who sleeps when, who supports feeding, who comes to help and what they do,
the recovery milestones and red-flag symptoms, the dates each parent's mood will be
checked, and exactly what happens at each level of concern.

**When to Use:**
- A family is in late pregnancy, awaiting an adoption placement, or in the first
  weeks home, and wants a plan before exhaustion sets in.
- A partner's leave is ending and the household needs to restructure.
- Relatives are offering help and it needs organising so it actually helps.
- **Not this prompt if** something **already feels wrong** with a parent's mood and
  you want to sort baby blues from postpartum depression, anxiety, OCD, PTSD, or
  psychosis — use
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/ages-0-3/parenting_postpartum_parent_capacity_check.md`.
  If you have an EPDS or PHQ-9 score and want to know what it means for you, use
  `domain-psychology/client-self-use/specialty/clientself_perinatal_self_screen_and_plan.md`.
  For a physical symptom right now, use
  `domain-healthcare-clinical/prompts/specialty/medicine_postpartum_warning_signs_triage_support.md`.
  This prompt is the proactive household plan.

## Safety Block

- **Emergency (911 / ED now):** thoughts of harming the baby or self with intent;
  confusion, hallucinations, or strange beliefs (possible postpartum psychosis — a
  medical emergency; do not leave the parent alone with the baby); chest pain,
  trouble breathing, seizure.
- **Urgent post-birth physical signs (call 911 or go to the ED; up to 6 weeks and
  beyond):** bleeding soaking a pad an hour or clots the size of an egg; a
  headache that won't go away or vision changes (possible postpartum preeclampsia);
  fever 100.4 °F / 38 °C or higher; a red, swollen, painful leg; an incision that is
  opening or oozing. These follow the AWHONN POST-BIRTH warning signs.
- **Any thought of self-harm, or EPDS item 10 scored above 0** → 988 Suicide &
  Crisis Lifeline (US) and the clinician the same day.
- **Support lines (US):** Postpartum Support International 1-800-944-4773;
  National Maternal Mental Health Hotline 1-833-852-6262 (24/7, call or text).
- Outside the US, use your local emergency number, maternity unit, and perinatal
  mental-health services.

## Inputs / Context

1. **Birth or adoption details:** date (or due date), type of birth or recovery
   needs, NICU, multiples.
2. **Parents:** history of depression, anxiety, bipolar disorder, or previous
   postpartum illness (raises risk — tell the care team now); other children.
3. **Feeding plan:** breastfeeding, formula, combination, pumping, donor milk.
4. **Leave:** each parent's dates and flexibility.
5. **Helpers:** who, when available, what they're good at, any tension.
6. **Health contacts:** OB/midwife, pediatrician, lactation (IBCLC), GP.

## Method

1. **Predict the failure points (DP-07).** Common ones: nights 3–14 with no sleep
   block; feeding pain or supply problems that nobody fixes for a week; visitors who
   need hosting; the partner's return to work around week 2–6; a parent who stops
   eating properly; mood symptoms nobody asks about because "everyone's tired."
2. **Write the sleep shifts (ST-02).** Aim for each adult to get one protected core
   block of about 4–5 hours most nights. Example: Parent A sleeps 21:00–02:00
   (earplugs, other room); Parent B covers with expressed milk or formula, or brings
   the baby for feeds and resettles. Then swap. Safe-sleep rules apply to whoever is
   on shift.
3. **Plan feeding support whatever the method.** Who handles bottles, washing,
   pump parts, and night feeds; an IBCLC contact booked before day 5 if
   breastfeeding; the rule that feeding pain, poor latch, or worry about intake gets
   help in days, not weeks.
4. **Organise helpers with a briefing sheet (NE-20).** One page for each helper:
   what helps (laundry, meals, holding the baby while a parent sleeps, older
   siblings), what doesn't (expecting to be hosted, advice the parents didn't ask
   for), house rules, safe-sleep basics, and which signs they should mention to a
   parent. A visitors rule: short visits, by invitation, no one ill.
5. **Track physical recovery.** Contact with the birth clinician within about 3
   weeks and a full postpartum visit by 12 weeks (ACOG's current recommendation);
   lifting limits after a caesarean; pelvic-floor and wound checks; the warning signs
   on the fridge.
6. **Pre-schedule mood check-ins for both parents (DP-05).** The pediatrician can
   screen the birthing parent at the 1-, 2-, 4-, and 6-month well-baby visits (AAP
   guidance); add the 2-week and 6-week marks at home. Partners also develop
   depression (about 1 in 10). Use the EPDS or the clinic's tool. Escalation ladder:
   - **Next appointment:** low mood or anxiety on some days, coping.
   - **Clinician this week:** EPDS about 10–12, or symptoms most days for 2 weeks.
   - **Clinician within 24 hours:** EPDS 13 or more, not sleeping even when the baby
     sleeps, not eating, panic attacks.
   - **Same day / emergency:** anything in the Safety Block.
   Thresholds vary between services; follow the local team's cut-offs.
7. **Plan the return-to-work cliff.** The week before a partner goes back, re-run
   the sleep shifts and line up daytime help for the first 2 weeks alone.

## Output Format

```
# Fourth-Trimester Plan — [family], baby [name/date], weeks 0–12

## Predicted failure points and our counter for each
## Sleep shifts (who, when, how feeds happen)
## Feeding support (roles; IBCLC contact; when to get help)
## Helpers rota + one-page helper briefing
## Physical recovery: appointments, limits, warning signs (on the fridge)
## Mood check-in dates for both parents + escalation ladder
## Return-to-work week plan
## Contacts (care team, support lines)
```

## Verification

- [ ] Each adult has a protected sleep block in the shift plan.
- [ ] Feeding support has named roles and a lactation or feeding contact.
- [ ] Helper briefing lists what helps and what doesn't.
- [ ] Urgent physical warning signs and the emergency psychosis rule are written.
- [ ] Mood check-ins are scheduled for **both** parents, with a four-step ladder.
- [ ] Return-to-work week is planned.

## False-Positive Prevention

| Misfire | What it looks like | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Normalising everything | "All new parents feel like this" for 3 weeks of panic | Use the ladder; persistent symptoms get assessed. |
| Pathologising the blues | Alarm at tears on day 4 | Baby blues peak around days 3–5 and fade by ~2 weeks. |
| Ignoring the partner | Only the birthing parent screened | Screen both. |
| Help that adds work | Visitors to be fed and entertained | Briefing sheet; helpers do tasks. |
| Feeding as one person's job | Night feeds always the birthing parent | Shifts and roles for every feeding method. |
| Plan made after the crisis | Writing it at 3 a.m. in week 2 | Write it before birth or in the first days. |

## Adaptations

- **Adoptive or surrogacy parents:** no physical recovery, but sleep, bonding, and
  post-adoption depression are real; screen both parents.
- **NICU stay:** plan hospital-visit rotas, pumping logistics, and the parent's own
  meals; NICU parents carry higher risk of anxiety and trauma symptoms.
- **Previous postpartum illness or bipolar disorder:** a perinatal psychiatry plan
  before birth; sleep protection is part of treatment.
- **Single parent:** the helper rota is the sleep plan; ask for overnight help in
  the first 2 weeks.
- **Twins or more:** feeding and sleep plans double; ask early for help.
- **Cultural postpartum practices** (e.g. confinement periods): build them into the
  plan.

## Example Output

```
# Fourth-Trimester Plan — Dana and Luis, baby Noor (due 12 Nov), weeks 0–12

## Failure points
Nights 3–14 (no sleep) → shifts below. Luis back at work 1 Dec → week-
before plan. Dana's history of anxiety → told midwife 30 Sep; check-ins
below. Dana's mum's visits can turn into advice → briefing sheet.

## Sleep shifts (combination feeding)
Dana sleeps 21:00–02:00 in the spare room; Luis gives one expressed or
formula bottle at ~23:30 and resettles. Luis sleeps 02:00–07:00; Dana
breastfeeds overnight. Weekend naps 13:00–15:00 alternate.

## Feeding
IBCLC Maria booked for day 3 home visit. Luis washes bottles and pump
parts nightly. Pain or worry about wet nappies → call Maria same day.

## Helpers
Week 1–2: Dana's mum 10:00–15:00 weekdays. Briefing: laundry, lunch, hold
Noor so Dana naps; please don't comment on feeding. Back to sleep, flat
cot, nothing in it. Tell us if Dana isn't eating or seems very low or
"not herself."
Meal train (8 friends) weeks 1–4.

## Recovery
Birth check: midwife day 3–5; clinic contact by week 3 (3 Dec); full visit
by week 12. POST-BIRTH signs on the fridge.

## Mood check-ins (both parents)
Week 2 (26 Nov) and 6 (24 Dec) at home with EPDS; pediatrician screens
Dana at 1, 2, 4, 6-month visits; Luis asks his GP at week 6.
Ladder: next appt (some low days) → this week (EPDS 10–12 or most days 2
wks) → 24 h (EPDS ≥13, can't sleep when Noor sleeps) → same day / 911
(any self-harm thought, confusion, strange beliefs).

## Return-to-work week (24–30 Nov)
Re-run shifts: Dana's core block 20:00–01:00 on work nights; Dana's sister
covers Mon/Wed mornings for 2 weeks.

## Contacts
Midwife, Dr. Chen (peds), IBCLC Maria, PSI 1-800-944-4773, National
Maternal Mental Health Hotline 1-833-852-6262, 988, 911.
```

## Techniques Used

- **DP-07 Failure Mode Prediction** — the predictable crunch points (nights 3–14, return to work, unorganised help) each get a counter.
- **ST-02 Structured Sequential Instructions** — shift schedule and step-by-step escalation ladder.
- **NE-20 Third-Party Handoff Package** — a one-page helper briefing sheet.
- **DP-05 Stakes-Based Gate Policy** — four escalation levels tied to EPDS scores and symptoms.
