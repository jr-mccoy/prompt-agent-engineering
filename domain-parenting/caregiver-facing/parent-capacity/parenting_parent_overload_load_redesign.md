# Parent Overload and Depletion — Redesigning the Load

**Objective:** Produce a load-redesign plan for one depleted parent: a burnout check
that is not a diagnosis, an honest inventory of what they carry (including the
planning nobody sees), concrete decisions to drop, defer, delegate, or simplify, a
"minimum viable week," named asks, and clear thresholds for depression or crisis.

**When to Use:**
- A parent says "I can't keep doing this," snaps more than they want, feels numb
  toward their children, or fantasises about running away.
- The family's schedule has grown (activities, therapies, school demands, a child
  with high needs) and nobody has redesigned it.
- One parent carries most of the remembering, planning, and organising.
- **Not this prompt if** you are the main carer for an **ill, disabled, or ageing
  relative** and need an emotional and health check — use
  `domain-psychology/client-self-use/specialty/clientself_caregiver_burnout_plan.md`.
  If you need to calm yourself **in the moment** before responding to your child, use
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/ages-4-8/parenting_parent_coregulation_reset.md`.
  To set up the chore rota once tasks are reassigned, use
  `domain-productivity/home-life/home_chore_rotation_designer.md`. If the load problem
  is mainly a fight with your partner, also see
  `parenting_couple_strain_under_parenting_load.md` in this folder. This prompt
  redesigns the parenting load.

## Safety Block

- **Thoughts of ending your life, or that your family would be better off without
  you** → 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (US, call/text) now. Immediate danger → 911.
- **Fear you will hurt your child, or you have hit, shaken, or screamed at your
  child in a way that scared you** → put the child somewhere safe and step away; call
  the National Parent Helpline 1-855-427-2736 or Childhelp 1-800-422-4453 (US) for
  parent support today.
- **Signs of depression for two weeks or more** — low mood or loss of interest most
  days, plus changes in sleep, appetite, energy, concentration, or feelings of
  worthlessness → see your GP or a mental-health clinician this week. Load redesign
  helps burnout; it does not treat depression.
- **Within 12 months of a birth** → see also the perinatal routes in
  `parenting_fourth_trimester_support_plan.md` (this folder).
- **Drinking or using substances to get through the day** → GP; SAMHSA helpline
  1-800-662-4357 (US).
- Outside the US, use your local crisis, parenting, and health services.

## Inputs / Context

1. **Household:** adults, children's ages and needs, work hours.
2. **A typical week:** every recurring task and commitment, who does it, how long.
3. **Invisible work:** who remembers, plans, books, notices, decides.
4. **Signs:** sleep, mood, irritability, distance from children, health, alcohol.
5. **Support:** partner, family, friends, paid help, money for help.
6. **Standards:** which "shoulds" drive the load (homemade food, every activity,
   spotless house).

## Method

1. **Run a burnout check, not a diagnosis (DS-01).** Parental burnout research
   (Mikolajczak, Roskam) describes four features specific to the parent role:
   overwhelming **exhaustion** from parenting, **emotional distancing** from the
   children (doing the minimum, going through the motions), being **fed up** with the
   role, and **contrast** with the parent you used to be. Rate each 0–3 for the last
   month. Several at 2–3 = burnout pattern; plan below. Depression signs in the Safety
   Block override this step.
2. **Inventory the load (NE-11).** List every recurring task with weekly hours and
   owner. Include the cognitive work — **anticipating** needs, **identifying**
   options, **deciding**, and **monitoring** — which is often invisible and uneven.
   `Weekly load = Σ(task hours) + cognitive hours`. Compare adults' totals including
   paid work.
3. **Find the single binding constraint (DP-09).** Often: no protected sleep, the
   4–7 pm crunch, one child's high-needs schedule, or being the only one who knows
   how things work. Redesign that first.
4. **Decide each task: drop / defer / delegate / simplify.**
   - **Drop:** commitments that cost more than they give (third activity, the
     birthday party standard).
   - **Defer:** non-urgent projects until after a crunch period.
   - **Delegate:** whole tasks with the thinking included ("You own the dentist —
     booking, reminders, going"), to a partner, older children, family, or paid help.
   - **Simplify:** rotating meals, uniforms laid out, groceries delivered, lower
     standards.
5. **Write a minimum viable week.** The fewest things that keep children fed, safe,
   at school, connected, and slept — the floor for bad weeks.
6. **Protect recovery.** At least one block a week where the parent is off duty and
   not on call, plus a sleep protection plan.
7. **Make specific asks** with dates.
8. **Gate the follow-up (DP-05).** Re-rate the four burnout features at 2 and 6
   weeks. No improvement, or any depression or safety sign → professional help.

## Output Format

```
# Load Redesign — [parent], [household]

## Burnout check (exhaustion / distance / fed up / contrast: 0–3 each)
## Depression and safety screen result
## Load inventory: tasks, hours, owner; cognitive work; totals per adult
## Binding constraint
## Decisions: drop / defer / delegate (whole task) / simplify
## Minimum viable week
## Recovery block and sleep protection
## Asks (who, what, by when)
## Re-check at 2 and 6 weeks; when to get professional help
```

## Verification

- [ ] Depression and safety screen is completed before redesign advice.
- [ ] Burnout features are rated, not assumed.
- [ ] Cognitive work is counted, with owners.
- [ ] Delegated tasks include the thinking, not just the doing.
- [ ] Minimum viable week is written.
- [ ] Re-check dates and professional-help thresholds are stated.

## False-Positive Prevention

| Misfire | What it looks like | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Self-care as the fix | "Schedule a spa day" | The load must change; rest alone refills a leaking tank. |
| Missing depression | Treating 2 months of hopelessness as burnout | Screen first; refer. |
| Delegating the doing only | Partner "helps" when asked | Hand over whole tasks with the planning. |
| Parent-blame | "You need better time management" | Burnout comes from demand exceeding resources. |
| Cutting what refuels | Dropping the parent's one hobby first | Protect what restores; cut low-value obligations. |
| Pathologising a hard week | Burnout label after one bad week | Look at the last month; a bad week needs rest, not a plan. |

## Adaptations

- **Child with disability or chronic illness:** count therapy and appointment
  coordination; ask about respite and care-coordination services.
- **Single parent:** delegation goes to people outside the home and paid help; see
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/coparenting-family-structure/parenting_solo_parent_load_and_support.md`.
- **Neurodivergent parent:** reduce decision load (fixed menus, visual routines) and
  sensory load (noise, clutter) before adding systems.
- **Low income:** drop and simplify carry more than paid help; check school meals,
  after-school programmes, 211 (US).
- **Cultural expectations of self-sacrifice:** name them; the plan protects the
  children by protecting the parent.

## Example Output

```
# Load Redesign — Rachel, with Ben (partner) and kids 8, 5, 2

## Burnout check (last month)
Exhaustion 3, distance 2 ("I'm going through the motions with the kids"),
fed up 3, contrast 2 → burnout pattern.

## Screen
Mood low on bad days but not most days; enjoys reading still; no thoughts
of harm; sleep 5–6 h. Not meeting depression threshold today — re-screen
at 2 weeks.

## Load inventory (weekly)
Rachel: 32 h paid work; 24 h childcare/household; ~6 h cognitive (meal
planning, school emails, birthday gifts, doctor bookings, activity
sign-ups). Total 62 h.
Ben: 40 h paid work; 12 h childcare/household; ~1 h cognitive. Total 53 h.

## Binding constraint
The 4–7 pm crunch: three pickups, two activities, dinner, toddler.

## Decisions
Drop: Monday swimming for the 5-year-old (re-start in spring).
Defer: redecorating the kids' room to January.
Delegate: Ben owns school emails and all medical appointments end to end;
Ben owns Tuesday and Thursday 4–7 pm. The 8-year-old packs her own bag.
Simplify: 7 rotating dinners; grocery delivery Sunday.

## Minimum viable week
Kids at school/nursery, fed (rotating meals, cereal dinner allowed), bath
alternate nights, one story each night, one outdoor hour at the weekend.

## Recovery
Saturday 9–12 off duty, out of the house. Ben takes the 05:30 toddler
wake-up on weekends.

## Asks
Ben: start Tuesday 7 Oct. Rachel's mum: Wednesday pick-up from 15 Oct.

## Re-check
20 Oct and 17 Nov. If ratings don't fall, or mood is low most days for
2 weeks → GP. Any thought of harm → 988.
```

## Techniques Used

- **DS-01 Framework Application** — the four features of parental burnout structure the self-check.
- **DP-09 Single Primary Constraint Identification** — the binding constraint (often the 4–7 pm crunch) is redesigned first.
- **NE-11 Embedded Calculation Formulas** — weekly load including cognitive hours, compared per adult.
- **DP-05 Stakes-Based Gate Policy** — depression and safety screens gate the plan; re-checks at 2 and 6 weeks.
