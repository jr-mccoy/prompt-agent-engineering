# Couple Strain Under Parenting Load

**Objective:** Produce a plan for two parents (married, partnered, or co-residing)
whose relationship is wearing down under parenting: a safety check first, a fair
division of whole tasks, an agreement for parenting disagreements, rules for conflict
that protect the children, a separated logistics meeting, and a clear point at which
couples therapy is the next step.

**When to Use:**
- Arguments are mostly about the kids: who does what, discipline, screens, bedtime,
  sleep training, grandparents.
- One parent feels they "do everything"; the other feels criticised whatever they do.
- Parents undermine each other in front of the children ("Dad said no, so ask Mum").
- Children have seen more arguing than the parents want.
- **Not this prompt if** there is **fear, threats, control of money or movement,
  monitoring, or violence** in the relationship — go to the Safety Block and use
  `domain-psychology/client-self-use/relational/clientself_is_this_relationship_healthy_check.md`;
  couple exercises are not safe there. A **therapist** planning couples work uses
  `domain-psychology/family-couples-systems/psychology_gottman_intervention_planner.md`.
  If one parent is depleted and the issue is mostly load, start with
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/parent-capacity/parenting_parent_overload_load_redesign.md`.
  If you have **separated**, use `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/co-parenting/`.

## Safety Block — Screen Before Any Couple Exercise

Ask each parent privately (not together):
- Are you ever afraid of your partner, or of what they will do if you disagree?
- Has your partner pushed, hit, choked, or threatened you or the children?
- Does your partner control money, your phone, where you go, or whom you see?
- Do you change what you say or do to avoid their reaction?

**Any "yes" → stop the couple plan (DP-02).** Joint "communication" work and
couples therapy can increase danger when there is abuse or coercive control.
Instead: National Domestic Violence Hotline 1-800-799-7233 (US), text START to
88788, or thehotline.org chat; a safety plan; individual counselling. If children
are at risk of harm, child protective services. Immediate danger → 911.
Also: thoughts of self-harm → 988 (US); heavy drinking or drug use by either
parent → GP or SAMHSA 1-800-662-4357. Outside the US, use local services.

## Inputs / Context

1. **Household:** children's ages and needs; each parent's work hours.
2. **What the fights are about** — top three recurring topics, with a recent example.
3. **Division of work now:** tasks and who owns the planning.
4. **Parenting philosophies:** where they differ (strictness, screens, food, sleep).
5. **What the children see and hear.**
6. **What each parent wants** the relationship to feel like again.

## Method

1. **Safety screen first (DP-02).** Above. Only proceed if both private screens are
   clear.
2. **Normalise without minimising.** Research following couples after a first baby
   (Shapiro, Gottman, and Carrère) found about two-thirds reported a drop in
   relationship satisfaction in the first three years. Strain is common; it still
   needs work.
3. **Audit whole tasks, not chores.** List recurring tasks; for each, who
   **notices** it needs doing, **plans** it, **does** it, and **follows up**. Assign
   whole tasks to one owner, including the planning. Compare total hours, including
   paid work and cognitive work, without scoring who is "better."
4. **Compare parenting positions without judgement (NE-16).** For each disagreement,
   each parent writes their rule and the reason, then the pair lays them side by
   side: both may be reasonable. Pick one of: one approach for now with a review
   date; a middle approach; or "the parent on duty decides" for low-stakes items.
5. **Map the interests behind positions (MP-10).** "No screens before 7" may be
   about sleep; "screens after dinner are fine" may be about a parent's only
   20 minutes of rest. Solve for both interests.
6. **Build a co-parenting alliance (DS-01).** Feinberg's co-parenting framework:
   agreement on child-rearing, division of labour, **support versus undermining**,
   and joint management of family interactions. Rules: no overruling in front of the
   children ("Let me talk with Dad and we'll tell you"); back each other on safety
   and agreed rules; disagree in private.
7. **Make conflict constructive.** Children exposed to frequent, hostile, or
   unresolved conflict show more anxiety and behaviour problems; children who see
   calm disagreement **and resolution** learn from it (Cummings' emotional security
   research). Rules: soft start-up ("I'm stressed about bedtime — can we plan
   tonight?"), no contempt or name-calling, a time-out word, and **repair in front
   of the children** when they saw the fight ("Dad and I argued loudly. We've
   sorted it out. It wasn't about you.").
8. **Separate logistics from the relationship.** A 20-minute weekly family logistics
   meeting (calendar, tasks, money) and a separate protected time for the couple,
   even 30 minutes, without logistics.
9. **Set the therapy threshold.** Couples therapy (Gottman, EFT, or a
   parenting-focused programme) when: the same fight recurs weekly for over a month
   despite the plan; contempt or stonewalling is routine; either parent talks about
   leaving; or one is depressed.

## Output Format

```
# Parenting Partnership Plan — [parents], [children, ages]

## Safety screen: both private screens clear (Y/N) — if N, stop here
## Top 3 recurring fights (with example)
## Whole-task ownership table (notice / plan / do / follow up; hours)
## Disagreements: positions, reasons, interests, our decision + review date
## Alliance rules (in front of the kids)
## Conflict rules and repair script
## Weekly logistics meeting (when, agenda) + couple time (when)
## When we book couples therapy
```

## Verification

- [ ] Safety screen was done privately with each parent before any plan.
- [ ] Any abuse indicator stops the couple plan and routes to safety resources.
- [ ] Tasks are owned whole, including noticing and planning.
- [ ] Each disagreement shows both positions, reasons, and a decision with a review date.
- [ ] Repair in front of children is scripted.
- [ ] Logistics and couple time are separate.

## False-Positive Prevention

| Misfire | What it looks like | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Couples exercises over abuse | "Use I-statements" to a parent who is afraid | Screen first; stop and route to DV support. |
| 50/50 by task count | "He does 10 tasks, she does 10" | Count hours and planning; own whole tasks. |
| Picking a winner | Deciding which parent's rules are right | Compare reasons; solve for both interests. |
| Shielding children from all conflict | Never disagreeing in front of them | Calm disagreement plus repair teaches them. |
| "Helping" framing | "I help with the kids" | Co-owner, not helper. |
| Therapy as last resort | Waiting until one wants to leave | Use the threshold earlier. |

## Adaptations

- **Blended family:** step-parent's authority is a separate negotiation; see
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/co-parenting/parenting_coparenting_blended_family_coordination.md`.
- **Same-sex couple:** less gendered default division but the same notice/plan
  imbalance can develop; audit the same way.
- **Shift work or military deployment:** a hand-over brief at each change of
  parent-on-duty; keep rules stable across absences.
- **Child with high needs:** count care coordination; ask about respite so the
  couple gets time.
- **Cultural or extended-family roles:** grandparents' involvement is a decision for
  both parents together.

## Example Output

```
# Parenting Partnership Plan — Sam and Jordan, kids 6 and 3

## Safety screen
Both answered privately: no fear, threats, control, or violence. Proceed.

## Top 3 fights
1. Bedtime: Jordan lets the 6-year-old stay up for "one more show."
2. "I do everything" (Sam) vs "Nothing I do is right" (Jordan).
3. Overruling: Sam said no to sweets; Jordan said yes in front of the kids.

## Whole-task ownership (weekly hours)
Sam: school comms, meals (plan + shop + cook), doctor/dentist,
birthday gifts → 14 h + ~4 h planning.
Jordan: bath + bedtime Mon–Thu, laundry, bins, car → 8 h + ~0.5 h.
Rebalanced: Jordan now owns all meals Tue/Thu/Sat (plan, shop, cook),
and doctor/dentist end to end (≈ 4 h + 1 h planning moved).
New: Sam ~10 h + ~3 h planning; Jordan ~12 h + ~1.5 h planning.

## Disagreements
Bedtime: Sam (lights out 7:30 — sleep), Jordan (one show after bath —
it's his only time with the kids after work). Interests: sleep and
connection. Decision: Jordan's 15-minute story-and-chat 7:15–7:30
replaces the show. Review in 3 weeks.
Sweets: the parent on duty decides; no overruling.

## Alliance rules
"Let me check with Mum/Dad and we'll tell you." Disagree after bedtime.

## Conflict and repair
Time-out word "pause"; 20 minutes, then back. Repair after the 3 Oct
argument the kids heard: "We argued too loudly about bedtime. We've
sorted it out. It wasn't your fault."

## Meetings
Sunday 19:30 logistics (20 min: calendar, tasks, money). Wednesday 20:00
couple time: walk or tea, no logistics.

## Couples therapy if
Bedtime or load fight still weekly by 1 Nov, contempt creeps in, or either
of us talks about leaving.
```

## Techniques Used

- **DP-02 Refuse Path Protocol** — any abuse or coercive-control indicator stops the couple plan and routes to safety resources.
- **NE-16 Non-Judgmental Comparison** — both parents' rules and reasons laid side by side without a winner.
- **MP-10 Position Mapping** — positions traced to interests (sleep, connection, rest) and solved for both.
- **DS-01 Framework Application** — Feinberg's co-parenting alliance and Cummings' constructive-conflict findings structure the rules.
