# Picky Eating Mealtime Plan

**Objective:** First screen out the feeding and growth problems that need a doctor;
then, for ordinary selective eating, produce a four-week mealtime plan that removes
pressure, sets structure, and widens the child's accepted foods through repeated
exposure — with scripts for the moments that usually turn into battles.

**When to Use:**
- A child aged 2–12 eats a narrow range of foods, refuses new foods, or meals have
  become a nightly fight.
- Caregivers disagree about bribes, "one more bite," or separate meals.
- A pediatrician has said growth is fine and the family wants a home plan.
- **Not this prompt if** the child is under 12 months or the issue is breast/bottle
  feeding — use `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/ages-0-3/parenting_infant_feeding_troubleshooter.md`;
  for first foods and allergen introduction use
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/ages-0-3/parenting_starting_solids_planner.md`.
  If a teen (or a child of any age) is restricting because of **weight or shape
  concerns**, purging, or over-exercising, use
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/ages-13-18/parenting_teen_eating_disorder_signal_response.md`
  — that is an eating-disorder pathway, not picky eating.

## Safety Block — Escalation Screen (Run Before Any Plan)

**See the pediatrician before starting a home plan if any of these are present:**

- **Growth faltering:** weight loss; dropping across two or more major percentile
  lines on the growth chart; height growth slowing; or not regaining after illness.
- **Very narrow range:** roughly fewer than 20 accepted foods, or whole food groups
  or textures dropped and the list shrinking (foods are lost and not replaced).
- **Fear-driven refusal:** refusing to eat after a choking, gagging, or vomiting
  episode; panic or extreme distress at new foods.
- **Oral-motor or swallowing signs:** gagging or vomiting at most meals, coughing or
  wet voice when eating or drinking (aspiration risk), cannot chew age-typical
  textures, meals routinely longer than 30–40 minutes, drooling beyond toddlerhood.
- **Medical signs:** reflux, abdominal pain, constipation, diarrhoea, blood in stool,
  rash or swelling with foods (allergy), fatigue or pallor (iron deficiency),
  dependence on formula, supplements, or a feeding tube.
- **Eating-disorder thinking at any age:** talk of being fat, calorie counting, fear
  of weight gain, hiding food, vomiting after meals → eating-disorder pathway.

These can indicate **ARFID** (avoidant/restrictive food intake disorder — restriction
driven by sensory sensitivity, fear of aversive consequences, or low interest, with
weight, nutrition, or functional impact), a **paediatric feeding disorder**, or a
medical cause. The usual route is pediatrician → feeding team (speech-language
pathologist or occupational therapist trained in feeding, paediatric dietitian, GI as
needed; ARFID-trained psychologist for fear-based patterns). Fainting, dehydration,
or refusing all food or fluid for a day → urgent care or ED.

## Inputs / Context

1. **Child:** age, growth history (ask for the chart), medical history, neurodivergence.
2. **Accepted foods list** — every food eaten reliably, by group and texture.
3. **Current routine:** meal and snack times, grazing, milk/juice volume, screens
   at meals, who eats together.
4. **Current tactics:** bribes, rewards, dessert rules, "two more bites," separate
   meals, punishment.
5. **Family:** caregivers' own eating, cultural foods, budget, time.

## Method

1. **Run the escalation screen (DP-05).** Any item → pediatrician first; the home
   plan may continue only alongside medical guidance.
2. **Normalise what is developmental (DS-01).** Food neophobia peaks around 2–6;
   toddler appetite drops after the first-year growth spurt; children may need 8–15
   neutral exposures before accepting a food.
3. **Install the Division of Responsibility.** Parent decides *what* is served,
   *when*, and *where*; child decides *whether* to eat and *how much*. Both halves
   matter: no pressure and no short-order cooking.
4. **Set the schedule.** Three meals and two to three planned snacks, about every
   2.5–3 hours; water between; milk ≤ 500 mL/day for most children over 2 (excess
   milk and juice blunt appetite); no grazing.
5. **Build every meal the same way.** One or two "safe" foods the child reliably
   eats, plus family foods, served family-style so the child serves themselves.
   Small portions; seconds allowed.
6. **Remove pressure (DP-04).** No bribes, praise for eating, "one more bite,"
   or dessert as reward. If dessert is served, a small portion with the meal on some
   nights, not earned.
7. **Widen with exposure and chaining.** Repeated neutral exposure; food chaining
   from accepted foods to similar ones (plain pasta → pasta with butter → with a
   little parmesan); involve the child in shopping, washing, cooking, growing.
8. **Troubleshoot by symptom (IT-23):** "Only eats snacks" → tighten schedule and
   milk. "Fills up on bread" → serve it alongside, not first. "Throws food" → end the
   meal calmly. "Won't come to the table" → table is still the place; brief sit.
9. **Validate the child's experience (NE-07).** "That's new. You don't have to eat
   it. You can look at it or smell it."
10. **Review at four weeks:** accepted-food count, meal duration, conflict level.

## Output Format

```
# Mealtime Plan — [child, age]

## Escalation screen (each item: present / absent) → outcome
## Accepted foods (count, by group and texture)
## Division of Responsibility (who decides what, in this family)
## Schedule (meal/snack times, milk/juice limits)
## Plate template (safe food + family food)
## Pressure we are stopping (and what replaces it)
## Exposure and food-chaining targets (4 weeks)
## Symptom-based troubleshooting
## Four-week review measures
```

## Verification

- [ ] Escalation screen completed; any positive routes to the pediatrician.
- [ ] Both halves of the Division of Responsibility are present.
- [ ] Every meal includes a safe food.
- [ ] Milk/juice and grazing addressed.
- [ ] All pressure tactics named and replaced.
- [ ] Four-week measures are countable.

## False-Positive Prevention

| Misfire | What it looks like | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Calling ARFID "picky eating" | 12 foods, falling percentiles, home plan only | Screen first; refer. |
| Calling normal neophobia a disorder | Referral for a 3-year-old who refuses vegetables | Neophobia peaks 2–6; growth decides. |
| Pressure disguised as encouragement | "Just one bite for Mummy" | Any pressure increases refusal. |
| Short-order cook | Separate chicken nuggets nightly | Safe food on the shared menu instead. |
| Dessert as reward | "No dessert unless you finish" | Teaches dessert is prized, vegetables are a price. |
| Applying an ED frame to sensory refusal | Talking about weight with an autistic 7-year-old | ARFID is not body-image driven. |

## Adaptations

- **Autistic / sensory-sensitive child:** expect slower chaining; predictable
  presentation (same plate, foods not touching); exposure can start with touching or
  smelling; feeding therapist familiar with ND-affirming approaches.
- **ADHD medication:** appetite suppression at lunch; shift calories to breakfast and
  evening; discuss with prescriber.
- **Two homes:** share the plan and safe-food list; consistency matters more than
  the exact menu.
- **Cultural and budget context:** the family's own cuisine is the target; safe
  foods can be inexpensive staples.

## Example Output

```
# Mealtime Plan — Theo, 4

## Escalation screen
Growth: following 25th percentile since age 2 (chart reviewed) — absent.
Accepted foods: 31 — absent. No gagging, coughing, GI symptoms, or fear
episodes — absent. → Home plan; recheck at next well visit.

## Accepted foods (31)
Breads/grains 9, dairy 6, fruit 7, protein 5 (chicken nuggets, eggs scrambled,
peanut butter, ham, fish fingers), vegetables 2 (cucumber, corn), other 2.

## Division of Responsibility
Parents: menu, times, table. Theo: whether and how much.

## Schedule
7:00 breakfast · 9:30 snack · 12:00 lunch · 3:00 snack · 5:45 dinner.
Milk 400 mL/day in a cup with meals; water between; juice stops.

## Plate template
Every dinner: one safe food (bread, cucumber, or rice) + family meal,
self-served from bowls.

## Pressure stopping → replacement
"Two more bites" → "You decide how much." Dessert-for-dinner deal → small
cookie with dinner on Fridays and Sundays, no conditions.

## Exposure / chaining
Fish fingers → baked breaded fish → plain baked fish.
Corn → peas mixed with corn → peas.
Theo washes the vegetables and stirs the pot twice a week.

## Troubleshooting
Asks for snacks at 5:00 → "Dinner is soon; kitchen's closed."
Leaves the table at 2 minutes → "Okay, dinner's done for you." No food until
the evening snack.

## Four-week review
Accepted foods (target 33+), dinner conflict nights per week (target ≤1),
meal length 15–25 min.
```

## Techniques Used

- **DS-01 Framework Application** — Satter's Division of Responsibility and developmental neophobia.
- **DP-05 Stakes-Based Gate Policy** — escalation screen gates the home plan.
- **IT-23 Symptom-Based Troubleshooting Organization** — fixes indexed by what the parent sees.
- **NE-07 Emotional Validation First** — the child's reluctance is named, not overridden.
- **DP-04 Must-Not Constraints** — no bribes, pressure, earned dessert, or short-order cooking.
