# Health, Body, Sleep & Feeding Prompts (Caregiver-Facing)

Everyday child-health problems that families manage at home — selective eating, bedtime battles, bedwetting, and getting ready for a medical procedure — each built **pediatrician-first**: the prompt screens for the signs that need a doctor before it produces any home plan, and the home plan never replaces medical care.

## Scope

**In scope:**
- Picky / selective eating (2–12) with a Division-of-Responsibility mealtime plan.
- Bedtime resistance in school-age children (4–12), matched to its driver.
- Night-time wetting (5+) with a shame-free routine and treatment options to discuss with the doctor.
- Preparing a child for vaccines, blood draws, MRI, anaesthesia, surgery, or admission (child-life approach).

## Safety notes

Each prompt opens with an escalation screen. Key routes: picky eating with growth faltering, a very narrow or shrinking food list, fear-driven refusal, gagging/coughing at meals, or any weight/shape talk → pediatrician (ARFID, paediatric feeding disorder, or eating-disorder pathway). Bedtime problems with regular snoring, gasping, or daytime sleepiness → pediatrician (possible obstructive sleep apnoea); melatonin only after discussion with the pediatrician. Bedwetting with daytime symptoms, new onset after six dry months, pain, thirst, or constipation → pediatrician first; **never punish wetting**. Procedures: follow the hospital's own fasting instructions exactly. Any talk of self-harm → 988 (US); emergencies → 911; outside the US, use local equivalents.
