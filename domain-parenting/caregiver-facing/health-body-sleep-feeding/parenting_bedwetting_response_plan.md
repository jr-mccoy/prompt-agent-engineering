# Bedwetting Response Plan

**Objective:** Route the child to the pediatrician when the pattern needs medical
eyes, and otherwise produce a shame-free home plan: what the family says, the daytime
and evening routine, the treatment options to discuss with the doctor, the logistics
that protect sleep and dignity, and a plan for nights away.

**When to Use:**
- A child aged 5 or older still wets the bed at night.
- A family is ready to act on bedwetting (often because the child is bothered, or
  sleepovers and camp are coming).
- Caregivers disagree — one wants to wait, one wants rewards or consequences.
- **Not this prompt if** the child has never been toilet trained during the day or
  is under 5 — use
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/ages-0-3/parenting_toilet_training_readiness_and_plan.md`;
  night dryness lags day dryness and wetting under 5 is not considered enuresis. If
  the night issue is fighting bedtime rather than wetting, use
  `parenting_bedtime_resistance_plan_4_12.md`.

## Safety Block — See the Pediatrician First If

- **Daytime wetting**, urgency, holding manoeuvres (squatting, crossing legs), or
  frequent voiding — suggests bladder dysfunction, not simple bedwetting.
- **New wetting after at least six months dry** (secondary enuresis) — check for
  urinary infection, constipation, diabetes, sleep apnoea, and stressors.
- **Pain or burning, blood, cloudy or foul urine, fever** → same-day visit (UTI).
- **Excessive thirst, frequent large voids, weight loss, tiredness** → same-day
  (possible type 1 diabetes).
- **Constipation or soiling** (hard stools, fewer than 3 a week, stool accidents).
- **Weak or interrupted stream, straining, constant dribbling.**
- **Snoring, pauses in breathing** (sleep apnoea is linked to bedwetting).
- **Neurological signs:** back abnormalities, leg weakness, gait changes.
- **Possible abuse or major stress:** new wetting with fearfulness, sexualised
  behaviour, or disclosure → see `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/safety-risk/parenting_body_safety_consent_lessons.md`;
  Childhelp 1-800-422-4453 (US).
- **Never punish wetting.** Bedwetting is a recognised trigger for physical abuse;
  if an adult in the home is angry or rough about it, the child needs protection and
  the adult needs support.

## Inputs / Context

1. **Child:** age, ever dry for six months?, wet nights per week, daytime symptoms,
   stool pattern, snoring, how the child feels about it.
2. **Family history** (one parent with a history roughly doubles-to-triples the
   likelihood; both parents higher still).
3. **Fluid pattern:** what and when the child drinks, especially after 5 p.m.;
   caffeine, fizzy drinks.
4. **Sleep:** bedtime, deep sleeper?, shared room.
5. **What's been tried:** waking at night, limiting fluids, rewards, consequences,
   pull-ups.
6. **Upcoming events:** sleepovers, camp, school trips.

## Method

1. **Run the pediatrician-first screen (DP-05).** Any item → visit before a home
   plan; many causes (constipation, UTI) are quick fixes.
2. **Reframe for the whole family (DS-01).** Night-time wetting at 5+ is common
   (about 15% of 5-year-olds, 5% of 10-year-olds, 1–2% of teens) and mostly resolves
   with time (around 15% per year spontaneously). Causes are mainly physiological: a
   bladder that is small at night, high night-time urine production, and difficulty
   waking to bladder signals. It is not laziness or defiance.
3. **Say it to the child (NE-07).** "Lots of kids your age wet the bed. Your body
   hasn't learned to wake you up yet. It's not your fault and you're not in trouble.
   We'll work on it as a team."
4. **Daytime habits (ST-02).** Drink most fluids before mid-afternoon (front-loading,
   not restriction); void 4–7 times a day, including before leaving school; treat
   constipation (it is a common, overlooked cause); avoid caffeine and fizzy drinks
   in the evening.
5. **Evening routine.** Moderate drinks after dinner; void at the start of the
   bedtime routine and again just before lights-out ("double void").
6. **Protect sleep and dignity.** Waterproof mattress cover plus a layered bed (sheet,
   protector, sheet) so night changes take a minute; a change kit by the bed;
   the child helps strip the bed as routine, not punishment; absorbent pants are fine
   when the family is not actively treating.
7. **Discuss treatment with the pediatrician (QA-04).** Options vary by child and
   family readiness:
   - **Enuresis alarm:** first-line for motivated families; success in about
     two-thirds; needs 6–16 weeks of nightly use and a parent who will wake the child
     at first; lower relapse after stopping.
   - **Desmopressin:** prescription; reduces night urine; fast; useful for
     sleepovers and camp; requires limiting fluids from one hour before until morning
     (risk of low sodium otherwise); relapse common when stopped.
   - Combination or specialist referral if neither works.
8. **Reward effort, not dry nights.** Dry nights are not in the child's control;
   reward drinking pattern, double void, helping change the bed.
9. **Plan nights away.** Absorbent pants in a sleeping bag liner; a discreet
   change plan with one trusted adult; desmopressin trial before camp if the doctor
   agrees.

## Output Format

```
# Bedwetting Plan — [child, age]

## Pediatrician-first screen (each item: present / absent) → outcome
## Pattern (wet nights/week, primary or secondary, family history)
## What we say (to the child, to siblings)
## Daytime habits
## Evening routine
## Bed set-up and change kit
## Treatment options to discuss with the doctor
## What we reward
## Nights-away plan
## Review date
```

## Verification

- [ ] Screen completed; daytime symptoms, secondary onset, pain, thirst, constipation, snoring asked.
- [ ] Child-facing language is blame-free; siblings told teasing isn't allowed.
- [ ] Fluids front-loaded, not severely restricted.
- [ ] Constipation addressed.
- [ ] Treatment options framed as decisions with the pediatrician.
- [ ] Rewards target controllable behaviours.

## False-Positive Prevention

| Misfire | What it looks like | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Treating it as behaviour | Consequences for wet beds | It's involuntary; punishment worsens it and endangers the child. |
| Missing daytime symptoms | Alarm for a child with urgency | Daytime signs → pediatrician first. |
| Missing constipation | Ignoring hard, infrequent stools | Treat constipation; wetting often improves. |
| Heavy fluid restriction | No drinks after 3 p.m. | Front-load; moderate evening drinks. |
| Rewarding dry nights | Sticker only when dry | Reward effort the child controls. |
| Panicking at age 5 | Specialist push for a 5-year-old untroubled child | Waiting is a valid option. |

## Adaptations

- **ADHD / autism:** bedwetting is more common; visual routine; alarm may need
  adjustment for sensory sensitivity.
- **Deep sleeper:** parent-assisted waking to the alarm for the first weeks.
- **Shared room / siblings:** privacy plan; quiet change kit; no-teasing rule.
- **Two homes:** same cover, routine, and alarm protocol at both.
- **Teen:** route to the doctor regardless; prioritise privacy and autonomy.

## Example Output

```
# Bedwetting Plan — Ben, 8

## Screen
Daytime wetting: none. Ever dry 6 months: no (primary). Pain, thirst,
weight loss: none. Stools: hard, every 3 days → constipation present.
Snoring: no. → Pediatrician visit booked for constipation; home plan alongside.

## Pattern
Wet 5 nights/week. Dad wet the bed until 10.

## What we say
"Dad did this too when he was a kid. Your body hasn't learned the night
signal yet. Not your fault — we're a team." Sister (11) told: no teasing,
no telling friends.

## Daytime habits
Water bottle at school, finish by 2 p.m.; toilet before leaving school.
Constipation plan per doctor (fibre, fluids, toilet sit after dinner).

## Evening routine
One cup of water with dinner; toilet at 7:45 and again at 8:15 lights-out.

## Bed set-up
Sheet / waterproof protector / sheet. Change kit: pyjamas, wipes, bag.
Ben strips the top layer; parent helps.

## Treatment options to discuss
Alarm after constipation is treated (Ben wants to try; Dad will wake him for
the first 3 weeks). Desmopressin for Scout camp in July, if doctor agrees.

## Rewards
Sticker for drinking pattern + double void + helping with bed. Not for dry
nights.

## Nights away
Sleepovers: absorbent pants in sleeping bag liner; Ben changes in the
bathroom; host parent knows quietly.

## Review
6 weeks after constipation treatment starts.
```

## Techniques Used

- **DP-05 Stakes-Based Gate Policy** — the pediatrician-first screen gates the home plan.
- **NE-07 Emotional Validation First** — blame-free language to the child before any intervention.
- **DS-01 Framework Application** — primary vs. secondary, monosymptomatic vs. non-monosymptomatic enuresis.
- **ST-02 Structured Sequential Instructions** — daytime, evening, and night routines.
- **QA-04 Uncertainty Acknowledgment** — treatment choice is framed as a decision with the doctor.
