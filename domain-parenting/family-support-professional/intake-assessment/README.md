# Intake & Assessment Prompts (Family-Support Professional)

The worker's side of a family's first weeks with a program — for intake coordinators, home visitors, parent educators, family support workers, and program managers in voluntary family-support, home-visiting, and parent-education programs. Each one opens with consent and the limits of confidentiality, maps strengths before needs, and routes safeguarding and clinical concerns out of the routine workflow the same day. **No prompt here diagnoses** a parent or child: workers record observable behaviour, the family's own words, and frequency and duration, and route to licensed clinicians for assessment.

## Scope

**In scope:**
- Program-side referral triage: safety read first, eligibility, anchored urgency tiers, program fit vs better-fit services, warm handoffs, and a waitlist with scheduled safety check-ins.
- First intake in a voluntary program: consent and confidentiality limits, a protective-factors strengths map, concrete needs, safety questions asked of each adult alone, and a summary led by the family's priorities.
- Parent-completed developmental and social-emotional screening (ASQ-3, ASQ:SE-2 type): set-up, scoring against published cutoffs, on-track / monitor / refer conversations, and Part C, Child Find, or pediatrician referral with consent.

## Safety notes

Reporting duties vary — verify locally. Who must report, the threshold (reasonable suspicion, not proof), deadlines, and whether a supervisor consult counts differ by state, province, country, and agency; in most US states the duty is personal and is not discharged by telling a supervisor, and consulting must never delay a report. Immediate danger → **911** or the local emergency number first. Abuse concerns → the state or county child-protection hotline or law enforcement (**Childhelp 1-800-422-4453** can help find the number). Suicide talk → **988** (US). Partner violence → **National DV Hotline 1-800-799-7233**. Possible ingestion → **Poison Control 1-800-222-1222**. Outside the US: findahelpline.com. Consent comes before questions: explain the limits of confidentiality in plain words, get written releases before sharing with other agencies (except where law requires or permits otherwise), share the minimum, and de-identify anything pasted into an AI tool. A waitlist is never a safety plan.
