# Family Strengths and Needs Intake

**Objective:** Plan and write up the first intake with a family entering a voluntary
program. The family hears what stays private, and what cannot, before sharing anything.
The worker leaves with a strengths map, concrete needs, privately asked safety answers,
and a summary that opens with the family's own priorities.

**When to Use:**
- First meeting with a family in a home-visiting, parent-education, or family-resource
  program, or re-intake after a long gap.
- **Not this prompt if** you are a licensed clinician taking a child's clinical history
  with a caregiver — use
  `domain-psychology/intake-assessment/psychology_pediatric_intake_with_caregiver.md`.
  Responding to a positive social-needs screen in a clinic →
  `domain-healthcare-clinical/prompts/allied-health/allied_health_sdoh_screening_response.md`.
  A new parent checking their own capacity →
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/ages-0-3/parenting_postpartum_parent_capacity_check.md`.
  Developmental screening at intake →
  `domain-parenting/family-support-professional/intake-assessment/parenting_developmental_screen_results_talk.md`.
  Deciding whether to accept a referral at all →
  `domain-parenting/family-support-professional/intake-assessment/parenting_program_referral_triage_waitlist.md`.

## Safeguarding, Consent, and Scope

- **Consent comes first.** Before any questions, explain in plain words: the program is
  voluntary; they can skip any question; what you write down and who can see it; and the
  **limits of confidentiality** — "If you tell me a child is being hurt or is in danger,
  or someone is about to hurt themselves or someone else, or a court orders our records,
  I can't keep that private." Check
  understanding with teach-back. Get a written release before sharing with another
  agency, except where law requires or permits sharing without consent, such as a
  child-protection report (verify locally). Share the minimum necessary. Families may
  read their records; write as if they will. FERPA, HIPAA, 42 CFR Part 2, or GDPR-type
  rules may apply — verify locally.
- **This intake does not diagnose.** Record behaviour, the family's words, frequency,
  and duration — never a label. Not "mum seems depressed" but "said
  she has slept less than 3 hours a night for two weeks and has stopped going out."
- **Mandated reporting — verify locally.** Who must report, the threshold (reasonable
  suspicion or reasonable cause to believe, not proof), the deadline, and whether a
  supervisor consult counts all vary by state, province, country, and agency. In most US
  states the duty is personal and is not discharged by telling a supervisor. Consult your
  supervisor, but never let that delay a report or an emergency call. Report to the
  state or county child-protection hotline or law enforcement; Childhelp 1-800-422-4453
  can help find the right number. Do not investigate, interrogate, photograph injuries,
  or promise secrecy. Write the exact words, what you saw, when, and who you told. Decide
  with your supervisor, case by case, whether telling the family about a report is safe —
  never when it could endanger the child or anyone else.
- **Immediate danger** → 911 or the local emergency number first.
- **Same-day routes:** talk of suicide or self-harm → 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (US,
  call or text) or emergency services; partner violence → National DV Hotline
  1-800-799-7233 and a safety plan with a DV advocate; possible ingestion → Poison
  Control 1-800-222-1222. Outside the US: local emergency number and findahelpline.com.
- **Route to a licensed clinician** (with consent, same week) when a parent describes
  low mood, hopelessness, or panic most days for two weeks or more, or heavy alcohol or
  drug use; same day if a new parent cannot sleep when the baby sleeps or has
  frightening thoughts about the baby. Feeding, weight, or development worries →
  pediatrician. You support, refer, and follow up. You do not treat.

## Inputs / Context

1. **De-identify first** unless your agency approved the AI tool for client data:
   initials or roles ("mother, 24"), no names, addresses, dates of birth, or case numbers.
2. **Referral:** who referred, why, and what the family was told about the program.
3. **Household:** adults and children (ages only), languages, who joins the intake.
4. **Program:** what it offers, required forms or screens, time available.
5. **Setting and language:** home, office, or video; a private space; a professional
   interpreter if needed — never a child or relative.

## Method

1. **Open with consent and the limits of confidentiality (DP-04).** Say it before the
   first question. Must-nots: no questions on immigration status unless a funder
   requires it; no partner-violence questions with the partner, a verbal child, or a
   family interpreter present; no promised secrecy; no diagnoses or guesses; no
   requirement to answer every question.
2. **Ask what they want first.** "If this program helped, what would be different in
   three months?" Write the answer in their words. This leads the summary.
3. **Map strengths with the protective factors (DS-01).** Use the Strengthening Families
   five protective factors: parental resilience, social connections, knowledge of
   parenting and child development, concrete support in times of need, and the
   children's social and emotional competence. Ask one open question for each ("Who do
   you call when things get hard?") and note a real example, not a compliment.
4. **Review concrete needs across domains (RT-02).** Housing, food, utilities, income,
   transport, childcare, health care, phone, baby supplies, legal worries, school. For
   each: current state, urgency (today / this month / later), and whether the family
   wants help. Do not push help they decline.
5. **Ask safety questions of each adult alone.** Use your program's tool, or: "Because I ask everyone: Does anyone at home hurt you, threaten you, or
   make you feel afraid?" and "Has anyone forced you to do things you didn't want to?"
   If you cannot get privacy, do not say why; ask at the next visit. Also ask about
   each adult's own mood and stress, and where guns and medicines are stored.
6. **Gate what you heard (DP-05).** Sort every concern into a lane.
   **Emergency now** (danger, suicide talk, a child hurt or alone) → 911 or 988, then
   supervisor. **Report or same day** (reasonable suspicion of abuse or neglect, partner
   violence, urgent clinical sign) → report per local law, DV advocate, or same-day
   clinician; supervisor today. **Support plan** → the goals conversation.
7. **Close.** Read back priorities and next steps. Ask: "Anything I wrote that you'd put
   differently?" Give crisis numbers in writing.
8. **Write the intake summary (NE-20).** One page a covering worker could act on
   without calling you: priorities in quotes, strengths, needs with urgency, safety
   outcomes (facts only), referrals with consent status, next visit.

## Output Format

```
# Intake Summary — [family initials]   Date: [..]   Worker: [role]   Setting: [..]

## Consent
Voluntary explained: [Y]  Limits of confidentiality explained: [Y, said back]
Releases signed: [agency, scope, expiry]  Interpreter: [language / none / declined]

## What the family wants (their words)
1. "..."

## Strengths (protective factors)
| Factor | Example the family gave |

## Concrete needs
| Domain | Current state (facts) | Urgency | Family wants help? | Action |

## Safety questions (asked privately)
Adult 1: [asked alone Y/N] [answer, facts]   Adult 2: [..]
Lane outcomes: emergency / report / support — [facts, actions, times, who was told]

## Referrals (consent status)
## Clinical routing (observations only)
## Next visit and who does what
```

## Verification

- [ ] Consent and the limits of confidentiality appear before the first disclosure.
- [ ] The summary opens with the family's priorities in quotation marks.
- [ ] Each protective factor has a concrete example or is marked "not discussed."
- [ ] Every need has an urgency and a "family wants help?" answer.
- [ ] Safety questions are marked as asked alone, or deferred with a reason.
- [ ] No diagnostic labels; every concern is written as behaviour, words, and frequency.
- [ ] No referral is listed as sent without a signed release or a stated legal basis.

## False-Positive Prevention

| Misfire | What it looks like | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Labelling instead of describing | "Dad has anger issues"; "mum is bipolar" | Write what was said or seen, how often, how long. |
| Needs list as a deficit list | Ten problems, no strengths | Lead with what the family wants and what works. |
| Poverty read as neglect | Empty fridge flagged as a report | A need, not a report by itself; neglect thresholds are local — ask your supervisor. |
| Safety question with partner present | "Do you feel safe?" asked to both | Ask each adult alone or defer. |
| Worker's priorities lead | Summary opens with "parenting skills"; family asked for housing | Family priorities first; worker concerns separately. |
| Cultural difference read as risk | Co-sleeping or grandparent authority flagged as a concern | Ask; record safety facts only where safety or law sets a line. |

## Adaptations

- **Prenatal or newborn family:** add feeding, safe sleep, and birth recovery.
- **Parent who is a minor:** fewer questions per visit; consent rules may differ — verify
  locally.
- **Parent with intellectual disability or low literacy:** one question at a time, a
  picture consent sheet, teach-back.
- **Neurodivergent parent:** offer the questions in writing in advance; allow breaks.
- **Tribal or Indigenous family:** offer tribal family services; in the US, ICWA may
  apply if child protection becomes involved.
- **Video intake:** confirm the adult is alone before safety questions; agree a code
  phrase to end the call.
- **LGBTQ+ or military family:** use the family's own terms for roles; ask about moves.

## Techniques Used

- **DS-01 Framework Application** — the Strengthening Families five protective factors structure the strengths map.
- **RT-02 Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework** — needs reviewed domain by domain, each with urgency and the family's wish for help.
- **DP-04 Must-Not Constraints** — no diagnoses, no promised secrecy, no immigration-status questions, no safety questions with others present.
- **DP-05 Stakes-Based Gate Policy** — every concern is sorted into emergency, report or same-day action, or support-plan lanes.
- **NE-20 Third-Party Handoff Package** — a one-page summary a covering worker can act on.
