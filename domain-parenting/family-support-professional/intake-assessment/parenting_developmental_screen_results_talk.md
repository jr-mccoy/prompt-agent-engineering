# Developmental Screen Results Talk

**Objective:** Give a parent-completed developmental or social-emotional screener
properly and talk through the results. The parent learns that a screen is a quick
check, not a diagnosis. Each area lands in a plain band — on track, keep watching, or
worth a closer look — and the talk ends with a dated next step the parent chose.

**When to Use:**
- Your program uses an ASQ-3, ASQ:SE-2, or similar screener and you need to prepare,
  score, or explain one.
- A score falls in a monitor or refer zone and you need words for the parent.
- A parent is worried even though the scores look fine, or calm when scores are low.
- **Not this prompt if** a parent is checking milestones themselves — use
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/ages-0-3/parenting_developmental_red_flags_0_3.md`.
  A clinician interpreting a mental-health screening battery →

## Safeguarding, Consent, and Scope

- **Screening is not diagnosis.** A screen tells you whether a closer look is worth
  doing. Never say "delay," "autism," "ADHD," or any label. Say "this area is below the
  line where we'd want a specialist to take a closer look." Record observed behaviour and
  the parent's words ("does not yet point to show me things"), not conclusions.
- **Consent.** Before you start, explain why you screen, who sees results, and the
  limits of confidentiality: "What you tell me stays in your file, except if I
  learn a child is being hurt or is in danger, or a court orders it." Get a written
  release before sending results to early intervention, a school district, or a doctor,
  except where law permits or requires sharing without consent (verify locally). Share
  the minimum. The parent may read the record. HIPAA, FERPA, or GDPR-type rules may
  apply — verify locally.
- **Use licensed materials.** ASQ-type screeners are copyrighted. Use purchased forms,
  published cutoffs, and published translations — never translate on the fly.
- **Same-day clinical routes, regardless of score:** loss of skills the child had (words,
  walking, eye contact), seizures or staring spells, a baby who is floppy, very stiff, or
  feeding poorly, no response to sound, or a parent describing a fall or injury →
  pediatrician the same day; breathing trouble or unresponsiveness → 911. Possible
  ingestion → Poison Control 1-800-222-1222.
- **Mandated reporting — verify locally.** If the screen conversation reveals possible
  abuse or neglect (an unexplained injury, a child not being fed), your duty, threshold
  (reasonable suspicion, not proof), and timelines depend on your state, province,
  country, and agency. In most US states the duty is personal and is not discharged by
  telling a supervisor; consulting must never delay a report or emergency call. Report to
  the child-protection hotline or law enforcement (Childhelp 1-800-422-4453 can help find
  the number). Do not investigate, photograph, or promise secrecy; record exact words,
  what you saw, when, and who you told. Decide with your supervisor whether telling the
  family is safe — never when it could endanger anyone. **Immediate danger → 911 first.**
- **Parent crisis:** suicide talk → 988 (US); partner violence → National DV Hotline
  1-800-799-7233; outside the US, local emergency number and findahelpline.com. You
  support, refer, and follow up. You do not assess or treat.

## Inputs / Context

1. **De-identify.** Child's age in months and initials only; no names, dates of birth, or
   case numbers unless your agency approved the tool.
2. **Child's age** on the screening date, and weeks born early if premature.
3. **Screener and interval** used, and the scoring sheet's cutoffs for that interval.
4. **Area totals** and the parent's answers to the overall questions.
5. **Context:** home languages, hearing checks, recent illness or move, prior screens,
   services in place.
6. **Local referral routes:** Part C early intervention intake (birth to 3), school
   district Child Find (3 and up), pediatrician, audiology.

## Method

1. **Set it up so the parent is the expert (DP-04).** Pick the interval from the child's
   age, adjusted for prematurity where the screener's manual says to (for ASQ-3, under 24
   months for babies born 3 or more weeks early). The parent answers; you can try items
   together. Must-nots: no scoring from your own impression, no coaching
   answers, no screening in an illness or crisis week, no child or relative as
   interpreter, no label in any result.
2. **Score against the published cutoffs (DS-01).** Use the screener's own zones. For
   ASQ-3, each area total is compared with that interval's cutoff: above (on schedule),
   close to the cutoff (monitor), below the cutoff (further assessment). For ASQ:SE-2,
   higher totals mean more concern; zones are no or low risk, monitor, and refer. Then
   read the overall questions: a parent's concern can justify a referral on its own.
3. **Check what could skew the result.** Missed items, no chance to try a skill (no
   stairs at home), language, hearing, illness, or a new placement. These change the
   plan, not the score.
4. **Gate the next step (DP-05).**
   - **On track** everywhere, no parent concern → a strength, two or three activities,
     rescreen at the next interval.
   - **Monitor** (an area close to the cutoff, or a mild concern) → targeted activities,
     rescreen in 2–3 months, tell the pediatrician with consent.
   - **Refer** (any area below the cutoff, a social-emotional score above the cutoff,
     loss of skills, or a strong parent worry) → offer a referral now: Part C for children
     under 3, the school district's Child Find for 3 and up, and the pediatrician; add a
     hearing test if communication is low.
5. **Start the talk with the child.** "Tell me what you've noticed about A. lately." Then
   one real strength from the screen. Then the results, one area at a time.
6. **Use plain words for each band.** On track: "This area is right where we'd expect."
   Monitor: "This one is close to the line, so let's do some things together and check
   again in a couple of months." Refer: "This area is below the line where it's worth
   having a specialist take a closer look. That's free through early intervention. It
   doesn't mean something is wrong — it means we'd rather know." Then pause and ask:
   "What's going through your mind?"
7. **Respect the parent's decision.** A parent may wait, decline, or want the doctor
   first. Record their choice and a date to revisit. If they agree, get the release,
   make the call together or send the referral, and agree who follows up. Federal
   Part C rules expect primary referral sources to refer within 7 days of identifying a
   concern; how that works with your consent policy is set by your state and program —
   verify locally.
8. **Send a referral package (NE-20).** With consent: screener, interval, date; area
   zones; the parent's concerns in their words; skewing factors; what the parent wants.
   Part C evaluation and plan meeting are usually due within 45 days of referral; school
   timelines vary — verify locally.

## Output Format

```
# Screening Summary — [child initials], [age in months] (adjusted: [..])
Screener: [name, interval]   Date: [..]   Completed by: [parent role] with [worker role]
Language: [..]  Interpreter: [professional / none]

## Results by area
| Area | Total | Zone (on track / monitor / refer) | What the parent noticed |

## Parent's concerns (their words)
## Things that may have affected the results
## Strengths seen
## Conversation notes (what was said, parent's response)
## Next step chosen by the parent
- Activities: [..]   Rescreen: [date]
- Referral: [Part C / Child Find / pediatrician / audiology] — consent [signed date] — sent [date]
- Follow-up call: [date, who]
## Same-day concerns (if any): [facts, route, time]
```

## Verification

- [ ] Interval matches the child's (adjusted) age, and every zone comes from published cutoffs.
- [ ] Parent concerns are recorded in their own words and considered for referral.
- [ ] No diagnostic words appear in the summary or the script.
- [ ] Each refer-zone area has an offered referral and the parent's recorded decision.
- [ ] Any shared results have a signed release or a stated legal basis.
- [ ] A dated rescreen or follow-up exists for every outcome.

## False-Positive Prevention

| Misfire | What it looks like | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Labelling instead of describing | "Signs of autism" in the summary | Behaviour and zone only; evaluators diagnose. |
| Screen treated as diagnosis | "He has a speech delay" | "Communication was below the line; a closer look is free." |
| Watch-and-wait by default | Refer zone, "let's see in six months" | Offer the referral now; a parent can still decline. |
| Ignoring parent worry | All areas on track, mother very worried | Parent concern alone can justify referral. |
| Bilingual learning read as delay | Low communication score in a two-language home | Count words in all languages; use the home-language form. |
| No-opportunity read as inability | Stairs item "not yet" in a flat with no stairs | Note lack of chance; try the skill at the visit. |
| Scaring the parent | Results opened with the low area | Start with the child and a strength; one area at a time. |

## Adaptations

- **Child in foster or kinship care:** a new carer may not know the child yet; consider
  waiting a few weeks, and check who can consent to referrals.
- **Child already identified as autistic or disabled:** ask what the parent wants from
  the screen; describe results as support needs, not deficits.
- **Multilingual family:** use the published translation and a professional interpreter;
  ask about skills in every language.
- **Parent with intellectual disability or low literacy:** read items aloud, show the
  pictures, and use teach-back for results.
- **Neurodivergent parent:** give the form ahead of time; allow written questions.
- **Tribal or Indigenous family:** offer tribal early-childhood programs where they exist.
- **Telehealth or rural:** parent completes on screen or by phone.

```
# Screening Summary — A., 18 months (born full term)
Screener: ASQ-3, 18-month interval   Date: 2 Oct 2026
Completed by: mother (D.) with home visitor   Language: English   Interpreter: none

## Results by area (cutoffs from the 18-month scoring sheet)
| Communication   | 10 | refer    | "He says mama and ball. Doesn't point to show me." |
| Gross motor     | 60 | on track | Walks and climbs the couch. |
| Fine motor      | 50 | on track | Stacks two blocks. |
| Problem solving | 40 | on track | Copies wiping the table. |
| Personal-social | 35 | monitor  | "He doesn't bring things to show me." |

## Parent's concerns
"His cousin talks way more. I wondered but didn't want to say."

## Things that may have affected the results
None found. English only at home. Ear infections x3 since Feb; no hearing test yet.

## Strengths seen
Laughs at peekaboo; reaches for D. when the dog barks; very good climber.

## Conversation notes
Started with "What's he into lately?" — D. said trucks and stairs. Shared the
climbing result first. Communication: "below the line where we'd want a specialist
to take a closer look. It's free and they come to you." D.: "So something's wrong?"
Worker: "Not necessarily. It means we'd rather know than guess."

## Next step chosen by D.
- Activities: name what he points or reaches at; pause for him to fill in songs.
- Referral: Part C early intervention — consent signed 2 Oct — sent 3 Oct.
- Pediatrician: release signed; D. will ask for a hearing test at the 6 Oct visit.
- Rescreen personal-social with ASQ:SE-2 at next visit, 16 Oct.
- Follow-up call: worker, 9 Oct, to check EI contact was made.

## Same-day concerns: none.
```

## Techniques Used

- **DS-01 Framework Application** — the screener's own intervals, area totals, and published cutoff zones structure scoring.
- **DP-05 Stakes-Based Gate Policy** — on track, monitor, and refer bands each trigger a set next step and timeline.
- **DP-04 Must-Not Constraints** — no labels, no worker-scored items, no family interpreters, no screening in a crisis week.
- **NE-20 Third-Party Handoff Package** — a consented referral package for Part C, Child Find, or the pediatrician.
