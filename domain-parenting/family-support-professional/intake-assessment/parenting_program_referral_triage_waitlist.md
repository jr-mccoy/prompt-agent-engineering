# Program Referral Triage and Waitlist

**Objective:** Turn incoming referrals into decisions a supervisor can defend. Each
referral is read for safety first, then gets an eligibility check, an anchored urgency
tier, and one outcome — accept, waitlist, redirect with a warm handoff, or decline with
a reason. Waitlisted families get check-ins; safeguarding concerns leave the queue at
once.

**When to Use:**
- Referrals arrive faster than the program has open places.
- You need consistent tiers so that two coordinators would sort the same referral the
  same way.
- A referral note mentions something worrying and you are unsure what to do with it.
- **Not this prompt if** you are a licensed clinician deciding whether something you
  heard in therapy is reportable — use
  `domain-psychology/risk-crisis/psychology_mandated_reporter_decision_walkthrough.md`.
  Screening members for a clinical therapy group →
  `domain-psychology/populations/cross-population/psychology_group_therapy_member_screening.md`.
  A clinician writing the handoff narrative →
  `domain-psychology/care-coordination/psychology_warm_handoff_narrative.md`.
  The first conversation once a family is accepted →
  `domain-parenting/family-support-professional/intake-assessment/parenting_family_strengths_needs_intake.md`.
  A family attending because a court or agency required it →
  `domain-parenting/family-support-professional/coaching-education/parenting_mandated_parent_engagement.md`.

## Safeguarding, Consent, and Scope

- **The waitlist is not a safety plan.** A referral that describes a child in danger, a
  likely injury, a child left without care, partner violence, or suicide talk is acted on
  that day. It is never parked in a queue.
- **Immediate danger** → 911 or the local emergency number first.
- **Mandated reporting — verify locally.** Who must report, the threshold (reasonable
  suspicion or reasonable cause to believe, not proof), the deadline, and whether a
  supervisor consult counts all vary by state, province, country, and agency. In most US
  states the duty is personal and is not discharged by telling a supervisor. A referrer
  saying "CPS is aware" does not end your duty if you now hold reasonable suspicion —
  confirm, and report yourself if in doubt. Consulting must never delay a report or an
  emergency call. Report to the state or county child-protection hotline or law
  enforcement (Childhelp 1-800-422-4453 can help find the number). Do not investigate or
  call the family to "check the story." Record the exact words in the referral, when you
  read it, what you did, and who you told. Decide with your supervisor, case by case,
  whether telling the family is safe — never when it could endanger anyone.
- **Crisis routes for check-ins:** suicide talk → 988 (US, call or text); partner violence
  → National DV Hotline 1-800-799-7233; possible ingestion → Poison Control
  1-800-222-1222; outside the US, the local emergency number and findahelpline.com.
- **Route to licensed clinicians** when the main need is treatment: a parent's severe
  mood symptoms, psychosis, active substance use with an infant, or a child's
  developmental, medical, or mental-health concern. Triage does not diagnose. Record what
  the referral says happened, in its own words, with frequency and duration.
- **Consent and privacy.** A voluntary program needs the family's own yes. Check that
  the family knows about the referral and agreed to it. Information goes back to a
  referrer only with a signed release, except where law requires or permits it (verify
  locally). Share the minimum necessary. Families may read their records. FERPA, HIPAA,
  42 CFR Part 2 (substance-use records), or GDPR-type rules may apply — verify locally.
  De-identify before pasting referrals into an AI tool unless your agency has approved it.

## Inputs / Context

1. **De-identify.** Replace names with referral numbers you make up for this exercise
   (R1, R2), ages only, no addresses, dates of birth, or case numbers.
2. **Each referral:** source, date received, reason in the referrer's words, household
   ages, language, whether the family knows and agreed, and any safety content.
3. **Program rules:** eligibility (child age, pregnancy stage, geography, income, first
   child, model enrolment windows), what is offered, and funder response-time standards.
4. **Capacity:** open places now, expected openings, caseload limits.
5. **Current waitlist:** size, oldest date, check-in status.
6. **Local alternatives:** better-fit services with real contact routes and wait times.

## Method

1. **Read every referral for safety before eligibility (AG-19).** Work in time windows.
   **Within the hour:** immediate danger → 911; reasonable suspicion of abuse or neglect
   not known to be reported → start the report per local law and tell your supervisor.
   **Same day:** ask the referrer to clarify safety facts (not to investigate), whether
   a report or DV advocate is involved, and a safe way to reach the family. **Within the program's standard:** first contact for everyone else.
2. **Check consent and voluntariness.** Does the family know? Did they agree? Is the
   referral required by child protection or a court? If the family has not agreed, the
   first contact is an offer. If required, note what gets reported back, and to whom.
3. **Analyse four dimensions separately (RT-02).** For each referral: **eligibility**
   (meets / does not / needs info), **urgency** (tier), **fit** (does our service match
   the main need?), and **capacity** (can we start in the tier's window?).
4. **Assign an anchored urgency tier (DP-03).** Defaults — set your own numbers to match
   funder standards:
   - **Tier S (safety):** a reportable concern or crisis in the referral → safety steps
     above, today. Program enrolment is a separate decision.
   - **Tier 1 (urgent):** a risk that worsens within weeks without support — newborn under
     4 weeks with a parent alone and unsupported, late pregnancy with no prenatal care,
     family losing housing this month, enrolment window closing. Contact in 2 business
     days; start in 2 weeks.
   - **Tier 2 (priority):** a clear need with some supports in place. Contact in 5
     business days; start in 30 days.
   - **Tier 3 (routine):** a wish for support, no time-sensitive risk. Contact in 10
     business days; waitlist acceptable.
5. **Decide fit and name the dominant driver (DP-06).** Write one sentence: "Decision:
   redirect. Dominant driver: the main need is substance-use treatment, which we do not
   provide." A redirect is a warm handoff: call with consent, share the minimum, confirm
   the service can see them. Our program may still be an add-on.
6. **Gate the waitlist with check-ins (DP-05).** Higher stakes, more contact:
   Tier 1 waiting → weekly call; Tier 2 → every 2 weeks; Tier 3 → monthly. Each check-in:
   "How are things since we last talked? Anything new that's worrying you? Is everyone
   at home safe?" Give crisis numbers and interim supports. Any new safety content →
   Tier S. Any change in need → re-tier. Three missed contacts → supervisor decides on
   outreach before closing.
7. **Run a monthly equity check.** Compare waitlist time by language, geography, referral
   source, and family structure. If interpreter-needing families wait longer, fix the
   process, not the families.
8. **Close the loop with referrers.** With a release, tell the referrer the outcome in one
   or two lines. Without one, confirm only that the referral was received, if your policy
   allows.

## Output Format

```
# Referral Triage Log — [program]   Batch date: [..]   Coordinator: [role]
Open places: [..]   Waitlist: [n], oldest [date]

## Safety read (all referrals)
| Ref | Safety content (referrer's words) | Action, time, who told | Report status |

## Triage
| Ref | Source | Family agreed? | Eligibility | Tier | Fit | Capacity | Outcome |

## Decisions with dominant driver
R[n]: [accept / waitlist / redirect / decline] — driver: [..]

## Warm handoffs
| Ref | Service | Consent | Contact made | Can start |

## Waitlist check-in schedule
| Ref | Tier | Next check-in | Interim supports | Re-tier triggers |

## Equity check (monthly)
## Supervisor sign-off: declines, Tier S items, three-missed-contact closures
```

## Verification

- [ ] Every referral has a safety read recorded before its eligibility decision.
- [ ] No Tier S item sits on the waitlist without same-day action recorded.
- [ ] Each tier is justified by an observable fact from the referral.
- [ ] Every outcome names a dominant driver in one sentence.
- [ ] Redirects show consent and a confirmed contact, not just a phone number.
- [ ] Every waitlisted family has a dated next check-in that matches its tier.
- [ ] Information returned to referrers has a release or stated legal basis.

## False-Positive Prevention

| Misfire | What it looks like | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Labelling instead of describing | Triage note says "drug-addicted mum" | Quote the referral: "referrer reports daily alcohol use, amount not stated." |
| Waitlist as safety plan | DV mention, family placed on 3-month list | Tier S; same-day steps; enrolment decided separately. |
| Trusting "CPS is aware" | No check, no own judgement | Confirm; your duty is your own. |
| First come, first served | Oldest referral always next | Tier first, then date within tier. |
| Redirect as rejection | "Not eligible" letter with a phone list | Warm handoff with consent and a confirmed contact. |
| Thin referral read as low need | Two-line note from a busy clinic | "Needs info" — call the referrer before tiering. |
| Poverty read as neglect | Eviction notice tiered as a report | Housing loss is an urgent need (Tier 1), not by itself abuse. |

## Adaptations

- **Prenatal referrals:** model enrolment windows may close by a set week of pregnancy;
  that can make a referral Tier 1.
- **Teen parent:** a parent who is a minor may have different consent rules — verify
  locally.
- **Language or interpreter need:** book the interpreter for first contact; never let
  interpreter scheduling push a family down the list.
- **Tribal or Indigenous family:** offer tribal family services as an option, not a
  substitute; in the US, ICWA may apply if child protection is involved.
- **Immigrant family:** never ask documentation status; fear of systems can look like
  non-response — try several contact methods.
- **Rural:** count travel time in capacity; offer video starts.
- **Parent with intellectual disability, low literacy, or who is neurodivergent:** ask
  the preferred contact method; phone or text, not letters.

## Techniques Used

- **AG-19 Time-Critical Response Protocol** — safety read with within-the-hour, same-day, and standard contact windows.
- **DP-03 Anchored Scoring Scales** — urgency tiers anchored to observable facts with contact and start times.
- **RT-02 Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework** — eligibility, urgency, fit, and capacity assessed separately.
- **DP-06 Dominant Driver Identification** — each accept, waitlist, redirect, or decline names its main reason.
- **DP-05 Stakes-Based Gate Policy** — check-in frequency and supervisor sign-off scale with tier.
