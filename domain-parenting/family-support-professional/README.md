# Family-Support Professional Prompts

Prompts for the people who support families, not for the caregivers themselves:
parent educators, parenting coaches, family support workers, home visitors,
caseworkers, family-time (visit) supervisors, and community program staff. Each
prompt produces a working document for one family, group, or program task — an
intake summary, a coaching plan, a visit note, a referral, a safety response — and
keeps the worker inside their role. **No prompt here diagnoses a parent or child or
gives legal advice.**

The caregiver's own side of the same situations lives in
[`../caregiver-facing/`](../caregiver-facing/). Licensed-clinician protocols
(assessment, treatment, clinical notes, the clinician's reporting decision) live in
`domain-psychology/`; custody, dependency, and immigration law in `domain-legal/`.

## Cross-cutting safeguards (every prompt)

- **Describe, don't diagnose.** Workers record observable behaviour, the family's
  own words, frequency and duration. Labels are for licensed clinicians.
- **Mandated reporting is jurisdiction-specific — verify locally.** Who must report,
  the threshold (reasonable suspicion, not proof), timelines, and whether telling a
  supervisor satisfies the duty all vary by state, province, country, and agency. In
  most US states the duty is personal. Supervisor consultation never delays a report
  or an emergency call; immediate danger goes to 911 or the local emergency number
  first.
- **Escalation to licensed clinicians** has named triggers in every prompt, with
  same-day and emergency routes (988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline in the US; local
  equivalents via findahelpline.com).
- **Consent and privacy.** Limits of confidentiality are explained before anyone
  discloses; a written release comes before sharing with another agency except where
  law requires or permits otherwise; sharing is minimum-necessary; records are written
  as if the family will read them; client details are de-identified before being
  pasted into an AI tool unless the agency has approved it for client data.

## Conventions

Same frontmatter as the rest of the domain (`intended_use: model-testing`), with
`category: parenting/<subfolder>` and the tags `family-support-professional` and the
subfolder name. Every prompt has a "Safeguarding, Consent, and Scope" block, a
false-positive table, and adaptations (language, disability, Indigenous families,
neurodivergence, rural or remote work).
