# Home Visitor Field Safety

**Objective:** Keep the worker safe on home visits without treating families as
suspects. The output is a one-visit safety plan: risk level and gate, check-in
arrangement, exit notes, words for leaving early, and — if something happened — the
incident record, the child-safety check, and support.

**When to Use:**
- You visit families alone, in homes, shelters, motels, or cars.
- Something about an upcoming visit feels off: a new adult, a threat in the file, a dog,
  a known weapon, a past incident.
- Something happened on a visit and you need to record it and get support.
- **Not this prompt if** your worry is the **child's** safety — use
  `domain-parenting/family-support-professional/home-visit-fieldwork/parenting_home_visit_child_safety_concern.md`
  (you may need both). Planning the content of a visit →
  `domain-parenting/family-support-professional/home-visit-fieldwork/parenting_home_visit_plan_and_interaction.md`.
  A formal hazard analysis → `domain-operations/quality-safety/ops_job_hazard_analysis.md`.
  An agency-level incident review →
  `domain-operations/quality-safety/ops_safety_incident_investigation.md`.

## Safeguarding, Consent, and Scope

- **Your safety and the child's safety both count.** Leaving, rescheduling, or moving
  to the office keeps you safe. It does not end your duty if a child may be in danger.
  If you leave because an adult is intoxicated, armed, or violent and a child is still
  there, call 911 or the local emergency number from a safe place, then report.
- **Mandated reporting.** Who must report, the threshold (reasonable suspicion — not
  proof), and timelines vary by state, province, country, and agency — verify locally.
  In most US states the duty is personal and is not discharged by telling a supervisor;
  a consult must never delay a report or an emergency call. Routes: the state or county
  child-protection hotline or law enforcement (Childhelp 1-800-422-4453 can help find
  the number). Do not investigate, go back in to check, photograph, or promise secrecy.
- **Describe; do not diagnose.** "Slurred speech, unsteady, said 'I've had a few'" —
  not "she was drunk" or "he is psychotic." Families, courts, or police may read it.
- **Crisis routes for the people you visit.** Suicide talk → 988 (US) if you can stay
  safely; overdose or threats to others → 911 (naloxone only if trained and safe);
  possible poisoning → Poison Control 1-800-222-1222; partner violence → National DV
  Hotline 1-800-799-7233. Outside the US: local emergency number, findahelpline.com.
  You refer; you do not treat.
- **Your wellbeing.** After an incident, support is the agency's job: EAP and
  supervision; a licensed clinician if distress lasts more than a few weeks.
- **Privacy.** A risk flag describes a situation, not a character. Share it only with
  staff who need it; the family may read it. US agencies can draw on OSHA's
  workplace-violence guidance for healthcare and social service workers; elsewhere,
  national rules apply — verify locally.

## Inputs / Context

1. **De-identify first.** Roles and initials only; describe the location type ("rural
   road, poor signal"), not the address. No names, dates of birth, or case numbers
   unless your agency has approved the AI tool for client data.
2. **The visit:** time, length, who should be there, purpose.
3. **What is known:** past incidents, threats, weapons, dogs, other adults, substance
   use, recent stressors (a removal, a court date).
4. **Setting:** parking, signal.
5. **Agency rules:** lone-worker policy, check-in method, two-worker rule.
6. **Your state today** — it counts.

## Method

1. **Rate the visit on anchored levels (DP-03).** **Low:** known family, no threats,
   no weapons or loose dogs, daytime, good signal. **Medium:** any one of — a new or
   unknown adult, an unrestrained dog, recent distressing news for the family, verbal
   hostility last visit, poor signal or isolation, after dark. **High:** any one of —
   a threat to harm a worker, a weapon shown or reported within reach, a past assault on
   staff, a known violent adult present, intoxication reported today. Write the anchor
   that set the level. Never rate on race, poverty, neighbourhood, or a diagnosis.
2. **Apply the gate (DP-05).** Low → standard check-in and check-out. Medium → tell the
   supervisor before you go and agree changes (shorter visit, daylight, a public place,
   dog shut away, a second worker). High → no lone home visit: office, public place,
   two workers, or police if policy allows, with written supervisor approval.
3. **Leave a handoff package (NE-20).** Your check-in contact gets, through the agency
   system: where you are, who you expect, your car and plate, your end time, and the
   code phrase. Agree the missed-check-out steps: call you at +10 minutes, the supervisor
   at +20, police for a welfare check at +30 (per policy). A code phrase sounds ordinary
   — "Can you pull the blue file for me?" — and means "call police to my location now."
4. **Write the time-based response (AG-19).**
   - **Arriving:** park facing out, not blocked in. Pause 30 seconds; look for shouting,
     people outside, loose dogs. Keys and phone on you.
   - **At the door:** if anything feels wrong, do not go in: "It's not a good time. I'll
     call you to rebook."
   - **First minutes inside:** dogs in another room, door shut. Sit near the exit;
     nobody between you and the door. Notice weapons and who else is home.
   - **If it escalates:** lower voice, fewer words, open hands, name the feeling: "I can
     see you're really angry about the court date." Do not argue, touch, or corner.
   - **Leaving early:** "I'm going to stop here and call you tomorrow." If in danger,
     use the code phrase or just go. Lock the car and drive off before you call.
5. **Hold the must-nots (DP-04).** Do not enter if a weapon is shown or anyone is
   violent. Do not stay to finish paperwork after a threat. Do not go back in alone —
   ask police for a welfare check. Do not hide an incident. Do not leave a child in
   immediate danger without calling 911.
6. **Record any incident the same day.** Time, who was there, exact words of any
   threat, what happened, injuries, what you did, who you told. Tell the supervisor;
   file the agency form. Reporting a threat to police is your choice unless policy
   requires it.
7. **Close the loop on the child.** Did anything you saw or heard meet the reporting
   threshold for any child there? If yes, report now. If unsure, consult the same day
   without delaying a report that is due.
8. **Debrief and review.** Within 1–2 working days: debrief, EAP, a new risk level, and
   how services continue (office visits, a co-worker, a different worker).

## Output Format

```
# Field Safety Plan — [family initials], visit [date, time]
## Risk level: [low / medium / high]   Anchor(s): [ ]
## Gate: [standard / changes agreed / no lone home visit]   Supervisor: [who, when]
## Check-in package: contact / end time / car / code phrase / +10 +20 +30 steps
## Arrive → door → inside → escalation → leave early (scripts)
## Must-nots for this visit
## (If incident) Facts: time / who / exact words / what happened / injury / action
## (If incident) Child-safety check: report due? [no / yes → route, time]
## (If incident) Reported to: supervisor / agency form / police (choice)
## Debrief date, support, new risk level, service plan
```

## Verification

- [ ] The risk level names its anchor and uses no demographic or place proxy.
- [ ] The gate matches the level; medium or high shows supervisor sign-off.
- [ ] Check-out time, escalation steps, and code phrase are written.
- [ ] Exit, parking, and dog arrangements are stated.
- [ ] Leaving-early words are scripted, with "drive away, then call."
- [ ] Any incident record uses facts and exact words, not labels.
- [ ] A child-safety check after leaving is recorded, with the route if a report is due.
- [ ] A debrief date is set.

## False-Positive Prevention

| Misfire | What it looks like | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Labelling instead of describing | "He's a psycho"; "she was drunk" | "Shouted 'get out of my house' twice; slurred speech; unsteady." |
| Poverty or place as risk | Medium "because of the estate" | Rate on behaviour, threats, weapons, dogs, setting. |
| Leaving as the end of it | Leaves an impaired sole carer with a baby | Call 911 from safety; report. |
| One bad day as a permanent flag | "High" two years after one shout | Review the level after each visit. |
| Toughing it out | Stays after a threat to "finish the form" | Leave; the form can wait. |
| Self-blame | "I should have seen it coming" | Review the system, not the person. |

## Adaptations

- **Rural visits:** check in before you lose signal and after; use a lone-worker device.
- **Telehealth:** a parent may be with a violent partner; agree a phrase ("Is the
  pharmacy open?") that means "I can't talk."
- **Shelter, motel, or car:** meet in a common area.
- **Interpreter on the visit:** covered by the same plan; brief them on exits and code.
- **Worker who is pregnant or disabled:** adjust stairs, seating, and staffing.
- **Worker facing slurs or harassment:** that is an incident; report it; reassignment is
  an option, not a failure.
- **Military housing or tribal land:** check entry rules and whether base security or
  tribal police respond first — verify locally.

## Techniques Used

- **DP-03 Anchored Scoring Scales** — low, medium, and high are defined by observable anchors, not a gut score.
- **DP-05 Stakes-Based Gate Policy** — each level triggers its own approval and staffing gate.
- **AG-19 Time-Critical Response Protocol** — actions set for arriving, the door, the first minutes, escalation, and leaving.
- **DP-04 Must-Not Constraints** — no entry with a weapon shown, no lone return, no hidden incidents, no leaving a child in danger without a call.
- **NE-20 Third-Party Handoff Package** — the check-in contact holds everything needed to act if the worker goes silent.
