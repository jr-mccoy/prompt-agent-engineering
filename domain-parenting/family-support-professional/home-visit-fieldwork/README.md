# Home Visit Fieldwork Prompts (Family-Support Professional)

For home visitors, parent educators, family support workers, and caseworkers who meet
families at home. These prompts cover the visit itself — the family's agenda and
parent–child interaction — plus two moments fieldwork makes harder: keeping the worker
safe, and acting when something on a visit worries you about a child. Workers describe
what they saw and heard. **No prompt here diagnoses** a parent or child, scores
development, or replaces a licensed clinician.

## Scope

**In scope:**
- Planning and running a single home visit: family-set agenda, one parent–child activity
  the worker sets up and steps back from, specific strengths feedback, observation without
  scoring, wrap-up and next-visit plan.
- The worker's own field safety: anchored pre-visit risk check, check-in and check-out,
  positioning and exits, leaving early, incident record, after-incident support.
- A child-safety concern seen or heard on a visit: immediate-danger, report, and support
  tiers; mandated-reporting steps; what to tell the family; the record and report package.

## Safety notes

Mandated-reporting duties — who must report, the threshold (reasonable suspicion, not
proof), timelines, and whether a supervisor consult counts — vary by state, province,
country, and agency: **verify locally.** In most US states the duty is personal and is
not discharged by telling a supervisor; a consult never delays a report or an emergency
call. Immediate danger → **911** or the local emergency number first. Report routes: the
state or county child-protection hotline or law enforcement (**Childhelp
1-800-422-4453** helps find the number). Other routes: **988** Suicide & Crisis Lifeline
(US), **Poison Control 1-800-222-1222**, **National DV Hotline 1-800-799-7233**; outside
the US, the local emergency number and findahelpline.com. Workers do not investigate,
photograph injuries, or promise secrecy. Families hear the limits of confidentiality
before they disclose; sharing with other agencies needs a written release except where
law requires or permits it; records are written knowing families may read them; and
anything pasted into an AI tool is de-identified unless the agency has approved the tool.
Leaving an unsafe visit protects the worker; it never ends the duty to act for a child.
