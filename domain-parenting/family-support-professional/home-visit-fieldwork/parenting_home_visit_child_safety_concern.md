# Child Safety Concern on a Home Visit

**Objective:** Turn a worrying moment on a home visit into the right action, in the right
order: each observation tiered as immediate danger, report, or support; actions for the
next minutes, hours, and days; words for the family; a factual record and report package.

**When to Use:**
- During or right after a visit you saw or heard something about a child's safety.
- A child or parent said something that may be a disclosure.
- You must decide today whether it is reportable.
- **Not this prompt if** you are a licensed clinician weighing a report and therapy
  continuity — use `domain-psychology/risk-crisis/psychology_mandated_reporter_decision_walkthrough.md`.
  Your own safety → `domain-parenting/family-support-professional/home-visit-fieldwork/parenting_home_visitor_worker_safety.md`.
  A practice that differs from your norms →
  `domain-parenting/family-support-professional/culturally-responsive/parenting_cultural_practice_vs_safety_check.md`.
  An ordinary visit note →
  `domain-parenting/family-support-professional/plans-documentation/parenting_family_contact_note_writer.md`.

## Safeguarding, Consent, and Scope

- **Immediate danger → 911 or the local emergency number first,** before anything else.
- **Reporting rules vary — verify locally.** Who must report, the threshold (reasonable
  suspicion or reasonable cause to believe — not proof), the timeline (often immediately
  by phone, then a written report within set hours), and what counts as neglect differ
  by state, province, country, and agency. In most US states the duty is personal:
  telling your supervisor does not discharge it, and a consult must never delay a report.
  Routes: the state or county child-protection hotline or law enforcement (Childhelp
  1-800-422-4453 can help find the number).
- **You are not the investigator,** and you **describe, not diagnose.** No questioning
  for details, examining, photographing injuries, confronting, or promising secrecy.
  Record exact words, what you saw, when, and who you told: "three round marks about
  1 cm across, left forearm; Mum said 'he fell off the bed'" — not "burns" or "abuse."
- **Clinician and crisis routes.** A bruise on a baby not yet pulling to stand, an
  injury to a young child's head, face, ears, or neck, or an explanation that does not
  fit → same-day medical evaluation plus a report. Possible ingestion → Poison Control
  1-800-222-1222 (911 if drowsy, struggling to breathe, or seizing). Parent talks about
  suicide → 988 (US). Partner violence → National DV Hotline 1-800-799-7233. Outside
  the US: local emergency number, findahelpline.com. You refer; you do not treat.
- **Consent and privacy.** Families heard at intake that danger to a child limits
  confidentiality. A child-protection report generally needs no consent — verify
  locally; other sharing needs a written release. The family may read the record.

## Inputs / Context

1. **De-identify first.** Roles and initials only; no names, addresses, dates of birth,
   or case numbers unless your agency has approved the AI tool for client data.
2. **What you saw and heard:** each item separately, with time and exact words.
3. **The child:** age, mobility (rolling, cruising, walking), disability.
4. **The adults:** who was there, their observed state, what they said and did.
5. **Protective factors**, where you are now, and your local reporting rules.

## Method

1. **Break the concern into separate observations (RT-02).** For each: what you saw or
   heard; how long or how often; the child's vulnerability; the adult's state and
   response; protective factors; tier. Then together — two support items can add up to
   a report.
2. **Tier by stakes, not discomfort (DP-05).**
   - **Tier 1 — immediate danger:** a child injured and needing care now; a young child
     alone with no adult; an infant beside an adult who cannot be woken; violence
     happening; a loaded gun or drugs (pills, powder, fentanyl) a child can reach; no
     heat in freezing weather with an infant; possible ingestion. Gate: 911 now, then
     report.
   - **Tier 2 — report:** reasonable suspicion, no danger this minute: an unexplained
     injury, a disclosure, a carer repeatedly too impaired to care, needs unmet when the
     parent has the means. Whether a child witnessing partner violence is reportable by
     itself varies — verify locally. Gate: report within the legal timeline.
   - **Tier 3 — support:** a risk a willing parent can fix with help: a sober parent
     putting a baby down on her front; benefits ran out; medicines in reach. Gate:
     teaching today, a written plan, referrals, a check next visit. In many places lack
     of money alone is not neglect — verify locally.
   - **Unsure?** Treat it as the higher tier; consult the same day without missing the
     reporting timeline.
3. **Act in time order (AG-19).**
   - **First minutes:** make the child safe if you safely can — baby to the cot, pills up
     high. If you cannot, or an adult turns hostile, leave and call 911 from the car.
   - **Within the hour:** write exact words and times; call the hotline; consult the
     supervisor in parallel, not first.
   - **Same day:** written report if required; record; Tier 3 referrals with consent.
   - **Next 48 hours:** follow up the plan; check in with the family.
4. **Hold the must-nots (DP-04).** At most one open prompt to a child ("Tell me what
   happened"), then listen. No secrecy: "I can't keep this just between us, but I will
   help." No waiting to "be sure." No telling a suspected person a report is coming if
   that could endanger anyone. No safety lesson used as a threat.
5. **Decide with the supervisor what to tell the family.** Case by case; never when it
   could endanger the child, a parent, or you. When safe, be brief: "I have to let
   child protection know about the mark on J's arm. That's the law for me. I'm still
   your visitor." For Tier 3: "Babies are safest on their back,
   alone, in the cot. Can I show you what helps?"
6. **Build the report package (NE-20)** so the hotline can act without calling back:
   child's age and location, household, what you saw and heard with times and exact
   words, injuries by size, colour, and place, the adult's explanation, protective
   factors, any danger to a visiting worker, your contact. Keep the reference number.
7. **Write the record.** Facts, observations, and actions on separate lines, with report
   and consult times. No guesses about who caused an injury.
8. **Keep the relationship.** Many families stay in services after a report. Name the
   anger ("It makes sense you're upset"). Debrief with your supervisor.

## Output Format

```
# Child Safety Concern — [family initials], visit [date, time]
Child: [initials, age, mobility]   Present: [roles]   I am now: [home / car / office]
## Observations
| # | Seen/heard (exact words, time) | Duration/frequency | Child vulnerability | Adult state & response | Protective factors | Tier |
## Combined tier: [1 / 2 / 3] — dominant reason
## Actions: first minutes / within the hour / same day / next 48 h
## Calls: 911 [time] / hotline [time, ref #] / supervisor [time] / medical
## What I told the family and why that was safe — or why I did not
## Report package
## Record entry: facts / observations / actions
## Tier 3 plan and referrals (with consent)   ## Follow-up date
```

## Verification

- [ ] Each observation has its own row with exact words, time, and tier.
- [ ] Any Tier 1 item shows 911 called first.
- [ ] Any Tier 2 item shows a report within the local timeline and a reference number.
- [ ] The supervisor consult did not delay the report.
- [ ] No diagnostic or causal label appears for an injury, parent, or child.
- [ ] The decision about telling the family states the safety reason.
- [ ] Each Tier 3 item has a teaching step, a plan, and a follow-up date.

## False-Positive Prevention

| Misfire | What it looks like | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Labelling instead of describing | "Signs of abuse"; "neglectful mum" | Size, colour, place; exact words; what was in the cupboard. |
| Poverty treated as neglect | Report because the flat is cold and bare | Heat and food help first; check local neglect definitions. |
| Difference treated as danger | Report for a family bed with sober parents and a 6-year-old | Separate difference from harm; use the cultural-practice check. |
| Under-reacting to a "good" family | Bruised 3-month-old, "they're so loving" | Non-mobile infant bruise → medical check and report. |
| Waiting for proof | "I'll check again next visit" | Threshold is reasonable suspicion. |
| Supervisor as the report | "I told my supervisor, so it's done" | In most US states the duty is yours. |

## Adaptations

- **Infant:** lower thresholds for sleep and bruising; a non-mobile baby has few
  innocent ways to be hurt.
- **Teen:** may disclose more; no secrecy promise; explain next steps plainly.
- **Disabled or neurodivergent child:** higher risk of abuse and of not being believed;
  record their own words, signs, or device output.
- **Parent with intellectual disability:** not knowing is not neglect by itself; teach
  with pictures and practice unless the child is in danger.
- **Interpreter:** a professional, never the child or a relative.
- **Tribal or Indigenous child (US):** the Indian Child Welfare Act may apply; say in the
  report that the family is tribal if you know.
- **Telehealth:** for Tier 1 danger, call 911 to the family's location.

## Techniques Used

- **AG-19 Time-Critical Response Protocol** — actions for the first minutes, first hour, same day, and next 48 hours.
- **DP-05 Stakes-Based Gate Policy** — each tier triggers its own gate.
- **RT-02 Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework** — every observation is analysed on the same dimensions before tiering.
- **DP-04 Must-Not Constraints** — no questioning, photographing, secrecy promises, waiting for proof, or unsafe tip-offs.
- **NE-20 Third-Party Handoff Package** — the report package lets the hotline act without calling back.
