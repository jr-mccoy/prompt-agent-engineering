# Home Visit Plan and Parent–Child Interaction

**Objective:** Plan and run one home visit that the family experiences as theirs: the
parent's agenda first, one short parent–child activity the worker sets up and then steps
back from, specific strengths feedback, plain observation notes, and a wrap-up with one
small home step and the next date.

**When to Use:**
- You are a home visitor, parent educator, or family support worker preparing a regular
  visit with a parent and a child from birth to about 5.
- Visits have drifted into paperwork, crisis talk, or the worker "teaching at" the parent.
- You want to support parent–child interaction without taking over or scoring.
- **Not this prompt if** this is the first meeting — use
  `domain-parenting/family-support-professional/intake-assessment/parenting_family_strengths_needs_intake.md`.
  Giving or explaining a developmental screener →
  `domain-parenting/family-support-professional/intake-assessment/parenting_developmental_screen_results_talk.md`.
  Something on the visit worries you about the child's safety →
  `domain-parenting/family-support-professional/home-visit-fieldwork/parenting_home_visit_child_safety_concern.md`.
  A licensed clinician delivering PCIT or behavioural parent training →
  `domain-psychology/modalities/behavioral-parent-training/psychology_behavioral_parent_training_module.md`.

## Safeguarding, Consent, and Scope

- **Consent and its limits.** Remind the family in plain words when a hard topic opens,
  before anyone discloses: "What you tell me stays with me and my supervisor, unless I
  think a child or someone else is in danger, or a court orders our records."
- **Describe; do not diagnose or score.** Write what you saw and heard, how often, and
  for how long. Not "mum seems depressed" but "said she has slept about 3 hours a night
  for two weeks and has not gone out since Tuesday." If your model uses a structured
  observation tool, use it only as trained; this prompt does not score.
- **Mandated reporting travels with you.** Who must report, the threshold (reasonable
  suspicion, not proof), and timelines vary by state, province, country, and agency —
  verify locally. In most US states the duty is personal; telling a supervisor does not
  discharge it, and a consult must never delay a report. Immediate danger → 911 or the
  local emergency number first. Do not investigate, photograph injuries, or promise
  secrecy; switch to the child-safety-concern prompt.
- **Route to licensed clinicians.** Same day: a parent talks about suicide (988 in the
  US; elsewhere the local emergency number or findahelpline.com), sees or hears things
  others do not, or has thoughts of harming the baby; a baby under 3 months with fever,
  poor feeding, or unusual sleepiness. Possible ingestion → Poison Control
  1-800-222-1222. Partner violence → private safety talk and the National DV Hotline
  1-800-799-7233. Routine referral, with consent: any skill loss, no babbling by 12
  months, no words by 16 months, a parent's ongoing low mood or worry. You support and
  refer; you do not treat.
- **Privacy.** Families may be able to read their records; write notes you could read
  aloud to them. Share outside your agency only with a written release, except where law
  requires or permits it (such as a child-protection report) — verify locally. HIPAA,
  FERPA, 42 CFR Part 2, or GDPR-type rules may apply.

## Inputs / Context

1. **De-identify first.** Initials or roles only ("Mum, 24", "baby R, 9 months"). No
   names, addresses, dates of birth, or case numbers unless your agency has approved the
   AI tool for client data.
2. **Family:** who will be there, languages, what the parent wants from visits, strengths.
3. **Child:** age (adjusted if premature), what they do and enjoy, any disability.
4. **Last visit:** what was planned, the home step chosen, anything unfinished.
5. **Model requirements:** curriculum topic due, screenings due, visit length.

## Method

1. **Ask for the agenda before you set it.** "What would make today useful for you?"
   Write the parent's items first; fit the model's content around them. If their item
   is a crisis (eviction notice, no formula), deal with it first and shorten the rest.
   Name one small trust deposit you keep every visit — on time, bring what you promised,
   text if late (DP-18).
2. **Choose one activity using a recognised framework (DS-01).** Use the interaction
   approach your program trains in, such as Facilitating Attuned Interactions (FAN) or
   your model's parent–child component. Use the family's own things for 5–10 minutes:
   0–6 months, face-to-face talk and copying sounds; 6–18 months, back-and-forth play
   with one toy; 18 months–3 years, book sharing with pointing and naming; 3–5 years,
   pretend play the child leads.
3. **Set it up, then get out of the middle.** Hand the toy to the parent, not the child.
   Sit to the side. "Show me how you two usually play with this — I'll just watch."
   Do not demonstrate unless asked. Do not talk to the child over the parent's head.
4. **Watch for strengths and write what you see.** Track warmth (smiles, gentle touch),
   responsiveness (parent answers the child's cue), encouragement (lets the child try),
   and teaching (names, explains). Write actions with counts: "R dropped the spoon; Mum
   said 'uh-oh!'; R laughed and dropped it again — 5 rounds."
5. **Give specific, behaviour-linked feedback.** Name what the parent did and what the
   child did next: "When you waited, she handed you the cup. She's learning you'll wait
   for her." Ask first: "What did you notice?" Offer at most one idea, as a "wonder":
   "I wonder what would happen if you named the colour when she points?"
6. **Hold the must-nots (DP-04).** Do not score, rank, or label the interaction aloud or
   in the note. Do not correct the parent in front of the child, take the toy back, or
   compare with other families. Do not treat an unfamiliar cultural practice as a problem
   (safe-sleep teaching is separate and factual). Do not ask safety questions with a
   partner or older child in the room.
7. **Link required content to what just happened.** "That copying game is exactly what
   this week's handout is about." Materials at the parent's reading level and language.
8. **Wrap up in the parent's words (ST-03).** Ask what they will take from today. Agree
   one home step that fits this week ("bath song every night", not "read 30 minutes
   daily"). Confirm the next date and contact route. Record any referral and consent.

## Output Format

```
# Visit Plan — [family initials], visit [#] on [date], [length] min
Present: [roles]   Child: [initials, age / adjusted age]
## Parent's agenda (their words first)
## Required model content due today
## Trust deposit I keep this visit
## Interaction activity — framework / activity / family's own items / set-up script
## Observation notes (actions, counts, quotes — no scores or labels)
## Strengths feedback given (parent did → child did)
## One "wonder" offered (if any)
## Safety or clinician route triggered? [no / yes → route, who told, when]
## Wrap-up: parent's takeaway in their words
## Home step   ## Next visit and contact route   ## Referrals with consent
```

## Verification

- [ ] The parent's agenda is listed before the model's required content.
- [ ] The activity uses things in the home and fits the child's age.
- [ ] The worker watches for at least 5 minutes before any suggestion.
- [ ] Every observation is an action, count, or quote — no score, rank, or label.
- [ ] At least three feedback statements link a parent action to a child response.
- [ ] No more than one suggestion is offered, framed as a "wonder."
- [ ] The home step is small and written in the parent's words.

## False-Positive Prevention

| Misfire | What it looks like | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Labelling instead of describing | "Poor attachment"; "baby is delayed" | Write the action and count: "turned away 4 times in 10 min." Labels follow clinical assessment. |
| Worker becomes the star | Worker plays; parent watches | Toy to the parent; sit to the side. |
| Generic praise | "Great job, Mum!" | Name the action and the child's response. |
| Quiet parent read as disengaged | "Not interested in visits" | Ask about sleep, language, fear of judgement; watch for small responses. |
| Curriculum hijacks the visit | Handout covered, eviction worry ignored | Urgent need first; shorten the topic. |
| Difference read as deficit | No toys, so "no play" | Notice the songs, carrying, chores, and talk that are play here. |

## Adaptations

- **Infant under 6 months:** the activity is talk, feeding, or a nappy change; feedback
  on reading cues ("you saw him yawn and slowed down").
- **Neurodivergent child:** follow the child's interests and pace; lining up or little
  eye contact is not a problem to fix.
- **Parent with intellectual disability or low literacy:** one idea, shown not told
  when asked, picture cards, teach-back, the same order each visit.
- **Interpreter:** a professional, never a child or relative; brief them on the activity.
- **Telehealth:** the parent props the phone; agree what happens if the call drops.
- **Parent who is a minor:** include a grandparent only if the young parent wants them.
- **Tribal or Indigenous family:** ask about the family's own ways of teaching and play.

## Example Output

```
# Visit Plan — Family T, visit 7 on 14 Oct, 75 min
Present: Mum (22), baby R (9 months)

## Parent's agenda
1. "He's waking 3 times a night again — is that normal?"
2. "WIC letter — I don't understand it."
## Required model content
Topic 9: back-and-forth play. ASQ-3 due next visit.
## Trust deposit
Brought the bus-pass form promised last visit.
## Interaction activity
FAN. R's stacking cups, already on the rug.
"Show me how you and R play with these — I'll just watch."
## Observation notes
10:12–10:20. Mum on the floor facing R. R banged two cups; Mum
banged hers, said "bang bang!" — R looked up and smiled; 4 rounds.
R crawled off at 10:16; Mum followed, said "where you going?"
R handed Mum a cup twice; Mum said "thank you!" both times.
Mum said: "He never sits still for me."
## Strengths feedback given
- "When you copied his banging, he looked right at you and smiled."
- "You followed him when he crawled off — he went back to the cups."
- "He handed you the cup twice. Babies do that with people they trust."
## One "wonder"
"I wonder what he'd do if you hid the little cup under the big one?"
R lifted it on the second try. Mum laughed.
## Safety or clinician route triggered?
No. Night waking: common around 9 months; handout given. Mum says
he pulls his ears — she will raise it at his checkup on 21 Oct.
## Wrap-up: Mum's words
"Just copy him and see what he does."
## Home step
Cup game once a day after breakfast.
## Next visit
28 Oct, 10:00; text the work phone. WIC letter read together;
appointment 17 Oct, Mum will call to confirm.
```

## Techniques Used

- **DS-01 Framework Application** — a recognised interaction approach (FAN or the model's own) structures set-up, observation, and feedback.
- **DP-18 Trust Deposits Definition** — one small, repeatable worker behaviour is named and kept every visit.
- **DP-04 Must-Not Constraints** — no scoring, labelling, correcting in front of the child, taking over, or treating difference as deficit.
- **ST-03 Output Format Specification** — a fixed visit-plan template keeps the parent's agenda first and observations separate from feedback.
