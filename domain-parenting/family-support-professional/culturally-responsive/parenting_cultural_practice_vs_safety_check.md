# Cultural Practice vs Safety Check

**Objective:** Give a worker an honest, structured check when a family's parenting
practice differs from what the worker is used to. The output separates a practice
that is simply different from one with a specific, known risk and from one that
crosses a safety or legal line, plus a plan for raising it that keeps the
relationship.

**When to Use:**
- The worker reacts strongly to something a family does: bed-sharing, a grandmother
  making decisions, physical discipline, a traditional remedy, a child minding
  younger siblings.
- Someone's gut says "that's neglect," and the worker wants to check before acting.
- The worker needs words to raise a safety point without shaming the family.
- **Not this prompt if** you are a **licensed clinician** deciding whether to report
  and how to protect therapy — use
  `domain-psychology/risk-crisis/psychology_mandated_reporter_decision_walkthrough.md`.
  A clinician's cultural formulation for assessment →
  `domain-psychology/intake-assessment/psychology_cultural_formulation_interview_drafter.md`.
  A **parent** looking for discipline alternatives →
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/cross-age/parenting_natural_consequence_designer.md`.
  If you saw something concerning on a visit and need to act now, use
  `domain-parenting/family-support-professional/home-visit-fieldwork/parenting_home_visit_child_safety_concern.md`.
  For working through an interpreter, see
  `domain-parenting/family-support-professional/culturally-responsive/parenting_interpreter_mediated_family_session.md`.

## Safeguarding, Consent, and Scope

- **One standard for every family.** Culture never lowers the safety floor and never
  raises it. The question is always: is there harm, or a specific risk of serious
  harm, to this child?
- **Immediate danger** → 911 or the local emergency number first. Possible
  poisoning, including from a remedy → Poison Control 1-800-222-1222.
- **Mandated reporting.** Who reports, the threshold (reasonable suspicion or
  reasonable cause to believe — not proof), timelines, and whether a supervisor
  consult counts vary by state, province, country, and agency — verify locally. In
  most US states the duty is personal and is not discharged by telling a supervisor;
  consultation must never delay a report or an emergency call. Route: the state or
  county child-protection hotline or law enforcement (Childhelp 1-800-422-4453 can
  help find it). Do not investigate, interrogate, photograph injuries, or promise
  secrecy. Record exact words, what you saw, when, and who you told. Decide with your
  supervisor, case by case, whether telling the family is safe — never when it could
  endanger anyone.
- **Physical-discipline thresholds vary widely.** Some countries ban all physical
  punishment. US states generally allow "reasonable" parental discipline but differ
  on injury, marks, implements, head or face, and infants. Agency rules may be
  stricter than law. Verify locally.
- **Does not diagnose.** Describe facts, the family's words, frequency, and duration.
  Do not judge marks: describe them and route to a medical provider. Marks from
  coining or cupping, and some birthmarks, can look like bruises.
- **Escalate to licensed clinicians** for: any unexplained mark on a baby not yet
  moving around (same day); feeding or weight worries; an unidentified remedy given
  by mouth to a child; a parent who describes losing control while disciplining;
  suicide talk (988, US). Partner violence → National DV Hotline 1-800-799-7233.
  Outside the US: local emergency number, findahelpline.com.
- **Consent and privacy.** Remind the family of confidentiality limits before a
  sensitive conversation. Written release before consulting anyone outside the
  agency, except where law requires or permits sharing (verify locally). Families may
  read their records.

## Inputs / Context

1. **De-identify first:** initials or roles only; no names, addresses, dates of
   birth, or case numbers, unless your agency has approved the tool for client data.
2. **The practice:** what you saw or heard, in plain facts. Child's age and
   mobility.
3. **The family's words:** how they describe it and why they do it, if known.
4. **Your reaction:** what you felt and what you assumed.
5. **Location:** state, province, or country; agency policy on the topic.
6. **What else you know:** strengths, supports, other concerns, previous reports.

## Method

1. **Check your own reaction first.** "Would I flag this if a family from my own
   background did it? Am I reacting to harm, or to unfamiliarity or poverty?" Write
   down your assumption.
2. **Learn the practice in the family's words.** Ask, don't assume: "Tell me how
   bedtime works for the baby." Listen for meaning, who taught it, and flexibility.
3. **Analyse on five dimensions (RT-02).** (a) What the practice is, in facts.
   (b) Its purpose and meaning to the family. (c) The observed effect on this child:
   growth, mood, injuries, development, school. (d) Specific known risk, with its
   source: for example, infant bed-sharing on a sofa or after a parent has used
   alcohol or drugs is a high-risk sleep setting; some traditional remedies and
   cosmetics can contain lead. (e) The law and agency policy where the family lives.
4. **Apply the local rules (DS-33).** Name the jurisdiction and the rule that
   applies there — discipline limits, age guidance for minding siblings, female
   genital cutting (a crime in the US and many other countries), agency policy.
   Unknown rule → "verify locally" and who to ask. Never import another place's rule.
5. **Sort into three verdicts (DP-28).** Each verdict must name the criterion it
   meets.
   - **Green — difference.** No observed harm, no specific known risk. Respect it,
     learn from it, and build on it as a strength (extended family carrying care,
     babywearing, shared meals).
   - **Yellow — safer-version conversation.** A specific, documented risk, no harm
     seen, and a safer version exists (bed-sharing → baby on a separate firm surface
     in the parents' room; a remedy → bring it to the pediatrician). Raise it,
     support the change, follow up.
   - **Red — safety or legal line.** Harm, marks or injury meeting the local
     threshold, a practice the law prohibits, or imminent danger. Follow the
     emergency and reporting steps above. Supervision is consulted, not waited for.
6. **Raise it respectfully (NE-16).** Use "what you do now / a safer version / why"
   rather than right versus wrong. Script: "I can see how much you want her close.
   Many families sleep near their babies. The safest version doctors know of is her
   own flat, firm space right next to your bed. Could we look at what would make
   that work?" Offer something concrete (a portable crib, a lockbox, a clinic
   appointment).
7. **Agree a next step and a review date.** Record facts, the family's words, the
   verdict and criterion, what was offered, and when you will review. Re-check that
   the same response would apply to any family.

## Output Format

```
# Practice Check — [family initials], [date], [jurisdiction]

## What I saw or heard (facts only)
## My reaction and assumption (one-standard test answer)
## The practice in the family's words
## Five dimensions: practice / meaning / effect on child / specific risk + source / law + policy
## Local rule that applies (or "verify locally — ask [who]")
## Verdict: GREEN / YELLOW / RED — criterion met: [..]
## Conversation plan: current way / safer version / why (script)
## Practical offer
## Escalation or report steps (if RED), who, when
## Record entry (facts, family's words, verdict, follow-up date)
```

## Verification

- [ ] The one-standard question is answered in writing.
- [ ] The family's own explanation is recorded in their words.
- [ ] Every risk cited has a named, specific source; vague "it's unsafe" is absent.
- [ ] The jurisdiction is named, and unclear rules are marked "verify locally."
- [ ] The verdict names the exact criterion it meets.
- [ ] The script uses no judgement labels ("wrong", "neglect", "backward").
- [ ] A Red verdict lists emergency and reporting steps that do not wait on supervision.
- [ ] No diagnostic label appears for a parent or child.

## False-Positive Prevention

| Misfire | What it looks like | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Difference read as neglect | "Grandmother decides everything — Mum is disengaged" | Extended-family care is often a strength; ask who does what. |
| Marks judged by the worker | "Bruises on his back" after coining | Describe; ask; route to a medical provider. Do not label. |
| Labelling instead of describing | "Mum is too passive with discipline" | "Mum said she stopped using the belt in March; uses time with Dad instead." |
| Poverty read as culture or neglect | Three children sharing one mattress | Housing need; offer resources. Separate from the practice. |
| Cultural relativism past the line | "It's their culture" about cutting or injury | Safety and law apply to every family; follow Red steps. |
| Importing another place's law | Telling a family spanking is illegal where it is not | State the local rule; agency policy may differ — say which. |
| One conversation as resolution | Bed-sharing discussed once, never revisited | Agree a step and a review date. |

## Adaptations

- **Infant:** sleep and feeding risks weigh most; any unexplained mark on a
  non-mobile baby → same-day medical review.
- **Teen:** ask the teen alone about discipline and caring loads that block school.
- **Neurodivergent child:** restraint or seclusion "for calm" needs a clinician's
  view; affirm the family's knowledge of their child.
- **Parent with an intellectual disability:** concrete steps; do not mistake a skill
  gap for neglect.
- **Interpreter needed:** never a child; use the interpreter session plan.
- **Tribal or Indigenous family:** in the US, ICWA applies if custody is at stake;
  involve tribal liaisons where the family agrees.

## Example Output

```
# Practice Check — Family M. (mother R., baby T., 7 weeks), 9 Oct, Ohio, US

## What I saw or heard
10:15: T. asleep on the sofa next to R., thick quilt, two pillows. R.: "like
this most nights." No marks seen. Clinic weight 3 Oct "on track" per R.

## My reaction and assumption
Alarm; thought "unsafe parenting." One-standard test: I would flag a sofa
and quilt for any family — this is about the setting, not R.'s culture.

## The practice in R.'s words
"In my family the baby is never alone. My mother says a baby alone at
night is cold and scared. The cot is in the bedroom but the bedroom is
too cold — the heater broke."

## Five dimensions
Practice: night sleep on a sofa with a quilt, next to mother.
Meaning: closeness and warmth; grandmother's teaching.
Effect on T.: feeding and weight on track per R.; no marks seen.
Specific risk: sofa sleeping with soft bedding is a high-risk infant
sleep setting (AAP safe-sleep guidance).
Law/policy: no law on sleep location; agency policy requires a safe-sleep
conversation and follow-up within 7 days.

## Verdict: YELLOW — specific documented risk, no harm seen, safer
version available.

## Conversation plan
"I can see how much you want T. close and warm. Your mother is right that
babies sleep better near their parents. The safest way doctors know is T.
on his own flat, firm space right next to you. Can we work out how to make
the bedroom warm so you can both be in there?"

## Practical offer
Free portable crib (delivered 11 Oct); landlord heating letter; heater
fund referral.

## Escalation
None today. RED if T. has any mark or R. sleeps with T. after drinking.
Same-day pediatric line for any feeding or breathing worry.

## Record entry
Facts; R.'s words; YELLOW (risk criterion); crib 11 Oct; review 14 Oct.
```

## Techniques Used

- **RT-02 Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework** — practice, meaning, effect on the child, specific risk, and law are analysed separately.
- **DP-28 Traffic-Light Verdict System** — Green (difference), Yellow (safer-version conversation), Red (safety or legal line), each citing its criterion.
- **DS-33 Jurisdiction-Adaptive Output** — the rule applied is the one where the family lives, with "verify locally" where unknown.
- **NE-16 Non-Judgmental Comparison** — "current way / safer version / why" keeps the family's dignity in the conversation.
