# Immigrant and Refugee Family Support Plan

**Objective:** Produce a support plan that one immigrant or refugee family helps
write: what they want first, what stops them using services, a map of services that
fit, a trust-building routine, plans for trauma and parent-child gaps, and routes to
legal help for anything about immigration or benefits.

**When to Use:**
- A newly arrived family, a refugee family after resettlement services end, or a
  settled family that avoids clinics, schools, or offices.
- The family fears any agency contact will lead to immigration trouble.
- Children translate for parents, or parents feel they are losing their children to
  the new language and culture.
- The family asks the worker about visas, status, benefits, or "public charge."
- **Not this prompt if** you are a **lawyer** building an asylum case — use
  `domain-legal/immigration/legal_i589_asylum_declaration_framework.md` (immigration
  law lives in `domain-legal/immigration/`). A **clinical team** responding to a
  social-needs screen →
  `domain-healthcare-clinical/prompts/allied-health/allied_health_sdoh_screening_response.md`.
  A **teacher** planning outreach to multilingual families →
  `domain-education-teaching/instructor/reporting-communication/teaching_multilingual_family_outreach.md`.
  Running the session through an interpreter →
  `domain-parenting/family-support-professional/culturally-responsive/parenting_interpreter_mediated_family_session.md`.
  Following a referral to a real connection →
  `domain-parenting/family-support-professional/referral-resourcing/parenting_referral_follow_through_barriers.md`.

## Safeguarding, Consent, and Scope

- **Status is not asked or recorded** unless a program's eligibility rules require
  it; if they do, explain why and who sees it. Many family-support services, public
  schools, and community health centres do not require it — verify locally.
- **No immigration advice.** Do not say what is safe to apply for, what affects a
  case, or what rights apply with immigration officers. Route to an immigration
  lawyer or an accredited representative at a recognised nonprofit. Warn gently
  about "notarios" and consultants not authorised to give legal advice.
- **Public-benefit worries go to legal help.** The rules have changed many times; do
  not reassure or warn from memory. Children's eligibility can differ from parents'.
- **Never promise what you cannot guarantee.** Not "Nobody will tell immigration"
  unless agency policy and law support it. Say what *you* record and do not.
- **Mandated reporting applies to every family the same way.** Who reports, the
  threshold (reasonable suspicion, not proof), and timelines vary by state, province,
  country, and agency — verify locally. In most US states the duty is personal and is
  not discharged by telling a supervisor; consultation must never delay a report or
  an emergency call. Immediate danger → 911 or the local emergency number first.
  Report to the state or county child-protection hotline or law enforcement
  (Childhelp 1-800-422-4453 can help find it). Do not investigate, interrogate,
  photograph injuries, or promise secrecy. Record exact words, what you saw, when,
  and who you told. Decide with your supervisor whether telling the family is safe.
- **Escalate to licensed clinicians** when someone describes nightmares or
  flashbacks, weeks of little sleep, stops eating or going out, talks about dying
  (988, US, has interpreters), or has untreated pain or illness. Partner violence,
  including deportation threats used as control → National DV Hotline
  1-800-799-7233 and legal help. Forced work, debt bondage, or papers taken →
  National Human Trafficking Hotline 1-888-373-7888. Outside the US: local emergency
  number, findahelpline.com.
- **Does not diagnose.** "Said he wakes screaming three or four nights a week," not
  "has PTSD."
- **Consent and privacy.** Confidentiality limits first, through a professional
  interpreter, checked by teach-back. Written release before sharing with any agency,
  except where law requires or permits it — verify locally. Minimum necessary.
  Families may read records.

## Inputs / Context

1. **De-identify first:** initials or roles only; no names, addresses, dates of
   birth, case numbers — and never immigration details — unless your agency has
   approved the tool for client data.
2. **Family:** members, ages, who is here and who is not, languages, literacy.
3. **Arrival context the family chose to share:** how long here, resettlement
   services (if any), whether they are still active.
4. **The family's own priorities**, in their words.
5. **Barriers seen or named:** fear, transport, cost, language, work hours, stigma,
   housing.
6. **Location:** country, state or province, county; local services known.
7. **Program rules:** what eligibility data the program must collect.

## Method

1. **Set the must-nots before the first visit (DP-04).** No asking or recording
   status, travel history, or entry unless required. No immigration or benefits
   advice. No child interpreters. No confidentiality you cannot keep. No pressure to
   tell the trauma story.
2. **Start with the family's priority.** "What would make the biggest difference to
   your family this month?" Work on that first, even if unexpected (a driving
   licence, a winter coat, a job letter).
3. **Find the dominant barrier (DP-06).** For each service the family needs, ask
   what has stopped them. Name the one barrier that blocks the most: fear of
   systems, language, cost, transport, work hours, not knowing the system, or a
   past bad experience. Plan around that barrier first.
4. **Make trust deposits (DP-18).** 3–5 small, repeatable actions: arrive when you
   said; the same interpreter if the family wants; read your notes aloud; explain
   every form before signing; a written note in their language of what you record
   and what you never do. Count them.
5. **Map the service ecosystem (DS-30).** For each need: recommended service,
   alternatives, and fit criteria for this family (language access, cost, status
   questions asked, distance). Typical rows: school enrolment and language support;
   a sliding-fee community health centre; children's coverage; food; housing; adult
   language classes; immigration legal aid; a faith or diaspora group.
6. **Plan for trauma without digging.** Safety and predictability first; offer a
   refugee-experienced clinician at the triggers above, with consent.
7. **Plan for the acculturation gap.** Children often learn the language faster.
   Protect the parent's authority: speak to parents directly, never through the
   child; set up school apps for the parents. Treat the home language as a strength.
8. **Write the legal-help route, then review.** One line per worry: "[topic] → [legal
   aid], how to reach it, interpreter yes/no." Book with consent; never interpret the
   answer. Review at 30, 60, and 90 days.

## Output Format

```
# Family Support Plan — [family initials], written [date], review [dates]

## The family's priorities (their words)
## Must-nots for this worker and program
## Dominant barrier and how we plan around it
## Trust deposits (3–5, with how we count them)
## Service ecosystem map
| Need | Recommended | Alternatives | Fit criteria (language, cost, status asked, distance) | Status |
## Trauma: what helps now, signs to refer, who, consent
## Acculturation gap: what we do, what we avoid
## Legal-help routes (immigration, benefits) — referral only
## Safety and crisis routes
## Review: 30 / 60 / 90 days
```

## Verification

- [ ] No immigration status or entry history appears anywhere in the plan.
- [ ] Every immigration or benefits question routes to named legal help; no advice given.
- [ ] One dominant barrier is named, with the reason it outranks the others.
- [ ] Trust deposits are small, countable actions, not intentions.
- [ ] The ecosystem map lists alternatives and fit criteria for each need.
- [ ] No child interprets for a parent anywhere in the plan.
- [ ] Clinician triggers are specific and describe behaviour, not diagnoses.
- [ ] Review dates are set.

## False-Positive Prevention

| Misfire | What it looks like | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Fear read as non-compliance | "Family refuses to engage with services" | Name the barrier; build trust; ask what they fear. |
| Worker gives legal reassurance | "WIC won't affect your green card" | Route to accredited legal help every time. |
| Labelling instead of describing | "Father is traumatised and paranoid" | "Said he checks the door locks several times each night." |
| Trauma story as entry ticket | Asking about the journey at visit one | Safety and predictability first; clinicians take history. |
| Child as the family's voice | Teen books all appointments and interprets | Protect the parent's role; use professional interpreters. |
| One culture assumed | "Syrian families prefer…" | Ask this family; each family differs. |
| Recording status "just in case" | Notes say "undocumented father" | Not recorded unless a rule requires it. |

## Adaptations

- **Infant or toddler:** prioritise a medical home, immunisations, and developmental
  checks; postpartum mood checks for the parent with a clinician.
- **Teen:** school placement, credit transfer, work rules for minors, and identity
  stress; offer the teen their own time with the worker.
- **Unaccompanied minor or sponsored child:** legal questions go to the child's
  legal service provider.
- **Neurodivergent child or parent:** explain that screening is not diagnosis;
  interpreters at every meeting.
- **Low literacy or intellectual disability:** pictures, voice notes in their
  language, a one-page plan.
- **Mixed-status family:** legal help explains eligibility trade-offs.
- **LGBTQ+ family member:** check community safety before linking to a diaspora group.

## Example Output

```
# Family Support Plan — Family H. (mother A., father M., Y. 10, N. 4)
Written 6 Oct   Reviews 5 Nov / 5 Dec / 5 Jan
Language: Arabic (Levantine), video interpreter each visit

## Family's priorities (their words)
A.: "School for my son, and a doctor who speaks Arabic for the little one."
M.: "Work papers questions — I don't know who to trust."

## Must-nots
No status questions or records. No work-permit or benefits advice.
Y. never interprets. Never "no one will tell immigration."

## Dominant barrier: fear of systems
Named for all 3 needs. A.: "Last time an office asked many questions and
we left." Outranks transport (bus works) and cost (sliding fee).

## Trust deposits (count at each review)
1. Arrive within 10 minutes of the agreed time.
2. Same female interpreter (A. asked).
3. Read my notes aloud at the end of each visit.
4. Explain each form, line by line, before signing.
5. Give a written note in Arabic: "What I write down and what I never do."

## Service map
| Need | Recommended | Alternatives | Fit | Status |
| School for Y. | district welcome centre | direct enrolment at school | interpreter at enrolment; no status question per district — verified | booked 9 Oct |
| Doctor for N. (4) | community health centre | children's clinic | Arabic-speaking staff Tue/Thu; sliding fee | booked 14 Oct |
| Work-permit questions | legal aid immigration clinic | nonprofit accredited rep | free consult; Arabic interpreter | M. to call 8 Oct |
| Food | WIC (for N.) / food pantry | mosque pantry | M.'s benefit question → legal clinic first | waiting on legal answer |

## Trauma
Now: same visit day and time, clear agenda. M. said he sleeps 3–4 hours a
night since spring. Offered referral to refugee-health clinic, consent
given 6 Oct. Refer same day if anyone talks about dying (988, Arabic).

## Acculturation
School app set up in Arabic on A.'s phone; teachers reached via the school
interpreter, not Y.

## Legal-help routes
Work permits, status, benefits → legal aid clinic, Wednesdays, Arabic
interpreter. I help book; I do not interpret the answer.

## Safety routes
911; 988; DV Hotline 1-800-799-7233; county child-protection hotline.
```

## Techniques Used

- **DP-04 Must-Not Constraints** — no status questions or records, no immigration or benefits advice, no child interpreters, no unkeepable promises.
- **DP-06 Dominant Driver Identification** — names the one barrier that blocks the most services and plans around it.
- **DP-18 Trust Deposits Definition** — 3–5 small, countable actions that build trust with a family that fears systems.
- **DS-30 Ecosystem Mapping** — a service map with alternatives and family-specific fit criteria for each need.
