# Interpreter-Mediated Family Session

**Objective:** Plan and run one session through a professional interpreter so the
family hears everything the worker says, the worker hears everything the family
says, and no family member has to carry the conversation for the others. The output
is a session plan: booking, interpreter pre-brief, seating and speech rules, consent
script, disclosure plan, and debrief.

**When to Use:**
- A parent prefers a language other than the worker's, or uses sign language.
- The family has been "managing" through a child, a cousin, or a partner.
- The session is high-stakes: intake, consent, a safety concern, a plan with court or
  agency conditions, a closure.
- Only a phone or video interpreter is available.
- **Not this prompt if** you are a **teacher** planning outreach to multilingual
  families — use
  `domain-education-teaching/instructor/reporting-communication/teaching_multilingual_family_outreach.md`.
  A **licensed clinician** doing a cultural formulation →
  `domain-psychology/intake-assessment/psychology_cultural_formulation_interview_drafter.md`.
  A **parent** planning the family's home language →
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/identity-culture/parenting_multilingual_heritage_language_plan.md`.
  Whether a family practice is a difference or a safety issue →
  `domain-parenting/family-support-professional/culturally-responsive/parenting_cultural_practice_vs_safety_check.md`.

## Safeguarding, Consent, and Scope

- **Never use a child as an interpreter** for anything substantive — not a sibling,
  not the child the session is about. The only exception is a true emergency while a
  professional is reached, for the minimum words needed.
- **No relatives, friends, or partners** for consent, safety questions, or plans. If
  the family brings someone, thank them and use the professional too. A partner never
  interprets questions about partner violence.
- **Immediate danger** → 911 or the local emergency number first. **Suicide talk** →
  988 (US; interpreters available). Partner violence → National DV Hotline
  1-800-799-7233. Possible ingestion → Poison Control 1-800-222-1222. Outside the
  US: local emergency number, findahelpline.com.
- **Mandated reporting is the worker's duty, not the interpreter's.** Who reports,
  the threshold (reasonable suspicion, not proof), and timelines vary by state,
  province, country, and agency — verify locally. In most US states the duty is
  personal and is not discharged by telling a supervisor; consultation must never
  delay a report or an emergency call. Report to the state or county
  child-protection hotline or law enforcement (Childhelp 1-800-422-4453 can help find
  it). Do not investigate, interrogate, photograph injuries, or promise secrecy.
  Record exact words *as interpreted*, what you saw, when, and who you told.
- **Consent before disclosure.** Say the limits of confidentiality through the
  interpreter at the start, then check with teach-back, not "Do you understand?"
  Written release before sharing with another agency, except where law requires or
  permits it — verify locally.
- **Does not diagnose.** Mislabelling risk is higher through an interpreter. Record
  what was said and seen — never "seems paranoid" or "cognitively delayed."
- **Scope.** The worker supports, refers, and follows up; clinicians assess and treat;
  immigration and legal questions go to legal help. US programs receiving federal
  funds generally owe free, meaningful language access — your agency's policy says
  how; verify locally.

## Inputs / Context

De-identify everything you paste into an AI tool: initials or roles, no names,
addresses, dates of birth, or case numbers, unless your agency has approved the tool
for client data.

1. **Who will be present:** adults, children, roles; who holds legal decisions.
2. **Language:** language *and* dialect; literacy in any language; sign language
   (and whether a Deaf interpreter is needed).
3. **Purpose and stakes:** intake, consent, safety, plan, routine visit.
4. **Interpreter options:** in person, video, phone; vendor; time booked.
5. **Community size:** might the interpreter know the family?
6. **Family preferences:** interpreter gender; someone they trust or want to avoid.

## Method

1. **Set the stakes, then the mode (DP-05).** High stakes (consent, safety
   questions, a report, court or agency conditions, closure): qualified interpreter,
   in person or video. Medium (plan review, a parenting topic): video or phone. Low
   (rescheduling): phone. Book 1.5 to 2 times the usual length.
2. **Hold the must-nots (DP-04).** No child interpreters. No relatives, friends, or
   partners for substantive talk. No English side talk in front of the family. No
   asking the interpreter's opinion of the family. No leaving the interpreter alone
   with the family. No "just summarise that" — every word is interpreted.
3. **Write the pre-brief (NE-20).** Five to ten minutes before, family absent. A
   self-contained package: purpose, who is present, topics, terms you will use
   ("release of information", "mandated reporter"), what to do if they know the
   family, how to flag a cultural point, and a warning about distressing content.
4. **Seat for direct speech (DS-01).** Use the brief–session–debrief model for
   interpreted work. Face the parent; interpreter beside and slightly behind you, or in
   a triangle. Speak to the parent in first person: "How are you sleeping?" not "Ask
   her how she sleeps." The interpreter uses first person too.
5. **Open with roles and consent.** Script: "This is [interpreter]. Their job is to say
   everything we each say, in both languages, and keep it private, like me. Some
   things I cannot keep private. If I think a child is being hurt or someone is in
   danger, I must tell child protection. If a court orders it, I may have to share
   notes. I won't share with other agencies without your written permission unless
   the law requires it. Can you tell me in your own words what I just said?"
6. **Pace and check.** One or two sentences, then pause. No idioms, jokes, or
   acronyms. Teach-back after each key point. Read forms aloud through the
   interpreter before anyone signs.
7. **Keep interpreter and cultural broker separate.** The interpreter conveys meaning.
   If a word does not carry, they say to both sides: "Interpreter speaking: this word
   can mean X or Y." They do not explain the family to you in private. If you need
   cultural context, book a cultural broker in a separate, declared role.
8. **Handle a disclosure in the room.** Keep the interpreter interpreting. Use open
   prompts ("Tell me more"). Record the exact interpreted words and the interpreter
   ID. Make the report yourself.
9. **Debrief (5 minutes).** Words that did not carry, dialect fit, and how the
   interpreter is after hard content. Never ask their view of the family's honesty.

## Output Format

```
# Interpreted Session Plan — [family initials], [date]
Stakes: [high/medium/low]  Mode: [in person/video/phone]  Length: [..]
Language + dialect: [..]   Interpreter: [vendor, ID]   Preferences met: [..]

## Must-nots for this session
## Interpreter pre-brief
## Seating / camera setup
## Opening: roles + consent + confidentiality limits + teach-back
## Agenda (short segments; forms read aloud)
## If a disclosure happens: steps, who reports, what to record
## Crisis routes (with interpreter access)
## Debrief questions
## What the note records about the interpretation
```

## Verification

- [ ] No child, relative, friend, or partner interprets any substantive part.
- [ ] Mode and length match the stakes.
- [ ] The pre-brief stands alone and lists key terms.
- [ ] Confidentiality limits come before any disclosure and are checked by teach-back.
- [ ] Scripts use first person and short segments.
- [ ] The disclosure plan names the worker as reporter and the local route.
- [ ] The note records language, mode, and interpreter ID.
- [ ] No diagnostic label appears for any family member.

## False-Positive Prevention

| Misfire | What it looks like | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| "The daughter speaks English" | 12-year-old interprets the intake | Never; book a professional, reschedule if needed. |
| Labelling instead of describing | "Mum seems slow, answers don't make sense" | Record words and pauses; check dialect, mode, trust. |
| Nodding read as understanding | "Yes" to every point | Teach-back; "yes" may mean "I hear you." |
| Interpreter as cultural expert | "Is this normal in their culture?" after the visit | Ask the family; book a declared cultural broker. |
| Phone for everything | Safety questions on speakerphone in a kitchen | Match mode to stakes. |
| Partner as interpreter | Husband interprets private safety questions | Professional only; each adult alone. |
| Silence read as resistance | "She refused to engage" | Check interpreter fit, dialect, gender, and fear first. |

## Adaptations

- **Deaf parent:** certified sign-language interpreter, plus a Deaf interpreter for
  non-standard signing; good lighting. No lip-reading or notes for substantive talk.
- **Small language community:** offer a remote interpreter from another area; the
  family can ask for someone else.
- **Parent with an intellectual disability or low literacy:** shorter segments, more
  teach-back, pictures; no form signed unread.
- **Neurodivergent parent:** agreed breaks, extra processing time, a written summary in
  their language.
- **Teen present:** the teen speaks for themselves and never interprets for a parent.
- **Tribal or Indigenous family:** ask about language preference and tribal liaisons;
  in the US, ICWA may apply if custody arises — route to the caseworker or legal help.
- **Telehealth or rural:** test the link; agree what happens if it drops.
- **Refugee family:** see
  `domain-parenting/family-support-professional/culturally-responsive/parenting_immigrant_family_support_plan.md`.

## Example Output

```
# Interpreted Session Plan — Family A. (mother F., father K., S. 9, R. 3)
Date 14 Oct, 10:00   Purpose: intake + consent   Stakes: HIGH
Mode: video interpreter, 90 min (usual 50)
Language: Dari (Kabul)   Interpreter ID 4471, female (F. asked)

## Must-nots
- S. (9) is at school; if home, S. plays in the next room.
- K. does not interpret for F. (he offered on the phone).
- No English side talk. No private chat with the interpreter about the family.

## Pre-brief (09:50, family not on the call)
"Intake for a voluntary home-visiting program. Mother, father, toddler.
Topics: consent, strengths, needs, private safety questions to each parent
alone. Terms: 'home visitor', 'release of information', 'mandated
reporter'. If you know this family, tell me now and we'll rebook. Flag any
word that doesn't carry: 'Interpreter speaking…'. Some questions are about
violence at home."

## Seating
Laptop facing both parents; my face and the interpreter both visible. I
speak to F. and K., not to the screen corner.

## Opening (first person, 1–2 sentences at a time)
"I'm Dana. I visit families with young children. Everything is said in both
languages. What we say stays private, with limits. If I think a child is
being hurt or someone is in danger, I must tell child protection."
Teach-back: "So I know I explained it well — what will I keep private,
and what will I have to share?"
F.: "You keep our talk, but if a child is hurt you tell the office."  ✔

## Agenda
1. What the family wants from visits (their words)        10 min
2. Strengths, what is going well                           10 min
3. Needs: housing, food, clinic, school for S.             15 min
4. Private questions — K. steps out; then F. steps out      20 min
5. Consent form read aloud, questions, signatures           15 min
6. Next visit                                                5 min

## If a disclosure happens
Keep the interpreter going; "Tell me more about that." No digging. Record
exact interpreted words + ID 4471. I report myself (county hotline saved
in my phone). Supervisor told after, not instead.

## Crisis routes
911 (dispatcher interpreter); 988; DV Hotline 1-800-799-7233.

## Debrief (5 min)
Any words that didn't carry? Dialect OK? How are you after the safety part?
Note: "Interpreted in Dari, video, ID 4471. F. asked for female interpreter."
```

## Techniques Used

- **DS-01 Framework Application** — the brief–session–debrief model for interpreted work structures the session.
- **DP-04 Must-Not Constraints** — no child, relative, friend, or partner interpreters; no side talk; no interpreter opinions.
- **NE-20 Third-Party Handoff Package** — a self-contained interpreter pre-brief.
- **DP-05 Stakes-Based Gate Policy** — stakes set interpreter mode, qualification, and booked time.
