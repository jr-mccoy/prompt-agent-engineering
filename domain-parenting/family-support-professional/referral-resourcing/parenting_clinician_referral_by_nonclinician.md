# Referring to a Licensed Clinician When You Are Not One

**Objective:** Help a non-clinical worker act on a worry about a parent or child —
mood, anxiety, substance use, unusual thinking, or development — by writing down what
they saw and heard, deciding how fast a clinician is needed, talking with the parent
honestly, getting consent, and sending a referral the clinician can act on.

**When to Use:**
- Something has changed: a parent has stopped sleeping or eating, talks about being a
  burden, smells of alcohol at morning visits, or a child has lost words.
- You are not sure whether this is "a bad week" or needs a clinician, and how fast.
- You know a referral is needed but not how to raise it without the parent feeling
  judged or afraid of losing their child.
- **Not this prompt if** you are a **licensed clinician** writing to another clinician —
  use `domain-psychology/care-coordination/psychology_referral_letter_generator.md`. A
  **caregiver** choosing a specialist for their own child →
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/cross-age/parenting_when_to_seek_professional_help_all_ages.md`.
  A lay mentor setting a standing scope-and-referral policy →
  `domain-discipleship/mentor-equipping/discipleship_mentor_boundaries_and_referral.md`.
  Explaining a developmental screener result →
  `domain-parenting/family-support-professional/intake-assessment/parenting_developmental_screen_results_talk.md`.
  The referral is made and the family has not connected →
  `domain-parenting/family-support-professional/referral-resourcing/parenting_referral_follow_through_barriers.md`.

## Safeguarding, Consent, and Scope

- **You do not diagnose.** Record what you saw, the person's own words, how often, and
  how long. "Seems depressed" becomes "said she has not slept more than 3 hours a night
  for two weeks and has stopped going out." "Autistic" becomes "does not turn to his
  name; 4 words at 24 months, down from 10 at 18 months." The clinician decides what it is.
- **Emergency first.** Immediate danger, an attempt, an overdose, or a threat to harm
  someone → **911** (or local emergency number) now. Suicide talk → **988** (US, call or
  text). Possible poisoning or overdose → **Poison Control 1-800-222-1222**. Partner
  violence → **National DV Hotline 1-800-799-7233**. Pregnant or postpartum parent →
  **National Maternal Mental Health Hotline 1-833-852-6262** (US). Substance-use
  treatment → **SAMHSA 1-800-662-4357**. Outside the US: local emergency number,
  findahelpline.com. No supervisor approval is needed for an emergency call.
- **Mandated reporting is a separate duty.** A referral never replaces a
  child-protection report. Who must report, the threshold (reasonable suspicion or
  reasonable cause to believe, not proof), timelines, and whether a supervisor consult
  counts **vary by state, province, country, and agency — verify locally**. In most US
  states the duty is personal and is not discharged by telling a supervisor;
  consultation must never delay a report. Report to the state or county
  child-protection hotline or law enforcement (Childhelp 1-800-422-4453 can help find
  the number). Do not investigate, interrogate, photograph injuries, or promise secrecy.
  Record exact words, what you saw, when, and who you told. Decide with your supervisor,
  case by case, whether telling the family about a report is safe; never when it could
  endanger anyone.
- **Consent and confidentiality.** Say the limits of confidentiality (reporting,
  imminent danger, court orders) in plain words before sensitive talk. Get a **written
  release** before sharing anything with the clinician, unless law requires or permits
  sharing without one (verify locally). Share the minimum. 42 CFR Part 2 (substance-use
  treatment), HIPAA, FERPA, or GDPR may apply — verify locally. Families can often read
  their records; write as if they will.
- **Stay in role.** You support, refer, and follow up. No medication advice, no therapy.

## Inputs / Context

1. **De-identify first.** Use initials or roles (M., "mother," "baby, 7 weeks"). No
   names, addresses, dates of birth, or case numbers, unless your agency has approved
   this AI tool for client data.
2. **The worry, then the observations behind it:** what was seen or said, dates, how
   often, how long, what changed.
3. **Who:** parent or child, age, pregnancy or postpartum, supports.
4. **Safety facts:** suicide or self-harm talk, threats, weapons or medications at
   hand, substance use around children, partner violence.
5. **Existing care:** pediatrician, OB, therapist, waitlists, past help.
6. **Preferences and access:** language, cost, transport, phone, times, culture or faith.
7. **Your agency:** referral pathways, release forms, reporting policy.

## Method

1. **Translate the worry into observations (DP-04).** Each item: behaviour, quote,
   frequency, duration, change from before. Must-nots: no diagnostic label; no guessing
   at causes; no medication advice; no promise that "nobody else will know"; no threat
   that a refusal will be reported.
2. **Sort urgency by time window (AG-19).**
   - **Emergency (minutes):** suicide talk with a plan, intent, or means at hand; a
     recent attempt; threats to harm the child or others; seeing or hearing things with
     danger to self or child; overdose or severe withdrawal (seizure, confusion); a
     child who cannot be kept safe. → 911 / 988 now.
   - **Same day:** suicidal thoughts without a plan; a postpartum parent with
     frightening thoughts of harm coming to the baby; no sleep even when the baby sleeps
     for a week or more, with hopelessness; a parent intoxicated while sole carer; a
     child who has lost words or skills. → clinician, OB, or pediatrician today, with
     consent.
   - **Routine (days to weeks):** low mood, worry, or drinking the parent calls a
     problem; development behind on a screener. → referral this week.
3. **Match the gate to the stakes (DP-05).** Emergency: act, then tell the supervisor.
   Same day: supervisor told today; any reporting question is decided under your local
   rules, not deferred. Routine: next supervision, sooner if it rises a tier.
4. **Ask the direct question.** At any hint of suicide: "Sometimes when people feel
   this low they think about killing themselves. Are you having thoughts like that?"
   Asking does not plant the idea. Record the answer.
5. **Talk with the parent.** Ask permission, say what you noticed, ask what they make
   of it, offer choices. "Can I share something I've noticed? You've slept about 3
   hours a night since the baby came, and today you said you feel like a burden. What
   do you make of that?" Then: "A doctor or counsellor can help with this. Would you
   like me to help set it up?" Name the fear: "Getting help is something good parents
   do. On its own, it isn't a reason anyone takes a baby."
6. **Get consent for exactly what is shared.** Fill in the release together: which
   clinician, what information, how long, how to withdraw. Offer to make the call
   together (a warm referral).
7. **Build the referral package (NE-20).** One page: observations and quotes, tier and
   why, what has been tried, safety facts, family preferences and access needs, consent
   scope, your contact, and the feedback requested (attended yes/no, under the release).
8. **Set the follow-up.** Agree a date to check whether the appointment happened.

## Output Format

```
# Referral plan — [parent/child role, age]   Worker: [role]   Date: [..]

## Observations (behaviour | quote | how often | how long | change)
## Urgency tier: [Emergency / Same day / Routine] — because [..]
## Immediate actions taken (calls, who, time)
## Supervisor gate: [who, when, what was decided]
## Mandated-reporting question: [not indicated / consulted / report made — time, route]
## Conversation with parent: what I said, what they said, their choice
## Consent: release signed [Y/N], to whom, what, expires
## Referral package (to clinician)
   Reason (observations, quotes) | Tier | Tried so far | Safety facts |
   Family preferences/access | Consent scope | Feedback requested | Contact
## Follow-up: date, method, what counts as connected
```

## Verification

- [ ] Every concern is an observation, quote, frequency, or duration; no label appears.
- [ ] The urgency tier names the observation that set it.
- [ ] Any suicide hint has a direct question and the answer recorded.
- [ ] Emergency actions did not wait for supervisor approval.
- [ ] The mandated-reporting question is addressed separately from the referral.
- [ ] A signed release covers exactly what is shared, or a legal basis for sharing
      without one is named and verified locally.
- [ ] The referral package includes family preferences and access needs.
- [ ] A dated follow-up check is set.

## False-Positive Prevention

| Misfire | What it looks like | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Labelling instead of describing | "Mum has postpartum depression" in the note | Write the sleep hours, quotes, and weeks; the clinician names it. |
| Everything is an emergency | 911 for a parent who is tired and tearful | Use the tiers; emergency means danger now. |
| Waiting for the routine slot | Suicidal thoughts logged for next month's supervision | Any suicide talk is at least same day. |
| Referral as a report substitute | "I referred to counselling, so I don't need to call" | Referral and reporting are separate duties; check both. |
| Difference read as disorder | Quiet, avoids eye contact, prays aloud — flagged as concerning | Ask about culture, faith, and neurodivergence; refer on behaviour that causes distress or risk. |
| Over-sharing | Whole case file sent to the counsellor | Minimum necessary, within the signed release. |
| Referral as the end | "Referred" closes the task | Follow up until connected or declined. |

## Adaptations

- **Infant or toddler:** the pediatrician is often first; US Part C early intervention
  takes parent referrals.
- **Teen:** minor-consent rules for mental-health and substance treatment vary — verify
  locally; include the teen in the conversation.
- **Pregnant or postpartum parent:** route through the OB or midwife too.
- **Parent with an intellectual disability or low literacy:** read the release aloud;
  check with "Tell me back what this lets me share."
- **Interpreter needed:** a professional interpreter, never a child or relative.
- **Rural or telehealth:** check phone, data, and a private space before booking video.
- **Tribal or Indigenous family:** offer tribal health services; ICWA may apply in the
  US if a child-welfare case follows.
- **Neurodivergent parent or child:** describe distress and support needs, not eye
  contact, speech style, or stimming.

## Example Output

```
# Referral plan — mother (R.), baby girl 7 weeks   Worker: home visitor
Date: 3 Oct 2026, visit 10:00–11:15

## Observations
- R.: "I can't sleep even when she sleeps." Sleeping about 3 hrs a night,
  every night since about 18 Sep (15 days).
- Crying during the visit twice; said "I'm useless at this" and "they'd
  be better off with my mum."
- Not left the flat since 22 Sep except one clinic visit (her words).
- Baby: feeding 7–8 times a day, 2 wet nappies during visit, R. responds
  to crying within a minute, holds her close. Strength: warm handling.

## Direct question
Me: "Sometimes when people feel this low they think about killing
themselves. Are you having thoughts like that?"
R.: "No. I'd never. I just feel like I'm failing." No plan, no intent.
Gave 988 and the maternal mental health line (1-833-852-6262) on a card.

## Urgency tier: SAME DAY
Because: 15 days of ~3 hrs sleep with hopelessness, postpartum. Not
emergency: no suicidal thoughts, baby fed and safe, partner home by 18:00.

## Supervisor gate
Called supervisor K. at 11:30; agreed same-day OB contact; no
child-protection concern identified (baby's needs met, no injury, no
unsafe care seen). Reporting question considered and recorded.

## Conversation
Asked permission; named sleep and "useless" quotes. R. said she thought
it was "normal new-mum stuff." Offered: OB call today, GP, or the
hotline. R. chose OB. Said: "Getting help is something good parents do.
On its own, it isn't a reason anyone takes a baby."

## Consent
Release signed 11:10 — OB practice only; shares sleep, mood quotes,
visit date; expires 3 Jan 2027; R. can withdraw by telling me.

## Referral package (phoned OB nurse 11:45)
Reason: quotes and sleep hours above. Tried: partner doing night
feeds 2 nights (no change). Safety: no suicidal thoughts; baby fed.
Preferences: phone, afternoons. Feedback: appointment date.
OB nurse booked a call for 15:30 today.

## Follow-up
Text R. 16:30 today: "Did the call happen?" Visit 6 Oct. Connected =
first appointment completed.
```

## Techniques Used

- **AG-19 Time-Critical Response Protocol** — emergency (minutes), same-day, and routine windows, each with its own action.
- **DP-05 Stakes-Based Gate Policy** — supervisor involvement scaled to tier; emergencies act first and inform after.
- **DP-04 Must-Not Constraints** — no labels, no medication advice, no secrecy promises, no threats tied to refusal.
- **NE-20 Third-Party Handoff Package** — one-page referral a clinician can act on without calling back for basics.
