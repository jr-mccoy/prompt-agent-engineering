# Referral Follow-Through

**Objective:** Turn a list of "referred" into a list of "connected, declined, or still
working on it — and why": a tracking line, the main blocker, an agreed next step, a
check-in date set by the stakes, and, for a decline, a respectful record plus the
safety duties that continue.

**When to Use:**
- You made referrals weeks ago (food bank, early intervention, counselling, housing,
  substance-use treatment) and do not know if any happened.
- The family says "yes" every visit and nothing moves, or a service closed the
  referral because it "could not reach" them.
- A family has said no, and you need to know what that means for your role.
- **Not this prompt if** you have not yet made a clinical referral and need to decide
  urgency and raise it with the parent — use
  `domain-parenting/family-support-professional/referral-resourcing/parenting_clinician_referral_by_nonclinician.md`.
  A **clinic** responding to a positive social-needs screen →
  `domain-healthcare-clinical/prompts/allied-health/allied_health_sdoh_screening_response.md`.
  A **clinician** transferring a client to another clinician →
  `domain-psychology/care-coordination/psychology_warm_handoff_narrative.md`.
  A **caregiver** choosing a specialist themselves →
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/cross-age/parenting_when_to_seek_professional_help_all_ages.md`.
  Your **program** is deciding which incoming referrals to accept →
  `domain-parenting/family-support-professional/intake-assessment/parenting_program_referral_triage_waitlist.md`.

## Safeguarding, Consent, and Scope

- **You do not diagnose.** Barriers and reasons are written as the family's words and
  observable facts ("missed 3 calls from an unknown number; phone has no minutes until
  the 15th"), not labels ("non-compliant," "in denial," "unmotivated").
- **The family can decline voluntary services.** A clear, informed no is respected and
  recorded in their words. You do not pressure, shame, or make your own support
  conditional on accepting another service.
- **A decline does not lower the safety floor.** Your reporting duty, safety
  check-ins, and emergency responses continue whatever the family chooses. Who must
  report, the threshold (reasonable suspicion or reasonable cause to believe, not
  proof), timelines, and whether a supervisor consult counts **vary by state, province,
  country, and agency — verify locally**. In most US states the duty is personal and is
  not discharged by telling a supervisor; consultation must never delay a report.
  Report to the state or county child-protection hotline or law enforcement (Childhelp
  1-800-422-4453 can help find the number). Do not investigate, interrogate, photograph
  injuries, or promise secrecy; record exact words, what you saw, when, and who you
  told. Decide with your supervisor, case by case, whether telling the family about a
  report is safe; never when it could endanger anyone.
- **Crisis routes stay open.** Immediate danger → **911** or local emergency number.
  Suicide talk → **988** (US). Possible poisoning → **Poison Control 1-800-222-1222**.
  Partner violence → **National DV Hotline 1-800-799-7233**. Outside the US:
  findahelpline.com. A stalled clinical referral that now shows new risk goes back
  through urgency triage the same day.
- **Consent and privacy.** Say the limits of confidentiality (reporting, imminent
  danger, court orders) in plain words before sensitive talk. Feedback from a service
  ("did they attend?") needs a **signed release**, unless law requires or permits
  sharing without one — verify locally. Share the minimum. 42 CFR Part 2, FERPA, HIPAA,
  or GDPR may apply — verify locally. Families can often read their records.
- **No legal or immigration advice.** Benefit, status, or custody worries go to
  accredited legal help. Do not ask about or record documentation status unless a
  program legally requires it.

## Inputs / Context

1. **De-identify first.** Initials or roles, no names, addresses, dates of birth, or
   case numbers, unless your agency has approved this AI tool for client data.
2. **Each open referral:** service, reason (family's words), date, warm or cold,
   release signed, last known status.
3. **Since then:** calls, missed appointments, letters, what the family has said.
4. **Family context:** work, childcare, transport, phone, language, literacy,
   disability, past experience with agencies, current crises.
5. **Stakes:** linked to safety, a court or agency requirement, or a time-limited
   window (early intervention before age 3)?
6. **Service facts:** eligibility, cost, waitlist, hours, language access, phone options.

## Method

1. **Set a closed-loop status for each referral.** Made → contacted by service →
   booked → attended → ongoing; or not connected; or declined. "Referred" alone is not
   a status. Name the evidence (family report, service feedback under release, your
   own call).
2. **Set check-in timing by stakes (DP-05).**
   - **High** (linked to safety, suicide risk, partner violence, substance use while
     sole carer, a court-required step, a child's loss of skills): check in 2–3 working
     days; not connected by 1 week → supervisor review and a written safety plan.
   - **Medium** (routine mental-health care, early intervention, housing): check at 1
     and 3 weeks.
   - **Low** (parent group, clothing bank): check at the next visit.
3. **Analyse barriers across dimensions (RT-02).** Score each 0 (not a barrier), 1
   (slows it), or 2 (blocks it), with the family's words as evidence:
   - **Practical:** transport, childcare, work hours, phone minutes, forms.
   - **Access:** waitlist, eligibility, cost, documents requested.
   - **Language and literacy:** no interpreter, written-only instructions.
   - **Trust:** fear of child protection, police, or immigration; past bad experience.
   - **Stigma and identity:** "therapy is for crazy people"; no culturally matched or
     affirming option.
   - **Priority and fit:** a bigger crisis this week; the service is not what they want.
4. **Name the dominant barrier (DP-06).** One sentence: "The block is [X], because
   [evidence]; fixing [Y] alone would not connect them." Act on it first. A bus pass
   does not help a parent afraid the clinic will call child protection.
5. **Remove the barrier with the family.** Ask "What would make this easier?" first.
   Then match the move: a three-way call, a ride, an interpreter, a phone appointment.
6. **Rebuild trust where fear is the barrier (DP-18).** Pick 3–5 small, countable
   deposits: explain exactly what the service shares; keep every callback time;
   introduce the provider by name; go to the first visit if allowed; never use the
   referral as leverage. Say: "This is voluntary. You can stop any time. I'll still be
   here either way."
7. **Handle a decline clearly.** Check it is informed (what the service is, cost, how
   to reach it later). Record it in their words, dated. Ask permission to raise it
   again on a set date. Write down what continues (reporting duty, check-ins, crisis
   routes), and whether a court or agency requirement means the family should hear the
   consequences honestly from the right person.
8. **Close the loop in the record.** Each referral ends as connected, declined, or not
   connected after reasonable efforts (listed), with date and next step.

## Tracker
| Referral | Reason (family's words) | Made | Stakes | Status | Evidence | Next check |

## Barrier analysis (per stalled referral)
| Dimension | 0/1/2 | Evidence (family's words / facts) |
Dominant barrier: [..] because [..]

## Plan to remove it
Action | who | by when | agreed by family [Y/N]

## Trust deposits (if trust is a barrier)

## Declines
Service | date | family's words | informed? | ok to re-offer on [..]

## Safety floor (continues regardless)
Reporting duty | safety check-ins | crisis routes | court/agency requirements

## Supervisor gate (high-stakes items not connected at 1 week)
```

## Verification

- [ ] No referral has "referred" as its only status.
- [ ] Every referral has a stakes level and a matching check-in date.
- [ ] Each stalled referral has all six dimensions scored with evidence.
- [ ] One dominant barrier is named; the first action targets it.
- [ ] Declines are in the family's words, marked informed or not.
- [ ] The safety floor is filled in, including for families who declined.
- [ ] Service feedback is covered by a signed release or a verified legal basis.
- [ ] No label such as "non-compliant" or "unmotivated" appears.

## False-Positive Prevention

| Misfire | What it looks like | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Labelling instead of describing | "Mum is non-compliant with referrals" | Write what happened: missed 3 calls, no phone minutes. |
| Referral counted as service | Monthly report shows 12 referrals, 0 known outcomes | Track to attended; "referred" is a step, not a result. |
| Fixing the visible barrier | Bus pass given; parent still afraid of the clinic | Name the dominant barrier first; fear outranks fares. |
| Treating "yes" as agreement | Parent agrees each visit, never goes | A yes with no action often hides a barrier or a polite no; ask. |
| Pressure dressed as support | "Go, or I'll have to mention it" | Voluntary is voluntary; reporting is a separate duty, never a threat. |
| Decline read as the end of duties | Referral declined, safety check-ins stopped | The safety floor continues. |
| Service closure taken at face value | "They didn't respond, file closed" | Ask how the service tried: phone only? English only? |

## Adaptations

- **Infant or toddler:** US Part C eligibility ends at age 3; treat a stalled referral
  as medium-to-high stakes.
- **Teen:** may consent to some services alone — verify locally; ask the teen what
  blocks them.
- **Parent with an intellectual disability or low literacy:** calls and picture
  reminders instead of letters; book together.
- **Language:** confirm a professional interpreter; never a child or relative.
- **Rural:** count travel time and data costs; look for phone or mobile services.
- **Immigrant or refugee family:** fear of systems is reasonable; explain what each
  service shares; route status worries to accredited legal help.
- **Tribal or Indigenous family:** offer tribal or Indigenous-led services; ICWA may
  apply in the US if a child-welfare case is involved.
- **LGBTQ+ family:** check the service is affirming before referring.

```

## Barrier analysis — Early intervention
| Practical  | 2 | Phone out of minutes; ignores unknown numbers |
| Access     | 0 | Evaluation at no cost (J. had not been told) |

On time every visit. Read the EI leaflet with J. Say exactly what EI

Not required: no high-stakes item open. EI rises to high if not

## Techniques Used

- **RT-02 Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework** — six barrier dimensions scored 0–2 with evidence.
- **DP-06 Dominant Driver Identification** — one named barrier drives the first action.
- **DP-18 Trust Deposits Definition** — small, countable acts that rebuild trust where fear blocks connection.
- **DP-05 Stakes-Based Gate Policy** — check-in timing and supervisor review scale with the stakes.
