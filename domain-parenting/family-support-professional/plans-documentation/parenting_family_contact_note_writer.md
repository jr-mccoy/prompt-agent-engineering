# Family Contact Note Writer

**Objective:** Turn rough notes into a contact note that is accurate, fair, and
useful to the next reader: facts, observations (the family's words in quotes),
labelled worker interpretation, strengths, and next steps. It leaves out what does
not belong, reads well if the family sees it, and never stands in for a
child-protection report.

**When to Use:**
- After a home or office visit, call, video session, text exchange, or group contact.
- Your notes read as opinions ("chaotic home," "uncooperative").
- Something worrying happened and you need to know what goes in the note, what goes
  in a report, and in what order.
- **Not this prompt if** you are a licensed clinician writing a therapy progress
  note — use `domain-psychology/documentation/psychology_dap_progress_note.md`; a
  clinician's note about a call with a school or doctor →
  `domain-psychology/documentation/psychology_collateral_contact_note.md`. A parent
  keeping their own co-parenting log →
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/custody/parenting_custody_communication_log_template.md`.
  What to do in the moment at a worrying home visit →
  `domain-parenting/family-support-professional/home-visit-fieldwork/parenting_home_visit_child_safety_concern.md`.
  Notes for supervised family time → `domain-parenting/family-support-professional/foster-kinship-adoption/parenting_family_time_visit_supervision.md`.

## Safeguarding, Consent, and Scope

- **The note does not diagnose.** Write observable behaviour, the family's words,
  and frequency or duration. "Seems depressed" becomes "said she has slept no more
  than 3 hours a night for two weeks and has not left the flat since Monday."
- **Report first, write second.** If what you saw or heard gives reasonable
  suspicion (reasonable cause to believe — not proof) of abuse or neglect, make the
  report before finishing the note. Immediate danger → 911 or the local emergency
  number first. Reports go to the child-protection hotline or law enforcement
  (Childhelp 1-800-422-4453 can help find the number). Who is a mandated reporter,
  the threshold, timelines, and whether a supervisor consult counts **vary by state,
  province, country, and agency — verify locally**. In most US states the duty is
  personal and is not discharged by telling a supervisor; a consult must never
  delay a report or emergency call. Do not investigate, interrogate, photograph
  injuries, or promise secrecy. Decide with your supervisor, case by case, whether
  telling the family about a report is safe — never when it could endanger anyone.
- **Clinician triggers to record and act on the same day:** suicide or self-harm
  talk (988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US); a parent describing voices or not
  sleeping for days; a child losing skills; possible poisoning (Poison Control
  1-800-222-1222); partner violence (National DV Hotline 1-800-799-7233). Outside the
  US: local emergency number, findahelpline.com. The note records the referral and
  follow-up; the worker does not treat.
- **Consent and privacy.** The family heard the limits of confidentiality at the
  start of services ("I keep what you tell me private, except if a child or someone
  else may be in danger, or a court orders records"). Notes go to other agencies
  only with a signed release, unless law requires sharing — verify locally.
  Minimum-necessary content only. Families can often read their records. HIPAA,
  FERPA, 42 CFR Part 2, or GDPR-type rules may apply — verify locally.

## Inputs / Context

1. **De-identify what you paste.** Initials or roles ("Mum," "child J, 4"). No names,
   addresses, dates of birth, or case numbers unless your agency has approved the
   AI tool for client data. Put identifiers back in the official system yourself.
2. **Contact basics:** date, start and end time, type (home, office, phone, video,
   text), location, who was present (including interpreter).
3. **Purpose** of the contact and any plan goals it related to.
4. **Your rough notes** — as messy as they are, including exact phrases you remember.
5. **Anything worrying**, and what you have already done about it (report made? to
   whom, when, reference number?).
6. **Agency format and deadline** (often 24–72 hours — verify locally).

## Method

1. **Sort raw notes into four labelled layers (ST-03).** **Facts:** who, when,
   where, how long, what was done. **Observations:** what you saw and heard, in
   specifics. **Interpretation:** the worker's tentative view, marked as such and tied
   to an observation ("I wonder whether… because…"). **Plan:** next steps, who, when.
   Nothing moves from interpretation into facts.
2. **Quote rather than paraphrase.** Use quotation marks for important statements,
   attributed ("Mum said, '…'"). Note when words came through an interpreter. If you
   are not sure of the exact words, write "in substance" rather than inventing a
   quote.
3. **Record at least one specific strength.** Not "Mum is great," but "Mum noticed J
   rubbing his eyes, said 'You're tired, let's sit,' and he settled on her lap."
4. **Rewrite judgement into description (NE-16).** Show "current wording → revised
   wording" for each loaded phrase so the worker learns the move:
   - "Home was filthy" → "Dishes from several meals in the sink; floor sticky in the
     kitchen; bedrooms not seen."
   - "Dad was uncooperative" → "Dad said, 'I'm not talking about that today,' when
     asked about the school letter."
   - "Mum seemed high" → "Mum's speech was slow, her eyes closed twice mid-sentence;
     she said she had 'not slept.'"
   - "J is ADHD" → "J ran between rooms for most of the 40-minute visit and climbed
     the sofa back four times."
5. **Apply the must-not-record list (DP-04).** The note **must not** contain:
   diagnoses or labels you are not qualified to give; rumours or third-party claims
   without who said them; private details about people outside the case (the
   neighbour's affairs); immigration status unless your program requires it; detail
   from substance-use treatment covered by 42 CFR Part 2 without the right consent;
   your guesses about motive ("trying to manipulate me"); other clients' information;
   or anything recorded "off the record" — you cannot promise that, so say so
   beforehand.
6. **Read it as the family.** "Could I read this aloud to the parent unchanged?" If
   not, rewrite — but never remove a safety fact. Fair is not soft.
7. **Gate any concern by tier (DP-05).**
   - **Routine** — no concern: note as usual.
   - **Worry below threshold** — facts in the note; consult your supervisor the same
     day per policy; record the consult and the agreed follow-up.
   - **Reasonable suspicion** — report first (911 first for danger). Then record, in
     the place your agency specifies (note, incident form, or both): the child's or
     parent's exact words, what you saw, times, who you reported to, when, and the
     reference number, and whether the family was told and why or why not. Write
     facts; do not write "abuse confirmed."
8. **Finalise properly.** Write within the agency deadline. Mark late entries as
   late with the date written. Correct by dated addendum, never by deleting. Sign and
   date.

## Output Format

```
# Contact Note — [family initials]   Date: [..]  Time: [..–..]  Type: [..]
Present: [roles; interpreter yes/no, language]   Purpose: [.. / plan goal ..]

## Facts
## Observations (exact words in quotes)
## Strengths observed
## Worker interpretation (tentative; tied to observations)
## Plan / next steps (who, what, when)
## Safeguarding (only if applicable): tier [routine / worry / report]
   Exact words: ".."   Seen: [..]   Reported to: [..] at [time]  Ref: [..]
   Supervisor consulted: [..]   Family told: [yes/no — reason]
## Wording changes made: [current → revised]
Written: [date/time]   Late entry: [yes/no]   Worker: [role]
```

## Verification

- [ ] Facts, observations, interpretation, and plan are in separate labelled sections.
- [ ] Every interpretation is marked tentative and points to an observation.
- [ ] Important statements are in quotes and attributed, or marked "in substance."
- [ ] At least one specific, observed strength is recorded.
- [ ] No diagnostic label or must-not-record item appears.
- [ ] If a report was made, the note records who, when, reference, and exact words — and the report time comes before the note time.
- [ ] Every sentence passes the "read it aloud to the parent" test.

## False-Positive Prevention

| Misfire | What it looks like | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Labelling instead of describing | "Mum is bipolar and neglectful" | Observable behaviour, words, frequency; no labels. |
| Interpretation dressed as fact | "Dad is drinking again" | Observations in Observations; "I wonder whether…" in Interpretation. |
| Softening away safety facts | Bruise left out "to keep trust" | Fair wording, all safety facts kept; report if threshold met. |
| The note as the report | "Concern noted in file" and nothing else | A note is not a report; report to the hotline or police first. |
| Invented quote | Paraphrase in quotation marks | Exact words or "in substance." |
| Over-recording | Neighbour's divorce, a relative's immigration status | Minimum necessary for the purpose. |
| Deficit-only note | Five problems, no strengths | Record a specific strength every time. |

## Adaptations

- **Interpreter-mediated contact:** name the interpreter's role and language; quotes
  are the interpreter's rendering.
- **Parent with an intellectual disability or low literacy:** if the family reads the
  record with you, use short sentences and read it aloud.
- **Neurodivergent child or parent:** describe behaviour and its context ("covered
  ears when the TV was loud") without judging stimming, eye contact, or flat tone.
- **Infant vs. teen:** for infants, record feeding, sleep, and interaction cues; for
  teens, record the teen's own words and note who else was present.
- **Tribal or Indigenous family:** use the family's terms for kin roles; in the US,
  flag possible ICWA relevance to your supervisor.

## Example Output

```
# Contact Note — Mum "D." (31), J (4), M (14 months)
Date: 1 Oct 2026   Time: 10:05–10:50   Type: home visit
Present: D., J, M, worker. No interpreter.   Purpose: Goal A1 (mornings)

## Facts
Visit at family's flat as scheduled. Reviewed morning picture chart.
Gave D. the nursery application form.

## Observations
Chart on fridge with 4 ticks this week. D. said, "Two mornings we made
it by 8:15. The other days M was up all night." J showed me the chart
and said, "I did shoes!" M crawled to D. three times; each time D. picked
her up and talked to her. Kitchen: dishes from several meals in sink.
D. said, "I'm so tired I could cry."

## Strengths observed
D. praised J specifically: "You put your shoes on by yourself."
D. responded to M each time within seconds.

## Worker interpretation (tentative)
I wonder whether broken nights with M are the main barrier to the
morning goal, since both on-time days followed nights M slept through.

## Plan
Worker: send sleep-resource list and ask D. if she wants to raise M's
night waking with the GP — by 3 Oct. D.: keep the chart; note M's
wake-ups for a week. Next visit 8 Oct, 10:00.

## Safeguarding
Tier: worry below threshold — D. said "I could cry"; asked directly
about thoughts of harming herself; D. said, "No, nothing like that,
just tired." Supervisor (K.) consulted 1 Oct 12:30; agreed: offer GP
or perinatal mood support at next visit; give 988 number today (given).
No report threshold met.

## Wording changes made
"Messy, chaotic kitchen" → "Dishes from several meals in sink."
"Mum is depressed" → "D. said, 'I'm so tired I could cry.'"
Written: 1 Oct 2026, 14:10   Late entry: no   Worker: home visitor
```

## Techniques Used

- **ST-03 Output Format Specification** — fixed labelled layers (facts, observations, strengths, interpretation, plan, safeguarding) keep content from blending.
- **NE-16 Non-Judgmental Comparison** — loaded phrases are shown as "current wording → revised wording," preserving dignity while improving the record.
- **DP-04 Must-Not Constraints** — an explicit list of what the note must not contain: labels, rumour, outside parties' details, status, motive guesses, off-the-record promises.
- **DP-05 Stakes-Based Gate Policy** — routine, worry, and reasonable-suspicion tiers set what happens before and alongside the note.
