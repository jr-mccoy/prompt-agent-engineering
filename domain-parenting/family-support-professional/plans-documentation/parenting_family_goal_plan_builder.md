# Family Goal Plan Builder

**Objective:** Produce a family goal plan the parent recognises as theirs: one to
three goals in the family's words, small visible steps, who does what (worker
included), supports, a family-rated progress scale, and review dates. Court, agency,
or school conditions sit in their own section, copied exactly, so nobody confuses
"what we chose" with "what we were told to do."

**When to Use:**
- Writing the first plan after intake, or redoing it at a review.
- The current "plan" is really the worker's list.
- Family goals and required conditions are getting mixed together.
- A funder wants measurable objectives without losing the family's voice.
- **Not this prompt if** this is the first intake conversation —
  `domain-parenting/family-support-professional/intake-assessment/parenting_family_strengths_needs_intake.md`.
  A licensed clinician's treatment plan → `domain-psychology/documentation/psychology_initial_treatment_plan.md`.
  A separating parent's custody "parenting plan" → `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/custody/parenting_custody_parenting_plan_builder.md`.
  A parent attending only because they were required to →
  `domain-parenting/family-support-professional/coaching-education/parenting_mandated_parent_engagement.md` first.

## Safeguarding, Consent, and Scope

- **This plan does not diagnose.** Goals describe observable behaviour, the family's
  words, and frequency. "Address Mum's depression" becomes "Mum wants to be dressed
  by 8:30 on school days; now that happens about 1 day in 5."
- **Safety sits above the plan.** A goal plan is never the response to a
  safeguarding concern. Reasonable suspicion (reasonable cause to believe — not
  proof) of abuse or neglect means a report. Who is a mandated reporter, the
  threshold, timelines, and whether a supervisor consult counts **vary by state,
  province, country, and agency — verify locally**. In most US states the duty is
  personal and is not discharged by telling a supervisor; a consult must never delay
  a report or an emergency call. Immediate danger → 911 or the local emergency
  number first. Reports go to the child-protection hotline or law enforcement
  (Childhelp 1-800-422-4453 can help find the number). Do not investigate,
  interrogate, photograph injuries, or promise secrecy. Record exact words, what you
  saw, when, and who you told. Decide with your supervisor, case by case, whether
  telling the family about a report is safe — never when it could endanger anyone.
- **Route to licensed clinicians** when planning surfaces: suicide or self-harm talk
  (988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US; same day); a parent hearing or seeing things
  others do not, or not sleeping for days; a child losing skills; heavy substance use
  while caring for a child; infant feeding or weight worries. Partner violence →
  National DV Hotline 1-800-799-7233. Possible poisoning → Poison Control
  1-800-222-1222. Outside the US: local emergency number, findahelpline.com. Plans
  can include "get to the appointment," never treatment.
- **Consent and privacy.** Before planning, say the limits of confidentiality
  plainly: "What you tell me stays with me and my supervisor, unless I'm worried a
  child or someone else is in danger, or a court orders records." Share the plan
  with another agency only with a signed release, unless law requires it. Families
  can often read their records; write for them. HIPAA, FERPA, 42 CFR Part 2, or
  GDPR-type rules may apply — verify locally.

## Inputs / Context

1. **De-identify first.** Initials or roles ("Mum, 27"; "baby A, 7 months"). No
   names, addresses, dates of birth, or case numbers in an AI tool unless your
   agency has approved it for client data.
2. **Household:** members and roles, children's ages, home language, reading level.
3. **The family's priorities, in their words** — quotes from intake or the last visit.
4. **Strengths** you have seen (specific, dated) and **concrete needs** (housing,
   food, heat, benefits, childcare, transport).
5. **Required conditions:** source (court order, CPS plan, school agreement), exact
   wording, deadline, who verifies.
6. **Program frame:** voluntary or required, length, funder domains, review cycle,
   and resources actually available this month.

## Method

1. **Ask for the goal in the family's words first.** "If things were going better in
   three months, what would be different at home?" Write the answer verbatim as the
   goal headline. Your framing can sit beneath as a note; it never replaces the quote.
2. **Sort into two lanes and hold the must-nots (DP-04).** Lane A: goals the family
   chose. Lane B: required conditions, word for word, with source, deadline, and
   verifier. Link overlaps openly ("this step also counts toward B1"). The plan
   **must not**: rewrite a condition as if the family chose it; add a goal the
   family did not agree to; target another adult's behaviour the parent cannot
   control; make voluntary help depend on accepting the worker's goal; or contain a
   diagnosis.
3. **Keep it to one to three goals.** Ask: "Which one, if it moved, would make the
   others easier?" Heat, food, and safe sleep usually come first. Park the rest on
   a "later" list.
4. **Break each goal into measurable steps (DS-02).** Each step names the action,
   who, by when, and how we will know — a count, a frequency, or a thing that exists
   ("confirmation number," "3 of 5 mornings out by 8:15"). Size test: "From 0 to 10,
   how sure are you that you can do this before our next visit?" Below 7 → shrink it.
5. **Write the worker's commitments as trust deposits (DP-18).** Two to four small,
   reliable actions the worker owes the family — "call the housing office by
   Tuesday." Report on these first at every review.
6. **Name supports and barriers, function-curious.** Who helps? What got in the way
   last time? A missed step is information about step size or a barrier.
7. **Build a family-rated progress scale (DP-03).** Anchors in the family's words:
   0 = how things are at their worst, 5 = halfway, 10 = goal met. The family rates
   today and at each review. It records the family's view; it is not a compliance
   score and does not replace verification of Lane B.
8. **Set review dates and read the plan back.** Review every 4–6 weeks, sooner if a
   Lane B deadline is close. Read it aloud in plain language; the family keeps a copy
   in their language. Lane A can change at any review; say honestly that Lane B
   changes only through whoever set it.

## Output Format

```
# Family Goal Plan — [initials]   Written: [date]   Next review: [date]
Program: [voluntary / required]   Language: [..]   Copy given: [yes/no]

## Our strengths
## Lane A — Goals we chose
### A1: "[family's own words]"     Worker note: [optional]
  | Step | Who | By when | How we'll know | Confidence 0–10 |
  Supports / barriers: [..]
  Scale: 0 = [..]  5 = [..]  10 = [..]   Today: [..]
## Lane B — Required conditions (verbatim; not chosen by the family)
  | Condition | Source | Deadline | Who verifies | Linked Lane A steps |
## Worker commitments
## Later list
## If things get hard: program line, 988, 911
## Read back on [date]; family comments: "[..]"
```

## Verification

- [ ] Every Lane A goal headline is a direct quote from a family member.
- [ ] No more than three active goals.
- [ ] Every step has an action, a person, a date, and an observable "how we'll know."
- [ ] Each Lane B condition is verbatim, with source, deadline, and verifier.
- [ ] The worker has at least two dated commitments.
- [ ] No diagnostic label appears anywhere.
- [ ] Each goal has 0/5/10 anchors and today's family rating.
- [ ] A review date is set; the plan was read back and given in the family's language.

## False-Positive Prevention

| Misfire | What it looks like | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Worker goal in family clothing | "Mum will improve parenting skills" | Use the family's quote; the worker's view goes in a note or nowhere. |
| Condition disguised as choice | "Family has chosen to attend 8 classes" | Move to Lane B with source and verbatim wording. |
| Labelling instead of describing | "Treat Dad's anger issues" | "Dad wants to stop yelling at bedtime; now most nights." |
| Vague step | "Work on routines" | "Picture chart on fridge by Fri; used 3 of 5 mornings." |
| Too many goals | Seven goals at the first plan | One to three; the rest go on the later list. |
| Missed step read as non-compliance | "Mum non-compliant" | Review barriers; shrink the step; note what got in the way. |

## Adaptations

- **Parent with an intellectual disability or low literacy:** one goal at a time,
  picture steps, teach-back ("Tell me what you'll do this week"), voice-note copy.
- **Interpreter:** a professional interpreter, never a child or relative;
  translate the written copy.
- **Parent who is a minor:** consent rules vary — verify locally; include their own
  education goals.
- **Teen in the household:** invite the teen to name and rate one goal themselves.
- **Tribal or Indigenous family:** in the US, ICWA may apply if a child-welfare case
  is open; ask who in the extended family and tribe should help plan.

## Techniques Used

- **DS-02 Metric Specification** — every step carries an observable "how we'll know" measure: a count, a frequency, or a document that exists.
- **DP-03 Anchored Scoring Scales** — 0/5/10 anchors in the family's words let the family rate progress consistently across reviews.
- **DP-04 Must-Not Constraints** — no disguised conditions, unagreed goals, goals about another adult, conditional voluntary help, or diagnoses.
- **DP-18 Trust Deposits Definition** — the worker's own small, dated commitments are written into the plan and reported on first.
