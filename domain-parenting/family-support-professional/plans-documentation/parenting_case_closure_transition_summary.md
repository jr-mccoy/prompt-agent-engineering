# Case Closure and Transition Summary

**Objective:** End services safely and well. Classify the ending; for unplanned
endings, complete a safety check and outreach before closing; then produce, from one
set of facts, a file summary, a consented minimum-necessary hand-off, and a
plain-language family letter — plus the family's own plan for after you leave.

**When to Use:**
- Goals are met, the program's time limit is near, the family chose to stop, or the
  family is moving or ageing out.
- The family stopped answering calls and texts, and you are deciding whether you can
  close.
- Funding ended or the worker is leaving, and the family needs a new connection.
- **Not this prompt if** you are a licensed therapist ending treatment — use
  `domain-psychology/documentation/psychology_termination_summary.md`. A clinical
  hand-off script → `domain-psychology/care-coordination/psychology_warm_handoff_narrative.md`;
  a clinical referral letter → `domain-psychology/care-coordination/psychology_referral_letter_generator.md`.
  Getting a mid-service referral to stick →
  `domain-parenting/family-support-professional/referral-resourcing/parenting_referral_follow_through_barriers.md`.

## Safeguarding, Consent, and Scope

- **The summary does not diagnose.** Describe behaviour, the family's words, and
  frequency. "Mum's anxiety improved" becomes "Mum said, 'I go to the shop on my own
  now'; she reported panic in shops once in September, down from most weeks in June."
- **Losing contact is not, by itself, abuse or neglect**, and a family may leave a
  voluntary program. But a live safeguarding concern is never closed by closing the
  case. If you have reasonable suspicion (reasonable cause to believe — not proof),
  report. Immediate danger → 911 or the local emergency number first. Reports go to
  the child-protection hotline or law enforcement (Childhelp 1-800-422-4453 can help
  find the number). Who is a mandated reporter, the threshold, timelines, and
  whether a supervisor consult counts **vary by state, province, country, and agency
  — verify locally**. In most US states the duty is personal and is not discharged
  by telling a supervisor; a consult must never delay a report or emergency call.
  Do not investigate, interrogate, photograph injuries, or promise secrecy. Record exact words, what you saw, when,
  and who you told. Decide with your supervisor whether telling the family about a
  report is safe.
- **Clinician hand-off triggers before closing:** any recent suicide or self-harm
  talk (988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US), a parent describing voices or days
  without sleep, a child losing skills, substance use while caring for a child, or
  partner violence (National DV Hotline 1-800-799-7233). Possible poisoning →
  Poison Control 1-800-222-1222. Outside the US: local emergency number,
  findahelpline.com. These get a consented clinical referral and a confirmed first
  appointment, not just a phone number in a letter.
- **Consent and privacy.** Share anything with a receiving service only with a
  signed, current release, unless law requires it — verify locally. Send the minimum
  needed. Families can often read their records and will read the letter; write
  for them. HIPAA, FERPA, 42 CFR Part 2, or GDPR-type rules may apply — verify
  locally.

## Inputs / Context

1. **De-identify first.** Initials or roles; no names, addresses, dates of birth, or
   case numbers in an AI tool unless your agency approved it for client data.
2. **Service history:** start date, number and type of contacts, last contact, plan
   goals with first and latest family ratings.
3. **Reason for ending** and who initiated it.
4. **For unplanned endings:** every outreach attempt (date, route, result) and any
   open concern.
5. **Last known safety picture:** open child-protection case, prior reports, infant
   in the home, partner violence, suicide talk, required conditions outstanding.
6. **Referrals** made and their status; releases on file.
7. **Family's language and reading level**, and agency closure rules.

## Method

1. **Classify the ending.** Planned: goals met, time limit, family choice with
   notice, move, ageing out. Unplanned: no contact, abrupt withdrawal, funding cut,
   worker departure. Planned endings get notice — name the end two or three visits
   ahead so nobody is surprised.
2. **Gate unplanned closure by stakes (DP-05).**
   - **Low:** no open concerns, no infant, no recent disclosures → at least three
     outreach attempts by different routes over 2–3 weeks (call, text, letter; drop-by
     only if policy allows), then supervisor sign-off.
   - **Elevated:** infant in the home, open child-protection case, recent partner
     violence or suicide talk, or required conditions outstanding → supervisor
     review **before** closure; contact the case's other professionals within release
     limits; consider whether the unknowns now meet a report threshold.
   - **Report threshold met** → report now; closure waits on the agency's decision.
3. **Check what can and cannot be undone (QA-09).** Reopening is usually possible —
   say how. Some things are not: information already shared, a slot given away, a
   court deadline passed. Name these before closing and tell the family the route
   back ("Call the program line; you go to the front of the waitlist for six months"
   — only if true).
4. **Build the family's own plan with them.** In the closing visit ask: "What's
   working that you want to keep doing?" "Who will you call if things get hard?"
   "What would tell you it's time to ask for help again?" Record their words. Add
   crisis numbers and the program line.
5. **Make warm hand-offs, not lists.** With written consent, introduce the next
   service in a three-way call or joint visit, confirm the first appointment date,
   and agree who checks it happened. If the family declines, record the offer and
   the decline with respect.
6. **Write one set of facts for three readers (NE-14).** **File summary:** complete
   and neutral. **Receiving-service hand-off:** only what the release covers and the
   service needs. **Family letter:** plain language (about a 5th-grade reading
   level), in the family's language, strengths first, what they achieved in their
   own words, the door back, crisis numbers. No new information appears in the
   letter or hand-off that the file does not support.
7. **Make the hand-off self-contained (NE-20).** The receiving worker should be
   able to act without calling you: why services started, what helped, what is
   still open, last known safety status **with its date**, family's stated
   preferences, and a contact for questions within the release.
8. **End the relationship well.** Mark the ending (a certificate, a goodbye for the
   children in their terms). No ongoing personal or social-media contact.

## Output Format

```
# Closure — [family initials]   Opened: [..]  Last contact: [..]  Closed: [..]
Type: [planned / unplanned]   Reason: [..]   Initiated by: [..]

## Safety gate: tier [low / elevated / report], outreach log, supervisor sign-off
## Reversibility: can reopen how; what cannot be undone
## File summary: reason for services; contacts (n, type); goals with first →
   latest family ratings; strengths; still open; last known safety status (date)
## Family's own plan (their words) + crisis numbers
## Warm hand-offs: | Service | Consent (date) | How introduced | First appt | Who checks |
## Receiving-service hand-off (minimum necessary, per release)
## Closure letter to family (plain language, family's language)
```

## Verification

- [ ] The ending is classified, with who initiated it.
- [ ] Unplanned closures show at least three outreach attempts by different routes, or a documented reason why fewer.
- [ ] Elevated-tier cases show supervisor review dated before the closure date.
- [ ] The last known safety status carries a date.
- [ ] Every shared item maps to a signed release or a stated legal basis.
- [ ] The family's plan uses their quoted words and includes crisis numbers.
- [ ] The letter contains no diagnosis, jargon, or fact absent from the file summary.

## False-Positive Prevention

| Misfire | What it looks like | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Silent closure | Case closed after two missed visits, no outreach | Outreach log and supervisor sign-off first. |
| Disengagement read as neglect | Report made only because a voluntary family stopped answering | Not a report on its own; weigh the pre-existing concerns. |
| Closing over a live concern | "Family moved; case closed" with an open safety worry | Report or hand the concern to the right agency; then close. |
| Labelling instead of describing | "Mum's depression resolved" | Her words and the change in frequency. |
| Referral as hand-off | A list of five numbers in the letter | Consented introduction and a confirmed first appointment. |
| Blame in the letter | "Because you missed visits, your case is closed" | Neutral fact, the door back, strengths first. |

## Adaptations

- **Parent with an intellectual disability or low literacy:** read the letter
  aloud at the last visit; picture version of the family plan.
- **Interpreter:** closing visit through a professional interpreter; translate the
  letter; never a child as interpreter.
- **Teen parent or teen in the home:** give the teen their own copy of their part of
  the plan and a youth-friendly line; confidentiality rules for minors vary.
- **Tribal or Indigenous family:** offer hand-off to tribal family services; in the
  US, ICWA may apply where a child-welfare case is open.
- **Military family moving:** consented hand-off to the new installation's family
  support program.

## Example Output

```
# Closure — Mum "T." (24), K (2)   Opened: 3 Feb 2026
Last contact: 6 Sep   Closed: 5 Oct 2026   Type: unplanned → family choice

## Safety gate — tier: low
No open CPS case; no infant; no disclosures in the record. Required
conditions: none (voluntary program).
Outreach: 12 Sep call (voicemail); 17 Sep text; 22 Sep letter; 29 Sep
text. 30 Sep T. replied: "We moved to my sister's in another county.
We're ok. Don't need visits but thank you."
Supervisor (L.) sign-off 2 Oct.

## Reversibility
Can reopen within 6 months by calling the program line if T. returns
to the county. Cannot undo: K's spot in the program's toddler group.

## File summary
Reason for services: T. asked for help with K's sleep and her own
isolation. Contacts: 22 home visits, 3 group sessions.
Goal A1 "K sleeps in his own bed": rating 1 → 8.
Goal A2 "Have someone to call": 0 → 6 (sister, one friend from group).
Strengths: consistent bedtime routine; T. reads K's tired cues.
Still open: K's 2-year check-up not yet booked.
Last known safety status (30 Sep): T. reports "we're ok"; no concerns
noted in the record.

## Family's own plan (from 30 Sep text, with T.'s consent)
"Keep bedtime at 7:30. Call my sister when I'm stressed. Book K's
check-up." Crisis: 988; 911 for danger.

## Warm hand-off
T. agreed by text (29 Sep) and signed an e-release (1 Oct) for one
referral: new county's home visiting program. Three-way call 3 Oct;
first visit 14 Oct; worker checks with T. on 15 Oct.

## Closure letter (extract)
Dear T., It was a pleasure working with you and K. You built a
bedtime routine that works. K now sleeps in his own bed most nights —
you did that. We are closing your file on 5 Oct because you have moved.
Your first visit with the new program is 14 Oct. If you move back, call
us and we will reopen. If you ever feel unsafe or have thoughts of
hurting yourself, call or text 988, or 911 in an emergency.
```

## Techniques Used

- **DP-05 Stakes-Based Gate Policy** — low, elevated, and report-threshold tiers decide how much outreach and review an unplanned closure needs.
- **NE-20 Third-Party Handoff Package** — a self-contained, dated hand-off the receiving worker can act on without calling back.
- **NE-14 Multi-Audience Documentation Targeting** — one set of facts becomes a file summary, a minimum-necessary hand-off, and a plain-language family letter.
- **QA-09 Reversibility Assessment** — the closure states how to reopen and what cannot be undone before it happens.
