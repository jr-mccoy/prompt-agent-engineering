# Engaging a Mandated Parent

**Objective:** Plan the first sessions with a parent who is here because someone else
required it: be honest about what is required and what is their choice, what you report
back, and your separate duty to report child-safety concerns; lower defensiveness
without arguing about the case; find one goal the parent wants; and set up a factual
report-back the parent sees before it goes.

**When to Use:**
- A court, child-protection agency, probation, or school has required a parent to
  attend parenting education, coaching, or home visiting.
- The parent is angry, silent, or says "I don't need this" or "I'm only here for the
  certificate."
- You need to explain your reporting role in words the parent will believe.
- **Not this prompt if** the parent chose to come — use
  `domain-parenting/family-support-professional/coaching-education/parenting_parent_coaching_session_plan.md`.
  You are a licensed clinician deciding whether to make a report →
  `domain-psychology/risk-crisis/psychology_mandated_reporter_decision_walkthrough.md`.
  Exploring a parent's mixed feelings about one change, clinician-led →
  `domain-psychology/modalities/motivational-interviewing/psychology_mi_decisional_balance_facilitator.md`.
  Separating required conditions from family goals in the service plan →
  `domain-parenting/family-support-professional/plans-documentation/parenting_family_goal_plan_builder.md`.
  Questions about the court case, rights, or the order itself → the parent's attorney.

## Safeguarding, Consent, and Scope

- **Two reporting channels, explained separately, before anything is disclosed.**
  (1) **Report-back to the referrer:** what your program sends (attendance, completion,
  participation, sometimes skills observed), when, and under what signed release. (2)
  **Mandated reporting:** a separate duty that applies to every family you work with.
  Also name the other limits: imminent danger and court orders for records.
- **Mandated reporting — verify locally.** Who is a mandated reporter, the threshold
  (reasonable suspicion or reasonable cause to believe — not proof), deadlines, and
  whether a supervisor consult counts vary by state, province, country, and agency —
  check agency policy, statute, and your supervisor. In most US states the duty is
  personal: telling a supervisor does not discharge it, and a consult must never delay
  a report or an emergency call. An open child-protection case does not remove your
  duty; a new concern is still reported. Immediate danger → 911 or local emergency
  number first. Report to the state or county child-protection hotline or law
  enforcement (Childhelp 1-800-422-4453 can help find the number). Do not investigate,
  question the child, photograph injuries, or promise secrecy. Record exact words, what
  you saw, when, and who you told. Decide with your supervisor whether telling the
  family is safe — never when it could endanger the child or anyone else.
- **Does not diagnose.** Report-backs and notes describe behaviour, the parent's words,
  and dates. "Arrived on time for 6 of 6 sessions; practised praise in role-play twice"
  — not "in denial", "personality-disordered", or "resistant".
- **Route to a licensed clinician** (with consent) when the parent describes low mood,
  panic, or trauma symptoms most days for two weeks or more; alcohol or drug use
  affects care; or they describe thoughts of harming the child. **Same day:** suicide
  talk → 988 (US); partner violence with fear → private safety talk and National DV
  Hotline 1-800-799-7233; possible poisoning → Poison Control 1-800-222-1222. Outside
  the US: local emergency number; findahelpline.com. You support, refer, follow up; you
  do not treat.
- **Privacy.** A written release for each report-back recipient, saying what it covers.
  The parent can usually decline; say honestly that the referrer may then record
  non-completion (verify locally). Share the minimum. Write as if the parent and a
  judge will read it. HIPAA, 42 CFR Part 2, or GDPR-type rules may apply — verify
  locally.

## Inputs / Context

1. **De-identify first.** Initials or roles only; no names, addresses, dates of birth,
   or case or docket numbers in an AI tool unless your agency has approved it.
2. **Referral:** who required it, the stated concern, the exact requirement (sessions,
   hours, certificate), deadline.
3. **What the referrer expects back:** form, content, frequency, recipient.
4. **Signed releases** in place, and what they cover.
5. **What the parent has said so far**, in their words.
6. **Strengths known:** from the referral, intake, or the parent.
7. **Practical barriers:** work, transport, childcare, language, cost.
8. **Your program's limits:** what you can and cannot change.

## Method

1. **Know the deal before you meet.** If the requirement or report-back content is
   unclear, ask the referrer in writing before session 1.
2. **Clarify roles: three lists.** **Required** (set by the order or plan: attendance,
   number of sessions, completion criteria). **Negotiable** (times, place, order of
   topics, interpreter, phone or in-person). **Yours** (your goal, what you share
   beyond the requirement, whether you use what you learn). Say it out loud: "You don't
   get a say in whether you come. You get a lot of say in what we do with the time."
3. **Explain reporting in plain words, before disclosure.** "Here's what I send to [the
   caseworker]: whether you came, whether you finished, and a short note on what we
   practised. I'll show you each one before it goes. Separately, the law says if I
   think a child is being harmed, I have to report it. That's true for every family I
   see. I won't do it behind your back unless telling you would put someone at risk."
   Ask: "What questions do you have about that?"
4. **Respond to anger with MI, not argument (DS-01).** Treat discord as a signal about
   the relationship, not a trait of the parent. Reflect: "You feel judged before
   anyone's heard your side." Emphasise autonomy: "You don't have to agree with the
   referral to get something useful out of this." Do not argue about the allegation;
   you are not the investigator or the judge.
5. **Make trust deposits (DP-18).** Name 4–6 small, countable acts for the first four
   weeks and keep every one: start on time; return calls within one working day; show
   every report-back before it is sent; no surprises — explain any change first; follow
   through on practical help (bus pass, childcare); remember what the parent told you.
6. **Find the parent's own goal.** Ask: "What would make this worth your time?" "What
   would [the agency] need to see to be out of your life?" "What do you want your kids
   to say about you when they're grown?" Link it to the required goal where it fits.
   Where it doesn't, keep the two separate in writing.
7. **Hold the must-nots (DP-04).** No legal advice or predictions ("Finish this and
   you'll get them back"); no promise of a good report; no siding for or against the
   referrer; no asking the parent to admit the allegation; no opinion on custody or
   reunification unless your role requires it; no report-back the parent has not seen.
8. **Write the report-back as a handoff package (NE-20).** Self-contained, factual:
   dates attended, completion status, skills practised and observed, strengths seen,
   the parent's own words where the release allows, what remains. Show the parent
   first; note any disagreement in their words.

## Output Format

```
# Mandated Engagement Plan — [parent role, child initials/ages] | Referrer: [type]
## Referral facts: requirement / deadline / stated concern (as written)
## Report-back: content / recipient / frequency / release status
## Role clarification: Required / Negotiable / Yours
## Reporting script (both channels) + check-for-understanding question
## Anticipated discord → planned reflections and autonomy statements
## Trust deposits (4 weeks) — tracked
## Parent's own goal (their words) + link to required goal, or "kept separate"
## Must-nots for this case
## Safety/clinical screen: triggers checked, action
## Report-back draft (shown to parent: date, parent's comments)
```

## Verification

- [ ] Required, negotiable, and parent-owned items are listed separately.
- [ ] Both reporting channels are explained in plain words before any disclosure.
- [ ] Report-back content matches the signed release and the referrer's stated need.
- [ ] The parent's own goal is quoted in their words.
- [ ] No legal advice, outcome prediction, or promise of a good report appears.
- [ ] The report-back is factual, dated, free of labels, and was shown to the parent.
- [ ] Clinician and same-day triggers were checked and recorded.

## False-Positive Prevention

| Misfire | What it looks like | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Labelling instead of describing | "Parent in denial, resistant" in a report-back | Describe: "Said 'I don't agree with the referral'; attended 5 of 5; practised two skills." |
| Anger read as risk | Raised voice in session 1 treated as a safety concern | Anger at a system is common and understandable. Safety concerns are about the child. |
| Worker as investigator | Asking what really happened that night | Not your role; it can harm the parent's case. Focus on the skills ahead. |
| Silence read as non-engagement | "Did not participate" after a quiet first session | Record attendance and what was offered; build trust first. |
| Compliance read as change | Certificate earned, so the risk is gone | Report what was observed, not a prediction. |
| Over-promising to win trust | "I'll write you a great report" | Promise only process: what you send, and showing it first. |
| Hiding the reporting role | Explained only after a disclosure | Explain it in the first five minutes. |

## Adaptations

- **Infant or toddler in care:** practise skills at family time, with agency agreement.
- **Teen in the home:** the teen may be angry too; plan joint and separate time.
- **Parent with intellectual disability or low literacy:** read releases aloud, picture
  the three lists, use teach-back, and offer a support person or advocate.
- **Neurodivergent parent:** written agenda, no surprises, direct words.
- **Language:** a professional interpreter, never a child or relative.
- **Tribal/Indigenous family (US):** the Indian Child Welfare Act may apply in
  child-protection cases; ask about tribal services and involve the tribe's
  representative as the agency requires — verify locally.
- **Parent who is a minor:** check consent rules and whether a guardian or the young
  parent's own caseworker is involved.
- **Rural or telehealth:** confirm privacy at the start of every call.

## Example Output

```
# Mandated Engagement Plan — Dad (T, 34), children M 6 and K 2
# Referrer: county child-protection agency (in-home case)

## Referral facts
Required: 10 coaching sessions by 15 Jan; certificate on completion.
Stated concern (as written): "physical discipline leaving marks on M."
Report-back: monthly attendance + short skills summary to caseworker L.
Release: signed 2 Oct (attendance, completion, skills). Not covered:
anything else T says.

## Role clarification (said in session 1)
Required: 10 sessions, by 15 Jan.
Negotiable: Tues or Thurs evening; home or centre; order of topics.
Yours: your goal; what you tell me beyond the requirement.

## Reporting script
"Each month I send L: dates you came, and two or three lines on what we
practised. You see it first. Separately, if I think a child is being
hurt, I have to report it — same for every family." T: "So you're a spy."
Reflect: "It feels like everything you say gets used against you."
T: "Yeah." "What I send is what's on that list. Nothing else without
your okay — except that one safety rule."

## Trust deposits (weeks 1–4)        Kept
Start on time                        4/4
Return calls within 1 working day    3/3
Show report-back before sending      1/1
Bus pass for centre sessions         done 9 Oct

## T's own goal
"I want M to stop flinching when I raise my voice." Links to required
goal (discipline without physical punishment) — recorded together.

## Must-nots
No predictions about case closure. No questions about the incident.

## Screen
No clinician triggers at session 1; ask again at session 4.

## Report-back draft (Oct) — shown to T 30 Oct
Attended 4 of 4 (3, 10, 17, 24 Oct), on time. Practised: calm
instructions, when–then, time-in. Role-played time-in twice on 24 Oct.
T's words (with permission): "I want M to stop flinching." T comment:
"Fine."
```

## Techniques Used

- **DS-01 Framework Application** — MI spirit, reflections, and autonomy support frame the response to discord.
- **DP-18 Trust Deposits Definition** — small, countable, kept acts tracked over four weeks.
- **DP-04 Must-Not Constraints** — no legal advice, predictions, investigation, or unseen reports.
- **NE-20 Third-Party Handoff Package** — a self-contained, factual report-back the parent sees first.
