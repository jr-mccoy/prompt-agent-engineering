# Parent Coaching Session Plan

**Objective:** Plan one 45–60-minute coaching session around a goal the parent chose:
strengths first, an OARS conversation in the parent's words, one skill taught by
model–practice–feedback, a home step the family can actually do this week, and a
session note. The coach stays a coach; anything clinical goes to a licensed clinician.

**When to Use:**
- You are a parent coach, educator, home visitor, or family support worker, and the
  parent has agreed to coaching.
- The parent has named something to change ("bedtime takes two hours", "I shout before
  school every morning").
- You want a planned session and a home step that fits the parent's capacity.
- **Not this prompt if** the parent is attending because a court, agency, or school
  required it and is guarded — start with
  `domain-parenting/family-support-professional/coaching-education/parenting_mandated_parent_engagement.md`.
  Delivering a manualised program and needing to adapt it →
  `domain-parenting/family-support-professional/coaching-education/parenting_curriculum_fidelity_adaptation.md`.
  A licensed clinician delivering PCIT or behavioural parent training →
  `domain-psychology/modalities/behavioral-parent-training/psychology_behavioral_parent_training_module.md`.
  The whole-service goal plan →
  `domain-parenting/family-support-professional/plans-documentation/parenting_family_goal_plan_builder.md`.

## Safeguarding, Consent, and Scope

- **Consent and the limits of confidentiality come first.** Before the parent shares
  anything personal, explain what you write down and who sees it, then the limits:
  "What you tell me stays between us and my supervisor, with three exceptions: if I
  think a child is being hurt or at risk of harm, if someone is in danger right now, or
  if a court orders my records. I'll tell you if that happens, unless telling you would
  put someone at risk." Use your agency's approved wording.
- **Mandated reporting — verify locally.** Who is a mandated reporter, the threshold
  (reasonable suspicion or reasonable cause to believe — not proof), the deadline, and
  whether a supervisor consult counts all vary by state, province, country, and agency —
  check agency policy, statute, and your supervisor. In most US states the duty is
  personal: telling a supervisor does not discharge it, and a consult must never delay
  a report or an emergency call. Immediate danger → 911 or local emergency number
  first. Report to the state or county child-protection hotline or law enforcement
  (Childhelp 1-800-422-4453 can help find the number). Do not investigate, question the
  child, photograph injuries, or promise secrecy. Record exact words, what you saw,
  when, and who you told. Decide with your supervisor whether telling the family is
  safe — never when it could endanger the child or anyone else.
- **Does not diagnose.** Describe behaviour, the parent's own words, frequency, and
  duration: "said she cries most evenings and has not left the flat in a week", not
  "depressed".
- **Coach, not therapist — refer to a licensed clinician** (with consent, this week)
  when the parent describes low mood, worry, or panic most days for two weeks or more;
  trauma memories take over sessions; alcohol or drug use affects care; the parent has
  frightening thoughts of harming the child; or the child shows self-harm, sexual
  behaviour beyond age expectations, loss of skills, or a developmental concern
  (pediatrician or early intervention). **Same day:** suicide talk → 988 Suicide &
  Crisis Lifeline (US), stay while they connect; partner violence with fear → private
  safety talk and National DV Hotline 1-800-799-7233; possible poisoning → Poison
  Control 1-800-222-1222. Outside the US: local emergency number; findahelpline.com.
  You support, refer, and follow up. You do not treat.
- **Privacy.** Written release before talking to another agency, except where law
  requires or permits sharing without consent (e.g. a child-protection report) —
  verify locally. Share the minimum needed. Parents may be able
  to read their records; write as if they will. HIPAA, FERPA, 42 CFR Part 2, or
  GDPR-type rules may apply — verify locally.

## Inputs / Context

1. **De-identify first.** Use initials or roles ("Mum, 27; son D, 3"). No names,
   addresses, dates of birth, or case numbers in an AI tool unless your agency has
   approved the tool for client data.
2. **The parent's goal in their own words**, and why it matters now.
3. **Child:** age, temperament, what they enjoy, needs as the parent describes them.
4. **What the parent already does well.**
5. **The family's week:** shifts, other children, housing, transport, sleep.
6. **Session history:** session number; how last week's home step went.
7. **Setting:** home, centre, phone, or video; minutes; interpreter needed.
8. **Program constraints:** curriculum, coaching model, note rules.

## Method

1. **Set the agenda together (DS-01).** The MI spirit — partnership, acceptance,
   compassion, evocation — frames the session. "Last time you said mornings are
   hardest. Still the focus today?" The parent chooses. A safety issue goes first.
2. **Strengths before problems.** Ask what went well this week; reflect it in detail:
   "You got down to his level before asking about shoes. That is exactly the move."
3. **Understand with OARS (DS-01).** Open questions, Affirmations, Reflections,
   Summaries; about two reflections per question. Get one recent example: before, what
   the child did, what the parent did, what happened next. Ask what the behaviour might
   be doing for the child before suggesting anything.
4. **Elicit–provide–elicit.** "What have you tried?" … "Can I share what has worked for
   other parents?" … "What do you make of that?" Offer two or three options; the parent
   picks one.
5. **Teach one skill: model → practise → feedback.** One-sentence description. Model it
   in role-play, then the parent practises twice with you as the child. Feedback: two
   things done well, one to adjust. With the child present, coach live in short, quiet
   prompts ("Try naming what he's doing").
6. **Size the home step with an anchored confidence scale (DP-03).** Agree when, where,
   how long, and what counts as done. Confidence 0–10: **0** = "I can't see this
   happening"; **5** = "Some days, if nothing goes wrong"; **10** = "Even on a bad day."
   Below 7 → shrink the step (fewer days, shorter) until it reaches 7. Plan for the
   likeliest obstacle.
7. **Hold the scope line (DP-04).** Must-nots: no diagnosing; no therapy (trauma
   processing, treating low mood); nothing that contradicts a pediatrician's plan; no
   endorsing physical punishment; no confidentiality you cannot keep; no legal advice.
8. **Gate safety and clinical signs (DP-05).** **Tier 1:** note and continue. **Tier 2
   (this week):** clinician trigger → raise it, get consent, refer, tell your
   supervisor. **Tier 3 (same day):** suicide talk, partner violence with fear, or a
   reportable concern → safety section now; the plan waits.
9. **Close and record.** Summarise in the parent's words; confirm the home step and next
   date; ask "What was most useful today, and what wasn't?" Note facts, quotes,
   strengths, home step, tier, and any referral.

## Output Format

```
# Coaching Session Plan — Session [n] | [parent role, child initial + age] | [setting, minutes]
## Parent's goal (their words)
## Last week's home step: what happened
## Agenda (parent-chosen) + time split
## Strengths to reflect (specific)
## OARS: opening questions + planned reflections
## Function check: before / behaviour / after (one recent example)
## Skill: one-sentence description / model script / 2 practice rounds / feedback focus
## Home step: when / where / how long / done means / confidence 0–10 / obstacle plan
## Scope and safety: tier (1 / 2 / 3), triggers checked, action taken
## Session note: facts, quotes, strengths, plan, referrals
## Next session: date, focus
```

## Verification

- [ ] The goal is quoted in the parent's words, and the parent chose the agenda.
- [ ] At least two specific strengths appear before any problem is discussed.
- [ ] Advice follows elicit–provide–elicit, and the parent picked the option.
- [ ] The skill has a model step, two practice rounds, and two-plus-one feedback.
- [ ] The home step states when, where, how long, and done; confidence is 7+.
- [ ] A safety tier is recorded, with the action taken for tier 2 or 3.
- [ ] No diagnostic label appears in the plan or the note.
- [ ] Consent and confidentiality limits were explained (first session) or confirmed.

## False-Positive Prevention

| Misfire | What it looks like | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Labelling instead of describing | "Mum is anxious; D has ADHD" in the note | Write behaviour, words, frequency, duration. Labels belong to clinicians who assess. |
| Coach's goal replaces the parent's | Session on screens when she asked about bedtime | Parent chooses; raise your concern once, with permission. |
| Advice dump | Ten tips, no practice | One skill, practised twice. |
| Home step too big | "20 minutes of special time daily" for a parent on night shifts | Shrink until confidence is 7+. |
| Missed step read as non-compliance | "Mum did not engage with homework" | Ask what got in the way; the step was too big or the week was hard. |
| Drifting into therapy | Forty minutes on the parent's own childhood | Acknowledge, contain, refer; return to her goal. |
| Coaching through a safety concern | Bruise seen, session runs as planned | Safety section first. The plan waits. |

## Adaptations

- **Infant:** the skill is usually reading cues and serve-and-return, with the baby
  present. Feeding or sleep worries beyond safe-sleep basics → pediatrician.
- **Teen:** skills shift to listening, negotiating, and repair; rehearse a hard
  conversation with you playing the teen.
- **Neurodivergent child:** start from what helps the child regulate; aim for access,
  not making the child look typical.
- **Neurodivergent parent or parent with intellectual disability:** one small step,
  picture cards, repeated practice. Check understanding by asking the parent to show
  you, not "Do you understand?"
- **Interpreter:** professional only, never a child or relative.
- **Telehealth:** model with a soft toy; first ask "Can anyone else hear us?"
- **Low literacy:** picture card or voice note, not a handout.
- **Parent who is a minor:** check your agency's consent rules; the young parent is
  still your client.

## Example Output

```
# Coaching Session Plan — Session 3 | Mum (R, 27), son D, 3 | home, 50 min

## Parent's goal
"I want bath and bed to stop being a war. It goes till 9:30 and I'm
shouting by the end."

## Last week's home step
Two-minute warning before bath, 4 nights. Done 3 of 4.
R: "He still screamed, but shorter. Maybe 5 minutes, not 15."

## Agenda (R chose)
Bath-to-bed 35 min / daycare form 5 min / wrap-up 10 min

## Strengths to reflect
- Gave the warning 3 nights after 10-hour shifts.
- Noticed the scream got shorter.

## OARS
O: "Walk me through Tuesday from dinner." Reflect: "So the warning
helps, and the hard bit now is getting out."

## Function check (Tuesday)
Before: 7:40, bath toys out, R says "Out now."
Behaviour: D screams and kicks water, about 8 minutes.
After: R lets him stay 10 more minutes; out at 8:05.
Pattern: screaming gets more bath time.

## Skill: when–then with a visual timer
"Say what he gets next that he likes; let the timer be the boss."
Model: "When the timer runs out, then it's towel-cape time."
Practice 1: calm, short (good). Adjust: name the fun part, not "out".
Practice 2: "When the sand runs out, you're Super-D in the towel."

## Home step
Mon, Tue, Thu bath nights (Grandma does Fri). 5-minute sand timer.
Done = timer used + towel-cape offered, even if he screams.
Confidence: 6 at 4 nights → shrunk to 3 nights → 8.
Obstacle: D grabs the timer → timer on the sink, out of reach.

## Scope and safety
Tier 1. R: "tired but okay", sleeping about 5 hours, sees her sister
weekly. No clinician triggers.

## Session note
Home visit, 50 min. R chose bath-to-bed. Warning used 3 of 4 nights;
screaming down from about 15 to 5 minutes (R's estimate). Practised
when–then twice. Home step agreed. No safety concerns.

## Next session
14 Oct, 4:30 pm. Review the timer. R wants to add teeth brushing.
```

## Techniques Used

- **DS-01 Framework Application** — MI spirit and OARS frame the conversation; model–practice–feedback frames skill teaching.
- **DP-03 Anchored Scoring Scales** — a 0/5/10-anchored confidence scale sizes the home step.
- **DP-04 Must-Not Constraints** — the coach's scope line.
- **DP-05 Stakes-Based Gate Policy** — three tiers: routine, clinician referral, same-day safety.
