# Adapting an Evidence-Based Parenting Curriculum

**Objective:** Fit a licensed evidence-based parenting curriculum to one family or group
without breaking what makes it work: core vs adaptable components from the developer's
documents, a risk sort for each change, an adaptation log, per-session fidelity
ratings, and a clear point for asking the developer — or choosing another program.

**When to Use:**
- You are trained in a manualised program and the materials do not fit a family or
  group (language, literacy, culture, schedule, disability, child's age, telehealth).
- A supervisor or funder asks how you are keeping fidelity.
- You keep skipping the same part and want to know if it matters.
- **Not this prompt if** you are planning an ordinary one-to-one coaching session
  without a manual — use
  `domain-parenting/family-support-professional/coaching-education/parenting_parent_coaching_session_plan.md`.
  Designing one group session from scratch →
  `domain-parenting/family-support-professional/group-facilitation/parenting_parent_group_session_design.md`.
  A clinician building a behavioural parent training module →
  `domain-psychology/modalities/behavioral-parent-training/psychology_behavioral_parent_training_module.md`.

## Safeguarding, Consent, and Scope

- **The curriculum never overrides safeguarding.** Mandated reporting applies in every
  session and group. Who is a mandated reporter, the threshold (reasonable suspicion or
  reasonable cause to believe — not proof), deadlines, and whether a supervisor consult
  counts vary by state, province, country, and agency — check agency policy, statute,
  and your supervisor. In most US states the duty is personal: telling a supervisor
  does not discharge it, and a consult must never delay a report or an emergency call.
  Immediate danger → 911 or local emergency number first. Report to the state or county
  child-protection hotline or law enforcement (Childhelp 1-800-422-4453 can help find
  the number). Do not investigate, question the child, photograph injuries, or promise
  secrecy. Record exact words, what you saw, when, and who you told. Decide with your
  supervisor whether telling the family is safe — never when it could endanger anyone.
- **Consent and confidentiality limits** at enrolment and at the start of any group, in
  the family's language: what is recorded, who sees it, and the limits (child harm or
  risk, imminent danger, court orders).
- **Does not diagnose.** Adaptations are driven by described needs ("reads at about a
  second-grade level", "child does not speak in groups and covers ears in noise"), not
  by labels the worker assigns.
- **Clinical step-up.** A curriculum is not treatment. Refer to a licensed clinician
  (with consent) when the parent describes low mood, panic, or trauma symptoms most days
  for two weeks or more, alcohol or drug use affects care, or the child shows self-harm,
  injurious aggression, or a developmental concern. Ask whether a more intensive level
  fits better than adapting down. **Same day:** suicide talk → 988 (US); partner violence with fear →
  National DV Hotline 1-800-799-7233; possible poisoning → Poison Control
  1-800-222-1222. Outside the US: local emergency number; findahelpline.com.
- **Privacy.** Written release before sharing with another agency (except legally
  required sharing, such as a child-protection report — verify locally). Logs sent to a
  developer or funder are de-identified. Families may read their records. HIPAA,
  FERPA, or GDPR-type rules may apply — verify locally.
- **Licensing and IP.** Most named programs require trained or accredited facilitators
  and licensed materials. Translations, recordings, and redistributed copies usually
  need permission. Check your license or the developer — do not assume.

## Inputs / Context

1. **De-identify first.** Initials or roles only; no names, addresses, dates of birth,
   or case numbers in an AI tool unless your agency has approved it. Do not paste
   licensed curriculum text into an AI tool unless your license allows it; describe it.
2. **Program and version**, your training or accreditation level, license terms.
3. **The developer's fidelity documents:** core components, minimum dose, fidelity
   checklist, approved adaptations or translations.
4. **The family or group:** size, children's ages, languages, literacy, culture,
   disabilities, schedule, setting.
5. **What is not working**, and how you know.
6. **Adaptations already made**, with attendance or outcome data.

## Method

1. **Start from the developer's documents.** Core components come from the program's
   own manual, fidelity checklist, or model requirements; if missing, ask your program
   lead or the developer. Mark anything inferred "to confirm".
2. **Map each component (RT-02).** For each session or activity: what it is; why it
   works (mechanism — e.g. practice with feedback, positive attention before
   limit-setting); dose; delivery method; core or adaptable per the documents; and the
   need that clashes with it.
3. **Name the level.** **Surface** (words, names, images, examples, reading level,
   timing) or **deep** structure (family roles, beliefs about discipline and respect,
   elders' authority)? Surface changes are usually safe. Deep changes need the family's
   own voice.
4. **Sort each proposed change (DP-05).**
   - **Green — proceed and log:** local examples and names; plain-language wording;
     approved translations; picture cards; splitting one session over two visits;
     adding childcare, food, transport, or reminders; an interpreter.
   - **Yellow — supervisor or program lead approves first, check developer guidance:**
     reordering sessions; replacing video vignettes; shortening role-play; group to
     individual delivery; telehealth delivery; combining sessions; serving a child at
     the edge of the age range.
   - **Red — do not proceed without developer consultation:** removing or replacing a
     core component (e.g. dropping practice, skipping positive-attention sessions to go
     straight to discipline); cutting dose below the stated minimum; adding content that
     contradicts the model (any physical punishment); delivering to a population the
     model excludes; an untrained facilitator. Red may mean a different program fits.
5. **Hold the must-nots (DP-04).** No copying, translating, recording, or sharing
   licensed materials beyond the license. No calling it the named program once core
   components are gone — say "informed by". No dropping safety content (safe sleep,
   supervision, body safety, non-violent discipline).
6. **Log every adaptation.** Date; what changed; component; planned or reactive; who
   decided; why; fidelity-consistent (yes / no / unsure); what happened next.
7. **Rate fidelity per session with anchors (DP-03).**
   **Adherence:** 0 = core content skipped; 5 = covered as talk only, no practice;
   10 = covered as written, with practice and a home activity agreed.
   **Responsiveness:** 0 = not taking part; 5 = listens, does not practise; 10 =
   practises in session and reports trying it at home.
   Two sessions in a row at 5 or below on either scale → review the adaptation plan
   with your supervisor.
8. **Consult the developer when:** any red change is on the table; yellow changes are
   piling up; the family is outside the eligible population; outcomes are not moving
   after the minimum dose; no approved translation exists.

## Output Format

```
# Curriculum Adaptation Plan — [program, version] | [family or group, de-identified] | [setting]
## Source documents used (core components, dose, fidelity checklist) / missing: [...]
## Component map
| Component | Mechanism | Dose | Core/Adaptable (source) | Family/group need | Proposed change |
## Change decisions
| Change | Surface/Deep | Green/Yellow/Red | Approved by / date | Developer consulted? |
## Must-nots for this delivery
## Adaptation log
| Date | What changed | Component | Planned/Reactive | Who | Why | Fidelity-consistent? | Result |
## Fidelity ratings
| Session | Adherence 0–10 | Responsiveness 0–10 | Note |
## Review triggers + developer questions
## Safeguarding and clinical step-up: checked / actions
```

## Verification

- [ ] Core components are taken from named developer documents or marked "to confirm".
- [ ] Every proposed change has a green, yellow, or red decision and an approver.
- [ ] No red change goes ahead without a recorded developer consultation.
- [ ] Each adaptation log entry states planned or reactive and fidelity-consistent.
- [ ] Fidelity ratings use the 0/5/10 anchors, and the review trigger is applied.
- [ ] No licensed text is copied, translated, or shared beyond the license.
- [ ] Safety content and reporting duties are intact in the adapted delivery.

## False-Positive Prevention

| Misfire | What it looks like | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Labelling instead of describing | "Adapted for a low-IQ, ADHD mum" | Describe the need: "reads short sentences; loses track after 10 minutes of talk." |
| Fidelity as rigidity | Reading the script word for word to a family that does not follow it | Surface changes keep the mechanism; make them and log them. |
| Drift called adaptation | Role-play quietly dropped for six weeks "to save time" | Practice is usually core; that is a red change. |
| Culture as surface only | New names and photos, but discipline content clashes with family roles | Ask the family; treat as deep structure; consult. |
| Engagement as fidelity | Family loves sessions, adherence 3/10 | Score both scales separately. |
| Shrinking instead of stepping up | Cutting content for a family in crisis | Ask whether a more intensive level or clinician fits. |
| Branding a gutted program | "We deliver [program]" with half the core removed | Say "informed by", or restore the core. |

## Adaptations

- **Infant vs teen:** check the version's age range; use a teen version rather than
  stretching an early-years one.
- **Parent with intellectual disability:** shorter chunks, pictures, teach-back; keep
  the practice.
- **Neurodivergent child or parent:** sensory-friendly room, visual agenda, movement
  breaks; aim for access, not looking typical.
- **Language:** approved translations, or a professional interpreter (never a child or
  relative); log it.
- **Telehealth:** check for a developer remote-delivery protocol first.
- **Tribal/Indigenous family:** ask the family and, where they wish, tribal programs
  about culturally grounded versions; ICWA may apply in US child-welfare cases —
  verify locally.
- **LGBTQ+ family:** use the family's own words for parents (green).
- **Low literacy:** read aloud, pictures, voice notes.

## Techniques Used

- **RT-02 Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework** — each component is analysed for mechanism, dose, delivery, core status, and clashing need.
- **DP-03 Anchored Scoring Scales** — adherence and responsiveness rated against 0/5/10 anchors with a review trigger.
- **DP-04 Must-Not Constraints** — licensing, branding, and safety-content lines.
- **DP-05 Stakes-Based Gate Policy** — green, yellow, and red changes need increasing approval, up to developer consultation.
