# Parent Group Hard Moments

**Objective:** Give a parent-group facilitator words for the first minute of a hard
moment, a clear call on how serious it is, the private follow-up, any reporting or
referral steps, a factual incident note, and a plan to repair the group.

**When to Use:**
- A parent says something in group that suggests a child is being harmed, or describes
  violence from a partner.
- A member dominates, the group splits into an argument, or a member defends hitting or
  shaming and others nod along.
- A member cannot stop crying, says "they'd be better off without me," or arrives
  slurring and unsteady.
- **Not this prompt if:**
  - you are planning a session — use
    `domain-parenting/family-support-professional/group-facilitation/parenting_parent_group_session_design.md`;
  - the concern arose on a home visit — use
    `domain-parenting/family-support-professional/home-visit-fieldwork/parenting_home_visit_child_safety_concern.md`;
  - you are writing a routine contact note — use
    `domain-parenting/family-support-professional/plans-documentation/parenting_family_contact_note_writer.md`;
  - you are a licensed clinician deciding whether to report — use
    `domain-psychology/risk-crisis/psychology_mandated_reporter_decision_walkthrough.md`;
    for acute risk mid-session, `domain-psychology/risk-crisis/psychology_crisis_de_escalation_session_plan.md`;
  - you lead a church Bible study — use
    `domain-biblical-studies/group-leader-facilitation/biblical_groupleader_facilitation_dynamics.md`.

## Safeguarding, Consent, and Scope

- **Immediate danger → 911 or the local emergency number first.**
- **Mandated reporting varies — verify locally.** Who must report, the threshold
  (reasonable suspicion or reasonable cause to believe — not proof), the timeline, and
  whether a supervisor consult counts vary by state, province, country, and agency. In
  most US states the duty is personal and is not discharged by telling a supervisor;
  consulting must never delay a report or an emergency call. Reports go to the state or
  county child-protection hotline or law enforcement (Childhelp 1-800-422-4453 can help
  find the number). Whether a child's exposure to partner violence is reportable differs
  between jurisdictions.
- **Do not investigate.** No questioning for detail, no asking to see injuries, no
  photographs, no confronting the person alleged to have caused harm, no promise of
  secrecy. Record exact words, what you saw, the time, and who you told.
- **Telling the family about a report** is decided case by case with your supervisor —
  never when it could endanger anyone.
- **Clinical routes.** Suicide talk → ask directly, privately, then 988 Suicide & Crisis
  Lifeline (US) or emergency services. Partner violence → National DV Hotline
  1-800-799-7233. Possible ingestion by a child → Poison Control 1-800-222-1222. Outside
  the US: local emergency number and findahelpline.com. You support, refer, and follow
  up; licensed clinicians assess and treat.
- **No diagnosis.** Write "speech slurred, smelled of alcohol, unsteady," not "drunk";
  "cried 10 minutes, said she has slept under 3 hours a night for two weeks," not
  "depressed."
- **Privacy.** The rest of the story happens in private, not in group. Share with other
  agencies only with a written release, except where law requires or permits it (such
  as a child-protection report — verify locally). Members may read their records;
  FERPA, HIPAA, 42 CFR Part 2, GDPR, or similar rules may apply — verify locally.

## Inputs / Context

**De-identify first:** roles or initials only — no names, addresses, dates of birth, or
case numbers in an AI tool unless your agency has approved it for client data.

1. **What happened:** exact words if you have them, what you saw, time, who was present.
2. **The member:** voluntary or required attendance, children's ages, whether a child or
   partner is on site.
3. **Setting:** in person or virtual, co-facilitator present, supervisor on-call number,
   agency incident and reporting policy.
4. **Jurisdiction:** state, province, or country — then verify locally.
5. **What you have already done.**

## Method

1. **Classify the stakes before choosing words (DP-05).**
   - **Tier 1 — Emergency now:** child in danger now, weapon, medical emergency,
     intoxicated parent about to drive a child. Call 911 first.
   - **Tier 2 — Report today:** what was said gives reasonable suspicion of child abuse
     or neglect. Report within your local timeline; consult in parallel, not instead.
   - **Tier 3 — Same-day clinical route:** suicide talk; a parent describing hearing or
     seeing things others don't; partner violence with fear for tonight. Private talk
     today and a warm referral (988, DV hotline, crisis team).
   - **Tier 4 — Private support:** distress, a hard week, a values clash.
   - **Tier 5 — Group process only:** monopolising, side-talk, a safe disagreement.
2. **Work in time windows (AG-19).**
   - **First 60 seconds:** slow down, protect the speaker and the group, contain.
   - **Rest of session:** support facilitator takes the private conversation out; lead
     settles the room and continues or ends early.
   - **Within the hour:** private follow-up, Tier 1–3 actions, supervisor told.
   - **Within 24–48 hours:** follow-up call, incident note finished, next-session plan.
3. **Use protective interrupting for disclosures.** Stop the detail without shutting the
   person down: "R., I can hear this is really important, and I want to give it proper
   time. Can we talk just the two of us? Sam can come with you now." To the group:
   "Thank you for listening. Some things need a private space. Let's take a breath."
   In private: restate the confidentiality limits first; listen; ask only what safety
   needs ("Is anyone in danger tonight?").
4. **Hold the must-nots (DP-04).** Do not let a disclosure continue in detail in group;
   promise secrecy; question for detail, ask to see marks, or photograph; confront a
   member's partner or say anything that could reach them; debate a parent into a corner
   in front of others; let an intoxicated parent drive off with a child; label anyone.
5. **Use the moment-specific scripts.**
   - **Monopolising:** "I want to hear from people we haven't heard from yet — quick
     round." Privately at the break: "You bring a lot of experience. Could you help me
     make space for others?"
   - **Harsh punishment endorsed:** honour the goal ("You want kids who respect you —
     everyone here does"), widen it ("What else has worked when you needed them to
     listen?"), share the evidence briefly (physical punishment is linked with more
     behaviour problems over time, not fewer). Hitting that left marks or injury is a
     Tier 2 disclosure, not a discussion; legal thresholds vary — verify locally.
   - **Open conflict:** "Let's pause." Name the agreement ("We disagree without
     put-downs"), take a short break, speak to each person separately.
   - **Tearful:** "Take your time; it's okay to feel this here." Offer a step-out with the
     support facilitator; ask privately how they are getting home and who is with them.
   - **Suicide talk:** privately and directly: "When people say they'd be better off gone,
     sometimes they're thinking about killing themselves. Are you?" If yes, stay with
     them and call or text 988 together; 911 if there is a plan, means at hand, or they
     cannot stay safe.
   - **Intoxication:** privately: "You seem unsteady tonight. I'm worried about you
     getting home safely." Ask whether a child is with them and how they are travelling;
     offer a ride, relative, or taxi. Driving with a child → 911. Reporting the child's
     situation is a Tier 2 question.
6. **Repair the group next session.** No member details: "Last week was a hard night.
   Here's a reminder of our agreements. If anything stayed with you, I'm around after."
   Offer private check-ins.
7. **Write the incident note and handoff (NE-20).** One self-contained record a
   supervisor or the next worker can act on: exact words, observations, times, tier,
   actions, who was told and when, report reference, follow-up owner. Facts only,
   written knowing the member may read it.

## Output Format

```
# Hard-Moment Plan — [moment]   Group: [..]   Session: [n]   Date: [..]
## What happened (exact words, observations, time)
## Tier [1–5] — reason
## First 60 seconds: to the member / to the group
## Rest of session: lead does / support does
## Within the hour: private conversation, actions, supervisor told
## Reporting or referral steps (jurisdiction: [..] — verify locally)
## Within 24–48 hours
## Next session: group repair, private check-ins
## Incident note
| Time | Exact words / observation | Action | Who told | Owner |
```

## Verification

- [ ] The moment has a tier and a stated reason.
- [ ] Any Tier 1 step names 911 or the local emergency number first.
- [ ] Any Tier 2 step says the reporter reports, and consultation neither replaces nor
      delays it.
- [ ] The disclosure script stops detail in group without silencing the member.
- [ ] No step promises secrecy, interrogates, inspects injuries, or photographs.
- [ ] Suicide talk gets a direct question and 988 or emergency services.
- [ ] The incident note holds exact words and observations, no diagnostic labels.
- [ ] A group-repair step is planned.

## False-Positive Prevention

| Misfire | What it looks like | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Labelling instead of describing | "She was drunk, clearly an addict" | "Slurred speech, smell of alcohol, stumbled at 6:40." |
| Letting the disclosure run | Ten minutes of abuse detail in group | Protective interrupting; take it private. |
| Silencing the disclosure | "We don't talk about that here" | "This matters; let's talk privately now." |
| Supervisor call treated as the report | "I told my manager, it's handled" | In most US states the duty is personal; verify locally. |
| Every heated moment as a crisis | Calling a supervisor over a bedtime disagreement | Tier 5: handle it in the room. |
| Public values debate | Lecturing a parent who defends spanking | Honour the goal, widen options, check privately for harm. |
| Investigating | "Show me the bruise," "Who exactly did it?" | Ask only what safety needs; report; investigators investigate. |

## Adaptations

- **Infants vs teens:** with infants, any mention of shaking, falls, or unexplained marks
  is Tier 2; a parent afraid of their teen's violence may need a family safety plan,
  not a report.
- **Neurodivergent parent or parent with an intellectual disability:** a blunt remark
  may not mean what it sounds like — ask privately; plain words, one question at a time.
- **Interpreter present:** pause interpretation during protective interrupting; the
  private talk uses the interpreter too, never a family member.
- **Virtual group:** breakout room with the support facilitator; confirm members'
  locations at the start.
- **Tribal or Indigenous family (US):** ICWA may apply if a report leads to child-welfare
  involvement; know your agency's tribal-notification practice.
- **LGBTQ+ parent:** partner violence happens in every kind of relationship; don't
  assume the partner's gender.
- **Parent who is a minor:** reporting rules may cover the young parent's own safety —
  verify locally.

to give more detail.

## Tier 2 + 3 — children present during violence (reportability of exposure

## Techniques Used

- **AG-19 Time-Critical Response Protocol** — actions split into the first 60 seconds, rest of session, the hour after, and 24–48 hours.
- **DP-05 Stakes-Based Gate Policy** — five tiers decide between 911, a report today, a same-day clinical route, private support, or handling it in the room.
- **DP-04 Must-Not Constraints** — no detail in group, no secrecy, no investigating, no confronting partners, no intoxicated driving with a child, no labels.
- **NE-20 Third-Party Handoff Package** — a self-contained incident note a supervisor or the next worker can act on.
