# Parent Group Session Design

**Objective:** Produce a ready-to-run plan for one session of a parent education group:
one learning goal, a timed agenda where parents practise rather than listen, group
agreements that say plainly what stays private and what cannot, access settled before
content, clear facilitator roles, and an after-session check that catches safety
concerns, absences, and what to change next week.

**When to Use:**
- You run or co-run a parent group (community program, family centre, school, home-
  visiting program, faith or tribal community, court- or agency-referred class) and need
  to plan the next session, or a strong first session.
- Attendance is dropping and you suspect the format, not the parents, is the problem.
- **Not this prompt if:**
  - a hard moment is happening or has happened (disclosure, a member taking over,
    conflict, an intoxicated attendee) — use
    `domain-parenting/family-support-professional/group-facilitation/parenting_parent_group_hard_moments.md`;
  - you are adapting an evidence-based curriculum and need core vs adaptable — use
    `domain-parenting/family-support-professional/coaching-education/parenting_curriculum_fidelity_adaptation.md`;
  - you are a licensed clinician designing a treatment group — use
    `domain-psychology/modalities/group/psychology_group_therapy_curriculum_builder.md`;
  - you teach health-professions students — use
    `domain-medical-education/educator-curriculum-design/curric_small_group_facilitation_guide.md`;
  - you are a parent running a meeting at home — use
    `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/cross-age/parenting_family_meeting_facilitator.md`.

## Safeguarding, Consent, and Scope

- **Education and support, not therapy.** The session teaches and practises skills. It
  does not process trauma, treat anyone, or assess anyone.
- **No diagnosis.** Describe what you see and hear ("cried twice, said she sleeps about
  3 hours a night"), never a label ("she's depressed," "he's obviously ADHD").
- **Confidentiality limits are said before anyone shares** — at enrolment and in
  session 1: what stays in the group; what facilitators must act on (a child at risk of
  harm, someone's life in danger, a court order); and that facilitators cannot guarantee
  other members will keep things private.
- **Mandated reporting varies — verify locally.** Who must report, the threshold
  (reasonable suspicion or reasonable cause to believe — not proof), the timeline, and
  whether a supervisor consult counts depend on state, province, country, and agency. In
  most US states the duty is personal and is not discharged by telling a supervisor;
  consulting must never delay a report or an emergency call. Immediate danger → 911 or
  local emergency number first. Reports go to the state or county child-protection
  hotline or law enforcement (Childhelp 1-800-422-4453 can help find the number). Do not
  investigate or promise secrecy; record exact words, what you saw, when, and who you told.
- **Routes on every session sheet:** 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (US) for suicide
  talk; 911 for immediate danger; National DV Hotline 1-800-799-7233; Poison Control
  1-800-222-1222; outside the US, local emergency number and findahelpline.com.
  Facilitators refer and follow up; licensed clinicians assess and treat.
- **Partner violence and composition.** Never place a survivor and the person harming
  them in the same group; check protective orders before enrolling co-parents together.
  Screen each adult alone before the group starts.
- **Privacy.** Written release before sharing anything with another agency (except where
  law requires it, such as a child-protection report — verify locally). Referrers of
  required attendees get only what the parent agreed to in writing. FERPA, HIPAA, 42 CFR
  Part 2, or GDPR-type rules may apply — verify locally. Members may read their records.

## Inputs / Context

**De-identify first:** roles or initials only — no names, addresses, dates of birth, or
case numbers in an AI tool unless your agency has approved it for client data.

1. **Program and session:** curriculum, session number, the one skill, length.
2. **Group:** number enrolled, children's ages, voluntary vs required (count only),
   languages, literacy range, access needs, attendance pattern.
3. **Setting:** in person, virtual, or hybrid; room; time of day.
4. **Facilitators:** who, languages, reporting lead on duty, supervisor on-call number.
5. **Resources:** screened childcare, food, transport help, interpreters, materials.
6. **Last session:** what landed, home-practice uptake, any open concern.

## Method

1. **Build one skill as a practice cycle (DS-01).** Apply Kolb's experiential learning
   cycle — concrete experience, reflection, a new idea, active experimentation: check in
   on last week's home practice, a short teaching point (8–12 minutes) with a
   demonstration, paired practice with coaching, then each parent's home-practice plan.
   If the session has three goals, cut two.
2. **Settle access before content.** Childcare in a separate room with agency-screened
   staff; food served first, never as a reward; named transport help; a start time that
   fits shifts and school pick-up. A qualified interpreter — never a child or relative —
   and picture-based handouts. Never ask anyone to read aloud or write in front of
   others. Virtual: camera optional, phone dial-in, a private-space check ("Is now a good
   time to talk about your kids?").
3. **Write agreements with the limits inside them.** Session 1 script: "What people share
   here stays here. There are times I can't keep something private: if I'm worried a
   child is being hurt, if someone's life is in danger, or if a court orders it. If that
   comes up, I'll talk with you first if it's safe to. I also can't promise everyone here
   will keep things private, so share what you're comfortable with." Add: you can always
   pass; talk about your own family; first names only for children; no recording. Later
   sessions: a 30-second reminder.
4. **Set the must-nots (DP-04).** The plan must not require sharing trauma or a "worst
   moment"; role-play hitting, abuse, or injury; ask parents to label their children;
   single out required attendees; put anyone on the spot to read, write, or perform;
   leave a harsh-discipline endorsement unaddressed; or promise outcomes.
5. **Design weekly trust deposits (DP-18).** Name 4–6 small, countable acts: start and
   end on time; greet every parent by name; the same opening ritual; return to what each
   parent planned last week ("You were going to try the 10-minute warning — how did it
   go?"); keep every small promise. Group trust builds in repetitions, not icebreakers.
6. **Make practice safe and specific.** Pairs or triads, not performances. A short script
   card. Coach a strength first ("You got down to her eye level — that's the move"),
   then one adjustment. Ask what the behaviour might be telling the parent before how to
   stop it.
7. **Assign roles.** **Lead** runs content and time. **Support** watches the room, sits
   near the door, and takes any private conversation out. Name the emergency caller, who
   holds the supervisor number, and a signal phrase ("Can you grab the extra handouts?"
   = please check on that person). Solo facilitator: name who in the building is on call.
8. **Fill the timed agenda (ST-03)** using the Output Format; times add up with 5
   minutes of slack.
9. **Plan the after-session check.** Fifteen minutes, both facilitators, before leaving:
   any disclosure or concern → reporting steps above, today; who to call this week; one
   change for next session; notes limited to attendance and participation. Watch for
   clinician triggers — any suicide talk; a parent describing hearing or seeing things
   others don't; a child's development a parent is very worried about; heavy substance
   use — and route them privately.

## Output Format

```
# Session [n] of [total] — [skill]   Date: [..]   Length: [..] min
Group: [enrolled, children's ages, languages, attendance mix]
Learning goal: each parent practises [skill] once and leaves with a home-practice plan.

## Access checklist (owner)
Childcare | Food | Transport | Interpreter | Materials | Disability | Virtual
## Group agreements (session 1 full; later 30-second reminder)
## Timed agenda
| Time | Segment | Who leads | What parents do |
## Practice activity (script card, pairing, coaching prompts)
## Must-nots this session
## Trust deposits this week
## Roles: lead / support / emergency caller / supervisor number / signal
## After-session check: concerns and steps taken / calls this week / observations (described) / one change
```

## Verification

- [ ] One named skill; every parent practises it at least once.
- [ ] Teaching is no more than one-third of the session.
- [ ] The confidentiality-limits script comes before any sharing segment.
- [ ] Every access item has an owner or "not needed" with a reason.
- [ ] No activity requires disclosure, reading aloud, writing in front of others, or
      role-playing harm.
- [ ] Roles name an emergency caller and the supervisor on-call number.
- [ ] The after-session check covers reporting steps, follow-up calls, and one change.
- [ ] No diagnostic label for any parent or child appears.

## False-Positive Prevention

| Misfire | What it looks like | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Lecture as a session | 60 minutes of slides | Teaching under one-third; practice is the core. |
| Agreements without limits | "Everything here is confidential" | Name reporting, danger, and court-order limits first. |
| Labelling instead of describing | "Mum in row 2 seems bipolar" in notes | Write what was said and seen, with frequency. |
| Low attendance read as low motivation | "These parents don't care" | Check time, childcare, transport, food, language, and felt safety first. |
| Sharing as a requirement | "Tell us your hardest moment" | Offer, never require; passing is always allowed. |
| Supervisor call treated as the report | "I told my manager, so it's done" | In most US states the duty is personal; verify locally. |
| Group drifting into therapy | Facilitator processing a parent's own childhood abuse | Acknowledge, contain, refer to a clinician privately. |

## Adaptations

- **Infants vs teens:** babies in the room and short segments; teen topics need more
  discussion, less demonstration.
- **Neurodivergent parents or children:** visual agenda, materials in advance, movement
  breaks, no surprise role-play; frame differences as support needs, not deficits.
- **Parent with an intellectual disability:** one idea per segment, repeated practice,
  picture cards; check understanding by doing.
- **Interpreter:** brief beforehand, short chunks, 40–50% more time; consider a
  same-language group.
- **Rural or telehealth:** shorter sessions, dial-in, mailed materials.
- **Tribal or Indigenous families:** invite knowledge-keepers where the community wishes;
  in the US, ICWA may affect child-welfare-referred families — verify with the agency.
- **LGBTQ+ and military families, parents who are minors:** use the family's words for
  roles; plan around deployments; teen parents may need different consent rules —
  verify locally.

## Example Output

```
# Session 4 of 8 — Giving clear instructions   Tue 14 Oct   105 min
Group: 9 enrolled, children 2–8; 6 English, 3 Spanish; 2 attending under an agency plan
Learning goal: each parent practises a "close, calm, clear" instruction once and
picks one time at home to use it this week.

## Access checklist
Childcare: room B, two screened staff (Rosa)   Food: tacos at 5:45, served first
Transport: 3 bus passes; J. rides with L. (carpool confirmed)
Interpreter: Ana (agency), briefed 5:30   Materials: EN/ES picture card; large print for P.

## Group agreements (reminder)
"You can pass, talk about your own family, first names for kids, no photos. What's
shared stays here, unless I'm worried about a child's safety or someone's life, or a
court orders it. I'd talk to you first if it's safe."

## Timed agenda
| 5:45 | Food, greet each parent by name         | Both       | Eat, settle              |
| 6:00 | Home-practice round (pass OK)           | Lead       | One thing they tried     |
| 6:15 | Teaching: close, calm, clear (10 min)   | Lead       | Listen, ask              |
| 6:25 | Demo: vague vs clear instruction        | Both       | Spot the difference      |
| 6:35 | Paired practice, 2 rounds each          | Both coach | Practise with card       |
| 7:00 | Break                                   | Support    | —                        |
| 7:10 | "What might the 'no' be telling you?"   | Lead       | Pairs, share if wanted   |
| 7:20 | Home-practice plan                      | Both       | Say or draw one plan     |
| 7:25 | Closing ritual + slack                  | Lead       | One word for the week    |

## Practice activity
Card: 1) Get close. 2) Calm voice. 3) "Shoes on, please." (one step, said once)
Coach: "You knelt down first — great." Then: "Try it without 'okay?' at the end."

## Must-nots
No worst moments. No reading aloud. No role-play of yelling or hitting.
Nobody identified as an "agency" parent.

## Trust deposits
Start 6:00 sharp. Ask M. how her bedtime warning went. Bring the promised
Spanish handout. Bus passes at the door.

## Roles
Lead: Dee   Support: Sam (by door)   Emergency caller: Sam
Supervisor on-call: inside folder cover   Signal: "Can you grab the extra handouts?"

## After-session check (7:35–7:50)
Concerns: none
Call this week: T. (absent 2 weeks); K. (left at break, tearful)
Change: demo ran 3 min over — one example next week
```

## Techniques Used

- **DS-01 Framework Application** — Kolb's experiential learning cycle structures the session: experience, reflect, new idea, practise.
- **DP-04 Must-Not Constraints** — no required disclosure, role-play of harm, reading aloud, singling out, or promised outcomes.
- **DP-18 Trust Deposits Definition** — small weekly acts (on time, names, follow-through) that build group safety.
- **ST-03 Output Format Specification** — a fixed template for access, agreements, agenda, roles, and the after-session check.
