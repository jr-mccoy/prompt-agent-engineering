# Family Time Visit Supervision

**Objective:** Run one supervised family-time visit between a child in care and a
birth parent so it is as safe and as good as it can be: both sides prepared, the
parent coached toward what goes well, problems redirected early, the visit ended only
at clear thresholds, and a factual note the court or agency can rely on.

**When to Use:**
- You supervise or coach visits between a child in foster or kinship care and a
  parent, sibling, or relative.
- A parent is struggling at visits (late, upset, talking about the case, harsh) and
  you need scripts and a ladder before the next one.
- Your notes are read by the caseworker, lawyers, the child's guardian, and the court.
- **Not this prompt if** you are drafting the **order or legal conditions** for
  supervised contact — use `domain-legal/custody/legal_supervised_visitation_and_safety_plan.md`.
  The **foster carer** preparing the child and handling the evening after uses
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/coparenting-family-structure/parenting_foster_placement_first_weeks.md`.
  A general contact note →
  `domain-parenting/family-support-professional/plans-documentation/parenting_family_contact_note_writer.md`;
  a clinician's collateral note → `domain-psychology/documentation/psychology_collateral_contact_note.md`;
  carer strain after hard visits →
  `domain-parenting/family-support-professional/foster-kinship-adoption/parenting_resource_parent_stability_support.md`.

## Safeguarding, Consent, and Scope

- **This prompt does not diagnose or recommend contact changes.** Record what you
  saw and heard. "Mum's speech was slurred and she held the table to stand at 3:10
  pm," not "Mum was drunk." "A. turned away and said 'no' when Dad reached for him,
  then sat on his lap at 3:25," not "poor attachment." Contact decisions belong to
  the caseworker and court.
- **Immediate danger → 911** (or local emergency number). Do not restrain a parent or
  block an exit; keep the child with you and move toward safety.
- **Disclosure during a visit.** Listen, stay calm, do not question for detail, do
  not confront the parent in front of the child, and write the exact words with the
  time. Who must report, the threshold (reasonable suspicion, not proof), timelines,
  and whether a supervisor consult counts vary by state, province, country, and
  agency — **verify locally**. In most US states the duty is personal and a consult
  must never delay a report. Report to the state or county child-protection hotline
  or law enforcement (Childhelp 1-800-422-4453 can help find the number) and tell
  the caseworker the same day. Do not photograph injuries or promise secrecy. Decide
  with your supervisor whether and when the parent is told.
- **Clinical and crisis routes:** a parent talks about suicide → 988 (US) now; a
  parent describes partner violence → National DV Hotline 1-800-799-7233, in
  private, never in front of the child; a parent in apparent mental-health crisis →
  end kindly, call the caseworker or mobile crisis; possible ingestion → Poison
  Control 1-800-222-1222. Outside the US: local emergency number, findahelpline.com.
  You support and route; you do not assess or treat.
- **Consent — say it before the first visit:** "I'm here to keep the visit safe and
  help it go well. I write down what I see and hear. The notes go to the caseworker
  and can be read by your lawyer and the court. If I see or hear something that
  means a child may be unsafe, I have to report it." Never share the carer's address
  or school unless the agency says so. Parents may read the notes.

## Inputs / Context

1. **De-identify first.** Roles or initials only ("Mum D.," "child J., 4"). No
   names, addresses, dates of birth, or case numbers, unless your agency has approved
   this tool for client data.
2. **The order and plan:** supervision level, who may attend, length, place,
   permitted topics, food, gifts, phones, photos, language.
3. **Child:** age, feeding, sleep, medication, sensory needs; how past visits went.
4. **Parent:** case-plan goals, strengths seen, past difficulties, language,
   disability or literacy needs.
5. **Safety:** protective orders, people barred, testing conditions, exits.

## Method

1. **Work in time windows (AG-19).** **24 hours before:** confirm with the parent;
   name one thing that went well last time and one to try. **15 minutes before:**
   room, age-right toys, your seat near the door, phone charged. **Arrival:** greet
   the parent alone first and check readiness. **During:** coach and observe.
   **Last 10 minutes:** goodbye. **After:** handover, parent debrief, note within 24
   hours.
2. **Prepare child and parent.** Child: "After lunch you'll see Mum at the centre. I'll
   be in the room the whole time." Parent: "What would you like to do with J. today?"
   Rules in plain words: "No talk about court or going home. If it comes up, I'll
   steer us back."
3. **Hold the must-nots (DP-04).** The supervisor must not: promise outcomes; take
   sides or discuss the carer's home; leave parent and child out of sight or hearing
   when the order says supervised; question the child afterwards; end a visit as
   punishment; use evaluative words in notes ("appropriate," "bonded"); share the
   carer's location.
4. **Coach toward strengths.** Name what works as it happens: "He looked at you when
   you sang — he loves that." One quiet suggestion at a time: "Try getting down on
   the floor with him." For infants, support feeding, nappy changes, and safe
   holding.
5. **Use the redirect → pause → end ladder (DP-05).** **Redirect** (once, calmly):
   case talk, promises, whispering, asking about the foster home, harsh words, phone
   use. "Let's keep today about you and J. — what about the blocks?" **Pause** (step
   out with the parent; child with a second staff member): the same issue after a
   redirect, rising anger. "Let's take two minutes outside. I want this visit to keep
   going." **End** (no pause needed): threats or violence, an attempt to leave with
   the child, a parent too impaired to keep the child safe, coaching the child about
   a disclosure, a barred person arriving, unsafe infant handling after a prompt. To
   the child: "We're finishing early today. It's not because of anything you did."
   Then call the caseworker.
6. **Manage the goodbye.** 10-minute warning. A short, warm goodbye, with a next date
   only if confirmed. Hand over to the carer with facts only.
7. **Write every line from evidence (RT-05).** Time stamps; who was present; what
   parent and child did and said, in quotes; strengths; each redirect, pause, or end
   with trigger and outcome. Interpretation, if your agency allows it, goes in a
   separate labelled section.
8. **Make the note stand alone (NE-20).** A reader who was not there — the court, a
   new caseworker — must understand it. Put action flags (disclosure, injury, ended
   early) at the top.

## Output Format

```
# Family Time Note — [child initials, age] with [parent role]
Date: [..]  Scheduled: [..]  Actual: [..]  Level: [..]  Venue: [type]
Present: [roles]   Interpreter: [yes/no, language]

## Action flags (disclosure / injury / ended early / none)
## Preparation
## Observations  | Time | Parent did/said | Child did/said |
## Strengths observed
## Coaching offered and response
## Redirects / pauses / ending (trigger → script → outcome)
## Goodbye and handover
## Worker interpretation (only if agency allows; labelled)
## Follow-up: [who, what, by when]
```

## Verification

- [ ] Every observation has a time and was seen or heard, not inferred.
- [ ] Parent's and child's words appear in quotes.
- [ ] At least two specific strengths are recorded.
- [ ] Each redirect, pause, or end names trigger, script, and outcome.
- [ ] No "appropriate," "bonded," "drunk," or diagnosis outside a labelled interpretation section.
- [ ] Any disclosure is flagged at the top with exact words and the report route.
- [ ] Confidentiality limits were explained before the first visit.

## False-Positive Prevention

| Misfire | What it looks like | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Labelling instead of describing | "Mum was inappropriate and aggressive" | "At 3:41 Mum said 'Shut up' to J. and slammed the bag down." |
| Reading distress as harm | Child cries at goodbye → "visits upset him" | Crying at goodbye is common and can show connection. |
| Reading quiet as unattached | "No bond observed" | Record what the child did; many children warm up slowly. |
| Ending too fast | Visit ended after one comment about court | Redirect first; end only at listed thresholds. |
| Missing strengths | Note lists only problems | Courts need the full picture. |
| Supervisor as investigator | Asking the child "Did Mum hit you?" | Listen, record, report; do not question. |
| Banning the home language | "English only so I can follow" | Professional interpreter; the family keeps its language. |

## Adaptations

- **Infant:** plan around feeds and naps; record eye contact, soothing, and turn-taking, not ratings.
- **Teen:** a say in activities and length; respect and record a refusal.
- **Sibling group:** enough staff; one-to-one time for each child.
- **Parent with intellectual disability or low literacy:** visual reminders, rehearsal, short coaching phrases; read the note aloud if wanted.
- **Neurodivergent child or parent:** quiet room, predictable routine; stimming or little eye contact is not a problem.
- **Tribal or Indigenous family (US, ICWA):** extended family, cultural activities, and the tribe's worker may be in the plan.
- **Virtual visits:** short and active (songs, books), carer nearby; record dropouts.

## Example Output

```
# Family Time Note — J., 4, and baby E., 9 months, with Mum (D.)
Date: 7 Oct  Scheduled: 3:00–4:30  Actual: 3:06–4:30
Level: coached  Venue: family centre   Present: Mum, J., E., supervisor

## Action flags: none

## Preparation
2:50 Mum in lobby: "I'm nervous, he cried last time." Agreed floor
play, snack at 3:45, no court talk. J. arrived 3:04 with carer.

## Observations
| 3:06 | "Hi, my J.!" knelt, arms open     | Stood by door 20 sec, ran to her |
| 3:15 | Fed E. upright, burped her        | E. drank 150 ml, watched her face |
| 3:30 | Built tower, counted blocks aloud | J.: "Again! Mummy do it again"    |
| 3:42 | "You'll be home for Christmas"    | J. looked at supervisor           |
| 3:50 | Gave sliced apple, asked J. to sit| Sat 6 min, ate                    |
| 4:10 | Changed E.'s nappy, talked to her | E. babbled, kicked legs           |

## Strengths observed
Greeted J. at his level. Safe feeding and nappy change without prompts.
Soft voice when J. threw a block at 4:02: "Gentle hands, we build."

## Coaching
3:20 Suggested narrating J.'s play. Mum: "Like a commentator?" Did so
for 10 min; J. laughed and stayed close.

## Redirect
3:42 Trigger: promise about going home. Script: "Let's keep today about
the tower — can you beat your record?" Mum: "Sorry." No repeat.

## Goodbye and handover
4:20 warning. Mum: "Bye, see you Tuesday" (confirmed). J. cried 2 min,
waved. Handover 4:30: both ate, E. no nap, no injuries.

## Follow-up
Supervisor texts Mum the Tuesday plan by 9 Oct.
```

## Techniques Used

- **AG-19 Time-Critical Response Protocol** — work set in windows: 24 hours before, 15 minutes before, arrival, during, goodbye, after.
- **DP-04 Must-Not Constraints** — the supervisor's hard limits on promises, side-taking, gaps in supervision, questioning, and note wording.
- **DP-05 Stakes-Based Gate Policy** — the redirect, pause, and end ladder with explicit triggers and scripts.
- **RT-05 Evidence-Based Reasoning** — every note line tied to a time and something seen or heard.
- **NE-20 Third-Party Handoff Package** — the note stands alone for readers who were not there, with flags on top.
