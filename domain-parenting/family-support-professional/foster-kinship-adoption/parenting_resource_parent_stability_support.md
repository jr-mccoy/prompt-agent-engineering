# Resource Parent Stability Support

**Objective:** Help a worker keep a strained foster or kinship placement together —
or, if it cannot hold, make the move planned rather than an emergency. The output is
a strain check on anchored scales, the one driver that matters most, a support plan,
and a stability meeting with a review date.

**When to Use:**
- A carer says "I don't know how much longer we can do this," or asks about the
  notice period.
- Behaviour calls, school exclusions, or missed visits have risen over recent weeks.
- A kinship carer is exhausted, unpaid, or unsure of their legal position.
- **Not this prompt if** you are the **carer** planning a new placement — use
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/coparenting-family-structure/parenting_foster_placement_first_weeks.md`
  or, for relatives,
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/coparenting-family-structure/parenting_kinship_care_first_months.md`.
  If the child is hurting people at home now, the carer also needs
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/mental-health-behavior/parenting_aggression_toward_family_safety_plan.md`
  today. Supervising contact →
  `domain-parenting/family-support-professional/foster-kinship-adoption/parenting_family_time_visit_supervision.md`;
  referring to a clinician →
  `domain-parenting/family-support-professional/referral-resourcing/parenting_clinician_referral_by_nonclinician.md`;
  kinship custody questions → a lawyer and `domain-legal/custody/legal_third_party_custody_visitation_analysis.md`.

## Safeguarding, Consent, and Scope

- **This prompt does not diagnose.** Describe behaviour, frequency, and the carer's
  own words. "Screamed and threw shoes at bedtime 5 of the last 7 nights, 20–40
  minutes each," not "attachment disorder." "Said she has cried in the car after
  pick-up three times this week," not "carer is depressed."
- **Support is not a substitute for a report.** Reasonable suspicion that the child
  is harmed in the placement — physical punishment, food withheld, a child locked in
  or out, an unapproved adult living there — is a safeguarding matter. Who must
  report, the threshold (reasonable suspicion, not proof), timelines, and whether a
  supervisor consult counts vary by state, province, country, and agency — **verify
  locally**. In most US states the duty is personal and is not discharged by telling
  a supervisor; a consult must never delay a report. Report to the state or county
  child-protection hotline or law enforcement (Childhelp 1-800-422-4453 can help
  find the number); foster-home concerns usually also go to licensing. Immediate
  danger → **911** first. Do not investigate, interrogate, photograph injuries, or
  promise secrecy. Record exact words, what you saw, when, and who you told. Decide
  with your supervisor whether telling the carer is safe.
- **Route to a licensed clinician when:** the child talks about dying or self-harm
  (988, US, same day); aggression injures someone; sexualised behaviour toward other
  children in the home (same-day safety plan and assessment); nightmares or rages on
  most nights for over a month; a carer talks about harming themselves (988) or
  drinking or using to cope; partner violence comes to light (National DV Hotline
  1-800-799-7233). Possible ingestion → Poison Control 1-800-222-1222. Outside the
  US: local emergency number, findahelpline.com. You support, refer, and follow up;
  you do not treat.
- **Consent.** Before the check, say: "What you tell me goes in your supervision
  record. If I hear that a child may be unsafe, I have to report it, and a court can
  order records." Share the child's information only as the agency authorises, and
  the carer's own health information only with their written release. Carers and
  young people may read these records later.

## Inputs / Context

1. **De-identify first.** Roles or initials only ("carer R.," "child T., 10"). No
   names, addresses, dates of birth, or case numbers, unless your agency has approved
   this tool for client data.
2. **Placement:** foster or kinship, length, prior placements, permanency goal.
3. **What changed:** the carer's words, when strain began, incidents with frequency
   and duration, school calls, visit and court changes.
4. **Household:** other children, partner, shifts, carer health.
5. **Supports in place:** respite, therapy, school support, training, money.
6. **Agency rules:** respite limits, notice period, discipline policy.

## Method

1. **Read the signal and set the clock (DP-05).** **Support** — strain rising, carer
   wants to continue: act within two weeks. **Stability meeting** — the carer
   mentions ending: meet within 5 working days. **Urgent** — "come and get him
   tonight," or someone hurt: same day, with your supervisor. **Safeguarding** — any
   concern about the care itself: report route first.
2. **Check strain across eight dimensions (RT-02).** Child behaviour; carer sleep and
   health; other children and partner; practical load; support and respite;
   relationship with the agency; family time and birth-family contact; training fit.
   Placements often fail on the dimension nobody asked about.
3. **Score each on an anchored 1–3 scale (DP-03).** 1 = manageable ("one hard evening
   a week, carer recovers by morning"); 2 = strained ("hard most days, carer has
   cancelled own appointments"); 3 = breaking ("carer not coping, other children
   affected, ending mentioned"). Score with the carer: "Which of these is a 3?"
4. **Name the dominant driver (DP-06).** Ask: "If one thing changed, which would help
   most?" Check the incident log and the 30 minutes before incidents. A driver is
   specific — "bedtimes on the three nights K. works" — not "behaviour."
5. **Build behaviour support around the driver.** Stay function-curious: what is the
   behaviour doing for the child (safety, control, closeness, escape)? Build on the
   carer's strengths with one or two regulation-first changes. Dangerous or
   long-standing behaviour → in-home behaviour support or a trauma-informed
   assessment. You do not design therapy.
6. **Offer respite as planned support.** Same respite carer each time if possible.
   Tell the child: "It's a sleepover, not a move. You're back Sunday."
7. **Close the training gap.** Match one session to the driver — for example,
   trauma reminders (NCTSN Resource Parent Curriculum or TBRI-based training, if your
   agency offers them), managing contact, neurodivergence, or caring across cultures.
8. **Hold a stability meeting before any notice (NE-20).** Carers, caseworker, the
   child's voice (in person or their words brought by a trusted adult), school or
   therapist if relevant. Write a summary a new worker could act on: strengths,
   driver, supports with owners and dates, review date. If notice still comes, plan
   an ending: goodbye, belongings packed with care, life-story record kept, contact
   afterwards if safe and wanted.

## Output Format

```
# Placement Stability Plan — [child initials, age] with [carer roles]
Tier: [support / stability meeting / urgent / safeguarding]   Date: [..]

## The carer's words (quoted)
## Strain check  | Dimension | Score 1–3 | Evidence (observable, frequency) |
## Dominant driver (and why)
## What is working
## Supports  | Support | Owner | Start | Sign it helped |
## Clinical routes (triggers met? referral? who consents?)
## Safeguarding check (concern about care? route taken)
## Stability meeting: attendees, child's voice, decisions
## Review date and what would change the tier
```

## Verification

- [ ] Every strain score has observable evidence with frequency or duration.
- [ ] One dominant driver is named, and supports target it.
- [ ] No diagnostic label is applied to the child or carer.
- [ ] Respite is explained to the child as temporary, with a return date.
- [ ] Each support has an owner, a start date, and a sign that it helped.
- [ ] Any concern about the care itself is routed to a report.
- [ ] A stability meeting is booked before notice is accepted, unless urgent.

## False-Positive Prevention

| Misfire | What it looks like | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Labelling instead of describing | "He has RAD"; "carer is burnt out" | What happened, how often, the carer's words. |
| Treating notice as the problem | All effort on persuading the carer to stay | Find the driver; heard carers often stay. |
| Respite as failure | Carer told respite "looks bad" | Planned respite protects placements. |
| Folding harm into strain | Carer smacked the child "because she's exhausted" | Physical punishment → report route. |
| Blaming visits | "Contact unsettles him, stop it" | Post-visit upset is common; the court decides contact. |
| Kinship carer held to stranger rules | Grandmother lectured in licensing jargon | Plain words; kinship navigator and legal help. |

## Adaptations

- **Infant:** strain sits in sleep and feeding; count night wakings; offer day respite.
- **Teen:** include them in the meeting; phone, friends, and independence often drive conflict.
- **Neurodivergent child:** check sensory load, transitions, and school supports before calling it "defiance."
- **Kinship carer:** often older, unpaid, grieving; check money, legal status, and pressure from the birth parent.
- **Tribal or Indigenous child (US, ICWA):** involve the tribe's worker; placement preferences and active efforts may apply.
- **LGBTQ+ youth:** ask whether identity is part of the conflict; affirming care is a stability factor.
- **Language:** professional interpreter, never the child.

## Example Output

```
# Placement Stability Plan — T., 10, with carers R. and K. (foster, 7 months)
Tier: stability meeting   Date: 6 Oct

## The carer's words
R., 3 Oct: "We can't do another month like this. What's the notice?"
K.: "We want to keep him. We just can't sleep."

## Strain check
| Child behaviour    | 3 | Bedtime screaming, kicking door 5 of 7 nights, 20–40 min |
| Carer sleep/health | 3 | R. 4–5 hrs sleep for 3 weeks; missed own GP appt          |
| Other children     | 2 | Son, 6, asked to sleep in parents' room 4 nights          |
| Practical load     | 2 | 3 school pick-ups after calls in 4 weeks; R. lost shifts  |
| Support/respite    | 3 | No respite in 7 months; no family nearby                  |
| Agency             | 2 | "Nobody calls back after 5 pm"                            |
| Family time        | 2 | Thursday visits; worst nights Thu and Fri                 |
| Training fit       | 2 | Pre-approval training only                                |

## Dominant driver
Bedtimes on the 3 nights K. works: R. alone with two children.
6 of 8 logged incidents fell on those nights.

## What is working
T. calls K. "my K." Mornings are calm. R. and K. keep every visit and
speak well of T.'s mum.

## Supports
| Same respite carer, 1 weekend/month      | Me     | 18 Oct  | R. sleeps 7+ hrs |
| Support worker 7–9 pm on K.'s shifts, 4 wks | Me  | 9 Oct   | Fewer incidents those nights |
| Bedtime: same story, lamp on, R. in doorway | R.  | tonight | Episodes under 20 min |
| Quiet Thursday evenings after visits     | R./K.  | 9 Oct   | Thu/Fri log |
| Resource Parent Curriculum, trauma reminders | Trainer | 15 Oct | Both attend |

## Clinical routes
Rages 5 of 7 nights for 3+ weeks → caseworker requesting trauma-informed
assessment (agency consents). No self-harm talk, no injuries. R. will
book her GP; signed release to mention sleep loss in the meeting only.

## Safeguarding check
Asked how bedtimes end. R.: "I sit with him until he's done." No
punishment or locked doors described. No concern identified.

## Stability meeting — 10 Oct, carers' home
R., K., caseworker, me. T.'s words via K.: "I don't want another house.
I hate night-time." Notice question parked until review.

## Review: 31 Oct
Urgent if anyone is hurt or R. says "tonight." Support tier if bedtime
incidents fall to 2 a week or fewer.
```

## Techniques Used

- **RT-02 Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework** — strain is checked across eight separate dimensions.
- **DP-03 Anchored Scoring Scales** — each dimension is scored 1–3 against observable anchors.
- **DP-06 Dominant Driver Identification** — the plan names the one factor driving strain and targets it.
- **DP-05 Stakes-Based Gate Policy** — support, stability-meeting, urgent, and safeguarding tiers set speed and who is involved.
- **NE-20 Third-Party Handoff Package** — the meeting summary lets any worker act on it.
