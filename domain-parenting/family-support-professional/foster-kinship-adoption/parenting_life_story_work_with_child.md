# Life Story Work with a Foster or Adopted Child

**Objective:** Help a child in care, or adopted from care, understand their own story
with sourced facts, words that fit their age, and a pace they can manage. The output
is a sourced history, a wording sheet, a session plan, a trigger calendar, and a
decision on who leads.

**When to Use:**
- A child in long-term foster, kinship, or adoptive care has gaps or confusions in
  their story ("Did I do something bad?", "Is my real mum dead?").
- An adoption or permanency plan needs a life-story book, or carers ask how to
  answer questions about the birth family honestly.
- **Not this prompt if** a **relative carer** needs words for the first months —
  use `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/coparenting-family-structure/parenting_kinship_care_first_months.md`.
  A clinician taking a **trauma history** uses
  `domain-psychology/intake-assessment/psychology_trauma_history_intake_module.md`.
  A foster carer's first 30 days →
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/coparenting-family-structure/parenting_foster_placement_first_weeks.md`.
  Carers under strain →
  `domain-parenting/family-support-professional/foster-kinship-adoption/parenting_resource_parent_stability_support.md`;
  contact with birth family →
  `domain-parenting/family-support-professional/foster-kinship-adoption/parenting_family_time_visit_supervision.md`.

## Safeguarding, Consent, and Scope

- **Not therapy, not an investigation, no diagnosis.** The child is not asked to
  recount trauma. Describe reactions: "woke screaming three nights after the session
  about her first home," not "PTSD"; "stared at the wall and did not answer to his
  name for about two minutes," not "dissociating."
- **New disclosures.** Stop the activity, listen, do not question for detail, write
  the exact words. Who must report, the threshold (reasonable suspicion, not proof),
  timelines, and whether a supervisor consult counts vary by state, province,
  country, and agency — **verify locally**. In most US states the duty is personal
  and a consult must never delay a report. Report to the state or county
  child-protection hotline or law enforcement (Childhelp 1-800-422-4453 can help find
  the number); immediate danger → 911 first. Do not photograph, promise secrecy, or
  confront anyone named. Decide with your supervisor who tells the family.
- **A therapist must lead or co-lead when:** the history includes sexual abuse, a
  parent's violent death or a death the child witnessed, or extreme neglect; the
  child has nightmares, rages, or "zoning out" on most days for over a month; the
  child self-harms or talks about dying (988, US, same day); the child is already in
  trauma therapy (coordinate first); or reactions after sessions last more than 48
  hours. Outside the US: local emergency number, findahelpline.com. You support,
  refer, and follow up; you do not treat.
- **Consent and records.** Whoever holds legal authority (custodial agency, adoptive
  parent, guardian) consents; the child assents and can stop at any time. Ask birth
  parents and former carers before using their words or photos. Access to case files
  and adoption records is set by law and agency policy — **verify locally**. Keep
  other people's private information (a sibling's history, a parent's diagnosis) out
  unless policy allows. Tell the child: "What you tell me stays with me and your
  carer, unless it means someone is not safe. Then I have to tell people whose job
  is keeping children safe." Write everything as if the child will read it.

## Inputs / Context

1. **De-identify first.** Initials or roles only ("birth mum," "child K., 8"). No
   names, addresses, dates of birth, or case numbers, unless your agency has approved
   this tool for client data.
2. **Child:** age, development, language, neurodivergence, placement and plan.
3. **What the child already knows**, in their own words.
4. **Sources:** case files, court summaries, health records, relatives, former carers,
   teachers, photos, objects.
5. **Who could do the work:** carer, caseworker, specialist worker, therapist.
6. **Trigger dates:** birthdays, removal anniversary, Mother's Day, court, contact changes.

## Method

1. **Use the four-question frame (DS-01).** Organise everything around the questions
   children in care ask: Where did I come from? Why don't I live with my birth
   family? Who has looked after me? What happens next? Each book section and each
   session answers one.
2. **Source every fact (RT-05).** Build a table: fact, source (file page, court
   summary, person), confidence (confirmed, reported by one person, unknown). Gaps
   stay gaps: "We don't know who your birth dad is. Here's what we do know." Never
   fill a gap with a comforting invention.
3. **Write hard facts in honest, age-matched words (RP-02).** Same truth, different
   depth. **Under 7:** short, concrete, not their fault. "Your birth mum had a
   grown-up illness called addiction. It made her brain want drugs more than
   anything, and she couldn't keep you safe. That was not your fault." **7–11:** add
   causes and choices: "Mum went to treatment twice. Each time the drugs came back,
   and a judge decided you needed a home where you'd be safe every day." **12+:** the
   fuller account and how to read the file later. No kind lies ("Mum was too busy");
   children uncover them.
4. **Hold the must-nots (DP-04).** No current addresses, schools, or identifying
   details of birth family in the book; nothing posted online; no promises of contact
   or reunion; no blaming or demonising the birth parent; no gathering evidence; no
   starting during a placement crisis or the week of a court hearing.
5. **Decide who leads (DP-05).** **Carer-led with worker support:** simple history,
   settled placement, calm and curious child. **Worker-led with clinical
   consultation:** complex history, distress that settles within a day.
   **Therapist-led or co-led:** any trigger in the safety section. Record the gate
   and the reason.
6. **Pace it.** 20–40 minute sessions, same time and place, carer nearby, a calming
   activity at the end. The child leads. Expect questions weeks later, often at
   bedtime.
7. **Plan for triggers.** List the year's trigger dates and give the carer a script:
   "It's nearly your birthday. Some kids think about their birth mum then. You can
   talk to me, or not."
8. **Make it the child's own.** A book, memory box, or digital version the child
   shapes; the agency keeps a secure copy. For adoption, add a later-life letter.

## Output Format

```
# Life Story Plan — [child initials, age], placement: [type], plan: [..]

## What the child knows now (their words)
## Sourced history  | Question | Fact | Source | Confidence |
## Gaps (stated honestly)
## Wording sheet for age [..] (and next-stage version)
## Must-nots for this child
## Who leads: [carer / worker / therapist] — gate and reason
## Session plan  | Session | Question | Activity | Who | Calm-down |
## Trigger calendar (dates, carer script)
## Product and copies; later-life letter
## Review date and stop-and-refer signs
```

## Verification

- [ ] Every fact has a source and a confidence rating.
- [ ] Gaps are written as gaps, with no invented detail.
- [ ] Wording matches the child's age, says "not your fault," and has no untrue euphemism.
- [ ] No current addresses or identifying details of birth family appear in the book.
- [ ] The gate decision is recorded with a reason, and therapist triggers are checked.
- [ ] A 12-month trigger calendar with carer scripts exists.
- [ ] No diagnostic label is applied to the child or birth parent.

## False-Positive Prevention

| Misfire | What it looks like | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Labelling instead of describing | "K. has attachment disorder"; "Mum is bipolar" in the book | Describe behaviour; a parent's diagnosis stays out unless needed and allowed. |
| Kind lies | "Mum couldn't look after you because she was working" | The true reason, in age-matched words. |
| Reading upset as failure | Child cries at a photo; worker stops for good | Sadness is expected; watch for reactions over 48 hours. |
| Reading calm as "fine" | "She doesn't care about her past" | Some children shut down; go slower. |
| Waiting until the child asks | No work until age 12 | Silence gets filled with self-blame; start early and simply. |
| Worker as investigator | "Tell me what Mum's boyfriend did" | Use known facts; new disclosures go to a report. |

## Adaptations

- **Infant or toddler:** build the book now; the carer reads it as an ordinary story.
- **Teen:** the fuller account, file access, and supported contact with relatives found online.
- **Neurodivergent child or learning disability:** visual timelines, concrete words, a picture per fact, repetition.
- **Tribal or Indigenous child (US, ICWA):** tribe, clan, and cultural history; involve the tribe's worker and relatives.
- **Transracial or intercountry adoption:** birth culture, language, and heritage; records may be incomplete.
- **Sibling group:** shared history plus a separate book for each child.

## Example Output

```

## What K. knows now

Did I make Mum sick?"

## Sourced history
| Where from   | Born healthy in a city hospital         | Birth summary     | Confirmed |
| Why not home | Mum's drug use; K. alone at night, no food | Court summary p.3 | Confirmed |
| Why not home | Mum went to treatment twice             | Mum's caseworker  | Confirmed |
| Who cared    | Nan (age 3–4); L. and M. (4–5); B. and C. now | Placement record | Confirmed |
| Who cared    | Nan stopped because of her own illness  | Nan, interview    | Reported  |
| Next         | Adoption hearing planned, date not set  | Caseworker        | Confirmed |

"We don't know who your birth dad is. Mum didn't know his full name."

## Wording sheet (age 8)
"Your birth mum has an illness called addiction. It made her brain want

## Must-nots
No Mum's current town. No Nan's address. No adoption promises before

## Who leads

history. Therapist consulted 14 Oct.

## Session plan (Tuesdays 4:30, 30 min, K.'s room)
| 1 | Where from   | Baby photos, hospital bracelet   | Worker + B. | Lego  |
| 2 | Who cared    | Map of homes drawn by K.         | Worker + B. | Park  |
| 3 | Why not home | Wording sheet; K. picks words    | Worker + B. | Bath, story |
| 4 | Why not home | Question box; repeat core line   | Worker      | Lego  |
| 5 | Next         | "My family now" page             | Worker, B., C. | Pizza |

Mother's Day, K.'s birthday, move anniversary (March), adoption hearing.

## Product
Paper book with K.'s drawings; agency holds a scanned copy.

After session 3. Stop and refer if nightmares last over 2 nights, K.
```

## Techniques Used

- **DS-01 Framework Application** — the four-question frame structures the history, sessions, and book.
- **RT-05 Evidence-Based Reasoning** — every fact carries a source and confidence; gaps are named, not filled.
- **RP-02 Audience-Specific Framing** — the same truth is written at three depths for under 7, 7–11, and 12+.
- **DP-04 Must-Not Constraints** — no identifying details, online posting, promises, blame, evidence-gathering, or starting in a crisis.
- **DP-05 Stakes-Based Gate Policy** — carer-led, worker-led with consultation, or therapist-led, with explicit triggers.
