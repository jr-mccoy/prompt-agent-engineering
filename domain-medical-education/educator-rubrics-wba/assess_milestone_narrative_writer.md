# Milestone Narrative Writer

**Objective:** Produce a complete, defensible ACGME milestone narrative assessment for a specified learner and subcompetency — integrating behavioral evidence, a milestone level determination with sub-level anchoring, a developmental framing paragraph, a growth edge statement, and CCC-ready summary format.

## When to Use
- ✅ Attending physicians and supervising faculty writing milestone-level assessments for residents or fellows
- ✅ Clinical Competency Committees (CCCs) synthesizing multiple data sources into a milestone level assignment
- ✅ Program directors drafting narrative summaries for ACGME annual milestone reporting
- ✅ Faculty writing end-of-rotation narratives that must be translatable to milestone levels
- ✅ Program coordinators supporting faculty who lack familiarity with milestone language and behavioral anchoring
- ❌ Not for student (M1–M4) milestone assessments — ACGME milestones apply to GME (residents and fellows); use `assess_rubric_builder.md` for student assessment
- ❌ Not for patient care decisions or clinical management recommendations

## Inputs Required
- **Learner:** PGY level, specialty, rotation or clinical context, time period covered (e.g., "PGY-2, Internal Medicine, ICU rotation, July–September 2025")
- **ACGME competency domain:** Patient Care / Medical Knowledge / Interpersonal and Communication Skills / Professionalism / Practice-Based Learning and Improvement / Systems-Based Practice
- **Specific subcompetency:** Name the milestone subcompetency (e.g., "Internal Medicine PC-1: History"; "Surgery MK-2: Medical Knowledge Application"; "Emergency Medicine ICS-3: Communication with Healthcare Professionals")
- **Behavioral evidence:** Supervisor describes 3–5 specific observed behaviors (what they saw the resident do or say) — NOT impressions, NOT adjectives
- **Current assessed milestone level (if known):** Supervisor's initial level estimate (1–5), or leave blank for AI-assisted level determination from evidence
- **CCC context (if applicable):** Whether this narrative is for a routine reporting cycle, a concern review, or a remediation tracking period

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Ground every milestone level assignment in specific behavioral evidence from the inputs — not impressionistic assessment
- Use the ACGME specialty-specific milestone sub-level descriptors for the named subcompetency (not generic descriptions)
- Distinguish between the three competency domain types in narrative language: Patient Care requires clinical act evidence; Interpersonal/Communication Skills requires interaction evidence; Professionalism requires behavioral pattern evidence across time
- Include a growth edge statement that describes what development toward the next milestone level looks like (forward-looking, specific, actionable)
- Provide CCC summary table format as an output option
- Include coaching conversation framing: how the supervisor presents this narrative to the resident

**Must Not:**
- Assign a milestone level without citing the specific behavioral evidence that supports it
- Assign milestones based on time in training (PGY level ≠ milestone level — milestones are developmental, not calendar-based)
- Use the same narrative template for all competency domains — the evidence type and narrative language differ by domain
- Frame milestone narratives as performance evaluations or grades — they are developmental descriptions of a learner's current position on a continuum
- Use evaluative adjectives without behavioral backing ("excellent communicator," "demonstrates good judgment") — these are conclusions, not evidence

## Instructions

1. **Identify the subcompetency and retrieve its milestone descriptor**
   - Name the ACGME specialty and subcompetency code (e.g., IM-ICS-2, EM-PC-3, GS-MK-1)
   - Describe the milestone continuum for this subcompetency: what does a Level 1 resident do, and what does a Level 5 resident do? (This frames the range before placing the learner)
   - Note whether this subcompetency has a published "harmonized" milestone (ACGME specialty-specific) or uses the general competency framework
   - If the specialty milestone document is not specified, use the general ACGME milestone framework and note the assumption

2. **Audit the behavioral evidence inputs**
   Before writing the narrative, audit the supervisor's submitted evidence:

   - **Behavioral evidence check:** Is each item an observable behavior (what the resident said or did) or an impression (what the supervisor concluded)? Convert impressions to behavioral prompts if needed:
     - Impression: "She has good situational awareness" → Behavioral prompt: "Describe one specific moment when she noticed something others missed or adjusted her approach based on situational cues"
   - **Evidence density check:** Is there sufficient evidence to anchor a milestone level? Minimum: 3 specific behavioral examples for a non-concern narrative; 5+ for a concern or remediation narrative
   - **Evidence recency check:** Is the evidence from the current reporting period? Flag evidence older than the current rotation or evaluation period
   - **Evidence domain match:** Does the evidence match the competency domain being assessed? (Patient Care evidence cannot anchor an Interprofessionalism milestone)

3. **Determine the milestone level**
   Using the behavioral evidence and the specialty milestone descriptors:

   - **Level 1 (Early learner):** Behaviors consistent with entry-level competency; requires close supervision and structured guidance for basic tasks
   - **Level 2 (Advanced beginner):** Demonstrates awareness of what needs to be done; performs basic tasks under direct supervision; recognizes complexity but cannot yet manage it independently
   - **Level 3 (Competent):** Core competency for mid-training; performs expected tasks with indirect supervision; recognizes and manages common complexity independently
   - **Level 4 (Proficient):** Above expected for training stage; manages complex or atypical situations; beginning to generalize and adapt principles
   - **Level 5 (Expert/Aspirational):** Uncommon at any training stage; models behavior for peers; contributes to the field or the program; functions as a resource for others

   - State the assigned level (e.g., "Level 3, trending toward Level 4")
   - Cite the specific behavioral evidence that anchors each level assignment
   - If evidence is mixed (some behaviors at Level 3, some at Level 2), state this explicitly and explain which behaviors pull in each direction

4. **Write the milestone narrative paragraph**
   Structure the narrative paragraph in three parts:

   **Part A — Evidence statement (2–3 sentences):**
   "During [time period and context], [Resident name] demonstrated [specific behaviors from evidence input]. Specifically, [most compelling behavioral example with clinical context]."

   **Part B — Level assignment with justification (1–2 sentences):**
   "These behaviors are consistent with Milestone Level [X] for [subcompetency name]: [one-sentence description of what Level X represents]. [If trending: 'Behaviors in [specific domain] suggest movement toward Level X+1 in [specific area].']"

   **Part C — Developmental framing (1 sentence):**
   "At this stage in training, this level [is expected / represents accelerated development / warrants attention] for a [PGY-X] [specialty] resident."

5. **Write the growth edge statement**
   The growth edge is a forward-looking, specific description of what the next milestone level looks like for this learner:

   - State what specific behaviors, at what level of complexity, in what clinical contexts, would represent Level X+1 performance
   - The growth edge is not a deficit statement ("you need to improve your communication") — it is a developmental horizon ("when you can [specific behavior] in [specific context type] without [specific prompt or support], that represents Level 4 performance")
   - Example: "Moving toward Level 4 will look like: independently adapting your communication approach when you detect patient anxiety or health literacy concerns, without supervisor prompting — naming what you noticed and why you adjusted"

6. **Write the CCC summary format (optional)**
   Provide a table-format summary for Clinical Competency Committee reporting:

   | Field | Content |
   |---|---|
   | Resident | [Name, PGY level] |
   | Specialty | [Specialty] |
   | Subcompetency | [Code and name] |
   | Reporting period | [Dates] |
   | Current milestone level | [Level X] |
   | Level trajectory | [Progressing / Stable / Concern] |
   | Key behavioral evidence | [2–3 bullet points of specific behaviors] |
   | Growth edge | [One-sentence forward-looking statement] |
   | Recommended CCC action | [Routine / Monitor / Targeted coaching / Remediation review] |

7. **Write the coaching conversation framing**
   Provide language for how the supervisor presents this milestone narrative to the resident:

   - **Opening frame:** "Milestone narratives describe where you are in your development on a specific competency dimension — they're not a grade or an evaluation of you as a person. I want to walk you through what I observed and where that places you."
   - **Evidence sharing:** "Here's what I specifically observed: [evidence]. That's what I'm anchoring this to."
   - **Level communication:** "Based on that, I'd place you at Level [X] on [subcompetency]. For context, [what Level X means in plain language]."
   - **Growth edge delivery:** "Where you're headed — what Level [X+1] looks like for you — is [growth edge statement]."
   - **Closing:** "What questions do you have about where I'm placing you and why?"
   - Note: Residents often conflate milestone level with performance satisfaction; normalize that Level 3 at PGY-2 is the expected trajectory, and that Level 5 is aspirational at any stage

8. **Flag concerns for CCC review**
   If the behavioral evidence reveals:
   - A pattern of behaviors at Level 1–2 in a mid- or late-training resident
   - Inconsistency across multiple evaluators suggesting rater disagreement vs. developmental inconsistency
   - A plateau (same level across three or more consecutive reporting periods without progression)
   - Any professionalism behaviors that rise to a regulatory or patient safety concern

   Then: flag the specific concern, recommend CCC action level (Monitor / Targeted coaching / Remediation plan), and note what additional evidence would clarify the picture.

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Common Mistake | ✅ Correct Approach |
|---|---|
| Milestone level assigned without behavioral evidence | Every level assignment must cite observable behavioral evidence: "Level 3 based on the following specific behaviors observed during [context]..." — "seems competent" is not evidence |
| Time-based milestone assignment ("She's PGY-2 so she should be Level 2") | Milestones are developmental, not calendar-based; a PGY-2 can be at Level 3 and a PGY-3 can be at Level 2 on the same subcompetency; time in training is context, not justification |
| Same narrative template for all competency domains | Patient Care narratives require clinical act evidence; ICS narratives require specific interaction examples; Professionalism narratives require behavioral pattern evidence across multiple observations — the evidence type and narrative language must match the domain |
| Milestone narrative framed as a performance grade | Milestone narratives are developmental descriptions ("here is where you are on a continuum") not evaluative judgments ("you performed at a B level") — framing matters for how residents receive and use the information |
| Growth edge stated as a deficit | "You need to improve your history-taking" is a deficit statement; "Moving toward Level 4 means eliciting the social history elements that connect directly to the chief complaint before the supervisor prompts for them" is a growth edge — specific, forward-looking, and actionable |
| Glossing over mixed evidence | If some behaviors are at Level 3 and others at Level 2, name this explicitly: "History-taking behaviors suggest Level 3; however, clinical judgment behaviors on complex cases remain at Level 2" — averaged impressions obscure developmental signal |

## Example Output Snippet

> **MILESTONE NARRATIVE — Internal Medicine ICS-2: Communicating with Patients and Families**
>
> **Behavioral Evidence:**
> - During an ICU family meeting for a patient with septic shock and multi-organ failure, Dr. Chen initiated the meeting by explicitly asking the family what they already understood about their father's condition before presenting new information
> - When the patient's daughter began crying during the goals-of-care discussion, Dr. Chen paused, named the emotion ("It sounds like this is very hard to hear"), and allowed 30 seconds of silence before continuing
> - During three observed outpatient encounters, Dr. Chen used teach-back in two of three cases; in one case, skipped teach-back after a patient appeared to understand, and did not verify comprehension
>
> **Milestone Narrative:**
>
> During the July–September ICU and ambulatory medicine rotations, Dr. Chen demonstrated communication behaviors consistent with Milestone Level 3 on ICS-2. Specifically, she applied patient-centered communication techniques adaptively in a high-stakes goals-of-care family meeting — eliciting prior understanding, responding to emotional cues with named empathy, and maintaining comfort with therapeutic silence. These behaviors are consistent with ICS-2 Level 3: reliably uses core communication skills in complex clinical encounters with appropriate adaptation to patient and family emotional state. Application of teach-back in outpatient encounters was inconsistent, representing the primary gap from Level 4 performance.
>
> **Growth Edge:**
>
> Moving toward Level 4 will look like consistently applying teach-back across encounter types — including outpatient visits where time pressure may otherwise prompt skipping the verification step — and doing so independently without supervisor reminder or debrief prompting.

## Verification Checklist
- [ ] Subcompetency code and specialty-specific milestone descriptor identified
- [ ] Behavioral evidence audited: each item confirmed as observable behavior, domain-matched, and current period
- [ ] Milestone level assigned with specific behavioral evidence citations (not impressionistic language)
- [ ] Time-based reasoning explicitly avoided — level justified by behavior, not PGY year
- [ ] Narrative uses domain-appropriate evidence type (clinical act / interaction / behavioral pattern)
- [ ] Growth edge is forward-looking and specific — not a deficit statement
- [ ] CCC summary table included if requested or if CCC reporting context specified
- [ ] Coaching conversation framing included with specific language
- [ ] Concern flag generated if evidence pattern warrants CCC escalation
