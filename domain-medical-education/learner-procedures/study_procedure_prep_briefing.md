# Procedure Prep Briefing for Health-Professions Learners

**Objective:** Produce a structured self-brief for a learner before they observe, assist, or perform a clinical procedure. Cover indications, contraindications, anatomy, consent talking points, equipment, step-by-step technique, anticipated complications, post-procedure care, and a "what could go wrong" stress test calibrated to the learner's role.

## When to Use
- ✅ Before a procedure block, simulation lab, or skills station
- ✅ Self-prep before assisting on a procedure during rotations
- ✅ Reviewing technique between attempts
- ❌ Real-patient procedural decision-making — supervisor oversight and verified procedural references required
- ❌ Independent procedural performance without supervision

## Inputs Required
- **Discipline & learner level**
- **Procedure:** e.g., paracentesis, thoracentesis, central line, lumbar puncture, arterial line, intubation, chest tube, joint injection, IO access, IV cannulation, NG placement, urinary catheter, suturing, incision and drainage, dental extraction, dental local anesthesia block, splinting
- **Role on team:** observer / assistant / first operator (under direct supervision) / scrub
- **Setting:** sim lab / supervised real procedure / OSCE rehearsal

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Begin with indications and contraindications — what makes this the right procedure for this patient
- Cover relevant anatomy (especially landmarks and danger structures)
- Provide explicit consent-conversation talking points (procedure, alternatives, risks, benefits, what they'll feel, what could go wrong)
- List equipment with a setup sequence
- Step-by-step technique calibrated to learner role
- Anticipated complications with early recognition signs
- Post-procedure care and disposition
- A "what could go wrong" pre-mortem covering 3-5 plausible adverse events

**Must Not:**
- Replace direct supervisor instruction
- Provide patient-specific medication doses
- Imply that this brief is sufficient to perform the procedure independently
- Substitute for institutional checklist / time-out protocol — always reinforce that the formal time-out is mandatory

## Instructions

1. **Confirm procedure and role.** Calibrate detail: an observer needs less technical step detail and more anticipatory awareness; a first operator under supervision needs deeper step-by-step.

2. **Indications & contraindications.** When is this procedure appropriate? When is it not? Absolute vs relative contraindications.

3. **Anatomy block.** Key landmarks (palpable + sonographic if relevant) and danger structures (nerves, vessels, organs) to avoid. Reference `domain-medical-education/learner-foundational-sciences/study_anatomy_drill_generator.md` for deeper review.

4. **Consent talking points.** A short script covering:
   - Procedure name and purpose (plain language)
   - Alternatives
   - Risks (general + procedure-specific; include common minor and serious rare)
   - Benefits
   - What the patient will feel during and after
   - Right to refuse / stop
   - Note: in many settings the supervising clinician obtains consent — the learner's role may be to support, not lead

5. **Equipment & setup sequence.** Itemized list grouped by phase (sterile preparation, anesthesia, primary instruments, dressing). Note ergonomic positioning of patient and operator.

6. **Step-by-step technique** (calibrated to role). Each step:
   - What to do
   - What to watch for
   - Common error
   - Decision point if outcome differs from expected

7. **Anticipated complications.** For each, early recognition signs and the immediate response. Examples:
   - Pneumothorax post-thoracentesis: dyspnea + ↓ breath sounds; CXR; consider chest tube if symptomatic / large
   - Bleeding at puncture site: pressure; coag check; vascular consult if persistent
   - Vasovagal reaction: position supine, fluids, monitor

8. **Post-procedure care.** Patient positioning, monitoring parameters, documentation requirements, follow-up imaging or labs, patient education.

9. **Pre-mortem ("what could go wrong").** 3-5 plausible adverse events. For each:
   - What it would look like (early sign)
   - What you would do *in the next 60 seconds*
   - When you would call for help (escalation criteria)
   - Mandatory time-out reminder

10. **Self-check block:**
    - State indications and absolute contraindications from memory
    - Trace the danger anatomy near the procedure site
    - Three sentences of the consent script from memory
    - One complication and your first 60 seconds of response

## Discipline-Specific Anchors

| Discipline | Notes |
|---|---|
| Medicine / PA | Most procedural prompts apply directly |
| Nursing | Assistant / circulator / setup / monitoring role; familiarity with technique enables anticipation |
| EMS | Field procedures (IO access, needle decompression, surgical airway, tourniquet, NG/OG); scene safety; protocol triggers |
| Dental | Local anesthesia blocks, extractions, restorations; armamentarium; ergonomics |
| Allied health (RT) | Airway / suction / arterial sampling; circuit setup; troubleshooting |
| Allied health (PT/OT) | Joint mobilization safety, vestibular maneuvers, transfers; precautions |

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Common Mistake | ✅ Correct Approach |
|---|---|
| Skip indications and jump to technique | Indications first; technique is downstream |
| Vague risks in consent ("bleeding, infection") | Itemize specific risks with frequency framing (common minor / uncommon serious) |
| No anatomy danger structures | Always identify what could be injured |
| Generic complications | Include early-recognition signs and 60-second response |
| Imply this replaces supervisor instruction | Always reinforce supervisor oversight |
| Skip time-out reminder | Mandatory in formal procedural settings |
| Patient-specific dosing | Class and qualitative principle only |

## Output Format

```
### Procedure / Role / Setting / Discipline

### Indications & Contraindications

### Anatomy (landmarks + danger structures)

### Consent Talking Points (5-7 sentences)

### Equipment & Setup Sequence
(grouped: prep / anesthesia / instruments / dressing)

### Step-By-Step Technique (calibrated to role)
1. ...
2. ...

### Anticipated Complications
- Complication → early sign → immediate response

### Post-Procedure Care

### Pre-Mortem ("What could go wrong")
1-5: event / early sign / 60-second response / escalation

### Mandatory Time-Out Reminder

### Self-Check
1. Indications + absolute contraindications
2. Danger anatomy
3. Consent script (3 sentences from memory)
4. One complication + 60-second response
```

## Verification Checklist
- [ ] Indications and contraindications stated
- [ ] Anatomy includes danger structures
- [ ] Consent script covers procedure, alternatives, risks, benefits, sensations
- [ ] Equipment with setup sequence
- [ ] Step-by-step calibrated to role
- [ ] Complications include early signs and 60-second response
- [ ] Pre-mortem includes 3-5 events
- [ ] Time-out reminder present
- [ ] Self-check uses retrieval
- [ ] Real-patient redirect language present
