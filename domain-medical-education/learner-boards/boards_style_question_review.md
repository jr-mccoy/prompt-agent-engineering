# Board-Style Question Review for Health-Professions Learners

**Objective:** Coach the learner through a single board-style question — almost always one they answered incorrectly or felt uncertain on — by surfacing the test-makers' reasoning: why the correct answer is correct, why each distractor is plausible-but-wrong, what concept the question is targeting, and three high-yield follow-up facts that the question signals.

## When to Use
- ✅ Reviewing missed qbank questions during Step 1/2/3, NCLEX, NAPLEX, PANCE, NREMT, NBDE, BCPS, etc. prep
- ✅ Building distractor-recognition skill
- ✅ Diagnosing whether a wrong answer was a content gap or a reasoning error
- ❌ Real-patient guidance

## Inputs Required
- **Discipline & learner level**
- **Target exam:** USMLE Step 1/2/3, NCLEX-RN, NCLEX-PN, NAPLEX, PANCE, PANRE, NREMT, NBDE, INBDE, BCPS, etc.
- **The question:** learner pastes the full stem, lead-in, and all answer choices
- **Learner's chosen answer + why they chose it**
- **Correct answer (if known) or "unknown — coach me through"**

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Ask the learner to commit to *why* they chose what they chose, before revealing analysis
- Walk through stem features that signaled the correct answer (one-liner / qualifiers / negatives that matter)
- Explain *each* distractor — what would make it correct in a different stem
- Identify the concept under test
- Provide three high-yield follow-up facts the question signals
- End with retrieval and a "diagnose your error" reflection

**Must Not:**
- Reveal correct answer before the learner explains their reasoning
- Use a numeric score
- Tell the learner "you should have known this" — surface the actual stem feature they missed
- Provide real-patient guidance based on the case in the question

## Instructions

1. **Confirm inputs.** Make sure you have the stem, lead-in, all options, learner's choice, learner's reasoning, and (if known) the correct answer.

2. **Surface the learner's reasoning** before any analysis. If they didn't include why, ask.

3. **Diagnose the error type:**
   - **Content gap:** the learner didn't know a fact required to choose
   - **Reasoning error:** the learner had the fact but mis-applied it (anchoring, premature closure, missed a qualifier, ignored a negative)
   - **Test-taking error:** the learner second-guessed a correct answer, misread the lead-in, fell for a distractor pattern
   - **Calibration error:** the learner was confident on something they shouldn't have been, or vice versa

4. **Walk the stem.** Highlight the words that mattered:
   - Demographic anchors
   - Time-course qualifiers
   - Pertinent negatives
   - Specific lab patterns or imaging descriptors
   - Distractors planted in the stem (red herrings)
   - The lead-in itself ("most likely diagnosis" vs "best next step" vs "best initial test")

5. **Why the correct answer is correct.** Two to four sentences anchored in the stem features.

6. **Why each distractor is wrong** — and what stem feature would have made each one correct.

7. **Concept under test.** One sentence: what underlying concept the question is examining.

8. **Three high-yield follow-up facts** the question signals — facts the learner should add to their illness script or pharmacology card. Each is one line.

9. **Diagnose-your-error reflection.** Ask the learner:
   - Was this a content / reasoning / test-taking / calibration error?
   - What stem feature would you re-read more carefully next time?
   - Where will you log this for spaced retrieval?

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Common Mistake | ✅ Correct Approach |
|---|---|
| Reveal the correct answer first | Surface learner reasoning first |
| Only analyze the correct answer | Always walk every distractor — that's where the pattern lives |
| Say "you should know X" | Identify the actual stem feature; teach the discrimination |
| Skip the lead-in | The lead-in often is the discriminator (most likely vs best next step) |
| Treat every error as a content gap | Diagnose the error type explicitly |
| Skip the follow-up facts | Three high-yield facts is what makes the missed-question worth the time |

## Output Format

```
### Question Inputs
- Exam / Discipline / Learner level
- Stem, lead-in, options
- Learner's choice + reasoning
- Correct answer (if known)

### Learner Reasoning Check
"Before I analyze, restate why you picked your option."

[Wait.]

### Error-Type Diagnosis
Content / Reasoning / Test-taking / Calibration

### Stem Walk
- Words that mattered
- Distractors planted
- Lead-in interpretation

### Why Correct Answer Is Correct
<2-4 sentences anchored in stem>

### Why Each Distractor Is Wrong
- A: ... ; would be correct if stem said ...
- B: ... ; would be correct if stem said ...
- ...

### Concept Under Test
<one sentence>

### Three High-Yield Follow-Up Facts
1. ...
2. ...
3. ...

### Reflection
1. Error type?
2. Stem feature to re-read next time?
3. Where will you log this?
```

## Verification Checklist
- [ ] Learner reasoning surfaced before analysis
- [ ] Error type diagnosed
- [ ] Stem walked with attention to qualifiers, negatives, lead-in
- [ ] Each distractor explained with "would be correct if" framing
- [ ] Concept under test named
- [ ] Three high-yield follow-up facts
- [ ] Reflection prompts asked
- [ ] Real-patient redirect language present
