# Clinical Learner Feedback Composer

**Objective:** Produce a specific, written or verbally delivered feedback document for a clinical learner based on directly observed behaviors, calibrated to the learner's training level, using a validated feedback framework.

## When to Use
- ✅ After directly observing a clinical encounter (H&P, patient presentation, procedure, patient communication, handoff)
- ✅ When completing a mid-rotation or end-of-rotation evaluation narrative
- ✅ After observing a learner's clinical reasoning during rounds or case discussion
- ✅ When a learner requests feedback and you need to structure your observations
- ✅ For written comments on a MedEd evaluation form (OSCE, mini-CEX, direct observation)
- ❌ Do not use as a substitute for urgent patient safety conversations — address immediate safety concerns in real time, then use this tool afterward
- ❌ Do not use when you have not directly observed the learner; secondhand reports require different documentation

## Inputs Required
- **Learner name and level:** (M1 / M2 / M3 / M4 / Intern PGY1 / Resident PGY2-3 / Senior Resident PGY4+ / Fellow / Nurse Practitioner student / PA student)
- **Clinical encounter observed:** (e.g., "new patient H&P for chest pain," "oral case presentation on rounds," "central line placement," "family meeting for goals of care")
- **Specialty and setting:** (e.g., Internal Medicine inpatient ward, Pediatric ED, Outpatient Family Medicine clinic)
- **Date and duration of observation:** (e.g., 2026-05-10, 25-minute observation)
- **Feedback framework preference:** (see Step 3 for selector — if unsure, leave blank and follow the selector logic)
- **Specific behaviors observed:** (2-4 concrete, behaviorally specific observations — what you saw or heard, not your interpretation)

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Name the specific observed behavior in every positive and constructive comment (not just the outcome or trait)
- Connect every observation to its clinical impact — on the patient, the team, or the learner's future practice
- Calibrate the performance standard explicitly to the learner's training level, not to an attending's performance
- Include one forward-looking, time-bound action the learner will take in the next encounter

**Must Not:**
- Use global trait language without behavioral anchors ("great bedside manner," "lacks confidence") — always translate to observable behavior
- List more than three growth areas — cognitive load limits what a learner can act on; prioritize the highest-leverage change
- Use minimizing language ("just," "simply," "a little bit") when framing constructive feedback
- Conflate formative feedback tone (coaching, forward-looking) with summative tone (judgment of readiness); label which this is

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Collect Observation Data
Gather the following before composing feedback:

1a. **Learner identifier:** Full name and current training level (be precise — PGY1 interns require different calibration than PGY3 seniors)

1b. **Encounter description:** One sentence naming the clinical task, the setting, and the patient context (e.g., "Presented a 67-year-old with acute COPD exacerbation to the attending on morning rounds")

1c. **Observation window:** Duration and completeness — did you observe the full encounter or a segment? Note this because it scopes the feedback

1d. **Behavioral notes:** List 3-5 specific things you saw or heard the learner do or say. Use past tense, active verbs, and direct quotation where possible:
   - "Asked the patient 'What brings you in today?' and waited silently for 90 seconds before asking a follow-up"
   - "Presented the HPI in chronological order without stopping to assess the attending's comprehension"
   - "Did not wash hands upon entering the room despite gloves being visible on the cart"

### Step 2 — Identify Strengths and Growth Areas
Before selecting the framework, draft your raw observations into two buckets:

2a. **Strengths (2-3 max):** Behaviors that should be reinforced because they positively impacted patient care, team function, or professional development. For each, note:
   - The specific behavior observed
   - Its clinical or educational impact

2b. **Growth areas (1-2 max):** Behaviors that, if changed, would most improve the learner's clinical effectiveness or safety. For each, note:
   - The specific behavior observed
   - The clinical impact of the gap (what went wrong or could go wrong)
   - A concrete alternative behavior the learner could use

2c. **Prioritization rule:** If you identified more than two growth areas, select only the highest-leverage one — the gap most likely to recur, most likely to affect patient safety, or most aligned with the learner's current developmental stage.

### Step 3 — Select Feedback Framework
Choose the framework that best fits your context:

| Framework | Best For | Structure |
|---|---|---|
| **R2C2** (Reflect, React, Content, Coach) | Formative, relationship-based, longitudinal | Learner reflects first, you react, explore content, coach forward |
| **SBI** (Situation, Behavior, Impact) | Professionalism feedback; specific one-time incidents | Name situation → describe behavior → state impact |
| **AID** (Action, Impact, Do differently) | Behavioral feedback; concise written comments | Describe action → name impact → specify alternative action |
| **Pendleton** (What went well → what could improve → summary) | Structured post-encounter feedback; learner-initiated | Learner self-assesses first, then educator adds |
| **TIPS** (Targeted, Individualized, Prioritized, Specific) | Written evaluation narratives; end-of-rotation summaries | High-specificity written format for formal documentation |

**Selection logic:**
- If this is a brief post-encounter conversation (< 10 minutes): use **AID** or **SBI**
- If this is a mid-rotation check-in conversation: use **R2C2** or **Pendleton**
- If this is a written evaluation comment: use **TIPS** or **AID**
- If the feedback concerns a professionalism incident: use **SBI**

### Step 4 — Compose the Strengths Section
For each strength identified in Step 2a, write one paragraph (2-4 sentences) following this structure:

4a. Name the behavior in specific, observable terms
4b. State the clinical or educational impact of that behavior
4c. (Optional) Connect to a competency domain (e.g., Patient Care, Interpersonal Communication, Medical Knowledge — use your program's framework: ACGME Milestones, CanMEDS, etc.)

**Example strength statement (AID format, PGY2, Internal Medicine):**
"During the patient presentation, you organized the HPI around the chief complaint and relevant positives/negatives rather than listing every system finding — this allowed the team to form a differential within two minutes of your presentation beginning. That efficiency is a hallmark of effective senior resident communication."

### Step 5 — Compose the Growth Area Section
For each growth area identified in Step 2b, write one paragraph (3-5 sentences) following this structure:

5a. Name the specific behavior observed (not the trait, not the outcome — the behavior)
5b. State the clinical impact of that behavior — what did it affect or what could it affect?
5c. Name the preferred alternative behavior with enough specificity that the learner knows what to do differently
5d. Avoid using "just," "simply," "try to be more," or "a little" — these minimize the change and signal it should be easy, undermining the learner's motivation when they struggle

**Example growth area statement (SBI format, M3 student):**
"During the history of present illness, I noticed you asked seven consecutive yes/no questions within the first minute of the encounter (behavior). This prevented the patient from sharing the narrative of her illness in her own words, which meant we missed that she had already tried two inhalers without relief — a clinically relevant detail (impact). In your next encounter, practice using open-ended questions for the first two minutes before narrowing to closed questions; this approach consistently surfaces information that structured questioning misses."

### Step 6 — Write the Forward Action Commitment
Write one forward-looking, time-bound statement that specifies:

6a. **What** the learner will do differently (specific behavior)
6b. **When** they will do it (the next encounter, by end of week, on your next night call)
6c. **How** you will know they've done it (what you or they will observe)

Format: "In [time frame], [learner name] will [specific behavior], with the goal of [specific observable outcome]."

Example: "In your next three patient presentations on rounds, focus on stating your assessment and plan before reviewing supporting data — this reflects how attendings and fellows think through cases, and practicing it now will accelerate your clinical reasoning development."

### Step 7 — Apply Level Calibration
Calibrate the performance standard explicitly to the learner's training level. Do not compare a third-year medical student to a fellow or compare a PGY1 to a chief resident. Use these anchors:

| Level | What Counts as Meeting Expectations |
|---|---|
| M3 (core clerkship) | Organized, complete history and physical; aware of major differentials; requires attending confirmation before acting |
| M4 (sub-intern/acting intern) | Functioning at intern-level independence; formulating a prioritized assessment; anticipates next steps |
| PGY1 Intern | Manages routine presentations independently; identifies sick vs. not sick; escalates appropriately |
| PGY2-3 Resident | Leads clinical reasoning; supervises interns; handles most calls without attending input |
| Fellow | Teaching-level depth in subspecialty; managing complex cases; producing publishable reasoning |

Add one sentence to your feedback explicitly naming the expected level: "At the PGY2 level, the expectation is that..."

### Step 8 — Distinguish Formative vs. Summative Tone
Label this feedback explicitly and apply the appropriate tone:

8a. **Formative (in-training):** Coaching tone. Focus on what to do differently next time. Uncertainty is expected. "Here's what to work on and how."

8b. **Summative (assessment of readiness):** Evaluative tone. Focus on whether performance meets the standard for advancement. "Here is my judgment of your current level of competency."

8c. Do not mix tones in the same document. If you are completing an end-of-rotation evaluation, you are writing summatively. If you are giving verbal post-encounter feedback, you are writing formatively.

### Step 9 — Final Assembly and Delivery Guidance

9a. **Written format:** Assemble the sections in this order: (1) Context sentence, (2) Strengths, (3) Growth area(s), (4) Forward action, (5) Level calibration statement.

9b. **Verbal delivery (formative conversations):** If using R2C2 or Pendleton, start by asking the learner to self-assess before you share your observations. Opening prompt: "Before I share what I noticed, tell me — how do you think that went? What felt strong and what would you do differently?"

9c. **Length guidance:** Written evaluation comment: 100-200 words per domain assessed. Post-encounter verbal debrief: 3-5 minutes. Mid-rotation conversation: 15-20 minutes including learner self-assessment.

9d. **Private setting requirement:** All developmental feedback must be delivered in private. Do not provide critical growth-area feedback in front of patients, peers, or other team members.

## Worked Example

**Context:** PGY2 Internal Medicine resident, oral case presentation on morning rounds, 8-minute presentation, observed by attending.

**Behaviors observed:**
- Presented HPI in clear chronological narrative without reading from notes
- Did not state the one-line summary at the start ("This is a 54-year-old with COPD presenting with...")
- Proposed three diagnoses in the assessment but did not rank them by likelihood
- Asked the attending "Does that sound right?" after stating the plan

**Framework selected:** AID (concise, appropriate for post-rounds verbal feedback)

---

**Feedback Document (AID Format):**

*Context:* This feedback reflects my observation of your morning presentation on 2026-05-10 for the 54-year-old patient with suspected COPD exacerbation.

*Strengths:*
You presented the HPI from memory in a clear timeline without relying on your notes — this signals to the team that you own the patient's story rather than reading it, which builds trust in your clinical reasoning. At the PGY2 level, this is a high-value communication skill and you demonstrated it consistently across all three patients today.

*Growth Area:*
You listed three diagnoses in your assessment without ranking them by likelihood (action). Without a ranked differential, the team cannot efficiently prioritize workup or disposition — it adds cognitive load to everyone listening (impact). In your next presentation, state your leading diagnosis first ("My top diagnosis is X because...") and then your alternatives in descending order of likelihood; this structure is what attendings and senior residents expect at your level (alternative behavior).

*Forward Action:*
In your presentations for the remainder of this week, practice opening your assessment with "My leading diagnosis is..." before listing alternatives. By Friday, aim to need no prompts from me to do this.

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Common Mistake | ✅ Correct Approach |
|---|---|
| Generic praise: "Great job today — keep it up!" | Name the behavior and impact: "You used open-ended questions for the first two minutes, which surfaced the patient's medication non-adherence before I had to ask." |
| Minimizing language: "Just try to be a bit more concise" | State the specific behavior change and why it matters: "Aim to complete your HPI in under three minutes; presentations over five minutes signal to attendings that you're not yet filtering for relevance." |
| Trait-based feedback: "You lack confidence" | Behavior-based alternative: "You asked the attending for confirmation after each plan item — at PGY2, the expectation is stating the plan and inviting questions at the end." |
| More than three growth areas in one session | Prioritize one: "The highest-leverage change right now is X. We'll revisit Y at your mid-rotation check-in." |
| Giving feedback before the learner self-assesses (in formative conversations) | Open with: "Before I share what I observed — how do you think that went? What would you do differently?" |
| Conflating formative and summative: "I'm just giving you feedback" on a high-stakes evaluation | Label clearly: "This is your end-of-rotation evaluation and reflects my judgment of your current competency level." |

## Output Format

The completed feedback document should contain the following sections in order:

1. **Context line** (1-2 sentences): Learner name, level, encounter observed, date
2. **Strengths** (1-2 paragraphs): Specific behavior → clinical impact → competency domain
3. **Growth Area(s)** (1-2 paragraphs): Specific behavior observed → clinical impact of gap → concrete alternative behavior
4. **Forward Action** (1 sentence): What, when, how you will know
5. **Level Calibration Statement** (1 sentence): "At the [level] level, the expectation is..."
6. **Delivery label** (1 word or phrase): Formative or Summative

For verbal delivery (R2C2 / Pendleton), the sequence becomes: Learner self-assessment prompt → Educator reactions → Content (feedback document above) → Coach (forward action).

## Verification Checklist
- [ ] Learner level is specified and the performance standard is calibrated to that level (not to an attending's performance)
- [ ] Every strength statement names a specific behavior and its clinical or educational impact
- [ ] Every growth area names the specific behavior, the impact of the gap, and a concrete alternative behavior
- [ ] No more than two growth areas are included; highest-leverage gap is prioritized
- [ ] Minimizing language ("just," "simply," "a little") is absent from all constructive statements
- [ ] Forward action is specific, time-bound, and observable
- [ ] Feedback is labeled as formative or summative, and the tone matches the label
- [ ] Delivery will occur (or occurred) in a private setting away from patients and peers
- [ ] Competency framework alignment is named if this will be entered into a formal evaluation system
