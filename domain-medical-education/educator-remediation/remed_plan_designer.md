# Learner Remediation Plan Designer

**Objective:** Produce an individualized, evidence-anchored remediation plan that correctly identifies the root cause of a performance deficit, prescribes root-cause-matched interventions, sets SMART goals with explicit exit criteria, and assigns clear faculty and learner responsibilities.

## When to Use
- ✅ A learner has received multiple feedback cycles on the same deficit without improvement
- ✅ A learner's milestone ratings fall below expected trajectory at a formal review (e.g., Clinical Competency Committee)
- ✅ Following a critical incident, patient complaint, or near-miss that reveals a skill or professionalism deficit
- ✅ A learner self-identifies a significant gap and requests structured support
- ✅ Program Director or clerkship director needs a documented, legally defensible remediation plan
- ❌ Do not use for a single isolated performance issue — remediation is reserved for persistent or serious deficits after standard feedback has been given
- ❌ Do not use when the presenting issue is primarily a wellness or mental health concern; those require referral to appropriate support services, not an academic remediation plan
- ❌ Do not use without at least two sources of documented performance evidence; single-source reports are insufficient to anchor a plan

## Inputs Required
- **Learner name and level:** (M3 / M4 / PGY1-PGY5 / Fellow / APP student — be precise)
- **Program/specialty:** (e.g., Internal Medicine residency, third-year clerkship, PA program)
- **Performance deficit description:** (specific behaviors observed, not trait labels)
- **Evidence supporting the deficit:** (assessment results, direct observation records, 360 evaluations, patient feedback — list sources and dates)
- **Clinical consequences observed or anticipated:** (what has already happened or what could happen if uncorrected)
- **Remediation period available:** (4 weeks / 8 weeks / one rotation / academic year — program constraints)
- **Faculty resources available:** (who can supervise, mentor, or coach; simulation center access; dedicated teaching time)

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Perform a root cause analysis before designing interventions — the root cause drives the intervention type
- Write at least one SMART goal per root cause category identified
- Define explicit, observable reassessment criteria that mark the end of remediation
- Assign every action item to either the learner or a named faculty/program resource with a due date
- Include an escalation pathway describing what happens if remediation objectives are not met

**Must Not:**
- Prescribe reading-only interventions for skill, procedural, or professionalism deficits — root cause must drive intervention type
- Produce a plan without documented learner sign-off; collaborative goal-setting is ethically required
- Leave the reassessment criterion vague ("demonstrates improvement") — criteria must be specific and observable
- Conflate a wellness or impairment issue with an academic performance issue — different pathways apply
- Set an open-ended remediation period without a defined end date or exit criterion

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Document the Performance Deficit
Describe the deficit with precision before doing anything else. Vague problem statements produce vague plans.

1a. **Behavioral description:** What specific behaviors have been observed? (Use past tense, active verbs, direct quotation where possible. Avoid trait labels.)
   - Weak: "Lacks clinical reasoning skills"
   - Strong: "On three consecutive observed case presentations (2026-03-12, 2026-04-01, 2026-04-18), the resident listed diagnoses without ranking by likelihood, did not connect exam findings to the differential, and proposed a workup that did not address the leading diagnosis"

1b. **Evidence inventory:** List every documented source of evidence and its date. A plan grounded in a single observation is legally and educationally weak. Acceptable sources:
   - Direct observation tools (mini-CEX, DOPS, direct observation forms)
   - Milestone ratings from CCC review
   - 360-degree evaluations from nurses, peers, or patients
   - In-training examination scores (with context — one low score is insufficient alone)
   - Written case presentations or H&P notes reviewed by faculty

1c. **Clinical consequence statement:** State what has already happened or what the realistic risk is if the deficit continues. This anchors the urgency level and the remediation intensity.

### Step 2 — Perform Root Cause Analysis
Map the deficit to its root cause category. This step is non-negotiable — the intervention must match the root cause. A mismatch (e.g., assigning reading for a professionalism deficit) produces no measurable improvement and creates documentation liability.

Assess each category systematically:

| Root Cause Category | Diagnostic Questions | Primary Intervention Type |
|---|---|---|
| **Knowledge gap** | Can the learner identify what they don't know? Do they struggle with factual recall or conceptual understanding? | Targeted reading, self-study modules, didactic sessions, question banks |
| **Skill/procedural gap** | Can they describe the skill correctly but perform it inconsistently? Do they need repetitions under supervision? | Supervised clinical practice, simulation, procedural training labs |
| **Clinical reasoning gap** | Do they gather data adequately but fail to synthesize it into a prioritized assessment? Do they skip illness script building? | Illness script development, cognitive coaching, supervised case discussions, think-aloud exercises |
| **Professionalism/behavioral gap** | Are there patterns of conduct, reliability, communication, or ethical behavior that fall below standards? | Behavioral contracting, coaching, reflective exercises, peer feedback, structured observation with accountability |
| **Wellness/personal issue** | Are there signs of burnout, mental health concerns, substance use, or personal crisis driving the deficit? | **Referral to resident wellness resources, Employee Assistance Program (EAP), fitness-for-duty evaluation — NOT a teaching intervention** |

2a. For each root cause category, mark: Present / Absent / Uncertain (requires further assessment)
2b. If Wellness/Personal is present or suspected, stop and follow program wellness/impairment protocol before proceeding with an academic remediation plan. Document the referral.
2c. A learner may have more than one root cause; each requires its own targeted intervention set.

### Step 3 — Write SMART Goals
For each root cause identified, write one SMART goal. Every goal must meet all five criteria:

- **Specific:** Names the exact behavior or competency to be addressed
- **Measurable:** States how progress will be quantified (score, observed rating, direct observation rating)
- **Achievable:** Realistic given the learner's baseline and the available remediation window
- **Relevant:** Directly tied to the root cause and the clinical consequence
- **Time-bound:** States a deadline (a date or a milestone event)

**SMART goal template:**
"By [date], [learner name] will [specific observable behavior] as measured by [assessment method], demonstrating [specific performance standard]."

**Example (clinical reasoning gap):**
"By 2026-07-01, Dr. Martinez will present a ranked differential diagnosis (minimum 3 diagnoses in order of likelihood with supporting evidence for each) on 80% of observed case presentations, as rated by the supervising attending using the observed case presentation direct observation tool."

### Step 4 — Design Interventions by Root Cause
For each root cause identified in Step 2, prescribe a specific intervention set. Do not use generic "remediation activities" — each activity must be linked to a root cause.

**4a. Knowledge gap interventions:**
- Assign specific chapters, review articles, or case-based modules (not "review the medicine textbook")
- Set a completion schedule with verification (quiz, discussion, or written summary)
- Consider pairing with an online question bank (USMLE-style for residents, specialty-specific for fellows) with a target score or improvement trajectory

**4b. Skill/procedural gap interventions:**
- Identify specific simulation lab sessions with a named skills educator or attending supervisor
- Set a minimum number of supervised repetitions with direct observation forms completed each time
- Specify the competency standard (e.g., "performs central line insertion using sterile technique without prompting across three consecutive observed attempts")

**4c. Clinical reasoning gap interventions:**
- Weekly one-hour case discussion sessions with a faculty mentor using think-aloud methodology
- Illness script building exercises: learner writes out illness scripts for the five most common presentations in their current rotation
- Observed case presentations (minimum 2 per week) with immediate structured feedback using the direct observation form

**4d. Professionalism/behavioral gap interventions:**
- Written behavioral contract signed by learner and program director specifying expected and prohibited conduct
- Bi-weekly coaching sessions with a faculty mentor focused on reflection and behavior pattern identification
- 360-degree reassessment (peers, nurses, support staff) at the midpoint and endpoint of remediation

### Step 5 — Build the Weekly Activity Schedule
Produce a week-by-week schedule for the full remediation period. Shorter plans (4 weeks) should be specified at weekly granularity. Longer plans (8+ weeks) may be specified at two-week intervals.

For each week include:
- Readings or self-study tasks (with page ranges or module names — not just "read about X")
- Supervised clinical encounters (minimum number and type)
- Simulation or skills lab sessions
- Faculty check-in meeting (frequency, format, who is responsible)
- Deliverable due (written reflection, completed observation form, question bank session log)

**Week structure template:**
```
Week [N] (Dates: [start] – [end])
- Self-study: [specific resource, section, estimated time]
- Supervised encounters: [minimum N encounters, encounter type, observer]
- Simulation/skills: [session type, facility, supervisor]
- Check-in: [date, with whom, agenda]
- Deliverable: [what the learner submits or completes by end of week]
```

### Step 6 — Define Milestone Check-Ins and Reassessment Criteria
Remediation must have structured checkpoints. Do not wait until the end to assess progress.

6a. **Check-in schedule:** Set formal check-in meetings at the midpoint and endpoint of the plan (and at 25% for plans longer than 8 weeks). At each check-in, review:
   - Progress against the weekly activity log
   - Trend in direct observation ratings
   - Learner self-assessment against the SMART goals
   - Faculty assessment of trajectory

6b. **Reassessment method:** Specify how final competency will be assessed:
   - Direct observation (minimum number of encounters required)
   - Simulation assessment (pass/fail against named criteria)
   - Knowledge test (specific instrument, required score)
   - Structured oral examination or case presentation

6c. **Explicit exit criterion:** Write one sentence per SMART goal stating the observable, measurable criterion that signals successful remediation completion:
   - "Remediation is complete when [specific criterion] is achieved by [date]."
   - Example: "Remediation is complete when Dr. Martinez achieves a rating of 'Meets Expectations for PGY2' or above on the Ranked Differential Diagnosis item of the direct observation form on 4 of 5 consecutive observed presentations, by 2026-07-01."

### Step 7 — Assign Responsibilities
Create a responsibility matrix assigning every action item to the learner or to a specific faculty member or program resource. Unassigned items will not be completed.

| Action | Responsible Party | Due Date | Verification Method |
|---|---|---|---|
| [action 1] | Learner | [date] | [how verified] |
| [action 2] | Program Director | [date] | [how verified] |
| [action 3] | Faculty Mentor | [date] | [how verified] |

### Step 8 — State the Escalation Pathway
Every remediation plan must name what happens if objectives are not met. Escalation pathways are program-specific, but the plan must reference the applicable steps:

8a. If midpoint check-in shows insufficient progress: [extend by X weeks with added supervision frequency / intensify interventions / convene emergency CCC review]
8b. If end-of-remediation reassessment is not passed: [refer to program director for formal review / extend probationary status / convene CCC for determination of advancement vs. dismissal]
8c. If a patient safety event occurs during remediation: [immediate removal from clinical duties pending review — supersedes the remediation plan timeline]

### Step 9 — Document and Obtain Sign-Off
9a. **Documentation standard:** The completed plan must be saved in the learner's academic file. Include: plan creation date, all parties who contributed, all signatures, and a statement that the learner was given an opportunity to respond.

9b. **Learner sign-off:** The learner must sign the plan acknowledging they have reviewed it, understand the exit criteria, and agree to the activities. Disagreement should be documented and escalated, not silently overridden.

9c. **Legal review trigger:** If the plan includes probation language, academic dismissal as a possible outcome, or follows a patient safety event, consult with the institution's legal counsel or GME office before finalizing.

9d. **Confidentiality:** Remediation plans are part of the learner's protected academic record. Do not share details with clinical team members beyond those with a legitimate educational need.

## Worked Example

**Context:** PGY2 Internal Medicine resident, clinical reasoning gap identified at 6-month CCC review.

**Deficit description:** On three consecutive direct observations (2026-03-12, 2026-04-01, 2026-04-18), Dr. Martinez listed diagnoses without ranking by likelihood and proposed workups that did not address the leading diagnosis. Mini-CEX scores rated "Below Expectations for PGY2" on Clinical Reasoning across all three raters.

**Root cause identified:** Clinical reasoning gap (illness script synthesis deficit — gathers adequate data but does not prioritize or connect findings to differential)

**SMART Goal:**
"By 2026-07-01, Dr. Martinez will present a ranked differential (minimum 3 diagnoses in descending order of likelihood with supporting exam/lab evidence for each) on 80% of observed case presentations, as rated using the direct observation form Clinical Reasoning subscale."

**Week 1–4 Activity Schedule:**

```
Weeks 1–2 (2026-05-19 – 2026-05-30)
- Self-study: Complete 3 illness script exercises (choose from provided template) for chest pain, dyspnea, and altered mental status. Submit to Dr. Chen by Friday each week.
- Supervised encounters: Minimum 3 observed case presentations per week; Dr. Chen completes direct observation form after each.
- Check-in: Weekly 30-minute meeting with Dr. Chen (Tuesdays, 12:00 PM) to review illness scripts and presentation feedback.
- Deliverable: 2 completed illness scripts submitted by end of Week 2.

Weeks 3–4 (2026-06-02 – 2026-06-13)
- Self-study: Read Bowen (2006) "Educational Strategies to Promote Clinical Diagnostic Reasoning" — complete 5-question reflection submitted to Dr. Chen.
- Supervised encounters: Minimum 3 observed case presentations per week with direct observation forms.
- Simulation: One 90-minute cognitive coaching session in simulation center (scheduled by program coordinator by 2026-06-06).
- Check-in: Midpoint formal review with Dr. Chen and Program Director — 2026-06-13. Review direct observation trend, SMART goal trajectory.
- Deliverable: Midpoint self-assessment memo (1 page) submitted 48 hours before midpoint meeting.
```

**Reassessment Criterion:**
"Remediation is complete when Dr. Martinez achieves 'Meets Expectations for PGY2' or above on the Clinical Reasoning subscale of the direct observation form on 4 of 5 consecutive observed presentations by 2026-07-01."

**Escalation:** If midpoint review (2026-06-13) shows fewer than 50% of presentations rated at 'Meets Expectations,' the Program Director will convene an emergency CCC review within 10 business days to consider extending the remediation period or modifying the intervention.

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Common Mistake | ✅ Correct Approach |
|---|---|
| Assigning reading for a professionalism or skill deficit | Root cause must drive intervention: professionalism deficits require behavioral contracting and coaching; skill deficits require supervised practice and simulation — not textbooks |
| Open-ended remediation with no exit criterion | Every plan must include: "Remediation is complete when [specific observable criterion] is achieved by [date]" |
| Designing the plan without the learner's participation | Collaborative goal-setting is ethically required and predicts better outcomes; the learner must review, respond to, and sign the plan |
| Treating wellness or impairment issues as academic performance deficits | Wellness/impairment requires referral to EAP, fitness-for-duty evaluation, or disability accommodations — a teaching plan will not address the root cause and may delay appropriate care |
| Vague improvement language ("must show improvement") | Every criterion must be measurable and observable: "Rated 'Meets Expectations' on Clinical Reasoning subscale across 4 of 5 observed presentations" |
| Assigning a reading or task with no verification method | Every activity must have a deliverable or verification mechanism — completed reflection, submitted log, signed direct observation form |

## Output Format

The completed remediation plan should be structured as a formal document with the following sections in order:

1. **Header:** Learner name, level, program, plan creation date, plan authors, plan period
2. **Performance Deficit** (1-3 paragraphs): Behavioral description, evidence inventory, clinical consequence statement
3. **Root Cause Analysis** (table or list): Each category assessed, marked present/absent/uncertain, with brief rationale
4. **SMART Goals** (one per root cause): Written in full SMART format with explicit measurable criteria
5. **Intervention Plan** (organized by root cause): Specific activities, resources, supervision requirements
6. **Weekly Activity Schedule** (table or structured list): Week-by-week plan with deliverables
7. **Milestone Check-Ins** (list): Dates, assessors, agenda
8. **Reassessment Criteria** (one sentence per SMART goal): "Remediation is complete when..."
9. **Responsibility Matrix** (table): Action, responsible party, due date, verification method
10. **Escalation Pathway** (list): What happens at midpoint if off-track; what happens at endpoint if criteria not met; patient safety override clause
11. **Signatures:** Learner, faculty mentor, program director, date

## Verification Checklist
- [ ] Performance deficit is described in specific behavioral terms with at least two documented sources of evidence
- [ ] Root cause analysis is complete — all five categories assessed and wellness/impairment ruled out or referred appropriately
- [ ] Every intervention is matched to a root cause category — no reading-only plans for skill or professionalism deficits
- [ ] At least one SMART goal is written per root cause, meeting all five SMART criteria
- [ ] Weekly activity schedule specifies named resources, minimum encounter counts, and a deliverable for each week
- [ ] Reassessment criterion is explicit, observable, and includes a deadline date
- [ ] Responsibility matrix assigns every action to a named person with a due date
- [ ] Escalation pathway is documented for midpoint and endpoint non-completion
- [ ] Learner sign-off is obtained before implementation begins
- [ ] Legal review trigger is evaluated and GME/legal is consulted if probation or dismissal is a named outcome
