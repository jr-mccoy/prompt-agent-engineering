# Biblical Theology Method

**Subdirectory of:** `domain-biblical-studies/`
**Audience:** Seminary/academic (A) and pastors (P) working at the methodological level of biblical theology as a discipline.
**Prompts:** 4
**Added:** 2026-06-25 (Phase 3B)

---

## Why This Exists

The rest of `domain-biblical-studies/` helps a person study individual passages (exegesis), trace themes (theology-research), teach and preach (sermon-devotional, study-methods-teaching), or manage a teaching program (church-staff-ministry-ops). This subdirectory occupies a specific gap: **method-level questions that sit above exegesis and below systematic theology** — questions about how biblical theology works as a discipline, what its organizing principles are, how it differs from systematic theology, and how different traditions practice it.

These are the questions a seminary student asks in a biblical theology prolegomenon course, or a pastor asks when deciding how to structure a sermon series around "the storyline of the Bible." They are not passage-level questions, and they are not doctrinal questions — they are questions about *how to do* biblical theology.

---

## Conventions

All prompts inherit the domain's two load-bearing conventions:

1. **Tradition-neutral** — method-level prompts are especially exposed to tradition-specific assumptions masquerading as neutral frameworks (e.g., one tradition's epoch scheme presented as "the" biblical storyline). Every prompt presents methodological options as positions held by identifiable traditions, never as settled, universal frameworks.
2. **Anti-fabrication first** — scholar attributions are the primary fabrication risk in this subdirectory. Models routinely assert which scholars hold which methodological positions, fabricate publication titles and dates, and invent scholarly consensus that does not exist. All four prompts carry verify-required flags on scholar attributions; three of the four carry STRONG-GUARD banners.

| Subdirectory | What it does | How this subdirectory relates |
|---|---|---|
| `exegesis-interpretation/` | Close reading of individual passages | These method prompts provide the framework *above* passage-level exegesis — they answer "how does this passage fit the larger picture?" |
| `theology-research/` | Topical/systematic theological synthesis, doctrine, theme tracing | These method prompts address the *prior* question: how should biblical theology be done before you do it? |
| `sermon-devotional/` | Sermon and devotional preparation | Pastors use these method prompts to decide *how* to connect a passage to the biblical storyline in a sermon. |
