# Biblical Theology vs. Systematic Theology — Clarifying the Distinction

**Objective:** Clarify the distinction between biblical theology (attending to individual authors, historical progression, and canonical shape) and systematic theology (topical organization, logical coherence, and constructive claims) using a user-specified doctrine as a worked example — so the user can see how the same doctrine looks different through each lens and understand why the distinction matters for study, preaching, and teaching.

**When to use:**
- You want to understand what "biblical theology" means as a discipline distinct from systematic theology.
- You are studying a doctrine and want to see how the approach changes the results.
- You are teaching or preaching and need to decide which lens to apply.
- You encounter conflicting treatments of a doctrine and suspect the difference is methodological, not substantive.

**When NOT to use:**
- You want to trace a single theme across the canon — use `biblical_theme_canonical_trajectory.md`.
- You want to study a doctrine across traditions — use `biblical_doctrine_study_neutral.md`.
- You want to do exegesis of a specific passage — use `biblical_passage_exegesis_workflow.md`.
- You want to compare interpretive positions on a disputed text — use `biblical_interpretive_views_comparison.md`.

**Audience:** Seminary/academic (A), pastor (P).

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **Doctrine or theme.** The doctrine the user wants to use as a worked example (e.g., atonement, justification, the kingdom of God, the Holy Spirit, covenant, eschatology).
2. **Current understanding (optional).** What the user currently understands about the distinction — so the response can build on rather than repeat what they already know.
3. **Purpose (optional).** Why the user needs to understand this — academic study, sermon preparation, curriculum design, resolving a specific confusion.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Define both disciplines on their own terms — as practitioners of each would describe their own work.
- Use the user-specified doctrine as a concrete worked example throughout, not just in an appendix.
- Show how the same doctrine yields different questions, different focal texts, different structures, and different results depending on which method is applied.
- Acknowledge that the boundary between the two disciplines is itself debated — some scholars see a sharp line, others a spectrum. Present this as a live discussion.
- Name identifiable scholars or traditions associated with major positions where possible, flagging all attributions as verify-required.

### Must Not
- Present one method as superior to the other — both are legitimate and complementary.
- Fabricate what specific scholars argue, which school they belong to, or what they have published.
- Collapse the distinction ("they're basically the same") or overstate it ("they always disagree").
- Assert a single definition of "biblical theology" as settled — the term is used differently by different scholars and traditions.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** note where confessional traditions shape how each discipline is practiced (e.g., Reformed vs. Catholic vs. dispensationalist approaches to biblical theology) without endorsing one approach as correct.
- **Must Not:** present one tradition's version of biblical theology as "biblical theology" and others as departures.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Define each discipline
Define biblical theology and systematic theology as distinct disciplines:
- Biblical theology: what does it attend to? (individual authors, historical progression, canonical storyline, intra-biblical development, diversity within the canon)
- Systematic theology: what does it attend to? (topical organization, logical coherence, constructive doctrinal claims, engagement with philosophy/tradition/experience, contemporary application)
- Note that practitioners define these differently — identify at least two different definitions of "biblical theology" held by identifiable scholars or schools (verify-required).

### Step 2 — Show the distinction through the worked example
Take the user's specified doctrine and show how each discipline approaches it differently:
- **Biblical theology asks:** How does this doctrine develop across the canon? What does each biblical author contribute? Where is there diversity or progression?
- **Systematic theology asks:** What is the church's doctrinal position? How do the biblical data, tradition, reason, and experience cohere into a unified statement?
- Trace 3-4 specific differences in questions asked, texts prioritized, and conclusions reached.

### Step 3 — Identify where the methods complement and where they tension
Show where the two disciplines:
- Complement each other (systematic theology needs biblical theology's exegetical grounding; biblical theology needs systematic theology's integrative framework).
- Create productive tension (biblical theology may resist premature harmonization that systematic theology requires; systematic theology may draw on extra-biblical sources that biblical theology brackets).
- Genuinely disagree about how to handle canonical diversity.

### Step 4 — Practical implications
For the user's stated purpose (study, preaching, teaching, or general understanding):
- When should you lead with a biblical-theological approach?
- When should you lead with a systematic approach?
- How can you use both without conflating them?

## Verification

- [ ] Both disciplines are defined on their own terms, not as caricatures.
- [ ] The worked example concretely shows how results differ by method.
- [ ] Multiple definitions of "biblical theology" are presented and attributed.
- [ ] No method is presented as superior — both are treated as legitimate.
- [ ] Scholar attributions are flagged verify-required.
- [ ] No fabricated scholar positions, publication claims, or school affiliations.
- [ ] Tradition-neutral language is maintained throughout.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

DON'T:
- Present systematic theology as "less biblical" or biblical theology as "less theological."
- Assert a single definition of "biblical theology" as the settled one.
- Fabricate what scholars argue or which school they represent.

DO:
- Show that the distinction is real and methodologically important, even when the boundary is fuzzy.
- Use the worked example to make abstract methodological points concrete.
- Flag all scholar attributions and definitional claims as verify-required.
