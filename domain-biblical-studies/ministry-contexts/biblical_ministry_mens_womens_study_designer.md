# Men's / Women's Study Designer

**Objective:** Design a Bible study for a men's or women's group that (1) engages the text with real depth, (2) addresses life concerns genuinely common to the group's context, and (3) handles gender-role passages honestly — presenting the range of faithful readings without stereotyping, proof-texting, or flattening the text.

**When to use:**
- You are leading or designing a men's or women's Bible study (retreat, small group, ongoing series).
- The passage or theme touches gender roles, identity, or relationships and you want to handle it faithfully and honestly.
- You want a study that goes deeper than surface-level "what real men/women do" framing.

**When NOT to use:**
- Your group is mixed-gender and doesn't need gender-specific framing — use `biblical_smallgroup_discussion_guide.md`.
- You are building a couples'/marriage study — use `biblical_ministry_marriage_enrichment_study.md` (Phase 3B).
- You only need exegesis of a passage, not a group study — use `biblical_passage_exegesis_workflow.md`.

**Audience:** Ministry-context teachers (M) and group leaders (G) — men's/women's ministry leaders, retreat planners, small-group facilitators.

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **The passage or theme.** Scripture reference plus the text in a named translation (pasted by the user). The model references by address and works from the supplied text.
2. **Group type.** Men's or women's group. Note: the prompt works for either; the model adapts framing without stereotyping.
3. **Group profile.** Spiritual maturity range, approximate size, age range, how long the group has been meeting, and whether participants know each other well.
4. **Format.** Single session, multi-week series, or retreat intensive. Session length.
5. **Tradition (optional).** If the group belongs to a specific tradition, the model shifts emphasis accordingly — but always preserves alternative readings.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Engage the text exegetically — the study is built on what the passage says in context, not on pre-decided "men's issues" or "women's issues" layered onto it.
- When the passage touches gender roles (Ephesians 5, 1 Timothy 2, Proverbs 31, Titus 2, etc.), present the range of faithful readings across traditions: complementarian, egalitarian, and positions between. Attribute each to identifiable streams. Let the leader/group decide.
- Address life concerns that are genuinely common to the group's context without resorting to stereotypes (e.g., not all men struggle with porn; not all women struggle with comparison; avoid "men are X, women are Y" framing).
- Include discussion questions that invite honest engagement — including questions the group might be afraid to ask.
- Provide application that is concrete and contextually specific, not generic "be a godly man/woman."

### Must Not
- Stereotype the group: do not assume uniform life stage (married, parenting), uniform struggle, or uniform personality type based on gender.
- Proof-text gender-role passages by pulling verses out of literary and historical context.
- Present one tradition's reading of gender roles as the settled biblical position.
- Fabricate commentary claims, scholar attributions, cultural-background assertions, or original-language data.
- Patronize the group (e.g., "men need to be reminded to lead" or "women need to be reminded they matter").

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** when gender-role texts arise, present complementarian, egalitarian, and intermediate readings with attribution and charity.
- **Must Not:** privilege one reading as "the biblical view" — multiple traditions claim biblical warrant; the model describes, doesn't adjudicate.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Confirm passage, group, and approach
Restate the passage (by address), group type (men's or women's), group profile, format, and any declared tradition. Flag if the passage directly addresses gender roles — if so, note the tradition-neutral handling required.

### Step 2 — Exegetical foundation
Provide a brief exegetical scan of the passage: genre, literary context, key interpretive questions, and how the passage has been read across traditions. If gender-role language is present, map the major positions. All textual claims are verify-required against the user's supplied text.

### Step 3 — Design the study
Build the study session(s):
- **Opening:** a way in that connects the passage to real life without stereotyping.
- **Text engagement:** guided reading with observation, interpretation, and application layers.
- **Discussion questions:** tiered (safe → honest → brave). Include at least one question the group might be afraid to ask.
- **Application:** concrete, specific to the group's life contexts (not generic "be more godly").
- **Leader notes:** where the discussion might get hard, and how to facilitate honestly.

### Step 4 — Handle sensitive intersection points
If the passage or theme intersects with sensitive topics (singleness, divorce, sexuality, infertility, abuse, identity), flag it and provide:
- How to create safety in the room.
- What not to assume about participants' experiences.
- When to route to pastoral care or professional help.

### Step 5 — Multi-session arc (if series)
If the study is a multi-week series, map the arc: how sessions build on each other, what the series is moving toward, and how to handle participants who miss sessions.

## Verification

- [ ] The study is built on the text, not on pre-decided "gender issues."
- [ ] Gender-role passages are handled with multi-view honesty and tradition attribution.
- [ ] No stereotyping of the group's experiences, struggles, or personality.
- [ ] Discussion questions include honest/brave-tier questions.
- [ ] Application is concrete and contextually specific.
- [ ] No fabricated commentary, cultural claims, or original-language data.
- [ ] Sensitive intersection points are flagged with safety notes.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

DON'T:
- Assume all men/women share the same struggles, life stage, or personality.
- Present one tradition's gender-role reading as the biblical position.
- Use "men are wired to..." or "women naturally..." framing — let the text speak.

DO:
- Build the study on the passage, then connect to the group's real life.
- Present gender-role readings with attribution and let the group decide.
- Include questions the group might be afraid to ask — that's where the real study happens.
