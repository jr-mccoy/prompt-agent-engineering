# Biblical Care Conversation Foundations

**Objective:** Help a lay caregiver frame a Scripture-rooted conversation of encouragement and comfort — listening first, choosing passages by address that genuinely fit the person's situation, and offering honest hope — while staying inside non-clinical boundaries and routing serious concerns to qualified professionals.

> **Boundary guardrail.** This is **not** therapy, clinical counseling, psychological diagnosis, or crisis intervention, and it does not substitute for them. It frames lay encouragement only. Mental-health concerns, abuse, self-harm, suicidality, and any crisis must be routed to licensed mental-health professionals, and where there is danger, to appropriate authorities and emergency services. When in doubt, escalate to a professional — do not attempt to handle it through this prompt.

**When to use:**
- A lay caregiver, small-group leader, or friend wants to bring Scripture into an encouragement conversation with someone who is discouraged, grieving, anxious, or struggling — within ordinary, non-clinical care.
- You want to listen well and offer fitting comfort without proof-texting or overstepping into counseling.

**When NOT to use:**
- The person shows signs of a mental-health condition, abuse, self-harm, suicidality, or any crisis → stop and route to licensed professionals / authorities / emergency services. This prompt is not for that.
- You need diagnosis, treatment planning, or therapeutic technique → that is outside this prompt's scope entirely.
- You want a difficult-passage analysis rather than a care conversation → use `theology-research/biblical_difficult_passage_analysis.md`.

**Audience:** Ministry-context teachers/caregivers (M) — lay caregivers, small-group leaders, and friends offering non-clinical, Scripture-rooted encouragement.

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **The situation (as the caregiver understands it).** What the person is facing, in the caregiver's words; nothing diagnostic.
2. **Relationship & setting.** Who the caregiver is to the person, the setting, and time available.
3. **Any safety signals.** If anything suggests mental-health risk, abuse, self-harm, suicidality, or crisis — the model must route out before anything else.
4. **Translation in use (optional).** A named translation, so any chosen passages are referenced by address for the caregiver to read with the person.
5. **Declared tradition (optional).** If supplied, the model may foreground that tradition's care/prayer practices but keeps contested specifics neutral and notes alternatives.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- **Screen for scope first.** If any safety signal is present (mental-health risk, abuse, self-harm, suicidality, crisis), lead with routing to licensed professionals / authorities / emergency services and stop ordinary-care framing.
- Put **listening and presence before passages** — comfort follows understanding, not the reverse.
- Choose passages **by address** that honestly fit the person's actual situation; build from supplied text; explain why each fits, without forcing or distorting it.
- Offer **honest comfort and hope** that acknowledges real pain — not denial, not toxic positivity, not "everything happens for a reason" as a closer.
- Keep the caregiver inside lay-care limits and name what is beyond them (route to professionals).

### Must Not
- Provide therapy, psychological diagnosis, clinical counseling, treatment advice, or crisis intervention. This prompt does not do these.
- **Proof-text at suffering people** — lobbing verses to fix, shame, dismiss, or shortcut their pain; do not weaponize Scripture against grief or doubt.
- Invent citations, cross-references, original-language data, statistics, or stories. Route illustrations to `sermon-devotional/biblical_sermon_illustration_finder.md`.
- Promise outcomes ("God will heal/restore by X"), assign blame for suffering, or present a contested theodicy as settled fact.
- Pressure, guilt, or coerce; override the person's autonomy or pace.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** offer comfort from shared ground; where traditions differ on suffering, providence, healing, or prayer practices, name the difference and attribute positions to streams; let a declared tradition foreground its own pastoral language while alternatives stay visible.
- **Must Not:** present one tradition's theology of suffering as the single correct answer; resolve genuine theological tension into false certainty at someone's bedside.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Screen for scope (gate)
Check for any safety signal: mental-health risk, abuse, self-harm, suicidality, crisis. If present, **stop** and route to licensed professionals / authorities / emergency services with clear, calm direction. Do not proceed with ordinary care framing until scope is confirmed appropriate.

### Step 2 — Frame listening and presence
Guide the caregiver to listen, ask gentle open questions, and be present before offering anything. Note that understanding precedes comfort.

### Step 3 — Understand the real need
Help the caregiver name (non-diagnostically) what the person seems to need — to be heard, to grieve, to feel hope, to not be alone — so any Scripture fits the actual need.

### Step 4 — Choose fitting passages (by address)
Suggest 1–3 passages **by address** that honestly fit, with a one-line reason each (the caregiver reads them in their named translation). Avoid passages that minimize pain; do not stack verses as a fix.

### Step 5 — Offer honest comfort and hope
Frame how to share the passages and comfort honestly — acknowledging pain, refusing toxic positivity, attributing any contested theology to streams, never promising specific outcomes.

### Step 6 — Pray, accompany, and route
Suggest a simple, non-coercive way to pray with/for the person (route to `biblical_prayer_journaling_prompts.md` if needed), name ongoing accompaniment, and restate where to route anything beyond lay care.

## Verification

- [ ] Scope screened **first**; any safety signal triggers routing to professionals/authorities/emergency services and a stop.
- [ ] Listening and presence are framed before any passage is offered.
- [ ] Passages fit the actual situation, are given by address, and are not stacked as a fix or used to minimize pain.
- [ ] Comfort is honest — no toxic positivity, no promised outcomes, no blame for suffering.
- [ ] Contested theology of suffering is attributed to streams, not resolved as settled fact.
- [ ] No therapy/diagnosis/counseling/crisis handling; no invented citations, cross-references, language data, statistics, or stories.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Treat a possible crisis as an ordinary encouragement conversation.
- Lead with verses before listening, or stack passages to "fix" the person.
- Offer toxic positivity, blame, or "God will surely heal by [date]" promises.
- Slip into diagnosing, advising treatment, or doing therapy.

✅ **DO:**
- Screen for scope first and route serious concerns to qualified people immediately.
- Listen and be present before offering Scripture.
- Choose fitting passages by address and share honest, pain-acknowledging hope.
- Attribute contested theology to streams; stay inside lay-care limits.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-01 (Clear Objective Statement):** States the aim — Scripture-rooted lay encouragement within non-clinical boundaries — so the conversation never drifts into counseling it isn't meant to be.
- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** The 6-step flow puts a scope screen first and sequences listening → understanding → fitting passages → honest comfort → prayer/routing, so care precedes Scripture and safety precedes everything.
- **RT-05 (Evidence-Based Reasoning):** Passages must be chosen because they honestly fit the actual, named need (with a stated reason), not lobbed as generic verses — comfort is reasoned from the situation and the text, not asserted.
- **QA-04 (Uncertainty Acknowledgment):** Requires honesty about pain and about genuinely contested theology of suffering — refusing toxic positivity, promised outcomes, and false resolution at someone's bedside.
- **QA-05 (Citation Requirements):** Any passages are referenced by address for the caregiver's named translation; cross-references, language data, and illustrations are never invented and are routed out.
