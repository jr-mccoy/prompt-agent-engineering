# Special-Needs Inclusive Bible Teaching

**Objective:** Take a children's Bible lesson and make it work for the actual children in the room — including disabled and neurodivergent children — by building accessible defaults everyone gets, adding specific accommodations parents have asked for, keeping the Bible story faithful, and putting the safety arrangements in writing.

> **Child-safety and boundary note.** This prompt **never diagnoses, screens, or guesses** whether a child has a condition, and never asks a teacher to. Information about a child comes **only from parents/guardians**, with their consent, and is shared only with the adults who need it. Safety rules follow the church's written **safeguarding policy**, which overrides anything here: screened adults only, never alone with a child out of sight of another adult, personal care (toileting, feeding, changing) only as the policy and the parents' written plan allow, medical, allergy, and seizure plans supplied by parents and kept in the room, an agreed plan if a child leaves the room, and **no physical restraint or seclusion** as a teaching or behaviour tool. Content stays age-appropriate, concrete, and gentle. Medical, therapy, and behaviour-support questions go to the family's own professionals; for home-side support see `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/neurodivergence/` and `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/ages-4-8/`.

**When to use:**
- A child with a disability or neurodivergence is joining (or already in) your class and the parents have shared what helps.
- You want a lesson that works by default for children who need movement, quiet, visuals, or extra time.
- You are teaching a healing or disability-related passage and want to handle it with care.

**When NOT to use:**
- You need the base lesson first — use `domain-biblical-studies/ministry-contexts/biblical_ministry_kids_bible_lesson_builder.md`, then bring it here.
- You are designing adult or one-to-one discipleship sessions — use `domain-discipleship/session-and-lesson/discipleship_session_accessibility_design.md`.
- A parent wants help at home with sensory regulation or routines — use `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/ages-4-8/parenting_sensory_at_home_toolkit.md` or `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/ages-4-8/parenting_adhd_executive_function_scaffold.md`.
- You are training your volunteer team on child safety generally — use `domain-biblical-studies/church-staff-ministry-ops/biblical_churchstaff_teacher_training_plan.md`.

**Audience:** Ministry-context teachers (M) — children's ministry leaders, Sunday school teachers, buddies, and inclusion coordinators.

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **The lesson.** Passage by address with the user's supplied translation text, age band, length, room, and helpers.
2. **What parents have shared** (with consent), in their words: what helps, what overwhelms, how the child communicates, mobility needs, medical/allergy/seizure plan, personal-care plan, what to do if the child leaves the room. If nothing is shared, design universal defaults only.
3. **The child's interests** (from parents), to use as hooks.
4. **Safeguarding policy** essentials: ratios, buddy screening, personal-care rules, incident reporting.
5. **Declared tradition (optional).**

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Build **universal defaults first** (visual schedule, predictable routine, movement options, quiet space, multiple ways to respond), then layer **individual accommodations** from parent information.
- Cover four access areas: **sensory** (sound, light, touch, smell, crowding), **cognitive** (one big idea, repetition, concrete props, first–then structure, extra processing time), **communication** (speech, sign, picture cards, the child's AAC device), **physical** (routes, seating, table height, roles that do not require standing or fine-motor speed).
- Keep the story **faithful to the supplied text**; simplify by omission, never by changing it.
- For healing or disability texts, **preview with parents**, centre what the text emphasises (often faith, friendship, God's action, forgiveness, or belonging), and avoid any framing that a disabled child is broken, lacking faith, or being punished.
- Put safety arrangements in a **written plan** (who, where, what if).
- Use the child's **interests** as hooks where parents named them.

### Must Not
- Diagnose, label, or speculate about a child; ask a child or parent to disclose a diagnosis to receive accommodations.
- Recommend therapies, medication, diets, weighted items, or behaviour programmes.
- Remove or restrict a child's AAC device or fidget as a consequence; treat it as the child's voice or tool.
- Use restraint, seclusion, or withholding of snack or activity as behaviour management.
- State prevalence figures or "most autistic children…" generalisations.
- Promise a child or family healing.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** note that traditions differ on healing today and on how disability is understood theologically; keep the lesson on what the passage says, and let the declared tradition set any further emphasis.
- **Must Not:** teach that disability results from sin or weak faith; present one tradition's view of healing as fact.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Read the room
Summarise the lesson and what parents have shared, child by child, in neutral functional terms ("uses an AAC device", "needs to move every 10 minutes") — no labels.

### Step 2 — Universal defaults
Design the default lesson shape: visual schedule, start/stop signals, a quiet corner, movement breaks, choice of response (say, point, draw, act, press).

### Step 3 — Four-area accommodations
For each child with shared needs, list sensory, cognitive, communication, and physical accommodations and who provides each.

### Step 4 — Story adaptation
Tell the story with concrete props and repeated key phrase; check every adaptation against the text. Flag hard or sensitive content (including healing narratives) for parent preview.

### Step 5 — Participation roles
Give every child a real role (prop holder, sound maker, page turner, AAC "speaker" of the key phrase).

### Step 6 — Safety plan
Buddy assignments, ratios, personal care, medical plan location, leaving-the-room plan, incident reporting — all per policy.

### Step 7 — Parent follow-up
One line to parents: what was taught, what worked, a question to ask at home.

---

## Verification

- [ ] No diagnosis, label, or speculation; all child information parent-shared.
- [ ] Universal defaults precede individual accommodations.
- [ ] All four access areas considered for each child with shared needs.
- [ ] Story faithful to the supplied text; sensitive content flagged for parents.
- [ ] No restraint, seclusion, device removal, or therapy advice.
- [ ] Safety plan names people and follows the written policy.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Treat stimming, pacing, or leaving the circle as misbehaviour to correct.
- Teach a healing story as "Jesus fixes people who are different".
- Swap in an easier "story" that is not in the text.
- Design for a diagnosis instead of for the child the parents described.

✅ **DO:**
- Make access the default so no child has to be singled out.
- Model on the AAC device yourself and wait for the answer.

## Example Output

```
# Inclusive Lesson — Mark 2:1–12 — ages 6–8, 40 min, 9 children, 2 adults + 1 buddy

## Room summary (parent-shared)
- M.: speaks using an AAC device; covers ears in loud singing; loves trains.
- R.: wheelchair user; full participation in everything; parents asked that the story not imply he needs fixing.
- J.: needs to move often; does best with a job.

## Universal defaults
Picture schedule on the wall (welcome → story → build → pray → snack); a chime instead of a shout to
gather; quiet corner with cushions and ear defenders; every question answerable by saying, pointing,
pressing, or acting.

## Individual accommodations
| M. | ear defenders offered during songs; dim one light | first–then card | key phrase pre-loaded on AAC by parents ("Jesus forgives", "friends help"); adults model on device; 10-second wait | — | Buddy |
| R. | — | — | — | table at chair height; clear aisle to the "house"; role as one of the four friends | Lead |
| J. | — | job list | — | role: lowers the mat (rope + toy figure) | Assistant |

## Story adaptation + preview flags
Props: a cardboard house with a lift-off roof, a mat, four friend figures. Repeated phrase: "Friends
helped. Jesus forgave." Story follows 2:1–12 in order: crowd fills the house (2:2); four friends carry
their friend (2:3); they open the roof (2:4); Jesus sees their faith and forgives (2:5); questions from
the teachers of the law (2:6–7); Jesus tells the man to get up, take his mat, and go home, and he gets up and goes out (2:11–12).
Emphasis: the friends' determination to bring him, and that Jesus forgives — the text's own focus in 2:5–10.
Preview flag: discussed with R.'s parents beforehand; they chose to keep the passage. Teacher does not
say or imply that R. or anyone needs to be made able to walk to belong.
Tradition note: churches differ on how they understand healing today; the lesson stays with the text.

## Participation roles
Four friends (R. leads the group carrying the mat on his tray), roof-opener, sound-maker for the crowd
(M. chooses to press "busy!" on the device or use a shaker), mat-lowerer (J.).

## Safety plan
Two screened adults always in sight of each other; buddy screened per policy. No personal care
needed per parents. M.'s parents' plan if he leaves: buddy follows at a distance and keeps him in
view; lead notifies parents through the check-in system. Snack: no nuts (room rule); M. brings own. Incident form in
the drawer.

## Parent follow-up
"Today: Mark 2:1–12 — friends brought their friend to Jesus, and Jesus forgave. Ask: who are friends
who help you?" For M., the same words were added to his device page.
```

## Techniques Used

- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** Room → defaults → accommodations → story → roles → safety → follow-up.
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework):** Each child's access is planned across sensory, cognitive, communication, and physical areas.
- **CM-02 (Constraint Specification):** Safeguarding policy, parent-shared information, and text fidelity bound every adaptation.
- **ED-04 (Personalization Hooks):** Children's interests, as parents describe them, become hooks and roles.
- **DP-04 (Must-Not Constraints):** Explicit bans on diagnosis, restraint, device removal, therapy advice, and healing promises.
