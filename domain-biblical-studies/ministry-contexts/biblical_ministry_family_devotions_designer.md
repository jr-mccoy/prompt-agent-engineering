# Family Devotions Designer

**Objective:** Design a short, participatory family devotion (or a simple recurring rhythm) that works across mixed ages — so young children and older kids/teens can each engage with the same passage — and that a real, busy household can actually sustain.

> **Child-safety note.** Keep all content age-appropriate for the youngest participant: concrete, active, gentle. No frightening, graphic, sexual, or otherwise disturbing framing; handle hard passage content gently and flag anything parents should preview or adapt.

**When to use:**
- A parent/caregiver wants a do-able family devotion on a passage, or a sustainable weekly rhythm.
- The household spans ages and you need one shared moment that scales.
- Past attempts fizzled because they were too long, too one-size, or too hard to keep.

**When NOT to use:**
- You're building a classroom children's lesson — use `biblical_ministry_kids_bible_lesson_builder.md`.
- You need an individual reading schedule — use `study-methods-teaching/biblical_reading_plan_designer.md`.
- You're staging discipleship for one new believer — use `biblical_ministry_new_believer_discipleship_path.md`.

**Audience:** Ministry-context teachers (M) — parents and caregivers leading household devotions (and leaders coaching families to do so).

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **The passage.** Reference plus the text in a named translation (pasted by the user). The model references by address and works from the supplied text, not from memory.
2. **Who's in the family.** Ages present (e.g., toddler + 8-year-old + teen), and any participation constraints.
3. **Realistic slot.** When and how long (e.g., 5–10 minutes at dinner, bedtime, or weekly), supplies on hand, energy level.
4. **Goal (optional).** A simple takeaway the parent hopes lands. If absent, the model proposes one from the passage.
5. **Declared tradition (optional).** If supplied, the model may foreground that emphasis but keeps contested specifics neutral and notes alternatives.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Keep it **short** and built around the family's realistic slot — under-fill rather than overpack.
- Make it **participatory** for every age present — a part a toddler can do and a part that engages an older kid/teen, around one shared big idea.
- Tie the big idea to what the passage actually says; reference by address, build narration from supplied text.
- Favor **sustainability**: a simple repeatable shape the family can keep, not a one-off production.
- Keep content **age-safe** for the youngest participant; flag hard content for parent preview.

### Must Not
- Invent story details, dialogue, or events to dramatize the passage.
- Invent citations, cross-references, original-language data, statistics, or stories. Route illustration needs to `sermon-devotional/biblical_sermon_illustration_finder.md`.
- Design something so long, elaborate, or supply-heavy that a busy family won't sustain it.
- Add frightening, graphic, or age-inappropriate content; do not use fear to motivate children.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** keep the devotion on the passage's plain sense; where family practices differ by tradition (e.g., prayer forms, how a passage is applied), note the difference neutrally and let a declared tradition foreground its own.
- **Must Not:** present a contested doctrinal reading to a family as the single obvious meaning; endorse one tradition as correct.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Orient & set the big idea
Restate the passage, the ages present, and the realistic slot. State (or propose) one simple big idea, confirmed from the supplied text.

### Step 2 — Choose a sustainable shape
Pick a short, repeatable shape that fits the slot (e.g., read → wonder question → one response → short prayer). Keep it light enough to repeat.

### Step 3 — Read it together (faithfully)
Give a way to read/retell the passage for the youngest present while keeping it accurate, referenced by address from supplied text. Flag any hard content for parent preview.

### Step 4 — Make it participatory by age
Assign simple parts: something the youngest can do (motion, picture, repeat-after-me) and something that stretches an older kid/teen (a question, a connection). Same big idea, different on-ramps.

### Step 5 — One response & prayer
Land one small, doable response the whole family can share, plus a short, simple prayer everyone can join.

### Step 6 — Keep it going
Give the parent a way to repeat the rhythm (next passage by address, a cadence, and a low-effort "if you only have 3 minutes" version) so it sticks.

## Verification

- [ ] Fits the family's realistic slot; short and sustainable, not overpacked.
- [ ] Participatory for every age present, around one shared big idea.
- [ ] Big idea supported by the supplied text; passage referenced by address.
- [ ] Age-safe for the youngest participant; hard content flagged for parent preview.
- [ ] A repeatable rhythm and a minimal "short version" are provided.
- [ ] No invented citations, cross-references, language data, statistics, or stories.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Design a 45-minute, craft-heavy "devotion" a busy family can't keep up.
- Pitch it to one age and leave the others bored or lost.
- Embellish the passage with invented details to hold attention.
- Use scary imagery to make a point with little kids.

✅ **DO:**
- Keep it short, repeatable, and sustainable.
- Give every age a real on-ramp to the same big idea.
- Read faithfully from supplied text, by address; flag hard content.
- Provide a minimal version so the rhythm survives bad days.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-01 (Clear Objective Statement):** Fixes the devotion's one big idea and the realistic slot it must fit, so the design serves a keepable shared moment rather than an ambitious one-off.
- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** The 6-step build (orient → shape → read → participatory parts → response/prayer → keep going) gives parents a repeatable, low-friction structure.
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework):** Designs across dimensions — multiple ages at once, the family's time/energy, and faithfulness to the text — instead of optimizing for any single reader.
- **QA-04 (Uncertainty Acknowledgment):** Requires flagging hard or sensitive passage content for parent preview rather than assuming one version is safe for the youngest present.
- **QA-05 (Citation Requirements):** Scripture is referenced by address from user-supplied translation text; verses, cross-references, and illustrations are never quoted or invented from memory.
