# Marriage Enrichment Bible Study Designer

**Objective:** Design a couples' Bible study or marriage enrichment series that engages real marital themes — communication, conflict, forgiveness, intimacy, shared purpose, roles, suffering together — with honest biblical grounding drawn from across the canon. The study handles gender-role texts (Ephesians 5, Colossians 3, 1 Peter 3, 1 Corinthians 11, Genesis 1-3) by presenting complementarian, egalitarian, and moderate positions as identifiable tradition-readings rather than settling the question, and contextualizes patriarchal-context passages within their literary and historical setting.

**When to use:**
- You are designing a marriage enrichment study for couples in a church, small group, or retreat setting.
- You want to teach on marriage-related passages without either ignoring the gender-role question or making it the entire study.
- You need a study that works across traditions (or within a declared tradition while keeping alternatives visible).
- You are preparing a couples' retreat, premarital study series, or marriage-strengthening curriculum.

**When NOT to use:**
- You need a family devotions guide for parents and children — use `biblical_ministry_family_devotions_designer.md`.
- You want a general small-group discussion guide on any topic — use `study-methods-teaching/biblical_smallgroup_discussion_guide.md`.
- You want an academic comparison of interpretive views on gender-role texts — use `theology-research/biblical_interpretive_views_comparison.md`.
- You need marriage counseling or crisis intervention — this is a Bible study designer, not therapy. Route to a licensed counselor.

**Audience:** Ministry-context teachers, pastors, and group leaders (M, P, G) — those designing or leading couples' Bible studies.

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **Group profile.** Number of couples, approximate ages and marriage stages (newlywed, mid-marriage, long-married, mixed), spiritual maturity range.
2. **Setting.** Weekly small group, Sunday class, retreat (half-day, full-day, weekend), premarital series, or sermon companion.
3. **Number of sessions.** How many sessions the study should span.
4. **Themes requested.** Specific marital themes the leader wants to address (communication, conflict, forgiveness, intimacy, roles, finances, parenting together, suffering, shared mission), or "let the study cover the essentials."
5. **Declared tradition (optional).** If supplied, foreground that tradition's teaching on marriage while keeping alternatives visible with attribution. If not supplied, present all positions neutrally.
6. **Sensitive situations.** Any awareness of participants experiencing marital difficulty, separation, infertility, loss, or domestic concerns. (If domestic violence or abuse is indicated, route to professionals immediately — this study does not address those situations.)
7. **Translation in use (optional).**

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Draw from the **full canon's marriage-relevant material** — Genesis 1-2 (creation), Song of Songs (desire and intimacy), Proverbs (wisdom for partnership), Ruth (loyalty), Hosea (covenant faithfulness), the Gospels (Jesus on marriage, divorce, and servanthood), Pauline household codes, and the broader theology of covenant — not just Ephesians 5 on repeat.
- **Handle gender-role texts honestly.** When a passage is read differently across traditions (complementarian, egalitarian, moderate/soft-complementarian), present each reading with attribution. Note the literary and historical context (Greco-Roman household codes, patriarchal social structures) as background, flagged verify-required.
- **Engage real marital themes.** The study should address what couples actually face — not just theology about marriage in the abstract. Each session should connect biblical text to lived experience through discussion and application.
- **Design for couples, not individuals.** Discussion formats should include couple-conversation time (not just group discussion), and application should be actionable within the marriage.
- **Screen for safety.** If the leader indicates domestic violence, abuse, or coercive control, stop the study design and route to appropriate professionals. A marriage enrichment study is not a tool for those situations.

### Must Not
- **Adjudicate the gender-role question.** Do not declare complementarian, egalitarian, or any other position as "the biblical view." Present them as readings held by identifiable traditions.
- **Reduce marriage passages to a single theme.** Ephesians 5 is not only about submission/headship — it is about mutual sacrifice, Christ-and-church typology, and lived partnership. Present the full passage.
- **Proof-text.** Do not rip passages from context to support marriage advice that the text does not actually offer. Proverbs 31 is not a checklist for wives; Song of Songs is not a sex manual with techniques.
- **Fabricate historical claims, original-language data, cross-references, or citations.** All background information is verify-required.
- **Ignore the hard passages.** If the study touches on divorce (Matthew 19, 1 Corinthians 7), remarriage, or submission, engage them honestly rather than skipping them.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** present complementarian, egalitarian, and moderate readings of contested texts as positions held by named traditions, with their biblical and theological reasoning. If a tradition is declared, foreground it while alternatives remain visible.
- **Must Not:** dismiss any tradition's reading as obviously wrong, unbiblical, or outdated. The study presents; the couples and their community discern.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Confirm group profile, setting, and themes
Restate the group profile, setting, number of sessions, requested themes, declared tradition (if any), and any sensitive situations. If domestic violence or abuse is indicated, stop and route to professionals. Map requested themes to sessions.

### Step 2 — Select passages for each session theme
For each session, select 1-2 primary passages and 1-2 supporting passages from across the canon. Ensure the study draws from multiple genres and testaments — not just Pauline epistles. For each passage, note the genre, context, and what the text actually addresses.

### Step 3 — Handle gender-role and contested texts
For any session that touches on roles, submission, headship, or household codes, design the study to present the passage in its literary and historical context, then present the major tradition-readings (complementarian, egalitarian, moderate) with attribution. Provide discussion questions that let couples explore the readings without the study declaring a winner.

### Step 4 — Design discussion format for couples
Structure each session with: (a) text reading, (b) contextual notes, (c) group discussion questions (open, not leading), (d) couple-conversation time (private questions for spouses to discuss together), and (e) application exercise (a concrete action for the week). Couple-conversation questions should be designed for safety — nothing that forces disclosure of private conflict in a group setting.

### Step 5 — Include application exercises
For each session, design an application that couples do together during the week — a conversation prompt, a shared activity, a practice to try, or a reflection to write and discuss. Applications should be concrete and proportioned to the theme.

### Step 6 — Build the study package
Assemble the complete study: session-by-session plan with passages, contextual notes, discussion questions, couple-conversation prompts, and application exercises. Include a leader's guide with notes on handling sensitive moments and when to refer couples to pastoral care or professional counseling.

## Verification

- [ ] Passages are drawn from across the canon (multiple genres and testaments), not just Ephesians 5.
- [ ] Gender-role and contested texts are handled with tradition-attributed readings, not adjudicated.
- [ ] Literary and historical context is provided for patriarchal-context passages (flagged verify-required).
- [ ] Discussion format includes both group and couple-conversation time.
- [ ] Application exercises are concrete and couple-focused.
- [ ] No proof-texting — passages are presented in context with what they say and do not say.
- [ ] Safety screening: domestic violence or abuse routes to professionals.
- [ ] No fabricated citations, historical claims, or original-language data.

## False-Positive Prevention

DON'T:
- Build the entire study around Ephesians 5 and ignore Song of Songs, Genesis 1-2, Ruth, Hosea, and the Gospels.
- Present one tradition's reading of gender-role texts as "what the Bible teaches about marriage."
- Design discussion questions that force couples to disclose private marital conflict in a group setting.
- Proof-text Proverbs 31 as a wife-performance checklist or Song of Songs as a prescriptive intimacy manual.

DO:
- Draw from the full canon's marriage-relevant material across genres.
- Present complementarian, egalitarian, and moderate readings with attribution and let couples discern.
- Design couple-conversation time that is private and safe alongside group discussion.
- Engage hard passages (divorce, remarriage, submission) honestly rather than skipping them.
