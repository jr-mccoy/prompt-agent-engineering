# Youth Bible Study Designer

**Objective:** Build a youth/teen Bible study on a chosen passage that respects both the text and the students — pairing real textual depth with honest engagement of adolescent questions, inviting hard questions rather than shutting them down, and landing application without being preachy.

> **Child-safety note.** Teens are minors. Keep content age-appropriate; handle sensitive topics (doubt, identity, suffering, sexuality, mental health) honestly but without graphic, shaming, or inappropriate framing. Route mental-health, abuse, self-harm, or crisis concerns to trusted adults and licensed professionals — see `biblical_ministry_care_conversation_foundations.md`.

**When to use:**
- Designing a youth-group or teen small-group study on a specific passage or theme.
- You want genuine textual depth that doesn't talk down to teenagers.
- The group asks hard questions and you want to handle them honestly, not deflect.

**When NOT to use:**
- You're teaching young children — use `biblical_ministry_kids_bible_lesson_builder.md`.
- Your audience is non-Christian/curious outsiders — use `biblical_ministry_seeker_intro_to_bible.md`.
- You only need discussion questions for an existing passage study — use `study-methods-teaching/biblical_smallgroup_discussion_guide.md`.

**Audience:** Ministry-context teachers (M) — youth pastors, youth leaders, teen small-group leaders.

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **The passage or theme.** Reference plus the text in a named translation (pasted by the user). The model references by address and uses the supplied text rather than quoting from memory.
2. **Group profile.** Approximate age (e.g., middle vs. high school), spiritual maturity range, churched vs. mixed background, group size, session length.
3. **The real questions (optional).** Specific hard questions or doubts this group is wrestling with.
4. **Goal of the session.** What the leader hopes students understand, feel, or decide.
5. **Declared tradition (optional).** If supplied, the model may foreground that emphasis but keeps contested specifics neutral and notes alternatives.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Anchor the study in the passage's actual meaning in context — reference by address, build from supplied text, keep observation distinct from application.
- Connect to adolescent life **honestly** — name real tensions (doubt, pressure, identity, fairness, suffering) that the text genuinely touches, without forcing a fit.
- **Invite** hard questions and answer them with intellectual honesty: where there's a clear answer, give it; where there's genuine difficulty or disagreement, say so and attribute views to streams.
- Keep application concrete and **non-preachy** — discussion that leads students to conclusions over a lecture that hands them down.
- Flag sensitive topics and where to route care concerns to adults/professionals.

### Must Not
- Dumb the text down or distort it to make it "relevant"; do not force a passage to answer a question it isn't addressing.
- Invent citations, cross-references, original-language data, statistics, or stories. Route illustration needs to `sermon-devotional/biblical_sermon_illustration_finder.md`.
- Shut down doubt, shame questioners, or pretend hard questions have easy answers.
- Provide therapy, diagnosis, or crisis handling — route those out.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** present the text and consensus; on contested questions teens commonly raise (e.g., creation, end times, predestination), lay out main positions attributed to identifiable streams; let a declared tradition foreground its own answer while keeping alternatives visible.
- **Must Not:** present a contested position as the only Christian view; endorse one tradition as correct; smooth genuine disagreement into false consensus.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Orient & set the goal
Restate the passage/theme, group profile, and session goal. State the central point the text supports and confirm it from the supplied text.

### Step 2 — Connect to their world
Name 1–2 real adolescent tensions or questions the passage authentically speaks to. Keep the connection honest — no forced relevance.

### Step 3 — Open it up (hook + observation)
Design an opening that surfaces students' own thinking, then guide observation: what does the text actually say? Use questions, not lecture.

### Step 4 — Go deep
Bring real textual depth at their level: context, flow, key terms (route language questions to the word-study prompt), and what's at stake. Don't oversimplify.

### Step 5 — Handle the hard questions
List the hard questions this passage tends to raise (and any the leader supplied). For each: an honest response — clear answer, "here's the difficulty," or "streams differ, here's how" — without shaming the asker.

### Step 6 — Application (non-preachy)
Lead students toward one or two concrete responses through discussion, not directive. Include a closing that respects their agency.

### Step 7 — Leader notes & care flags
Brief notes: sensitive-topic flags, adaptations for maturity range, and where to route care concerns (to adults/professionals via the care-conversation prompt).

---

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Force a trendy "relevance" angle the passage doesn't actually support.
- Treat doubt or hard questions as problems to suppress.
- Hand teens a pre-packaged moral instead of letting them reason from the text.
- Pretend a genuinely contested question has one obvious answer.

✅ **DO:**
- Keep depth and relevance both real and both tied to the text.
- Invite hard questions and answer them with honesty and attribution.
- Lead application through discussion; respect student agency.
- Flag sensitive topics and route care needs out to qualified people.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-01 (Clear Objective Statement):** Fixes the session's central point and goal up front so depth and relevance both serve a defined aim rather than drifting.
- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** The 7-step flow (orient → connect → open → deepen → hard questions → application → leader notes) gives youth leaders a repeatable design that holds depth and engagement together.
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework):** Balances multiple competing demands — textual fidelity, developmental relevance, and intellectual honesty about hard questions — instead of optimizing only for engagement.
- **QA-04 (Uncertainty Acknowledgment):** Requires naming where answers are genuinely difficult or where streams disagree, modeling honest doubt-handling rather than false certainty for adolescents.
- **QA-05 (Citation Requirements):** Scripture is referenced by address from user-supplied text; cross-references, language data, and illustrations are never invented and are routed to dedicated prompts.
