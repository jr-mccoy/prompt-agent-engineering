# New Believer Discipleship Path

**Objective:** Lay out a staged, achievable discipleship path that takes a new believer from first steps through core practices and foundational passages at a humane pace — grounding them in Scripture and basic Christian life while staying neutral on contested specifics and attributing differing views to streams.

**When to use:**
- A new believer (or someone new to faith) needs a structured "where do I start" path.
- A mentor or church wants a foundations sequence that isn't tied to one tradition's distinctives.
- You want passages, practices, and pacing organized into stages rather than a single overwhelming list.

**When NOT to use:**
- The person is a curious skeptic not yet a believer — use `biblical_ministry_seeker_intro_to_bible.md`.
- You only need a Scripture reading schedule — use `study-methods-teaching/biblical_reading_plan_designer.md`.
- You're discipling a whole household — pair with `biblical_ministry_family_devotions_designer.md`.

**Audience:** Ministry-context teachers (M) — mentors, discipleship leaders, pastors, small-group leaders walking alongside new believers.

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **The new believer's situation.** Background (churched/unchurched), time available per week, learning style, life stage; any specific questions or struggles.
2. **Translation in use.** The named translation the disciple reads, so foundational passages can be referenced by address for the user to supply/read in that translation.
3. **Time horizon.** Over how long the path should unfold (e.g., first 90 days, first year) and desired pace.
4. **Declared tradition (optional).** If supplied, the model may foreground that tradition's practices and emphases but keeps contested specifics neutral and notes alternatives. No declaration → ecumenical, multi-view default.
5. **Available resources.** People (mentor, group, church), materials, and rhythms the disciple can realistically plug into.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Organize the path into **stages** with clear, achievable first steps before later ones; sequence from foundational to fuller.
- Ground each stage in **foundational passages by address** (user reads them in their named translation) and **core practices** (e.g., reading Scripture, prayer, gathering, service).
- Set a **realistic pace** matched to the person's time and life stage — under-promise rather than overwhelm.
- Keep contested specifics neutral and **attribute differing views to identifiable streams** so the disciple knows where their own community may land.
- Point to people and resources (mentor, church, group) — discipleship is relational, not a solo reading plan. Catalog external resources generically; do not fabricate specific titles/authors.

### Must Not
- Invent citations, cross-references, original-language data, statistics, stories, or specific book/author recommendations presented as verified. Route illustration needs to `sermon-devotional/biblical_sermon_illustration_finder.md`.
- Present a single tradition's distinctives (baptism mode, spiritual gifts, eschatology, sacraments, church government) as the universal Christian position.
- Front-load advanced or contested material that a new believer isn't ready for.
- Replace relational discipleship with a content checklist.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** teach the shared core (who Jesus is, the gospel, Scripture, prayer, the church) as common ground; on contested practices, name that traditions differ and attribute positions to streams; let a declared tradition foreground its own path while alternatives stay visible.
- **Must Not:** endorse one tradition's distinctives as correct; bury disagreement; imply there's only one valid way to grow.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Orient & assess readiness
Restate the disciple's situation, time, and time horizon. Note where they're starting and any pressing questions to address early.

### Step 2 — Define the stages
Lay out 3–4 stages from first steps to fuller foundations (e.g., First steps → Core practices → Knowing the story → Living it out). Give each stage a plain aim.

### Step 3 — Foundational passages per stage
For each stage, list foundational passages **by address** appropriate to that stage (the disciple reads them in their named translation). Note why each fits the stage; do not quote from memory.

### Step 4 — Core practices per stage
Attach core practices to each stage (Scripture reading rhythm, prayer, gathering, service, generosity). Keep practices concrete and small at first.

### Step 5 — Handle contested specifics
Where the path touches contested topics, flag them, attribute the main positions to streams, and let a declared tradition foreground its own — without ruling for the disciple.

### Step 6 — Pace & relational supports
Set a realistic cadence and name the people/resources (mentor, group, church) who should walk with the disciple at each stage. Catalog resource *types* generically; flag specific titles as verify-required.

### Step 7 — Check-ins & next horizon
Define simple check-in questions for the mentor and what "ready for the next stage" looks like, plus where to go after the path's horizon.

---

## Verification

- [ ] Path is genuinely staged — first steps precede later ones; pace is realistic.
- [ ] Foundational passages are given by address for the disciple's named translation, not quoted from memory.
- [ ] Core practices are concrete and small early, fuller later.
- [ ] Contested specifics are flagged and attributed to streams, not ruled.
- [ ] Relational supports are named; resources cataloged by type with specifics flagged verify-required.
- [ ] No invented citations, cross-references, language data, statistics, stories, or verified-sounding titles.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Hand a new believer one giant undifferentiated reading list.
- Bake one tradition's distinctives into the "foundations" as if universal.
- Recommend specific books/authors as if their titles and content were verified.
- Reduce discipleship to content consumption with no people involved.

✅ **DO:**
- Stage the path; start small and concrete; pace it humanely.
- Reference passages by address for the disciple's translation.
- Flag contested specifics and attribute positions to streams.
- Name mentors/groups and catalog resource types generically with verify flags.

---

## Techniques Used

- **ST-01 (Clear Objective Statement):** States the path's aim — staged grounding at a humane pace — so the design serves formation, not information dumping.
- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** The 7-step build (assess → stages → passages → practices → contested specifics → pace/supports → check-ins) produces an ordered, repeatable pathway.
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework):** Designs across dimensions — content (passages), practice (habits), pace (time), and relationship (mentors/community) — rather than treating discipleship as reading alone.
- **OC-12 (External Reference Catalog):** Organizes external supports — mentors, groups, churches, and resource *types* — into a usable catalog while flagging specific titles/authors as verify-required rather than asserting them.
- **QA-05 (Citation Requirements):** Foundational passages are listed by address for the disciple's named translation and never quoted from memory; cross-references and titles are not fabricated.
