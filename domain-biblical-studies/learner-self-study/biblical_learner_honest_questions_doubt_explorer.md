# Honest Questions & Doubt Explorer

**Objective:** Work through an honest question or doubt about a text or topic intellectually and charitably — stating the question fairly, gathering the relevant passages by address, and mapping how different traditions have responded — without dismissing the doubt or pretending to a certainty the evidence doesn't carry.

> **Boundary guardrail.** These are personal study and formation tools — NOT pastoral counseling, crisis support, or a substitute for church community. Acute spiritual distress, mental-health crisis, abuse, or self-harm routes to a pastor, a licensed counselor, or appropriate emergency services / professionals. **If this question is bound up with acute distress, a faith crisis you can't carry alone, despair, or thoughts of self-harm, please reach out to a trusted pastor, a licensed counselor, or emergency services now — this tool is for intellectual exploration only and is not a substitute for that support.**

**When to use:**
- You have a genuine intellectual question or nagging doubt and want to think it through honestly.
- You want the real range of responses, not a pat answer or a dismissal.
- You want to read the relevant passages yourself, by address.

**When NOT to use:**
- The doubt is bound up with acute distress, despair, or crisis — please contact a pastor, counselor, or emergency services (see the guardrail).
- You want a structured analysis of one hard passage — use `biblical_difficult_passage_analysis.md`.
- You want to map positions on a doctrine you're forming a view on — use `biblical_learner_doctrine_self_exploration.md`.

**Audience:** The self-directed individual learner (S).

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **The question/doubt.** Stated as honestly and specifically as you can.
2. **What's underneath it (optional).** Whether it's intellectual, moral, experiential, or a mix — to keep the response on the right footing.
3. **Passages or topics involved.** Any references; the model helps gather more by address.
4. **Translation.** The named translation you'll read in.
5. **What you've already heard.** Answers you've found unsatisfying, so the response doesn't just repeat them.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Take the question seriously and state it fairly and charitably before responding.
- Gather relevant passages by address for the learner to read; reason from the text.
- Map the range of responses across identifiable streams, including "this remains genuinely hard."
- Be honest (QA-04) about what is well-supported, what is interpretation, and what stays open.
- Keep the boundary guardrail visible and route acute distress out, repeatedly if needed.

### Must Not
- Dismiss, shame, or minimize the doubt; or imply that doubt is sinful or disqualifying.
- Manufacture false certainty or pretend a hard question has a tidy answer it doesn't.
- Quote Scripture from memory; invent citations, original-language data, scholar names, or apologetic "facts."
- Provide pastoral counseling, crisis intervention, or mental-health treatment.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** present responses across traditions descriptively; attribute each to identifiable streams; treat them as positions, not settled fact; name where the question stays genuinely open.
- **Must Not:** privilege/endorse one tradition's answer as the answer; present a contested response as the plain meaning; flatten disagreement into false consensus.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Restate the question charitably
Mirror the question back fairly and specifically, and (if shared) note whether it's intellectual, moral, or experiential. Surface the guardrail; if distress is present, route to a pastor/counselor before continuing.

### Step 2 — Gather the relevant text (by address)
Assemble the passages bearing on the question, by address, for the learner to read. Flag verify-required.

### Step 3 — Lay out the responses
Map how different streams have responded, each with its reasoning and the passages it leans on. Include the honest "still hard / unresolved" option as a legitimate response.

### Step 4 — Mark certainty levels
Separate what is well-supported, what is interpretation/inference, and what remains genuinely open (QA-04). Do not overclaim.

### Step 5 — Keep it the learner's question
Offer ways to keep exploring (reading, talking with a pastor or thoughtful community) rather than closing the question with a verdict.

### Step 6 — Re-check the boundary
Close by re-noting that intellectual exploration is not pastoral or crisis care, and where to go if the question is heavier than the intellect.

## Verification

- [ ] Question restated fairly before any response.
- [ ] Boundary guardrail visible; distress routed to pastor/counselor/emergency.
- [ ] Responses mapped across streams, including the honest "unresolved" option.
- [ ] Certainty levels separated; no manufactured certainty.
- [ ] Passages by address; no quoting from memory or invented apologetic facts.
- [ ] Doubt never shamed or treated as disqualifying.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Give a single tidy "answer" that papers over a genuinely hard question.
- Imply the learner shouldn't have the doubt.
- Try to counsel acute distress instead of routing it out.
- Invent a quotation, statistic, or scholar to settle the matter.

✅ **DO:**
- Restate the question charitably and check the guardrail first.
- Map the range of responses with their textual basis and the honest unresolved option.
- Separate well-supported from interpretation from genuinely open.
- Keep it the learner's question and route distress to real support.

---

## Techniques Used

- **ST-01 (Clear Objective Statement):** Opens with the single objective — explore a doubt intellectually and charitably without forcing resolution — setting the honest, non-dismissive tone.
- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** The numbered sequence (restate → gather → respond → certainty → keep open → re-check boundary) keeps the exploration disciplined and safe.
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework):** Examines the question across dimensions — its footing, the relevant text, and the range of stream responses — rather than a single rebuttal.
- **QA-04 (Uncertainty Acknowledgment):** Certainty levels are explicitly separated and the "genuinely open" response is treated as legitimate; false certainty is prohibited.
- **QA-05 (Citation Requirements):** Passages are given by address for the learner to read; quotations, statistics, and scholar attributions are never invented or recalled from memory.
