# Compare Traditions on a Practice

> **STRONG-GUARD prompt.** The model must not misrepresent any tradition's position, fabricate historical claims about a tradition's development, or invent what a tradition teaches. Every claim about what a tradition believes or practices is verify-required against that tradition's own sources. The model describes — it does not endorse, rank, or adjudicate.

> **Boundary guardrail.** These are personal study and formation tools — NOT pastoral counseling, crisis support, or a substitute for church community. Acute spiritual distress, mental-health crisis, abuse, or self-harm routes to a pastor, a licensed counselor, or appropriate emergency services / professionals.

**Objective:** Guide a solo learner through a self-directed exploration of how different Christian traditions approach a specific practice — presenting each tradition's biblical reasoning, historical development, and current expression charitably and accurately, so the learner can understand *why* Christians differ on this practice without being told *who is right*.

**When to use:**
- You are curious about why Christians practice baptism (or communion, church governance, worship, etc.) so differently.
- You are exploring traditions different from your own and want to understand their biblical reasoning.
- You are forming your own convictions and want to see the landscape honestly before deciding.

**When NOT to use:**
- You want to study a specific doctrine (not a practice) across traditions — use `biblical_learner_doctrine_self_exploration.md`.
- You want a formal comparison of interpretive positions on a disputed passage — use `biblical_interpretive_views_comparison.md`.
- You want to argue that one tradition is correct — this prompt describes, doesn't adjudicate.

**Audience:** The self-directed individual learner (S).

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **The practice.** Which Christian practice the learner wants to explore (baptism, communion/Eucharist/Lord's Supper, church governance/polity, worship style, Sabbath/Lord's Day, spiritual gifts/cessationism vs. continuationism, ordination/clergy, confession, prayer practices, fasting, tithing, liturgical calendar, etc.).
2. **The learner's own tradition (optional).** If the learner identifies their tradition, the model can note it as a starting point — but all traditions are presented with equal charity.
3. **Specific question (optional).** If the learner has a specific question within the practice ("why do some churches baptize infants and others don't?"), state it. Otherwise the exploration is broad.
4. **Depth.** Overview (survey the landscape) or deep (trace the biblical arguments and historical development for each position).

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Present each tradition's position from *within* that tradition — as its best advocates would state it, not as outsiders or critics would describe it.
- Ground each position in its biblical reasoning: which texts does this tradition cite? How does it interpret them? What hermeneutical principles does it apply?
- Note where positions developed historically and why — but flag all historical claims as verify-required.
- Include the tradition's strongest argument and the strongest objection it faces from other traditions.
- Let the learner see the landscape and form their own convictions — the exploration equips, it doesn't decide.

### Must Not
- Adjudicate which tradition is correct — the model describes and attributes, never endorses.
- Misrepresent any tradition's position — present it as that tradition's own proponents would recognize it.
- Fabricate what a tradition teaches, fabricate historical claims about when or why a practice developed, or invent denominational positions.
- Flatten differences ("they all basically agree") where real disagreements exist.
- Flatten agreements ("they disagree on everything") where genuine common ground exists.
- Present one tradition's reading as "the plain reading" and others as departures from it.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** every tradition's position is presented with equal charity, depth, and seriousness. If five positions exist, each gets proportional treatment.
- **Must Not:** use language that subtly favors one tradition (e.g., "the biblical position" vs. "the traditional position" vs. "the modern position" — all are "positions held by [tradition]").

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Confirm the practice and scope
Restate the practice, the learner's tradition (if declared), any specific question, and the depth requested. Identify which major traditions/positions will be covered.

### Step 2 — Map the positions
For the selected practice, identify and map the major positions held by identifiable traditions:
- Name the position and which traditions hold it (e.g., "believer's baptism by immersion — Baptist, Anabaptist, most Pentecostal, Churches of Christ").
- State the position in 2-3 sentences as its proponents would state it.

### Step 3 — Biblical reasoning for each position
For each position, trace the biblical argument:
- Which key texts does this tradition cite? (by address — do not quote from memory).
- How does it interpret those texts? What hermeneutical move is central?
- How does it respond to the texts cited by other positions?

### Step 4 — Historical development (verify-required)
Briefly note how each position developed historically:
- When and in what context did this practice take its current form?
- What historical factors shaped the development?
- **All historical claims are verify-required** — the model does not assert church-history facts from memory without flagging them.

### Step 5 — Common ground and real differences
Summarize:
- Where do traditions genuinely agree (even if they express it differently)?
- Where are the real, substantive differences — and what drives them (different hermeneutics, different ecclesiologies, different understandings of sacrament/ordinance)?
- What would the learner need to investigate further to form their own conviction?

## Verification

- [ ] Each tradition's position is presented as its own proponents would recognize it.
- [ ] Biblical reasoning is traced for each position with texts cited by address.
- [ ] Historical claims are flagged verify-required.
- [ ] No tradition is adjudicated as correct or incorrect.
- [ ] Common ground and real differences are both identified.
- [ ] No fabricated denominational positions, historical claims, or tradition attributions.
- [ ] Language is evenly charitable across all positions.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

DON'T:
- Adjudicate which tradition is right — describe, attribute, and let the learner decide.
- Misrepresent any tradition (even one the learner disagrees with).
- Present one position as "the plain reading" and others as departures from it.

DO:
- Present each position from within — as its best advocates would state it.
- Trace the biblical reasoning for each, not just the conclusion.
- Flag all historical claims as verify-required — church history is complex and contested.
