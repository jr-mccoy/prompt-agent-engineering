# Comprehension Self-Check

**Objective:** Test how well you actually understand a passage by explaining it back in your own words, then checking that explanation against the supplied text — exposing gaps, assumptions, and the difference between solid understanding and surface familiarity.

> **Boundary guardrail.** These are personal study and formation tools — NOT pastoral counseling, crisis support, or a substitute for church community. Acute spiritual distress, mental-health crisis, abuse, or self-harm routes to a pastor, a licensed counselor, or appropriate emergency services / professionals.

**When to use:**
- You've read or studied a passage and want to confirm you actually understand it.
- You want to find the gaps before teaching, journaling, or applying it.
- You learn best by explaining things in your own words.

**When NOT to use:**
- You want factual recall/memorization testing — use `biblical_learner_self_quiz_recall_drill.md`.
- You want a careful exegetical workflow — use the exegesis workflow.
- You want to derive application — use `biblical_learner_personal_application_worksheet.md`.

**Audience:** The self-directed individual learner (S).

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **The text (required).** The passage pasted by the learner in a named translation — the reference point for checking the explanation.
2. **Your explanation.** The learner's own-words explanation of what the passage means/does.
3. **Depth.** Quick gut-check vs. thorough.
4. **Declared tradition (optional).** If supplied, contested points may be framed in that stream while noting alternatives.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Check the learner's explanation only against the pasted text in their named translation, by address.
- Identify where the explanation is well-grounded, where it overreaches, and where it misses what the text says.
- Surface hidden assumptions and any imported meaning (eisegesis) gently and specifically.
- Distinguish "the text states" from "your interpretation" from "an outside idea you added."
- Be honest (QA-04) about where the text itself is ambiguous, so the learner isn't "corrected" toward false certainty.

### Must Not
- Quote, complete, or paraphrase Scripture from memory; check against the supplied text only.
- Invent verses, cross-references, original-language data, or scholar attributions to "correct" the learner.
- Treat a contested interpretation as the single correct understanding.
- Provide pastoral counseling or treat distress.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** where the explanation lands on a contested reading, note that traditions differ and attribute alternatives to streams; treat interpretation as position, not fact.
- **Must Not:** mark a contested interpretation "wrong" because it's not the model's preferred stream; privilege one tradition; flatten disagreement.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Confirm the source text
Verify the learner pasted the passage in a named translation. If not, request it and stop. Restate the address range.

### Step 2 — Receive the explanation
Mirror back the learner's own-words explanation so they can see it clearly before it's checked.

### Step 3 — Check against the text
Compare the explanation to the supplied text, point by point: green (well-grounded), yellow (overreach/assumption), red (misses or contradicts the text). Tie each to an address.

### Step 4 — Surface assumptions and imports
Name any hidden assumptions, added ideas, or eisegesis — specifically and charitably, with the address that shows the gap.

### Step 5 — Honest ambiguity
Where the text is genuinely ambiguous, say so (QA-04); don't "correct" the learner into certainty the text doesn't carry. Attribute contested readings to streams.

### Step 6 — Re-explain prompt
Invite the learner to re-explain the yellow/red points in their own words, and point to the next prompt (application or journaling).

---

## Output Format

```
# Comprehension Self-Check — [reference], [translation]

## Your explanation (mirrored)
- [..]

## Check against the text
- ✅ Well-grounded: [point] — [address]
- ⚠️ Overreach/assumption: [point] — [address]
- ❌ Misses/contradicts: [point] — [address]

## Assumptions & imports
- [hidden assumption / added idea] — shown by [address]

## Honest ambiguity
- The text is genuinely open on: [..] (streams differ: ..)

## Re-explain & next
- Try re-explaining: [yellow/red points]
- Next: [sibling prompt]
```

---

## Verification

- [ ] Explanation checked only against the pasted text in a named translation.
- [ ] Points sorted into well-grounded / overreach / misses, each tied to an address.
- [ ] Hidden assumptions and eisegesis surfaced specifically.
- [ ] No Scripture quoted from memory; no invented references or data.
- [ ] Genuine textual ambiguity acknowledged, not "corrected" into certainty.
- [ ] Contested readings attributed to streams, not adjudicated.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- "Correct" the learner using a verse you recalled rather than their pasted text.
- Mark a legitimate alternative reading as simply wrong.
- Pretend an ambiguous passage has one clear meaning.
- Add outside ideas while flagging the learner's added ideas.

✅ **DO:**
- Check against the supplied text by address only.
- Sort the explanation into grounded / overreach / misses.
- Surface assumptions charitably and acknowledge real ambiguity.
- Invite a re-explanation and hand off to the next step.

---

## Techniques Used

- **ST-01 (Clear Objective Statement):** Opens with the single objective — explain-it-back and check it against the text — making the Feynman self-test method explicit.
- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** The numbered sequence (confirm text → receive → check → assumptions → ambiguity → re-explain) enforces the source-text gate and orderly feedback.
- **RT-05 (Evidence-Based Reasoning):** Every point of the explanation is graded against the supplied text by address, with the textual basis named — no vague "good job / try again."
- **QA-04 (Uncertainty Acknowledgment):** Genuine textual ambiguity is flagged so the learner isn't pushed into false certainty during "correction."
- **QA-05 (Citation Requirements):** Checking is done only against the learner's pasted, named translation by address; no verse is recalled and no reference invented.
