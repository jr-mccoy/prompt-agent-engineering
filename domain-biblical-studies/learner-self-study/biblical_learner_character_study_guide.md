# Self-Guided Character Study

**Objective:** Study a biblical figure on your own by gathering the relevant passages by address, reading them in their narrative and historical context, and rigorously distinguishing what the text shows from what tradition, legend, or familiar retellings have added.

> **Boundary guardrail.** These are personal study and formation tools — NOT pastoral counseling, crisis support, or a substitute for church community. Acute spiritual distress, mental-health crisis, abuse, or self-harm routes to a pastor, a licensed counselor, or appropriate emergency services / professionals.

**When to use:**
- You want to understand a person in the Bible (e.g., Ruth, Peter, Deborah) for yourself.
- You suspect what you "know" about a figure mixes the text with sermon lore and want to test that.
- You're building a personal study around a life rather than a single passage.

**When NOT to use:**
- Your real question is how a single story is *told* — use `biblical_narrative_analysis.md`.
- You want a teaching outline for a group — that's a teaching prompt.
- You want to study a doctrine, not a person — use `biblical_learner_doctrine_self_exploration.md`.

**Audience:** The self-directed individual learner (S).

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **The figure.** Who you want to study and why.
2. **Passages you already know.** Any references you can name (the model helps you gather, by address, what else to read).
3. **Translation.** The named translation you'll read the passages in.
4. **Scope.** Whole-life overview vs. one episode or relationship.
5. **What you've heard.** Optional: claims about this person you want to test against the text.
6. **Declared tradition (optional).** If supplied, may foreground that stream's framing while noting alternatives.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Build the study from passages the learner reads by address, in their named translation.
- Read each passage in its narrative and historical context, not as isolated proof-texts.
- Sharply separate **text shows** (stated/depicted) from **inference** from **tradition/legend** (extra-biblical accretion), labeling each.
- Note where the text is silent — and resist filling silence with invented detail.
- Where character or motive is contested, attribute readings to identifiable streams without ruling.

### Must Not
- Quote Scripture from memory or assert verse wording; reference by address only.
- Invent passages, cross-references, dates, genealogies, original-language data, or scholar/tradition attributions.
- Present sermon embellishment or legend as if the text stated it.
- Psychologize the figure beyond what the text supports.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** present what the text depicts; where traditions read a figure differently (hero vs. flawed, etc.), attribute each reading to identifiable streams; treat character judgments as positions, not fact.
- **Must Not:** privilege/endorse one tradition's portrait as correct; present a contested characterization as the plain meaning; smooth disagreement into false consensus.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Define the figure and scope
Restate who is being studied, the scope (whole life vs. episode), and any claims to test. Name the translation.

### Step 2 — Gather the passages (by address)
Help the learner assemble the relevant references — primary appearances, key relationships, and parallels — all by address. Flag any reference as verify-required; route gathering help to the study-tool prompt.

### Step 3 — Read in context
For each passage the learner reads, note its setting (where in the story, what's happening) so the figure is seen in motion, not as a static label.

### Step 4 — Separate text from tradition
Build three labeled columns/lists: **the text shows**, **reasonable inference**, **tradition/legend (verify)**. Move familiar claims into the right column honestly.

### Step 5 — Trace development and tensions
Note how the figure changes, what tensions or ambiguities the text leaves open, and where the text is silent. Do not resolve silence with invention.

### Step 6 — Contested readings and confidence
Where the figure's character or motive is read differently, lay out the options attributed to streams. State your confidence on the central portrait and what would change it.

### Step 7 — Personal reflection handoff (optional)
If the learner wants application or journaling from the study, hand off to the application worksheet or journal companion rather than improvising.

## Verification

- [ ] Study built from passages read by address in a named translation.
- [ ] Each passage read in context, not as an isolated proof-text.
- [ ] "Text shows" / "inference" / "tradition-legend" kept distinct and labeled.
- [ ] No invented passages, dates, genealogies, original-language data, or attributions.
- [ ] Silences noted, not filled with invention.
- [ ] Contested characterizations attributed to streams, not adjudicated.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Repeat a familiar sermon detail as if the text stated it.
- Invent a backstory, date, or motive the text never gives.
- Flatten a complex figure into a single moral label.
- Quote verses from memory instead of citing addresses.

✅ **DO:**
- Gather and read the passages by address, in context.
- Sort claims into text-shows / inference / tradition-legend.
- Leave silences open and note ambiguity honestly.
- Attribute contested portraits to streams and state confidence.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-01 (Clear Objective Statement):** Opens with a single objective — study a figure by address and in context, separating text from tradition — fixing scope up front.
- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** The numbered sequence (define → gather → context → separate → trace → contested → handoff) keeps the study disciplined.
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework):** Examines the figure across dimensions — appearances, relationships, development, and tensions — rather than a single snapshot.
- **RT-05 (Evidence-Based Reasoning):** Every claim is sorted into text-shows, inference, or tradition-legend, with the textual basis named.
- **QA-05 (Citation Requirements):** All passages are referenced by address and read in a named translation; nothing is recalled from memory and no references are invented.
