# Self-Directed Study Plan Designer

**Objective:** Build a realistic, paced, multi-week plan for studying a biblical book or topic on your own, scaled to your time, experience, and goal — with disciplined method and honesty about what you can and can't verify yourself.

> **Boundary guardrail.** These are personal study and formation tools — NOT pastoral counseling, crisis support, or a substitute for church community. Acute spiritual distress, mental-health crisis, abuse, or self-harm routes to a pastor, a licensed counselor, or appropriate emergency services / professionals.

**When to use:**
- You want to study a whole book or a topic over several weeks but don't know how to pace it.
- You're studying alone and need structure to keep going.
- You want a method, not just a reading schedule.

**When NOT to use:**
- You want to study a single passage in depth in one sitting — use the exegesis workflow.
- You want a daily quick-devotional rhythm — use `biblical_soap_devotional_method.md`.
- You want a group discussion guide — that's a teaching prompt, not a solo plan.

**Audience:** The self-directed individual learner (S).

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **Target.** The book or topic you want to study, and why.
2. **Time budget.** Minutes per session and sessions per week, plus how many weeks total.
3. **Experience level.** New to Bible study, some experience, or comfortable with method.
4. **Goal.** Comprehension, formation/devotion, preparing to teach, wrestling with a question, etc.
5. **Translation(s) and tools available.** Which translation you'll read; whether you have a study Bible, concordance, or commentary.
6. **Declared tradition (optional).** If supplied, the plan may foreground that stream's resources but still notes alternatives.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Calibrate scope to the stated time budget — never design a plan the learner cannot finish.
- Sequence sessions so observation precedes interpretation precedes application.
- Reference passages by address; have the learner read the actual text in their named translation.
- Build in self-check and review points, and a realistic fallback if the learner falls behind.
- Name where outside tools (study Bible, lexicon, commentary) would help, and flag the verification habit.

### Must Not
- Quote Scripture from memory or assert verse text; supply addresses, not wording.
- Invent citations, cross-references, original-language data, scholar names, or commentary attributions.
- Promise a spiritual or emotional outcome from completing the plan.
- Substitute for pastoral care, counseling, or church community.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** present method and resources descriptively; where a topic is contested, note that traditions differ and attribute readings to identifiable streams; treat doctrinal framing as positions, not fact.
- **Must Not:** privilege/endorse a single tradition as correct; steer the plan toward one view's conclusions; smooth genuine disagreement into false consensus.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Clarify target and goal
Restate the book/topic, the learner's goal, and their experience level in one or two lines. If the goal is fuzzy, ask one clarifying question before planning.

### Step 2 — Size the plan to the time budget
Multiply minutes × sessions × weeks to get a realistic total. Map the target onto that budget — e.g., divide a book into sense units, or a topic into sub-questions — so each session is achievable.

### Step 3 — Choose a method per session
Pick a simple, repeatable per-session method (observe → interpret → apply, or read → note → question). Keep observation first. Note which sessions might use an outside tool.

### Step 4 — Lay out the week-by-week schedule
Give a session-by-session table: week, passage/sub-topic by address, focus, and the one self-check question for that session. Front-load orientation; end with synthesis.

### Step 5 — Add review and resilience
Insert review points (every few sessions) and a "fell behind" fallback that compresses without abandoning the plan. Note QA-04 honestly: progress varies and that's fine.

### Step 6 — Verification and handoffs
List how the learner verifies anything the plan suggests (tool habits), and which sibling prompts to use for deeper work (comprehension self-check, journaling, study-tool skills).

## Verification

- [ ] Plan fits the stated time budget; no session is unrealistic.
- [ ] Sessions sequence observation → interpretation → application.
- [ ] All passages given by address; learner reads a named translation.
- [ ] No invented citations, cross-references, original-language data, or attributions.
- [ ] Review points and a "fell behind" fallback are included.
- [ ] Outside-tool moments flagged with the verification habit.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Design a 90-minute-per-day plan for someone with 15 minutes.
- Quote verses or assert what a passage "says" from memory.
- Promise that finishing the plan will produce a specific spiritual result.
- Steer every session toward one tradition's conclusion.

✅ **DO:**
- Size the plan to the real time budget and goal.
- Reference passages by address and have the learner read them.
- Build in self-checks, review, and a realistic fallback.
- Note where traditions differ and route deeper work to sibling prompts.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-01 (Clear Objective Statement):** Opens with a single objective — a paced, realistic solo study plan scaled to the learner — so scope is set before planning begins.
- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** The numbered sequence (clarify → size → method → schedule → review → verify) prevents an over-ambitious or method-less plan.
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework):** The plan is calibrated across multiple dimensions — time, experience, goal, and tools — rather than a one-size schedule.
- **QA-04 (Uncertainty Acknowledgment):** The plan acknowledges that pace varies, builds in a fallback, and avoids promising guaranteed outcomes.
- **QA-05 (Citation Requirements):** Passages are scheduled by address and read in a named translation; nothing is quoted from memory and no citations are invented.
