# Audio & Podcast Learning for Bible Study

**Objective:** Make listening — to an audio Bible and to podcasts — an active, verifiable part of Bible study that feeds the learner's notes and supports reading the text rather than replacing it.

> **STRONG-GUARD prompt — product and podcast claims.** This prompt **never** states that a named audio-Bible app, podcast, host, or episode has particular content, a translation, a theological stance, credentials, a sourcing practice, a price, or availability. Every judgement about a specific show is **[VERIFY: check the show's current episode list and stated "about" information]** and rests only on what the learner reports or on episodes the learner has actually listened to, marked **(user-reported)**. Curation criteria are given in general terms; the learner applies them.

> **Anti-fabrication.** Verses appear by address only; the model never quotes Scripture, summarises an episode it has not been given, or attributes a view to a host. AI chat tools — including this one — can invent episode content, quotations, and sources; anything an AI says about a show or a verse is **[AI — unverified]** until checked against the episode itself or a real translation.

> **Boundary guardrail.** These are personal study and formation tools — NOT pastoral counseling, crisis support, or a substitute for church community. Acute spiritual distress, mental-health crisis, abuse, or self-harm routes to a pastor, a licensed counselor, or appropriate emergency services / professionals.

**When to use:**
- You commute, exercise, or work with your hands and want that time to support Bible study.
- You listen to Bible podcasts and want a way to judge them and keep what you learn.
- Reading is hard for you (sight, dyslexia, fatigue, preference) and you want audio to be a full route into study.

**When NOT to use:**
- You want a daily reading habit with pacing and gap recovery — use `domain-biblical-studies/learner-self-study/biblical_learner_bible_reading_habit_builder.md`. This prompt is distinct: it adds listening and podcast learning alongside reading.
- You want to memorise specific verses with spaced review — use `domain-biblical-studies/study-methods-teaching/biblical_memorization_retention_plan.md`; audio re-listening here supports familiarity, not verse-accurate memorisation.
- You are designing discipleship for oral-preference learners or communities — use `domain-discipleship/cross-cultural/discipleship_oral_preference_learners.md`. This prompt serves an individual learner's own study.
- You need a system for storing what you capture — use `domain-biblical-studies/learner-self-study/biblical_learner_pkm_bible_study_notes.md`.

**Audience:** Self-directed learners (S) and laypeople doing personal study (L). Beginner.

## Inputs / Context

1. **Listening windows.** When and for how long you can listen (commute, walk, chores), and whether you can pause or take notes then.
2. **Reading time.** How much time you have for reading the text itself.
3. **Goal.** Hear whole books, get familiar with a book before studying it, learn background, keep up with a podcast, or study primarily by ear.
4. **Audio Bible.** The translation and source you use, as you report them (user-reported).
5. **Podcasts.** Shows you listen to and what you have observed about each: host's stated background and tradition, how they handle sources, format (user-reported).
6. **Accessibility needs (optional).** Anything that shapes how you take in or record information; the prompt does not assess or diagnose.
7. **Note system.** Where insights should go (any app, a notebook, voice memos).
8. **Declared tradition (optional).** May foreground familiar shows; curation criteria stay the same.

## Constraints

### Must
- State the listening plan's objective in one line and tie each audio activity to a reading or note step.
- Build listening skills in stages: passive whole-book listening → listen-then-observe → pause-and-recall → one question per session (ED-01).
- Give podcast curation criteria in general terms and apply them only to the learner's reports or to episodes they supply, each conclusion marked [VERIFY] and (user-reported).
- Include a capture routine that sends each kept insight to notes with a provenance tag: [TEXT: audio, translation as user-reported], [PODCAST: show/episode/timestamp — user-reported], [MY OBSERVATION], [AI — unverified].
- Include a **balance check**: for each week, audio supplements but does not replace looking at the text, unless the learner relies on audio for access, in which case re-listening and an accessible text format serve as the check.
- Hedge any learning-science point (e.g., recall practice tends to help retention) generally; cite no studies.

### Must Not
- Assert what a named podcast, host, episode, or audio-Bible app contains, believes, costs, or offers.
- Quote Scripture or summarise an episode from memory; verses by address only.
- Treat a podcast claim as verified because the host sounds confident or credentialed.
- Rank traditions or label a show "safe" or "dangerous" by its conclusions.
- Assert a translation's licensing terms for downloading or sharing audio — **[VERIFY the translation's permissions statement]**.
- Infer anything about a learner's literacy, sight, or cognition.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** treat a host's stated tradition as information to identify, not a verdict; encourage at least one source from another stream on contested topics; judge shows on sourcing and attribution, not conclusions.
- **Must Not:** describe any tradition's podcasts as the neutral default, or advise dropping a show because of its tradition.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Objective and windows
Write one objective sentence. Map listening windows and reading time; flag if audio is being asked to carry study that needs the text open.

### Step 2 — Audio-Bible plan
Choose from: whole-book listening (a short book in one sitting), re-listening (same book several times over a week or two), and pairing (listen to a section, then read it, or read then listen). Set the pairing per week.

### Step 3 — Active-listening ladder
Introduce one technique at a time across weeks: listen-then-observe (after listening, write what you noticed); pause-and-recall (stop after a section and retell it); one question per session (carry one question to the text). Note when listening cannot include pausing (driving) and move recall to afterward.

### Step 4 — Podcast curation
For each show the learner names, apply the criteria: host's stated training and tradition; whether claims are sourced (texts by address, named works); whether views are attributed to streams or asserted as fact; episode format (teaching, interview, devotional, debate); whether corrections are made. Record the learner's evidence and mark the overall judgement [VERIFY] (QA-04 where evidence is thin).

### Step 5 — Capture routine
After each session: one to three insights, each with its provenance tag and, for podcasts, episode and timestamp as the learner records them. Claims a host makes about the text become questions to check, not notes of fact.

### Step 6 — Balance check and accessibility
Weekly: minutes listened vs minutes with the text; did any podcast claim get checked against the text? For accessibility uses, adapt (audio-first with re-listening, voice-memo capture, larger checks less often) without lowering the verification standard.

## Verification

- [ ] Objective stated in one sentence; every audio activity links to reading or notes.
- [ ] Listening techniques introduced in stages, not all at once.
- [ ] No named podcast, host, episode, or app credited with content, stance, credentials, price, or availability.
- [ ] Every judgement about a show is [VERIFY] and based on learner reports or supplied episodes.
- [ ] Captured insights carry provenance tags; host claims become questions.
- [ ] Balance check present; audio does not silently replace the text.
- [ ] No Scripture quoted; no invented learning statistics or study citations.
- [ ] Traditions identified, not ranked.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Say "Podcast X is a trustworthy evangelical/Catholic/academic show" — you have not heard it, and labels change.
- Summarise what an episode "teaches" from its title.
- Write a host's claim about a Greek word straight into notes as a fact.
- Count a month of listening as a month of studying the text.

✅ **DO:**
- Ask the learner what they heard, and apply the criteria to that evidence.
- Turn "the host said v. 12 means…" into "check v. 12 in my translation and a lexicon".
- Pair each listened book with at least one reading or re-listening pass where the learner looks for something specific.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-01 (Clear Objective Statement):** Each plan opens with a one-sentence objective that ties listening to reading and notes.
- **ED-01 (Iterative Scaffolding):** Active-listening techniques are introduced one per stage, from passive listening to carrying a question to the text.
- **QA-04 (Uncertainty Acknowledgment):** Show judgements are provisional, marked [VERIFY], and weighted by how much the learner has actually heard.
- **DP-04 (Must-Not Constraints):** Forbids claims about named shows, hosts, and apps, episode summaries from memory, and quoted Scripture.
- **RT-23 (Input Provenance Tagging):** Captured insights carry tags separating audio text, podcast claims with timestamp, the learner's observations, and AI output.
