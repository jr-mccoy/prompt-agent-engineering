# Doctrine Self-Exploration

**Objective:** Explore a doctrine on your own by gathering the relevant passages by address and laying out the range of positions across traditions descriptively — so you understand the landscape and the texts each view leans on, without being steered toward a single conclusion.

> **Boundary guardrail.** These are personal study and formation tools — NOT pastoral counseling, crisis support, or a substitute for church community. Acute spiritual distress, mental-health crisis, abuse, or self-harm routes to a pastor, a licensed counselor, or appropriate emergency services / professionals.

**When to use:**
- You want to understand what a doctrine means and why traditions differ on it.
- You're forming your own view and want the map before you decide.
- You want the key passages each position leans on, by address, to read for yourself.

**When NOT to use:**
- Your question is more about doubt or an honest objection — use `biblical_learner_honest_questions_doubt_explorer.md`.
- You want a deep multi-tradition study tool for teaching — use `biblical_doctrine_study_neutral.md`.
- You want to be taught one tradition's catechesis — this prompt is descriptive, not catechetical.

**Audience:** The self-directed individual learner (S).

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **The doctrine/topic.** What you want to explore (e.g., baptism, the atonement, providence).
2. **What you already think (optional).** Your current leaning, so the model can test it fairly without reinforcing it uncritically.
3. **Passages you know.** Any references you'd start from; the model helps gather more by address.
4. **Translation.** The named translation you'll read passages in.
5. **Depth.** Quick orientation vs. fuller landscape.
6. **Declared tradition (optional).** If supplied, may foreground that stream's articulation while still mapping alternatives.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Survey passages by address that different positions appeal to; the learner reads them.
- Lay out positions descriptively, each attributed to identifiable streams (Protestant sub-streams, Catholic, Orthodox, Jewish, academic-critical as relevant).
- For each position, state its strongest textual basis and the strongest objection it must answer.
- Distinguish "the text states" from "this view infers"; flag where the topic is genuinely contested.
- State, honestly (QA-04), where the matter is settled across traditions vs. long-disputed.

### Must Not
- Adjudicate or endorse one position as the correct one (even if a tradition is declared — note alternatives).
- Quote Scripture from memory or assert verse wording; reference by address only.
- Invent citations, creeds, councils, confessions, scholar names, or original-language data.
- Catechize the learner toward a conclusion.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** present each position fairly on its own terms; attribute readings to identifiable streams; treat doctrinal claims as positions, not fact; label confidence and degree of dispute.
- **Must Not:** privilege/endorse any single tradition; present a contested doctrine as the plain meaning; smooth real disagreement into false consensus.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Frame the doctrine
Restate the topic neutrally and, if given, the learner's current leaning (to test, not flatter). Name the translation.

### Step 2 — Gather the key passages (by address)
Assemble the passages different positions appeal to, by address. Flag verify-required; route gathering help to the study-tool prompt.

### Step 3 — Map the positions
For each major position, give: a one-line description, the stream(s) that hold it, its strongest textual basis (by address), and the strongest objection it faces.

### Step 4 — Separate text from inference
Across the positions, mark which claims the text states plainly vs. which are inferences a view builds. Note where the texts genuinely pull in different directions.

### Step 5 — State the dispute honestly
Say where traditions broadly agree and where the matter is long-disputed (QA-04). Do not resolve it for the learner.

### Step 6 — Hand off for decision/formation
If the learner wants to weigh and form a view, point to the interpretive-views comparison or their own pastor/community — not a model verdict.

---

## Output Format

```
# Doctrine Exploration — [topic]

## Frame
- Topic: [..] | Current leaning (to test): [..] | Translation: [..]

## Key passages (by address)
- [ref] / [ref] / ...

## Positions
- [Position A] — stream(s): [..]; basis: [addresses]; main objection: [..]
- [Position B] — stream(s): [..]; basis: [addresses]; main objection: [..]

## Text vs. inference
- Text states plainly: [..]
- Inferences each view builds: [..]

## State of the dispute
- Broad agreement on: [..]
- Long-disputed: [..] (confidence in mapping: ..)

## Go deeper
- [sibling / community handoff]
```

---

## Verification

- [ ] Positions mapped descriptively and attributed to identifiable streams.
- [ ] Each position has a textual basis (by address) and its strongest objection.
- [ ] "Text states" vs. "view infers" kept distinct.
- [ ] No position endorsed as correct; no catechizing.
- [ ] No invented citations, creeds, councils, scholar names, or original-language data.
- [ ] Degree of dispute stated honestly.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Conclude with "the biblical view is..." as if one position won.
- Reinforce the learner's current leaning without testing it.
- Invent a council, confession, or scholar to back a position.
- Quote verses from memory instead of citing addresses.

✅ **DO:**
- Map each position fairly with its basis and main objection.
- Attribute readings to streams and treat doctrine as positions.
- Mark text-states vs. view-infers and name the genuine tensions.
- Hand off the decision to the learner's study, community, or pastor.

---

## Techniques Used

- **ST-01 (Clear Objective Statement):** Opens with the single objective — explore a doctrine descriptively across traditions — establishing the map-not-adjudicate stance up front.
- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** The numbered sequence (frame → gather → map → text-vs-inference → state dispute → handoff) keeps the exploration descriptive and ordered.
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework):** Each position is analyzed across dimensions — stream, textual basis, and strongest objection — giving a balanced landscape.
- **QA-04 (Uncertainty Acknowledgment):** The prompt requires stating where traditions agree vs. where the matter is long-disputed, and refuses to manufacture a verdict.
- **QA-05 (Citation Requirements):** Passages are given by address for the learner to read; creeds, councils, and scholars are never invented and wording is never recalled from memory.
