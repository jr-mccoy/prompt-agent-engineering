# Personal Application Worksheet

**Objective:** Derive honest, text-grounded personal application by moving deliberately from the text, to the point the original author/audience were dealing with, to a timeless principle, to a concrete step today — without reading yourself into the text or reducing it to moralism.

> **Boundary guardrail.** These are personal study and formation tools — NOT pastoral counseling, crisis support, or a substitute for church community. Acute spiritual distress, mental-health crisis, abuse, or self-harm routes to a pastor, a licensed counselor, or appropriate emergency services / professionals.

**When to use:**
- You've understood a passage and want to apply it to your life honestly.
- You tend to jump to "what it means to me" and want a disciplined bridge instead.
- You want application that respects the text's genre and original point.

**When NOT to use:**
- You haven't confirmed you understand the passage — use `biblical_learner_comprehension_self_check.md` first.
- You want a teaching/preaching application bridge — use `biblical_application_bridge_builder.md`.
- You want open-ended reflection rather than action steps — use `biblical_learner_reflection_journal_companion.md`.

**Audience:** The self-directed individual learner (S).

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **The text (required).** The passage pasted in a named translation — the basis for the bridge.
2. **Genre.** What kind of text it is (narrative, law, poetry, epistle, etc.), since application differs by genre.
3. **Your situation (optional).** Relevant context so application is concrete, not generic.
4. **Declared tradition (optional).** If supplied, contested points may be framed in that stream while noting alternatives.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Build application on the bridge: text → original point → timeless principle → today, in that order.
- Anchor the original point in the passage by address (and its context), not in the learner's situation.
- Derive the principle from the text's actual point, respecting genre (don't turn a narrative into a command).
- Keep application honest and specific while flagging where it's the learner's inference, not the text's command.
- Be honest (QA-04) when a passage resists direct application or when application is genuinely a matter of wisdom.

### Must Not
- Skip to "today" without establishing the original point and principle (eisegesis).
- Reduce the passage to a moral lesson or behavior checklist when its point is larger.
- Quote Scripture from memory; invent cross-references, original-language data, or attributions.
- Manufacture guilt, prescribe specific life decisions, or act as a counselor.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** where the principle or application is read differently across traditions, note it and attribute to streams; treat application claims as the learner's responsible inference, not divine command.
- **Must Not:** present one tradition's application as the required obedience; flatten disagreement; bind the conscience.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Confirm text and genre
Verify the passage is pasted in a named translation; identify the genre, since it shapes application. If no text, request it and stop.

### Step 2 — Establish the original point
State, by address and in context, what the author/audience were actually dealing with — the point in their setting. Tag claims text-supported vs. inference.

### Step 3 — Distill the timeless principle
Move from the original point to a principle that holds across time, respecting genre. Keep it tied to the text's actual point, not a generic platitude.

### Step 4 — Bridge to today
Translate the principle into the learner's world, producing concrete but honest application. Mark which steps are the text's clear implication vs. the learner's wise inference.

### Step 5 — Guard the bridge
Check for eisegesis, moralizing, and over-binding. Note where application is genuinely a matter of freedom/wisdom (QA-04), not command.

### Step 6 — Hand off to reflection
Point the learner to journaling or comprehension self-check if the bridge revealed gaps.

## Verification

- [ ] Application follows text → original point → principle → today, in order.
- [ ] Original point anchored in the passage by address and context.
- [ ] Principle respects genre; not reduced to a behavior checklist.
- [ ] Steps marked as text's implication vs. learner's wise inference.
- [ ] No Scripture from memory; no invented references or data.
- [ ] Freedom/wisdom acknowledged; conscience not over-bound.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Jump straight to "what this means for me" before the original point.
- Turn a narrative or poem into a list of commands.
- Manufacture guilt or prescribe a specific major life decision.
- Quote verses from memory to support the application.

✅ **DO:**
- Walk the full bridge and anchor the original point by address.
- Distill a genre-respecting principle, then apply concretely.
- Mark clear implications vs. wise inferences and note where it's open.
- Hand off to reflection or comprehension check when gaps appear.

---

## Techniques Used

- **ST-01 (Clear Objective Statement):** Opens with the single objective — honest application via a disciplined bridge — naming the anti-eisegesis aim up front.
- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** The numbered sequence (confirm text → original point → principle → today → guard → handoff) prevents jumping to application before the text's point is established.
- **RT-05 (Evidence-Based Reasoning):** The original point and principle are anchored in the text by address, and each application step is tagged as the text's implication or the learner's inference.
- **QA-04 (Uncertainty Acknowledgment):** The prompt requires noting where application is genuinely a matter of freedom/wisdom rather than command, avoiding false prescription.
- **QA-05 (Citation Requirements):** The bridge works from the learner's pasted, named translation by address; no verse is recalled from memory and no cross-references or data are invented.
