# Teacher & Volunteer Training Plan

**Objective:** Design a training program that equips volunteer Bible-study teachers with the minimum viable skills they need: how to read a passage responsibly (basic hermeneutics), how to teach it to their age group, how to handle questions they can't answer, and how to maintain a safe environment — all in a format that respects volunteers' limited time.

**When to use:**
- You are launching a new teaching ministry and need to train leaders from scratch.
- You are onboarding new volunteers into an existing program.
- You want to raise the quality of teaching across your volunteer team.

**When NOT to use:**
- You are building a single lesson — use `biblical_lesson_plan_builder.md`.
- You are designing volunteer role descriptions — use `biblical_churchstaff_volunteer_recruitment_role_design.md`.
- You need seminary-level hermeneutics — use `biblical_passage_exegesis_workflow.md`.

**Audience:** Pastors (P) and education directors/group leaders (G) who train others.

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **Ministry area.** Which volunteers this training serves (children, youth, adult small group, etc.).
2. **Current skill level.** What the volunteers already know about Bible study and teaching.
3. **Time budget.** How many sessions, how long each, and over what timeframe.
4. **Format.** In-person, online, or hybrid; lecture, workshop, or apprenticeship.
5. **Non-negotiables.** Any specific topics (e.g., child-safety policy, denominational distinctives) that must be included.
6. **Declared tradition (optional).** May shape hermeneutical emphases.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Include basic hermeneutics (observation, context, genre awareness) at a level appropriate for volunteers.
- Cover age-appropriate teaching methods for the target ministry area.
- Address handling hard questions, controversial topics, and "I don't know" moments.
- Include child-safety / vulnerable-population protocols if the ministry involves minors.
- Build in practice activities (not just lecture) so volunteers apply skills during training.
- Respect volunteers' limited time — keep sessions focused and achievable.

### Must Not
- Assume seminary-level knowledge or vocabulary.
- Fabricate statistics about volunteer effectiveness, teaching research, or program outcomes.
- Skip child-safety if the ministry involves children or youth.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** teach hermeneutics as method, not as a vehicle for the trainer's doctrinal conclusions.
- **Must Not:** embed a contested doctrinal position into the training as "what the Bible clearly says."

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Confirm scope and constraints
Restate the ministry area, skill level, time budget, format, and non-negotiables. Flag if the time budget is insufficient for the required topics and propose a minimum.

### Step 2 — Design the session sequence
Map sessions to topics. Each session includes:
- Objective (what volunteers will be able to do after this session)
- Content (what is taught)
- Practice activity (how volunteers apply it during the session)
- Take-home (what to do before the next session)

### Step 3 — Hermeneutics core
Design the hermeneutics sessions: observation, context, genre, and application — at volunteer level, with a real passage as the worked example.

### Step 4 — Teaching methods
Design sessions on age-appropriate teaching: how to explain, ask questions, manage time, and engage the specific age group.

### Step 5 — Hard questions and safety
Design sessions on handling questions you can't answer, controversial moments, and (if minors are involved) child-safety protocols.

### Step 6 — Ongoing development
Recommend a lightweight ongoing development plan (observation, feedback, refresher cadence) after initial training concludes.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

DON'T:
- Use seminary vocabulary without defining it.
- Skip child-safety for children's/youth ministries.
- Design training that is all lecture with no practice.

DO:
- Teach hermeneutics as a method, not as a doctrinal delivery system.
- Include real-passage worked examples at the volunteers' level.
- Build in ongoing development, not just initial training.
