# Volunteer Recruitment & Role Design

**Objective:** Produce role descriptions, qualification criteria, time-commitment estimates, onboarding checklists, and recruitment messaging for volunteer positions in a Bible-teaching ministry — so the ministry leader can recruit clearly and volunteers know exactly what they are signing up for.

**When to use:**
- You are launching or expanding a teaching ministry and need to define volunteer roles.
- You want to clarify expectations for existing volunteers.
- You need recruitment language for announcements, sign-ups, or personal invitations.

**When NOT to use:**
- You are training volunteers who are already recruited — use `biblical_churchstaff_teacher_training_plan.md`.
- You are designing the curriculum the volunteers will teach — use `biblical_churchstaff_curriculum_scope_sequence.md`.

**Audience:** Pastors (P) and education directors/group leaders (G).

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **Ministry area.** Which ministry these roles serve (children, youth, adult small groups, etc.).
2. **Roles needed.** How many distinct roles and what they do (lead teacher, assistant, greeter, tech/AV, etc.).
3. **Time commitment.** How often volunteers serve (weekly, biweekly, rotating) and for how long per session.
4. **Qualification expectations.** Any requirements (membership, background check, doctrinal agreement, training completion).
5. **Recruitment channel.** How roles will be advertised (bulletin, stage announcement, personal ask, sign-up form).
6. **Declared tradition (optional).** May shape doctrinal-agreement or membership requirements.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Produce a clear role description for each position: title, purpose, responsibilities, time commitment, term length, and who they report to.
- Define qualification criteria honestly (don't set the bar so high nobody qualifies or so low nobody is screened).
- Include child-safety requirements (background checks, two-adult policies) if the ministry involves minors.
- Provide onboarding steps: what happens between "yes" and first day of service.
- Draft recruitment messaging the user can adapt for their context.

### Must Not
- Fabricate volunteer-retention statistics, recruitment best-practice studies, or church benchmarks.
- Design roles that require unsustainable time commitments without flagging the risk.
- Skip child-safety screening for children's or youth ministries.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** note where doctrinal-agreement requirements reflect a tradition-specific standard.
- **Must Not:** impose a doctrinal-agreement requirement the user hasn't specified.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Confirm ministry context and roles
Restate the ministry area, roles needed, time expectations, and qualification standards. Flag if any role has an unrealistic time commitment.

### Step 2 — Write role descriptions
For each role, produce: title, purpose statement, key responsibilities (3–6 bullets), time commitment, term length, reporting line, and qualifications.

### Step 3 — Onboarding checklist
Design the steps from "yes" to first day: application, background check (if applicable), orientation, training, shadowing, and first solo session.

### Step 4 — Recruitment messaging
Draft 2–3 recruitment messages the user can adapt: a short announcement (bulletin/slide), a personal-invitation script, and a sign-up form description.

### Step 5 — Sustainability check
Flag any role that risks burnout (weekly commitment with no rotation, no term limit, unclear reporting). Suggest adjustments.

## Output Format

```
# Volunteer Roles — [ministry area]

## Role descriptions
### [Role title]
- Purpose: [..] | Reports to: [..]
- Responsibilities: [3–6 bullets]
- Time: [frequency, duration, term] | Qualifications: [..]

## Onboarding checklist
1. [step] → 2. [step] → ... → First day

## Recruitment messaging
- Bulletin/slide: [short version]
- Personal invitation: [conversational script]
- Sign-up form: [description + expectations]

## Sustainability check
- [flags and adjustments]
```

---

## Verification

- [ ] Every role has a clear description, time commitment, and qualifications.
- [ ] Child-safety requirements are included for ministries involving minors.
- [ ] Onboarding steps bridge the gap between recruitment and first day.
- [ ] Recruitment messaging is ready to adapt.
- [ ] No fabricated statistics or best-practice claims.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

DON'T:
- Set qualification bars without the user's input on expectations.
- Skip background-check requirements for children's/youth roles.
- Design roles with unsustainable commitments and no rotation.

DO:
- Make time commitments explicit and honest.
- Include onboarding — recruitment without onboarding creates confusion.
- Flag burnout risks and suggest term limits or rotation.
