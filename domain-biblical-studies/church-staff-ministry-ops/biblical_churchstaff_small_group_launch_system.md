# Small-Group Launch System

**Objective:** Produce a system design for launching (or relaunching) a small-group ministry — addressing how groups form, who leads them, what they study, how often they meet, how the church monitors health, and how new people get connected — so the ministry has structure without becoming bureaucratic.

**When to use:**
- You are launching a small-group ministry from scratch.
- You are restructuring an existing group system that has drifted or stalled.
- You want a documented system to hand to a groups pastor or coordinator.

**When NOT to use:**
- You are writing discussion questions for one group session — use `biblical_smallgroup_discussion_guide.md`.
- You are designing a discipleship pathway that includes but goes beyond small groups — use `biblical_churchstaff_discipleship_pathway_design.md`.

**Audience:** Pastors (P) and group leaders/coordinators (G).

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **Church size and context.** Approximate attendance, number of existing groups (if any), and campus/site structure.
2. **Group philosophy.** Open vs. closed groups, semester vs. ongoing, affinity-based vs. geographic vs. mixed.
3. **Leader pool.** How many leaders are available or need to be recruited and trained.
4. **Curriculum approach.** Church-directed (everyone studies the same thing) vs. leader-choice vs. hybrid.
5. **Meeting rhythm.** Weekly, biweekly, or flexible; on-campus vs. off-campus vs. online.
6. **Goals.** What the church wants small groups to accomplish (Bible study, community, care, outreach, or a mix).
7. **Declared tradition (optional).** May shape group expectations (e.g., covenant membership, elder oversight).

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Address all six system components: formation, leadership, curriculum, rhythm, reporting/care, and connection (how new people join).
- Produce a launch timeline with milestones from decision to first meetings.
- Design a leader-support structure (coaching, check-ins, or huddles) — not just leader recruitment.
- Include a plan for groups that are unhealthy (leader burnout, cliques, stagnation, doctrinal drift).
- Note where the system touches contested ecclesiological ground (elder authority, membership, etc.) and handle neutrally.

### Must Not
- Fabricate small-group research statistics, church-growth data, or benchmarks.
- Design a system so complex that it requires full-time staff to maintain in a context that doesn't have it.
- Assume every church uses the same group philosophy (open/closed, semester/ongoing, etc.).

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** note where group-system design reflects ecclesiological commitments (membership, elder oversight, accountability).
- **Must Not:** embed one tradition's polity as the default model.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Confirm context and philosophy
Restate church size, existing groups, philosophy, leader pool, curriculum approach, rhythm, and goals. Flag if the goals and philosophy conflict (e.g., "community and outreach" goals with closed, ongoing groups).

### Step 2 — Design the six components
For each component, provide a concise design:
1. **Formation:** How groups are created (sign-up, placement, organic, or hybrid).
2. **Leadership:** Qualifications, recruitment, training, and ongoing support.
3. **Curriculum:** What groups study, who chooses, and how it cycles.
4. **Rhythm:** Meeting frequency, semester/year structure, and breaks.
5. **Reporting & care:** How the church monitors group health and provides pastoral care through groups.
6. **Connection:** How new people find and join a group.

### Step 3 — Build the launch timeline
Map milestones from "we've decided to do this" to "groups are meeting": leader recruitment, training, curriculum selection, promotion, sign-up, group formation, and first meetings.

### Step 4 — Unhealthy-group plan
Define what "unhealthy" looks like (3–4 indicators) and what the response is (coaching conversation, leader change, group restructuring, or closure).

### Step 5 — Sustainability check
Flag components that require more staff or leader capacity than the church currently has. Suggest a phased approach if needed.

- [ ] All six system components are addressed.
- [ ] A launch timeline with milestones is included.
- [ ] Leader support (not just recruitment) is designed.

- [ ] No fabricated statistics or church-growth claims.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

DON'T:
- Design a system that only works with full-time groups staff.
- Fabricate small-group research or growth statistics.
- Assume one group philosophy fits every church.

DO:
- Address all six components, even briefly.
- Include leader support and unhealthy-group response.
- Flag sustainability risks and suggest phased rollout.
