# Sermon Feedback & Debrief

**Objective:** Produce a structured debrief of a sermon or teaching session — assessing what was said (theological accuracy, textual faithfulness), how it was said (clarity, pacing, engagement), and what it called people to do (application) — so the communicator gets specific, actionable feedback rather than "great job" or vague criticism.

**When to use:**
- After a sermon or teaching session, the communicator (or a coach) wants structured feedback.
- A teaching team wants a regular debrief cadence to improve together.
- A less-experienced preacher is being mentored and needs specific growth areas.

**When NOT to use:**
- You are preparing a sermon (before delivery) — use `biblical_expository_sermon_prep.md`.
- You are coordinating multiple communicators for the same message — use `biblical_churchstaff_multi_service_coordination.md`.

**Audience:** Pastors (P) — both the preacher receiving feedback and the coach giving it.

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **The message.** Passage, title, big idea, and series context.
2. **The communicator.** Who preached, their experience level, and any specific growth goals.
3. **What happened.** The user describes or provides notes on the sermon — what was said, how it went, and any moments that stood out (positively or negatively).
4. **Feedback source.** Self-assessment, peer feedback, coach observation, or congregation response.
5. **Debrief goals.** What the communicator most wants feedback on (theology, delivery, application, time management, etc.).

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Evaluate across at least three dimensions: textual/theological faithfulness, delivery/communication, and application/response.
- Provide specific, actionable notes — not just "good" or "needs work" but what specifically to keep, change, or try next time.
- Separate observation (what happened) from evaluation (whether it worked) from recommendation (what to try next).
- Handle theological concerns with care: if the message mishandled a text, note it directly but constructively.
- Note what went well — debrief is not only about problems.

### Must Not
- Fabricate audience-response data, communication research, or homiletics statistics.
- Evaluate the communicator's character or calling — this is about the message and its delivery.
- Pre-decide a contested interpretive question as "the right reading" against which the sermon is measured.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** evaluate textual faithfulness by whether the sermon represented the passage honestly, not by whether it matched a specific tradition's reading.
- **Must Not:** grade the sermon against one tradition's homiletical standards as though they were universal.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Confirm the message and debrief goals
Restate the passage, big idea, communicator, and what specific feedback is sought.

### Step 2 — Assess textual/theological faithfulness
Did the sermon represent the passage accurately? Was the big idea grounded in the text? Were any claims made that the text doesn't support? Were contested interpretive issues handled honestly?

### Step 3 — Assess delivery and communication
Clarity of structure, pacing, use of illustrations, transitions, opening and closing, energy, and audience engagement. Note specific moments that worked or didn't.

### Step 4 — Assess application and response
Was the application concrete and actionable? Did it flow from the text (not just tacked on)? Was the response invitation clear?

### Step 5 — Produce the debrief summary
Organize findings into: what to keep doing, what to adjust, and what to try next time. Prioritize 2–3 actionable items rather than an exhaustive list.

---

## Output Format

```
# Sermon Debrief — [title / passage]

## Message summary
- Passage: [..] | Big idea: [..] | Communicator: [..]

## Textual/theological faithfulness
- Strengths: [..] | Concerns: [..] | Contested areas handled: [..]

## Delivery & communication
- Strengths: [..] | Adjust: [..] | Specific moments: [..]

## Application & response
- Strengths: [..] | Adjust: [..]

## Action items (prioritized)
1. Keep: [..]
2. Adjust: [..]
3. Try next time: [..]
```

---

## Verification

- [ ] Three dimensions are assessed (theology, delivery, application).
- [ ] Feedback is specific and actionable, not generic.
- [ ] What went well is noted alongside what to improve.
- [ ] Observation, evaluation, and recommendation are separated.
- [ ] No fabricated audience data or homiletics research.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

DON'T:
- Evaluate the communicator's calling or character — evaluate the message.
- Give only criticism with no affirmation.
- Grade the sermon against one tradition's homiletical style.

DO:
- Separate observation from evaluation from recommendation.
- Prioritize 2–3 actionable items over an exhaustive critique.
- Handle theological concerns directly but constructively.
