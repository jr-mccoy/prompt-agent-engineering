# Church Curriculum Scope & Sequence

**Objective:** Produce a multi-quarter or multi-year scope-and-sequence for a church's Bible-teaching program — mapping which books, passages, genres, doctrines, and application areas are covered when, ensuring balanced exposure and avoiding the common failure of cycling through favorites while neglecting large portions of Scripture.

**When to use:**
- You are planning a teaching calendar that spans multiple quarters or years.
- You want to ensure your congregation gets balanced exposure to the whole Bible over time.
- You need to coordinate curriculum across age groups or ministry areas.

**When NOT to use:**
- You are planning a single sermon series — use `biblical_sermon_series_planner.md`.
- You are designing one lesson or session — use `biblical_lesson_plan_builder.md`.
- You are evaluating published curriculum materials — use `biblical_churchstaff_curriculum_selection_evaluation.md`.

**Audience:** Pastors (P), education directors, and group leaders (G) responsible for teaching-ministry planning.

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **Age groups / ministry areas covered.** Which groups this scope-and-sequence serves (children, youth, adults, all-church, or a specific ministry).
2. **Time horizon.** How many quarters or years the plan covers.
3. **Current state.** What has been taught recently (last 1-2 years), so the plan avoids immediate repetition.
4. **Priorities or gaps.** Any books, doctrines, or topics the user specifically wants to include or knows are underserved.
5. **Congregational context.** Size, theological tradition (optional), maturity level, and any seasonal rhythms (school year, summer, etc.).
6. **Declared tradition (optional).** May shape doctrinal emphasis; alternatives stay visible.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Cover a balanced spread of OT and NT across the time horizon.
- Include a mix of genres (narrative, poetry, prophecy, epistle, wisdom, law, apocalyptic).
- Identify which doctrinal themes each unit addresses so coverage gaps are visible.
- Respect the congregation's seasonal rhythms (lighter summer units, Advent/Easter alignment if applicable).
- Note where age-group curricula should align (e.g., whole church studying the same book) and where they should diverge.
- Flag any unit that falls on a contested passage and note it for honest, multi-view handling.

### Must Not
- Fabricate curriculum product names, publisher claims, or statistics about what churches commonly teach.
- Design units that require the model to supply the text from memory — reference by address; user verifies.
- Privilege a contested doctrine as the unit's settled conclusion.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** note where doctrinal units touch contested ground and flag for multi-view handling.
- **Must Not:** build the sequence around a single tradition's distinctive emphases as though they were universal.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Confirm scope and constraints
Restate the age groups, time horizon, recent teaching history, priorities, and seasonal rhythms. Flag if the time horizon is too short for balanced coverage and propose an adjustment.

### Step 2 — Audit recent coverage
From the user's recent-teaching input, identify which parts of the Bible (by testament, genre, and book) are overserved and which are underserved.

### Step 3 — Build the sequence grid
Map quarters (or semesters/terms) to units. Each unit specifies:
- Book or passage range (by address)
- Genre
- Primary doctrinal theme(s)
- Application focus
- Approximate number of sessions

### Step 4 — Balance check
Review the grid for balance: OT/NT ratio, genre coverage, doctrinal coverage, and difficulty progression. Flag and adjust imbalances.

### Step 5 — Age-group alignment notes
For multi-group plans, note which units should run in parallel across groups and which should be age-specific.

### Step 6 — Contested-text flags
List any units that include contested passages and note them for multi-view handling.

## Verification

- [ ] The plan covers a balanced spread of OT and NT.
- [ ] Multiple genres are represented across the time horizon.
- [ ] Doctrinal themes are identified per unit; coverage gaps are visible.
- [ ] Seasonal rhythms are respected.
- [ ] Contested passages are flagged, not pre-decided.
- [ ] No fabricated product names, statistics, or publisher claims.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

DON'T:
- Cycle through the pastor's favorite books while neglecting large sections of Scripture.
- Fabricate curriculum products or statistics about what churches commonly teach.
- Design units around a contested doctrine as though it were settled.

DO:
- Audit recent coverage to avoid repetition and expose gaps.
- Reference passages by address; let the user verify text.
- Flag contested units for honest handling.
