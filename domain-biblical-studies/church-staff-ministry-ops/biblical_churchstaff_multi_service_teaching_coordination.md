# Multi-Service Teaching Coordination

**Objective:** Produce a coordination brief that ensures multiple communicators delivering the same message across services, campuses, or venues stay aligned on the text, big idea, and key moves — while preserving each communicator's authentic voice and pastoral instincts.

**When to use:**
- Multiple communicators preach the same series across services or campuses.
- A teaching team needs a shared framework that allows individual voice.
- You want consistency without reading from the same manuscript.

**When NOT to use:**
- One communicator preparing one message — use `biblical_expository_sermon_prep.md`.
- Planning the series arc — use `biblical_sermon_series_planner.md`.

**Audience:** Lead pastors and teaching-team leads (P).

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **The message.** Passage (by address), big idea, and series context.
2. **Communicator team.** How many communicators, their styles, and experience levels.
3. **Service contexts.** Differences between services/campuses (audience, length, format).
4. **Coordination level.** How tight the alignment needs to be (verbatim anchor points vs. shared outline with freedom).
5. **Declared tradition (optional).** May shape application emphasis.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Distinguish **non-negotiable anchor points** (text, big idea, key theological moves) from **flexible zones** (illustrations, application, personal stories, humor, transitions).
- Produce a shared outline that each communicator can adapt without betraying the message.
- Note where the passage touches contested ground and provide a shared handling approach.
- Address practical coordination: shared visuals/media, response elements, and handoff to next week.

### Must Not
- Force all communicators into one voice — the brief enables consistency, not cloning.
- Fabricate illustrations, statistics, or stories for the communicators.
- Pre-decide a contested passage's reading as the team's settled position.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** flag contested texts and agree on a handling approach (present views, not assert one).
- **Must Not:** embed a contested reading as the team's non-negotiable anchor.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Confirm the message and team
Restate passage, big idea, series position, communicator team, and service differences.

### Step 2 — Identify anchor points
Define the non-negotiable elements every communicator must include: the text (by address), the big idea (one sentence), and the key theological/applicational moves.

### Step 3 — Define flexible zones
Identify where communicators have freedom: illustrations, personal stories, application specifics, humor, transitions, and pacing.

### Step 4 — Coordination notes
Address shared elements (visuals, response, media), contested-text handling, and the handoff to next week's message.

### Step 5 — Communicator-specific notes (optional)
If the user describes the communicators, offer brief notes on how each might lean into their strengths within the shared framework.

---

## Output Format

```
# Teaching Coordination Brief — [series / week title]

## Passage & big idea
- Passage: [address] | Big idea: [one sentence]

## Non-negotiable anchor points
1. [anchor point — what every communicator must hit]
2. [..]

## Flexible zones
- Illustrations: [communicator's choice]
- Application: [adapt to service context]
- [..]

## Coordination notes
- Shared media/visuals: [..] | Response element: [..] | Next-week handoff: [..]
- Contested-text handling: [..]

## Communicator-specific notes (optional)
- [Communicator A]: [..]
```

---

## Verification

- [ ] Non-negotiable anchors are clearly distinguished from flexible zones.
- [ ] Each communicator can adapt the brief without betraying the message.
- [ ] Contested passages have a shared handling approach.
- [ ] Practical coordination (media, response, handoff) is addressed.
- [ ] No fabricated illustrations, statistics, or stories.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

DON'T:
- Produce a single manuscript that every communicator must read verbatim.
- Fabricate illustrations or stories for the teaching team.
- Embed a contested reading as a non-negotiable anchor.

DO:
- Separate anchor points from flexible zones.
- Let each communicator bring their voice to the flexible zones.
- Agree on a handling approach for contested texts.
