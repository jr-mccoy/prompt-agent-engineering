# Discipleship Pathway Design

**Objective:** Produce a discipleship pathway that maps the stages a person moves through in a church's formation process — from first visit through spiritual maturity to active ministry deployment — with clear stage definitions, recommended experiences (classes, groups, mentoring, service), transition criteria, and a plan for people who plateau or disengage.

**When to use:**
- You are designing or redesigning your church's overall discipleship strategy.
- You want a visible pathway people can understand and navigate.
- You need to identify where people get stuck and what to do about it.

**When NOT to use:**
- You are designing one-on-one new-believer discipleship content — use `biblical_ministry_new_believer_discipleship_path.md`.
- You are launching a small-group system (one component of a pathway) — use `biblical_churchstaff_small_group_launch_system.md`.

**Audience:** Lead pastors (P) responsible for congregational formation strategy.

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **Church context.** Size, culture, existing discipleship efforts, and what's working or not.
2. **Current stages (if any).** Any existing pathway, classes, or programs already in place.
3. **Theological commitments.** What the church believes discipleship should produce (knowledge, character, mission, community, or a specific framework).
4. **Capacity.** Staff and volunteer bandwidth for classes, mentoring, and tracking.
5. **Target population.** Whole congregation, or a specific cohort (new members, emerging leaders, etc.).
6. **Declared tradition (optional).** May shape stage expectations (catechesis, membership class, ordinance requirements, etc.).

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Define 3–6 stages with clear descriptions of what characterizes a person at each stage.
- Recommend experiences (classes, groups, mentoring, service, etc.) appropriate to each stage.
- Define transition criteria — what indicates readiness to move to the next stage (observable, not just time-based).
- Address people who stall: how the church identifies them and what it offers (not just "try harder").
- Acknowledge that spiritual growth is non-linear — the pathway is a map, not a conveyor belt.

### Must Not
- Fabricate discipleship research, spiritual-maturity assessment tools, or growth statistics.
- Reduce discipleship to a program checklist — transformation is the goal, not completion.
- Design a pathway that only works for people who are already highly engaged.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** note where stage expectations (membership, baptism, doctrinal agreement, spiritual gifts assessment) reflect tradition-specific commitments.
- **Must Not:** embed one tradition's formation theology as the default (e.g., assuming catechesis, or assuming believer's baptism as a stage gate).

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Confirm context and goals
Restate the church's context, existing efforts, theological commitments, and capacity. Flag if the current pathway has a known failure point.

### Step 2 — Define the stages
Map 3–6 stages from entry to deployment. For each stage:
- Stage name and description (what characterizes someone here).
- Duration (typical, not prescribed).
- Key question the person is asking at this stage.

### Step 3 — Map experiences to stages
For each stage, recommend experiences: classes, groups, mentoring, service opportunities, and self-directed study. Note which are required vs. recommended.

### Step 4 — Define transition criteria
For each stage transition, define observable indicators that suggest readiness (e.g., consistent attendance + completing foundations class + serving in one ministry, not just "spiritual maturity").

### Step 5 — Plateau and re-engagement plan
Identify common stall points and design responses: re-invitation, mentoring, a different group format, or an honest conversation about where the person is.

### Step 6 — Communication and visibility
Suggest how the pathway is communicated to the congregation: visual map, next-steps language, new-member orientation, and how people know where they are.

## Verification

- [ ] 3–6 stages are defined with clear descriptions.
- [ ] Experiences are mapped to each stage.
- [ ] Transition criteria are observable, not just time-based.
- [ ] Plateau and re-engagement is addressed.
- [ ] The pathway acknowledges non-linear growth.
- [ ] No fabricated research or maturity metrics.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

DON'T:
- Reduce discipleship to a program checklist.
- Design a pathway only for the already-engaged.
- Fabricate spiritual-growth research or assessment tools.

DO:
- Define stages by what characterizes a person, not just what they've completed.
- Address people who stall — the pathway isn't just for people who keep moving.
- Acknowledge that growth is non-linear and the pathway is a map, not a mandate.
