# Midweek Program Design

**Objective:** Design a sustainable midweek gathering format — Bible study, prayer meeting, teaching night, or a hybrid — that serves a distinct purpose from Sunday services, fits the congregation's capacity, and can survive the reality that midweek attendance is typically a fraction of Sunday.

**When to use:**
- You are launching a new midweek gathering.
- Your existing midweek program has stalled or lost purpose.
- You want to clarify what midweek does that Sunday doesn't.

**When NOT to use:**
- You are planning a small-group system (decentralized, not a single gathering) — use `biblical_churchstaff_small_group_launch_system.md`.
- You are building a single lesson for an existing program — use `biblical_lesson_plan_builder.md`.

**Audience:** Pastors (P) and group leaders (G).

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **Current state.** Does a midweek program exist? If so, what's working and what isn't?
2. **Purpose.** What the midweek gathering should do that Sunday doesn't (deeper study, prayer, fellowship, training, etc.).
3. **Audience.** Who attends midweek — the whole congregation, a self-selecting subset, or a specific group?
4. **Practical constraints.** Day, time, duration, facility, childcare availability, and leader bandwidth.
5. **Content approach.** Book study, topical series, prayer focus, lectio divina, Q&A, or rotating format.
6. **Declared tradition (optional).** May shape format expectations (liturgical prayer, expository study, testimony, etc.).

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Define a clear purpose statement that distinguishes midweek from Sunday.
- Design a format (order of service or session flow) with time allocations.
- Plan a content cycle: what is studied or practiced, how it rotates, and how long each unit lasts.
- Address sustainability: what happens when attendance dips, the leader is unavailable, or energy flags.
- Keep the format simple enough to survive with limited volunteer support.

### Must Not
- Design a second Sunday service — the midweek gathering should have its own identity.
- Assume midweek will draw Sunday-level attendance or energy.
- Fabricate statistics about midweek attendance patterns or program effectiveness.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** respect the user's tradition in format design (liturgical prayer, free-form, expository, etc.).
- **Must Not:** impose a specific worship or prayer tradition as the default midweek format.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Confirm purpose and constraints
Restate the purpose, audience, practical constraints, and content approach. Flag if the purpose is unclear or if the constraints make the program unsustainable (e.g., expecting a 90-minute program with no childcare and one leader).

### Step 2 — Design the session format
Create a session flow with time allocations. Example components: arrival/fellowship, opening, teaching/study, discussion, prayer, closing. Note which components are essential vs. optional for short nights.

### Step 3 — Plan the content cycle
Define what the group studies or practices across the year: rotating topics, book-by-book study, prayer focus, or a hybrid. Note how long each unit lasts and how transitions happen.

### Step 4 — Leadership and backup plan
Identify who leads, who assists, and what happens when the primary leader is unavailable. Design a format simple enough for a backup leader to run.

### Step 5 — Sustainability design
Address the three common midweek killers: attendance decline, leader fatigue, and purpose drift. Build in seasonal breaks, format variety, and periodic purpose reviews.

- [ ] Session format has time allocations.

- [ ] No fabricated statistics or effectiveness claims.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

DON'T:
- Design a second Sunday service under a different name.
- Assume midweek draws Sunday-level attendance.
- Create a format that requires the senior pastor every week with no backup.

DO:
- Define a distinct purpose for midweek.
- Design for sustainability — breaks, backup leaders, simple format.
- Plan the content cycle so it doesn't drift into "whatever we feel like this week."
