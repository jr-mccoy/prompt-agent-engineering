# Canonical Theme Trajectory Tracer

**Objective:** Trace a theme's movement across the canon — where it's introduced, how it develops, its turning points, and how it resolves — keeping clear the line between development the texts demonstrate and the larger trajectory a tradition constructs.

> **STRONG-GUARD prompt.** Tracing a theme invites invented developmental links and references the texts don't actually support. This prompt anchors every stage by address (verify-required) and marks each link as textually demonstrated vs. tradition-constructed.

**When to use:**
- Studying a theme as biblical theology (its canonical development), not just a topical list.
- Preparing a teaching/paper that follows a theme through Scripture.

**When NOT to use:**
- You want a topical gather-and-organize of passages — use `biblical_thematic_topical_study.md`.
- You want typology specifically — use `biblical_crossreference_typology_map.md`.

**Audience:** Seminary/academic (A), pastors (P).

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **The theme.** Stated precisely.
2. **Key texts in hand (optional).** To organize, not invent.
3. **Declared tradition (optional).** May foreground that tradition's trajectory; alternatives noted where it's read differently.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Trace the theme in canonical stages (introduction → development → turning points → culmination), each anchored **by address** (verify-required).
- Distinguish **textually demonstrated development** (the texts build on each other explicitly/probably) from **constructed trajectory** (a tradition's synthesis).
- Read each text in context; do not force passages into the trajectory (no proof-texting).
- Note where traditions trace the theme differently and why.

### Must Not
- Invent references, quotations, or developmental links the texts don't support.
- Present a constructed trajectory as the texts' explicit self-understanding.
- Force a tradition's reading of the culmination as the settled one.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** attribute construed trajectories to streams; note differences.
- **Must Not:** present one tradition's canonical reading as the established one.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Define the theme
State the theme and the question about its development.

### Step 2 — Stages
Lay out the canonical stages, each anchored by address (verify-required) and read in context.

### Step 3 — Demonstrated vs. constructed
For each link between stages, mark whether the development is textually demonstrated or a tradition's construction.

### Step 4 — Turning points & culmination
Identify the major turning points and how the theme resolves; where traditions differ on the culmination, present the options.

### Step 5 — Honest summary
Summarize the trajectory, flagging what is text-driven vs. construed and where it's contested.

## Verification

- [ ] Stages anchored by address (verify-required) and read in context.
- [ ] Each link marked demonstrated vs. constructed.
- [ ] No proof-texting; no forced passages.
- [ ] Tradition differences on the trajectory noted.
- [ ] No invented references or developmental links.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Assert a smooth development the texts don't actually show.
- Present a tradition's synthesis as the texts' explicit self-understanding.
- Pull passages into the trajectory by keyword, out of context.

✅ **DO:**
- Anchor each stage by address, in context, verify-required.
- Mark each link demonstrated vs. constructed.
- Note where traditions trace the theme differently.
