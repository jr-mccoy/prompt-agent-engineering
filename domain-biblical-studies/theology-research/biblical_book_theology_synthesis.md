# Theology of a Single Biblical Book

**Objective:** Draw out what a single biblical book distinctively contributes theologically — how it portrays God, what it is centrally arguing or burdened with, its recurring emphases, and how those relate to the rest of the canon — grounded throughout in the book's own text and kept distinct from the systematic categories later readers bring to it.

> **STRONG-GUARD prompt.** Book-theology invites importing a system's categories and quoting the book from memory. This prompt grounds every claim in the book's own text by address (verify-required), distinguishes the book's emphasis from imposed categories, and attributes contested readings to identifiable streams.

**When to use:**
- Synthesizing a book's distinctive theology (e.g., the theology of Romans, of John, of James, of Deuteronomy) — the bridge from exegesis to systematic work.
- Preparing a theology-of-the-book section for a paper, course, or sermon series.

**When NOT to use:**
- You want structure, themes, and argument for *study* (not a theological synthesis) — use `biblical_book_overview_synthesis.md`.
- You want a theme traced *across* the canon — use `biblical_theme_canonical_trajectory.md`.
- You want a doctrine gathered across *all* of Scripture and traditions — use `biblical_topical_theology_synthesis.md`.

**Audience:** Seminary/academic (A), pastors (P).

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **The book.** Named precisely; optionally the translation the user is working from.
2. **Focus question (optional).** A particular theological angle (e.g., "John's Christology," "Romans on righteousness").
3. **Key texts in hand (optional).** To organize, not invent.
4. **Declared tradition (optional).** May foreground that stream's reading of the book; alternatives noted where contested.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Ground every theological claim in the **book's own text**, by address (verify-required), read in context.
- State the book's **central burden/argument** and its **distinctive emphases** in the book's own terms before relating them to anything external.
- Distinguish the book's **emphasis** from later **systematic categories** imposed on it (e.g., don't read a full systematic locus into a book that handles the topic in passing).
- When relating the book to the canon, mark whether the connection is **textually demonstrated** (the book itself makes it) or a **constructed** canonical reading.
- Attribute **contested** readings of the book's theology to identifiable streams.

### Must Not
- Invent references, quotations, or emphases the book does not contain; do not quote the book from memory.
- Flatten the book's distinctive voice into a generic theology, or harmonize away tensions the book itself leaves.
- Present one tradition's reading of the book as the settled one.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** present the book's theology descriptively; attribute contested readings.
- **Must Not:** privilege one tradition's construal of the book as the established reading.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Frame the book and focus
Name the book and any focus question; note the translation in use.

### Step 2 — Central burden and emphases
State, in the book's own terms and anchored by address, its central argument/burden and its recurring distinctive emphases (including its portrait of God).

### Step 3 — Emphasis vs. imposed category
For each major theme, mark whether it is a genuine emphasis of the book or a category readers commonly import; keep the book's proportions.

### Step 4 — Relation to the canon
Relate the book's theology to the wider canon, marking each link as textually demonstrated (the book makes it) or constructed (a reading); note contested construals, attributed.

### Step 5 — Honest synthesis
Summarize the book's distinctive contribution, the tensions it leaves open, and where its theology is read differently — keeping the book's own voice in the foreground.

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Pour a full systematic doctrine into a book that only touches the topic, distorting its proportions.
- Quote the book from memory or assert emphases it does not actually carry.
- Smooth the book into a generic theology that erases its distinctive voice and tensions.

✅ **DO:**
- Anchor every claim in the book's text by address and keep the book's own proportions.
- Mark each theme as a genuine emphasis vs. an imported category; mark canonical links demonstrated vs. constructed.
- Preserve the book's distinctive voice and attribute contested readings to streams.
