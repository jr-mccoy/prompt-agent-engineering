# Stress-Test Your Own Theological Position

**Objective:** Pressure-test a position the user holds — not to overturn it, but to make it honest — by exposing its load-bearing assumptions, steelmanning the best opposing case, finding its weakest links and decisive cruxes, and showing what would strengthen it versus what should genuinely trouble it.

> **STRONG-GUARD prompt.** Adversarial work invites both a manufactured "gotcha" against the user and fabricated counter-authorities. This prompt steelmans the opposition without inventing scholars or quotations, references texts by address (verify-required), and ends with honest pressure points rather than a verdict.

**When to use:**
- You hold a position and want it tested before you teach, write, or commit to it.
- You want to know where your view is strong, where it's vulnerable, and what would change your mind.

**When NOT to use:**
- You want a neutral, no-declared-position comparison of views — use `biblical_interpretive_views_comparison.md`.
- The pressure point is one hard text — use `biblical_difficult_passage_analysis.md`.
- You want the descriptive landscape of a doctrine — use `biblical_doctrine_study_neutral.md`.

**Audience:** Seminary/academic (A), pastors (P).

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **Your position.** Stated as precisely as possible (the claim and, if known, why you hold it).
2. **Your main supports (optional).** Texts/arguments you'd lean on (by address) — to test, not pad.
3. **The opposition you most want tested against (optional).** Otherwise the strongest available counter-position is used.
4. **What would change your mind (optional).** If you can state it up front, the test sharpens.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Restate the user's position **charitably and accurately** before testing it (no straw-manning the user's own view either).
- Surface the position's **load-bearing assumptions** — the things that, if false, collapse it.
- **Steelman the strongest opposing case** with its best support (by address, verify-required) — the version a thoughtful opponent would actually give.
- Locate the **cruxes**: the specific points the disagreement turns on and where the evidence is genuinely contested.
- Separate **what would strengthen** the position (verification, better support) from **what should give pause** (real weaknesses), honestly.

### Must Not
- Build a straw man of the opposition, or a fake weakness in the user's view, just to produce a result.
- Invent scholars, councils, quotations, citations, or cross-references for either side.
- Impose a verdict that the user must abandon or keep the position.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** treat the user's position and the opposition as serious, attributed to their streams.
- **Must Not:** tilt the test toward the model's own preferred conclusion; manufacture a decisive refutation or vindication.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Restate the position
Restate the user's claim charitably and accurately; confirm the version being tested.

### Step 2 — Expose assumptions
List the load-bearing assumptions the position rests on; mark which are widely shared and which are contested.

### Step 3 — Steelman the opposition
Give the strongest opposing case with its best support (by address, verify-required), attributed to its stream — the version its ablest proponents hold.

### Step 4 — Cruxes and weakest links
Name the cruxes the disagreement turns on and the position's weakest links; mark where evidence is genuinely contested.

### Step 5 — Honest close
Separate what would strengthen the position from what should give the user pause; state what (if stated) would change the user's mind — no verdict imposed.

## Output Format

```
# Stress-Test — [the position]

## Position as I understand it
> [charitable, accurate restatement]

## Load-bearing assumptions
- [assumption] — widely shared / contested

## Strongest opposing case (steelmanned; support by address, verify-required; attributed)
- [..]

## Cruxes & weakest links
- The disagreement turns on: [..]
- Weakest links in the position: [..]
- Genuinely contested evidence: [..]

## Honest close
- Would strengthen it: [..] | Should give you pause: [..]
- What would change your mind: [..]
- (No verdict imposed.)
```

---

## Verification

- [ ] User's position restated charitably and accurately before testing.
- [ ] Load-bearing assumptions surfaced and marked shared vs. contested.
- [ ] Opposition steelmanned with support by address (verify-required), attributed to its stream.
- [ ] Cruxes and weakest links named; contested evidence flagged.
- [ ] Strengtheners separated from real pause-points; no verdict imposed; no fabricated sources for either side.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Hand the user a flattering test that finds only easily-answered objections.
- Straw-man the opposition (or invent a weakness in the user's view) to manufacture a result.
- Invent a counter-authority or quotation, or end by ruling the position right or wrong.

✅ **DO:**
- Restate the position charitably, then expose its real load-bearing assumptions.
- Steelman the opposition with support by address, verify-required, attributed to its stream.
- Separate genuine strengtheners from genuine pause-points and leave the verdict with the user.
