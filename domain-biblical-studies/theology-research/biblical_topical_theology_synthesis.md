# Topical / Systematic Theology Synthesis

**Objective:** Produce a structured synthesis of a doctrine or topic that organizes the relevant biblical texts (read in context) and presents how the major traditions construe them — descriptively, with disagreement preserved and every claim attributable, without fabricating sources.

> **STRONG-GUARD prompt.** Systematic synthesis invites fabricated scholars, councils, creeds, quotations, and cross-references. This prompt attributes positions to identifiable traditions/streams, references texts by address, and flags any named source as verify-required.

**When to use:**
- Studying a doctrine (e.g., atonement, providence, baptism) across Scripture and traditions.
- Building the synthesis layer of a paper or teaching responsibly.
- You want the biblical data and the range of positions, not a verdict.

**When NOT to use:**
- You want a single doctrine compared across traditions in depth — use `biblical_doctrine_study_neutral.md`.
- You want one passage's interpretations — use `biblical_multiview_interpretation_map.md`.

**Audience:** Seminary/academic (A), pastors (P), equipped leaders (G).

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **The doctrine/topic.** Stated precisely, with any sub-questions.
2. **Texts/positions in hand (optional).** To organize, not invent.
3. **Declared tradition (optional).** May foreground that tradition's construal; alternatives still presented.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Gather the key biblical texts **by address**, read in context (route gathering discipline to the thematic-study prompt; no proof-texting).
- Organize the **biblical data** into the facets of the topic.
- Present the **major traditions'** construals descriptively, attributed to identifiable streams; preserve genuine disagreement.
- Distinguish **consensus** (broadly shared), **majority/minority**, and **contested**; note **silence** (what Scripture doesn't directly address).
- Mark every named scholar/council/creed/quotation as **verify-required**.

### Must Not
- Invent citations, cross-references, scholars, councils, creeds, dates, or quotations.
- Present one tradition's systematic conclusion as the biblical/settled position.
- Smooth real disagreement into false consensus, or proof-text.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** present each tradition's view as a position, fairly stated and attributed.
- **Must Not:** privilege/endorse one tradition's synthesis as correct.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Define the topic
State the doctrine/topic and its sub-questions.

### Step 2 — Biblical data
Gather key texts (by address, verify-required, in context) and organize them by facet.

### Step 3 — Traditions' construals
For each major position, summarize how that stream construes the data, attributed descriptively; mark named sources verify-required.

### Step 4 — Map agreement/disagreement
Distinguish consensus / majority / minority / contested, and name silences.

### Step 5 — Honest synthesis
State what is broadly held, where traditions genuinely diverge and why, and what remains contested or unaddressed — without ruling (unless a tradition was declared, and then still with alternatives).

---

## Verification

- [ ] Texts by address, in context, verify-required; no proof-texting.
- [ ] Traditions' positions descriptive and attributed to streams.
- [ ] Consensus/majority/minority/contested distinguished; silences named.
- [ ] No invented scholars/councils/creeds/quotes/citations; named sources flagged verify-required.
- [ ] No tradition's synthesis presented as the settled biblical position.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Cite a council, creed, scholar, or quotation from memory as established.
- Assemble proof-texts to support one tradition's system.
- Flatten the atonement/baptism/etc. debates into a single "biblical view."

✅ **DO:**
- Gather texts by address, in context, and mark verify-required.
- Attribute construals to identifiable traditions and preserve disagreement.
- Name silences and flag every named source verify-required.
