# Evangelistic Message Preparation

**Objective:** Prepare a gospel-presentation message that is anchored in an actual passage, clear about the gospel, honest, non-manipulative, and sensitive to its audience — while staying tradition-neutral on contested soteriological specifics by attributing differing framings to identifiable streams rather than ruling between them.

**When to use:**
- You are preparing a message whose central aim is to present the gospel and invite a response.
- You want the presentation grounded in a real text rather than a free-floating appeal.
- You want guardrails against manipulation, fabricated testimony, and overclaiming on contested points.

**When NOT to use:**
- You are preaching a passage for a believing congregation without an evangelistic aim — use `biblical_expository_sermon_prep.md`.
- You are gathering multiple texts on a theme — use `biblical_topical_sermon_prep.md`.
- You need an illustration or testimony — route to `biblical_sermon_illustration_finder.md` (do not invent stories or statistics here).
- You need the bridge to application/discipleship — use `biblical_application_bridge_builder.md`.

**Audience:** Pastors (P) and equipped teachers/evangelists (G). Intermediate.

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **The anchor passage.** Reference plus the text in a named translation, supplied by the user. The model references by address and uses supplied text rather than quoting from memory.
2. **The gospel core (optional).** Any points the user wants foregrounded; the model otherwise keeps the presentation grounded in the anchor text.
3. **Audience & setting.** Who will hear it (e.g., seekers, skeptics, mixed, churched), the occasion, time available, and the response being invited.
4. **Declared tradition (optional).** If supplied, the model may foreground that stream's soteriological framing but must still flag contested specifics and name alternatives. No declaration → neutral default.
5. **Depth / output length.** Short brief vs. full outline.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Anchor the message in the **supplied passage**, read in context; show how the gospel arises from the text rather than being imposed on it.
- Present the gospel **clearly and honestly** — the genuine message, including its cost and call, not a softened or inflated version.
- Be **non-manipulative**: no fabricated emotional stories, no invented statistics, no pressure tactics, no false urgency manufactured from invented facts.
- Be **audience-sensitive**: meet listeners where they are, define terms, and respect their freedom to respond.
- On **contested soteriological specifics** (e.g., the order/means/extent of salvation, the nature of the response, assurance), present positions attributed to streams without ruling; state confidence where the text underdetermines the question.

### Must Not
- Invent testimonies, conversion stories, statistics, quotations, cross-references, or original-language data. Route illustrations/testimonies to the illustration prompt.
- Use the text as a pretext for an appeal it does not support (proof-texting the gospel onto an unrelated passage).
- Present one stream's soteriological system as the plain, settled meaning of the text.
- Manufacture urgency or guilt with fabricated facts or coercive framing.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** present text + consensus; attribute contested soteriological framings to identifiable streams; treat doctrinal claims as positions, not fact; label confidence where the text underdetermines.
- **Must Not:** privilege/endorse any single tradition's order of salvation or response model as correct (unless the user declared one — and even then, note alternatives); smooth genuine disagreement into false consensus.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Orient
Restate the anchor passage, the audience/setting, and the response being invited. Name the genre and immediate context of the text in a line.

### Step 2 — Draw the gospel from the text
Show what *this passage* contributes to the gospel — what it reveals about God, the human condition, Christ, and the call — strictly from the supplied text in context. Note where the full gospel needs other texts (by address, not invented).

### Step 3 — State the gospel core clearly and honestly
Articulate the central message plainly: the need, the provision, the response. Keep it honest about cost and call; avoid both softening and inflation.

### Step 4 — Flag contested soteriological specifics
Where the message touches disputed questions (means/order/extent of salvation, nature of the response, assurance), lay out the positions attributed to streams, with confidence on where the text underdetermines — without ruling (unless a tradition was declared).

### Step 5 — Shape for the audience, non-manipulatively
Adapt language and entry point to the listeners; define terms; invite rather than pressure. Mark where an illustration or testimony would help and route it to the illustration prompt rather than inventing one.

### Step 6 — Confidence, gaps, and handoffs
- Note the message's central claim and its confidence; flag any reliance on contested ground.
- Identify gaps (illustration/testimony → illustration prompt; follow-up discipleship/application → application prompt) instead of improvising them.

## Output Format

```
# Evangelistic Message — [anchor reference]

## Orientation
- Audience/setting: [..] | Response invited: [..] | Genre/context of text: [..]

## Gospel from the text
- What [reference] contributes (in context): [God / condition / Christ / call]
- Needs other texts (by address, verify): [..]

## Gospel core (clear & honest)
- Need: [..] | Provision: [..] | Response: [..]

## Contested soteriological specifics
- [question] — [Option A — stream] | [Option B — stream] (confidence; text underdetermines at ..)

## Audience-sensitive shape
- Entry point / terms defined / non-manipulative invitation: [..]
- Illustration needed at [point] → illustration prompt

## Confidence, gaps & handoffs
- Central claim: [..] (confidence; leans on contested ground at ..)
- Follow-up/discipleship → application prompt
```

- [ ] Contested soteriological specifics attributed to streams, not adjudicated (unless tradition declared).

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Invent a moving conversion story or a statistic to drive the appeal.
- Manufacture urgency or guilt with fabricated facts or coercive framing.
- Force the gospel onto a passage that does not actually carry it.
- Present one stream's order/means of salvation as the text's settled meaning.

✅ **DO:**
- Draw the gospel from the supplied text in context, and note where other texts are needed (by address).
- State the message honestly — need, provision, response — without softening or inflation.
- Attribute contested soteriological specifics to streams and flag where the text underdetermines.
- Route illustrations/testimonies and follow-up to their prompts; invite rather than pressure.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-01 (Clear Objective Statement):** Opens by naming the goal — a text-anchored, honest, non-manipulative gospel presentation — so the integrity guards govern the entire prep rather than being an afterthought.
- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** The numbered sequence (Orient → Draw from text → State core → Flag contested → Shape for audience → Confidence/handoffs) keeps the gospel grounded in the passage before any appeal is shaped.
- **RT-05 (Evidence-Based Reasoning):** The gospel must be shown to arise from the supplied text in context, and points needing other texts are flagged by address rather than asserted, so the message rests on what the passage actually supports.
- **QA-04 (Uncertainty Acknowledgment):** Contested soteriological specifics are flagged with stream-attributed alternatives and a note of where the text underdetermines the question, and the central claim carries a confidence level.
- **QA-05 (Citation Requirements):** Verses are referenced by address from user-supplied text; testimonies, statistics, quotations, cross-references, and lexical data are never fabricated and illustrations are routed to the illustration prompt.
