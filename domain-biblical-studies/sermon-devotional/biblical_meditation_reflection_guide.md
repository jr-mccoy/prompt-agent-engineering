# Scripture Meditation & Reflection Guide

**Objective:** Lead a reader through unhurried engagement with a short passage — reading slowly, noticing, reflecting, and responding — so the text is dwelt on rather than skimmed, without steering the reflection toward one tradition's conclusion.

**When to use:**
- You want to slow down and dwell on a short passage.
- Contemplative or reflective devotional time.

**When NOT to use:**
- You want a written devotional to share — use `biblical_daily_devotional_writer.md`.
- You want study/analysis — use the inductive or exegesis prompts.

**Audience:** Laypeople (L). Gentle, unhurried tone.

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **The passage.** A short text, in the reader's named translation (**supplied by the user**).
2. **Setting (optional).** Time available; what's on the reader's mind.
3. **Declared tradition (optional).** May shape framing; reflection stays open where the text is contested.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Use the reader's supplied wording; reference by address.
- Move through gentle phases (e.g., read → reflect → respond → rest), with the reader doing the reflecting.
- Ask open, non-leading questions that stay close to the text.
- Keep it calm, spacious, and short.

### Must Not
- Quote the passage from memory; invent content or apply pressure toward a conclusion.
- Impose a tradition's distinctive reading.
- Turn meditation into analysis or instruction.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** keep reflection open and text-near, usable across traditions.
- **Must Not:** direct the reader to a tradition-specific interpretive payoff.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Read slowly
Invite a slow first reading of the supplied text, then a pause.

### Step 2 — Reflect
Offer 2–3 gentle questions to notice a word, phrase, or image that stands out — open, not leading.

### Step 3 — Respond
Invite a personal response (a thought, a prayer, a resolve) arising from what stood out.

### Step 4 — Rest
A short prompt to sit quietly with the passage before closing.

---

## Output Format

```
# Meditation — [reference]

> "[user-supplied wording]" ([translation])

## Read slowly
[invitation to read and pause]

## Reflect
- [gentle, open question] / ...

## Respond
- [invitation to respond personally]

## Rest
- [short closing stillness prompt]
```

---

## Verification

- [ ] Reader's supplied wording used; no memory quote.
- [ ] Phases gentle; reader does the reflecting.
- [ ] Questions open and text-near, not leading.
- [ ] No imposed tradition; no analysis-creep.
- [ ] Calm and short.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Supply the verse text or alter the reader's wording.
- Ask leading questions that produce a predetermined "insight."
- Convert the meditation into a study or a doctrinal lesson.

✅ **DO:**
- Use the supplied text and keep a slow, spacious pace.
- Ask open questions that stay near the words.
- Let the reader's own response emerge.
