# Sermon Delivery Coaching

**Objective:** Help a preacher assess and improve their delivery skills — producing a personal improvement plan based on their self-reported strengths, struggles, and delivery context — so they grow as communicators without requiring a live coach.

**When to use:**
- A preacher wants to improve a specific delivery skill (pacing, nerves, notes management, vocal variety).
- A preacher is transitioning from one delivery style to another (manuscript to notes, or vice versa).
- A preacher is new to preaching and wants a self-coaching framework.

**When NOT to use:**
- You are preparing sermon content (outline or manuscript) — use `biblical_expository_sermon_prep.md` or `biblical_sermon_manuscript_draft.md`.
- You want post-sermon feedback on a specific message — use `biblical_churchstaff_sermon_feedback_debrief.md`.

**Audience:** Pastors (P).

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **Experience level.** New preacher, a few years in, or experienced but wanting to sharpen.
2. **Delivery style.** Manuscript, semi-manuscript, detailed notes, minimal notes, or no notes.
3. **Self-assessed strengths.** What the preacher thinks they do well (or has been told they do well).
4. **Self-assessed struggles.** What they want to improve (nerves, pacing, eye contact, vocal monotony, going long, losing their place, transitions, openings/closings, etc.).
5. **Context.** Typical audience size, service style, and any amplification/staging factors.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Base coaching on the preacher's self-reported experience, not assumptions.
- Provide specific, practicable exercises (not just "practice more") — things the preacher can do this week.
- Address the mental/emotional side of preaching (nerves, confidence, post-sermon crash) alongside the technical.
- Recommend self-assessment methods: recording and reviewing, asking a trusted listener, or using a self-check rubric.
- Respect diverse delivery styles — manuscript preaching is legitimate; notes-free preaching is legitimate; neither is inherently superior.

### Must Not
- Fabricate communication-science research, homiletics statistics, or audience-retention data.
- Promote one delivery style as universally best.
- Provide therapy-level intervention for anxiety — note when professional help may be warranted and route accordingly.
- Assume the preacher has a coach, a recording setup, or a preaching team.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Assess current state
Restate the preacher's experience, style, strengths, and struggles. Identify 1–2 priority growth areas (not everything at once).

### Step 2 — Delivery skill coaching
For each priority area, provide:
- **What's happening:** why this is common and what causes it.
- **Practice exercise:** a specific, repeatable exercise the preacher can do before next Sunday.
- **In-the-moment technique:** something to try during the actual sermon.
- **Progress indicator:** how the preacher will know they're improving.

### Step 3 — Notes/manuscript management
If the preacher's struggle relates to their notes format, help them:
- Assess whether their current format serves them.
- Try an alternative format (larger font, fewer words per page, color-coded sections, keyword-only cards).
- Practice the transition between looking down and looking up.

### Step 4 — Nerves and mental preparation
Address the mental side:
- Pre-sermon routine (physical warm-up, breathing, prayer rhythm).
- Reframing nerves as energy, not failure.
- Post-sermon decompression (the crash is normal; how to process it).

### Step 5 — Self-assessment framework
Provide a simple rubric the preacher can use after each sermon (or after reviewing a recording):
- Did I make eye contact? (scale)
- Did I vary my pace and volume? (scale)
- Did I stay within time? (scale)
- What was the strongest and weakest moment?

## Verification

- [ ] Specific, practicable exercises are provided (not "practice more").
- [ ] Mental/emotional side (nerves, post-sermon crash) is addressed.
- [ ] A self-assessment method is recommended.
- [ ] No fabricated research or homiletics statistics.
- [ ] No single delivery style is promoted as superior.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

DON'T:
- Tell a manuscript preacher they should go notes-free (or vice versa) without hearing their context.
- Fabricate audience-retention data or communication research.
- Suggest the preacher just needs more confidence — provide techniques, not platitudes.

DO:
- Provide specific exercises, not generic advice.
- Address nerves and the post-sermon crash — these are universal.
- Respect that effective preaching comes in many styles.
