# Facilitation Dynamics

**Objective:** Equip a Bible-study group leader with specific strategies and scripts for the most common in-room facilitation challenges — so they can keep the discussion productive, inclusive, and on-text without becoming controlling or passive.

**When to use:**
- A group leader is struggling with a specific dynamic (silence, one person dominating, frequent tangents, emotional moments).
- A leader wants to prepare in advance for likely facilitation challenges.
- A groups pastor is coaching a leader through a recurring issue.

**When NOT to use:**
- The issue is theological disagreement or conflict — use `biblical_groupleader_conflict_resolution.md`.
- The issue is a heretical or heterodox claim — use `biblical_groupleader_heretical_claim_response.md`.
- You need discussion questions, not facilitation strategies — use `biblical_smallgroup_discussion_guide.md`.

**Audience:** Group leaders (G) and pastors who coach them (P).

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **The challenge.** Which dynamic the leader is facing (silence, dominance, tangents, emotional disclosure, disengagement, side conversations, or a combination).
2. **Group context.** Group size, how long they've been together, and the relational dynamics.
3. **Leader experience.** New leader or experienced; comfort level with confrontation and emotional situations.
4. **What they've tried.** Any strategies already attempted and how they worked.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Provide specific, usable scripts (actual words the leader can say), not just abstract principles.
- Offer a range of responses from gentle to direct so the leader can escalate as needed.
- Distinguish between one-time incidents (handle in the moment) and patterns (address outside the group).
- Address the leader's own emotional response — facilitation is stressful; normalize that.

### Must Not
- Fabricate group-dynamics research, facilitator statistics, or pedagogical studies.
- Provide scripts that are manipulative, passive-aggressive, or shaming.
- Assume the leader has formal training in facilitation or counseling.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** strategies should work across church traditions and group cultures.
- **Must Not:** assume a specific authority structure (e.g., elder-led vs. peer-led groups).

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Identify the challenge
Restate the specific dynamic, the group context, and what the leader has already tried. Diagnose whether this is a one-time incident or a pattern.

### Step 2 — Provide in-the-moment strategies
For each challenge, offer 2–3 specific strategies with scripts:
- **Silence:** types of silence (thinking vs. confused vs. disengaged), re-ask techniques, pair-share, written response.
- **Dominance:** redirect phrases, structured turn-taking, private conversation.
- **Tangents:** acknowledge-and-redirect scripts, parking-lot technique.
- **Emotional disclosure:** validate, contain, and follow up; when to pause the study.
- **Other dynamics as specified by the user.**

### Step 3 — Provide pattern-level strategies
If the challenge is recurring, offer strategies for addressing it outside the group: private conversation scripts, group-norm setting, and when to involve a pastor or groups coordinator.

### Step 4 — Leader self-care
Acknowledge the stress of facilitation. Suggest debriefing (with a co-leader, spouse, or pastor) and remind the leader that not every session will go perfectly.

## False-Positive Prevention

DON'T:
- Provide only abstract advice ("be a good listener") without specific scripts.
- Offer shaming, passive-aggressive, or manipulative language.
- Assume the leader has counseling or facilitation training.

DO:
- Give actual words the leader can say.
- Distinguish in-the-moment from pattern-level responses.
- Normalize that facilitation is hard and not every session goes well.
