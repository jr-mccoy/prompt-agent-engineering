# Handling Hard Questions

**Objective:** Equip a group leader with strategies and scripts for three categories of hard questions: (1) questions the leader can't answer, (2) questions on theologically contested topics, and (3) emotionally charged questions — so the leader responds with honesty and grace rather than bluffing, shutting down, or panicking.

**When to use:**
- A leader is preparing for a passage they know will raise difficult questions.
- A leader just encountered a hard question and wants help responding at the next meeting.
- A groups pastor is coaching leaders on how to handle hard moments.

**When NOT to use:**
- The question involves a heretical or heterodox claim — use `biblical_groupleader_heretical_claim_response.md`.
- The issue is interpersonal conflict, not a theological question — use `biblical_groupleader_conflict_resolution.md`.
- You need to research a difficult passage — use `biblical_difficult_passage_analysis.md`.

**Audience:** Group leaders (G) and pastors who coach them (P).

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **The question (or type of question).** What was asked, or what kind of question the leader expects.
2. **Category.** Does the leader not know the answer, is the topic contested, or is the question emotionally charged (or a combination)?
3. **Group context.** How mature the group is, how safe the environment feels, and whether this is a first occurrence or a pattern.
4. **Leader's comfort level.** How comfortable the leader is with uncertainty and controversy.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Provide category-specific strategies: "I don't know" responses, contested-topic handling, and emotionally charged responses.
- Include specific scripts — actual words the leader can say.
- Normalize not knowing — a leader who says "I don't know, let me look into that" models integrity.
- For contested topics, present the range of views without pre-deciding (consistent with domain tradition-neutral stance).
- For emotional questions, prioritize the person over the answer.

### Must Not
- Encourage the leader to bluff or make up an answer.
- Provide a "correct answer" to a contested theological question as the leader's script.
- Fabricate research on group dynamics, question frequency, or leader effectiveness.
- Suggest the leader provide counseling-level support for emotional questions — route to a pastor or professional when appropriate.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** help the leader present contested topics honestly, attributing views to identifiable traditions.
- **Must Not:** script a specific tradition's answer as "the right response" to a contested question.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Classify the question
Restate the question and classify it: unknown-answer, contested-topic, emotionally-charged, or a combination. Note the group context and leader comfort level.

### Step 2 — "I don't know" strategies
If the leader doesn't know the answer:
- Scripts for admitting it honestly.
- Follow-up plan: how to research and bring it back next week.
- When to redirect to the pastor or a more knowledgeable resource.

### Step 3 — Contested-topic strategies
If the topic is theologically contested:
- How to name the disagreement ("Christians have held different views on this").
- How to present 2–3 major positions briefly and fairly.
- How to hold the tension without forcing resolution.
- When to table the discussion and offer further resources.

### Step 4 — Emotionally-charged strategies
If the question carries emotional weight (suffering, doubt, personal crisis):
- Prioritize the person: validate the emotion before addressing the theology.
- Scripts that acknowledge pain without minimizing it.
- When to pause the group discussion and follow up privately.
- When to refer to a pastor or professional.

### Step 5 — Follow-up plan
For all categories, provide a follow-up plan: what to do between now and the next meeting (research, private conversation, consult the pastor, bring resources).

## Verification

- [ ] Specific scripts are provided for in-the-moment response.
- [ ] "I don't know" is normalized, not avoided.
- [ ] Contested topics are handled with multi-view honesty.
- [ ] Emotional questions prioritize the person over the answer.
- [ ] Follow-up plan is included.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

DON'T:
- Encourage bluffing or making up answers.
- Script a single tradition's answer as the "correct" response to a contested topic.
- Suggest the group leader provide counseling for emotional questions.

DO:
- Normalize "I don't know" as a sign of integrity, not weakness.
- Present contested topics with multi-view honesty.
- Prioritize the person over the theological answer when emotion is involved.
