# Apprentice Leader Development

**Objective:** Produce a staged apprenticeship plan that takes a potential group leader from observation through co-leading to independent leadership — with clear responsibilities at each stage, feedback checkpoints, and readiness criteria for launch.

**When to use:**
- A group leader has identified someone in their group who could lead.
- A church wants a reproducible pathway for developing new group leaders.
- A groups pastor needs a framework for leader multiplication.

**When NOT to use:**
- You are training a cohort of volunteers from scratch (classroom-style) — use `biblical_churchstaff_teacher_training_plan.md`.
- You are designing volunteer roles (not developing a specific apprentice) — use `biblical_churchstaff_volunteer_recruitment_role_design.md`.

**Audience:** Group leaders (G) developing apprentices, and pastors (P) overseeing leader development.

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **The apprentice.** Who they are, their current role in the group, their spiritual maturity, and their strengths.
2. **The leader's group.** Group type, size, and how long it has been running.
3. **Timeline.** How long the apprenticeship should last (one semester, two semesters, open-ended).
4. **Goal.** Is the apprentice being developed to take over this group, launch a new group, or become a floater/substitute leader?
5. **Support structure.** What coaching or training the church provides beyond the in-group mentoring.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Design a staged progression: observe → assist → co-lead → lead (with the mentor present) → lead independently.
- Define specific responsibilities at each stage (not just "help out more").
- Build in feedback checkpoints: after each stage, the mentor and apprentice debrief before advancing.
- Define readiness criteria for independent launch — observable indicators, not just time served.
- Address the apprentice's development as a *person*, not just as a *facilitator* — character, spiritual health, and relational skills matter.

### Must Not
- Rush the apprentice to independence before they're ready to protect a promotion timeline.
- Design an apprenticeship that is all observation with no progressive responsibility.
- Fabricate leadership-development research or multiplication statistics.
- Assume the apprentice will definitely become a leader — the plan should include an honest off-ramp if it's not the right fit.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** readiness criteria should work across traditions.
- **Must Not:** impose tradition-specific leadership qualifications (elder criteria, ordination, etc.) unless the user declares them.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Confirm context and goals
Restate the apprentice's profile, the group context, timeline, goal, and support structure. Flag if the timeline is too short for the goal.

### Step 2 — Design the stages
Map 4–5 stages with specific responsibilities at each:
1. **Observe:** Attend as a participant with a development lens. Notice what the leader does and why.
2. **Assist:** Take on specific tasks (welcome, prayer, logistics) while the leader facilitates.
3. **Co-lead:** Facilitate portions of the study (one question set, one segment) with the leader present.
4. **Lead (mentored):** Lead a full session with the mentor present as backup.
5. **Lead independently:** Run the group (or launch a new one) with periodic check-ins.

### Step 3 — Feedback checkpoints
Design a debrief conversation for each stage transition:
- What went well?
- What was hard?
- What would you do differently?
- Are you ready for the next stage?

### Step 4 — Readiness criteria for launch
Define observable indicators that the apprentice is ready to lead independently:
- Can facilitate a full session without intervention.
- Handles at least one hard moment (silence, tangent, question they can't answer) effectively.
- Group members relate to them as a leader, not just an assistant.
- The apprentice *wants* to lead (not just compliant).

### Step 5 — Off-ramp
Acknowledge that not every apprentice will become a leader. Provide a gracious off-ramp: how to have the conversation, how to affirm the person's value to the group, and alternative roles they might thrive in.

## Verification

- [ ] Stages have specific responsibilities, not just vague descriptions.
- [ ] Feedback checkpoints are built in at each transition.
- [ ] Readiness criteria are observable, not just time-based.
- [ ] An honest off-ramp exists for apprentices who aren't the right fit.
- [ ] Character and spiritual health are part of the development, not just skills.
- [ ] No fabricated research or multiplication statistics.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

DON'T:
- Rush the apprentice to fill a staffing need.
- Design all observation with no progressive responsibility.
- Assume every apprentice will become a leader — include an off-ramp.

DO:
- Define specific responsibilities at each stage.
- Build in feedback checkpoints — don't advance without debriefing.
- Address the whole person (character, spiritual health), not just facilitation skills.
