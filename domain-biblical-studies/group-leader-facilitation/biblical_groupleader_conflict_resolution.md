# Conflict Resolution

**Objective:** Help a group leader navigate conflict in a Bible-study group — distinguishing healthy theological disagreement (which should be welcomed) from destructive interpersonal conflict (which must be addressed) — with specific strategies for de-escalation, private follow-up, and escalation to church leadership.

**When to use:**
- Two group members are in open tension during a session.
- A theological disagreement is becoming personal or divisive.
- A leader senses unresolved tension affecting the group's health.

**When NOT to use:**
- The issue is a heretical claim, not interpersonal conflict — use `biblical_groupleader_heretical_claim_response.md`.
- The issue is general facilitation (silence, tangents), not conflict — use `biblical_groupleader_facilitation_dynamics.md`.
- You need a difficult-conversation framework for yourself (not a group setting) — use `difficultconvo_pre_brief.md`.

**Audience:** Group leaders (G) and pastors who coach them (P).

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **The conflict.** What happened — theological disagreement, personality clash, offense taken, or unresolved history.
2. **Parties involved.** Who is in tension, their relationship, and their maturity.
3. **Severity.** Mild tension, heated exchange, ongoing cold conflict, or one person threatening to leave.
4. **Group impact.** How the group is affected — sides forming, avoidance, reduced participation, or unaware.
5. **Leader's relationship to the parties.** Neutral, closer to one side, or directly involved.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Distinguish healthy theological disagreement (valuable) from destructive conflict (needs intervention).
- Provide in-the-moment de-escalation scripts (actual words).
- Provide private follow-up scripts for conversations with each party.
- Define escalation criteria — when the leader should involve the pastor or church leadership.
- Protect the group: conflict between two people should not hijack the entire group's experience.

### Must Not
- Take sides in a theological disagreement — help the leader facilitate, not adjudicate.
- Provide scripts that are passive-aggressive, manipulative, or triangulating.
- Fabricate conflict-resolution research or group-dynamics statistics.
- Suggest the leader provide counseling — route to a pastor or professional when appropriate.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** strategies should work across church traditions and authority structures.
- **Must Not:** assume a specific conflict-resolution theology (e.g., Matthew 18 as the only framework) without the user declaring it.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Assess the conflict
Restate the conflict, classify it (theological disagreement vs. interpersonal vs. both), and assess severity. Note the group impact and leader's position.

### Step 2 — In-the-moment de-escalation
Provide 2–3 scripts for de-escalating in the room:
- Acknowledge both perspectives without taking sides.
- Name the tension ("I can tell this is important to both of you").
- Redirect to the text or table the discussion.
- Protect the group ("Let's come back to this — [names], can we talk after?").

### Step 3 — Private follow-up
Script separate conversations with each party:
- Listen to each person's perspective.
- Identify the real issue (theology, personality, unresolved history, or different expectations).
- Explore resolution: apology, agreement to disagree, changed behavior, or mediated conversation.

### Step 4 — Mediated conversation (if needed)
If the conflict requires bringing the parties together, provide a framework:
- Ground rules (listen, don't interrupt, seek understanding).
- Leader's role (facilitator, not judge).
- Possible outcomes (reconciliation, agreement to disagree, one party stepping back).

### Step 5 — Escalation criteria
Define when the leader should involve the pastor or church leadership:
- One party refuses to engage.
- The conflict is causing group members to leave.
- The leader feels out of their depth.
- The conflict involves abuse, threats, or safety concerns.

## False-Positive Prevention

DON'T:
- Take sides in a theological disagreement.
- Script passive-aggressive or triangulating responses.
- Suggest the leader become a counselor for interpersonal issues.

DO:
- Distinguish healthy disagreement from destructive conflict.
- Protect the group's experience while addressing the individuals.
- Define when the leader should escalate — not every conflict is theirs to resolve.
