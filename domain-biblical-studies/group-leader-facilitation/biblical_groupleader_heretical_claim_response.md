# Heretical Claim Response

> **STRONG-GUARD prompt.** This prompt must distinguish ecumenical-creed-level orthodoxy (Apostles', Nicene, Chalcedonian) from intra-Christian disputed questions. It must never adjudicate disputed questions (cessationism/continuationism, baptismal mode, eschatological systems, gender roles, etc.) as "heretical" — those are within-family disagreements. "Orthodox" in this prompt means the ecumenical creeds, not any single tradition's theological system. Every claim about what the creeds affirm or deny is verify-required against the actual creed text.

**Objective:** Help a group leader respond when a participant makes a claim that appears to contradict historic Christian orthodoxy (creed-level) — with a graduated response that preserves the person's dignity, protects the group, and distinguishes genuine creedal departures from intra-Christian disagreements the leader may personally find wrong but that do not cross the creedal line.

**When to use:**
- A participant denies a creed-level doctrine (Trinity, incarnation, bodily resurrection, etc.).
- A participant presents teaching from a non-Christian or pseudo-Christian source as equivalent to biblical teaching.
- A leader isn't sure whether a claim is heretical or just a different Christian position.

**When NOT to use:**
- The disagreement is about a contested intra-Christian question (baptism, end times, spiritual gifts, gender roles) — use `biblical_groupleader_handling_hard_questions.md` or `biblical_groupleader_conflict_resolution.md`.
- The leader needs to research a doctrine across traditions — use `biblical_doctrine_study_neutral.md`.

**Audience:** Group leaders (G) and pastors who coach them (P).

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **The claim.** What the participant said (as accurately as possible).
2. **Context.** Was it stated as personal belief, a question, or a teaching? How did the group react?
3. **The participant.** New believer exploring, longtime member with a new influence, or someone from a different religious background?
4. **Leader's assessment.** Does the leader think this is a creedal departure, an intra-Christian disagreement, or are they unsure?
5. **Declared tradition (optional).** The church's tradition may have additional boundaries beyond the creeds.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Define "orthodox" at the ecumenical-creed level (Apostles', Nicene, Chalcedonian) — not at any single tradition's theological system level.
- Distinguish three categories: (a) creedal departure (deny what the creeds affirm), (b) intra-Christian disagreement (within the family), (c) uncertain (needs further conversation).
- Provide a graduated response: in-the-moment (gentle, non-shaming), after the meeting (private conversation), and escalation (involve the pastor).
- Preserve the person's dignity — the goal is truth and love, not public correction or humiliation.
- All claims about what the creeds affirm or deny are verify-required — the model must not assert creedal content from memory without flagging it for verification.

### Must Not
- Classify intra-Christian disagreements (cessationism, baptismal mode, eschatology, gender roles, soteriology details) as heretical.
- Script a public confrontation or shaming response.
- Fabricate church-history claims, creed texts, or heresy-tribunal precedents.
- Adjudicate whether a specific modern group or teacher is heretical — the prompt helps the leader assess a *claim*, not label a *person or group*.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** distinguish creed-level orthodoxy from tradition-specific orthodoxy. A church may have boundaries tighter than the creeds; note that these are tradition-specific, not universal.
- **Must Not:** treat one tradition's theological distinctives as the line between orthodoxy and heresy.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Assess the claim
Restate the claim and classify it: creedal departure, intra-Christian disagreement, or uncertain. Cite the relevant creedal affirmation (verify-required) if it's a creedal issue. If it's an intra-Christian disagreement, name the traditions on each side and redirect to `biblical_groupleader_handling_hard_questions.md`.

### Step 2 — In-the-moment response
Provide a gentle, non-shaming script for the moment:
- Acknowledge the person ("That's an important question / claim").
- Name the historic Christian position without attacking the person.
- Invite further conversation ("Can we talk more about this after the group?").
- Redirect the group back to the text.

### Step 3 — Private follow-up
Script a private conversation that:
- Listens first — understand where the claim comes from (a book, a podcast, a background, genuine wrestling).
- Explains the historic position clearly and charitably.
- Offers resources (not a reading assignment, but an invitation).
- Sets a boundary if the person is teaching the claim to the group.

### Step 4 — Escalation
Define when the leader should involve the pastor or church leadership:
- The person continues to teach the claim after private conversation.
- The claim is causing confusion or division in the group.
- The leader is unsure how to handle it.

### Step 5 — Leader coaching
Remind the leader: this is one of the hardest moments in group leadership. Affirm their instinct to take it seriously. Encourage them to debrief with their pastor or coach.

## Verification

- [ ] "Orthodox" is defined at creed level, not tradition level.
- [ ] Intra-Christian disagreements are not classified as heretical.
- [ ] Creedal claims are flagged verify-required.
- [ ] The response is graduated: in-moment → private → escalation.
- [ ] The person's dignity is preserved throughout.
- [ ] No fabricated church-history claims or creed texts.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

DON'T:
- Call an intra-Christian disagreement "heresy."
- Script a public confrontation or shaming response.
- Assert creed content from memory without flagging it verify-required.
- Label a person or group as heretical — assess the *claim*.

DO:
- Distinguish creedal departure from within-family disagreement.
- Provide gentle, dignity-preserving scripts.
- Escalate to the pastor when the leader is unsure or the claim persists.
