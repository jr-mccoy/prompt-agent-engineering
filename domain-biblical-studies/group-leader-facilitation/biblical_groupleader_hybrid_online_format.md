# Hybrid & Online Format

**Objective:** Help a group leader adapt a Bible-study session for hybrid or fully online delivery — addressing the practical (platform, screen sharing, breakout rooms) and the relational (how to build community, manage discussion, and read the room when you can't see everyone's body language).

**When to use:**
- A group is moving from in-person to online or hybrid.
- A leader is running their first online group and needs a format.
- A hybrid group struggles to include online participants equally.

**When NOT to use:**
- You need discussion questions (not format advice) — use `biblical_smallgroup_discussion_guide.md`.
- The issue is in-room dynamics (silence, dominance), not technology — use `biblical_groupleader_facilitation_dynamics.md`.

**Audience:** Group leaders (G).

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **Format.** Fully online or hybrid (some in-person, some joining remotely)?
2. **Platform.** What video platform the group uses (Zoom, Google Meet, Teams, etc.) or is choosing between.
3. **Group size.** How many participants (small groups work differently online at 4–6 vs. 10+).
4. **Tech comfort.** How comfortable the leader and participants are with the technology.
5. **Current challenges (if any).** What isn't working (online people are invisible, discussion feels flat, hybrid is chaotic).

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Address both practical setup (camera, microphone, screen sharing, chat, breakout rooms) and relational dynamics (community building, engagement, discussion flow).
- Provide specific techniques for online discussion facilitation (not just "ask good questions" — how to manage turn-taking, use chat, handle dead air, and include remote participants in a hybrid).
- For hybrid groups, address the inclusion gap: online participants are typically second-class citizens unless the leader actively prevents it.
- Keep recommendations platform-agnostic where possible (note platform-specific features when relevant).

### Must Not
- Recommend specific paid tools, subscriptions, or platforms from memory — note feature categories and let the user choose.
- Fabricate research on online learning effectiveness or engagement statistics.
- Assume all participants have high-speed internet or modern devices.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Confirm format and constraints
Restate the format (online/hybrid), platform, group size, tech comfort, and current challenges.

### Step 2 — Technical setup
Provide a pre-session checklist: camera position, microphone, lighting, screen sharing setup, and (for hybrid) room setup so online participants can see and hear. Note accessibility considerations (captions, chat for hearing-impaired participants).

### Step 3 — Session format adaptation
Adapt the study session flow for digital delivery:
- Opening: how to start when people join at different times.
- Discussion: turn-taking strategies (round-robin, chat-first-then-voice, hand-raise, direct name-calling).
- Engagement: how to use chat, polls, reactions, and breakout rooms for pair-share or small-group discussion.
- Closing: how to end with prayer and connection when you can't make eye contact.

### Step 4 — Hybrid-specific strategies (if applicable)
For hybrid groups:
- Designate a "remote advocate" in the room who monitors online participants.
- Position the camera so online participants see faces, not the backs of heads.
- Use a single microphone or conference mic so online participants can hear everyone.
- Call on online participants by name to prevent their invisibility.

### Step 5 — Community building
Online groups lose incidental fellowship (pre-session chat, post-session lingering). Suggest intentional alternatives: early-arrival social time, post-session hangout, mid-week check-in text, shared document for prayer requests.

## False-Positive Prevention

DON'T:
- Recommend specific paid tools or platforms from memory.
- Apply in-person facilitation techniques to online without adaptation.
- Treat online participants as an afterthought in hybrid settings.

DO:
- Provide specific online discussion techniques (turn-taking, chat use, name-calling).
- Address the hybrid inclusion gap directly.
- Build in intentional community to replace the fellowship lost online.
