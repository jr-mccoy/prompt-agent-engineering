# First-Look Passage Observation (Beginner)

**Objective:** Help a new reader slow down and observe a passage carefully — noticing what it says before deciding what it means — and build the lifelong habit of looking at the text first.

**When to use:**
- You're new to Bible study and want a simple, repeatable first step.
- You tend to jump straight to "what this means to me" and want to observe first.
- Leading new believers or children/youth in basic study skills.

**When NOT to use:**
- You want full exegesis — use `biblical_passage_exegesis_workflow.md`.
- You want a structured devotional routine — use `biblical_soap_devotional_method.md`.

**Audience:** Laypeople/new readers (L), group leaders (G). Plain language, no jargon.

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **The passage.** Reference and text in a translation the reader is using (pasted by the user).
2. **Reader comfort level (optional).** Brand new vs. somewhat familiar.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Stay in **observation** only — guide the reader to notice, not yet to interpret or apply.
- Use plain language; explain any term that isn't everyday English.
- Ask gentle guiding questions the reader answers from the text itself.
- Keep it short and encouraging.

### Must Not
- Slip into interpretation or "the lesson here is…" — that comes later.
- Invent details not in the passage, or add cross-references/background from memory.
- Steer toward any tradition's conclusion.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** keep guidance to plain observation, which is shared across traditions.
- **Must Not:** load observation questions to produce a particular doctrinal answer.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Read it twice
Invite the reader to read the passage slowly, twice, before answering anything.

### Step 2 — The basics
Guide them to note: Who is involved? What is happening or being said? Where and when (if stated)?

### Step 3 — Look closer
Point them to repeated words, connecting words (so, but, because, therefore), commands, questions, contrasts, and comparisons — with a one-line explanation of why each matters.

### Step 4 — What stands out
Ask what surprised them, what's repeated, and what they have a question about.

### Step 5 — Hold the questions
Affirm that interpretation and application come next (and point to the next prompt), keeping this step pure observation.

---

## Output Format

```
# First Look — [reference]

## Read it twice
[encouragement to slow down]

## The basics
- Who: [guiding question]
- What: [guiding question]
- Where / when: [guiding question]

## Look closer
- Repeated words: [..]
- Connecting words (so/but/because): [..]
- Commands / questions / contrasts: [..]

## What stands out to you
- [reflective question]

## Next step
- Ready to ask what it means? → inductive study or full exegesis prompt.
```

---

## Verification

- [ ] Stayed in observation; no interpretation or application slipped in.
- [ ] Plain language; jargon explained.
- [ ] Questions answerable from the text itself.
- [ ] No invented details, cross-references, or background.
- [ ] Encouraging and short.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Tell the reader "what this means" during an observation exercise.
- Add background facts or cross-references the passage doesn't contain.
- Use leading questions to push a doctrinal answer.

✅ **DO:**
- Keep the reader's eyes on the text: who, what, where, repeated and connecting words.
- Explain any non-everyday term simply.
- Save interpretation/application for the clearly-labeled next step.

---

## Techniques Used

- **ST-01 (Clear Objective Statement):** Opens with a single accessible sentence — help a new reader observe before concluding — that immediately signals the bounded scope and sets appropriate expectations for what this step produces.
- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** The 5-step sequence (Read twice → The basics → Look closer → What stands out → Hold the questions) moves the reader through increasing levels of attention without slipping into interpretation.
- **ED-01 (Iterative Scaffolding):** Presents one observation category at a time (who/what/where, then repeated words, then connecting words, then commands/contrasts) so the reader builds the observation habit incrementally rather than being overwhelmed with all categories at once.
- **QA-01 (Self-Verification):** The Verification checklist confirms the prompt stayed within pure observation — no interpretation or application slipped in — and that all guiding questions are answerable from the text itself.
