# Genre-Aware Reading Guide

**Objective:** Identify the literary genre of a passage and translate that into concrete reading rules — what to expect, what kinds of claims the genre makes, and the characteristic misreadings to avoid — so the passage is read on its own terms.

**When to use:**
- A passage feels confusing because you may be reading it in the wrong "key" (e.g., taking poetic imagery literally).
- Teaching readers to handle a genre they find difficult (prophecy, apocalyptic, wisdom).
- The first interpretive move before a full exegesis.

**When NOT to use:**
- You already know the genre and need the full workflow — use `biblical_passage_exegesis_workflow.md`.
- You need original-language analysis — use the word-study prompt.

**Audience:** Laypeople (L), group leaders (G), pastors (P). Beginner-friendly framing, intermediate depth.

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **The passage.** Reference and text in a named translation (pasted by the user).
2. **Reader level (optional).** New reader vs. experienced — sets explanation depth.
3. **Declared tradition (optional).** May shape emphasis; default is neutral. Genre identification itself is largely tradition-independent, but note where a genre judgment is itself contested.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Identify the primary genre (and any sub-genre or embedded genre, e.g., a hymn inside an epistle).
- Give the **reading rules** for that genre: what it does, how its claims work (e.g., proverbs state general truths, not unconditional promises).
- Name the **characteristic misreadings** of that genre and how to avoid them.
- Note where the genre judgment is itself debated and how that affects reading.

### Must Not
- Force one genre's rules onto another (read poetry as statute, apocalyptic as chronology).
- Invent citations, cross-references, or original-language data.
- Use genre to pre-decide a contested interpretation; route contested readings to the multi-view map.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** keep genre rules descriptive and broadly shared; flag contested genre judgments.
- **Must Not:** present a tradition-specific reading strategy as the only valid one.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Identify the genre
State the primary genre and any embedded/sub-genres, with the textual signals that indicate it.

### Step 2 — Reading rules
List 3–6 concrete rules for reading this genre well (what it asserts, how figurative language functions, what kind of truth-claim it makes).

### Step 3 — Characteristic misreadings
Name the typical errors for this genre and the correction for each.

### Step 4 — Apply to the passage
Walk the rules onto the specific passage: what they mean for reading these particular verses.

### Step 5 — Genre caveats
Note any debate about the passage's genre and how a different genre judgment would change the reading.

---

## Genre Quick Reference

Broadly shared reading conventions to draw on — descriptive, not exhaustive. Where a convention is itself tradition-sensitive (noted below), describe the range rather than ruling.

| Genre | What it is doing | Core reading rule | Characteristic misreading |
|---|---|---|---|
| Narrative | Story told through selection and arrangement | Description is not endorsement — the narrator often reports without approving | Treating every character's action as a model to imitate |
| Law | Covenant stipulations given to a particular people | Read within its covenant setting; traditions differ on present-day application | Applying ancient case law directly without asking how/whether it transfers |
| Poetry / Psalms | Prayer, praise, and lament in imagery and parallelism | Parallel lines develop one idea; imagery works affectively, not technically | Building doctrine on a single poetic image read literally |
| Wisdom / Proverbs | Distilled generalizations about how life usually goes | Proverbs state general truths, not unconditional promises | Reading a proverb as a guarantee of outcomes |
| Prophecy | Covenant indictment, warning, and hope to its own audience first | Foretelling is a subset; most prophecy addresses its original hearers | Treating prophecy as chiefly predictions about the modern reader's headlines |
| Gospel | Theologically arranged testimony about Jesus | Each evangelist selects and orders material purposefully | Flattening four distinct portraits into one harmonized blur |
| Parable | A story designed to provoke response | Locate the main point(s); details usually serve the story | Allegorizing every detail into a hidden meaning |
| Epistle | Occasional argument addressed to a specific situation | Follow the argument; reconstruct the occasion cautiously | Quoting isolated verses apart from the letter's argument |
| Apocalyptic | Symbolic visions sustaining endurance under pressure | Imagery is symbolic and conventional; traditions differ sharply on referents | Decoding symbols into a newspaper timeline |

## Verification

- [ ] Primary genre (and embedded genre) identified with textual signals.
- [ ] Concrete reading rules given, not generic advice.
- [ ] Characteristic misreadings named with corrections.
- [ ] Rules actually applied to the specific passage.
- [ ] Contested genre judgments flagged; no fabricated data.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Treat a proverb as an unconditional promise, or apocalyptic imagery as a literal timeline.
- Assume one genre for a passage that's genuinely debated without saying so.
- Use "it's poetry" to dodge a passage's real claim, or "it's literal" to flatten imagery.

✅ **DO:**
- Name the genre's signals and its specific reading rules.
- Flag genre disputes and their interpretive consequences.
- Apply the rules concretely to the passage in front of you.

---

## Techniques Used

- **ST-01 (Clear Objective Statement):** Opens with a single-sentence objective — identify the genre and translate it into concrete reading rules — setting a clear, bounded scope before the structured steps begin.
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework):** Requires analysis across multiple dimensions: genre signals, reading rules for that genre, characteristic misreadings and their corrections, application to the specific passage, and genre caveats — preventing a single-angle verdict.
- **ED-03 (Guided Discovery):** Rather than asserting "this passage is X genre," the instructions guide readers through identifying genre signals from the text itself, making the identification process teachable and transferable.
- **QA-01 (Self-Verification):** The Verification checklist confirms that reading rules were concrete rather than generic, that they were actually applied to the specific passage, and that contested genre judgments were flagged.
