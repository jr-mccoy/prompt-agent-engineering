# Reading Old Testament Law

**Objective:** Read an Old Testament legal text by its genre conventions — anchoring it in its covenant/Sinai setting, distinguishing apodictic from casuistic forms, treating the moral/civil/ceremonial division as an attributed interpretive framework rather than a given, and laying out the continuity/discontinuity debate without ruling.

**When to use:**
- Studying, teaching, or preaching a law or legal collection in the Pentateuch.
- You want the covenant setting and the law's form handled with genre discipline.
- You want the "how does this apply to Christians?" question framed fairly across streams.

**When NOT to use:**
- You need to decide the passage's genre first — start with `biblical_genre_aware_reading.md`.
- You want a full options map on one disputed application question — use `biblical_multiview_interpretation_map.md`.
- You want the general staged method for any passage — use `biblical_passage_exegesis_workflow.md`.
- The question is one Hebrew legal term's range — route the language part to `biblical_word_study_original_language.md`.

**Audience:** Pastors (P) and seminary/academic (A) primarily; equipped group leaders (G) with care.

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **The passage.** Reference plus the text in a named translation (pasted by the user). The model references by address and uses the supplied text rather than quoting from memory.
2. **The interpretive question (optional).** A specific focus (e.g., "How does this law apply, if at all, to Christians today?").
3. **Declared tradition (optional).** If supplied, the model may foreground that stream's framework (e.g., a covenantal threefold division or a dispensational/new-covenant reading) but must keep alternatives visible. No declaration → neutral default.
4. **Covenant context supplied (optional).** The collection the law belongs to, its place in the Sinai narrative, and any framing the text itself gives (motive clauses, penalties, addressee).
5. **Depth / output length.** Quick read vs. full law brief.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Locate the law in its **covenant/Sinai setting** — the relationship it presupposes between God and Israel — using only framing the user supplies or the text signals.
- Distinguish the law's **form**: apodictic (unconditional command) versus casuistic (case law, "if… then…"), and note motive clauses or penalties as the text presents them.
- Treat the **moral/civil/ceremonial division** as an *interpretive framework* attributed to identifiable streams (used heavily in some, rejected or reworked in others), not as a feature the text itself states.
- Lay out the **continuity/discontinuity debate** — how the NT and various traditions relate this law to Christians — attributing each position to a stream without ruling.
- State confidence on the central reading and what would change it.

### Must Not
- Present the moral/civil/ceremonial categories as something the text itself asserts, or as the settled answer.
- Declare how the law "applies to Christians today" as fact; route the application question through attributed streams.
- Invent citations, cross-references (including NT uses of the law), original-language data, archaeological/ancient-Near-Eastern claims, or scholar/commentary attributions; route language questions to the word-study prompt and deep background to the historical-cultural prompt.
- Collapse genuine disagreement into false consensus, or privilege a single tradition as correct (unless the user declared one — and even then, note alternatives).

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** present text + consensus; describe differing positions fairly, attributed to identifiable streams; treat doctrinal/interpretive claims as positions, not fact; label confidence on contested points.
- **Must Not:** privilege/endorse any single tradition as correct; present a contested reading as the plain meaning; smooth genuine disagreement into false consensus.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Orient
Restate the passage reference, identify it as Old Testament law, name the legal collection and its place in the Sinai narrative, and state (if given) the interpretive question.

### Step 2 — Covenant setting
Describe the covenant relationship the law presupposes and how the law functions within it, using only framing the user supplies or the text signals. Label any background by confidence and route deep historical/ANE background to `biblical_historical_cultural_context.md` rather than inventing it.

### Step 3 — Form & structure of the law
Classify the law's form: apodictic or casuistic (or a mix). Note motive clauses ("because…"), conditions, penalties, and addressee as the text presents them. For case law, trace the "if… then…" structure.

### Step 4 — Category frameworks (attributed, not given)
Present the moral/civil/ceremonial division as an interpretive framework — explaining that some streams (e.g., covenantal) use it heavily while others (e.g., various dispensational/new-covenant readings) reject or rework it — and make clear the text does not label itself this way. Attribute, do not adjudicate.

### Step 5 — Continuity / discontinuity & interpretation
State the law's meaning in its own setting first (text-supported), then lay out how the major positions relate it to Christians (continuity, discontinuity, or transformed/fulfilled), each with its strongest basis and attributed to a stream — without ruling, unless a tradition was declared. Tag claims as text-supported or inference (stream).

### Step 6 — Confidence & open questions
- Central reading + confidence (low/moderate/high) + what would change it.
- Remaining open questions and where to take them (the multi-view prompt, commentary, lexicon).

## Verification

- [ ] Covenant/Sinai setting addressed using only supplied/text-signaled framing.
- [ ] Law's form (apodictic vs. casuistic) classified; motive clauses, conditions, and penalties noted from the text.
- [ ] Moral/civil/ceremonial division presented as an attributed interpretive framework, not as something the text states.
- [ ] Continuity/discontinuity positions attributed to streams with their basis, not adjudicated (unless tradition declared).
- [ ] No invented citations, NT cross-references, lexical data, ANE/archaeological claims, or scholar attributions.
- [ ] Meaning in the law's own setting stated before any application to Christians.
- [ ] Central reading carries confidence + a change condition.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Assert the moral/civil/ceremonial split as a feature the text itself names or as the settled answer.
- Declare how the law binds (or does not bind) Christians today as plain fact.
- Read the law detached from its covenant setting, or invent ANE/archaeological background to fill gaps.
- Recall NT uses of the law from memory and present them as established cross-references.

✅ **DO:**
- Anchor the law in its covenant/Sinai setting first, labeling background by confidence.
- Classify the law's form and note its motive clauses, conditions, and penalties from the text.
- Frame the category division and the continuity/discontinuity debate as attributed positions across streams.
- State the law's own-setting meaning before application, and mark cross-references verify-required.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** The 6-step sequence (Orient → Covenant setting → Form → Category frameworks → Continuity/discontinuity → Confidence) keeps the law's own setting and form in view before any modern-application question.
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework):** Requires analysis across dimensions — covenant setting, legal form, category frameworks, and continuity/discontinuity positions — so the law is read on its full genre and canonical terms.
- **RT-05 (Evidence-Based Reasoning):** Form classification and own-setting meaning are grounded in the supplied text, and each continuity/discontinuity position must carry its strongest basis rather than assertion.
- **QA-04 (Uncertainty Acknowledgment):** The central reading states confidence and a change condition; the category division and application question are treated as labeled, contested, attributed positions.
- **QA-05 (Citation Requirements):** Cross-references (including NT uses), ANE/archaeological claims, and canonical links are given by address and marked verify-required; nothing recalled from memory is presented as authoritative.
