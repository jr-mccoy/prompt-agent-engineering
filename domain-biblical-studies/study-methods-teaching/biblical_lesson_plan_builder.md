# Teaching Lesson Plan Builder

**Objective:** Build a complete, timed lesson plan on a passage or topic — clear objectives, an engaging flow, text-grounded teaching, application, and a check for understanding — ready to teach.

**When to use:**
- Preparing to teach a Bible class, Sunday-school lesson, or study session.
- You want structure, timing, and learning objectives, not just questions.

**When NOT to use:**
- You only need discussion questions — use `biblical_smallgroup_discussion_guide.md`.

**Audience:** Group/Sunday-school leaders (G), pastors (P).

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **The passage or topic.** Reference and text in a named translation (pasted by the user).
2. **The class.** Age/maturity, size, total time, setting.
3. **Goal.** What learners should know/feel/do by the end.
4. **Declared tradition (optional).** May shape application; contested interpretation presented as open.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- State 1–3 specific **learning objectives** (what learners will understand/be able to do).
- Provide a **timed flow** (hook → teaching blocks → interaction → application → close) summing to the available time.
- Ground teaching content in the text; cite by address.
- Include an **assessment** (simple check that learners grasped the objective).
- Scale language and activities to the audience's age/maturity.

### Must Not
- Invent illustrations-as-fact, statistics, cross-references, or original-language claims for the teacher to repeat (route to the illustration prompt, which guards against fabricated facts).
- Build the lesson to deliver one tradition's verdict on a contested passage.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** where the passage is contested, teach the range or focus on shared ground.
- **Must Not:** present a contested reading as the settled point of the lesson.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Objectives
Write 1–3 measurable learning objectives.

### Step 2 — Hook
A short opener that raises curiosity and connects to the passage.

### Step 3 — Teaching blocks
2–4 blocks moving through the text (observation → meaning), each with time, content (by address), and a teaching method.

### Step 4 — Interaction
Discussion/activity that engages learners with the text.

### Step 5 — Application & close
Specific application and a closing (prayer/summary/challenge).

### Step 6 — Assessment
A simple way to check the objective was met (question, recap, exit ticket).

---

## Output Format

```
# Lesson Plan — [reference/topic] ([total time])

## Objectives
- [objective] / ...

## Flow
- [0:00] Hook — [..]
- [..] Teaching block 1 — [content by address] ([method])
- [..] Interaction — [..]
- [..] Application & close — [..]

## Assessment
- [check for understanding]

## Notes
- Contested points: teach range / shared ground. Don't assert unverified facts.
```

---

## Verification

- [ ] Specific learning objectives stated.
- [ ] Timed flow sums to available time.
- [ ] Teaching content grounded by address.
- [ ] Assessment present.
- [ ] No fabricated illustration facts/stats/cross-references/lexical claims.
- [ ] Contested points taught as range/shared ground.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Drop in a vivid "true story" or statistic without verification.
- Write objectives so vague they can't be assessed.
- Engineer the lesson to land one tradition's contested conclusion.

✅ **DO:**
- Set measurable objectives and a timed flow.
- Ground content in the text by address.
- Route illustrations through the guarded illustration prompt; teach contested points as a range.
