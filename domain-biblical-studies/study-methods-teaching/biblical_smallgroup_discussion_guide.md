# Small-Group Discussion Guide Builder

**Objective:** Produce a ready-to-use set of discussion questions on a passage that get a group genuinely engaging the text — open rather than leading, grounded in the text, and honest about where faithful readers differ.

**When to use:**
- Leading a small group, Bible study, or Sunday-school class.
- You want questions that spark discussion, not one-word answers.
- A passage has multiple readings and you want to host the conversation fairly.

**When NOT to use:**
- You need a full timed lesson plan — use `biblical_lesson_plan_builder.md`.

**Audience:** Group/Sunday-school leaders (G). Beginner-friendly.

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **The passage.** Reference and text in a named translation (pasted by the user).
2. **The group.** Size, maturity/familiarity, time available, any focus.
3. **Declared tradition (optional).** May shape application emphasis; contested interpretations are still presented as open.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Write **open** questions (not yes/no, not leading to a single predetermined answer).
- Sequence questions observation → interpretation → application.
- Scale difficulty to group maturity.
- Where the passage is contested, frame the question to invite the range of readings, not to corner the group into one.
- Include a few questions that connect the text to life honestly.

### Must Not
- Write leading questions engineered to extract a specific doctrinal answer.
- Invent cross-references, background, or original-language "facts" for leaders to repeat.
- Steer the group toward one tradition's conclusion on a contested point.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** keep interpretation questions genuinely open where the text is contested.
- **Must Not:** embed one tradition's verdict in the wording of a question.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Warm-up
One accessible opener connecting to the passage's theme.

### Step 2 — Observation questions
2–4 questions drawing the group into what the text says.

### Step 3 — Interpretation questions
2–4 questions on meaning; where contested, phrase to surface multiple readings (route leaders to the multi-view map for prep).

### Step 4 — Application questions
2–3 honest, specific application questions.

### Step 5 — Leader notes
Brief, neutral notes flagging likely points of disagreement and reminders not to assert unverified "facts."

---

## Output Format

```
# Discussion Guide — [reference]

## Warm-up
- [opener]

## Observation
- [open question] / ...

## Interpretation
- [open question] (contested? surfaces views) / ...

## Application
- [honest, specific question] / ...

## Leader notes
- Likely disagreement points: [..]
- Reminder: don't assert unverified background/word claims.
```

---

## Verification

- [ ] Questions are open, not leading or yes/no.
- [ ] Sequence runs observation → interpretation → application.
- [ ] Contested points framed to surface multiple readings.
- [ ] No invented cross-references/background/lexical facts.
- [ ] Difficulty scaled to the group.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Write "Don't you agree that…?" questions that pre-load the answer.
- Supply leaders with background "facts" to repeat that aren't verified.
- Funnel a contested passage toward one tradition's reading.

✅ **DO:**
- Keep questions open and text-grounded.
- Surface the range of readings on contested texts.
- Flag disagreement points neutrally in leader notes.
