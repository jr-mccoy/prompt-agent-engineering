# Whole-Book Overview & Synthesis

**Objective:** Produce a clear overview of a whole biblical book — how it's structured, what it's about, why it was written, and how it moves — so a reader, teacher, or preacher can see the forest before the trees.

**When to use:**
- Starting a study or series on a book.
- You want the big-picture map before studying parts.
- Orienting a group to a book they're about to read.

**When NOT to use:**
- You're focused on one passage — use the exegesis or literary-context prompts.

**Audience:** Group leaders (G), pastors (P), seminary/academic (A).

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **The book.** Which book, and the reader's translation/edition if relevant.
2. **Known data (optional).** Authorship/date positions the user holds, to organize rather than invent.
3. **Declared tradition (optional).** May shape canon placement and emphasis; contested introductory questions remain attributed to streams.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Cover: genre, structure/outline, major themes, purpose/occasion, argument or narrative flow, and canonical placement.
- Label introductory questions (authorship, date, occasion, audience) by confidence and attribute contested positions to streams.
- Keep the outline text-driven; cite sections by address (chapters/units), not invented detail.

### Must Not
- Assert contested authorship/date as settled.
- Invent quotations, statistics, cross-references, or background detail.
- Privilege one tradition's introduction as the established account.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** present introductory questions descriptively, noting where traditions/scholarship differ.
- **Must Not:** assert a tradition's preferred authorship/date/purpose as fact.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Genre & placement
Name the book's genre and where it sits in the canon/section.

### Step 2 — Structure / outline
Provide a clean outline by units (with chapter/section addresses), reflecting the book's actual movement.

### Step 3 — Major themes
Identify 3–6 major themes with where they appear (by address).

### Step 4 — Purpose & occasion
State the book's apparent purpose and occasion; label confidence and note debate.

### Step 5 — Flow
Summarize how the book develops from start to finish.

### Step 6 — Introductory questions
Note authorship/date/audience positions, attributed to streams, with confidence labels and verification routing.

---

## Output Format

```
# Book Overview — [book]

## Genre & canonical placement
- [..]

## Structure / outline
- I. [unit] ([addresses]) ...

## Major themes
- [theme] (appears: [addresses])

## Purpose & occasion
- [purpose] (confidence: ..; debated: ..)

## Flow
- [start → development → end]

## Introductory questions (described, not ruled)
- Authorship/date/audience: [Stream A] / [Stream B] (verify in [resource])
```

---

## Verification

- [ ] Genre, structure, themes, purpose, flow, placement all covered.
- [ ] Outline by address, text-driven.
- [ ] Introductory questions confidence-labeled and attributed to streams.
- [ ] No invented quotations, stats, cross-references, or background.
- [ ] No tradition's introduction asserted as settled.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- State authorship/date as fact when scholarship is divided.
- Invent an outline that doesn't match the book's movement.
- Add background detail or statistics from memory.

✅ **DO:**
- Build the outline from the book's actual units (by address).
- Label introductory questions by confidence and attribute to streams.
- Keep themes anchored to where they actually appear.
