# Personalized Reading Plan Designer

**Objective:** Build a realistic reading plan matched to the reader's available time, experience level, and goal, with daily portions (by address), checkpoints, and a catch-up strategy so the plan survives a missed day.

**When to use:**
- You want to read the whole Bible, a Testament, or a book on a schedule.
- Starting or restarting a reading habit and want it to be sustainable.

**When NOT to use:**
- You want to memorize (not read through) — use `biblical_memorization_retention_plan.md`.

**Audience:** Laypeople (L), group leaders (G). Beginner-friendly.

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **Scope.** Whole Bible / Testament / section / book(s).
2. **Time budget.** Minutes/day or chapters/day; target duration.
3. **Approach (optional).** Chronological, canonical order, thematic, or mixed.
4. **Experience.** New reader vs. experienced (affects portion size and any orientation notes).
5. **Declared tradition (optional).** May affect canon/order (e.g., which books included); follow the user's canon as stated.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Calibrate daily portions to the stated time budget; keep them realistic and consistent.
- Reference readings **by address** (book + chapters); do not reproduce Scripture text.
- Include **checkpoints** (e.g., weekly milestones) and a **catch-up/grace strategy** for missed days.
- Match the approach to the goal (e.g., chronological for narrative flow, book-by-book for depth).

### Must Not
- Promise unrealistic pace, or pack days unevenly without reason.
- Assert a particular canon/book order as the only correct one — follow the user's.
- Add doctrinal commentary; this is a schedule.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** accommodate the user's canon and order without comment on tradition.
- **Must Not:** insist on one tradition's canon or reading order.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Confirm scope, time, approach
Restate scope, daily budget, approach, and target duration; flag if the math is unrealistic and propose an adjustment.

### Step 2 — Compute the pace
Work out portions per day to finish on target, kept even and realistic.

### Step 3 — Lay out the schedule
Provide the plan by address (a representative stretch plus the pattern, or a full table for short scopes).

### Step 4 — Checkpoints & catch-up
Add weekly/section milestones and a grace strategy (e.g., a flex day, or "read the gist" catch-up) so a missed day doesn't derail it.

### Step 5 — Sustain tips
Brief habit tips (anchor time, pairing, tracking).

---

## Output Format

```
# Reading Plan — [scope] ([duration], [time/day])

## Setup
- Scope: [..] | Approach: [..] | Pace: [portion/day]

## Schedule (by address)
- Day 1: [book ch–ch] ... [pattern or full table]

## Checkpoints & catch-up
- Milestones: [..] | Catch-up strategy: [..]

## Sustain tips
- [tip] / ...
```

---

## Verification

- [ ] Daily portions match the time budget and are realistic.
- [ ] Readings referenced by address; no Scripture text reproduced.
- [ ] Checkpoints and a catch-up/grace strategy included.
- [ ] Approach matches the goal.
- [ ] User's canon/order respected.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Set a pace the user's time budget can't sustain.
- Reproduce Scripture text instead of referencing by address.
- Impose one canon/order as the "right" one.

✅ **DO:**
- Match portions to the available minutes and finish on target.
- Reference by address; add checkpoints and a grace strategy.
- Follow the user's canon and reading approach.
