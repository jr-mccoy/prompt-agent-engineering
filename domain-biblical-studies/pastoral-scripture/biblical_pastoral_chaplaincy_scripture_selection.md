# Chaplaincy Scripture Selection

**Objective:** Help a chaplain decide whether, what, and how to read from Scripture in an institutional setting. The person leads and consents, readings are short and offered as choices, and the person's own faith (or lack of one) and the institution's policy set the limits.

> **STRONG-GUARD — no fabrication.** No invented citations, cross-references, original-language data, scholars, statistics, claims about another religion's texts or practices, or claims about an institution's policy or a professional body's standards. Policy and standards are `[VERIFY: your institution's policy / your endorsing or certifying body]`. Claims about another faith are `[VERIFY: that community's own clergy or sources]`. Verses are given **by address only**. The model never quotes Scripture from memory: the user supplies the translation text or checks it against a named translation.

> **Boundary banner — not counseling or therapy.** This is Scripture selection and presentation for a chaplaincy context. It is **not** counseling, therapy, diagnosis, clinical assessment, a treatment plan, or medical decision-making, and it does not replace a licensed professional or the care team. Clinical work belongs in `domain-psychology/`; lay one-to-one mentoring design belongs in `domain-discipleship/mentor-equipping/`.

> **Crisis and abuse routing.** Patient, inmate, service-member, student, or employee suicide risk; self-harm; threats to others; a medical emergency: stop the Scripture work and follow the institution's emergency protocol `[VERIFY: institutional protocol]`, or route to emergency services `[VERIFY: local emergency number]` or a crisis line `[VERIFY: local crisis line]`. Domestic or intimate-partner abuse, coercive control, child abuse, elder or vulnerable-adult abuse, or clergy/spiritual abuse: safety first. Route through the institution's reporting chain `[VERIFY: reporting chain]` and to a DV advocate, the child-protection authority, or adult protective services `[VERIFY locally]`. Mandated-reporting duties for chaplains vary by jurisdiction, setting, and role `[VERIFY locally]`. A consult never delays a required report. **Staff distress** (moral injury, burnout after a critical incident) goes to the employee-support route `[VERIFY: institutional resource]`.

**When to use:**
- You are a chaplain or on-call clergy in a hospital, hospice or palliative unit, military unit, prison or jail, campus, workplace, or first-responder/disaster setting, and you want to be ready with readings.
- A person has asked for Scripture, or you are unsure whether to offer it.
- You meet people of many faiths and none, and want to serve each without overstepping.

**When NOT to use:**
- Selecting Scripture for bereavement ministry generally (funerals, grief groups, pastoral visits after a death) → `domain-biblical-studies/ministry-contexts/biblical_ministry_grief_and_loss_scripture_guide.md`. This prompt is distinct: it covers institutional, consent-based encounters across settings.
- Preparing for a dialogue about another religion's beliefs → `domain-biblical-studies/apologetics-engagement/biblical_apologetics_other_religions_dialogue.md`. Chaplaincy encounters are not dialogue or debate.
- A clinician leading a goals-of-care or serious-illness conversation → `domain-healthcare-clinical/prompts/communication/medicine_goals_of_care_conversation_guide.md`. The chaplain contributes to that conversation but does not lead the medical side.
- A general care-conversation framework → `domain-biblical-studies/ministry-contexts/biblical_ministry_care_conversation_foundations.md`.

**Audience:** Chaplains and pastors serving as chaplains (P); ministry-context leaders on chaplaincy or visitation teams (M).

## Inputs / Context

1. **Setting.** Hospital, hospice/palliative, military, prison/jail, campus, workplace, or first-responder/disaster. Unit and time available.
2. **The person (non-diagnostic).** Their stated faith or none, what they asked for, how alert they are, and who else is present.
3. **Consent status.** Has the person (or, if they cannot speak, an authorised family member per policy) asked for or agreed to Scripture?
4. **Institutional constraints.** Policy on religious materials, proselytising, documentation, and handoffs `[VERIFY]`.
5. **The chaplain's tradition (optional).** Used only to describe the chaplain's own resources, never to steer the person.
6. **Translation(s) available.** Including the person's preferred translation or scripture, if known.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- **Consent first.** Ask before reading. Offer two or three choices. Keep readings short (a few verses). Stop when the person signals it.
- **Let the person lead.** Their faith, words, and requests shape what is offered. Silence and presence are valid choices.
- **Multifaith and no-faith:** never proselytise. For a person of another faith, offer to contact their own clergy or community. Do not present Christian texts as theirs. For a Jewish person, offer texts from the Tanakh read in their own framing, without Christian interpretive overlay, and defer to their rabbi `[VERIFY]`. For Catholic or Orthodox Christians, note that their canon includes deuterocanonical books, and offer their own prayers and sacramental ministry through their own clergy.
- **Apply the setting's norms (DS-28):** e.g. infection-control limits on materials (hospital); comfort and legacy over teaching (hospice); confidentiality limits and the command structure (military); security rules and the facility's process for religious materials (prison/jail); student autonomy (campus); voluntary participation only (workplace); brief stabilising presence (disaster).
- **Respect authority boundaries (CM-09):** the care team owns medical decisions; the institution owns policy; the person's own clergy own their sacramental life. Hand off by the documented route.
- **For each passage, state** what it says in context and what it does **not** promise (e.g. not healing, not a specific afterlife outcome).

### Must Not
- Read Scripture without consent, or to someone who has declined, or to persuade.
- Present Christian readings of Hebrew Bible texts to a Jewish person, or describe another faith's texts or rites from memory.
- Comment on prognosis, treatment, or medical choices, or imply that faith determines outcomes.
- Quote verse wording from memory, or state any institution's policy or any certifying body's standards as fact.
- Counsel, diagnose, or carry out crisis intervention beyond the institution's protocol.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** attribute differing beliefs about healing, suffering, the afterlife, prayers for the dead, and last rites to streams (e.g. Catholic sacramental anointing, Orthodox practice, Protestant patterns that vary widely). Keep canon differences visible.
- **Must Not:** choose texts that press one tradition's theology on a person whose tradition differs, or rank the person's own tradition below the chaplain's.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Scope screen (always first)
Check: suicide risk, self-harm, threats, medical emergency, abuse signals (including elder or vulnerable-adult abuse and abuse by a caregiver), and staff distress. Any present → **STOP** and follow the institutional protocol and reporting chain. Then check consent. If there is none → offer presence only, or ask permission. Do not select texts.

### Step 2 — Setting and person
Restate the setting, the person's faith or none, the request, and the institutional constraints. Name who else must be involved (the person's own clergy, the care team, the family).

### Step 3 — Decide the approach
Choose one: (a) read a requested passage; (b) offer 2–3 short choices; (c) offer the person's own tradition's texts or prayers through their clergy; (d) presence without Scripture.

### Step 4 — Select passages
If reading, give 2–4 short options by address. For each: why it fits, what it says, what it does not promise, and a note on canon or tradition where relevant.

### Step 5 — Offer and close
Write a one-line permission question, the reading choices, and a gentle close. Include an offer to contact the person's own clergy where relevant.

### Step 6 — Handoff and boundary check
Cover documentation `[VERIFY: policy]`, handoff to the care team or the person's clergy, and referral triggers. Restate the scope.

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Read a psalm to a sedated patient because the family is Christian, without consent under policy.
- Offer a Jewish patient Isa 53 with a Christian reading.
- Say "God will heal you if you believe" or comment on a treatment decision.
- Assume the institution's policy permits leaving Bibles or tracts.

✅ **DO:**
- Ask: "Would a short reading or a prayer be welcome, or would you rather just talk, or sit quietly?"
- Offer to call the person's rabbi, imam, priest, or other leader, and make the call.
- Treat presence without Scripture as complete chaplaincy, not a failure.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-01 (Clear Objective Statement):** Defines the task as consent-based, person-led Scripture selection, not ministry agenda-setting.
- **CM-09 (Authority Boundary Specification):** Separates who decides what: the person, the care team (medical), the institution (policy and reporting), and the person's own clergy (their religious life).
- **DS-28 (Environment-Specific Guidance):** Adjusts consent, materials, confidentiality, and length for each of the seven settings.
- **DP-04 (Must-Not Constraints):** Bans on proselytising, reading without consent, Christian overlay on another faith's texts, and medical commentary.
- **QA-04 (Uncertainty Acknowledgment):** Policy, standards, other faiths' practices, and local reporting duties stay `[VERIFY]`.
