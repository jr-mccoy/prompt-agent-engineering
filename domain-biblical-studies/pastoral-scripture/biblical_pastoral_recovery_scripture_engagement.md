# Recovery Scripture Engagement

**Objective:** Help a pastor or ministry leader choose passages and a reading rhythm for someone in recovery from a substance or behavioural addiction. The texts should meet shame with grace, keep honesty and community in view, and treat relapse as a reason to keep going. All of it sits alongside treatment and recovery support, never in place of them.

> **STRONG-GUARD — no fabrication.** No invented citations, cross-references, original-language data, scholars, statistics, or claims about recovery programs. That includes a program's steps, their wording, its history, or its outcomes. Anything about a named program is `[VERIFY: program's own literature]`. Verses are given **by address only**. The model never quotes Scripture from memory: the user supplies the translation text or checks it against a named translation.

> **Boundary banner — not counseling or therapy.** This is Scripture selection and presentation for a pastoral context. It is **not** addiction counseling, therapy, diagnosis, clinical assessment, detox or withdrawal management, medication advice, or a treatment plan, and it does not replace a licensed professional or a treatment program. Clinical work belongs in `domain-psychology/`; lay one-to-one mentoring design belongs in `domain-discipleship/mentor-equipping/`.

> **Medical and crisis routing.** Alcohol or benzodiazepine withdrawal can be dangerous: route to medical care and do not advise stopping abruptly. A suspected overdose is a **medical emergency** `[VERIFY: local emergency number]`. Suicidality, self-harm, or threats to others: stop the Scripture work and route to emergency services, a crisis line `[VERIFY: local crisis line]`, or a licensed clinician. Domestic or intimate-partner abuse, coercive control, child abuse or neglect, elder or vulnerable-adult abuse, or spiritual abuse: safety first. Route to a DV advocate `[VERIFY: local DV service]`, the child-protection authority, or the appropriate professional. Mandated-reporting duties for clergy vary by jurisdiction and role `[VERIFY locally]`. A consult never delays a required report.

**When to use:**
- Someone in your congregation or ministry is in recovery (in treatment, in a recovery community, or after either) and has asked for Scripture to read.
- You want a reading rhythm that fits their days, not a heavy plan they will fail at.
- You want passages that do not shame, and you want help avoiding texts that are commonly misused against people with addictions.

**When NOT to use:**
- The person is building their own coping plan from their faith, with their therapist → `domain-psychology/client-self-use/specialty/clientself_faith_integrated_coping_plan.md`. This prompt is distinct: a pastor selects Scripture for someone else.
- The person wants to look honestly at their own drinking → `domain-psychology/client-self-use/habit-lifestyle/clientself_alcohol_use_re_evaluation.md`. That is a self-assessment. This prompt assesses nothing.
- You need a general framework for a care conversation → `domain-biblical-studies/ministry-contexts/biblical_ministry_care_conversation_foundations.md`.
- You are designing a discipler's referral boundaries → `domain-discipleship/mentor-equipping/discipleship_mentor_boundaries_and_referral.md`.

**Audience:** Pastors (P); ministry-context leaders, including recovery-ministry leaders (M).

## Inputs / Context

1. **The person and their recovery (non-diagnostic, in their own words).** Substance or behaviour. Current supports: treatment, a clinician, medication or MAT, a recovery community, a sponsor or peer. How long in recovery. Any recent relapse.
2. **What they asked for.** Comfort, structure, honesty, hope, or community.
3. **Capacity.** Reading ability, time, energy, and whether they want to read alone, with you, or in a group.
4. **Shame load.** Anything they have said about feeling condemned, or Scripture that has been used against them.
5. **Declared tradition (optional).** Theirs. It foregrounds that tradition's language and practices.
6. **Translation.** The named translation they read.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- **Validate before selecting.** Open by naming the courage it takes to ask, and the weight of shame, before offering any passage (NE-07).
- **Select shame-aware texts** across five functions: grace and welcome, honesty and confession without contempt, lament, community and burden-sharing, and relapse without condemnation.
- **For each passage, state** what it says in context and what it **does not** say or promise. A passage does not promise instant deliverance, and relapse is not proof of weak faith.
- **Describe the range of views on addiction, attributed:** some Christian recovery ministries emphasise sin and spiritual bondage; 12-step-informed approaches emphasise surrender and fellowship; clinical models describe a treatable health condition; many people hold these together. Do not adjudicate between them.
- **Affirm medication and MAT** wherever they come up, as part of the care the person's clinicians provide.
- **Treat relapse as a reason to continue.** Give a short "after a relapse" reading set.
- **Keep the rhythm small and sustainable.** One short passage a day or a few a week, with a way back after missed days.

### Must Not
- Suggest stopping, reducing, or "trusting God instead of" medication or medication-assisted treatment. Do not imply MAT means weak faith.
- Frame addiction as **only** a sin problem or **only** a disease.
- Quote, paraphrase, or describe any program's steps, slogans, or literature from memory. Do not cite outcome statistics.
- Use texts on drunkenness or self-control as warnings aimed at the person.
- Promise healing, deliverance, or sobriety by a date or in exchange for reading.
- Quote verse wording from memory or invent cross-references.
- Counsel, diagnose, assess severity, or plan treatment.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** attribute views on sin, bondage, healing, and deliverance prayer to streams (e.g. many Pentecostal and charismatic communities practise prayer for deliverance; many Reformed readings stress ongoing sanctification; Catholic and Orthodox practice may include sacramental confession and anointing). Let a declared tradition foreground its own practices.
- **Must Not:** imply that a practice from one stream (deliverance prayer, confession, fasting) is required for recovery, or present one theology of addiction as the Christian view.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Scope screen (always first)
Check for: withdrawal risk (alcohol or benzodiazepines), recent overdose or overdose risk, suicidality, self-harm, threats to others, abuse in the home, and any pressure on the person to stop medication. Medical or crisis signals → **STOP** and route to medical care, emergency services, or a crisis line. Abuse → **STOP** and route for safety and reporting `[VERIFY locally]`. If the person has no treatment or clinical support at all, recommend a clinician or treatment referral `[VERIFY: local referral options]` and continue only as a supplement.

### Step 2 — Validate
Write a short opening the leader can say or adapt. It should acknowledge the person's honesty and the shame they may carry. Do not attach a verse to it.

### Step 3 — Name the views on addiction
Give a short, attributed paragraph on how different streams understand addiction. Note where the person's own tradition stands, if declared.

### Step 4 — Select passages
Choose 6–10 passages by address across the five functions. For each: why it fits, what it says, what it does not say or promise, and a misuse to avoid.

### Step 5 — Build the rhythm
Lay out a weekly rhythm that fits the person's capacity. Include a "missed days" rule (start again today, no catch-up) and an "after a relapse" set of 2–3 passages.

### Step 6 — Boundary check
Restate that the Scripture work sits alongside treatment. List medical and crisis triggers, and whom the leader contacts.

## Verification

- [ ] Step 1 ran first. Withdrawal and overdose are routed as medical emergencies.
- [ ] Validation comes before any passage.
- [ ] The views on addiction are attributed to streams and not adjudicated. Neither "only sin" nor "only disease" is used.
- [ ] Medication and MAT are affirmed and never questioned.
- [ ] Relapse has its own reading set and is treated as a reason to continue.
- [ ] No program steps, slogans, history, or outcome statistics. Program references are `[VERIFY]`.
- [ ] Every passage is given by address only, with what it says and what it does not say. No hotline numbers from memory.

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Start with a warning text about drunkenness to someone who came to you in shame.
- Hear "I want to rely on God, not pills" and agree. Affirm their faith and send medication questions to the prescriber.
- Give the "steps" from memory, or say "programs like this work for most people."
- Drop the reading plan after a relapse because "it isn't working."

✅ **DO:**
- Put lament and grace early. People in recovery often need permission to be honest before they can hear hope.
- Name that different Christians understand addiction differently, and that the person does not have to choose a theory in order to read.
- Keep the plan small enough to survive a bad week.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-01 (Clear Objective Statement):** Defines the job as Scripture selection that supplements treatment, so the prompt is never mistaken for recovery care itself.
- **DP-04 (Must-Not Constraints):** Hard bans on questioning medication or MAT, on single-cause framings, on reciting program content, and on promising outcomes.
- **OC-09 (Capability Boundary Specification):** Sends withdrawal, overdose, crisis, and abuse out of scope to medical, crisis, and safety services.
- **QA-04 (Uncertainty Acknowledgment):** The contested understandings of addiction stay attributed. Program and referral facts stay `[VERIFY]`.
- **NE-07 (Emotional Validation First):** A shame-aware opening comes before any passage, and the selection itself leads with grace and lament.
