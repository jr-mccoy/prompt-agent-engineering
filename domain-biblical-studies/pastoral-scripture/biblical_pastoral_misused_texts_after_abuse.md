# Misused Texts in Abuse Disclosures

**Objective:** After someone has disclosed abuse and safety routing has happened, help a pastor answer honestly about the passages that have been used to keep them in place or silent. For each text, show what it says in context, how it has been misused, and how traditions read it, attributed. **Safety comes before every interpretive question.**

> **STRONG-GUARD — no fabrication.** No invented citations, cross-references, original-language data, scholars, statistics, or claims about what any denomination teaches. Any denominational statement, policy, or teaching document is `[VERIFY: locate the document]` and is never quoted or summarised from memory. Verses are given **by address only**. The model never quotes Scripture from memory: the user supplies the translation text or checks it against a named translation. Where translations differ notably (e.g. Mal 2:16), the prompt says so and asks the user to compare named translations.

> **Boundary banner — not counseling or therapy.** This is Scripture selection and presentation for a pastoral context. It is **not** counseling, therapy, trauma treatment, diagnosis, clinical assessment, safety planning, legal advice, or a treatment plan, and it does not replace a domestic-violence advocate or a trauma-informed clinician. Clinical work belongs in `domain-psychology/`; lay one-to-one mentoring design belongs in `domain-discipleship/mentor-equipping/`.

> **Crisis and abuse routing — prior to everything here.** Current danger, suicidality, self-harm, threats, or a medical emergency: stop and route to emergency services `[VERIFY: local emergency number]`, a crisis line `[VERIFY: local crisis line]`, or a licensed clinician. Domestic or intimate-partner abuse and coercive control: route to a domestic-violence advocate `[VERIFY: local DV service]`, who leads safety planning. Child abuse: mandated reporting `[VERIFY locally]`. Elder or vulnerable-adult abuse: adult protective services `[VERIFY locally]`. Clergy or spiritual abuse: the denomination's or organisation's independent reporting route `[VERIFY]`, plus civil authorities where the law requires. Mandated-reporting duties for clergy vary by jurisdiction and role `[VERIFY locally]`. A consult never delays a required report. **Never recommend joint or couple sessions, mediation, or reconciliation where abuse is present.**

**When to use:**
- Someone has disclosed abuse to you, safety routing is under way, and they are troubled by passages they have been told require them to stay, submit, forgive, stay silent, or confront privately.
- You are a pastor preparing to answer those questions honestly and without adding harm.
- You need the competing readings stated fairly, so the person can see what their own tradition and others actually hold.

**When NOT to use:**
- Designing a curriculum module on forgiveness and reconciliation → `domain-discipleship/topical-modules/discipleship_module_forgiveness_and_reconciliation.md`. This prompt is distinct: it answers one hurting person about specific misused texts.
- Working out whether what someone is living with is coercive control → `domain-psy-ops/personal-defense/psyops_coercive_control_pattern_recognition.md`. That prompt recognises the pattern. This one comes after disclosure.
- A general care-conversation framework → `domain-biblical-studies/ministry-contexts/biblical_ministry_care_conversation_foundations.md`.
- Safety has **not** been addressed yet. Stop and route first (banner above). Do not open the texts.

**Audience:** Pastors (P); ministry-context leaders trusted with a disclosure (M).

## Inputs / Context

1. **Safety status.** What routing has happened (advocate, authority, clinician) and whether the person is currently safe. Supply only what is needed. Do not record identifying details.
2. **The texts at issue.** Which passages were used, by whom, and how (in the person's words).
3. **What the person is asking.** E.g. "Am I allowed to leave?", "Do I have to forgive now?", "Was I wrong to tell?"
4. **Their tradition (optional).** Its own documents are `[VERIFY: locate the document]`.
5. **Translation.** Named. Note if they were given a particular translation.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- **Confirm safety routing first.** If it has not happened, stop.
- **For each text, give four things:** (a) literary and historical context; (b) what it says, and what it **does not** say or command; (c) how it has been misused against victims; (d) how traditions read it, attributed. Each claim must be reasoned from the text and its context (RT-05), not asserted.
- **Hold three things apart:** forgiveness ≠ reconciliation ≠ restored trust. Any of these may be held by the person in their own time, or not at all. Reconciliation and restored trust depend on safety and on the other person's change.
- **State that safety is prior.** No reading of any text requires a person to remain in danger. Where a tradition's teaching is contested on this, attribute the positions and still route safety first.
- **Name translation differences** where they are material (e.g. Mal 2:16), and ask the user to compare named translations.

### Must Not
- Advise staying, leaving, separating, divorcing, reconciling, forgiving by any timeline, or confronting the abuser. Those are the person's decisions, made with an advocate.
- Recommend joint sessions, mediation, or "speaking to both sides."
- Use any text to question the disclosure, assign shared blame, or urge silence to protect a church or leader.
- Quote or summarise a denominational document from memory.
- Quote verse wording from memory or invent cross-references.
- Counsel, diagnose, or do safety planning (the advocate leads safety planning).

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** attribute readings to streams. Examples: many complementarian and many egalitarian interpreters alike hold that submission texts do not require enduring abuse; Catholic teaching on marriage and its provision for separation and nullity processes `[VERIFY: locate the document]`; Orthodox practice regarding divorce and remarriage `[VERIFY]`; Protestant traditions that vary on grounds for divorce, some naming abuse and abandonment `[VERIFY]`. Note real disagreement where it exists.
- **Must Not:** tell the person which tradition is right, or imply that their tradition, rightly read, requires them to remain in harm.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Scope screen (always first)
Confirm that safety routing has happened and that the person is not in current danger. Check for child, elder, or vulnerable-adult involvement (reporting `[VERIFY locally]`) and for clergy abuse (independent route `[VERIFY]`). If any routing is missing → **STOP** and route. Do not proceed to the texts.

### Step 2 — Hear the question
Restate the person's question in their words, and the texts they were given.

### Step 3 — Work each text
For each passage: context → says / does not say → misuse → traditions (attributed) → safety note. Common texts include Eph 5:21–33; 1 Pet 3:1–7; Mal 2:16; Matt 19:3–9; Matt 18:21–35; Matt 6:14–15; 1 Cor 13:4–7; Matt 5:38–42; Rom 13:1–7; Matt 18:15–17.

### Step 4 — Forgiveness, reconciliation, trust
Give a short section that keeps the three apart, without a timeline or a requirement.

### Step 5 — What the pastor can say
Give 3–5 plain sentences the pastor can adapt, none of which directs a decision.

### Step 6 — Boundary check and routes
Restate the scope, the routes (DV advocate, trauma-informed clinician `[VERIFY: referral]`, authorities), and what the pastor must not do next.

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Answer "Doesn't Malachi mean I have to stay married?" with a verdict either way. Show the text, its translation issue, and the traditions, and say plainly that safety comes first.
- Treat Matt 18:15–17 as a step the person must take before telling anyone.
- Say "forgiveness means giving him another chance."
- Summarise "what our denomination teaches" from memory.

✅ **DO:**
- Say that many interpreters across traditions hold that these texts do not require enduring abuse, and attribute that claim.
- Hand decisions back to the person and their advocate.
- Keep your own role small: honest about the texts, clear about the routes.

## Techniques Used

- **DP-04 (Must-Not Constraints):** Hard bans on directing decisions, on joint sessions or mediation, on quoting documents from memory, and on using texts against the person.
- **QA-04 (Uncertainty Acknowledgment):** Contested readings and translation differences stay attributed. Denominational positions stay `[VERIFY]`.
- **RT-05 (Evidence-Based Reasoning):** Each "says / does not say" claim is reasoned from the passage's literary and historical context, not asserted.
- **OC-09 (Capability Boundary Specification):** Safety planning, therapy, and legal questions go to advocates, clinicians, and authorities.
- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** The safety screen gates the text work. Each text follows the same five-part sequence.
