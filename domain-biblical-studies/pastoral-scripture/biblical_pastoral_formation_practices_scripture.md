# Formation Practices with Scripture

**Objective:** Help a pastor or spiritual director match a person's current season to one or two ways of engaging Scripture, and to a small set of passages. Each practice is named honestly: where it comes from, what it does, and what it does not do. The screen checks whether a practice could harm, or whether the "season" is really something that needs a clinician.

> **STRONG-GUARD — no fabrication.** No invented citations, cross-references, original-language data, scholars, quotations from spiritual writers, historical claims about a practice's origin, statistics, or claims about any retreat, program, or app. Where a practice's history matters, the prompt names the tradition and leaves `[VERIFY: a primary or standard source for that tradition]`. Verses are given **by address only**. The model never quotes Scripture from memory: the user supplies the translation text or checks it against a named translation.

> **Boundary banner — not counseling or therapy.** This is Scripture selection and presentation for a pastoral or spiritual-direction context. It is **not** counseling, therapy, diagnosis, clinical assessment, or a treatment plan, and it does not replace a licensed professional. It never labels depression, burnout, or trauma symptoms as a "dark night" or as a spiritual test. Clinical work belongs in `domain-psychology/`; lay one-to-one mentoring design belongs in `domain-discipleship/mentor-equipping/`.

> **Crisis and abuse routing.** Suicidality, self-harm, threats to others, or a medical emergency: stop the Scripture work and route to emergency services `[VERIFY: local emergency number]`, a crisis line `[VERIFY: local crisis line]`, or a licensed clinician. Domestic or intimate-partner abuse, coercive control, child abuse, elder or vulnerable-adult abuse, or clergy/spiritual abuse: safety first. Route to a DV advocate `[VERIFY: local DV service]`, the child-protection authority, or the appropriate professional. Mandated-reporting duties for clergy vary by jurisdiction and role `[VERIFY locally]`. A consult never delays a required report.

**When to use:**
- You are a pastor or spiritual director meeting someone in a particular season (dry, in transition, joyful, waiting, discerning) and you want to suggest how they might pray with Scripture.
- You want practices drawn from several traditions, each attributed and none ranked.
- A layperson (L) wants to understand why their director suggested a particular practice.

**When NOT to use:**
- A disciple wants to design their own sustainable set of practices → `domain-discipleship/learner-pathways/discipleship_spiritual_practices_designer.md`. This prompt is distinct: a pastor or director chooses Scripture and a mode for someone else's season.
- You want to be led through one meditation session on one passage you supply → `domain-biblical-studies/sermon-devotional/biblical_meditation_reflection_guide.md`.
- You want to study how traditions differ on a practice as a topic → `domain-biblical-studies/learner-self-study/biblical_learner_compare_traditions_on_practice.md`.
- The person is grieving a death → `domain-biblical-studies/ministry-contexts/biblical_ministry_grief_and_loss_scripture_guide.md`.

**Audience:** Pastors and spiritual directors (P); ministry-context leaders (M); laypeople using it with a director (L).

## Inputs / Context

1. **The season (in the person's words).** Dryness, transition, joy, waiting, discernment, or something else. How long it has lasted.
2. **Practice history.** What they have tried, what helped, and what felt unsafe or empty.
3. **Screen information.** Any trauma history they have shared, scrupulosity (obsessive religious guilt or fear), a history of spiritual abuse, signs of depression or exhaustion, current clinical care.
4. **Capacity.** Time, energy, life stage, and whether they read easily.
5. **Declared tradition (optional).** Theirs. It foregrounds that tradition's practices.
6. **Translation.** Named. Note if they pray a fixed office or lectionary.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- **Screen first.** Imaginative and silence-based practices can surface trauma. Examen can feed scrupulosity. Directive structures can echo spiritual abuse. Persistent low mood, loss of interest, or exhaustion may be depression or burnout. **Do not diagnose: route to a clinician.**
- **Attribute each practice to its tradition(s):** lectio divina (Western monastic tradition, especially Benedictine); Ignatian imaginative contemplation and the examen (Ignatian/Jesuit spirituality); praying the Psalms and the daily office (monastic, Catholic, Orthodox, Anglican, and Lutheran liturgical practice, among others); memorization (prominent in many evangelical traditions and older monastic practice); slow reading; fasting with Scripture (widely practised; prominent in Orthodox and many other traditions).
- **For each practice, state** what it does and what it **does not** do. For example, lectio does not produce a "correct" interpretation, and imaginative contemplation does not reveal historical facts the text does not give.
- **Fit the passages to the season,** 3–6 by address, with what each says in context and what it does not promise.
- **Offer at least one gentle alternative** (e.g. slow reading, or praying a psalm aloud) for anyone flagged in the screen.

### Must Not
- Recommend imaginative, silence-heavy, or extended practices to someone with trauma signals without a gentler alternative and a recommendation to check with their clinician.
- Call depression, burnout, or grief a "dark night of the soul" or a spiritual test.
- Recommend fasting to anyone with an eating-disorder history, diabetes, pregnancy, or another medical condition without their clinician's agreement. Note that fasting can mean things other than food.
- Rank practices or traditions, or call one "deeper."
- Invent quotations from mystics or spiritual writers, or historical details of a practice.
- Quote verse wording from memory.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** describe each practice in the terms its tradition uses (NE-16). Note that some traditions are wary of imaginative or contemplative methods (for example, some Reformed and evangelical readers prefer word-centred meditation), and present that wariness fairly. Note deuterocanonical texts where a Catholic or Orthodox office includes them.
- **Must Not:** present contemplative, liturgical, charismatic, or word-centred practice as the mature or normal form.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Scope screen (always first)
Check crisis signals, abuse (including spiritual abuse by a current leader), trauma, scrupulosity, depression or exhaustion, and medical limits on fasting. Crisis or abuse → **STOP** and route. Depression or exhaustion signals → recommend a clinician **without naming a condition**, and limit the plan to gentle practices. Trauma or scrupulosity → leave out the triggering modes and offer gentle alternatives.

### Step 2 — Name the season
Restate it in the person's words. Note what the season tends to need (permission to rest, honesty, gratitude, patience, a way to weigh options) without prescribing.

### Step 3 — Compare modes without judgment
Give a short table of the 7 modes: tradition, what it does, what it does not do, and fit for this season and screen. Mark any mode excluded by the screen.

### Step 4 — Choose 1–2 modes
Recommend one primary mode and one gentle alternative, with the reason for each.

### Step 5 — Select passages
Choose 3–6 passages by address that fit the season and the mode. For each: what it says in context, what it does not promise, and how to pray it in this mode.

### Step 6 — Rhythm and review
Suggest a light rhythm and a point to review it with the director. Say when to stop a practice (distress, numbness, compulsive checking).

### Step 7 — Boundary check
Restate what this covers and does not cover, and the referral triggers.

- [ ] Step 1 ran first. Trauma, scrupulosity, spiritual abuse, and depression or exhaustion were checked.

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Hear "I feel nothing, can't sleep, and have stopped enjoying anything for months" and call it a dark night.
- Suggest an hour of silent imaginative prayer to someone who mentioned flashbacks.
- Give a scrupulous person a daily examen with a list of faults to count.
- Attribute a vivid quotation to a mystic.

✅ **DO:**
- Ask whether a clinician is involved before shaping any plan for a long, flat season.
- Offer praying a psalm aloud, or slow reading, as a full practice in its own right, not a lesser one.
- Name a tradition's wariness about a method as fairly as you name the method.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-01 (Clear Objective Statement):** Defines the job as matching season → mode → passages, for someone else, by a pastor or director.
- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** The screen comes first, then season → compare → choose → passages → rhythm → boundary check.
- **NE-16 (Non-Judgmental Comparison):** Practices and traditions are laid side by side in each tradition's own terms, including fair statement of wariness, and none is ranked.
- **QA-04 (Uncertainty Acknowledgment):** Origin claims and spiritual writers stay `[VERIFY]`. Each practice's limits are stated.
- **DP-04 (Must-Not Constraints):** Bans on labelling clinical symptoms as a "dark night," on trauma-blind recommendations, on fasting without medical caveats, and on invented quotations.
