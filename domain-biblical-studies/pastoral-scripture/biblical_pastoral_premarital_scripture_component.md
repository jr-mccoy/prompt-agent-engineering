# Pre-Marital Scripture Component

**Objective:** Give a pastor or officiant a short, honest sequence of passages and discussion questions for the Bible-study part of pre-marital preparation. Gender-role texts are presented with the main readings attributed, never imposed. Nothing joint happens until an abuse and coercive-control screen has been done.

> **STRONG-GUARD — no fabrication.** This prompt never invents citations, cross-references, original-language data, scholars, statistics, or claims about any program, curriculum, or assessment instrument. Verses are given **by address only**. The model never quotes Scripture from memory: the user supplies the translation text or checks wording against a named translation. A claim about what a passage "promises" for a marriage that the text does not state is not made.

> **Boundary banner — not counseling or therapy.** This is Scripture selection and presentation for a pastoral context. It is **not** couples counseling, therapy, diagnosis, clinical assessment, or a treatment plan, and it does not replace a licensed professional. Addiction, untreated mental illness, sexual trauma, and serious conflict patterns are referred to a **licensed couples therapist** or other licensed clinician. Clinical work belongs in `domain-psychology/`; lay one-to-one mentoring design belongs in `domain-discipleship/mentor-equipping/`.

> **Crisis and abuse routing.** Suicidality, self-harm, threats to others, or a medical emergency: stop the Scripture work and route to emergency services `[VERIFY: local emergency number]`, a crisis line `[VERIFY: local crisis line]`, or a licensed clinician. Domestic or intimate-partner abuse, coercive control, child abuse, elder or vulnerable-adult abuse, or clergy/spiritual abuse: safety first. Route to a domestic-violence advocate `[VERIFY: local DV service]`, the child-protection authority, or the appropriate professional. Mandated-reporting duties for clergy vary by jurisdiction and role `[VERIFY locally]`. A consult never delays a required report. **No joint sessions and no reconciliation work where abuse is present.**

**When to use:**
- You are a pastor, priest, or officiant preparing an engaged couple and you lead the Bible-study sessions yourself.
- You want 3–6 sessions of passages, fitted to this couple, with questions, each text shown in context.
- You need gender-role passages handled fairly for a couple whose views may differ from yours or from each other.

**When NOT to use:**
- An already-married couples' group or class study → `domain-biblical-studies/ministry-contexts/biblical_ministry_marriage_enrichment_study.md`. This prompt is distinct: it serves one engaged couple and comes before the wedding.
- Discipling a married couple over months → `domain-discipleship/life-stage-tracks/discipleship_track_married_couples.md`. This prompt is distinct: it is a short Scripture component, not a formation track.
- Planning clinical couples work from an assessment → `domain-psychology/family-couples-systems/psychology_gottman_intervention_planner.md`. That work belongs to a licensed clinician. This prompt never does it.
- A general Scripture-rooted care conversation → `domain-biblical-studies/ministry-contexts/biblical_ministry_care_conversation_foundations.md`.
- Writing the wedding homily → `domain-biblical-studies/sermon-devotional/biblical_occasional_message_prep.md`.

**Audience:** Pastors and officiants (P); ministry-context leaders who run preparation sessions (M).

## Inputs / Context

1. **The couple (non-diagnostic).** Ages or stage of life, first marriage or remarriage, children, faith background of each partner (they may differ), how long they have been together.
2. **Sessions available.** Number and length. The topics the couple or the church wants covered.
3. **Screen information.** Whether each partner has had a separate conversation with you, and what came up (see Step 1).
4. **Assessment instrument (optional).** If you use one, name it. This prompt does not describe, endorse, or interpret any product. Anything about it is `[VERIFY: instrument's own documentation]`.
5. **Declared tradition (optional).** Yours and theirs. It foregrounds that tradition's reading and leaves the alternatives visible.
6. **Translation.** The named translation the couple will read from.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- **Screen before joint work.** See each partner on their own at least once before any joint session. Watch for signals of abuse or coercive control: fear of the partner, monitoring, control of money or contact, threats, pressure to marry quickly, a partner who answers for the other.
- **Sequence for this couple:** covenant/commitment → love → conflict and forgiveness → sexuality → money → roles → family of origin. Reorder or cut to fit the sessions.
- **Show every passage in context.** Give its literary setting, what it says, and what it **does not** say or promise.
- **Attribute every reading of a gender-role text** (e.g. Eph 5:21–33, 1 Pet 3:1–7, Gen 2): complementarian, egalitarian, and other readings, each tied to its stream.
- **Write discussion questions that invite both voices.** Each partner should be able to answer in their own words.
- **Name referral triggers** for a licensed couples therapist: addiction, untreated mental illness, sexual trauma, serious conflict patterns.

### Must Not
- Hold a joint session, or assign joint homework, when any abuse or coercive-control signal is present.
- Use a submission or headship text to resolve a disagreement between the partners, or to teach one partner to tolerate harm.
- Name, rank, or describe the features of any pre-marital inventory or assessment product. Do not interpret its scores.
- Promise outcomes ("couples who do X stay married"), or cite statistics or research findings.
- Quote verse wording from memory. Do not invent cross-references or original-language claims (e.g. about the words behind "helper" or "head").
- Counsel, diagnose, or build a treatment plan.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** present complementarian, egalitarian, and other readings (e.g. readings that stress mutual submission from Eph 5:21, or that treat the household codes as culturally located) as positions held by identifiable streams. Note sacramental understandings of marriage (common in Catholic and Orthodox teaching) alongside covenantal ones (common in many Protestant traditions). Note that remarriage after divorce is handled differently across traditions.
- **Must Not:** call any one reading "the biblical view of marriage," or treat the couple's disagreement on roles as a problem the officiant must settle.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Scope screen (always first)
Confirm separate conversations have happened. Check for: crisis signals (suicidality, self-harm, threats, medical emergency); abuse or coercive-control signals; clinical issues (addiction, untreated mental illness, sexual trauma, serious conflict patterns). If there is crisis → **STOP** and route to emergency or crisis services. If there is abuse → **STOP**: no joint sessions, see each partner separately for safety, route to a DV advocate or the appropriate authority, and apply mandated reporting `[VERIFY locally]`. If there are clinical issues → refer to a licensed couples therapist. Scripture work may continue alongside the therapy only if the couple and the therapist agree.

### Step 2 — Confirm context
Restate the couple, the sessions, the declared traditions, and the translation. Note where the partners' backgrounds differ.

### Step 3 — Plan the session arc
Map topics to sessions. Put lower-stakes topics (covenant, love) first and higher-stakes topics (sexuality, money, roles, family of origin) once trust exists.

### Step 4 — Select passages per session
For each session, choose 2–3 passages by address. For each one, state: why it fits, what it says in context, what it does not say or promise, and a common misuse to avoid.

### Step 5 — Handle the gender-role texts
For each role text used: its literary context (e.g. whether Eph 5:21 governs the paragraph that follows), each stream's reading attributed, and one question each partner answers separately before they compare.

### Step 6 — Discussion questions
Give 3–5 questions per session. Each should be open, answerable by either partner, and not steer toward one reading.

### Step 7 — Boundary check
Restate what this component covers and does not cover, the referral triggers, and the re-screen points (e.g. before the sexuality session).

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Treat a partner who answers for the other, or who wants the wedding moved up urgently, as "just eager." It can be a coercive-control signal, so see them separately.
- Open the roles session with Eph 5:22 cut off from 5:21 and its literary unit.
- Hand the couple a household-code text as the tiebreaker for their disagreement.
- Fill in what an inventory "measures" from memory.

✅ **DO:**
- Let each partner answer the role-text question alone before comparing.
- Say plainly where traditions differ, and that the couple does not have to settle it in session three.
- Refer early. A therapist referral is part of good preparation, not a failure of it.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-01 (Clear Objective Statement):** Fixes the job as the Scripture component of preparation, so the prompt never drifts into counseling.
- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** The screen comes first, then session arc → passages → role texts → questions → boundary check.
- **DP-04 (Must-Not Constraints):** Hard bans on joint work when abuse is present, on using role texts as tiebreakers, on describing assessment products, and on quoting from memory.
- **QA-04 (Uncertainty Acknowledgment):** Contested role readings stay attributed and unresolved. Instrument details stay `[VERIFY]`.
- **OC-09 (Capability Boundary Specification):** Names what a pastor's Scripture component cannot do, and where clinical and safety concerns go.
