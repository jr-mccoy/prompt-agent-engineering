# Comparative Worldview Analysis — Biblical and Alternative

> **STRONG-GUARD prompt.** The model must not caricature, straw-man, or misrepresent any worldview — each is presented from within, as its own best advocates would state it. The model must not fabricate philosophical arguments, attribute positions to thinkers who never held them, or invent historical evidence for or against any worldview. The model must not fabricate claims about what another religion or philosophy teaches. Every philosophical argument, historical claim, and worldview attribution is verify-required against that worldview's own primary sources and named advocates.

**Objective:** Compare the biblical worldview with a specified alternative worldview on a specific question — presenting each worldview from its own best advocates with equal depth and charity, so the user sees where they genuinely agree, where they genuinely differ, and what drives the differences.

**When to use:**
- You want to understand how Christianity and another worldview (naturalism, Buddhism, Islam, secular humanism, Hinduism, postmodernism, Marxism, etc.) address the same fundamental question (origin, purpose, morality, suffering, destiny, knowledge, human nature).
- You are preparing for a conversation, class, or paper that requires fair representation of both worldviews.
- You want to avoid the apologetics trap of presenting the alternative as a straw man.

**When NOT to use:**
- You want to compare Christian traditions with each other — use `biblical_interpretive_views_comparison.md` or `biblical_learner_doctrine_self_exploration.md`.
- You want to prepare for interfaith dialogue (relational focus) — use `biblical_apologetics_other_religions_dialogue.md`.
- You want to address a specific objection — use `biblical_apologetics_objection_engagement.md`.

**Audience:** Pastor/preacher (P), seminary/academic (A).

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **The alternative worldview.** Which worldview to compare with the biblical worldview (naturalism, Buddhism, Islam, secular humanism, Hinduism, postmodernism, Marxism, Stoicism, Confucianism, Indigenous spirituality, etc.).
2. **The question.** What specific question or domain to compare on (origin/cosmology, human nature, morality/ethics, suffering, meaning/purpose, death/afterlife, knowledge/epistemology, justice, beauty, community).
3. **Depth.** Survey (broad comparison on the question) or deep (tracing the philosophical/textual foundations of each position).
4. **Context (optional).** Preparing for a class, paper, conversation, sermon series?

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Present the alternative worldview from within — as its own best advocates, scholars, and practitioners would describe it. Use named sources from that tradition.
- Present the biblical worldview similarly — from its own best advocates, noting where Christian traditions differ among themselves on the question.
- Identify genuine common ground — not forced harmony, but real areas where the worldviews converge.
- Identify genuine differences — not inflated disagreements, but real divergence points and what drives them.
- Flag every philosophical argument, historical claim, and position attribution as verify-required.

### Must Not
- Caricature the alternative worldview — it must be recognizable and fair to someone who holds it.
- Present the biblical worldview as obviously superior without argument — let the comparison speak.
- Fabricate what the alternative worldview teaches, claims, or values.
- Attribute philosophical positions to named thinkers without verify-required flags.
- Flatten internal diversity within either worldview — if Buddhism has multiple schools on this question, note them.
- Invent historical interactions or debates between the worldviews.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** present the biblical worldview as a family of positions where Christian traditions differ (e.g., on human nature, different traditions emphasize different things). The alternative worldview also gets its internal diversity noted.
- **Must Not:** present one Christian tradition's framing as "the Christian position" — attribute each to its tradition.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Confirm the comparison
Restate the two worldviews, the specific question, and the depth. Note what "biblical worldview" will mean for this comparison — which aspects of the Christian tradition are most relevant.

### Step 2 — Present the alternative worldview on this question
From within that worldview, present its answer to the question:
- What are its core claims? Who are its best advocates? (Named — verify-required.)
- What texts, traditions, or reasoning support its position?
- Where does it have internal diversity — different schools or thinkers who disagree?

### Step 3 — Present the biblical worldview on this question
From within the Christian tradition, present its answer:
- What are the core biblical texts? (By address.)
- Where do Christian traditions agree?
- Where do they disagree — and which traditions hold which positions?

### Step 4 — Map common ground and genuine differences
Identify:
- Real areas of convergence — not forced, but genuine shared ground.
- Real areas of divergence — and what drives the difference (different starting assumptions, different authorities, different anthropologies).
- Where the conversation between these worldviews is most productive and where it reaches an impasse.

### Step 5 — Identify what drives the differences
Analyze the root-level divergence:
- Is it a difference in epistemology (what counts as knowledge)?
- A difference in metaphysics (what exists)?
- A difference in authority (what/who is trusted)?
- A difference in anthropology (what humans are)?

## Verification

- [ ] The alternative worldview is presented from within — recognizable to someone who holds it.
- [ ] The biblical worldview is presented with internal diversity noted.
- [ ] Genuine common ground is identified (not forced harmony).
- [ ] Genuine differences are identified (not inflated disagreement).
- [ ] Root-level divergence drivers are analyzed.
- [ ] Every position attribution and philosophical argument is verify-required.
- [ ] No fabricated claims about what any worldview teaches.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

DON'T:
- Caricature the alternative worldview — present a version its own advocates would recognize.
- Fabricate what another religion or philosophy teaches — every claim about its content is verify-required.
- Present the biblical worldview as having an obvious advantage without argument.

DO:
- Present each worldview from its own best advocates and primary sources.
- Note internal diversity within both worldviews — neither is monolithic.
- Identify root-level assumptions that drive the differences — this is where the real comparison happens.
