# Addressing Alleged Biblical Contradictions — Honest Engagement

> **STRONG-GUARD prompt.** The model must not fabricate harmonization solutions that no scholar has proposed. The model must not invent manuscript evidence, archaeological data, or historical context to resolve tensions. The model must not misrepresent what the biblical text actually says — all texts are referenced by address and the user supplies the text. The model must not attribute harmonization approaches to scholars who do not hold them. The model must not pretend a tension is resolved when mainstream scholarship considers it genuinely unresolved. Every harmonization approach, historical claim, and scholarly attribution is verify-required.

**Objective:** Help the user engage honestly with a specific alleged contradiction in the Bible — presenting the strongest form of the objection, the major scholarly harmonization approaches, and acknowledging where genuine tension remains. Not every alleged contradiction has a clean resolution, and honest engagement means saying so rather than forcing a resolution that doesn't hold.

**When to use:**
- Someone has pointed to a specific alleged contradiction (e.g., differing accounts of the same event, numerical discrepancies, theological tensions between books) and you want to engage honestly.
- You are preparing to teach or preach on a passage that contains a well-known tension and want to handle it with integrity.
- You want to understand the range of scholarly approaches — from harmonization to acknowledging irreducible tension.

**When NOT to use:**
- The difficulty is a "hard passage" (moral or theological, not a factual tension) — use `biblical_difficult_passage_analysis.md`.
- You want to study the broader question of biblical reliability — use `biblical_apologetics_bible_reliability.md`.
- You want a multi-view interpretation of a disputed passage (not specifically about contradiction) — use `biblical_multiview_interpretation_map.md`.

**Audience:** Pastor/preacher (P), seminary/academic (A).

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **The alleged contradiction.** State it as specifically as possible — which passages, what the apparent conflict is.
2. **The texts.** Provide or reference the specific passages by address. The model does not quote Scripture from memory.
3. **Where encountered (optional).** Did a critic, a congregation member, a student, or a personal reading raise this?
4. **Doctrine of Scripture (optional).** If the user holds a specific doctrine of Scripture (inerrancy, infallibility, etc.), declare it — but the analysis presents all approaches regardless.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Present the alleged contradiction in its strongest form — as a critic would phrase it, not as a pre-deflated version.
- Examine the texts carefully: genre, context, author, audience, purpose, literary conventions.
- Present the major harmonization approaches, each attributed to identifiable scholars or traditions (verify-required).
- Present approaches that accept genuine tension — some scholars conclude that certain tensions are irreducible and explain why this is theologically acceptable.
- Let the user see the full landscape and form their own conclusion.
- Reference all texts by address only — the user supplies the text in a named translation.

### Must Not
- Fabricate harmonization solutions that no scholar has proposed.
- Invent manuscript variants, archaeological evidence, or historical context to resolve tensions.
- Present one harmonization as THE solution — present the landscape.
- Pretend a tension is resolved when significant scholarly disagreement exists.
- Dismiss the objection as "trivial" or "easily resolved" when it is genuinely complex.
- Fabricate scholarly attributions, citations, or publication titles.
- Quote Scripture from memory — reference by address only.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** present inerrantist, infallibilist, and critical-historical approaches to the tension, each attributed to its tradition. Each approach is presented as a serious scholarly position.
- **Must Not:** assume inerrancy as the baseline (or assume it is false) — each doctrine of Scripture is a lens through which the tension is addressed differently.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — State the alleged contradiction precisely
Restate the alleged contradiction:
- Which passages are in tension? (By address.)
- What is the specific nature of the tension? (Factual, chronological, numerical, theological, ethical?)
- Present it at its strongest — how would a careful critic formulate this?

### Step 2 — Examine the texts
For each passage involved:
- What is the genre? (Narrative, poetry, prophecy, epistle, apocalyptic?)
- What is the literary and historical context?
- What is the author's purpose and audience?
- What literary conventions are at work that might affect how we read apparent discrepancies? (E.g., ancient historiographic conventions, topical vs. chronological arrangement, telescoping, approximation, paraphrase.)

### Step 3 — Present harmonization approaches (verify-required)
Present the major approaches scholars have taken:
- Each attributed to named scholars, traditions, or schools (verify-required).
- The reasoning behind each approach — not just the conclusion.
- The strengths of each approach — what does it explain well?
- The weaknesses of each approach — what does it struggle with?

### Step 4 — Present approaches that accept tension
Present scholarly approaches that accept genuine tension:
- Who holds this view and why? (Verify-required.)
- How do they reconcile accepted tension with a high view of Scripture? (Various models — errancy on details with reliability on message, progressive revelation, diverse voices within the canon, etc.)
- Why do some scholars consider forced harmonization more problematic than acknowledged tension?

### Step 5 — Summarize honestly
- Which approaches are most widely held in scholarship?
- Where is the scholarly conversation right now?
- What remains genuinely unresolved — and is that theologically acceptable?

## Verification

- [ ] The alleged contradiction is presented at its strongest — not pre-deflated.
- [ ] Texts are examined with attention to genre, context, purpose, and literary conventions.
- [ ] Multiple harmonization approaches are presented, each attributed to scholars (verify-required).
- [ ] Approaches that accept tension are presented as serious scholarly positions.
- [ ] No fabricated harmonizations, manuscript evidence, or historical context.
- [ ] The summary is honest about what is resolved and what remains open.
- [ ] No Scripture is quoted from memory — all referenced by address.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

DON'T:
- Fabricate a harmonization that no scholar has actually proposed — every approach must be attributable.
- Force a resolution where scholars genuinely disagree — acknowledge the unresolved.
- Dismiss the tension as trivial when it is complex — honest engagement means acknowledging difficulty.

DO:
- Present the tension at its strongest before presenting responses.
- Include approaches that accept genuine tension as legitimate scholarly positions.
- Let the user see the full landscape and form their own conclusion — that is more honest than pretending there is one answer.
