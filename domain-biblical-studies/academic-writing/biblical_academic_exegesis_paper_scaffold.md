# Exegesis Paper Scaffold

**Objective:** Convert an exegesis assignment into a writable scaffold: a provisional thesis the passage can bear, the sections a biblical-studies reader expects (text, translation, context, analysis, history of interpretation, synthesis), the specific question each section must answer for *this* passage, and a word budget — without supplying a single source, manuscript datum, lexical gloss, or quotation the student has not verified.

> **STRONG-GUARD prompt — academic fabrication.** An exegesis paper is graded partly on its sources and data, which makes it the single most tempting place for a model to invent them. This prompt **never** originates a scholar's name, a commentary's view, a page number, a manuscript siglum, an apparatus reading, a lexical gloss, a parsing, or a quotation of Scripture or scholarship. Where a section needs such material, the scaffold names the *kind* of resource (critical apparatus, standard lexicon, technical commentary, journal article) and writes **[VERIFY: student to supply]**. Anything the student has supplied is echoed as *user-supplied* and stays verify-required.

**When to use:**
- You have an exegesis assignment on one passage (typically 8–20 verses) with a word limit and a style guide, and you need a structure before you start writing.
- You have done some work (translation, notes, a few sources) and need it organized into a paper with a defensible thesis.
- Your instructor follows a standard exegesis-handbook sequence and you want each section to do its job rather than summarize commentaries.

**When NOT to use:**
- You want to *do* the exegesis itself (observation → context → structure → meaning) before writing — use `domain-biblical-studies/exegesis-interpretation/biblical_passage_exegesis_workflow.md` first; this prompt organizes the paper that reports it.
- You need to evaluate one textual variant in depth — use `domain-biblical-studies/original-languages/biblical_language_textual_criticism_primer.md`, then bring the result into the text-critical section here.
- The paper is a multi-chapter thesis rather than a single-passage paper — use `domain-biblical-studies/academic-writing/biblical_academic_thesis_dissertation_workshop.md`.
- The task is general academic structure with no biblical text at its centre (argumentative essay, research memo) — use `domain-research-academic/` prompts, which own general academic process.

**Audience:** Seminary and university students (A); pastors writing for a degree or publication (P).

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **Passage and testament.** Reference by address, and whether you work from Hebrew/Aramaic, Greek, or English only (this changes which sections are required).
2. **Assignment brief.** Word limit, required sections, style guide (commonly the *SBL Handbook of Style* — confirm the edition your instructor requires), number of sources required, due date.
3. **Your work so far (optional).** Draft translation, notes, variant units you noticed, sources you have actually read — all treated as user-supplied.
4. **Language competence.** Which languages you can use; the scaffold never asks you to make a claim beyond your competence.
5. **Declared tradition (optional).** If your institution is confessional, the scaffold can foreground that tradition's questions while keeping alternative readings visible.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Draft a **provisional exegetical thesis** (one sentence) that makes a claim about the passage's meaning in its context, and state what evidence would confirm or defeat it.
- Give each section a **question it must answer for this passage**, not a generic description.
- Allocate a **word budget** that sums to the limit and weights analysis over background (a common failure is a background section that eats the paper).
- Reference every verse **by address**; the student supplies all quoted wording from a named translation or critical edition.
- Mark every place where a source, datum, or quotation is needed as **[VERIFY: student to supply]**, naming the kind of resource.
- Keep the history-of-interpretation section **descriptive and attributed** to identifiable streams or periods.

### Must Not
- Name scholars, commentaries, articles, page numbers, or what any named work argues.
- Supply manuscript witnesses, apparatus readings, lexical glosses, semantic ranges, parsings, or frequency counts from memory.
- Write the paper's prose for submission; the scaffold is structure, questions, and checks. (Ghost-writing graded work is the student's academic-integrity problem, not a formatting choice.)
- Present one tradition's reading as the passage's plain meaning.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** where the passage is doctrinally contested, require the paper to state the main readings and attribute each before arguing for one; a thesis may take a side, but must show it has met the alternatives.
- **Must Not:** pre-decide the thesis on doctrinal grounds or treat a confessional reading as the exegetical default.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Fix the assignment
Restate passage, languages, word limit, style guide, required sections, and source minimum. If the brief is missing, ask once; then proceed with the default sequence below and label it **assumed**.

### Step 2 — Provisional thesis
Write one candidate thesis and one alternative the evidence might force. For each, state the verse(s) and the type of evidence (syntax, structure, context, intertext, variant) that would decide it.

### Step 3 — Section map with questions
Use the default sequence — Introduction and thesis; Text (variants); Translation with decisions; Historical context; Literary context and structure; Verse-by-verse analysis; History of interpretation; Theological synthesis and significance; Conclusion — dropping or merging only what the brief allows. For each section, write the one question it answers for this passage.

### Step 4 — Word budget
Allocate words: as a default, analysis 35–40%, context (historical + literary) 15–20%, text and translation 10–15%, history of interpretation 10–15%, introduction, synthesis, and conclusion the remainder. Adjust to the brief and say why.

### Step 5 — Resource slots
For each section, list the kinds of resource required (critical edition and apparatus; standard lexicon; reference grammar; technical commentary; journal literature; reception-history sources) with **[VERIFY]** slots. Route the student to `domain-biblical-studies/theology-research/biblical_commentary_evaluation.md` to choose commentary types and to `domain-biblical-studies/academic-writing/biblical_academic_annotated_bibliography_builder.md` to log sources.

### Step 6 — Risk flags
Name the two or three places this passage most invites error (an attractive word-study, a variant that changes the sense, a background claim that is often repeated without evidence) and the check for each.

### Step 7 — Completion checklist
End with the pre-submission checks, pointing to `domain-biblical-studies/academic-writing/biblical_academic_peer_review_self_check.md` for the full self-review.

## Output Format

```
# Exegesis Paper Scaffold — [passage] ([languages]) — [word limit], [style guide]

## Assignment (as supplied | assumed)
## Provisional thesis
- Candidate: [one sentence] — decided by: [verses + evidence type]
- Alternative the evidence might force: [..] — decided by: [..]

## Section map
| # | Section | Question it must answer for this passage | Words | Resource slots |
|---|---------|------------------------------------------|-------|----------------|

## Risk flags (where this passage invites error)
- [risk] → [check]

## Integrity notes
- Everything marked [VERIFY] is the student's to locate and confirm.
- Prose for submission is the student's.

## Before submitting
- [ ] checklist → run the peer-review self-check prompt
```

---

## Verification

- [ ] Thesis is a claim about meaning in context, with stated deciding evidence.
- [ ] Every section has a passage-specific question, not a generic label.
- [ ] Word budget sums to the limit; analysis is the largest share.
- [ ] No scholar, title, page, siglum, gloss, parsing, or quotation originated by the model.
- [ ] Every source or datum slot is marked [VERIFY] with a resource type.
- [ ] Contested readings are attributed to streams; thesis is required to meet them.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Fill the history-of-interpretation section with named Church Fathers and Reformers "who held" a reading — that is exactly where invented attributions hide.
- Treat a background section as a place to show reading; it earns words only if the analysis uses it.
- Let the thesis be a topic ("This paper examines peace in Romans 5") rather than a claim.
- Offer a gloss "from the Greek" to make a section look complete.

✅ **DO:**
- Make the text-critical section conditional on the student's apparatus work.
- Tie every background claim to a verse it explains.
- Name resource *types*; leave the titles to the student and the library.

## Example Output

```
# Exegesis Paper Scaffold — Romans 5:1–5 (Greek) — 4,000 words, SBL style (edition per syllabus)

## Assignment (as supplied)
NT Exegesis II; Greek required; min. 12 scholarly sources; sections free but must include text,
translation, and history of interpretation.

## Provisional thesis
- Candidate: Paul's "therefore" in 5:1 turns the argument of 3:21–4:25 from how one is justified to
  what the justified now have and can expect, and 5:3–5 grounds hope in a sequence whose last link
  is the gift of the Spirit (5:5), not in the believer's endurance.
  Decided by: the inferential particle and its scope (5:1); the chain's structure (5:3–4);
  the causal clause in 5:5.
- Alternative the evidence might force: if the subjunctive variant in 5:1 is adopted, the passage
  opens with exhortation ("let us have peace"), and the thesis must shift from status to response.
  Decided by: the 5:1 variant unit.

## Section map
| # | Section | Question for this passage | Words | Resource slots |
| 1 | Introduction & thesis | Where does 5:1 sit in the argument, and what is at stake? | 300 | — |
| 2 | Text | Indicative ἔχομεν or subjunctive ἔχωμεν in 5:1 — which is printed in your edition, which is in the apparatus, and what do external and internal evidence each favour? | 450 | NA28/UBS5 apparatus [VERIFY: student lists witnesses]; a textual commentary [VERIFY] |
| 3 | Translation | Where must you choose (5:2 "access … into this grace"; 5:3 "boast"/"rejoice"; 5:5 "love of God" — subjective or objective genitive)? | 300 | reference grammar on genitive [VERIFY]; standard lexicon for καυχάομαι [VERIFY] |
| 4 | Historical context | What about Roman house churches or honour/shame language does the analysis actually need? Keep only what a verse uses. | 350 | background reference works [VERIFY] |
| 5 | Literary context & structure | How does 5:1–11 relate to 3:21–4:25 and 5:12–21; is 5:1–11 a hinge? | 400 | your own structural outline; one structural proposal from a commentary [VERIFY] |
| 6 | Analysis 5:1–2 / 5:3–4 / 5:5 | What does each unit claim, and how does the chain move? | 1,500 | technical commentaries (2+ traditions) [VERIFY]; journal literature [VERIFY] |
| 7 | History of interpretation | How have readers handled the 5:1 variant and the genitive in 5:5 — by period or stream? | 450 | reception sources [VERIFY]; route via the literature-review prompt |
| 8 | Synthesis & conclusion | What does the passage contribute to Paul's account of hope, and what remains open? | 250 | — |
Total: 4,000

## Risk flags
- εἰρήνη read as carrying the full range of Hebrew šālôm by default → that is a transfer claim;
  argue it from Paul's usage, or drop it.
- "Most scholars accept the indicative" → do not write until your sources show it; count them.
- The tribulation → endurance → character → hope chain read as a psychological law → test against
  5:5's causal clause before generalizing.

## Integrity notes
All witnesses, glosses, commentary views and quotations above are slots for you to fill and verify.

## Before submitting
- [ ] Variant section matches your edition and apparatus.
- [ ] Every quotation has a page from a source you read.
- [ ] Run biblical_academic_peer_review_self_check.md.
```

## Techniques Used

- **ST-01 (Clear Objective Statement):** Fixes the paper's deliverable as a provisional, falsifiable thesis before any section is planned.
- **DT-01 (Hierarchical Task Breakdown):** Breaks the paper into sections, each with one passage-specific question and a word budget.
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework):** Requires text, translation, history, literature, analysis, and reception as distinct dimensions, so no single one stands in for exegesis.
- **QA-05 (Citation Requirements):** Every source, datum, and quotation is a [VERIFY] slot with a named resource type; the model originates none.
- **IT-24 (Template-Based Educational Scaffolding):** The section map is a template whose slots teach the student what each section must contain.
