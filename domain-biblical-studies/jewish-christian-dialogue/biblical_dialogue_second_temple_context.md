# Second Temple Jewish Context for a New Testament Passage

**Objective:** Show which features of Second Temple Judaism bear on a New Testament passage — with each claim labelled by how well it is evidenced and what kind of source it rests on — so the user can read the passage within a diverse, living Judaism rather than against a caricature of it.

> **STRONG-GUARD prompt — never invent ancient evidence.** This prompt **never** supplies a Dead Sea Scroll siglum, a Josephus or Philo book-and-section reference, an inscription, an excavation, a date, or a quotation from any ancient source unless the user supplied it; where one is needed it leaves `[VERIFY: locate in …]` naming the kind of source (a Dead Sea Scrolls translation, a Josephus or Philo edition, an edition of the Pseudepigrapha, an archaeological report). It **never** quotes or locates midrash, Talmud, Mishnah, Targum, or medieval commentary from memory and never gives a tractate and folio the user did not supply. It never invents scholars, books, or positions, and never quotes Scripture wording from memory — verses by address; the user supplies the translation.

> **No supersessionist framing; no caricature.** Second Temple Judaism is not "legalistic works-righteousness", "Pharisee" is not a synonym for hypocrite, and Judaism is not a backdrop that exists to make the New Testament look better. Judaism after the first century is a living tradition, not a fossil. Christian theologies of Israel and scholarly schools may be **described and attributed**; none is adopted as the frame. Where stakes are real, check the result with a Jewish-studies scholar or Jewish dialogue partner.

**When to use:**
- A New Testament passage turns on a Jewish institution, practice, or group (Sabbath controversy, purity, Temple, a festival, Pharisees or Sadducees, synagogue).
- You suspect your reading leans on a stock picture of "first-century Judaism" and want to test it.
- You are writing on Paul and the law, or on Gospel controversy stories, and need the debates mapped fairly.

**When NOT to use:**
- You need general historical-cultural background for any passage and period (honour/shame, patronage, geography, imperial realities) — use `domain-biblical-studies/exegesis-interpretation/biblical_historical_cultural_context.md`. This prompt is distinct: Second Temple Judaism specifically, with an anachronism gate and a caricature check.
- You want to compare how a New Testament text quotes the Hebrew Bible across the Hebrew and Greek traditions — use `domain-biblical-studies/original-languages/biblical_language_ot_in_nt_usage.md`.
- You need a full background research brief with a source-type catalogue for a period or topic, not one passage — use `domain-biblical-studies/theology-research/biblical_background_research_brief.md`.
- You want how Jewish tradition reads a Hebrew Bible text — use `domain-biblical-studies/jewish-christian-dialogue/biblical_dialogue_jewish_tradition_text_reading.md`.

**Audience:** Pastors and preachers (P); seminary and academic researchers (A); group and Sunday-school leaders (G).

## Inputs / Context

1. **The passage** by address, and the user's translation (wording pasted if a phrase matters).
2. **The user's draft reading** (optional but recommended) — a paragraph, sermon point, or lesson note to be checked for caricature.
3. **Sources already in hand** — with location as the user has it. Echoed as **USER-SUPPLIED** and still verify-required.
4. **Purpose and depth:** a lesson, a sermon, a paper.
5. **Declared tradition** (optional) — to foreground familiar resources, never to suppress alternatives.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Identify which groups, institutions, and practices genuinely bear on the passage — and say when one commonly invoked does not.
- Label every claim with **confidence** (ESTABLISHED / DEBATED / SPECULATIVE) and **source type**: Dead Sea Scrolls, Josephus, Philo, Apocrypha/deuterocanon, Pseudepigrapha, inscriptions/archaeology, New Testament itself, or later rabbinic literature.
- Tag provenance: **USER-SUPPLIED**, **GENERAL** (widely held, category-level), or **[VERIFY: locate in …]**.
- Run the **anachronism gate**: any appeal to the Mishnah (conventionally dated c. 200 CE) or later rabbinic literature is flagged **LATER** and cannot by itself establish first-century practice; say what earlier evidence would be needed.
- Foreground diversity: Judaism(s) of the period differed by group, region (Judea, Galilee, Diaspora), class, and language.
- Note where New Testament texts are themselves sources about Jewish groups — and are polemical or partial ones.
- Attribute scholarly debates (e.g. the New Perspective on Paul, its critics, and later proposals) to schools, not adjudicate them.
- End with how the context changes — or does not change — the reading, and a caricature check on the user's draft.

### Must Not
- Supply a siglum, section reference, inscription, excavation, date, or ancient quotation the user did not give.
- Read Mishnaic or Talmudic material back into the first century as if contemporary.
- Describe Second Temple Judaism as legalistic, Pharisees as hypocrites by type, or the Temple as merely corrupt.
- Name scholars as holders of positions from memory; attribute to schools with `[VERIFY: identify representative works]`.
- Overclaim: background that is debated or speculative must not be presented as the key that "unlocks" the passage.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** attribute Christian readings of the passage to identifiable streams, and Jewish-studies perspectives to their schools; show plurality on both sides.
- **Must Not:** adopt a supersessionist, post-supersessionist, or any other theology of Israel as the frame, or decide between Christian traditions on law, grace, or covenant.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Fix the passage and the question
Restate the passage by address and the interpretive question the context is meant to serve. If the user supplied a draft reading, note its key claims about Judaism.

### Step 2 — Relevance screen
List candidate contexts (Temple, synagogue, purity, Sabbath, festivals, Pharisees, Sadducees, Essenes/Qumran, scribes, Diaspora, Hellenistic and Roman rule). Keep only those that bear on the passage, and say why each kept item matters and why commonly cited ones were dropped.

### Step 3 — Build each context item
For each kept item: what can be said, confidence label, source type, provenance, and the internal diversity (e.g. Pharisees and Sadducees disagreed; Qumran texts reflect one group, not all Jews).

### Step 4 — Anachronism gate
Mark every claim resting on later rabbinic literature **LATER**. State whether earlier evidence (Scrolls, Josephus, Philo, Apocrypha, Pseudepigrapha, archaeology, the New Testament) supports it; if not, downgrade to SPECULATIVE.

### Step 5 — Scholarly debates
Where the passage sits in a live debate (Paul and the law, the identity of the Pharisees, the nature of synagogues before 70 CE), lay out the schools side by side with their main evidential claims, attributed and unranked.

### Step 6 — Effect on the reading
State plainly how the context changes the reading, sharpens it, or does not change it at all. Separate effects that rest on ESTABLISHED claims from those resting on DEBATED or SPECULATIVE ones.

### Step 7 — Caricature check
If the user supplied a draft, quote their own phrasing back and flag any caricature (legalism foil, hypocrite-Pharisee, corrupt-Temple-only, monolithic "the Jews", Judaism as background to be surpassed), with a suggested reframe. If none is found, say so.

## Verification

- [ ] No siglum, section reference, inscription, excavation, date, or ancient quotation originated by the model.
- [ ] No midrash, Talmud, Mishnah, or commentary quoted or located from memory.
- [ ] Every claim carries a confidence label and a source type.
- [ ] Every appeal to later rabbinic literature is flagged LATER and not used alone for first-century practice.
- [ ] Diversity within Judaism is foregrounded; no monolithic "the Jews".
- [ ] Scholarly debates are attributed to schools and left unadjudicated.
- [ ] No legalism foil, hypocrite-Pharisee stereotype, or supersessionist frame in the prompt's own voice.
- [ ] The "changes / does not change" section separates established from debated grounds.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Cite "a rabbinic rule" for a Gospel scene without flagging that the source is centuries later.
- Use Qumran texts as if they describe all Jews, or Pharisees as if they ran the Temple.
- Present one school on Paul as "what scholars now know".
- Invent a confident-sounding Josephus reference to anchor a point.

✅ **DO:**
- Say "the evidence is thin" when it is.
- Treat New Testament descriptions of Jewish groups as sources with a viewpoint.
- Allow the answer "this background does not change the reading much."

## Techniques Used

- **RT-05 (Evidence-Based Reasoning):** Every context claim is tied to a source type and a confidence label before it is allowed to affect the reading.
- **QA-04 (Uncertainty Acknowledgment):** ESTABLISHED / DEBATED / SPECULATIVE labels and the "does not change" option keep thin evidence from being overclaimed.
- **QA-05 (Citation Requirements):** Ancient and rabbinic references are user-supplied or `[VERIFY: locate in …]` slots naming the edition type.
- **RT-23 (Input Provenance Tagging):** USER-SUPPLIED / GENERAL / VERIFY tags, plus the LATER tag for rabbinic material, show where each claim comes from and when.
- **DP-04 (Must-Not Constraints):** Prohibitions on invented sigla and references, read-back anachronism, and legalism or hypocrite stereotypes.
