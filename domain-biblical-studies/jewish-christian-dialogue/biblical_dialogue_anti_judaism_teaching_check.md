# Anti-Judaism Check for Teaching on Hard New Testament Texts

**Objective:** Read a user's draft teaching on a New Testament text with a history of anti-Jewish use, locate each place where the framing turns the text against Jews or Judaism, and propose revisions that remove that framing without softening, hiding, or rewriting what the text itself says.

> **STRONG-GUARD prompt — never invent sources.** This prompt **never** quotes or summarises a church document, council statement, or denominational resolution on Jewish–Christian relations from memory; it may **name** such documents only as things to look up — `[VERIFY: locate and read the document]`. It **never** quotes or locates midrash, Talmud, Mishnah, Targum, or medieval commentary from memory, never gives a tractate and folio the user did not supply, and never invents scholars, dates, historical incidents, statistics about antisemitism, or reception-history claims. Scripture is cited by address only; the user supplies the translation, and translation comparisons use wording the user pastes.

> **Honest about the text; no supersessionist framing.** The texts reviewed here contain real conflict and sharp language; this prompt does **not** sanitise them, pretend the conflict is absent, or rewrite Scripture. It targets the *teacher's framing*: Judaism as foil, Pharisees as stock hypocrites, Jews collectively and timelessly blamed, Judaism as "Old Testament religion" or a fossil. Christian theologies of Israel may be described and attributed; none is imposed as the answer. For high-stakes settings, ask a Jewish dialogue partner, rabbi, or Jewish-studies scholar to read the revision.

**When to use:**
- You have drafted a sermon, lesson, devotional, or teaching plan on John 8, Matthew 23, Matthew 27:25, 1 Thessalonians 2:14–16, Hebrews' contrasts, a Pharisee controversy story, or a law-and-grace passage, and want it checked.
- You review others' teaching (curriculum, a volunteer's lesson) and want a consistent, located set of findings.
- Your congregation includes Jewish family members or partners, or you are preaching near Holy Week, and you want to avoid inherited patterns.

**When NOT to use:**
- You have not written the sermon yet and need to build it from the text — use `domain-biblical-studies/sermon-devotional/biblical_expository_sermon_prep.md`, then bring the draft here. This prompt is distinct: it reviews a draft, it does not build one.
- You are preparing from assigned lectionary readings and need to weave them — use `domain-biblical-studies/sermon-devotional/biblical_lectionary_sermon_prep.md`; run this check on the result.
- You want logic and method errors in an interpretation (word-study, grammar, background overreach) — use `domain-biblical-studies/theology-research/biblical_exegetical_fallacy_detector.md`. This prompt is distinct: it checks anti-Jewish framing, not exegetical fallacies.
- You need the first-century Jewish setting of the passage before revising — use `domain-biblical-studies/jewish-christian-dialogue/biblical_dialogue_second_temple_context.md`.

**Audience:** Pastors and preachers (P); group and Sunday-school leaders (G); seminary and academic readers (A).

## Inputs / Context

1. **The draft** — full text, outline, or lesson plan, with paragraph or section markers so findings can be located.
2. **The passage(s)** by address and the translation used; paste the wording of any verse the draft quotes.
3. **Setting and audience** — congregation, youth group, classroom, written devotional; season (e.g. Holy Week).
4. **Declared tradition** (optional) — to frame suggestions in familiar terms; it does not exempt any pattern from review.
5. **Church documents the user wants to consult** (optional) — named by the user; the prompt will not summarise them.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Review the draft element by element (paragraph, point, illustration, application, prayer, discussion question).
- Test each element against the pattern smell tests below; record location, pattern, why it reads as anti-Jewish, suggested revision, and severity (HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW).
- Quote the **user's own words** back when locating a finding; never quote Scripture from memory.
- Keep revisions honest to the text: the conflict, the sharp language, and the theological claims stay; only the anti-Jewish framing changes.
- Present translation options for *Ioudaioi* ("the Jews", "the Judeans", "the Jewish authorities/leaders", or retaining "the Jews" with an explanatory note) as attributed options with their trade-offs; the user checks their own translation.
- Name church documents only as `[VERIFY: locate and read the document]`.
- Allow a **clean pass**: if no pattern is found in an element or the whole draft, say so.

### Must Not
- Invent findings to appear thorough, or flag hard biblical language itself as anti-Jewish.
- Rewrite or soften the biblical text, or suggest omitting it to avoid discomfort.
- Quote or summarise church documents, scholars, or historical incidents from memory.
- Shame the author; findings concern framing, not motive.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)
- **Must:** where a draft relies on a theology of Israel (supersessionist variants, post-supersessionist, two-covenant, Messianic Jewish, others), name it as a Christian position and note that Christian traditions differ on it.
- **Must Not:** require the author to adopt any one theology of Israel; the check targets anti-Jewish *framing*, which can be removed within many Christian theologies.

## Pattern smell tests

| Code | Pattern | Smell test |
|---|---|---|
| AJ-1 | Collective blame / deicide charge | Does the draft make Jews as a people — then or now — responsible for Jesus' death? |
| AJ-2 | Pharisees as stock villains | Are "Pharisees" or "Pharisaical" used as synonyms for hypocrite or legalist? |
| AJ-3 | Judaism-as-legalism foil | Is grace made vivid by painting Judaism as works-righteousness? |
| AJ-4 | "Angry Old Testament God" | Is the God of Israel contrasted with a loving God of the New Testament? |
| AJ-5 | Replacement language | Is Israel "rejected", "replaced", or Judaism "obsolete" or "dead" in the teacher's own voice? |
| AJ-6 | Timeless "the Jews" | Does a text about specific first-century parties become a statement about all Jews? |
| AJ-7 | Judaism as fossil | Is Judaism treated as ending in the first century, with no living tradition? |
| AJ-8 | Caricatured practice | Are Jewish practices (Sabbath, purity, Temple) described as empty ritual? |

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Segment the draft
Number each element. Note the passage, translation, setting, and audience.

### Step 2 — Run the smell tests
For each element, apply AJ-1 to AJ-8. Record only patterns actually present in the user's wording; mark other elements "clean".

### Step 3 — Explain each finding
For each finding, say concisely why it reads as anti-Jewish — what a Jewish listener, or a listener who repeats it outside church, would hear.

### Step 4 — Revise honestly
Propose a revision that keeps the text's conflict and claims while removing the framing (e.g. naming the specific first-century parties in the scene; describing an intra-Jewish argument as such; drawing the warning against hypocrisy toward the hearers rather than toward Jews).

### Step 5 — Translation note (where *Ioudaioi* is involved)
List the translation options, which kinds of translations and interpreters favour each `[VERIFY: check your translation's notes]`, and each option's trade-off. Do not choose for the user.

### Step 6 — Severity and summary
Assign HIGH (collective blame, deicide, replacement in the teacher's voice), MEDIUM (stock villain, legalism foil, "angry OT God"), or LOW (ambiguous phrasing likely to be heard badly). Summarise counts and the top revision.

### Step 7 — Further reading as look-ups
List any church documents or resources the user named or might consult, each `[VERIFY: locate and read the document]`, and recommend a Jewish reader for high-stakes settings.

---

## Verification

- [ ] Every finding quotes the user's own wording and names a location.
- [ ] Every finding maps to a named pattern (AJ-1 to AJ-8); none invented to fill the table.
- [ ] No revision softens, omits, or rewrites the biblical text.
- [ ] *Ioudaioi* options are attributed and left to the user.
- [ ] No church document, scholar, or historical incident quoted or summarised from memory.
- [ ] Theologies of Israel, if mentioned, are attributed as Christian positions, not imposed.
- [ ] Clean pass stated where earned.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Flag the word "Pharisee" itself; flag its use as a type for hypocrisy.
- Treat any mention of Jesus' conflict with Jewish leaders as anti-Jewish; the conflict is in the text.
- Pad the table with LOW findings to look rigorous.
- Summarise a famous church document because it seems relevant.

✅ **DO:**
- Distinguish the text's language from the teacher's generalisation of it.
- Show the revision keeping the edge: the warning, the conflict, the claim.
- Say "clean" plainly when an element is clean.

## Techniques Used

- **QA-18 (Domain-Specific Smell Tests):** Eight named anti-Jewish patterns (AJ-1 to AJ-8), each with a one-line test, drive the review.
- **DT-05 (Element-by-Element Assessment Matrix):** Each draft element is assessed and logged in the location | pattern | why | revision | severity table.
- **DP-04 (Must-Not Constraints):** Prohibitions on invented findings, sanitised Scripture, and remembered church documents.
- **QA-04 (Uncertainty Acknowledgment):** Translation options carry trade-offs and verify slots; LOW severity marks ambiguous phrasing rather than certain harm.
- **RT-05 (Evidence-Based Reasoning):** Every finding is grounded in the user's quoted wording and a named pattern, with a clean-pass valve when evidence is absent.
