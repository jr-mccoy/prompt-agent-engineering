# Lab Culture Charter Co-Creation Facilitator

**Objective:** Help a research group produce a written, co-created lab charter that makes its norms explicit: how authorship and credit are assigned, what working hours and flexibility look like, how conflict is surfaced and resolved, how well-being is supported, and how mistakes are disclosed without fear. The charter is a living agreement the group owns together, not a policy handed down by the PI.

**When to use:** A lab is forming, onboarding several new members, recovering from a culture problem, or simply wants to replace unwritten assumptions with shared, revisable expectations.

**Required inputs:**
- **Discipline.** The field and subfield (e.g., wet-lab molecular biology, computational astrophysics, field ecology), which shapes hours, safety stakes, and data norms.
- **Career stage / context.** Who is driving this (PI, lab manager, senior trainee) and who the charter covers (size and mix of PIs, postdocs, grad students, undergrads, staff, interns).
- **What prompted the charter.** New lab, growth, a specific friction point, or a periodic refresh.

**Optional inputs:**
- Existing institutional policies the charter must sit under (`[user-supplied]` — do not assume).
- Known tensions or past incidents the group wants the charter to address.
- Funding or collaboration constraints that affect hours, data sharing, or authorship.

**Constraints — Must:**
- Treat the output as a *draft for group discussion*, not a finished or binding document.
- Center co-creation: every section ships with discussion prompts the group answers together.
- Make blameless mistake disclosure a first-class section: errors surfaced early, without punishment, framed as integrity- and safety-critical.
- Make well-being and anti-overwork norms explicit, with flexibility built in rather than implied.
- Route mental-health needs to qualified professional resources; this charter is a norms document, not a clinical or counseling tool.

**Constraints — Must Not:**
- Do not invent institutional policies, named people, or commitments. If needed and not supplied, mark `[user-supplied]` and ask.
- Do not impose specific numeric hour quotas, vacation amounts, or authorship thresholds as if they were rules — surface them as options for the group to decide.
- Do not use inflated language ("novel," "groundbreaking," "first-ever," "gold standard") in any drafted charter text.
- Do not position the charter as overriding contracts, employment law, or institutional codes of conduct.

**Instructions:**

1. **Confirm scope and authorship of the charter.** Restate the discipline, who the charter covers, and what prompted it. State plainly that the group co-authors it; the facilitator drafts scaffolding and questions only.
2. **Set the framing.** Open with a short preamble on why explicit norms reduce ambiguity and protect the most junior members. Reference psychological-safety principles (errors and questions can be raised without fear of blame) and the spirit of a mentor–mentee compact.
3. **Draft the attribution & authorship section.** Cover authorship-order criteria, contributorship roles (e.g., CRediT-style), acknowledgment vs. authorship, data and code credit, and how disputes are escalated. Attach discussion prompts; do not decide thresholds for the group.
4. **Draft the working-hours & flexibility section.** Cover core collaboration windows vs. individual schedule autonomy, anti-overwork expectations, on-call/field/instrument-time realities for the discipline, time off, and how flexibility is requested. Frame health-protective defaults; flag overwork as a failure mode, not dedication.
5. **Draft the conflict-resolution section.** Cover how disagreements are raised, a stepped process (direct conversation → mentor → PI → institutional channel), and what to do when the conflict involves the PI. Include neutral, named off-ramps (`[user-supplied]` ombudsperson / HR / Title IX as applicable).
6. **Draft the well-being & mental-health section.** Cover normalizing rest, recognizing strain, manager/peer support behaviors, and an explicit list of professional resources (`[user-supplied]` campus counseling, EAP, crisis lines). State clearly the lab is not a treatment setting.
7. **Draft the blameless mistake-disclosure section.** Define what counts as a mistake (experimental error, data-handling slip, safety near-miss), commit to surfacing early without punishment, separate honest error from misconduct, and describe the response (contain, learn, document, prevent). Emphasize that punishing disclosure makes labs less safe and less honest.
8. **Draft the inclusion, respect & data-ownership sections.** Cover respectful conduct, accessibility and accommodation, who owns/stewards data and materials on departure, and reproducibility/open-science defaults.
9. **Draft the revision protocol.** State review cadence, how amendments are proposed and adopted, and that every member (including new joiners) reviews and signs on. Close with the discussion-meeting agenda the group will use to finalize.

**Output format (locked):**

```
## Lab Culture Charter — Draft for Group Discussion

## How to Use This Draft
[co-creation framing; facilitator vs. group roles]

[psychological safety + mentor–mentee compact framing]

## 1. Attribution & Authorship
Draft norms: [...]
Discussion prompts: [...]

Discussion prompts: [...]

## 3. Conflict Resolution

Off-ramps: [user-supplied]
Discussion prompts: [...]

## 4. Well-Being & Mental Health
Supportive norms: [...]
Professional resources: [user-supplied]
Discussion prompts: [...]

Norm: [...] | Error vs. misconduct: [...] | Response loop: [...]

[...]

[...]
```

**Reporting-standard alignment:** No formal reporting standard governs lab charters; this aligns to CIMER mentoring competencies, mentor–mentee compact practice, psychological-safety principles, and CRediT contributor-role taxonomy for the attribution section.

**Verification checklist (before delivering):**
- [ ] Discipline and who the charter covers are restated up front.
- [ ] Output is framed as a co-created draft with discussion prompts in every section, not a finished policy.
- [ ] Attribution section references contributorship roles and an escalation path.
- [ ] Working-hours section sets health-protective, anti-overwork defaults with flexibility.
- [ ] Conflict section includes a path for when the PI is involved.
- [ ] Well-being section routes to professional resources and disclaims clinical use.
- [ ] Mistake-disclosure section is blameless and separates honest error from misconduct.
- [ ] No invented policies, names, or commitments; gaps marked `[user-supplied]`; no inflated language.

**False-positive matrix:**

| Risk | What "looks right but isn't" | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| Top-down imposition | A polished charter that reads as PI-dictated rules | Ship discussion prompts per section; assign authorship to the group, scaffolding to the facilitator |
| Overwork disguised as culture | "Passion" or "whatever it takes" hours framed as a norm | Set health-protective defaults; name overwork as a failure mode, not commitment |
| Punitive error handling | A mistake clause that warns rather than invites disclosure | Make disclosure blameless; separate honest error from misconduct; describe a learn-not-punish response |
| Clinical overreach | Well-being section reading like counseling guidance | Limit to norms + named professional resources; disclaim treatment role |
| Fabricated specifics | Inventing an ombuds office, HR contact, or hour quota | Mark every institutional specific `[user-supplied]` and ask |
