# Literature Review Synthesizer

**Objective:** Systematically analyze a collection of research papers on a specified topic, extracting methodologies, key findings, contradictions, and gaps. Generate a structured synthesis with citation mapping and research frontier identification.

**Instructions:**

1. **Define the scope and search parameters**
   * Request the specific research topic or question to be synthesized
   * Clarify temporal boundaries (e.g., last 10 years, seminal works from any period)
   * Identify the target disciplines or interdisciplinary scope
   * Establish inclusion/exclusion criteria for papers:
     - Study types (empirical, theoretical, review, meta-analysis)
     - Quality thresholds (peer-reviewed, impact factor, citation count)
     - Methodological requirements (quantitative, qualitative, mixed methods)
   * Confirm: "Based on your scope of [topic] covering [time period] in [disciplines], I will analyze papers meeting [criteria]. Please confirm or adjust."

2. **Create the paper intake framework**
   * For each paper to be analyzed, extract systematically:
     - **Citation:** Full bibliographic information
     - **Research Question(s):** Primary and secondary questions addressed
     - **Theoretical Framework:** Underlying theories or models used
     - **Methodology:** Design, sample, measures, analysis approach
     - **Key Findings:** Main results with effect sizes/significance where applicable
     - **Limitations Acknowledged:** Self-reported weaknesses
     - **Contribution Claimed:** What authors say they add to the field
   * Use consistent extraction format to enable cross-paper comparison
   * Note: "If analyzing papers provided by the user, request them to be shared or summarized. If discussing a field generally, rely on known literature with appropriate uncertainty acknowledgment."

3. **Analyze methodologies across studies**
   * Categorize methodological approaches used in the literature:
     - Research designs (experimental, correlational, longitudinal, cross-sectional)
     - Sampling strategies (random, convenience, purposive, snowball)
     - Measurement instruments and their psychometric properties
     - Analytical techniques (statistical methods, qualitative approaches)
   * Assess methodological trends:
     - Dominant approaches in the field
     - Emerging methodological innovations
     - Methodological limitations shared across studies
   * Identify methodological gaps:
     - Underutilized designs that could advance the field
     - Measurement challenges not yet addressed
     - Analysis approaches that could yield new insights

4. **Synthesize findings thematically**
   * Identify major themes across the literature:
     - Convergent findings (areas of agreement)
     - Divergent findings (contradictions requiring explanation)
     - Conditional findings (results that depend on moderators or context)
   * For each theme, construct a synthesis table:
     | Theme | Supporting Studies | Contradicting Studies | Conditions/Moderators | Evidence Strength |
     |-------|-------------------|----------------------|----------------------|-------------------|
   * Explain contradictions through:
     - Methodological differences
     - Sample/population differences
     - Temporal or contextual factors
     - Theoretical lens differences
   * Rate evidence strength using criteria:
     - **Strong:** Multiple high-quality studies with consistent findings
     - **Moderate:** Some high-quality studies or consistent lower-quality studies
     - **Limited:** Few studies or inconsistent findings
     - **Emerging:** New area with preliminary evidence only

5. **Identify research gaps and opportunities**
   * Systematically map gaps across dimensions:
     - **Empirical gaps:** Questions not yet studied
     - **Methodological gaps:** Approaches not yet applied
     - **Theoretical gaps:** Unexplained phenomena or untested predictions
     - **Population gaps:** Groups not yet studied
     - **Context gaps:** Settings or conditions not yet examined
   * Assess each gap for:
     - Scientific importance (theoretical advancement potential)
     - Practical importance (real-world application potential)
     - Feasibility (can it be addressed with current methods?)
     - Novelty (how much would addressing it advance the field?)
   * Prioritize gaps: "The most promising research opportunities appear to be [gap 1] because [rationale], followed by [gap 2]..."

6. **Map the research frontier**
   * Identify the current state of the field:
     - What is now established knowledge?
     - What is still contested?
     - What are the emerging questions?
   * Trace theoretical evolution:
     - Foundational theories and their development
     - Recent theoretical innovations
     - Unresolved theoretical debates
   * Predict future directions:
     - Where is the field heading based on current trajectories?
     - What methodological advances might enable new questions?
     - What interdisciplinary connections are emerging?
   * Note: "Predictions about field direction carry inherent uncertainty and reflect interpretation of current trends."

7. **Generate the synthesis output**
   * Produce a structured literature review containing:
     - Executive summary of key findings
     - Thematic analysis with evidence ratings
     - Methodological critique across studies
     - Gap analysis with prioritization
     - Research frontier map
     - Implications for future research
   * Include citation network visualization guidance:
     - Key foundational papers that influenced the field
     - Citation clusters and schools of thought
     - Bridge papers connecting different areas
   * Conclude with: "This synthesis is based on [X papers / general field knowledge] and represents the state of the literature as of [date]. Additional papers or more recent publications may alter these conclusions."

**Science-Specific Considerations:**
- All claims must be traceable to specific sources or clearly marked as interpretation
- Distinguish between what studies found (data) and what authors claimed (interpretation)
- Acknowledge that this synthesis represents one reading of the literature—other interpretations exist
- Be explicit about the search strategy's limitations and potential for missing relevant work
- Note publication bias: positive results are more likely published than null findings
- Recognize disciplinary conventions: what counts as "strong evidence" varies by field
- When contradictions exist, avoid prematurely resolving them without sufficient evidence

**Expected Output:** A comprehensive literature synthesis containing:
1. Scope confirmation and inclusion criteria
2. Paper extraction summaries (if specific papers provided)
3. Methodological analysis across the literature
4. Thematic synthesis table with evidence ratings
5. Gap analysis with prioritized research opportunities
6. Research frontier map with future directions
7. Limitations of this synthesis

**Example Output:**

---

## Literature Synthesis: [Topic]

### Scope
This synthesis covers [X] papers on [topic] from [years], focusing on [disciplines] and including [study types]. Inclusion criteria: [criteria].

### Methodological Landscape

**Dominant Approaches:**
- 65% of studies used cross-sectional survey designs
- Self-report measures predominate (80% of studies)
- Regression-based analyses most common

**Methodological Gaps:**
- Few longitudinal studies (15%) limit causal inference
- Limited use of objective behavioral measures
- Qualitative approaches underrepresented

### Thematic Synthesis

| Theme | Finding Summary | Evidence Strength | Key Studies |
|-------|-----------------|-------------------|-------------|
| Theme 1 | [Summary] | Strong | Smith, 2020; Jones, 2021 |
| Theme 2 | [Summary] | Moderate | Brown, 2019 |
| Theme 3 | [Summary: Contradictory] | Limited | Davis 2022 vs. Wilson 2021 |

**Explanation of Contradictions:**
Theme 3 shows divergent findings likely attributable to [methodological differences / sample differences / contextual factors]. Specifically...

### Research Gaps

**High Priority Gaps:**
1. **[Gap 1]:** [Description]. Important because [rationale]. Feasibility: [assessment].
2. **[Gap 2]:** [Description]. Important because [rationale]. Feasibility: [assessment].

**Lower Priority Gaps:**
3. [Gap 3]: [Brief description]

### Research Frontier

**Established:**
- [Finding now considered established]

**Contested:**
- [Ongoing debate]

**Emerging:**
- [New direction gaining attention]

### Synthesis Limitations
- This analysis relies on [general field knowledge / X specific papers provided]
- Search strategy may have missed relevant work in [related field / non-English sources]
- Interpretation reflects one reading; alternative framings exist

---

**Techniques Used:**
- **RT-05 (Evidence-Based Reasoning):** All claims linked to sources; evidence strength explicitly rated
- **QA-05 (Citation Requirements):** Systematic citation tracking and attribution throughout
- **DT-02 (Specific Focus Areas):** Detailed enumeration of what to extract from each paper
- **DT-04 (Layered Analysis):** Both micro (individual paper) and macro (field-level trends) analysis
- **CM-03 (Scope Definition):** Clear boundaries on what is and isn't included in the synthesis
