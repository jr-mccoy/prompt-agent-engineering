**Purpose:** Design proactive behavior support strategies, social-emotional learning (SEL) activities, behavior intervention plans, and restorative practice conversation guides — building classroom communities where students develop self-regulation, empathy, and responsible decision-making.

**When to use:** Addressing persistent behavioral challenges; integrating SEL into daily routines; preparing for restorative conversations after conflicts; designing positive behavior systems; building classroom community at the start of the year; supporting students through difficult transitions.

**Input needed:**
- Support type (proactive SEL, behavior intervention, restorative practice, community building)
- Grade level
- Specific behavioral concern or SEL focus area
- Classroom context

## Instructions

Generate behavior support and SEL resources through these structured phases:

### Step 1: Identify the Support Level

**MTSS/PBIS Tier Alignment:**

| Tier | Population | Focus | Examples |
|------|-----------|-------|----------|
| **Tier 1 — Universal** | All students (80%) | Proactive, prevention-focused | Morning meetings, SEL lessons, classroom expectations, community builders |
| **Tier 2 — Targeted** | Some students (15%) | Small-group support | Check-in/check-out, social skills groups, mentoring, behavior contracts |
| **Tier 3 — Intensive** | Few students (5%) | Individualized intervention | Behavior intervention plans (BIP), functional behavior assessment referral, wraparound services |

### Step 2: Proactive SEL Activities (Tier 1)

**Daily SEL Integration Options:**

**Morning Check-In (5 minutes):**

| Format | Description | Best For |
|--------|-------------|----------|
| **Mood Meter** | Students place name/magnet on feeling chart (zones: blue=low energy/sad, green=calm/ready, yellow=excited/anxious, red=angry/frustrated) | All grades; builds emotional vocabulary |
| **Rose, Thorn, Bud** | Share one good thing (rose), one challenge (thorn), one hope (bud) | Grades 2+; builds community |
| **Feelings Check** | "On a scale of 1-5, how are you feeling? Hold up fingers." | All grades; quick pulse |
| **Gratitude Round** | "Share one thing you're grateful for today." | Grades 1+; positive framing |

**SEL Mini-Lessons (10-15 minutes):**

| CASEL Competency | Activity | Grade Band | Time |
|-----------------|----------|-----------|------|
| **Self-Awareness** | "Feelings Detective" — Read scenario, identify emotion, describe body signals | K-2 | 10 min |
| **Self-Awareness** | Personal strengths inventory — List 5 strengths, get peer confirmation | 3-5 | 15 min |
| **Self-Management** | Breathing exercises — practice 3 strategies (belly breathing, 4-7-8, star breathing) | K-5 | 10 min |
| **Self-Management** | Goal-setting workshop — Set one academic and one personal goal with action steps | 3-8 | 15 min |
| **Social Awareness** | Perspective-taking scenarios — "How would you feel if...?" discussion | K-5 | 10 min |
| **Social Awareness** | Community needs investigation — Research a local issue, propose how to help | 6-12 | 20 min |
| **Relationship Skills** | Active listening practice — Partner share with reflection ("I heard you say...") | 2-8 | 10 min |
| **Relationship Skills** | Conflict resolution role-play — Practice I-statements with common scenarios | 3-8 | 15 min |
| **Responsible Decision-Making** | Consequence mapping — "If I choose X, then..." branching diagram | 2-5 | 10 min |
| **Responsible Decision-Making** | Ethical dilemma discussion — Age-appropriate dilemmas with no "right" answer | 6-12 | 15 min |

**Closing / Reflection (5 minutes):**
- "What's one thing you did today that you're proud of?"
- "Name one classmate who helped you or was kind today."
- "What's one thing you want to work on tomorrow?"

### Step 3: Behavior Intervention Plan (Tier 2/3)

**ABC Analysis Framework:**

Before designing interventions, analyze the behavior pattern:

| Component | Questions | Example |
|-----------|----------|---------|
| **Antecedent** (What happens before) | When does the behavior occur? During which activities? With which people? After what triggers? | "Student calls out during whole-group instruction, especially when asked to read aloud." |
| **Behavior** (Observable, measurable) | What exactly does the student do? How often? How long? How intense? | "Calls out 8-10 times per hour. Behavior lasts 1-2 seconds each time." |
| **Consequence** (What happens after) | What is the current response? Does the behavior get attention, avoid tasks, gain access to something? | "Teacher redirects verbally → student stops briefly → behavior resumes." |

**Function of Behavior:**

| Function | The student's behavior helps them... | Intervention Approach |
|----------|-------------------------------------|----------------------|
| **Attention** | Get social attention (peer or adult) | Teach replacement: raise hand. Increase positive attention for desired behavior. |
| **Escape/Avoidance** | Avoid a task, situation, or person | Modify task (scaffold, chunk). Teach coping skills. Offer appropriate breaks. |
| **Tangible** | Gain access to an object or activity | Earn preferred items/activities through desired behavior. Token economy. |
| **Sensory** | Meet a sensory need (movement, stimulation) | Provide sensory tools, movement breaks, fidgets. Teach self-regulation. |

**Behavior Intervention Plan Template:**

| Section | Content |
|---------|---------|
| **Target behavior** | [Specific, observable, measurable description] |
| **Replacement behavior** | [What the student should do instead — must serve the same function] |
| **Prevention strategies** | [Antecedent modifications to reduce triggers] |
| **Teaching strategies** | [How to explicitly teach the replacement behavior] |
| **Reinforcement** | [How desired behavior will be acknowledged — specific, immediate, consistent] |
| **Response to behavior** | [What adults do when the target behavior occurs — brief, calm, consistent] |
| **Data collection** | [How to track — frequency count, interval recording, duration] |
| **Review schedule** | [When to evaluate effectiveness — typically 2-4 weeks] |

### Step 4: Restorative Practice Guides

**Restorative Conversation Framework (1:1 with student):**

| Phase | Questions | Purpose |
|-------|----------|---------|
| **1. What happened?** | "Tell me what happened from your perspective." | Hear the student's story without judgment |
| **2. What were you thinking?** | "What were you thinking at the time?" | Understand thought process |
| **3. Who was affected?** | "Who has been affected by what happened? How?" | Build empathy and awareness |
| **4. What do you need?** | "What do you think needs to happen to make things right?" | Student ownership of repair |
| **5. What will you do?** | "What will you do differently next time?" | Forward-looking commitment |

**Restorative Circle Framework (group/class):**

| Step | Activity | Time |
|------|----------|------|
| 1. Opening | Pass talking piece. Each person shares one word about how they're feeling. | 3 min |
| 2. Topic introduction | Facilitator names the issue neutrally: "We're here to talk about..." | 1 min |
| 3. Rounds | Each person answers the prompt in turn. No interrupting. | 10-15 min |
| 4. Discussion | Open dialogue about what was heard. | 5-10 min |
| 5. Agreement | Group agrees on next steps or commitments. | 3 min |
| 6. Closing | Each person shares one word about how they're feeling now. | 2 min |

**Restorative vs. Punitive:**

| Punitive Approach | Restorative Alternative |
|-------------------|------------------------|
| "Go to the office" | "Let's talk about what happened and how to make it right" |
| Loss of recess | "During recess, let's work together on a plan" |
| Behavior chart (public shaming) | Private check-in with student |
| "You're in trouble" | "I noticed [behavior]. Help me understand what was going on." |
| Detention | Restorative conversation + repair action |

### Step 5: De-escalation Strategies

**When a student is in crisis — a step-by-step guide:**

| Level | Student Signals | Teacher Response |
|-------|----------------|------------------|
| **1. Anxiety** | Fidgeting, distracted, withdrawal | Proximity, quiet check-in: "I notice you seem [feeling]. What do you need?" |
| **2. Frustration** | Refusal, arguing, shutting down | Offer choice: "Would you like to take a break or try a different approach?" Validate: "I can see this is frustrating." |
| **3. Escalation** | Yelling, throwing materials, leaving seat | Stay calm. Lower your voice. Give space. "I want to help. I'm going to give you a minute." Remove audience if possible. |
| **4. Crisis** | Danger to self or others | Ensure safety. Clear the room if needed. Call for support. Do NOT physically restrain unless trained and authorized. |
| **5. Recovery** | Calm returning, quiet | Give time. Offer water. Brief check-in. Do NOT process the incident immediately — wait until fully calm. |

**De-escalation Principles:**
- Stay calm yourself (your regulation regulates them)
- Speak slowly, quietly, and briefly
- Avoid power struggles ("You need to sit down NOW")
- Offer limited choices, not ultimatums
- Validate the emotion, set the boundary: "It's okay to be angry. It's not okay to throw things."
- Process AFTER the student is fully calm (often 20+ minutes)

### Step 6: Positive Classroom Systems

**PBIS-Aligned Reward System Design:**

| Element | Options |
|---------|---------|
| **Expectations** | 3-5 positively stated rules (e.g., "Be Safe, Be Respectful, Be Responsible") |
| **Teaching** | Explicitly teach and model what each expectation looks like in each setting |
| **Acknowledgment** | Verbal praise (specific), tickets/tokens, class points, positive notes home |
| **Group incentives** | Class goal (marble jar, puzzle pieces, team points) → earned celebration |
| **Individual support** | Check-in/check-out, behavior contracts, mentoring |

## Output Format

Structure the behavior support package as:
1. **Tier Identification** (which level of support is needed)
2. **Proactive Strategies** (daily SEL activities, classroom systems)
3. **Intervention Plan** (if Tier 2/3 — ABC analysis, BIP template)
4. **Restorative Resources** (conversation guides, circle protocols)
5. **De-escalation Guide** (crisis response steps)
6. **Data Collection Tools** (tracking sheets, progress monitoring)

## Quality Indicators

**Effective behavior support plans include:**
- [ ] Function-based (interventions match the WHY behind behavior)
- [ ] Replacement behaviors taught (not just "stop doing X")
- [ ] Proactive strategies included (not just reactive consequences)
- [ ] Culturally responsive (accounts for cultural norms and biases)
- [ ] Relationship-preserving (maintains dignity and connection)
- [ ] Data-driven (includes tracking and decision rules)
- [ ] Realistic for the teacher to implement consistently
- [ ] Student voice included (student helps design the plan)

**Common Behavior Support Pitfalls:**

| Pitfall | Sign | Prevention |
|---------|------|------------|
| **Punishment-only approach** | Only consequences, no teaching | Teach replacement behaviors explicitly |
| **Ignoring function** | Same intervention regardless of why | Always conduct ABC analysis first |
| **Public shaming** | Behavior charts on the wall, calling students out | Use private signals, individual check-ins |
| **Inconsistency** | Rules enforced differently for different students | Write clear procedures; reflect on bias |
| **Reactive only** | No prevention, only crisis response | Build Tier 1 SEL and community first |
| **Adult-centered** | Plan designed without student input | Include student in goal-setting and strategy selection |
