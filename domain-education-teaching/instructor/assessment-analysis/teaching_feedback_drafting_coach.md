# Feedback Drafting Coach

## Objective

Produce a draft of written feedback on any student assignment — grounded in specific evidence from the work, focused on growth rather than evaluation, and checked for tone — so the teacher has something to customize rather than write from scratch, and the student has something actionable to do.

## When to Use

- Written feedback on any assignment type (not specific to essays, math, or labs)
- When you need to give feedback quickly but want it to be more than a score and a checkmark
- Drafting comments for multiple students on a common assignment
- Calibrating feedback across a team or department
- When you want to build revision, not just communication about grade

## When NOT to Use

- Essay-length feedback by criterion — use `grading_essay_feedback_by_rubric_criterion.md`
- Lab report feedback — use `grading_lab_report_feedback.md`
- Math process feedback — use `grading_math_work_feedback.md`
- Presentation / video feedback — use `grading_presentation_video_feedback.md`

## Instructions

### Step 1: Extract Evidence from the Work

Before writing any feedback, identify specific moments in the student's work to cite:

```
EVIDENCE EXTRACTION
─────────────────────────────────────────────
Evidence of strength: [Specific quote, step, element, or moment from the work]
→ What this demonstrates: [The skill or understanding this evidence shows]

Evidence of development area: [Specific quote, step, element, or moment where the gap is visible]
→ What this reveals: [The skill or understanding that needs development — describe it without judgment]
→ NOT: "this is wrong" — YES: "this shows the student is [specifically what they were thinking]"
```

### Step 2: Draft the Feedback

Produce a draft feedback comment following this structure:

```
FEEDBACK DRAFT
─────────────────────────────────────────────

STRENGTH OBSERVATION:
[1–3 sentences. Name what the student did well, cite the specific evidence, explain why it
matters. Avoid generic praise — "good work" is not feedback.
Example: "Your explanation in paragraph two — where you compared X to Y — shows you understand
that [concept]. That's exactly the kind of reasoning this assignment is looking for."]

DEVELOPMENT AREA:
[1–3 sentences. Name the specific gap, cite evidence, describe it in terms of what the student
was trying to do. Avoid judgment language — no "wrong," "failed to," "missing."
Example: "In your conclusion, you restated your argument but didn't show how the evidence
supports it. The connection between [evidence] and [claim] is the piece that would make the
argument complete."]

CONCRETE NEXT STEP:
[1–2 sentences. A specific, doable action — not "review your notes" but "try rewriting your
conclusion using this frame: [mini scaffold or sentence starter]."
Example: "For your revision, try starting the last paragraph with: 'The evidence shows this
because…' and then connect it directly to your opening claim."]

REFLECTION QUESTION (optional, for formative use):
[1 question that invites the student to think about their own process.
Example: "Before you revise, what do you think the reader needs to understand that they don't
yet?"]
```

### Step 3: Tone Check

Before delivering feedback, audit the draft for these patterns:

```
TONE CHECK
─────────────────────────────────────────────
Flag any of the following — these patterns should be revised:

❌ Empty praise (no evidence): "Great job!" / "Well done!" → Replace with cited observation
❌ Vague critique: "Needs improvement" / "Not quite there" → Replace with specific evidence
❌ Score-as-feedback: "You got 7/10" alone → Add what the work shows and what's next
❌ Negativity spiral: More than 1 development comment per feedback round (unless summative)
❌ Fixed mindset framing: "You don't understand X" → Replace with "The work shows you're
   approaching X as [description] — the next step is [reframe]"
❌ Conditional praise: "This would be better if…" (implies the work isn't good as-is) →
   Separate strength and development clearly

[Flagged phrases from the draft: (list any if found)]
[Revised versions: (rewrites if any were flagged)]
```

### Step 4: Differentiation Adjustments

If the student context includes accommodations, ELL status, or other notes, adjust:

```
CONTEXT ADJUSTMENTS
─────────────────────────────────────────────

ELL / language accessibility: [Simplify one sentence that uses figurative or complex language]
IEP / accommodation: [Adjust length, specificity, or modality of next step if needed]
Pattern of prior errors: [If this is the same gap as last time, name that: "This is connected to
what we noticed last time with X — here's a new way to approach it"]
Strong prior performer with unusual stumble: [Name that explicitly — "This isn't the work I
normally see from you, which tells me [this might be X]"]
```

---

## Output Format

1. Evidence extraction (strength + development area with citations)
2. Feedback draft (strength observation + development area + next step + optional reflection question)
3. Tone check (flagged phrases + revisions)
4. Context adjustments (if student context was provided)

---

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Write "good job" or "needs work" as complete sentences — they provide no information
- Give more than 1–2 development points per feedback comment — overwhelm produces nothing
- Write feedback that only the teacher would understand (implicit criteria, jargon)
- Give the same feedback comment to every student — specificity is the point
- Omit a concrete next step — feedback without a next action is a grade, not a coaching move

✅ **DO:**
- Cite specific evidence for both strengths and development areas
- Write next steps that are concrete enough to act on without teacher present
- Pass the tone check before delivering — flagged language is common and fixable
- Adjust for student context (ELL, IEP, prior patterns)
- Keep it brief — 3–5 sentences is almost always enough for formative feedback

---

## Quality Indicators

- [ ] Strength observation cites specific evidence from the student's work
- [ ] Development area describes what the student was thinking, not just what's wrong
- [ ] Concrete next step is actionable without teacher guidance
- [ ] Tone check completed — no empty praise, vague critique, or fixed mindset framing
- [ ] Total feedback length is appropriate for the grade level and assignment scope
