# Graduate TA Discussion Section Prep

## Objective

Prepare a graduate teaching assistant to run one section of a discussion, recitation, or lab break-out — with a clear relationship to the lecture, an active 50–75 minute structure, equitable participation moves, and a defensible grading protocol when participation is graded.

## When to Use

- New TA's first sections of the semester
- Mid-semester reset when section feels flat or cliquey
- Course where lecture is large and section is the only place students can speak
- Quantitative recitation where students work problems
- Lab break-outs that drift from facilitation to lecture

## When NOT to Use

- TA running lecture (full course delivery) — use `teaching_lesson_plan_generator.md`
- One-on-one office hours — use `hecorp_office_hours_prep.md`
- Pure Socratic philosophical discussion design — use `teaching_socratic_discussion_facilitator.md`

## Instructions

### Step 1: Clarify the Section's Purpose Relative to Lecture

Section is not lecture-2. Identify which it is — and confirm with the lecturer:

| Section type | Primary move | What it is NOT |
|--------------|--------------|----------------|
| Problem-solving recitation | Students work problems with TA support | Re-lecture of solutions |
| Reading discussion | Students engage text together | TA summarizes the reading |
| Concept clarification | Students surface confusions; TA diagnoses | TA covers material that wasn't covered in lecture |
| Lab | Students execute and reason | TA does the procedure for them |
| Mixed | Plan time blocks per purpose | Drift among them all |

If the TA isn't sure, the TA must ask the lecturer before the first section.

### Step 2: Map the Section to a Time Skeleton

For a 75-minute section, default skeleton:

| Block | Time | Move |
|-------|------|------|
| Opener | 0:00–0:05 | Connect to lecture / prior section, surface today's purpose |
| Activation | 0:05–0:15 | Quick retrieval (problem, prompt, or low-stakes question) |
| Main work | 0:15–0:55 | Pair/small-group work, problem rounds, structured discussion |
| Synthesis | 0:55–1:10 | Pull together patterns, name what's still confusing |
| Close | 1:10–1:15 | Exit signal: one takeaway + one remaining question |

Adapt for 50- or 90-minute sections.

### Step 3: Select Active Structures by Section Type

| Purpose | Default structures |
|---------|--------------------|
| Problem-solving | Pair-and-share solutions, board work rotation, gallery walk of solutions, structured think-aloud |
| Reading discussion | Save-the-last-word, structured academic controversy, jigsaw, claim/evidence/concern columns |
| Concept clarification | Muddiest point, misconception poll + discussion, student-generated questions |
| Lab | Pre-lab quiz, paired execution, post-lab CER (claim-evidence-reasoning) |

### Step 4: Build the Section Plan

For each block, write:
- The exact prompt or instruction
- What students do
- What TA does (circulate, listen for, ask)
- Time signal
- Transition to next block

Output as a runnable script the TA can hold.

### Step 5: Equitable Participation Moves

Default norms:
- Cold-call paired with think-time and pair-talk first (no ambush cold-call)
- Use a tracking method (sticky notes, mental tally) to notice who's spoken
- Names — learn them, use them
- Gender / racial / native-language patterns: watch for them, intervene without singling out
- Talk-token, response-rotation, or "step up / step back" if airtime is skewed
- Quiet students given low-cost on-ramps (write-then-share, vote-then-defend)

### Step 6: Handling Common Section Failure Modes

| Failure mode | Move |
|--------------|------|
| Three students do all the talking | Structured turn-taking; reset norms aloud |
| Silence after a question | Wait 8–10 seconds; if still silent, switch to write-then-share |
| Student gives wrong answer | Honor the thinking, redirect ("how did you get there?"); never humiliate |
| Off-topic tangent | Note it, park it ("good — let's hold that"), redirect to objective |
| Confusion you can't resolve | Name it: "I'm not sure — let me check with [lecturer] and report back" — then actually do it |
| Conflict over content | Surface multiple positions, return to evidence |
| Conflict over identity / values | Pause, set norms, follow institutional policy if needed |

### Step 7: Grading Protocol (If Participation Is Graded)

If grading participation:
- Define what counts (quality of contribution, not just volume)
- Use a simple rubric (e.g., 0/1/2 per session) communicated to students up front
- Track on a roster you can defend
- Adjust for accommodations (some students cannot speak in class for documented reasons — provide alternative)
- Don't penalize quietness — reward substantive engagement in any modality
- Be ready to justify grades to lecturer or grade appeal

If grading assignments:
- Use lecturer's rubric exactly
- Norm with co-TAs before grading
- Comment on patterns more than line edits
- Track time-per-paper to keep grading sustainable

### Step 8: Coordination With Lecturer & Co-TAs

- Weekly TA meeting agenda items (prep, calibration, escalations)
- Decision rights: what TA decides vs. defers to lecturer
- Shared messaging policy: don't contradict lecture in section
- Escalation path for academic integrity, distress, or accommodations

### Step 9: TA Self-Care & Boundaries

- Cap email response time (e.g., 24h M–F)
- Cap office hours attendance (don't overrun)
- Don't take grade appeals personally
- Use the lecturer when scope exceeds your role
- Sleep before grading

### Step 10: Reflection After First Three Sections

Capture:
- What worked, what didn't
- Names you don't know yet
- Students who haven't spoken
- Concepts the section couldn't crack
- Adjustments for next section

Bring to TA meeting.

## Output Format

1. Section-purpose statement (confirmed with lecturer)
2. Time skeleton for stated length
3. Selected active structures with rationale
4. Runnable section script (block-by-block)
5. Participation norms and equity moves
6. Failure-mode response moves
7. Grading protocol (if applicable)
8. Lecturer / co-TA coordination plan
9. TA boundaries
10. Three-section reflection template

---

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Re-lecture in section
- Cold-call without think-time
- Penalize quietness
- Improvise grading
- Stay silent when the section is failing — adjust mid-block

✅ **DO:**
- Confirm purpose with lecturer
- Plan a runnable script
- Track who speaks
- Norm grading with co-TAs
- Reflect and adjust after first three sections

---

## Quality Indicators

- [ ] Section purpose explicit and confirmed
- [ ] Time skeleton fits stated length
- [ ] At least one retrieval and one collaborative structure
- [ ] Participation tracking method specified
- [ ] Grading protocol (if graded) defensible
- [ ] Coordination with lecturer mapped
