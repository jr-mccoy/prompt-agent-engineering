**Purpose:** Design comprehensive course syllabi for college and university courses — including learning outcomes aligned to program goals, weekly schedules, assessment plans, AI use policies, accessibility statements, and inclusive course design elements.

**When to use:** Designing a new course; revising an existing syllabus; developing an AI use policy for your course; preparing for accreditation review; improving course accessibility and inclusion; creating a consistent syllabus template for a department.

**Input needed:**
- Course title, level, and credit hours
- Department and program learning outcomes
- Target enrollment and modality (in-person, online, hybrid)
- AI policy stance

## Instructions

Design the syllabus through these structured phases:

### Step 1: Course Learning Outcomes (CLOs)

Write 4-6 measurable learning outcomes using action verbs from Bloom's Taxonomy. Each CLO should be assessable.

| CLO # | Learning Outcome | Bloom's Level | Program Outcome Alignment | Assessed By |
|-------|-----------------|---------------|---------------------------|-------------|
| 1 | By the end of this course, students will be able to [verb + content] | [Level] | [PLO #] | [Assignment/Exam] |
| 2 | ... | [Level] | [PLO #] | [Assessment] |
| 3 | ... | [Level] | [PLO #] | [Assessment] |
| 4 | ... | [Level] | [PLO #] | [Assessment] |

**CLO Quality Criteria:**
- [ ] Uses measurable action verbs (not "understand" or "appreciate")
- [ ] Spans multiple Bloom's levels (not all lower-order)
- [ ] Maps to program learning outcomes
- [ ] Each outcome is assessed at least once
- [ ] Reflects what students can DO, not what the instructor will cover

### Step 2: Course Schedule

**Weekly Schedule Template:**

| Week | Dates | Topics | Readings/Prep | Activities | Assessments Due |
|------|-------|--------|---------------|------------|-----------------|
| 1 | [Dates] | Course introduction; [Topic 1] | [Chapter/article] | [In-class activity] | — |
| 2 | [Dates] | [Topic 2] | [Readings] | [Activity] | [If any] |
| 3 | [Dates] | [Topic 3] | [Readings] | [Activity] | [If any] |
| ... | ... | ... | ... | ... | ... |
| 8 | [Dates] | Midterm review / Midterm exam | [Study guide] | [Review session] | Midterm |
| ... | ... | ... | ... | ... | ... |
| 15 | [Dates] | Course synthesis and reflection | — | [Final presentations] | Final project/exam |

**Pacing Considerations:**
- Front-load foundational concepts; build toward synthesis
- Place major assessments after sufficient practice time
- Account for holidays, reading days, and institutional breaks
- Include at least one "buffer" week for reteaching or extension

### Step 4: AI Use Policy

**Select and customize your AI policy approach:**

**Option A — Restrictive:**
> AI tools (including ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot, and similar) are not permitted for any graded work in this course unless explicitly stated for a specific assignment. Unauthorized use of AI constitutes a violation of the academic integrity policy. This restriction exists because [specific pedagogical rationale — e.g., "developing your own analytical writing voice is a core learning outcome of this course"].

**Option B — Moderate (Recommended for most courses):**
> AI tools may be used for specific purposes as outlined below. For each assignment, the permitted level of AI use will be indicated using this scale:
>
> | Level | AI Use | Example |
> |-------|--------|---------|
> | **No AI** | Complete without AI assistance | In-class exams, personal reflections |
> | **AI for Brainstorming** | Use AI for ideas; all writing is your own | Topic exploration, outline generation |
> | **AI for Feedback** | Complete your work first; use AI for revision suggestions | Grammar checking, argument strengthening |
> | **AI as Collaborator** | Use AI throughout; document and reflect on its contributions | Research synthesis with AI annotation |
>
> **Disclosure requirement:** When AI is permitted, include an "AI Use Statement" describing what tool you used, what prompts you gave, and how you modified the output.

**Option C — Open:**
> AI tools are welcomed as part of your professional toolkit. Learning to use AI effectively and critically is a course goal. All assignments may use AI assistance unless marked "AI-Free." You must: (1) disclose all AI use, (2) verify AI output for accuracy, (3) demonstrate your own critical thinking beyond what AI provides.

**For all options, include:**
- Specific tools covered by the policy
- How violations will be handled
- Pedagogical rationale for the chosen approach
- How the policy may evolve during the semester

### Step 6: Inclusive Syllabus Design

**Accessibility Statement:**
> Students with disabilities are entitled to reasonable accommodations. If you have a disability and need accommodation, please contact [Disability Services Office] and provide your accommodation letter to me within the first two weeks of class. I am committed to making this course accessible to all students.

**Inclusive Language and Design Checklist:**
- [ ] Syllabus uses welcoming, first-person language ("I'm excited to work with you")
- [ ] Multiple ways to demonstrate learning (not only exams)
- [ ] Flexible policies that account for life circumstances
- [ ] Diverse authors and perspectives in reading list
- [ ] Mental health and support resources listed
- [ ] Basic needs statement (food, housing insecurity resources)
- [ ] Preferred name and pronoun acknowledgment
- [ ] Clear, navigable formatting (headings, tables, consistent structure)
- [ ] Available in accessible digital format (not only PDF image)

## Output Format

Structure the complete syllabus as:
1. **Course Information Header** (title, instructor, meeting times, contact)
2. **Course Description** (catalog description + expanded context)
3. **Learning Outcomes**
4. **Required Materials**
5. **Course Schedule** (week-by-week)
6. **Assignments and Grading**
7. **AI Use Policy**
8. **Course Policies** (attendance, late work, integrity, communication)
9. **Accessibility and Inclusion**
10. **Support Resources**

---

## Quality Indicators

**Effective syllabi include:**
- [ ] Measurable learning outcomes aligned to program goals
- [ ] Assessment plan that maps to every CLO
- [ ] Clear, specific AI use policy with rationale
- [ ] Inclusive design elements beyond minimum compliance
- [ ] Policies that are firm but humane
- [ ] Detailed weekly schedule with readings and due dates
- [ ] Tone that is welcoming and professional
- [ ] All institutional required statements included

**Common Syllabus Pitfalls:**

| Pitfall | Sign | Prevention |
|---------|------|------------|
| **Contract tone** | Reads like a legal document full of penalties | Balance policies with warmth and invitation |
| **Vague AI policy** | "Use your best judgment" | Specify permitted tools, purposes, and disclosure requirements |
| **Assessment pile-up** | Multiple major assignments due the same week | Map due dates on a calendar, spread the load |
| **Outcome-assessment mismatch** | CLO says "analyze" but exam is all multiple choice | Use alignment matrix to verify |
| **Inaccessible format** | Scanned PDF, no headings, decorative fonts | Use structured digital document with proper headings |
| **Missing support resources** | No mention of disability services, counseling, etc. | Include comprehensive resource list |
