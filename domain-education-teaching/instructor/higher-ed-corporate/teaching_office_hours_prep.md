# Office Hours Prep

## Objective

Prepare an instructor (faculty, TA, or trainer) for a useful office-hours session: anticipate the high-frequency questions, pre-build worked examples, structure the room (in-person or virtual), and produce a one-page recap that students who couldn't attend can still benefit from.

## When to Use

- Before each weekly office-hours block (especially before exams or major deadlines)
- When attendance is high and time fragments
- When attendance is low and you want to make the slot worth showing up for
- New TAs preparing for their first hours
- Online office hours where silence and lurking are common

## When NOT to Use

- One-on-one academic advising — use `advising_course_selection_advisor.md`
- TA discussion section (graded, structured) — use `hecorp_ta_discussion_section_prep.md`
- Misconception teaching during class — use `teaching_misconception_diagnoser.md`

- **Course / module context:** [What's being covered this week]
- **Recent assessments returned:** [What students just got back, if any]
- **Upcoming assessment:** [What's coming, when]

- **Time block:** [Length and time of day]
- **Expected attendance:** [None / 1–3 / 4–10 / 10+]
- **Recent question signal:** [LMS messages, forum posts, common errors on returned work]
- **Help-seeking norms:** [Is asking questions safe? Is "stupid question" anxiety high?]

## Instructions

### Step 1: Predict the Top 5 Questions

Triangulate from:
- LMS / email / forum activity in the last 7 days
- Errors on the most recent returned assessment
- Hard concepts in this week's reading or lecture
- Logistic questions (deadlines, formatting, technology)

Output a ranked list with frequency estimate and predicted source (concept, logistics, anxiety).

### Step 2: Pre-Build Worked Examples for the Top 3 Concept Questions

For each:
- Restate the question in student language
- Identify the underlying misconception or skill gap
- Build a worked example *one step* harder than what's been shown in class
- Anticipate the "but what about..." follow-up

Have these ready on paper or in a shared doc, not in your head.

### Step 3: Triage Plan for Mixed Attendance

Mixed-attendance is the most common failure mode. Plan tiers:

| Attendance | Move |
|------------|------|
| 0 attendees | Use time for prep, grading, or proactive outreach to flagged students |
| 1–3 attendees | Treat as 1:1 or small group; deep diagnose |
| 4–10 attendees | Run as group help — start with "what brought everyone here?", group similar questions |
| 10+ attendees | Restructure: 5-min intro → break into question clusters → rotate; consider adding extra slot |

### Step 4: Room or Virtual Setup

In-person:
- Visible whiteboard or doc-cam ready
- Seating that allows clustering
- Posted norm: "drop in, no appointment"
- Sign on door if you step out briefly

Virtual:
- Waiting room enabled (manage 1:1 privacy)
- Or open room with chat triage
- Norm posted: video optional, ask in chat OK
- Pre-shared doc for students to drop questions before joining

### Step 5: Help-Seeking Safety

Many students don't show up because asking feels exposing. Mitigate:
- Open with "this is a normal place to be confused — what's confusing?"
- Avoid "you should already know this" reactions
- Praise the question, then teach the gap
- For repeated basic questions, treat as signal that class instruction needs revision — not learner deficit

### Step 6: Diagnose Before You Teach

When a student asks "I don't get X," resist immediate explaining. Instead:

| Move | Purpose |
|------|---------|
| "Show me what you tried" | Surface where they actually got stuck |
| "What does this term mean to you?" | Detect vocabulary gap vs. concept gap |
| "Walk me through your thinking" | Catch reasoning vs. computation error |
| "Where did this start feeling unclear?" | Locate the upstream gap |

Then teach starting from the actual gap, not the surface question.

### Step 7: One-Page Recap for Non-Attendees

Equity move: students who can't attend (work, caregiving, illness, schedule) shouldn't lose access. After the session, post:

- Top 3 questions asked + answers (no names)
- Worked example that emerged
- 2–3 study suggestions
- Reminder of upcoming deadlines

This often becomes more-read than the office hour itself.

### Step 8: Refer Outward When Appropriate

Office hours can't fix everything. Have referrals ready:
- Tutoring / writing center
- Disability services
- Mental health / counseling
- Academic advising
- Tech support
- Financial aid

Know how to refer warmly without dismissing the question that brought them in.

### Step 9: Set Boundaries

- Explicit start/end time
- Cap individual time when others are waiting (e.g., "I want to make sure others get time too — let me share a resource and circle back if we have time")
- Norm around "I'll get back to you by X" rather than answering everything live

### Step 10: Post-Session Notes

Capture for next iteration:
- Who came (rough sense — first-timers? repeats?)
- Top questions
- Anything that signals class instruction needs revision
- Any flagged students needing follow-up

Feed this back into next week's instruction.

---

## Output Format

1. Top-5 predicted questions with sources
2. Pre-built worked examples (top 3)
3. Attendance-triage plan
4. Room / virtual setup checklist
5. Help-seeking safety norms
6. Diagnostic question script
7. One-page recap template
8. Referral list
9. Boundary cues
10. Post-session notes template

---

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Show up cold and improvise every week
- Lecture mini-class if only one student showed up — diagnose instead
- Answer the surface question without locating the actual gap
- Forget the recap — students who couldn't attend lose access
- Skip referrals when the issue is outside your scope (mental health, financial, accommodations)

✅ **DO:**
- Predict the top questions and pre-build examples
- Diagnose before teaching
- Run a triage plan that scales by attendance
- Make the session benefit non-attendees too
- Refer warmly when needed

## Quality Indicators

- [ ] Top-5 question prediction grounded in recent signal
- [ ] At least 3 worked examples ready
- [ ] Triage plan covers 0 to 10+ attendance
- [ ] Diagnostic question script in hand
- [ ] Recap template ready to fill post-session
- [ ] Referral list current
