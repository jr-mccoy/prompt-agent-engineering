# ELA Book Club Discussion Guide (4–6 Week)

## Objective

Produce a complete book-club facilitation plan running 4–6 weeks: weekly pacing with reading targets, the discussion protocol for each session, role rotation, accountable-talk language, conferring rotation for the teacher, and a culminating cross-book task. Output is what a teacher needs to launch and run book clubs with multiple titles running concurrently.

## When to Use

- Multi-title book clubs in a literature unit
- Replacing a whole-class novel where engagement is uneven
- Building student-led discussion habits over a sustained arc
- Differentiating by text difficulty and interest while keeping a shared structure

## When NOT to Use

- Whole-class novel study with one shared text and teacher-led discussion
- Single Socratic seminar — use `teaching_socratic_discussion_facilitator.md`
- Independent reading with no group component — use `teachsubj_ela_reading_conference_planner.md`

## Instructions

### Step 1: Confirm the Through-Line

Write one sentence: *"Across these [N] books, students will investigate ___."* This through-line is what makes the unit cohere. Without it, the books are just parallel reading.

If the through-line is "they're all good books," reject and clarify.

### Step 2: Weekly Pacing

Build a week-by-week pacing chart:

| Week | Reading Target | Session Focus | Teaching Move |
|------|---------------|---------------|---------------|
| 1 | First quarter | Characters & setting; setting up routines | Launch + roles + first protocol |
| 2 | Second quarter | Patterns and predictions | Add accountable-talk moves |
| 3 | Third quarter | Complications & turning points | Push deeper analysis |
| 4 | Final quarter | Resolution & big-question return | Synthesis-prep |
| 5 | (if 5–6 weeks) | Re-read key passages | Close reading inside the club |
| 6 | (if 6 weeks) | Cross-book comparison | Culminating task |

Adjust to actual book length and class periods.

### Step 3: Role Rotation Bank

Provide the role bank with what each role does each session. Limit to 4–5 roles to keep it manageable. Examples:

| Role | What they prepare | What they do in discussion |
|------|------------------|----------------------------|
| Discussion director | 3 open questions tied to the through-line | Opens and closes; protects airtime |
| Passage picker | 2 quotable passages with page numbers | Reads aloud and asks the group to respond |
| Connector | One connection (text-to-text, text-to-self, text-to-world) | Shares mid-discussion |
| Word watcher | 3 unfamiliar or interesting words with context | Raises one for group response |
| Process observer | Watches the discussion against norms | Names what worked and what to try next |

Roles rotate weekly. By week 3, students should be able to drop fixed roles and use moves fluidly.

### Step 4: Discussion Protocol by Week

Provide the structure of each session. Default arc:

```
Open (3 min): Director poses an opening question; everyone responds briefly.
Develop (15–20 min): Group works through prepared passages and questions.
Push (5–7 min): Teacher-or-director-introduced "yes, but" or "what if" move.
Close (3 min): Each person says one sentence about what shifted in their thinking.
```

Vary the protocol weekly to prevent staleness:

| Week | Variation |
|------|----------|
| 1 | Standard arc with explicit norm-setting |
| 2 | "Save the last word" protocol on a key passage |
| 3 | "Block, build, or question" rounds |
| 4 | Re-read protocol — group rereads two passages closely |
| 5 | Silent discussion on shared doc, then talk |
| 6 | Cross-book panel: one rep per book |

### Step 5: Accountable-Talk Bank

Provide sentence stems posted on the wall:

| Move | Stems |
|------|-------|
| **Build** | "I want to add to what ___ said…" / "Building on that…" |
| **Question** | "What do you mean by ___?" / "Where did you see that?" |
| **Disagree** | "I see it differently because…" / "I'm not sure I agree — when ___ happened…" |
| **Cite** | "On page ___, the author says ___. That makes me think…" |
| **Synthesize** | "So we're saying that ___. What does that mean for the book?" |

Hard rule: every claim must cite text by week 2.

### Step 6: Teacher Conferring Rotation

Plan how the teacher rotates among groups:

- Day 1 of each week: teacher launches with whole-class minilesson, then visits each group for 4–5 minutes
- Conferring focus by week: norms (W1), question quality (W2), evidence (W3), interpretation (W4), comparison (W5–6)
- Note template per visit: see `teachsubj_ela_reading_conference_planner.md` Step 7 for the template

### Step 7: Culminating Cross-Book Task

Design a final task that requires cross-book synthesis (this is what justifies running multiple titles):

```
TASK FRAMING: [Question that applies across all books]
EVIDENCE REQUIREMENT: At least 2 quoted passages from your book + one connection to another club's book
FORMAT OPTIONS: [Essay / panel discussion / multimodal piece — pick what fits the unit]
DELIVERABLE: [...]
RUBRIC ANCHORS: [What "meets" looks like]
```

Example task framing: "Across our books, characters change in different ways and for different reasons. What does your book argue about how change happens?"

### Step 8: Norms and Repair Moves

Provide:

- 4–5 group norms students co-create on day 1 (provide a model set)
- Two repair moves the teacher can deploy when groups break down: dominant talker, silent member, off-task drift
- A "circle back" protocol if a group has a poor session

### Step 9: Self-Check

- [ ] Is there a real cross-book through-line?
- [ ] Do roles fade by mid-unit so students use moves without scaffolding?
- [ ] Does each week have a different protocol or focus?
- [ ] Is there a culminating task that requires synthesis across books?
- [ ] Does the conferring rotation align with the weekly focus?

1. Through-line statement
2. Week-by-week pacing chart
3. Role rotation bank with descriptions
4. Discussion protocols by week
5. Accountable-talk stem bank
6. Teacher conferring rotation plan
7. Culminating cross-book task
8. Norms and repair moves
9. Self-check confirmation

---

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Run multiple books with no real through-line — that's parallel reading, not book clubs
- Lock students into roles all 6 weeks; they should fade
- Repeat the same protocol every week — discussion atrophies
- Skip the conferring rotation — book clubs without teacher presence drift
- Assess only individual response on individual book — that misses the cross-book payoff

✅ **DO:**
- Tie all books to a shared through-line that justifies the unit structure
- Rotate roles, then fade them
- Vary the protocol weekly
- Confer with each group every week with a focus that matches the unit phase
- Design a culminating task that requires cross-book synthesis

---

## Quality Indicators

- [ ] Cross-book through-line is named and trackable
- [ ] Pacing matches realistic reading volume
- [ ] Roles are designed to fade
- [ ] Each week's protocol differs in purpose, not just label
- [ ] Culminating task requires synthesis across books
- [ ] Conferring focus changes weekly and aligns with reading phase
