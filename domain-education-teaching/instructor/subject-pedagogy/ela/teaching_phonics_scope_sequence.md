# ELA K–2 Phonics Scope and Sequence

## Objective

Produce a year-long phonics scope and sequence for a single grade (K, 1, or 2) that orders sound-spelling correspondences from high-utility/high-frequency to low, integrates phonemic awareness, irregular high-frequency words, and decodable-text reading, and specifies assessment checkpoints. Output is what a literacy lead, instructional coach, or grade-level team needs to plan the year.

> **Scope note:** Phonics programs should align with adopted curricula and state requirements. This prompt produces a research-aligned scope; districts must verify alignment with their specific program (e.g., FundationsⓇ, UFLI, CKLA, Heggerty + decoding) before adopting.

## When to Use

- Designing or auditing a Tier 1 phonics block in K–2
- Filling gaps when an adopted program is incomplete
- PLC work to align across classrooms in a grade
- Re-grounding a Balanced-Literacy school in structured-literacy principles

## When NOT to Use

- Diagnostic / Tier 3 intervention for an individual struggling reader — use `inclusive_dyslexia_structured_literacy_plan.md`
- Comprehension or vocabulary scope — those are separate strands
- Grades 3+ where decoding is foundational and now multi-syllabic morphology takes over

## Instructions

### Step 1: Confirm Foundational-Skill Strands

For the chosen grade, lay out the strands the year must cover. Default for grade 1:

| Strand | What it covers |
|--------|----------------|
| Phonemic awareness | Blending, segmenting, manipulation at the phoneme level |
| Phoneme-grapheme correspondences | Letters and digraphs mapped to sounds |
| Decoding | Reading words by mapping sounds to graphemes |
| Encoding | Spelling — the inverse of decoding |
| High-frequency words | Including irregular ("heart") words taught with regularities flagged |
| Connected-text reading | Decodable texts containing taught patterns |
| Fluency | Accuracy → automaticity → prosody on decoded text |

Adjust strand emphasis by grade (K weights heavier on PA + letter-sound; 2 shifts toward multisyllabic and morphology).

### Step 2: Sequence the Phoneme-Grapheme Correspondences

Order from high-utility to low. Default progression principles:

1. Most frequent consonants first (m, s, t, p, n)
2. Short vowels before long
3. Continuous sounds before stops where pedagogically helpful
4. Single graphemes before digraphs (sh, ch, th)
5. CVC before CCVC and CVCC (consonant blends)
6. Closed-syllable patterns before open
7. Common vowel teams (ai, ee, oa, ea) before less common
8. R-controlled vowels (ar, or, er/ir/ur) after closed syllables
9. Silent-e / VCe pattern before less productive long-vowel patterns
10. Multisyllabic decoding by syllable type (closed, open, VCe, vowel team, R-controlled, consonant-le) for grade 2+

Output a sequence table:

| Week | PA Focus | Phoneme-Grapheme Focus | Sample Words | High-Frequency Words | Decodable Text Anchors |
|------|----------|------------------------|--------------|---------------------|----------------------|

### Step 3: Year Map by Period

Distribute the sequence across quarters/trimesters with checkpoints:

| Period | Skills introduced | Skills cumulatively reviewed | Checkpoint assessment |
|--------|-------------------|------------------------------|----------------------|

The "cumulatively reviewed" column is critical — phonics decay is real; teach to mastery and review.

### Step 4: Daily Block Architecture

Provide a default 30-minute (or program-aligned) daily block:

```
2 min — Phonemic awareness warm-up (quick, oral, no print)
5 min — Phoneme-grapheme review (sound drill on previously taught)
8 min — New skill explicit instruction (I do / We do / You do)
8 min — Word work: blending, decoding, encoding (chained word lists, dictation)
5 min — Connected text reading (decodable controlled text)
2 min — Quick check / exit
```

Adjust to district minutes.

### Step 5: High-Frequency Word Treatment

Treat high-frequency words by **regularity**, not as "sight words to memorize":

| Type | Treatment |
|------|----------|
| Fully decodable HFW | Decode like any word; flag as high-frequency |
| Mostly decodable HFW | Identify the "heart part" (irregular grapheme); orthographic mapping with the regular parts decoded |
| Fully irregular HFW | Orthographic mapping; map sounds to letters with explicit "heart" notation |

Output the HFW list for the year, sorted by week of introduction and tagged by treatment type.

### Step 6: Decodable Text Plan

For each cluster of weeks, specify:

- The decodable texts students will read (controlled to the patterns taught)
- Whether texts are from the adopted program or supplementary
- A flag if any week has no decodable text aligned to the new pattern (a real gap to fill)

Decodable texts must be ≥80% phonetically controlled to taught patterns; otherwise flag as not truly decodable.

### Step 7: Assessment Checkpoint Plan

| Period | Universal screener | Curriculum-based progress monitoring | Mastery checks |
|--------|-------------------|--------------------------------------|----------------|

Specify:
- Cut scores or proficiency thresholds (or note "use district benchmarks")
- Decision rules for moving students to Tier 2 / Tier 3
- Re-test cadence for at-risk students

### Step 8: Multilingual-Learner and Variation Notes

Provide:

- Phonemes that may not exist in students' first language(s) — list common ones for top L1s in your population (Spanish, Mandarin, Arabic, etc., as relevant)
- Pre-teach moves for those phonemes
- A note that home-language literacy development is a strength, not a deficit

### Step 9: Audit Against Common Failure Modes

Check the sequence against:

- [ ] No 3-cueing/MSV instruction (guessing from picture/context as primary)
- [ ] No teaching letter-of-the-week disconnected from blending
- [ ] PA lessons stay oral — no print confusion
- [ ] Decodable texts actually decodable
- [ ] HFW treatment uses orthographic mapping, not flashcard memorization
- [ ] Cumulative review present every week, not just at end of unit
- [ ] Encoding (spelling) integrated with decoding, not separate strand

### Step 10: Self-Check

- [ ] Sequence is high-utility-first
- [ ] Year map fits available days with cumulative review
- [ ] Daily block fits available minutes
- [ ] Decodable texts align to taught patterns
- [ ] Assessment checkpoints have decision rules
- [ ] Common failure modes audited

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Use letter-of-the-week or alphabet-order sequencing — this contradicts utility-first
- Mix PA with print in the same exercise — they're different skills
- Treat HFW as memorize-by-shape — that's the visual-word-form failure mode
- Use "decodable" texts that contain mostly untaught patterns
- Skip cumulative review — phonics decays without it

✅ **DO:**
- Sequence by utility and complexity
- Keep PA oral, then bridge to grapheme work
- Treat HFW with orthographic mapping, flagging irregularities
- Verify decodable text alignment to taught patterns
- Build cumulative review into every week
- Tie assessment to instructional decisions, not just data collection

- [ ] Assessment plan triggers tiered decisions
- [ ] Common failure modes audited and absent
