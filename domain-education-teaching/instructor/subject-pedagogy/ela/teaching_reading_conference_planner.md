# ELA Reading Conference Planner

## Objective

Produce a plan for a single 5–7 minute reading conference with one student. Output specifies the research questions to open with, the one teaching point to deliver, the named strategy taught, the brief student practice, and the note-taking template for follow-up. Designed to make conferring a repeatable instructional move, not a casual chat.

## When to Use

- Reading workshop, independent reading, book clubs
- After noticing a student's reading log, jot, or response suggests a specific strength or stuck point
- Building a year of conference data per student to track growth
- Differentiating instruction inside a workshop model

## When NOT to Use

- Whole-class minilesson — use `teachsubj_ela_close_reading_minilesson.md`
- Group discussion of a book — use `teachsubj_ela_book_club_discussion_guide.md`
- Generic encouragement or check-in conversation — that's not a conference

## Instructions

### Step 1: Pre-Read the Evidence

In one paragraph, summarize what the available evidence (jot, log, last conference) suggests about this reader. Identify:

- What they're doing well (be specific)
- What they're not yet doing
- One hypothesis about a stuck point or growth edge

If the evidence is too thin to hypothesize, the first 2 minutes of the conference will be research only.

### Step 2: Plan the Research Stance (90 seconds)

Provide 2–3 opening questions designed to surface what the reader is doing. Use this template:

```
Opening: "What's been happening in your book?"
Probe 1: [Question targeted to the hypothesized stuck point]
Probe 2: [Question that surfaces the reader's process, not just the plot]
```

Examples of process probes:
- "Show me a place where you had to slow down. What did you do?"
- "What are you wondering about in this part?"
- "How is this character changing?"

The teacher must listen for evidence that confirms or disconfirms the hypothesis before deciding the teaching point.

### Step 3: Decide the One Teaching Point

After research, choose **one** teaching point. Use this template:

```
Compliment-or-teach? [Compliment / Teach / Both]
If Compliment: "I want to name something I see you doing as a reader: ___."
If Teach: "Today I want to teach you that readers ___."
```

Hard rules:
- One teaching point per conference. Not two. One.
- The teaching point must be transferable beyond this book.
- It must connect to what just emerged in research, not what the teacher planned in the abstract.

### Step 4: Name and Demonstrate the Strategy (90 seconds)

Provide:

```
STRATEGY: [Named strategy in 5–8 words]
Demonstration: [Exact teacher language modeling the move using the student's current page]
```

The strategy must have a name the student could remember — e.g., "Stop and ask: who is talking now?" or "When something surprises you, look back to find why."

Demonstrate with the student's own book, not a teacher example.

### Step 5: Active Engagement (90 seconds)

Have the student try the strategy on the next page or two while the teacher watches. Provide:

- Exact prompt: "Try it right now on this next bit."
- What the teacher is listening/looking for
- Coaching prompts if the student struggles: "What do you do first?"

### Step 6: Link (30 seconds)

Close with a link that names the strategy and points forward:

```
"So today, [student name], you learned that ___. From now on, when you ___, you can ___. I'll come back tomorrow to see how it's going."
```

The link names the strategy and gives a transferable rule. It is not generic encouragement.

### Step 7: Conference Note Template

Output the note for the teacher's binder/spreadsheet:

```
DATE: [...]
STUDENT: [...]
BOOK: [...] (page [...])
RESEARCH OBSERVATIONS: [What the student showed]
TEACHING POINT: [The one move taught]
STRATEGY NAME: [...]
HOW IT WENT: [Independent / coached / didn't take]
NEXT TIME: [What to look for on follow-up]
```

### Step 8: Tonal Calibration

Provide one sentence each:

- The tone if the student is **resistant**: validate the read, lower stakes, push less
- The tone if the student is **strong**: name what's already working, push to a harder move
- The tone if the student is **stuck or anxious**: keep it short, single move, emphasize they've already done the hard part by showing up

### Step 9: Self-Check

- [ ] Is there a single teaching point?
- [ ] Does the strategy have a name the student could repeat?
- [ ] Is the demo grounded in the student's own book?
- [ ] Did the active engagement let the student try, not the teacher narrate?
- [ ] Is the note actionable for next time?

## Output Format

1. Pre-read summary and hypothesis
2. Research opening questions
3. Decided teaching point with reasoning
4. Named strategy + demonstration
5. Active engagement plan
6. Link statement
7. Conference note template
8. Tonal calibration
9. Self-check confirmation

---

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Plan three teaching points "in case the first two don't fit"
- Lecture about reading without grounding in the student's book
- Treat research as small talk — every question should yield evidence
- Name the strategy generically ("read carefully")
- Skip the active engagement — without it, the student didn't learn the move

✅ **DO:**
- Choose one teaching point that emerges from research
- Name the strategy with words the student can repeat
- Use the student's actual page for demo and practice
- End with a link that points forward to independent use
- Capture notes specific enough to drive the next conference

---

## Quality Indicators

- [ ] One teaching point, named and transferable
- [ ] Research questions surface process, not just plot
- [ ] Demo and practice use the student's current text
- [ ] Active engagement is genuine try-it, not narration
- [ ] Conference note enables follow-up
