# Social Studies Civic Action Project

## Objective

Produce a 4–8 week civic action project plan in which students identify an issue, research root causes, analyze stakeholders, design and execute an action, and reflect on outcomes. Output covers the full arc with weekly milestones, scaffolds for each phase, and built-in protections against tokenistic engagement and partisan capture.

## When to Use

- Civics, government, or social studies units that aim at participation, not just knowledge
- Capstone or unit-culmination project
- Service-learning programs
- Middle and high school where students have stakeholder analysis and research skills

## When NOT to Use

- Single-lesson connection between unit and current events — use `teachsubj_socialstudies_current_events_connector.md`
- A non-civic project-based learning unit — use `teaching_project_based_learning_designer.md`
- Single primary-source analysis — use `teachsubj_socialstudies_primary_source_analysis.md`

## Instructions

### Step 1: Frame Civic Action Non-Partisanly

Output a teacher framing statement that distinguishes civic action from partisan advocacy. Civic action is:

- Issue-focused (potholes, food access, voter information, school policy) not party-focused
- Process-focused (how change happens) as much as outcome-focused
- Open to multiple ideological approaches to the same issue

Provide a non-partisan rubric the teacher uses to vet student-proposed issues:

| Issue test | Pass | Fail |
|-----------|------|------|
| Can students take meaningful action in 4–8 weeks? | Local + concrete | National + abstract |
| Is the root cause investigable, not assumed? | Open question | Slogan |
| Are multiple defensible positions possible? | Yes | One-sided framing |
| Is the issue developmentally appropriate? | Yes | Trauma-adjacent without support |

### Step 2: Phase Map

Break the project into phases with weekly milestones:

| Week | Phase | Student work | Teacher move | Deliverable |
|------|-------|-------------|--------------|-------------|
| 1 | Issue exploration | Brainstorm and narrow issues | Vet against rubric | Issue + rationale |
| 2 | Root-cause research | Multi-source investigation | Source quality coaching | Root-cause map |
| 3 | Stakeholder analysis | Identify who's affected and who decides | Mapping protocol | Stakeholder matrix |
| 4 | Action design | Decide what to do, why this action | Feasibility check | Action plan |
| 5–6 | Execution | Carry out the action | Conferring + troubleshooting | Action artifact |
| 7 | Reflection + assessment | Reflect on outcomes vs. goals | Reflection scaffold | Reflection + final product |
| 8 | Public showcase (optional) | Present to peers, families, community | Public-presentation prep | Showcase |

Adjust by length. For 4 weeks: compress; combine phases 2–3 and 5–6.

### Step 3: Issue Selection Scaffold

Provide students with:

- A "What gets under my skin?" brainstorm prompt
- A narrowing protocol: from broad concern → specific issue → researchable question
- A feasibility filter: time, access, school constraints
- A stakeholder pre-check: who would care about this?

Output a sample narrowing arc:
- Broad: "Climate change"
- Narrower: "Energy use at our school"
- Specific researchable question: "How could our school reduce energy waste in unused rooms?"
- Feasible action: "Audit + proposal to facilities + student campaign"

### Step 4: Root-Cause Research Protocol

Coach students to investigate root causes, not just symptoms. Provide:

- A "5 Whys" exercise on their issue
- A multi-source research requirement (≥3 sources of different types: data, expert interview, news, government document, lived-experience source)
- A source-quality rubric (lateral reading, recency, sourcing transparency)
- An interview protocol if students will talk to community members (consent, identity protection if needed)

### Step 5: Stakeholder Analysis Matrix

Provide the matrix students complete:

| Stakeholder | What they care about (their interest) | What power do they have to affect this issue | What's their current position | How can students reach them |
|-------------|--------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------|------------------------------|----------------------------|

Push students to identify:
- Who is affected (sometimes invisible to the policy debate)
- Who decides (often a specific person or board)
- Who influences the deciders
- Whose voice is missing

### Step 6: Action Plan Template

The action plan must specify:

```
ISSUE: [Specific]
GOAL: [Specific, measurable change in 4–8 weeks]
THEORY OF CHANGE: "If we ___, then ___, because ___."
ACTION: [What students will actually do — concrete]
TARGET AUDIENCE: [Who needs to see / hear / decide]
DELIVERABLES: [Artifact: letter, proposal, video, campaign, presentation, art piece]
TIMELINE: [Week-by-week]
RISKS AND CONTINGENCIES: [What could go wrong; backup plan]
SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE: [How students will know if it worked, even partially]
```

Common student actions to consider (non-exhaustive):
- Letter / email campaign to a specific decision-maker (with names + addresses)
- Public-comment statement at a school board or town meeting
- Informational campaign with measurable reach (posters, social, school announcements)
- Data-collection report shared with stakeholders
- Service action paired with an advocacy ask
- Forum or panel hosted with community voices

### Step 7: Execution Conferring

During action execution, the teacher confers with each group:

- "What's working?"
- "What's stuck?"
- "Who haven't you talked to yet who matters?"
- "What's your next move?"

Output a conferring template (one per group per week).

### Step 8: Reflection Scaffold

End with a reflection task that distinguishes outcome from learning:

```
WHAT WE TRIED TO DO: ___
WHAT ACTUALLY HAPPENED: ___ (regardless of whether it worked)
WHAT WE LEARNED ABOUT THE ISSUE: ___
WHAT WE LEARNED ABOUT HOW CHANGE HAPPENS: ___
WHAT WE WOULD DO DIFFERENTLY: ___
WHAT'S THE NEXT MOVE IF WE WERE TO CONTINUE: ___
```

Civic projects often "fail" by their stated goal but teach significantly. The reflection must validate that.

### Step 9: Assessment Rubric

Score the project across:

| Criterion | Weight |
|-----------|--------|
| Issue framing (specificity, researchability) | 15% |
| Research quality (multi-source, root-cause) | 20% |
| Stakeholder analysis (depth, missing-voice attention) | 15% |
| Action design (feasibility, theory-of-change clarity) | 15% |
| Execution (effort, follow-through) | 15% |
| Reflection (depth, learning-vs-outcome distinction) | 20% |

Outcome (whether the action "worked") is not scored — process is.

### Step 10: Climate, Consent, and Safety Notes

Provide teacher notes on:
- District/school approval procedures for off-campus action
- Parent notification or consent if the project involves community contact, video, or public-facing artifacts
- Student-name/photo protections in public artifacts
- How to handle issues that touch students' lived experience (housing instability, immigration, identity-based discrimination) — students may have personal stake; provide opt-outs and emotional safety support

> **Scope note:** This is project design guidance, not legal or policy advice. Confirm specific requirements with school administration before launching.

### Step 11: Self-Check

- [ ] Is the framing non-partisan and process-focused?
- [ ] Are issues feasible in the time and context?
- [ ] Does the project teach root-cause investigation, not just symptom response?
- [ ] Does stakeholder analysis surface missing voices?
- [ ] Is action grounded in a theory of change?
- [ ] Does reflection separate outcome from learning?

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Allow surface "awareness campaigns" that don't change anything specific — set a higher bar
- Frame the project around a partisan position students must adopt
- Skip stakeholder analysis — action without it is misaimed
- Score outcome (whether they "won") instead of process and learning
- Treat reflection as optional or surface

✅ **DO:**
- Frame civic action as issue-focused and process-focused, not partisan
- Demand concrete, feasible action with named decision-makers
- Require multi-source root-cause investigation
- Surface missing voices in stakeholder analysis
- Score the process; honor the learning regardless of outcome

## Quality Indicators

- [ ] Non-partisan framing is built in, not bolted on
- [ ] Phase map is realistic for the time available
- [ ] Action design has a stated theory of change
- [ ] Stakeholder analysis includes missing-voice attention
- [ ] Reflection distinguishes outcome from learning
- [ ] Assessment rubric scores process, not outcome
