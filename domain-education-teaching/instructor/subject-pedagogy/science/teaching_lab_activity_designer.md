# Science Lab Activity Designer with CER Scaffold

## Objective

Produce a complete lab activity with investigation question, materials, procedure, data tables, safety protocol, and a Claim-Evidence-Reasoning scaffold that students complete after data collection. The lab must require students to construct an explanation from evidence — not verify a teacher-stated conclusion.

## When to Use

- Designing a hands-on lab for grades 6–12
- Replacing a "follow the recipe and get the right answer" lab with one that has investigative integrity
- Building toward NGSS performance expectations that require argument from evidence
- Creating labs aligned to a specific phenomenon launched earlier

## When NOT to Use

- Demonstration-only activity with no student data — that's a demo, not a lab
- Pure CER writing scaffold without a specific lab — use `teachsubj_science_cer_scaffold.md`
- Phenomenon vetting and storyline arc — use `teachsubj_science_ngss_phenomenon_selector.md`
- Student-facing lab report scaffold for a lab they already did — use `learnsci_lab_report_scaffold.md`

## Instructions

### Step 1: Vet the Investigative Integrity

Before designing the lab, confirm:

- [ ] The investigation question is genuinely investigable — students don't already know the answer
- [ ] The data collected can actually support a defensible claim
- [ ] The lab is not a "verify what the teacher said" exercise (cookbook lab)
- [ ] The phenomenon is observable and measurable in your room
- [ ] Variables are clear: what's manipulated, what's measured, what's controlled

If the lab fails any of these, redesign rather than proceeding.

### Step 2: Investigation Question

Write the question students will investigate. Properties:

- Specific (not "how does temperature affect things?")
- Answerable with available materials in available time
- Tied to the phenomenon or driving question
- Generates measurable data, not just qualitative observation

Provide 1–2 alternative phrasings if useful for student-friendly language.

### Step 3: Variables and Hypothesis Frame

Specify:

- **Independent variable** (what students manipulate)
- **Dependent variable** (what students measure)
- **Controlled variables** (what stays the same; list ≥3)

Provide a hypothesis sentence frame:
"If [independent variable] [increases/decreases], then [dependent variable] will [predicted change], because ___."

### Step 4: Materials and Setup

Output:

- Per-group materials list with quantities
- Setup diagram (text/ASCII description)
- Substitutions if a typical material isn't available
- Estimated cost per group (rough)

### Step 5: Procedure

Number every step. Each step must be:

- Action-specific ("Measure 25 mL of water using the graduated cylinder")
- Safety-aware (PPE worn during which steps, hazards flagged)
- Repeatable across groups (no ambiguous "some" or "a little")

Include:
- Steps 1–N with explicit numeric quantities
- A trial-replication note ("repeat steps 4–7 three times to compute an average")
- Cleanup steps

### Step 6: Data Collection Tables

Provide the exact data tables students will fill in. Pre-print the column headers, units, and the number of rows that match the procedure. Include a row for the average if multiple trials.

| Trial | Independent variable (units) | Dependent variable (units) | Observations |
|-------|------------------------------|---------------------------|--------------|

### Step 7: Safety Protocol

Provide:

- Required PPE
- Specific hazards in this lab and the protective response
- Disposal procedure for any waste
- Emergency response (eye wash, broken glass, spill)
- Pre-lab safety contract or sign-off if appropriate

### Step 8: CER Scaffold for Post-Lab Analysis

Output the student-facing CER scaffold:

```
CLAIM (1–2 sentences)
A claim is the answer to the investigation question. It does not include "I think" — it states what the data show.

Frame: "Based on our data, [dependent variable] [direction of change] when [independent variable] [direction of change]."

Your claim: ___

EVIDENCE (data + observations)
Evidence is the specific data from your lab — not your impression. Quote your data table.

Frame: "Our data show that when [IV value 1], [DV] = ___, and when [IV value 2], [DV] = ___. The trend is ___."

Your evidence: ___

REASONING (the science)
Reasoning is why the evidence supports the claim — using the science you've learned.

Frame: "This happens because [scientific principle from class — name it]. Specifically, [mechanism that connects IV to DV]."

Your reasoning: ___
```

Include a CER quality checklist:
- [ ] Claim answers the investigation question directly
- [ ] Evidence quotes specific numbers from the data
- [ ] Reasoning names a scientific principle, not a vague "because"
- [ ] No "I think" or "I feel" language

### Step 9: Discussion Questions

Provide 3–5 discussion questions:

1. One that asks about sources of error in this specific lab
2. One that asks students to predict an extension ("What if we changed ___?")
3. One that connects to the phenomenon or driving question
4. One that pushes toward a related disciplinary core idea

### Step 10: Differentiation and Accommodations

- **Stuck groups:** Pre-collected sample data they can analyze even if their procedure didn't yield clean results
- **Stretch:** Additional variable to investigate; design a follow-up
- **ELL support:** Pre-taught vocabulary list with definitions; sentence frames in the CER scaffold
- **IEP:** Lab-partner protocol for fine-motor / mobility accommodations; pre-printed data tables

### Step 11: Self-Check

- [ ] Is this lab investigative or cookbook? Confirm investigative.
- [ ] Can students collect enough data to support a defensible claim?
- [ ] Is the procedure executable in the time and with the materials?
- [ ] Does the CER scaffold force evidence and reasoning, not just claim?
- [ ] Are safety and accommodations explicit, not implicit?

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Tell students the answer in the lab handout
- Use phrases like "you should observe ___" — that's confirming, not investigating
- Skip controlled variables — without them the data don't support claims
- Treat CER as fill-in-the-blank — the scaffold structures, doesn't substitute thinking
- Bury the science principle in the procedure when it should emerge in reasoning

✅ **DO:**
- Frame the question so students don't already know the answer
- Make data quantitative wherever possible
- Force evidence-citation in CER (specific numbers from the table)
- Require a named scientific principle in reasoning
- Address safety, equity, and differentiation explicitly

---

## Quality Indicators

- [ ] Investigation question is genuinely investigable
- [ ] Procedure produces data sufficient to support a claim
- [ ] CER scaffold requires specific data evidence and named reasoning
- [ ] Safety and accommodations are explicit
- [ ] Discussion questions push beyond the lab's specific case
- [ ] Differentiation does not lower the cognitive demand
