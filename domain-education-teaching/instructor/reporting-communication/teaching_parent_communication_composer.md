**Purpose:** Generate professional, empathetic, and actionable parent/family communications across multiple formats — newsletters, conference notes, progress updates, behavior notices, and event announcements — with tone calibration and multilingual support guidance.

**When to use:** Writing weekly or monthly class newsletters; preparing for parent-teacher conferences; sending progress updates or behavior notifications; communicating about school events; addressing sensitive situations.

**Input needed:**
- Communication type (newsletter, conference prep, progress update, behavior notice, event announcement)
- Grade level and subject
- Key information to communicate
- Tone requirements (celebratory, informational, constructive, urgent)

## Instructions

Generate parent communications through these structured phases:

### Step 1: Audience and Purpose Analysis

**Identify the communication goal:**

| Element | Analysis |
|---------|----------|
| **Primary purpose** | [Inform / Celebrate / Request action / Address concern / Build relationship] |
| **Audience** | [All families / Individual family / Subset of families] |
| **Cultural considerations** | [Language needs, communication preferences, cultural norms] |
| **Desired outcome** | [What should families know, feel, or do after reading?] |
| **Sensitivity level** | [Routine / Moderate / High — affects tone and channel] |

### Step 2: Choose Structure by Communication Type

**Weekly/Monthly Newsletter:**
1. Warm greeting with class highlight or celebration
2. What we learned this week/month (brief, engaging summary)
3. What's coming up (previews with dates)
4. How families can help at home (1-2 specific actions)
5. Important dates and reminders
6. Warm closing

**Parent-Teacher Conference Prep:**
1. Student strengths (lead with positives — minimum 2 per concern area)
2. Growth areas with specific evidence
3. Goals and action steps (school + home)
4. Questions to invite from families
5. Follow-up plan and timeline

**Individual Progress Update:**
1. Positive opening (genuine, specific strength)
2. Current performance snapshot (academic and/or behavioral)
3. Specific observation or data point
4. Recommended next steps (home and school)
5. Invitation to connect

**Behavior Notice:**
1. Factual description of the situation (no judgment language)
2. How it was handled at school
3. Impact on learning or community
4. Collaborative next steps
5. Reassurance of care for the student

**Sensitive Situation Communication:**
1. Empathetic opening acknowledging the situation
2. Factual, clear information
3. What the school is doing
4. What families can do
5. Resources available
6. Contact information for questions

### Step 3: Draft the Communication

**Tone Calibration Guide:**

| Tone | Language Patterns | Avoid |
|------|------------------|-------|
| **Celebratory** | "I'm thrilled to share..." "Your child has made wonderful progress in..." | Comparisons to other students |
| **Informational** | "I'd like to update you on..." "This week in class, we..." | Jargon, acronyms without definition |
| **Constructive** | "I've noticed an area where we can work together to support..." | Deficit language, blame |
| **Urgent** | "I'm reaching out because this needs attention..." | Panic language, ultimatums |
| **Empathetic** | "I understand this may be concerning..." "I want you to know we care about..." | Dismissiveness, minimizing |

**Language Guidelines:**
- Use plain language (aim for 6th-8th grade reading level for general communications)
- Avoid educational jargon or define it when necessary (e.g., "formative assessment (a quick check of understanding)")
- Use strengths-based language: "growing in" rather than "failing at"
- Be specific: "scored 82% on the fractions quiz" rather than "doing okay in math"
- Include actionable items: "You can help by practicing multiplication facts for 5 minutes at bedtime"

### Step 4: Add Engagement Elements

**For Newsletters:**
- Student voice: Include a quote or reflection from the class
- Photo/visual suggestion: [describe a photo opportunity from the week]
- Discussion starter: "Ask your child about [specific topic] — they have great ideas!"
- Community building: Highlight a volunteer, share a class achievement

**For Individual Communications:**
- Specific anecdote: "Today during [activity], [student placeholder] demonstrated [skill]..."
- Data point: Include one concrete measure of progress
- Home connection: One specific activity families can do

### Step 5: Inclusive Communication Review

**Accessibility Check:**
- [ ] Clear, simple sentence structure (short paragraphs, bullet points)
- [ ] No idioms or cultural references that may not translate
- [ ] Contact information included with multiple contact methods
- [ ] Formatted for easy translation (tools like Google Translate, TalkingPoints)
- [ ] Key dates formatted clearly (Day, Month Date, Year — no abbreviations)
- [ ] Action items are explicit (not implied)

**Translation Guidance:**
- Note which languages are needed for your community
- Use translation tools for initial drafts, then have a bilingual staff member or community liaison review
- For critical communications, use professional translation services
- Consider visual elements that communicate across languages

### Step 6: Template Library

**Quick Templates:**

**Positive Update (2-sentence):**
> I wanted to share a quick highlight — [student placeholder] did an excellent job [specific achievement] today. Keep encouraging that [skill/behavior] at home!

**Concern with Partnership Framing (3-sentence):**
> I've noticed [student placeholder] has been [specific observation] during [context]. I'd love to work together to support [him/her/them] in [specific area]. Would you have a few minutes to connect by [phone/email/conference] this week?

**Newsletter Closing:**
> Thank you for your continued partnership in your child's learning. As always, my door (and inbox) are open. Have a wonderful [weekend/week]!

## Output Format

Structure the communication as:
1. **Draft Communication** (ready to personalize and send)
2. **Subject Line / Header** (for emails or newsletters)
3. **Key Talking Points** (bullet summary for phone calls or conferences)
4. **Follow-Up Plan** (next steps and timeline)
5. **Translation Notes** (languages needed, key terms to verify)

## Quality Indicators

**Effective parent communications include:**
- [ ] Strengths-based opening (even constructive messages lead with a positive)
- [ ] Clear, jargon-free language
- [ ] Specific rather than vague ("scored 85% on spelling test" not "doing well")
- [ ] Actionable next steps for families
- [ ] Warm, professional tone appropriate to the situation
- [ ] Invitation for two-way communication
- [ ] FERPA-compliant (no protected information shared inappropriately)
- [ ] Culturally responsive and accessible

**Common Communication Pitfalls:**

| Pitfall | Sign | Prevention |
|---------|------|------------|
| **Deficit-only framing** | Letter lists only problems | Always lead with strengths; 2:1 positive-to-concern ratio |
| **Jargon overload** | "DIBELS, RTI, Tier 2 intervention" | Use plain language, define terms |
| **No action items** | Families don't know what to do | Include specific, doable home activities |
| **One-way communication** | No invitation for response | Always include a question or invitation to connect |
| **Late or reactive only** | Only contacting about problems | Establish regular positive communication first |
| **Generic mass feel** | "Dear parents" with no specifics | Personalize with class-specific details |
