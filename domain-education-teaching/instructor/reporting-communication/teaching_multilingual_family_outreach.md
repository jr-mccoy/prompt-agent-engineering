# Multilingual Family Outreach

**Objective:** Make sure every family can receive, understand, and respond to what the
class sends them — by knowing each family's language and preferred mode, matching the
method to the stakes of the message, and checking that information actually arrived.

## When to Use
- ✅ Start of year: several families in your class or grade use languages other than
  English and you want a plan, not ad-hoc fixes.
- ✅ Messages go out in English and some families never respond.
- ✅ Conferences, IEP meetings, or a discipline matter are coming and you need
  interpreters arranged.
- ✅ A family's language has little written tradition or the family prefers voice
  messages, and written translation is not reaching them.
- ❌ **Not this prompt if** you are writing **one** message and need tone and format —
  use `teaching_parent_communication_composer.md` (its translation notes cover a single
  message; this prompt plans language access across the class). If the need is
  supporting the **student's** English development in class, use
  `instructor/student-support/teaching_ell_wida_scaffolding.md`. For preparing the
  conference itself, use `teaching_parent_teacher_conference_prep.md`.

## Inputs Required
- **Families' languages and preferences**: from the school's home-language survey or
  enrolment records, plus what families have told you (preferred language, written or
  spoken, phone/text/app/paper, best times).
- **School resources**: district interpreters, telephone or video interpretation
  service, translation office, bilingual staff, family or community liaisons, and any
  two-way translating messaging tool the school has approved.
- **Communication calendar**: routine items (newsletters, reminders), conferences,
  report cards, and anything high-stakes expected (evaluations, IEP/504 meetings,
  discipline, health, safety).
- **District language-access policy** and the contact for the language-access or
  English-learner office.

> **Privacy:** Use placeholders for students and families in any AI tool. Use only
> school-approved translation and messaging tools for content containing student
> information.

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Record each family's language **and** mode; do not assume a family reads its home
  language, or English.
- Classify every communication by stakes before choosing a method.
- Use a qualified interpreter or human translation for high-stakes matters —
  special education, discipline, health and safety, legal or custody issues, and
  anything requiring consent.
- Write the English source in plain language (short sentences, no idioms or
  acronyms) before translating.
- Check understanding on important messages (a reply, a call, a teach-back question).
- Track reach and response by language.
- Confirm obligations with the district's language-access office; in the United
  States, federal civil-rights guidance requires schools to communicate essential
  information to parents with limited English proficiency in a language they understand.

**Must Not:**
- Use the student, siblings, or other children as interpreters for anything beyond
  the most basic logistics — and never for concerns about the student.
- Send machine translation unreviewed for high-stakes content.
- Treat silence as disinterest.
- Rely on one channel for families who have not responded to it.

## Instructions

1. **Build the language-access map.** One row per family: language, written/spoken
   preference, channel, best time, interpreter needed (Y/N), notes (two households,
   regional variety, literacy preference). Fill gaps by asking — through a liaison or
   interpreter, not a form in English.
2. **Sort communications by stakes (DS-06).**
   - **Routine** — reminders, events, newsletters: plain-language English plus
     approved translation, with voice or picture options for families who prefer them.
   - **Important** — progress concerns, report cards, conference invitations:
     reviewed translation or a bilingual staff call.
   - **High-stakes** — special education, discipline, health, safety, legal: qualified
     interpreter or professional translation, always.
3. **Apply the stakes gate (QA-08).** Before any message goes out, check its tier
   against the method. A high-stakes message with only machine translation does not send.
4. **Write the source text (CM-02).** Under 120 words for routine messages; one action
   per message; dates in numbers; no idioms, acronyms, or sarcasm; key terms
   (field trip, permission slip, conference) defined the first time.
5. **Choose the channel per family (RP-02).** Text or voice for families who prefer
   spoken language; paper home in the backpack only as a backup; video calls with an
   interpreter for conferences when travel is hard.
6. **Arrange interpreters ahead.** Book for conferences and meetings at least a week
   ahead; brief the interpreter on the purpose and any terms; speak to the family, not
   the interpreter; allow double time.
7. **Check understanding.** For important messages, ask a reply question ("Reply 1 if
   Thursday works, 2 if not") or call with an interpreter; for meetings, end with
   "What will you do next?" rather than "Any questions?"
8. **Track reach.** Monthly: response rate by language group; follow up personally
   with any family who has not responded twice, through a liaison.

## False-Positive Prevention

| Common Mistake | Why It's Wrong | Correct Approach |
|---|---|---|
| Translated = understood | Some families do not read their home language | Record mode; offer voice and in-person options |
| Child as interpreter | Burdens the child and filters the message | Qualified interpreter; children only for basic logistics, never concerns |
| Machine translation for an IEP notice | Errors in high-stakes content harm rights and trust | Stakes gate: professional translation or interpreter |
| Translating jargon | "Tier 2 RTI" stays meaningless in any language | Plain-language source first |
| Silence as disinterest | Usually a channel, language, or timing mismatch | Personal follow-up through a liaison |
| One language label per family | Two households or a regional variety may differ | Record per household and variety |
| "Any questions?" | Families may not want to seem impolite | Ask what they will do next |
| Assuming the policy | Obligations vary by country, state, and district | Confirm with the language-access office |

## Verification Checklist

- [ ] Every family has language, mode, channel, and interpreter need recorded
- [ ] Every planned communication has a stakes tier and a matching method
- [ ] High-stakes items use a qualified interpreter or professional translation only
- [ ] Source text rules applied before translation
- [ ] No child interpreting for concerns about the student
- [ ] Interpreters booked with lead time for conferences and meetings
- [ ] Understanding checks defined for important and high-stakes items
- [ ] Reach tracked by language; non-responders followed up personally

## Techniques Used

- **RP-02 Audience-Specific Framing** — channel, mode, and timing chosen per family.
- **CM-02 Constraint Specification** — plain-language source-text rules before any translation.
- **QA-08 Gate-Based Verification** — high-stakes messages cannot go out without a qualified interpreter or translation.
- **DS-06 Prioritization and Severity Guidance** — routine / important / high-stakes tiers set the method.
- **QA-12 False Positives Identification** — guards against translated-equals-understood and silence-as-disinterest.
