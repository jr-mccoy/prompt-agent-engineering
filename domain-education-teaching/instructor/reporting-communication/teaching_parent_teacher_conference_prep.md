# Parent-Teacher Conference Preparation

**Objective:** Walk into conference week with a plan for every family: which
conferences need extra time or another adult present, a one-page evidence sheet per
student, a 15-minute agenda that leaves time for the family to talk, one agreed goal
with actions on both sides, and a follow-up note ready to send.

## When to Use
- ✅ Scheduled fall or spring conferences are coming and you have 20–30 families to see.
- ✅ A family has requested a meeting and you want it to be useful, not a recital of grades.
- ✅ Student-led conferences are planned and students need a structure.
- ✅ You have a serious concern to raise and want to do it fairly and without surprise.
- ❌ **Not this prompt if** you need to **write** the newsletter, invitation, or
  progress note itself — use `teaching_parent_communication_composer.md`, which writes
  family communications; this prompt prepares the meeting. If families need
  interpreters or translated materials planned across the class, use
  `teaching_multilingual_family_outreach.md` first. If you are the **parent**
  preparing to meet the school, use
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/ages-4-8/parenting_school_accommodation_conversation_prep.md`.
  For one-to-one reading conferences with students, use
  `subject-pedagogy/ela/teaching_reading_conference_planner.md`.

## Inputs Required
- **Grade, subject(s), and conference format** (in person, video, phone; slot length;
  student-led or not).
- **Per student, in your own records**: one specific strength, current performance
  against standards or grades, work samples, attendance, and any concern.
- **Known family context** relevant to the meeting: preferred language, previous
  contact, custody or who should be invited, accessibility needs.
- **School processes** for serious concerns (intervention team, counsellor, special
  education referral route).

> **Privacy:** Do not paste student names, grades, disability or health information
> into an AI tool. Use initials or placeholders and fill in from your own records.

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Lead with one specific, evidenced strength — an anecdote or work sample, not "a
  pleasure to have in class".
- Limit growth areas to one or two, each with evidence the family can see.
- Ask the family's view before giving yours on the growth area.
- End with one goal that has a school action and a home action, both realistic.
- Tell families about any new serious concern **before** the conference, not for the
  first time in it.
- Book interpreters in advance for families who need them; never use the student as
  interpreter.
- Send a written follow-up within two school days.

**Must Not:**
- Use jargon or acronyms (RTI, DIBELS, Tier 2) without plain explanation.
- Diagnose or suggest a disability, or promise evaluation outcomes; describe what you
  observe and the school's referral process.
- Compare the student with named classmates.
- Overrun one slot at the expense of the next family — book a second meeting instead.

## Instructions

1. **Sort the list (DS-06).** Routine (on track), concern (one area needs a joint
   plan), urgent (serious academic, behaviour, or wellbeing concern). Urgent families
   get a call beforehand and a longer slot, with a counsellor or specialist if needed.
2. **Build the evidence sheet (CM-01)** per student: strength with an example; one or
   two growth areas with a work sample and a number (reading level, assignments
   complete, standards met); what you are already doing in class; one question for the
   family.
3. **Set the agenda** for a 15-minute slot: welcome and family's questions (2 min);
   strength (2 min); growth area with evidence and family's view (5 min); agree one
   goal with school and home actions (4 min); next contact (2 min).
4. **Phrase for the family (RP-02).** Plain language; describe behaviour, not
   character ("turned in 6 of 14 assignments" not "lazy"); offer home actions that fit
   any schedule and do not assume a quiet desk, a printer, or English at home.
5. **Plan for the hard moment.** For urgent conferences: state the concern with
   evidence, pause for the family's response, explain the school's process and who
   leads it, and agree when you will next speak.
6. **Student-led option.** If students lead, give them a three-part script: what I'm
   proud of (with a work sample), what I'm working on, how you can help.
7. **Prepare logistics.** Interpreter booked; seating side by side, not across a
   desk; work samples ready; a sign-up for families who could not attend.
8. **Write the follow-up (DS-40).** Goal, both actions, and the date of the next
   contact — sent within two school days, in the family's language.

## Output Format

```
# Conference plan — [class], [dates]
Routine: [n]   Concern: [n]   Urgent: [n] (pre-calls by [date])

## Evidence sheet — [student initials]
Strength: [specific example]
Growth area(s): [area] — evidence: [sample, number]
Already doing in class: [..]
Question for the family: [..]

## Agenda (15 min)
[timed steps]

## Goal
Goal: [..]   School action: [..]   Home action: [..]   Check-in: [date]

## Logistics
Language/interpreter: [..]   Who attends: [..]   Materials: [..]

## Follow-up note (within 2 days)
[draft]
```

## False-Positive Prevention

| Common Mistake | Why It's Wrong | Correct Approach |
|---|---|---|
| Generic praise | Families hear it as not knowing their child | One specific strength with an example |
| Five growth areas | Nothing gets done; the family leaves discouraged | One or two, with evidence |
| Teacher talks for 15 minutes | Families have information you need | Ask their view before giving yours |
| Surprise serious concern | Feels like an ambush; trust drops | Pre-call before the conference |
| Diagnosing at the table | Outside a teacher's role; creates false expectations | Describe observations; explain the referral route |
| Home actions that assume resources | Unequal and often impossible | Actions that fit any schedule and language |
| Child as interpreter | Puts the child in the middle; information is filtered | Book a qualified interpreter |
| No written follow-up | Agreements fade within a week | Note within two school days |

## Verification Checklist

- [ ] Every student sorted routine / concern / urgent; urgent families pre-called
- [ ] Each evidence sheet has a specific strength and ≤2 evidenced growth areas
- [ ] Agenda gives the family time to speak before the goal is set
- [ ] One goal with a school action and a home action
- [ ] No jargon, diagnosis, or classmate comparisons
- [ ] Interpreters booked where needed; no student interpreting
- [ ] Follow-up drafted for sending within two school days
- [ ] No identifiable student data entered into the AI tool

## Example Output

```
# Conference plan — Grade 4, Room 12, Oct 21–23
Routine: 18   Concern: 6   Urgent: 1 (pre-call by Oct 16)

## Evidence sheet — D.R. (concern)
Strength: Explained the water-cycle model to his group using his own diagram (Sep 30
science journal) — the clearest explanation in the class.
Growth area: Reading stamina — reads 72 words per minute on grade-level text (fall
benchmark ~95); stops after 5–6 minutes of independent reading (3 observation notes).
Already doing in class: daily partner reading; books chosen from his interest list
(weather, sharks).
Question for the family: "When does he read at home, and what does he choose?"

## Agenda (15 min)
0–2 Welcome; "What would you like to make sure we talk about?"
2–4 Science journal — show the diagram.
4–9 Reading: show the benchmark passage; ask what they see at home.
9–13 Agree the goal.
13–15 Next contact; any other questions.

## Goal
Goal: Read independently for 15 minutes without stopping by the December check.
School action: 10-minute partner reading daily; weekly 1-minute fluency check, shared monthly.
Home action: 10 minutes of any reading he chooses (comics, game guides, recipes count),
4 nights a week; a check mark on the sheet in his folder.
Check-in: Nov 20, by phone.

## Logistics
Language: Spanish; district interpreter booked for Oct 22, 4:15. Both parents invited
(separate households — two copies of the follow-up).
Materials: science journal, benchmark passage, reading log sheet.

## Follow-up note (within 2 days)
"Thank you for meeting on Tuesday. We agreed D. will work toward 15 minutes of
independent reading by December. At school he'll do daily partner reading and a weekly
check; at home, 10 minutes of anything he likes, 4 nights a week. I'll call you on
Nov 20 to share his progress. — Ms. Okafor" (sent in Spanish and English)
```

## Techniques Used

- **CM-01 Explicit Context Framing** — an evidence sheet per student before the meeting.
- **RP-02 Audience-Specific Framing** — plain, behaviour-based language and home actions that fit any family.
- **DS-06 Prioritization and Severity Guidance** — routine / concern / urgent sorting and one or two growth areas.
- **DS-40 Follow-Up Action Extraction** — one goal with dated actions on both sides and a written follow-up.
- **QA-12 False Positives Identification** — guards against generic praise, ambushes, and diagnosis at the table.
