## Instructions

Design a Socratic discussion through these structured phases:

### Step 1: Text/Content Selection and Preparation

**Text Criteria Checklist:**

| Criterion | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| **Complexity** | Requires interpretation, not just recall |
| **Ambiguity** | Multiple valid interpretations possible |
| **Relevance** | Connects to students' lives or bigger questions |
| **Accessibility** | Within students' reading/comprehension level |
| **Length** | Can be read closely in available time |

### Step 2: Essential Questions Design

**Levels of Questions:**

| Level | Type | Example | Purpose |
|-------|------|---------|---------|
| **Opening** | Low-stakes, accessible | "What stood out to you?" | Build confidence, get voices in |
| **Core** | Interpretive, debatable | "What does the author mean by...?" | Drive inquiry |
| **Closing** | Connecting, applying | "How does this relate to...?" | Transfer learning |

**Follow-up Prompts:**

- "Does anyone see it differently?"
- "How does that connect to what [student] said?"
- "What's the evidence for that?"

### Step 3: Discussion Structure

**Classic Socratic Seminar Format:**

| Phase | Time | Activity |
|-------|------|----------|
| **Setup** | 3-5 min | Arrange room, review norms, state opening question |
| **Inner circle** | 15-20 min | First group discusses while outer observes |
| **Transition** | 2-3 min | Circles switch places |
| **Inner circle 2** | 15-20 min | Second group discusses, builds on first |
| **Debrief** | 5-8 min | Reflection on content and process |

**Alternative Structures:**

| Format | Best For | How It Works |
|--------|----------|--------------|
| **Fishbowl** | Large classes | Inner circle discusses, outer joins when ready |
| **Spider web** | Tracking participation | Discussion mapped visually |
| **Harkness** | Experienced students | Student-led, teacher observes only |
| **Structured Academic Controversy** | Debatable topics | Assigned positions, then consensus |
| **Philosophical Chairs** | Binary debates | Physical movement shows position changes |

### Step 4: Discussion Norms

**Establish Explicitly:**

**Speaking Norms:**
- [ ] Use "I think..." not "The answer is..."
- [ ] Refer to text with page/line numbers
- [ ] Build on others' ideas ("I agree with ___ and want to add...")
- [ ] Respectfully disagree ("I see it differently because...")
- [ ] Invite others ("What do you think, ___?")

**Listening Norms:**
- [ ] Look at the speaker
- [ ] Don't interrupt
- [ ] Take notes on what others say
- [ ] Be ready to paraphrase another's point

**Teacher Role:**
- [ ] Pose questions, don't answer them
- [ ] Redirect when off-track
- [ ] Draw in quiet students
- [ ] Remain neutral on interpretive questions

### Step 5: Facilitation Moves

**If Discussion Stalls:**

| Problem | Move |
|---------|------|
| **Silence** | Wait. Count to 10 silently. Then rephrase or simplify question. |
| **One-word answers** | "Can you say more?" or "What makes you think that?" |
| **Off-topic** | "That's interesting. How does it connect to [text/question]?" |
| **Dominant voices** | "Let's hear from someone who hasn't spoken yet." |
| **Conflict** | "Let's find where you agree and where you differ." |

**If Discussion Goes Well:**

| Opportunity | Move |
|-------------|------|
| **Rich disagreement** | "We have two different readings. Let's explore each." |
| **Deep insight** | "That's significant. Can others build on that?" |
| **Student question** | "What a great question. Who wants to respond?" |
| **Connection made** | "How does that change our understanding of [earlier point]?" |

### Step 7: Novice Discussion Scaffolds

**For Classes New to Discussion:**

**Pre-Seminar Written Response (ESSENTIAL for novice groups):**
Before the discussion, every student must complete a short written response to the opening question or a provided prompt. This is not optional for novice groups — it guarantees every student enters the seminar with at least one prepared thought, which dramatically reduces the "no one talks" problem. Collect or check these before the seminar begins.

**Progression Plan:**

**Week 1-2:** Structured partner talk
- Practice one skill at a time (e.g., "cite the text")
- Short discussions (5-10 minutes)
- Teacher models extensively

**Week 3-4:** Small group discussion (fishbowl recommended)
- Groups of 4-5 students
- Assign roles (facilitator, note-taker, summarizer)
- Use sentence starters (retire starters by Seminar 3)

**Week 5+:** Full Socratic seminar
- Move to full circle by Seminar 4
- Student-driven discussion
- Teacher as observer

**Sentence Starters for Novice Discussers:**

| Purpose | Starter |
|---------|---------|
| **Making a claim** | "I think... because in the text it says..." |
| **Agreeing** | "I agree with ___ because..." |
| **Disagreeing** | "I see it differently. I think... because..." |
| **Building** | "Adding to what ___ said, I noticed..." |
| **Questioning** | "I'm wondering why the author..." |
| **Clarifying** | "Are you saying that...?" |

**Additional Novice Scaffolding Techniques:**

| Technique | How It Works | When to Use |
|-----------|-------------|-------------|
| **"Phone a Friend"** | A student who is stuck can call on a specific classmate for help or to co-answer | Reduces anxiety for reluctant speakers; first 2-3 seminars |
| **"Golden Passage" Bookmark** | Each student selects and bookmarks one passage they found significant before the seminar, ready to read aloud and explain | Guarantees every student has a text-grounded contribution prepared |
| **Pre-Discussion Partner Share** | Students share their written response with a partner for 2-3 minutes before the full group discussion | Builds confidence; lets students rehearse ideas in low-stakes setting |
| **Outer Circle Observation Sheet** | Students in the outer circle track specific behaviors (text citations, building on others, questions asked) using a structured observation form | Keeps outer circle engaged; provides peer feedback data |
| **Visual Participation Tracker** | Teacher (or student observer) draws lines between speakers on a seating chart to visualize discussion flow | Makes participation patterns visible; helps identify gaps |

## Output Format

Structure the discussion plan as:
1. **Text/Topic Overview**
2. **Essential Questions** (opening, core, closing)
3. **Discussion Structure** (format, timing)
4. **Norms and Expectations**
5. **Facilitation Guide** (moves for common situations)
6. **Teacher Cheat Sheet** — a one-page quick-reference card for the teacher to keep during the seminar, including: key questions, facilitation reminders ("Before you speak, ask yourself: Is this redirecting or taking over?"), time checkpoints, and emergency moves for stalls
7. **Student Preparation Materials**
8. **Assessment Tools** (tracking sheet, rubric)
9. **Debrief and Reflection Prompts**

## Quality Indicators

**Effective Socratic discussions:**
- [ ] Questions have no single right answer
- [ ] Students talk more than teacher
- [ ] Ideas build and connect
- [ ] Text is referenced frequently
- [ ] All students participate
- [ ] Thinking changes or deepens

**Common Discussion Pitfalls:**

| Pitfall | Sign | Prevention |
|---------|------|------------|
| **Teacher takeover** | Teacher talks >25% | Count talk time, practice wait time |
| **Ping-pong pattern** | Student→Teacher→Student | Redirect: "What do others think?" |
| **Surface level** | Quick consensus | Push with "But what about...?" |
| **Unprepared students** | Vague references to text | Require written prep, check it |
| **Same voices dominate** | 3-4 students do all talking | Use tracking, invite others explicitly |
