# Restorative Conversation Script

## Objective

Produce a complete restorative conversation script for a specific incident — preparation steps, the conversation itself using the five restorative questions, agreements, and follow-through plan. Output is the full script the teacher will use, not a generic protocol.

## When to Use

- After harm between students (verbal, physical, social, online)
- After a broken classroom agreement (significant, not minor)
- Re-entry after a removal or suspension
- Repair after an adult-student rupture (e.g., teacher lost their temper)
- Building toward a student-led repair conversation

## When NOT to Use

- Active safety threat — handle safety first; restorative conversation later
- Trauma where one party is unsafe or unwilling — need a different protocol with counselor
- Ongoing pattern requiring formal behavior plan — use `teaching_behavior_support_planner.md`
- General classroom culture work — use `classops_management_plan_and_norms.md`

## Instructions

### Step 1: Confirm Readiness

Before scripting the conversation, check:

- [ ] All parties safe (physically, emotionally) right now
- [ ] Acute escalation has cooled (typically ≥30 min)
- [ ] Adult facilitator is regulated (not running the conversation while still angry)
- [ ] Each party has had time to think
- [ ] Setting is private and uninterrupted
- [ ] Time is sufficient — short conversations leave wounds open

If any of these is "no," delay the conversation and address that condition first.

### Step 2: Pre-Conversation Preparation

For each party, plan separately:

**Adult preparation:**
- What is my goal? (Repair, not punishment.)
- What do I need to say without blame?
- What might I be missing? (Curiosity check — what's the story I'm telling myself?)
- How will I regulate if I get triggered mid-conversation?

**Student preparation (one-on-one before joint conversation, if joint):**
- "I'm going to ask you the five questions [name them]. Take a few minutes to think."
- Provide the questions in writing for processing time, especially for students who need it.
- Offer paper-and-pencil notes if helpful.

### Step 3: The Conversation Script

Use the **five restorative questions** as the structural backbone. Adapt language to the grade band.

**Opening (1–2 min):**
- "Thanks for being here. I want to talk about what happened, hear from each of you, and figure out together what to do next."
- "We're going to follow these five questions. We'll go [order]. While someone is talking, the rest of us listen."

**The five questions (~10–15 min total):**

1. **What happened?** (Each party tells their version, uninterrupted)
   - Adult moves: "Tell me what happened from your side." "Anything else?"

2. **What were you thinking at the time?** (Surfaces the internal experience)
   - Adult moves: "What was going through your head?" "What did you think was going to happen?"

3. **What have you thought about since?** (Reflection)
   - Adult moves: "Since this happened, what's been on your mind?" "What's stayed with you?"

4. **Who has been affected by what happened? In what way?** (Surfaces harm)
   - Adult moves: "Who else got pulled into this?" "How do you think [other party] is feeling?"

5. **What needs to happen to make things as right as possible?** (Forward — agreement)
   - Adult moves: "What would help repair this?" "What can each of us do?"

**Adult micro-moves throughout:**
- Reflect what you hear before responding: "What I'm hearing is…"
- Use silence — don't fill every pause
- Validate feelings without endorsing actions: "I can hear you were really frustrated. And — what we did still affected ___."
- Avoid "but" — use "and" to hold both truths

### Step 4: Naming Agreements

After question 5, name 2–4 specific agreements:

- What each party will do
- How and when (concrete)
- Who follows up and when
- What happens if the agreement is broken (return to the conversation, not escalation)

Write the agreements down in language students used.

### Step 5: Closing (1–2 min)

- Each party says one thing they took from the conversation
- Adult thanks each party for engaging
- Adult names the next step ("I'll check in with each of you on [day]")
- If appropriate, a handshake, fist bump, or simple acknowledgment

Don't force reconciliation that hasn't happened — closure is acknowledgment, not pretend friendship.

### Step 6: Follow-Through Plan

| When | Action | Who |
|------|--------|-----|
| Same day / next day | Brief check-in: "How are you doing?" | Teacher |
| 1 week | Have agreements held? | Teacher + students |
| 2 weeks | Is anything else coming up? | Teacher |
| If broken | Return to the conversation, not consequence ladder | All parties |

### Step 7: Documentation

Log briefly (for teacher records, not student-facing):
- Date, parties, summary of agreements
- What worked in the conversation
- What might inform future conversations with these students

### Step 8: When to Escalate Out

Restorative conversations are not always sufficient. Loop in counselor or admin when:

- Threats of harm continue
- One party is not safe
- Pattern of harm continues despite agreements
- An adult was harmed beyond what the teacher can address solo
- Family-level conversation is needed

Escalation does not abandon restorative — it expands the team.

### Step 9: Adult-Student Repair (Special Case)

If the teacher needs to repair after losing their temper:

- Lead with naming what you did, not justifying
- "Yesterday I raised my voice at you about ___. That wasn't okay. I'm sorry."
- Don't ask the student to comfort you
- Name what you'll do differently
- Invite (don't require) the student's response

### Step 10: Cultural and Linguistic Adaptation

- Provide translation if family is involved or student's L1 isn't English
- Adapt proximity and eye-contact expectations to cultural norms
- Honor different communication styles (some students will need to write rather than speak)
- For neurodivergent students, allow processing time and modality choice

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Run the conversation while anyone is still escalated
- Let the conversation become a lecture
- Force apologies or reconciliation
- Skip the follow-through — the conversation is half the work
- Use restorative as cover for punitive intent
- Replace specialist support when harm exceeds teacher scope

✅ **DO:**
- Cool down first
- Use the five questions as actual structure
- Reflect what you hear before responding
- Document agreements in students' language
- Schedule follow-through
- Loop in counselor or admin when scope exceeds restorative conversation
