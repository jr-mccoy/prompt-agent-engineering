# Classroom Management Plan & Norms Builder

## Objective

Produce a full classroom management plan for the school year. Output includes: classroom values frame, norms co-construction protocol, a graduated response continuum (not a punishment ladder), restorative practice integration, family communication system, and a first-30-days rollout schedule.

## When to Use

- Pre-year planning
- Mid-year reset after the plan stops working
- New teacher induction
- Transitioning from compliance/consequence to community/restorative
- Co-teachers aligning systems

## When NOT to Use

- Single-incident response — use `classops_restorative_conversation_script.md`
- Behavior intervention plan for a specific student — use `teaching_behavior_support_planner.md`
- Routines and transitions only — use `classops_routines_and_transitions_designer.md`

## Instructions

### Step 1: Anchor in a Values Frame

Surface 3–5 classroom values that drive everything else. Frame each as a **community commitment**, not a teacher rule.

| Value | What it sounds like | What it looks like |
|-------|--------------------|--------------------|
| [e.g., We take care of each other] | "I noticed you needed help — here." | Students checking in on absent peers |

Avoid generic "respect / responsibility / readiness" unless concretely defined. Values must be observable.

### Step 2: Norms Co-Construction Protocol

Provide a 30-minute protocol for co-constructing norms with students in week 1:

1. **Frame (5 min):** "We're going to build the norms for this classroom together because we all live here."
2. **Brainstorm (10 min):** "What do you need from each other to learn well?" In K–2, prompt with images. In secondary, sticky notes or shared doc.
3. **Cluster (5 min):** Group similar ideas into 4–6 norm categories.
4. **Phrase (5 min):** Convert each category to a positive, present-tense, observable norm. ("We listen when others speak" — not "Don't interrupt.")
5. **Commit (5 min):** Each student signs or marks the norm chart.

Provide example norm phrasings appropriate to grade band.

### Step 3: Response Continuum (Not Punishment Ladder)

Build a graduated response system. The principle: low-tier responses preserve relationship; high-tier responses are rare and always include repair.

| Tier | Behavior trigger | Response | Who acts | Repair component |
|------|-----------------|----------|----------|------------------|
| 1 — Quiet redirect | Off-task, minor disruption | Proximity, nonverbal cue, name use | Teacher | None needed |
| 2 — Brief private redirect | Repeated tier 1, off-task pattern | Whisper-level conversation | Teacher | None needed |
| 3 — Reset / check-in | Escalation, dysregulation | Reset space in room, brief co-regulation | Teacher | Brief acknowledgment after |
| 4 — Restorative conversation | Harm to peer, broken agreement | Structured restorative conversation (use `classops_restorative_conversation_script.md`) | Teacher + students involved | Required |
| 5 — Family + admin loop | Pattern, safety, repeated harm | Family contact, admin support, restorative conference | Teacher + admin + family | Required |
| 6 — Formal discipline | Safety threat, district-defined offenses | District protocol | Admin | Required as part of return-to-class plan |

Critical rule: **escalation is for safety and pattern, not for adult frustration.** State that explicitly in the plan.

### Step 4: Proactive Systems

The 80% of management that prevents the 20% of incidents:

- **Routines and transitions:** [Reference `classops_routines_and_transitions_designer.md`]
- **Active engagement structures:** [Quick checks, partner work, choice]
- **Relationship inventory:** [System for ensuring you have a personal connection with every student in the first 2 weeks — track it]
- **Greeting at the door:** [Daily, by name]
- **Closing ritual:** [Daily, briefly affirms community]
- **Predictable schedule:** [Posted, walked through, known to students]
- **Movement and brain breaks:** [Built into instructional blocks]
- **Choice within structure:** [Where students get genuine voice]

### Step 5: Restorative Practice Integration

Specify when restorative practices are used:

- **Community circles:** [Frequency — weekly is common; topics, structure]
- **Affective statements:** [Teacher language: "When ___ happens, I feel ___ because ___"]
- **Restorative conversations:** [After harm — see linked prompt]
- **Re-entry conferences:** [When a student returns from removal]

Build students' restorative vocabulary explicitly.

### Step 6: Family Communication System

| Type | Frequency | Mode | What's communicated |
|------|-----------|------|---------------------|
| Welcome | Week 1 | Letter / call / video | Plan, values, contact preferences |
| Positive | Weekly minimum, distributed across class | Phone / postcard / app | Specific positive observation |
| Concern | As needed | Phone first, then written | What happened, what we're doing, what we ask |
| Updates | Monthly | Newsletter / class page | Class-wide news |

Translation provisions for non-English-dominant families.

### Step 7: First 30 Days Rollout

| Week | Focus | Key moves |
|------|-------|-----------|
| Week 1 | Belonging + values | Daily greetings; norms co-construction; relationship inventory; predictable schedule |
| Week 2 | Routines | Teach + practice every routine: arrival, materials, transitions, dismissal, restroom, technology |
| Week 3 | Restorative + responsive | First community circle; re-teach norms with student examples; first private check-ins |
| Week 4 | Re-norm and adjust | Return to norms chart with student input; surface what's working / what isn't; adjust |

Each week, specify daily moves the teacher repeats.

### Step 8: Self-Care and Sustainability

Add for the teacher:

- **What you'll do when dysregulated:** [Reset move; permission to step away; co-regulation with adult]
- **What you'll let go of:** [Compliance battles that aren't safety or learning issues]
- **When to ask for support:** [Triggers to loop in admin, counselor, coach]

### Step 9: Equity Audit

- [ ] Norms include the experience of students who are most often disciplined
- [ ] Response continuum doesn't disproportionately escalate for minor infractions
- [ ] Tone and language are culturally responsive
- [ ] Family communication is accessible across languages and channels
- [ ] Restorative practices are restorative in fact, not punishment in restorative clothing

### Step 10: Plan Audit

- [ ] Values are observable, not abstract
- [ ] Norms co-constructed, not imposed
- [ ] Response continuum is graduated and includes repair
- [ ] Proactive systems are detailed before reactive systems
- [ ] First 30 days is concrete and feasible

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Build a rules list framed as prohibitions
- Skip the relationship inventory — without it, the rest collapses
- Treat restorative as soft — it's harder than punitive, not easier
- Assume the same plan works K–12 — adjust developmentally
- Save culture-building for after content — culture IS content for week 1

✅ **DO:**
- Co-construct norms with students
- Build a graduated continuum with repair at every level
- Front-load proactive design
- Communicate with families before incidents
- Audit for equity and sustainability

## Quality Indicators

- [ ] Values are observable and concrete
- [ ] Norms protocol takes ≤30 minutes
- [ ] Response continuum has 5–6 tiers with repair built in
