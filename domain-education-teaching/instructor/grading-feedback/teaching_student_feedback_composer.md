**Purpose:** Transform assessment results and observations into constructive, specific feedback that students can use to improve while maintaining motivation and growth mindset.

**When to use:** Writing comments on student work, preparing report card narratives, having feedback conversations, creating individualized learning goals.

**Input needed:**
- Student work or performance description
- Learning objectives assessed
- Rubric or success criteria
- Student context (age, needs, history)
- Feedback purpose (formative/summative)

## Instructions

Compose effective feedback through these structured steps:

### Step 1: Analyze Against Criteria

**Criteria-Based Assessment:**

| Criterion | Expectation | Student Performance | Gap |
|-----------|-------------|---------------------|-----|
| [Criterion 1] | [What's expected] | [What student did] | [Difference] |
| [Criterion 2] | [What's expected] | [What student did] | [Difference] |
| [Criterion 3] | [What's expected] | [What student did] | [Difference] |

**Overall Assessment:**
- Met expectations: [Which criteria]
- Approaching expectations: [Which criteria]
- Not yet meeting: [Which criteria]

### Step 2: Identify Specific Evidence

**Strengths (Be Specific):**

| Strength | Specific Evidence from Work |
|----------|----------------------------|
| [Strength 1] | [Exact example: "In paragraph 2, you..." or "Your equation on line 3..."] |
| [Strength 2] | [Exact example] |
| [Strength 3] | [Exact example] |

**Areas for Growth (Be Specific):**

| Area | Specific Evidence | Impact |
|------|-------------------|--------|
| [Area 1] | [Exact example] | [Why this matters] |
| [Area 2] | [Exact example] | [Why this matters] |

### Step 3: Apply Feedback Framework

**The "What's Working / What to Try Next" Structure:**

**Opening - Acknowledge Effort/Growth:**
[Recognize something genuine about the work or effort]

**What's Working (Strengths):**
[2-3 specific things done well, with evidence]

**What to Try Next (Growth Areas):**
[1-2 specific, actionable improvements]

**Next Step (Single Focus):**
[One clear action the student should take]

### Step 4: Calibrate Tone for Audience

**Age-Appropriate Language:**

| Grade Level | Vocabulary | Tone | Example |
|-------------|------------|------|---------|
| **K-2** | Simple, concrete | Warm, encouraging | "I love how you... Next time, try..." |
| **3-5** | Clear, specific | Supportive, direct | "You did a great job with... To improve, focus on..." |
| **6-8** | More formal | Respectful, balanced | "Your work demonstrates... To strengthen this further..." |
| **9-12** | Professional | Collegial | "This analysis effectively... Consider how you might..." |

**Adjust for Student Needs:**

| Student Context | Adjustment |
|-----------------|------------|
| **Struggling learner** | Lead with strengths, smaller next steps, more encouragement |
| **Advanced learner** | More direct, higher-level suggestions, stretch challenges |
| **Anxious student** | Reassuring tone, normalize errors, emphasize growth |
| **Unmotivated student** | Connect to interests, highlight authentic strengths |
| **ELL student** | Clear language, define academic terms, provide examples |

### Step 5: Make Feedback Actionable

**Transform Vague → Specific:**

| Vague Feedback | Specific Feedback |
|----------------|-------------------|
| "Good job!" | "Your introduction hooks the reader with a strong question." |
| "Needs more detail" | "In paragraph 2, explain WHY the character made this choice." |
| "Watch your grammar" | "Check sentences 3 and 7 for subject-verb agreement." |
| "Work on organization" | "Add a transition sentence between paragraphs 2 and 3." |
| "Try harder" | "Next time, include at least two pieces of evidence for each claim." |

**Actionable Feedback Formula:**
[Specific observation] + [Why it matters] + [What to do differently]

### Step 6: Build Growth Mindset

**Language That Promotes Growth:**

| Fixed Mindset Language | Growth Mindset Alternative |
|------------------------|---------------------------|
| "You're so smart!" | "Your hard work on this paid off!" |
| "You're not a math person" | "Math is challenging. Let's find strategies that work for you." |
| "This is wrong" | "Not quite yet. Here's what to revise." |
| "You always do this" | "This is an area we're working on developing." |
| "Perfect!" | "This shows real growth in [specific skill]." |

**Growth-Oriented Phrasing:**
- "Yet" language: "You haven't mastered this yet..."
- Process praise: "I can see you [specific strategy]..."
- Effort acknowledgment: "The revision you did really strengthened..."
- Progress focus: "Compare this to your work from [earlier], you've grown in..."

### Step 7: Feedback Delivery Method

**Written Feedback Best Practices:**

| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Write in margins near relevant text | Write only at end |
| Use student's name | Use generic language |
| Limit to 2-3 key points | Overwhelm with all errors |
| Provide model of what you want | Only point out problems |
| End with encouragement | End with criticism |

**Verbal Feedback Best Practices:**

| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Start with open question: "How do you feel about this?" | Start with judgment |
| Listen first | Lecture |
| Focus on one area | Address everything |
| Co-create next steps | Dictate |
| Schedule follow-up | Leave open-ended |

### Step 8: Complete Feedback Draft

**For Written Assignment:**

```markdown
[Student Name],

[Opening - personal, acknowledges something specific]

**What's working well:**
• [Strength 1 with specific evidence]
• [Strength 2 with specific evidence]

**To strengthen your work:**
• [Growth area with specific suggestion and example/model]

**Your next step:**
[Single, clear action to take]

[Encouraging closing]
[Teacher signature]
```

**For Report Card/Progress Report:**

```markdown
[Student Name] has demonstrated [strength area] this [grading period].
Specifically, [concrete example]. [He/She/They] is working toward
[growth area] and has shown [progress indicator]. To continue growing,
[Student] should focus on [specific next step]. [Encouraging statement
about potential/trajectory].
```

## Output Format

Structure the feedback as:
1. **Assessment Summary** (strengths and growth areas)
2. **Feedback Draft** (formatted for delivery method)
3. **Tone Check** (appropriate for student context)
4. **Actionability Check** (student knows exactly what to do)
5. **Follow-up Plan** (how to check on progress)

---

## Quality Indicators

**Effective feedback:**
- [ ] Specific (references exact parts of work)
- [ ] Actionable (student knows what to do next)
- [ ] Balanced (strengths AND growth areas)
- [ ] Timely (given while work is still relevant)
- [ ] Appropriate (tone matches student and context)
- [ ] Limited (focuses on 1-2 key areas)

**Common Feedback Pitfalls:**

| Pitfall | Example | Better Approach |
|---------|---------|-----------------|
| **Too vague** | "Good work!" | "Your thesis clearly states your argument." |
| **All negative** | "You need to fix X, Y, Z" | Start with strengths, limit to 1-2 growth areas |
| **Too much** | Red pen on every line | Focus on patterns, not every instance |
| **Unclear action** | "Be more careful" | "Before submitting, read aloud to catch errors." |
| **Person vs. work** | "You're careless" | "This work would benefit from more careful editing." |
| **Only summative** | Grade with no explanation | Explain what led to grade, what would improve it |

## Feedback Templates

**Quick Formative Feedback:**
"[Specific strength]. Next step: [One action]."

**Comprehensive Written Feedback:**
"[Acknowledgment]. [2-3 strengths with evidence]. [1-2 growth areas with suggestions]. [Next step]. [Encouragement]."

**Conference Opening:**
"Thanks for meeting with me. Before I share my observations, I'd like to hear: How do you feel about this work?"

**Report Card Narrative:**
"[Name] has [growth/achievement] in [area]. Evidence includes [specific example]. To continue progressing, [Name] should focus on [specific skill/behavior]."
