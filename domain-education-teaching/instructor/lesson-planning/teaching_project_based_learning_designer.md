**Purpose:** Design rigorous project-based learning (PBL) units using the Gold Standard PBL framework — featuring driving questions, sustained inquiry, authentic audiences, student voice and choice, and public products that demonstrate deep understanding.

**When to use:** Designing multi-week inquiry-based projects; converting traditional units to PBL format; integrating cross-curricular learning through projects; creating capstone or exhibition experiences; building student-driven learning experiences.

**Input needed:**
- Subject area(s) and standards
- Grade level
- Project duration
- Real-world context or community connection
- Available resources and constraints

## Instructions

Design the PBL unit through these structured phases:

### Step 1: Driving Question Development

The driving question is the engine of PBL. It must be open-ended, engaging, and require sustained inquiry to answer.

**Driving Question Formula:**
```
How can we [action verb] + [product/solution] + for [authentic audience] + that [addresses real need]?
```

**Generate 3 driving question options:**

| # | Driving Question | Type | Standards Addressed |
|---|-----------------|------|---------------------|
| 1 | [Question] | [Problem-solving / Design challenge / Investigation / Opinion-forming] | [Standards] |
| 2 | [Question] | [Type] | [Standards] |
| 3 | [Question] | [Type] | [Standards] |

**Driving Question Quality Check:**
- [ ] Open-ended (cannot be answered with a Google search)
- [ ] Engaging (students genuinely want to know the answer)
- [ ] Aligned to standards (academic rigor is embedded)
- [ ] Requires sustained inquiry (not solvable in one class period)
- [ ] Connects to the real world (students see why it matters)
- [ ] Accessible yet challenging (all students can enter, none can fully exhaust it)

### Step 6: Scaffolding and Differentiation

**Scaffolds for All Learners:**

| Need | Scaffold | When Provided |
|------|----------|---------------|
| **Research skills** | Source evaluation graphic organizer, guided research template | Phase 2 |
| **Collaboration** | Team contract, role cards (facilitator, recorder, timekeeper, researcher) | Phase 1 |
| **Time management** | Project tracker / milestone checklist | Phase 1, updated weekly |
| **Content knowledge** | Mini-lesson notes, vocabulary guide, concept map | As needed |
| **Presentation skills** | Presentation checklist, rehearsal protocol | Phase 3 |

**Differentiation by Student Voice & Choice:**

| Choice Point | Options |
|-------------|---------|
| **Subtopic focus** | Students choose which aspect of the driving question to investigate |
| **Product format** | Presentation, video, website, model, infographic, podcast, written report |
| **Team role** | Students select roles based on strengths and growth goals |
| **Research method** | Reading, interviews, surveys, experiments, fieldwork |
| **Depth of inquiry** | All students meet baseline; advanced students extend with independent questions |

## Output Format

Structure the PBL unit as:
1. **Project Overview** (1-page summary — driving question, standards, timeline, product, audience)
2. **Project Calendar** (day-by-day with activities and milestones)
3. **Mini-Lesson Plans** (for direct instruction of essential content)
4. **Student Materials** (research guides, graphic organizers, rubrics, reflection prompts)
5. **Assessment Package** (formative checkpoints + summative rubric)
6. **Scaffolding & Differentiation Plan**
7. **Logistics** (materials list, community partner contacts, space needs, technology)

## Quality Indicators

**Gold Standard PBL includes:**
- [ ] Driving question that students care about and revisit throughout
- [ ] Sustained inquiry (not a one-day "project" glued onto a traditional unit)
- [ ] Authentic audience beyond the teacher (community, experts, public)
- [ ] Student voice and choice embedded (not teacher-directed from start to finish)
- [ ] Multiple critique and revision cycles (not one-and-done)
- [ ] Individual accountability within group work
- [ ] Standards-aligned content embedded naturally (not forced)
- [ ] Reflection on both content learning and collaboration skills

**Common PBL Pitfalls:**

| Pitfall | Sign | Prevention |
|---------|------|------------|
| **"Dessert project"** | Project is a fun add-on after the "real" learning | Make the project BE the learning — embed standards throughout |
| **All product, no learning** | Beautiful poster but students can't explain the content | Assess content knowledge separately (quiz, reflection, individual component) |
| **Teacher-directed** | Every step prescribed; no student decisions | Identify specific choice points and honor them |
| **Group free-riding** | Some students do all the work | Individual accountability measures + role rotation |
| **No audience** | Products only seen by the teacher | Arrange authentic presentation opportunity early in planning |
| **Scope creep** | Project grows too ambitious for the time available | Set clear milestones and "minimum viable product" expectations |
