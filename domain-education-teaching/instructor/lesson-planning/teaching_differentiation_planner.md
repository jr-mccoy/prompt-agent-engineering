**Purpose:** Create differentiated learning experiences that meet students where they are while maintaining high expectations and common learning goals.

**When to use:** Planning lessons for mixed-ability classrooms; supporting students with IEPs or 504 plans; challenging advanced learners; adapting curriculum for ELL students.

**Input needed:**
- Learning objective(s)
- Current lesson/activity to differentiate
- Student population details
- Available resources and constraints
- Learning context profile (band + delivery mode)

## Instructions

Design comprehensive differentiation through these approaches:

### Band Adaptation Matrix (Apply before Step 1)

Set baseline differentiation expectations from the selected **Learning Band**.

| Band | Vocabulary Ceiling | Cognitive Load Expectations | Timing Norms | Assessment Artifact Defaults |
|------|--------------------|-----------------------------|--------------|------------------------------|
| **K-2** | Everyday language first; 1-2 new terms with visuals/realia/repetition | Very short directions; concrete tasks; immediate guided practice | 5-10 minute rotations; rapid transitions with explicit routines | Teacher observation notes, oral retell, picture sort/draw-and-label evidence |
| **3-5** | Student-friendly academic language; 3-5 terms explicitly taught/reviewed | Short multi-step tasks with scaffold choices; frequent checks | 10-15 minute stations; guided-to-independent release | Exit ticket, completed organizer, or short response aligned to tier goals |
| **6-8** | Content vocabulary (4-6 terms) with context and morphology support | Increasing abstraction; structured collaboration + independent application | 12-20 minute cycles; mid-task progress checks | Reasoning response, annotated work sample, or tiered product rubric evidence |
| **9-12** | Discipline-specific terminology (5-8 terms) with precision expectations | Sustained analytical work; self-management with milestone checkpoints | 20-35 minute work blocks; seminar/workshop pacing | Performance product, analytical paragraph, or standards-based rubric row scores |
| **Higher-ed** | Domain/professional vocabulary; explicit threshold-concept framing | Complex transfer tasks; critique and metacognitive reflection expected | 20-40 minute segments or weekly modular cycles | Applied brief, case response, discussion synthesis, or criteria-referenced artifact |
| **Adult learning** | Plain-language and workplace vocabulary; relevance-driven terminology | Problem-centered and experience-driven; immediate utility prioritized | 15-30 minute practical cycles; flexible pacing for competing responsibilities | Workplace task evidence, competency checklist, reflection/action plan, peer feedback note |

**Delivery Mode Rules (from Context Profile):**
- **In-person:** Include movement/space adaptations, live grouping logistics, and immediate support touchpoints.
- **Hybrid:** Distinguish in-room supports from remote scaffolds; define continuity between modalities.
- **Online asynchronous:** Convert teacher-led scaffolds into templates, exemplars, discussion protocols, and scheduled asynchronous feedback.

## Quality Indicators

**Effective differentiation:**
- [ ] Same learning goal for all students
- [ ] Different pathways to reach goal
- [ ] Based on assessment data, not assumptions
- [ ] Maintains rigor while providing access
- [ ] Feels respectful, not stigmatizing
- [ ] Is manageable for the teacher

**Common Differentiation Pitfalls:**

| Pitfall | Sign | Prevention |
|---------|------|------------|
| **Watering down** | Lower expectations for struggling students | Scaffold access, not rigor |
| **More of the same** | Advanced students just do more problems | Increase complexity, not quantity |
| **Labeling** | Groups called "high" and "low" | Use neutral names, flexible grouping |
| **Overcomplicating** | Teacher burns out | Start small, build gradually |
| **Ignoring data** | Grouping by hunch | Use pre-assessment consistently |
