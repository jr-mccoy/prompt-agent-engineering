# Workflow: Cold Start Return to School

## Who This Is For

- Adult enrolled (or enrolling) in formal education after 1+ years away
- 1–2 weeks before classes start, or in week 1
- Wants a concrete onboarding plan, not just "you'll be fine"

## What You'll Have at the End

- A 4-week onboarding plan with concrete weekly tasks
- A syllabus spine showing the semester's actual deadlines and weights
- A checklist of what's changed in higher ed since you were last enrolled
- 5–10 questions to ask your instructor or advisor in the first two weeks
- Three success criteria you've defined for the semester

## What You Need to Bring

- Course list (titles, credits, formats)
- At least one syllabus
- Years since last enrollment
- Current work / family / time situation
- 2–4 hours over 1–2 sessions

## The Chain

### Step 1 — Run the cold-start prompt

**Prompt:** [`../../adult-learner/adult_cold_start_return_to_school.md`](../../adult-learner/adult_cold_start_return_to_school.md)

**Input:** course list, syllabi, time situation, specific worries

**What you'll get:**
- Your context restated
- Syllabus spine (assignments, weights, dates)
- What's changed in higher ed (LMS, AI policies, citation tools, accommodations frameworks)
- 4-week plan with concrete weekly tasks
- Questions to ask in the first 2 weeks
- Your definition of success
- A watch-out list of risks specific to your profile

**Carry forward:** the onboarding plan, the questions list, the success definition

**Time:** 90–120 minutes

---

### Step 2 — Set up the LMS and tools (Week 1)

Before classes start (or in week 1):

- LMS access confirmed for every course
- Phone notifications configured (or deliberately disabled — pick a policy)
- Calendar synced with course meeting times
- Library account active; library tour scheduled (online or in-person)
- Citation management tool installed (Zotero is free and widely supported)
- Course readings accessible (digital books loaded, physical books ordered)

This is execution work, not a prompt. Schedule it on the calendar from Step 1.

**Time:** 2–4 hours of admin spread across week 1

---

### Step 3 — Take 15 minutes per instructor in the first two weeks

For each course, in the first or second week:

- Drop into in-person office hours, or schedule a 15-minute meeting, or send a thoughtful email
- Introduce yourself: returning adult, your prior context, your goal for the course
- Ask 1–2 of the questions from Step 1
- Listen

Why: a 15-minute introduction radically changes the rest of the semester. The instructor remembers you. They flex grading marginally on your behalf. They tell you what their pet peeves are. They tell you what previous returning students said worked.

**Time:** ~1 hour total across all courses

---

### Step 4 — Run a weekly review starting week 2

**Prompt:** [`../../../domain-productivity/reviews/reviews_weekly_systems_review.md`](../../../domain-productivity/reviews/reviews_weekly_systems_review.md)

**Input:** your week — what was planned, what happened, what surprised you

**What you'll get:** a closing of the week and setup for next; specifically catches whether the 4-week plan is being followed

**Time:** 20–30 min weekly

This is the operating cadence for the rest of the semester. Don't skip.

## What "Done" Looks Like by End of Week 4

- LMS, library, citation tool: working
- All instructors know you're a returning adult
- Calendar has every major assignment marked with start dates (not just due dates)
- You've turned in at least one assignment and gotten feedback
- You know which courses are going to be hard and you've adjusted
- The schedule from the time-architecture workflow is holding (or you know what to adjust)
- The mid-term crisis hasn't hit yet, but you have systems in place for when it does

## Common Failure Modes

| Failure | What to do |
|---------|-----------|
| Skipped instructor intros because you felt "too old" or "too senior" | Do them. Instructors love returning adults; you're not imposing. |
| Didn't actually configure the LMS — figured it out as you go | This works for two weeks, then you miss a quiz buried in a Canvas module. Set up early. |
| Didn't set up citation management because you'll "figure it out for the first paper" | Setting up Zotero takes 30 min. Doing it the night before a paper is due takes 3 hours and is miserable. |
| Mid-week 2 panic: "I'm behind, this isn't working" | Normal. Run [`workflow_imposter_calibration_age_cohort.md`](workflow_imposter_calibration_age_cohort.md). Calibrate before concluding. |
| Decided to "wait and see" before scheduling time blocks | Run [`workflow_working_learner_time_architecture.md`](workflow_working_learner_time_architecture.md). The week structures itself; you don't get to "wait and see." |

## After the Cold Start

Once the first 4 weeks are stable:

- Time architecture is in place; review monthly
- Writing-rust workflow runs before the first paper
- Subsequent academic work uses general college-student workflows: [`../college-students/`](../college-students/)
- If imposter feelings recur, the calibration workflow is on call

The cold start is one workflow. The rest of the semester runs on the systems it sets up.

## Special Cases

- **Returning to grad school after a master's gap:** the cold start still applies; the rust is usually in formal academic writing more than in study habits. Run [`workflow_writing_rust_recovery.md`](workflow_writing_rust_recovery.md) in parallel.
- **Returning to a different field than your prior degree:** combine this workflow with [`../career-changers/`](../career-changers/) workflows. The pivot context changes some of the questions to ask instructors.
- **Online / async-only program:** the LMS and tools setup is even more important. Add 1–2 hours to step 2.
- **Professional school (law, med, business):** the syllabus spine especially matters; these programs have lockstep curricula where falling behind compounds fast.
