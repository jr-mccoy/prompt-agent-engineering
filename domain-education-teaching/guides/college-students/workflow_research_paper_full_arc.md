# Workflow: Research Paper — Full Arc

## Who This Is For

- Undergrad or grad student with a 10+ page research paper assignment
- Multiple sources required (typically 5–15)
- Original argument with literature engagement
- 2–4 week timeline (sometimes a semester)

For shorter essays with limited research, use [`workflow_essay_draft_to_submit.md`](workflow_essay_draft_to_submit.md).

## What You'll Have at the End

- A researchable, debatable, scoped research question you constructed
- A Boolean search strategy you can defend
- A synthesis chart mapping where your sources agree, disagree, and disagree about
- A thesis you developed from the synthesis (not from a single source)
- An outline you populated
- A draft you wrote with revision feedback
- A clean annotated bibliography
- A submittable paper you are confident is yours

## What You Need to Bring

- The assignment prompt (paste verbatim into step 1)
- Access to your library's databases (Google Scholar, JSTOR, Project MUSE, discipline-specific databases)
- A rough topic area (even just a phrase like "labor unions in the 1930s")
- 15–40 hours of focused work spread over 2–4 weeks
- A way to manage citations (Zotero, EndNote, or a simple spreadsheet)

## The Chain

### Stage 1: Question and Search (Week 1)

#### Step 1 — Refine your research question

**Prompt:** [`../../learner-research/learnresearch_question_refinement.md`](../../learner-research/learnresearch_question_refinement.md)

**Input:** rough topic + assignment requirements

**What you'll get:** diagnostic critique across four tests — statement vs. question, scope, researchability, debatability. AI asks; you rewrite.

**Carry forward:** a researchable question

**Time:** 30–60 min

---

#### Step 2 — Build a search strategy

**Prompt:** [`../../learner-research/learnresearch_keyword_search_strategy.md`](../../learner-research/learnresearch_keyword_search_strategy.md)

**Input:** your refined question

**What you'll get:** concept extraction, synonym clusters, Boolean combinations — you construct every query

**Carry forward:** 4–8 search strings to run in your databases

**Time:** 45–90 min

---

#### Step 3 — Run searches and gather sources (no AI)

You actually go to the databases, run your searches, and pull 10–25 promising sources. Read abstracts. Skim. Reject the irrelevant ones. **Aim for ~1.5x what your assignment requires** so you can be selective in step 4.

**Time:** 3–6 hours

---

#### Step 4 — Vet source credibility

**Prompt:** [`../../learner-writing/learnwrite_source_credibility_evaluator.md`](../../learner-writing/learnwrite_source_credibility_evaluator.md)

**Input:** each candidate source (or a batch)

**What you'll get:** SIFT + CRAAP + lateral-reading walkthrough — you decide credibility

**Carry forward:** a vetted source list (your assignment minimum + 2–3 spares)

**Time:** 20–30 min per source, batched

### Stage 2: Synthesis and Argument (Week 2)

#### Step 5 — Read sources critically (no AI)

Read every vetted source. Take notes by source. Mark direct quotes you might use. **Do not paste full source text into AI to "summarize" it** — the paper requires that you actually engage with the sources, not that you read a summary.

If your professor permits AI summary assistance, see the discussion in [`../shared/socratic_vs_direct_decision.md`](../shared/socratic_vs_direct_decision.md). Default: read the sources yourself.

**Time:** 4–10 hours depending on source count and length

---

#### Step 6 — Build a source synthesis chart

**Prompt:** [`../../learner-research/learnresearch_source_synthesis_chart.md`](../../learner-research/learnresearch_source_synthesis_chart.md)

**Input:** your reading notes across all sources

**What you'll get:** mapped agreement, disagreement, and outliers across sources — you name the themes

**Carry forward:** a synthesis chart that reveals where the scholarly conversation has tension, gaps, or contested ground (this is where good thesis arguments live)

**Time:** 1–2 hours

---

#### Step 7 — Develop your thesis from the synthesis

**Prompt:** [`../../learner-writing/learnwrite_thesis_with_critique.md`](../../learner-writing/learnwrite_thesis_with_critique.md)

**Input:** your synthesis chart + a draft thesis

**What you'll get:** diagnostic critique of your thesis — does it engage the synthesis, is it arguable given the evidence, is the scope right for the page count

**Carry forward:** a defensible thesis grounded in the literature

**Time:** 30–60 min

---

#### Step 8 — Generate counterarguments

**Prompt:** [`../../learner-writing/learnwrite_counterargument_generator.md`](../../learner-writing/learnwrite_counterargument_generator.md)

**Input:** your thesis + synthesis chart

**What you'll get:** the strongest objections rooted in your sources — not generic counters

**Carry forward:** the counterargument you'll address in the paper

**Time:** 30 min

### Stage 3: Draft and Revise (Weeks 3–4)

#### Step 9 — Outline and draft

**Outline prompt:** [`../../learner-writing/learnwrite_outline_generator.md`](../../learner-writing/learnwrite_outline_generator.md)

**Input:** thesis + counterargument + synthesis chart

Then draft sections by yourself, in order or non-linearly (intro often last). Quote your sources directly. Mark every claim with the source it draws on.

**Time:** 6–15 hours depending on page count

---

#### Step 10 — Revise

**Prompt:** [`../../learner-writing/learnwrite_revision_socratic_coach.md`](../../learner-writing/learnwrite_revision_socratic_coach.md)

**Input:** complete draft

**What you'll get:** Socratic revision feedback — quoted passages, diagnostic questions, pattern names

**Pass count:** 2–3 passes. Pass 1 = structure and argument. Pass 2 = paragraph cohesion. Pass 3 = sentence-level.

**Time:** 2–4 hours

### Stage 4: Bibliography and Submission

#### Step 11 — Annotated bibliography

**Prompt:** [`../../learner-writing/learnwrite_annotated_bibliography_helper.md`](../../learner-writing/learnwrite_annotated_bibliography_helper.md)

**Input:** each source

**What you'll get:** guidance on annotation elements — summary, credibility, relevance — without AI summarizing for you

**Time:** 20–40 min per source

---

#### Step 12 — Format citations

**Prompt:** [`../../learner-writing/learnwrite_citation_helper.md`](../../learner-writing/learnwrite_citation_helper.md)

**Input:** source elements (author, title, date, publisher, etc.) you supply

**What you'll get:** correctly formatted MLA / APA / Chicago / discipline-specific citations

**Time:** 30–60 min

---

#### Step 13 — Integrity self-check

**Prompt:** [`../../learner-writing/learnwrite_academic_integrity_self_check.md`](../../learner-writing/learnwrite_academic_integrity_self_check.md)

**Input:** final paper + bibliography

**What you'll get:** structured audit before submission

**Time:** 30 min

## Time Budget

| Stage | Steps | Hours |
|-------|-------|------:|
| Question + search | 1–4 | 5–10 |
| Synthesis + thesis | 5–8 | 6–13 |
| Draft + revise | 9–10 | 8–19 |
| Bibliography + integrity | 11–13 | 2–5 |
| **Total** | | **15–40** |

A 10-page paper at the low end is about 15 hr. A 25-page paper at the high end is closer to 40 hr.

## When to Skip Steps

| Situation | Skip / modify |
|-----------|---------------|
| Question is assigned | Step 1 |
| Sources are assigned | Steps 2, 3, 4 |
| Single-source close-reading paper | Steps 6, 11 |
| Replication / methods paper (not argument) | Step 8 (counterarguments) — replace with limitations |
| You've done 5 of these before and have a process | Skim the workflow; use it as a checklist not a script |

## Common Failure Modes

| Failure | What to do |
|---------|-----------|
| **Question is too broad** ("the causes of WWI"). | Re-run step 1 with the prompt's scope test. Tight, debatable, fits page count. |
| **Found 30 sources, can't decide which to use.** | Step 4 + the synthesis chart in step 6 force a vetted ~10 most-relevant set. Don't try to use them all. |
| **Drafting without an outline.** | Stop. Run step 9 outline. The draft will go 3x faster from an outline. |
| **Pasted sources into AI to "summarize."** | The summary will erase the nuance that makes your synthesis interesting. Re-read at least the abstract + intro + conclusion yourself. |
| **Started the draft Week 4 with the paper due Week 4.** | Take the late penalty. Don't shortcut steps 5–8 — without synthesis, the paper has no argument. |
| **Stuck on the thesis after step 7.** | The synthesis chart in step 6 may be too thin. Either find 2 more sources or read more closely the ones you have. The thesis comes from the tension in the literature, not from your gut. |

## Special Cases

- **Lit review papers** — emphasize steps 5–8 heavily; the synthesis IS most of the paper.
- **Empirical papers** with original data — the research-question, draft-revise, and bibliography steps apply; the data collection is outside this workflow.
- **Case study papers** — use the workflow but treat the case material as a primary source the AI never summarizes.
- **Capstone or thesis-length** (40+ pages) — break into 3–4 cycles of this workflow per chapter. Run [`../../../domain-productivity/deep-work/deepwork_chunk_project_to_calendar.md`](../../../domain-productivity/deep-work/deepwork_chunk_project_to_calendar.md) for the multi-month schedule.
