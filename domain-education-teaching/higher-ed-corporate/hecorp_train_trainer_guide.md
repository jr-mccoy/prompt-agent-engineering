# Train-the-Trainer Guide

## Objective

Prepare an internal subject-matter expert (SME) — manager, senior practitioner, or designated trainer — to deliver a training session reliably. Output is a trainer's playbook, facilitator-skill build, common-question pre-builds, and a calibration plan so multiple trainers deliver the same session at consistent quality.

## When to Use

- Scaling a training across multiple sites/cohorts that L&D can't deliver alone
- Onboarding new internal facilitators
- Refreshing an experienced trainer who's drifted from the design
- Manager-led training programs where managers must facilitate, not lecture
- Buddy/mentor training (use as a light version)

## When NOT to Use

- Designing the training itself — use the appropriate module designer first
- Replacing professional instructional design — use this for *delivery* readiness, not *design* quality
- Single-trainer one-off — overkill; brief them informally

## Instructions

### Step 1: Separate Three Trainer Capabilities

A trainer needs three distinct capabilities. Build each:

| Capability | What it means | Most-overlooked? |
|------------|---------------|------------------|
| **Content mastery** | Trainer can answer reasonable follow-ups beyond the slides | Often present in SMEs |
| **Facilitation skill** | Trainer runs activities, manages time, includes voices, recovers from disruption | Often missing in SMEs |
| **Design fidelity** | Trainer delivers what was designed, not what they prefer to teach | Often missing in confident SMEs |

Most train-the-trainer programs assume content mastery and skip facilitation and fidelity — that's the failure mode.

### Step 2: Trainer Pre-Work Plan

Before facilitator training, each trainer should:

- Read facilitator guide cover to cover
- Watch a recorded delivery (yours or a senior peer's) if available
- Try the activities themselves as a learner
- Identify their own confidence gaps in writing

### Step 3: Trainer Workshop Structure

For a typical 2-day or 8-hour trainer workshop:

| Block | Time | Purpose |
|-------|------|---------|
| Frame & purpose | 30 min | Why this training, what success looks like |
| Walk through the design | 60 min | Section-by-section, with rationale |
| Demonstration | 60 min | Lead facilitator delivers a key segment |
| Trainer rehearsal — small chunks | 2–4 hr | Each trainer practices a segment, gets feedback |
| Common-question drill | 60 min | Anticipated tough questions, how to answer |
| Recovery moves | 30 min | What to do when things go off-script |
| Calibration plan | 30 min | How we'll keep delivery consistent |

### Step 4: Demonstrate, Then Rehearse

A trainer who's never seen the session run almost always under-delivers it. Sequence:
- Watch a complete demonstration (live or recorded)
- Trainer breaks the design into segments
- Trainer rehearses a 5–15 minute segment
- Peers and lead facilitator give specific feedback (not "great job")
- Trainer revises and re-rehearses

### Step 5: Build the Facilitator Playbook

Output a per-session playbook that:

- States objectives in trainer-language
- Provides minute-by-minute timing with cues ("at 12:00 transition to pair work")
- Names the exact prompt words for each activity
- Lists materials and tech setup
- Anticipates the top 5 questions with model answers
- Names common pitfalls and recovery moves
- Provides debrief / facilitation script for activities
- Includes a "if running short on time, cut X" decision

### Step 6: Common-Question Pre-Build

For the topic, anticipate questions trainers will get:

| Predicted question | Best response | Escalation if pressed |
|---------------------|----------------|------------------------|
| "What if the policy says X but in my region it's Y?" | "Good question — defer to your local policy; here's the contact" | [Name] |
| "Why do we do this when [other org] doesn't?" | "Here's our rationale; the data is..." | [Name] |
| "I disagree with this approach" | Honor the perspective, note it, redirect to the design | [Name] |

A trainer with these in hand recovers fast and consistently.

### Step 7: Facilitation Skill Build

Cover (with practice, not lecture):

- **Asking & waiting** — wait time, tolerating silence
- **Cold-calling humanely** — pair-talk first, then call the pair
- **Handling dominant talkers** — naming, structuring turn-taking
- **Drawing in quiet voices** — write-then-share, vote-then-defend
- **Debriefing activities** — patterns over individuals, name the take-away
- **Time discipline** — soft and hard cuts, recovery cuts
- **Disruption recovery** — pause, name, return; escalate if needed
- **Energy management** — reading the room, restoring focus

### Step 8: Design Fidelity & Permitted Variation

Be explicit about what the trainer can and cannot change:

| Element | Locked / flexible |
|---------|---------------------|
| Learning objectives | Locked |
| Required content (compliance, policy) | Locked |
| Activity structure | Locked |
| Examples | Flexible — must serve same point |
| Personal stories | Flexible — must support, not redirect |
| Sequence | Locked unless explicitly noted |
| Pacing | Trainer judgment within bounds |

This prevents drift across sites without making trainers robots.

### Step 9: Calibration & Quality Plan

After trainers go live:

- Observe each trainer at least once in first cycle
- Use a simple observation form keyed to the design (objectives covered? activities run? time managed?)
- Collect learner feedback per trainer (anonymized, summarized)
- Norming session quarterly — trainers compare notes, surface drift, share improvements
- Retire / reassign trainers who consistently miss the bar (kindly, with feedback)

### Step 10: Trainer Self-Assessment Tool

Provide trainers a self-check after each delivery:

- [ ] Hit objectives?
- [ ] Activities ran as designed?
- [ ] Time on track?
- [ ] All voices included?
- [ ] Hard questions handled with confidence and accuracy?
- [ ] Anything to flag back to L&D?

This builds reflective practice and surfaces issues early.

### Step 11: Continuous Improvement Loop

Trainers see what learners do. They are the org's best signal source for design improvement. Build:

- Monthly trainer-feedback channel (async or async + meeting)
- Quarterly design retro
- Annual content refresh
- Trainer of the [period] recognition tied to quality, not popularity

10. Continuous improvement loop

---

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Assume SMEs know how to facilitate
- Skip rehearsal — confidence without practice fails on day one
- Lock everything (creates wooden delivery) or lock nothing (creates drift)
- Forget the observation cycle — quality erodes invisibly
- Recognize popularity over quality — the wrong signal scales

✅ **DO:**
- Build content, facilitation, AND fidelity
- Demonstrate before asking trainers to deliver
- Build common-question and recovery banks
- State what's locked vs. flexible
- Observe and calibrate

## Quality Indicators

- [ ] Three capabilities addressed
- [ ] Trainer workshop scheduled with rehearsal time
- [ ] Facilitator playbook complete
- [ ] Common-question bank built
- [ ] Locked vs. flexible clearly mapped
- [ ] Observation and calibration cycle planned
