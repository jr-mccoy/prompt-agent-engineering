# Workflow: Proof-of-Work for the Pivot

## Who This Is For

- Career changer 70%+ through their pivot plan
- Has shipped portfolio work and is preparing to apply for target roles
- Needs to frame the pivot for hiring managers who'll see the work + the prior career and need both to make sense together

## What You'll Have at the End

- A pivot narrative that links your prior career, your learning, and the target role into one story
- An application packet: resume positioning, cover letter template, portfolio framing, talking points for interviews
- A list of 10–20 target employers prioritized by fit
- A 30-day application sprint plan

## What You Need to Bring

- Completed (or near-complete) skill-pivot plan
- Shipped portfolio (3+ public artifacts in the target field)
- Specific target roles / employers in mind
- Your prior career story
- Your current networking state (informational interviews done, mentors, etc.)

### Step 4 — Run the sprint with weekly reviews

Each week during the application sprint:

**Prompt:** [`../../../../domain-personal-development/prompts/agency/agency_weekly_review.md`](../../../../domain-personal-development/prompts/agency/agency_weekly_review.md)

- Portfolio additions / iterations

**Time:** 30 min/week

## The Pivot Narrative That Works

Hiring managers seeing a career changer ask three questions, usually unspoken:

1. **Why this pivot?** (Push from old field, pull to new, or both?)
2. **What evidence have you built that you can do the new work?**
3. **What does your prior career give you that other candidates don't have?**

Your pivot narrative needs to answer all three in 90 seconds of conversation or 200 words of cover letter. The articulation prompt produces this.

Failing the narrative typically looks like:
- Burying the pivot ("I've always loved technology...") — the hiring manager has read the LinkedIn; the prior career is visible
- Apologizing for the pivot ("I know my background isn't typical...") — defensive, not informative
- Overselling the transfer ("My years in marketing make me uniquely qualified for engineering!") — implausible; hiring managers know transfer isn't perfect
- Underselling the work ("I'm just learning...") — undermines your portfolio

The pivot narrative is honest about the change, evidenced by the work, and grounded in specific prior assets. Three sentences, fluent.

## Sample Pivot Narrative (Outline)

A 41-year-old former corporate accountant pivoting to data engineering:

> "After 14 years in corporate accounting, I kept noticing that the most valuable work I did was the data pipelines I built to make month-end close less painful. I've spent the last 14 months learning data engineering formally — Python, dbt, modern warehouses — and have shipped four projects publicly, including [project that uses real-world data]. I bring deep familiarity with what financial data actually looks like in production at scale, which is a gap most data engineers don't have."

The structure: trigger → pivot → evidence → asset prior career provides.

## Common Failure Modes

| Failure | What to do |
|---------|-----------|
| Cover letter is generic | The articulation prompt produces specific language. Use it. |
| Portfolio is strong but hidden in resume | Lead with portfolio links. Resume is secondary signal once portfolio exists. |
| Applications are scattered (50 random) | Sprint plan focuses on top 10–20 targets with tailored applications. Quality beats quantity by 5x for career changers. |
| No referrals | Career changers who get hired typically get there through 1–2 strong referrals plus public work. Build the referrals during the sprint (info interviews, community work). |
| Discouragement after 3 weeks of rejections | Normal. Most pivots include 20+ rejections. Run [`../../../../domain-personal-development/prompts/agency/agency_burnout_recovery.md`](../../../../domain-personal-development/prompts/agency/agency_burnout_recovery.md) if it's affecting health, but otherwise this is the work. |
| Tempted to lie about timeline ("I have 3 years of Python experience") | Don't. It surfaces in interviews. Be honest about the timeline; the portfolio carries the credibility. |

## After Landing the Role

Career-change roles are often a year-long transition. The first 90 days matter disproportionately.

- Run [`../../../../domain-personal-development/career-transformation/career_90_day_repositioning_plan.md`](../../../../domain-personal-development/career-transformation/career_90_day_repositioning_plan.md) day 1 of the new role
- Use [`../../../../domain-personal-development/prompts/agency/agency_foundation_session.md`](../../../../domain-personal-development/prompts/agency/agency_foundation_session.md) to rebuild your operating cadence in the new context

The pivot completes when the new role feels normal. Usually 12–18 months in.
