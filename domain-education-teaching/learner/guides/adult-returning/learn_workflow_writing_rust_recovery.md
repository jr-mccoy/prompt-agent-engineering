# Workflow: Writing Rust Recovery

## Who This Is For

- Adult returning to school after 5+ years; has been writing professionally in the interim
- Has a first essay coming due (not imminent — at least 2 weeks out)
- Anxious specifically about academic writing
- Tired of generic "academic writing is different" advice

## What You'll Have at the End

- A diagnosis of what's specifically rusty (not "everything")
- 1–3 short rehearsal exercises calibrated to your rust
- A list of professional-writing skills to keep (not all rust; much transfers)
- A set of carry-forward notes to refer to while drafting the real paper

## What You Need to Bring

- Years since last academic writing
- Type of academic writing assigned (essay, research paper, lab report, case analysis, etc.)
- Type of professional writing you've done in the interim
- A sample of recent professional writing (paste a couple paragraphs)
- The assignment prompt for the upcoming paper, if available

## The Chain

### Step 1 — Run the writing-rust recovery prompt

**Prompt:** [`../../adult-learner/learn_writing_rust_recovery.md`](../../adult-learner/learn_writing_rust_recovery.md)

**Input:** professional sample + assignment context + years since academic writing

**What you'll get:**

- Carry-forward notes for drafting
- What to keep from professional writing

**Carry forward:** the rust list + carry-forward notes

**Time:** 60–90 min

---

### Step 2 — Do the rehearsals

The rehearsals are 15–30 min each, designed to surface and address specific rust patterns. Common patterns:

- **Paragraph translation:** take a professional paragraph, translate to academic register, compare
- **Claim-evidence-warrant:** write 3 paragraphs in Toulmin structure on the assignment topic
- **Hedge gradient:** take a strong claim, rewrite at 5 levels of hedging, notice which level fits your discipline

Do them in a notebook or doc you won't submit. The purpose is calibration, not output.

**Time:** 45–90 min total

---

### Step 3 — Now write the real paper

Use the general essay workflow:

**Workflow:** [`../college-students/learn_workflow_essay_draft_to_submit.md`](../college-students/learn_workflow_essay_draft_to_submit.md)

Keep the carry-forward notes from Step 1 visible while drafting. Refer to them before each paragraph.

The first paper after rehearsal won't be perfect, but it will be much closer than a first paper without rehearsal. Each subsequent paper compounds. By paper 3, the rust is largely gone.

## What the Rust Usually Looks Like

For professional writers returning to academia, the rust is rarely "I can't write." It's typically:

| Pattern | Pro context | Academic context |
|---------|-------------|------------------|
| Bottom-line-up-front | Memo norms | Academic builds to conclusion; thesis up front, but argument develops |
| Bullet-heavy structure | Easy to scan in business docs | Academic favors connected prose, sentence-level transitions |
| Unhedged claims | Professional confidence valued | Academic favors qualified, evidenced claims |
| Direct address ("you") | Common in marketing / training | Rare in formal academic; varies by discipline |
| No citation | Internal docs assume shared context | Academic requires explicit attribution |
| Familiar/conversational voice | Trust building | Academic favors more formal register |
| Short paragraphs | Easy to read | Academic paragraphs are typically longer, more developed |

The rust differs by discipline. STEM lab reports differ from history essays from law-school memos from MBA case write-ups. The prompt calibrates.

## What to Keep

Professional writing skills that transfer directly:

- **Clarity.** Academic writing often over-complicates. Keep your clarity.
- **Audience awareness.** You wrote for skimming managers; academic readers are skimming too.
- **Editing discipline.** Most professionals self-edit; most undergrads don't. This is an advantage.
- **Outline use.** Outlines work.
- **Parallel structure.** Improves prose in any register.
- **Plain language where the discipline allows.** Not all disciplines require Latinate jargon.

## Common Failure Modes

| Failure | What to do |
|---------|-----------|
| Convinced your professional writing is "lesser" — overcompensating with academic-speak | The prompt explicitly addresses this. Academic writing isn't better, just different. Keep your strengths. |
| Skipped the rehearsals because they felt silly | The rehearsals reveal patterns you don't see in your own writing. Do them. |
| Re-wrote the rehearsal prose to be "more academic" without understanding why | The prompt is teaching the why. If the rehearsal feels mechanical, slow down. |
| Tried to fix everything at once | Pick 1–2 rust items to focus on per paper. The rest will follow. |
| First paper feedback flags new issues | Normal. The first paper is recalibration data, not final state. Read feedback carefully and iterate. |

## Special Cases

- **Returning STEM / engineering:** rust is often in narrative scientific writing (intro/discussion sections of papers) more than in formal lab reports. Pay attention to the move-pattern of academic introductions.
- **Returning legal:** professional legal writing translates surprisingly well to academic law; the harder rust is humanities-style argumentation if entering an interdisciplinary program.
- **Returning to a doctoral program after years away:** the rust applies; also re-read 3–5 papers in your sub-field to absorb the rhythm and vocabulary before drafting anything.
- **First essay is a personal-statement / SOP:** different genre entirely — use [`learn_workflow_prior_learning_articulation.md`](learn_workflow_prior_learning_articulation.md) instead.
