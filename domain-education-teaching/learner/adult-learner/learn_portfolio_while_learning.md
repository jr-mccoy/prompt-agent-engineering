# Portfolio While Learning: Ship Public Artifacts Before Mastery

## Objective

Help an adult learner mid-pivot design a portfolio that ships *while* they learn, not after. The output: 3–5 specific artifacts they'll build over the next 3–9 months, each one calibrated to signal the right level of skill at the time of shipping, each one compounding into the next, and each one targeting an audience in the destination field.

The principle is simple and uncommonly practiced: **publish work at your current level, not your aspirational level, but pick projects whose finished form is genuinely useful to the destination field.** Mid-pivot work, honestly labeled, beats invisible polished work every time.

## When to Use

- Adult learner has a pivot plan (see `adult_skill_pivot_self_study_plan.md`) and now needs the portfolio strategy
- Has been "learning" for 3+ months and has nothing public to show
- Wants to start applying or networking soon but doesn't think they're "ready"
- Feels their work isn't good enough yet and keeps delaying shipping

**Not for:**
- Pre-pivot: still deciding which field. Use `adult_credential_pathway_decision.md` first.
- Already employed in the target field with an existing portfolio — different problem
- Regulated fields where personal portfolios don't apply (nursing, law, accounting); see field-specific guidance

## Inputs You'll Provide

Required:
- Target role / field (from your skill-pivot plan)
- Current skill level honest assessment (beginner, advanced beginner, intermediate)
- Time per week available for portfolio work
- Where the field's audience lives (Twitter / X, LinkedIn, GitHub, Dribbble, Medium, Substack, specific Slack / Discord, conferences, podcasts)
- Existing related work (anything from your current job that could be safely shared / adapted; side projects; coursework)
- Your honest read on what you're afraid of about shipping (perfectionism, judgment, employer policy, family reaction, "I'll be exposed as not knowing enough")

## Constraints

### Must

- Match artifact difficulty to current skill level — beginners ship beginner work and label it appropriately
- Compound: each artifact builds on the prior; the portfolio reads as a learning trajectory
- Anchor on the destination field's audience and conventions; ignore generic portfolio advice
- Calibrate "good enough to ship" thresholds that the learner will actually accept
- Address the specific fears the learner names (these are usually rational and need real responses, not "just ship it")
- Include the *distribution* step in every artifact plan (where you'll post it, who you'll tell)
- Use "in public" learning as a feature, not a hidden weakness

### Must Not

- Recommend hiding the learner's current skill level
- Suggest the artifact has to be "perfect" or "industry-standard"
- Imply that no one will notice or care about portfolio work; in many fields the difference between hire and no-hire is the portfolio
- Recommend volunteering for unpaid work as the default; portfolio work doesn't require working for free
- Encourage shipping fabricated or misleading work (claiming credit for work the learner didn't do, inflating role on past projects, etc.)

## Instructions to the Model

### Phase 1 — Honest Level Audit (Diagnostic)

Ask:

> "If a hiring manager in your target field saw your current work and asked you what your skill level is, what would you honestly say? Beginner, advanced beginner, intermediate, advanced?"

Then probe one level deeper:

- What can you do without looking it up?
- What can you do with documentation?
- What can you do with significant help?
- What can you not yet do at all?

This is the calibration. Portfolio artifacts will be built at the "can do with documentation" level, labeled honestly, and shipped.

### Phase 2 — Field Convention Map (Direct)

For the target field, identify:

- Where do people in this field publish portfolio work? (GitHub, Dribbble, Medium, Substack, specific platforms, personal sites, journals)
- What format does portfolio work take? (Code repos, case studies, design files, written analyses, video walkthroughs, talks)
- What's the standard "case study" length? (1 page, 5 pages, 20 pages?)
- What signals "real" vs. "tutorial-following" in this field?
- Who are 5–10 mid-level practitioners whose portfolios the learner should study?

Don't generalize across fields. UX portfolios look different from data analyst portfolios look different from technical writing portfolios. Calibrate to one.

### Phase 3 — The Compounding Artifact Sequence (Direct)

Design 3–5 artifacts that compound. Each:

- Builds on the prior artifact's skill base
- Is sized for current-skill-level execution
- Has a clear distribution plan (where it'll be posted, who'll see it)
- Includes the "learning in public" framing where appropriate

Example for a marketing pro pivoting to data analytics, 6 months in:

| # | Artifact | Skill level | What it demonstrates | Where it ships | Time |
|---|----------|-------------|---------------------|----------------|-----:|
| 1 | Analysis of public dataset on personal blog, ~1,500 words | Beginner | SQL + basic statistical reasoning, can find a story in data | Personal Substack + LinkedIn | 20 hr |
| 2 | Dashboard built from job-postings scrape | Adv. beginner | Data collection, cleaning, viz | GitHub + LinkedIn writeup | 30 hr |
| 3 | Replication of a known industry analysis with critique | Intermediate | Critical evaluation; can question methodology | Substack + Twitter / X | 25 hr |
| 4 | Original analysis for a real org (volunteer / pro bono if possible, or own employer) | Intermediate | Real stakeholder, real data, real recommendation | LinkedIn case study, deck shared | 40 hr |
| 5 | Talk or workshop at a meetup on what you learned | Intermediate+ | Synthesis, communication, leadership | Conference / meetup, video posted | 20 hr prep |

The compounding is visible: each artifact builds on the last. By artifact 5, the learner has demonstrated SQL, statistics, communication, real-stakeholder work, and leadership.

### Phase 4 — Honest Labeling Standards (Direct)

Each artifact should be honestly labeled:

- "This is the first analysis I've done in this domain; I'd love feedback."
- "I'm 4 months into self-study; this is intentionally a foundation-level project."
- "I'm aware [X] is a more sophisticated approach; here's why I chose this one for this iteration."

Honest labeling is signal, not weakness. Hiring managers can tell when someone is hiding versus learning in public.

### Phase 5 — Distribution Plan (Direct)

For each artifact, specify:

1. Primary platform — where it lives permanently
2. Distribution platforms — where you announce it (LinkedIn post, Twitter thread, relevant Slack)
3. Direct outreach — 3–5 specific people you'll send it to ("I'd love your thoughts when you have 10 minutes")
4. Cross-link — how it links to your prior and future artifacts (a portfolio that tells a story, not 5 disconnected files)

Most portfolios fail not at making the work but at distributing it. Half of the time-budget for each artifact should be on distribution and follow-up.

### Phase 6 — Address the Fears (Socratic + Direct)

For each fear the learner named, give a real response.

**"My current employer won't like me publishing about a pivot field."**
Direct response: Check your contract. Most allow personal writing. If concerned, write analyses about general industry problems, not your employer or its competitors. Avoid trade secrets. Use a pen name if needed. Many people have done a pivot quietly without burning bridges.

**"I'll be exposed as not knowing enough."**
Reframe: At your current skill level, you don't know enough to be a senior practitioner. You know enough to be a beginner who is learning. That is what your portfolio should show. The risk of looking like a beginner is much smaller than the risk of being invisible.

**"What if hiring managers Google me and see beginner-level work?"**
Direct: They will Google you, and they will find your work. Beginner work that's been shipped and labeled is much better than no work. Beginner work that pretends to be senior work is much worse than no work. Ship at your level.

**"What if I learn something wrong in the article?"**
Direct: You will. Edit it. Add a correction note. This is what serious practitioners do. Showing the correction is itself a signal of seriousness.

**"My family thinks this is silly."**
That's a separate conversation, not a portfolio problem. Continue with the portfolio. Bring family along when there's a concrete result.

### Phase 7 — The Shipping Cadence (Direct)

Set a publishing cadence the learner will actually keep:

- Major artifact every 6–8 weeks
- Small public thought (LinkedIn post, tweet thread, Substack short) every 1–2 weeks
- Update one existing artifact (improve, extend, add follow-up) every month

Cadence beats perfection. A consistent monthly cadence at modest quality outpaces irregular bursts of trying-to-be-great work.

## Output Format

A single deliverable:

1. **Honest Level Audit** — current skill level, what you can do at each level of independence
2. **Field Convention Map** — where work lives in your target field, what it looks like, who to study
3. **Compounding Artifact Sequence** — 3–5 specific artifacts with skill level, demo, platform, time
4. **Labeling Standards** — language to use to honestly position the work
5. **Distribution Plan** — for each artifact, where and to whom
6. **Fears Addressed** — direct response to each fear named
7. **Cadence** — what you'll ship weekly / monthly
8. **First Artifact Spec** — the artifact you'll start on this week, with deliverable defined

Length: 1,800–3,500 words.

## Verification

- [ ] Did I match artifact difficulty to honestly-assessed current skill?
- [ ] Do artifacts genuinely compound (each builds on the prior)?
- [ ] Are platforms specific (named) to the target field?
- [ ] Did I respond to specific fears with real responses, not "just ship it"?
- [ ] Did I include distribution / follow-up time in the time budget?
- [ ] Did I avoid recommending hiding skill level or misrepresenting work?
- [ ] Does the first artifact have a clear week-1 deliverable?

## False-Positive Prevention

This prompt does **not**:
- Promise that shipping will produce a job offer
- Recommend false credentials or misrepresenting work
- Suggest violating employer agreements; the prompt errs on the conservative side for employer policy questions
- Substitute for the underlying skill-building (the artifacts require real work)
- Treat the portfolio as a substitute for credentialing in fields that require credentials

## Worked Example (Outline)

A 39-year-old senior associate at a law firm pivoting to legal tech / contract automation, 4 months into self-study:

- Honest level audit: Intermediate at legal substance, beginner at JavaScript / web tech, advanced beginner at no-code automation tools
- Field convention: case studies on Medium / personal blog, GitHub for technical work, LinkedIn for thought leadership, specific Slack communities for legal tech (LawSites etc.)
- Compounding sequence:
  1. Case study: "Three contract review workflows I built with no-code tools" — 1,500 words, screenshots
  2. Open-source: simple GitHub repo with a contract-clause parser using regex + small LLM call
  3. Critique: "Why most CLM (contract lifecycle management) tools don't actually save time — a practicing lawyer's analysis"
  4. Real consulting engagement: pro-bono automation for a legal aid org, documented
  5. Talk at a local legal tech meetup
- Labeling: "I'm a practicing attorney learning to build; this is my self-taught engineering — feedback welcome"
- Distribution: Each artifact gets a Medium post, LinkedIn announcement, direct DM to 3 legal-tech founders the learner has been following
- Fears addressed: firm policy (drafted a clear "personal projects on personal time" stance with HR); imposter (the firm has him doing senior work; the gap is a vocabulary gap, not a capability gap)
- First artifact this week: Outline the first case study; install no-code tool of choice; build one workflow end-to-end
