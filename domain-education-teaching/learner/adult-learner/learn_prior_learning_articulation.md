# Prior Learning Articulation: From Career Experience to Academic Currency

## Objective

Help an adult learner translate years of career and life experience into academic currency: credit-for-prior-learning (CPL / PLA) portfolio narratives, graduate-school statement-of-purpose paragraphs, application essays, or course-equivalency petition documents. The output is artifact-ready language that names experience honestly, maps it to academic competencies, and avoids both undervaluing (modest "I just did my job") and overvaluing (resume inflation).

## When to Use

- Adult applying to undergraduate or graduate program who needs to write an application
- Returning learner whose institution accepts CPL / PLA portfolios for credit toward a degree
- Adult preparing course-equivalency or waiver petition (e.g., "I've been doing X for 10 years; can I skip the intro course?")
- Career changer writing a personal statement that needs to explain the pivot
- Working professional applying for executive education or fellowship that values experience

**Not for:**
- Writing a resume — use a resume-specific tool / template
- Writing a cover letter for a job — different genre
- LinkedIn profile updates

## Inputs You'll Provide

Required:
- Target artifact (CPL portfolio for course X, SOP for program Y, course-equivalency petition for course Z, application essay)
- The institution / program / department being applied to
- Specific requirements (page limit, prompts, rubric if available)
- Your full career history (roles, employers, years, key responsibilities)
- Major projects or accomplishments (3–10 most relevant; specific outcomes preferred)
- Your reason for pursuing this program (the *why*; without this, the artifact will be generic)

Useful:
- Sample artifacts the program publishes as exemplars
- Specific course descriptions if writing a CPL portfolio
- Any "competencies" or "learning outcomes" the program lists publicly

## Constraints

### Must

- Map experience to specific named competencies / learning outcomes the institution recognizes
- Use concrete, dated, specific instances ("In 2019, I led a 6-person team redesigning…") not vague generalizations
- Stay in the learner's voice; the artifact must sound like them, not like a model's generic SOP
- Honor scope limits (page count, word count) given by the institution
- Surface where experience is *equivalent to* coursework vs. where it's *complementary but not equivalent*; petitioning for equivalence where it doesn't hold wastes everyone's time
- Use the learner's *actual* projects as evidence — fabricated examples are non-starters here

### Must Not

- Inflate accomplishments ("I single-handedly transformed the company"). Reviewers recognize inflation; it backfires.
- Underclaim ("I just did my job"). Reviewers can't read minds; understatement loses credit you've earned.
- Generate generic SOP language (every essay says "I'm passionate about X"). Bland generic essays read as effort-light.
- Claim equivalence to coursework the learner hasn't actually done equivalent work to
- Write the artifact in academic-speak unfamiliar to the learner; the learner has to be able to defend the language in interview
- Invent specific projects, dates, employers, or outcomes; the model only restructures and rephrases learner-supplied facts

## Instructions to the Model

### Phase 1 — Career-to-Competency Mapping (Direct + Diagnostic)

Step 1a — Get the experience inventory:

> "Walk me through the 3–5 most significant projects or roles in your career. For each: what was the situation, what did you do, what was the outcome, what did you learn?"

Step 1b — Identify target competencies:

For SOPs: extract from the program's stated learning outcomes or "what we value" language.

For CPL portfolios: pull directly from the course description and learning outcomes for the courses the learner is petitioning credit for.

For application essays: derive from the prompt and the program's published values.

Step 1c — Cross-map:

| Experience | Maps to competency | Strength of fit | Notes |
|------------|---------------------|----------------|-------|
| Led migration of 200-person team to new CRM | "Change management" learning outcome from MGMT 540 | Strong | Quote specific change-management framework used (Kotter, ADKAR) |
| Negotiated 8 enterprise contracts $5M+ | "Strategic negotiation" outcome | Strong | Specific stakeholder mapping process is portfolio-grade |
| Mentored 15 junior team members | "People development" outcome | Moderate | Need to surface specific structured practice, not "I mentored them" |

Surface fits at all strengths — strong, moderate, weak — and flag honest gaps.

### Phase 2 — The Articulation Drafts (Direct)

Produce draft language for the target artifact, in the learner's voice. Use these patterns:

**Pattern A — STAR-style with academic framing.**
> "While leading [specific project] at [employer] in [year], I confronted [specific challenge]. I applied [named framework or approach] by [specific actions], resulting in [measurable outcome]. The experience taught me [specific insight], which I now want to formalize through [target program / course]."

**Pattern B — Competency claim with evidence.**
> "I have developed [competency] through [specific roles, durations]. Most directly, in [year], I [specific action and outcome]. I can demonstrate [skill] at the level expected of someone who has completed [equivalent coursework] because I have [specific evidence: artifacts I produced, decisions I made, outcomes I delivered]."

**Pattern C — The transition narrative (for SOPs that ask about career change).**
> "After [number] years in [field], I came to see [specific insight that prompted the change]. The fields share [genuine overlap], but [specific gap] motivates this program. What I bring is [3-4 specific assets]. What I need is [3-4 specific gaps the program fills]."

Draft 2–4 paragraphs. Have the learner read aloud. Adjust voice if it doesn't sound like them.

### Phase 3 — Evidence Inventory (Direct)

For each competency claim, list the evidence the learner can produce on request:

- Documents (presentations, reports, contracts you authored)
- Letters (recommendation letters from people who can speak to specific work)
- Outcomes (metrics, awards, promotions)
- Direct artifacts (code repos, design portfolios, published work)

CPL portfolios require this inventory upfront. Even SOPs and application essays benefit from knowing what evidence backs each claim — reviewers may probe in interview.

### Phase 4 — Boundary Honesty (Direct + adversarial)

Surface what the experience is **not** equivalent to:

- "Your 10 years of operations management is excellent preparation for the operations course, but it's not equivalent to the *theoretical statistics* course; that's a separate skill you'll genuinely need."
- "Your portfolio of design work is strong; your gap is in the formal *user research* method coursework. The portfolio could earn equivalence for the studio course but not for the research-methods course."
- "Your fluency in Spanish from family use is significant but not equivalent to the program's *linguistic structure of Spanish* course; the program will likely give credit for the language requirement but not the linguistics course."

Honest boundaries strengthen the petition / application by showing self-awareness. Reviewers respect this.

### Phase 5 — Voice Check (Socratic)

Read the draft aloud (or have the learner do so). Ask:

- Does this sound like me speaking?
- Would I be embarrassed to read this to my partner / a friend?
- Is there a sentence here I couldn't defend in a 15-minute interview?
- Is anything in here that's not literally true or that I'd need to qualify?

Revise based on the answers. Voice mismatch is the #1 reason application essays don't land.

### Phase 6 — Final Format Check (Direct)

Conform to:
- Word / page count
- Required sections / prompts
- Formatting conventions (CPL portfolios often have rubric-mapped sections)
- Submission format (PDF, online form, etc.)

## Output Format

A single deliverable: the artifact draft(s) plus an evidence inventory.

1. **Competency Map** — table mapping experience to target competencies
2. **Draft Artifact** — the actual text of the SOP / essay / portfolio narrative
3. **Evidence Inventory** — what backs each claim
4. **Boundary Honest List** — what experience is not equivalent to
5. **Voice Check Notes** — items the learner flagged for revision
6. **Format Confirmation** — word count, prompt coverage, requirements met

Length: artifact varies by requirement (typically 500–2,500 words for the artifact itself); supporting sections add 800–1,500 words.

## Verification

- [ ] Are all claims tied to specific, dated, learner-supplied experiences?
- [ ] Did I avoid both inflation and undervaluation?
- [ ] Does the artifact sound like the learner's voice?
- [ ] Are competencies mapped to the specific institution's language, not generic?
- [ ] Did I name what the experience is NOT equivalent to?
- [ ] Is the evidence inventory specific enough to produce on request?
- [ ] Did I respect word/page limits?

## False-Positive Prevention

This prompt does **not**:
- Invent experience, projects, dates, or outcomes
- Claim credit-equivalency for coursework the learner hasn't done equivalent work to
- Replace the institution's specific CPL / PLA assessment process
- Promise admission, credit, or financial aid outcomes
- Replace recommendation letters or other application components

If the learner can't supply specific concrete examples for a claim, the model surfaces "this claim needs evidence we don't have" and either trims the claim or asks the learner to think of a different example that fits.

## Worked Example (Outline)

A 51-year-old applying to a master's in public administration with 25 years in city government:

- Career experience: ran a city department of 80 people, managed a $40M budget, led adoption of new permit system, navigated 3 mayoral transitions
- Target program's competencies: public budgeting, policy analysis, change management, stakeholder management, ethical leadership in public service
- Strong fits: budgeting (direct line for 25 years), change management (system migration), stakeholder management (mayoral transitions)
- Moderate fits: policy analysis (consumed analyses, produced operational analyses, but didn't author policy proposals at scale)
- Boundary honest: ethical leadership in public service — the learner has *lived* this but hasn't done the *coursework on the ethical frameworks*; the program will provide that grammar
- Draft SOP frames the pivot as: "After 25 years building operational excellence, I want the theoretical grounding to influence policy upstream of operations."
- Evidence inventory includes: 3 specific budget cycles she can document, 2 mayors she's worked under who could write letters, the permit-system migration project plan she still has access to
- Voice check: she's not "passionate about transforming government"; she's "interested in moving from running departments well to shaping policy that defines what departments are for." Use her voice.
