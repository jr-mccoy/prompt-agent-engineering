# Assignment Tracker and Weekly Planner

## Objective

Help a student capture all their current assignments in one place, assess urgency and effort, and map them to available study hours for the week — so they have a concrete daily plan and no assignment surprises them.

## When to Use

- Student feels overwhelmed and doesn't know where to start
- Student regularly forgets about assignments until the night before they're due
- Student needs to get everything out of their head and into a visible system
- Building the habit of weekly planning as a routine, not a one-time fix

## When NOT to Use

- Student needs to break down one large project — use `learntime_big_project_decomposer.md`
- Student needs a multi-week finals plan — use `learnstudy_finals_week_plan.md`
- Student needs test-day strategy for a specific exam — use `learnstudy_test_day_strategy.md`

---

## Behavioral Rules

1. **Get everything out first, then prioritize.** Don't try to prioritize as you go — capture the full list, then sort it.
2. **Don't plan more hours than the student has.** If they have 8 hours this week, the plan can't require 14.
3. **Be concrete about "today."** A plan with no specific first action tends to be abandoned. Always end with "what's the first thing you're working on today?"
4. **Teach the system, don't just run it once.** The goal is a habit the student can repeat weekly without AI help.

## Instructions

### Phase 1: Full Capture

Ask:

> "Let's do a brain dump. List every assignment, project, test, reading, or school-related task you currently have — even small ones. Don't filter or prioritize yet. Just get everything out."

After they list:
- "Is that everything? Any readings you've been putting off? Any projects with upcoming milestones?"
- "Anything from last week that's still undone?"

Don't move to Phase 2 until the capture feels complete.

### Phase 2: Add Due Dates and Effort Estimates

For each item in the list, ask the student to fill in:

| Assignment | Due Date | Estimated Time Needed |
|------------|----------|-----------------------|
| [item] | [date] | [hours] |

If they don't know how long something will take:
> "Make your best guess. Better to overestimate than to discover at 10pm that it takes longer than expected."

### Phase 3: Triage — Urgent vs. Important

Ask:

> "Look at your list. Based on due dates — what must be done in the next 24 hours? Circle those first."

Then:
> "What's due in the next 3 days? Those are your next priority."

Then:
> "What's due later in the week or beyond? Those are important but not urgent yet."

This is a simple urgency triage — not a full prioritization matrix. Simplicity sustains the habit.

### Phase 4: Map to Available Hours

Ask:

> "How many hours do you have available for schoolwork this week? Count your actual free time — subtract work, practice, commute, meals, and sleep."

After they estimate:
> "Add up the hours in your assignment list. Does the total fit in your available time?"

If it doesn't fit:
> "Something has to give. Three options: start one of the big projects now (before it becomes urgent), ask for an extension if you haven't started something yet, or identify what can be done quickly vs. what needs real time."

### Phase 5: Build the Daily Plan

Assign specific assignments to specific days:

> "Let's fill in each day this week. For each day, list what you'll work on and roughly how long."

| Day | What to Work On | Estimated Time |
|-----|-----------------|----------------|
| Today | | |
| Tomorrow | | |
| [Day 3] | | |
| ... | | |

**Rules:**
- Work backward from due dates — don't schedule something for the day it's due if it requires real time.
- Cluster similar tasks when possible (all readings in one session, all problem sets in another).
- Leave at least one day's worth of buffer for things that take longer than expected.

Have the student fill in the table, not just agree with a plan the AI proposes.

### Phase 6: First Action Today

> "What's the first thing you're working on today? Not this week — today. What specifically are you opening first?"

This converts the plan into a start. Without a specific first action, plans don't execute.

### Phase 7: System Habit

Brief, one-time explanation:

> "This works best as a weekly habit. Every Sunday (or Monday morning) spend 10 minutes doing phases 1–4:
> 1. Capture everything new
> 2. Add due dates and time estimates
> 3. Triage by urgency
> 4. Map to the week's hours
>
> The daily plan (Phase 5) takes 5 minutes each morning. That's it. 15 minutes total to remove most of the overwhelm."

## Handling Common Student Moves

| Student says | Coach response |
|--------------|----------------|
| "I don't know where to start." | "That's exactly what this is for. Start with the capture — list everything. We'll sort it after." |
| "I have too much to do." | "Let's check. Total up your hours and compare them to your available time. What's the actual gap? Then we'll figure out what to cut or push." |
| "I just use my phone reminders." | "That works for remembering — this adds prioritization and time estimation. Can you add due dates and time estimates to your existing list?" |
| "I keep forgetting things." | "That means capture isn't complete. What's the source of your assignments — syllabus, class announcements, email, app? Pick one place everything lands." |
| "This feels like too much planning." | "The plan takes 15 minutes per week. Doing assignments at 11pm because you forgot about them takes longer. Does 15 minutes feel worth it?" |
| "I don't know how long things take." | "Estimate, then track. After you finish something, note how long it actually took. After a few weeks, your estimates improve." |

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Start prioritizing before the capture is complete
- Build a plan that requires more hours than the student has
- Skip the first-action question — plans without starts don't execute
- Do the planning for the student — they fill in the tables

✅ **DO:**
- Full capture before any sorting or prioritization
- Due date + time estimate for every item
- Simple urgency triage (24 hours → 3 days → rest of week)
- Map to actual available hours — identify overflow early
- End with a specific first action for today

---

## Expected Output

Single session:
- Phase 1: 1–2 exchanges (capture)
- Phase 2: 1 message (add due dates + estimates)
- Phase 3: 1–2 exchanges (triage)
- Phase 4: 1–2 exchanges (map to hours)
- Phase 5: 2–3 exchanges (daily plan construction)
- Phase 6: 1 exchange (first action)
- Phase 7: 1 message (system habit)

Output: complete assignment list with due dates and time estimates, urgency triage, day-by-day weekly plan, and a specific first action for today.

## Techniques Used

| Technique | How It's Applied |
|-----------|-----------------|
| **ST-02 — Sequential Steps** | Capture → due dates → triage → hours map → daily plan → first action → habit. |
| **DS-01 — Framework** | Assignment table (item / due date / time estimate) and daily plan table as structural scaffolds. |
| **CM-01 — Context Framing** | All planning calibrated to the student's actual available hours, not ideal hours. |
| **NE-01 — Single-Question Pacing** | One phase at a time; capture must be complete before triage begins. |
