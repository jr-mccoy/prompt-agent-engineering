## Top 10 Prompt Ideas for Finance & Economics

### 1. Financial Statement Analyzer
Systematically analyze financial statements (income statement, balance sheet, cash flow) to identify trends, anomalies, strengths, and concerns. Calculate key ratios, compare to industry benchmarks, and flag items requiring deeper investigation.

### 2. Investment Thesis Builder
Construct rigorous investment theses with clear catalysts, risk factors, valuation methodology, and exit criteria. Include explicit assumptions, sensitivity analysis on key variables, and disconfirming evidence that would invalidate the thesis.

### 3. Risk Assessment Framework
Evaluate financial risks across multiple dimensions (market, credit, liquidity, operational, regulatory) with probability estimates, potential impact quantification, correlation analysis, and mitigation strategy recommendations.

### 4. Economic Scenario Modeler
Develop multiple economic scenarios (base, optimistic, pessimistic, stress) with internally consistent assumptions across variables (GDP, inflation, rates, employment). Trace implications for specific sectors, assets, or business decisions.

### 5. Valuation Model Auditor
Review valuation models (DCF, comparables, precedents) for methodological soundness, assumption reasonableness, sensitivity to key inputs, and common errors. Identify areas where different assumptions would materially change conclusions.

### 6. Budget Variance Investigator
Analyze budget vs. actual variances to identify root causes, distinguish one-time from recurring factors, assess forecast accuracy patterns, and recommend process improvements for future planning cycles.

### 7. M&A Due Diligence Checklist Generator
Create comprehensive due diligence frameworks tailored to specific deal types and industries. Cover financial, legal, operational, commercial, and integration considerations with prioritized focus areas.

### 8. Regulatory Compliance Mapper
Map regulatory requirements (SEC, banking regulations, tax code) to specific business activities. Identify compliance gaps, deadlines, documentation requirements, and risk areas requiring attention.

### 9. Personal Financial Planning Scenario Analyzer
Model personal financial scenarios (retirement, education funding, major purchases) with multiple assumption sets. Calculate required savings rates, illustrate trade-offs, and stress-test against adverse scenarios.

### 10. Credit Analysis Structurer
Evaluate creditworthiness systematically covering capacity, capital, conditions, character, and collateral. Generate risk ratings, covenant recommendations, and monitoring triggers for credit decisions.

## Relevant Prompt Engineering Techniques

### Tier 1: Essential Techniques for Finance

#### NE-11: Embedded Calculation Formulas
**Relevance:** Quantitative analysis is core to finance
**Pattern:** Direct calculation formulas embedded in prompts for computational tasks
**Finance Application:** Ratio calculations, NPV/IRR, risk metrics, valuation formulas
**Why Essential:** Finance requires precise, auditable calculations with defined methodologies

#### NE-10: Probability-Weighted Scenarios
**Relevance:** Risk and uncertainty quantification
**Pattern:** Multiple scenarios with explicit probability weights
**Finance Application:** Investment scenarios, economic forecasts, risk assessment
**Why Essential:** Financial decisions require understanding range of outcomes, not point estimates

#### QA-01: Chain-of-Verification
**Relevance:** Financial accuracy and fiduciary responsibility
**Pattern:** Self-critique after initial analysis, verify calculations, revise
**Finance Application:** Valuation checks, financial statement analysis, risk calculations
**Why Essential:** Financial errors have material consequences; verification is mandatory

#### DS-02: Metric Specification
**Relevance:** Quantitative benchmarking
**Pattern:** Define specific, measurable criteria with thresholds
**Finance Application:** Financial ratios, return targets, risk limits, credit covenants
**Why Essential:** Finance operates on precise quantitative standards

#### RT-05: Evidence-Based Reasoning
**Relevance:** Investment and credit decisions
**Pattern:** Require specific evidence (data, financials, market information) for claims
**Finance Application:** Investment theses, credit analysis, economic forecasts
**Why Essential:** Financial claims must be supported by verifiable data

### Tier 2: Highly Valuable Techniques

#### QA-02: Adversarial Stress-Test
**Relevance:** Risk identification and thesis challenge
**Pattern:** Attack your own analysis to find weaknesses
**Finance Application:** Investment thesis challenges, stress testing, risk identification
**Why Valuable:** Identifies failure modes before they materialize in markets

#### RT-03: Tree of Thoughts
**Relevance:** Strategic options analysis
**Pattern:** Generate multiple approaches, compare systematically with pros/cons
**Finance Application:** Investment alternatives, strategic options, financing structures
**Why Valuable:** Ensures consideration of alternatives rather than anchoring on first idea

#### DT-02: Specific Focus Areas with Examples
**Relevance:** Comprehensive due diligence
**Pattern:** Detailed enumeration of analysis areas with concrete examples
**Finance Application:** Due diligence checklists, audit procedures, compliance reviews
**Why Valuable:** Prevents omissions in complex financial reviews

#### QA-04: Uncertainty Acknowledgment
**Relevance:** Risk communication
**Pattern:** State confidence levels, limitations, and assumptions explicitly
**Finance Application:** Forecasts, valuations, risk assessments
**Why Valuable:** Prevents overconfidence; enables appropriate risk-taking

#### RT-02: Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework
**Relevance:** Comprehensive assessment
**Pattern:** Analyze from multiple dimensions systematically
**Finance Application:** Credit analysis (5 Cs), SWOT, investment evaluation frameworks
**Why Valuable:** Ensures all relevant factors considered in financial decisions

## Financial Integrity Considerations

When building finance prompts, ensure:

1. **Calculation auditability** - All math traceable and verifiable
2. **Assumption transparency** - All inputs and assumptions explicit
3. **Scenario range** - Multiple outcomes considered, not just base case
4. **Source documentation** - Data sources identified and dated
5. **Limitation disclosure** - Model constraints and blind spots stated
6. **Conflict awareness** - Potential biases or conflicts acknowledged
7. **Regulatory compliance** - Applicable rules and requirements observed
8. **Time sensitivity** - Recognition that financial data has time value

### Common Financial Analysis Pitfalls to Address

- **Anchoring bias** - Over-relying on first number encountered
- **Confirmation bias** - Seeking data that supports predetermined conclusion
- **Recency bias** - Over-weighting recent data vs. full cycles
- **Survivorship bias** - Only analyzing successful examples
- **Precision illusion** - False confidence from detailed models
- **Correlation-causation confusion** - Assuming relationships are causal
- **Black swan blindness** - Under-weighting tail risks

### Key Financial Ratios Reference

When prompts involve ratio analysis, consider:

**Liquidity:** Current ratio, Quick ratio, Cash ratio
**Profitability:** Gross margin, Operating margin, Net margin, ROE, ROA, ROIC
**Leverage:** Debt/Equity, Debt/EBITDA, Interest coverage
**Efficiency:** Asset turnover, Inventory days, Receivables days, Payables days
**Valuation:** P/E, EV/EBITDA, P/B, P/S, Dividend yield
