# Investigative Project Plan

**Objective:** Turn a tip or a pattern into a plan an editor can approve — what we think
is true, what would prove or kill it, which records and people will tell us, what could
go wrong legally or for anyone's safety, and how the subject will get a fair chance to
answer before anything is published.

**When to Use:**
- A tip, a data anomaly, or a run of complaints suggests wrongdoing by an institution,
  company, or official, and it will take weeks or months to establish.
- An editor needs a scoped pitch with a timeline, costs, and a stopping rule.
- A small newsroom or freelancer is taking on its first records-heavy story.
- **Not this prompt if** you are verifying individual sources and documents as you go
  — use `journalism/journalism_source_verification_log.md`. For the pre-publication
  defamation screen to hand counsel, `domain-legal/ip/legal_defamation_publicity_risk_screen.md`.
  For shaping finished reporting into long-form narrative,
  `domain-creative-writing/creative-nonfiction/writing_narrative_nonfiction_and_literary_journalism.md`.
  A business or legal investigation of your own organisation is not journalism; use
  `domain-legal/` or `domain-risk/`.

## Inputs / Context

1. **The tip or pattern** — what was seen, by whom, with what evidence so far.
2. **The subject(s)** — institution, company, or official; public or private status.
3. **Jurisdiction** — records laws, recording-consent rules, and defamation law depend on it.
4. **Resources** — reporters, weeks, data skills, budget for records fees, legal access.
5. **Known risks** — vulnerable sources, litigious subject, physical or digital threats.
6. **Publication target** — outlet, format, any partner newsrooms.

## Method

1. **Write the hypothesis and its edges.** One falsifiable sentence (who did what, since
   when, to whom, at what scale). Then the **minimum story** (what is publishable if only
   the documents arrive) and the **maximum story** (if everything checks out).
2. **Set kill signals before reporting (DP-13).** Observable results that would end or
   shrink the project: the pattern disappears in the full data; an innocent explanation
   is documented; no first-hand source exists after N approaches. Name them now so they
   are not explained away later.
3. **Break the work into phases with gates (DT-01).**

   | Phase | Work | Gate to the next |
   |---|---|---|
   | 0 Feasibility | desk research, existing records, prior coverage | hypothesis still plausible; records exist |
   | 1 Records | requests filed, filings and court/inspection records pulled | core records in hand or refusal appealed |
   | 2 Data | cleaning, analysis, data diary | an independent colleague re-runs it and agrees |
   | 3 Sources | people approached outside-in | first-hand confirmation of the core finding |
   | 4 Draft + bulletproof | line-by-line check of every fact to the log | every line has a source row |
   | 5 Legal + reply | counsel review, no-surprises letter, responses | counsel's conditions met; deadline passed |
   | 6 Publish + after | publication, follow-ups, corrections | — |

4. **Write the records and data plan.** For each record: holder, the law or route that
   gives access (e.g. the US federal FOIA and state public-records acts, the UK Freedom of
   Information Act — verify the current local law), the exact wording of the request,
   expected time and fees, and the appeal route if refused. For data: the question each
   analysis answers, a **data diary** of every cleaning step, and a second person
   reproducing the result from raw data before any number enters the draft.
5. **Map sources and the order of approach.** Rings: documents → people who left →
   people still inside → regulators and experts → those affected → the subject last.
   Approaching the subject last avoids tipping them off before records are secured;
   approaching insiders is weighed against their exposure.
6. **Run the legal and safety review (QA-09).** Ask, for each step, what cannot be undone.
   - **Legal, early:** engage counsel at phase 0–1, not the night before; defamation,
     privacy, recording-consent law (one-party vs all-party varies by state and country),
     trespass and data-protection limits on how material is obtained.
   - **Safety:** source protection (secure channels, separate identity records), reporter
     physical and digital security, trauma exposure for anyone reviewing harmful material.
7. **Stress-test the story (QA-02).** Write the subject's best rebuttal and the strongest
   innocent explanation of the data; plan the reporting that answers each.
8. **Draft the no-surprises letter.** Every significant fact or allegation the subject
   would want to answer, stated specifically (dates, amounts, documents), with a
   reasonable deadline proportionate to complexity (often several working days for a
   long investigation), how to respond, and that a response will be fairly reflected.
   Record the reply or non-reply. New allegations after the letter → a new letter.

## Output Format

```
# Investigation plan — [working title]   Lead: [..]   Editor: [..]   Counsel: [..]

## Hypothesis
[one falsifiable sentence]
Minimum story: [...]   Maximum story: [...]

## Kill signals
1. [...]

## Phases and gates
| Phase | Work | Owner | Weeks | Gate |

## Records and data plan
| Record | Holder | Access route | Request wording | Expected time / fee | If refused |
Data questions: [...]  Data diary: [location]  Independent re-run by: [..]

## Source map (order of approach)
| Ring | Who | Why they would know | Risk to them | Approach |

## Legal and safety review
| Step | Irreversible? | Legal question | Safety measure |

## Best rebuttal and innocent explanations
[...] → reporting that answers each: [...]

## No-surprises letter (draft)
[allegations as numbered points; deadline; how to respond]
```

## Verification

- [ ] The hypothesis can be proved false by named evidence.
- [ ] Kill signals were written before phase 1.
- [ ] Every gate has an observable test; no phase begins with the previous gate open.
- [ ] Every data finding has a data diary and an independent re-run before drafting.
- [ ] The subject is approached last, and the reason is stated.
- [ ] Counsel is engaged by phase 1; recording-consent law for the jurisdiction is noted.
- [ ] The no-surprises letter lists every significant allegation in the draft, specifically.

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **A pattern is not proof of intent.** Data shows what happened; motive needs documents
   or first-hand sources, or it stays out.
2. **A kill signal is not a failure.** Ending a project on its own stopping rule protects
   the newsroom and the subject.
3. **A records refusal is not a finding.** "The city refused to release it" is a fact
   about the city, not evidence of what the record says.
4. **A vague letter is not a right of reply.** "Any comment on your practices?" gives the
   subject nothing to answer.
5. **Don't let the deadline shrink the reply window.** Fairness timing is set by
   complexity, not by the publication slot.
6. **Don't over-lawyer the plan.** Counsel advises; the editor decides what to publish.

## Example Output

Scenario: drivers complain that the city's sole towing contractor charges more than the
contract allows; reporter has 12 receipts; one reporter for 10 weeks plus a data
reporter part-time; US state with all-party recording consent.

```
# Investigation plan — "Towed and overcharged"   Lead: D. Park   Editor: S. Mills   Counsel: retained, 2 h/week

## Hypothesis
Since 2024, Metro Tow has charged storage fees above the contract cap ($35/day) on a
material share of city-ordered tows.
Minimum story: receipts + contract show overcharging in documented cases.
Maximum story: tow logs show systematic overcharging; the city knew from complaints.

## Kill signals
1. Full tow logs show overcharges on <2% of tows and each has a documented reason.
2. A contract amendment raised the cap before the receipts' dates.
3. No first-hand source at Metro Tow or the city after 8 approaches.

## Phases and gates
| 0 | contract, amendments, council minutes | Park | 1 | cap confirmed in force |
| 1 | records requests: tow logs, invoices, complaints | Park | 1–4 | logs received or appeal filed |
| 2 | fee vs cap per tow; data diary | Ruiz | 4–6 | Okoye re-runs from raw logs: same share |
| 3 | drivers → ex-employees → city staff → Metro Tow last | Park | 5–8 | one first-hand insider |
| 4 | draft; bulletproof every line to the log | both | 8–9 | every line sourced |
| 5 | counsel review; letter to Metro Tow and the city (7 working days) | Mills | 9–10 | replies or deadline |

## Records and data plan
| Tow logs 2024–26 | City transport dept | state public-records act | "all records of city-ordered
  tows by Metro Tow, 1 Jan 2024 – present, including dates in and out and fees charged" |
  ~20 business days, est. $150 | appeal to the records ombudsman |
Data questions: share of tows with storage fee > $35/day; trend by month.
Independent re-run by: J. Okoye (not on the project).

## Source map
| Documents | receipts, logs | — | none | collect |
| Ex-employees | 2 named in a lawsuit | fee practices | retaliation | personal contact, outside work |
| Inside | city complaints clerk | complaint volume | job risk | background terms only |
| Subject | Metro Tow owner; city contracts office | — | — | last, with the letter |

## Legal and safety review
| Recording calls | yes | all-party consent state → announce recording or take notes | — |
| Publishing receipts | yes | drivers' personal data → redact names and plates | consent from drivers |

## Best rebuttal
"Fees include separate after-hours release charges the contract allows." → pull the
contract's fee schedule; split storage from release fees in the analysis.

## No-surprises letter (draft)
1. On 214 of 3,980 tows (5.4%), storage was billed above $35/day … 2. Complaints to the
city … Deadline: 5 p.m., 7 working days from sending. We will reflect your response fairly.
```

## Techniques Used

- **DT-01 Hierarchical Task Breakdown** — hypothesis → phases → gates → owned tasks.
- **DP-13 Kill Signal Definition** — stopping rules written before reporting starts.
- **QA-02 Adversarial Stress-Test** — the subject's best rebuttal and innocent explanations, each with a reporting answer.
- **QA-09 Reversibility Assessment** — every irreversible step (approaches, recordings, publication) gets a legal and safety check.
