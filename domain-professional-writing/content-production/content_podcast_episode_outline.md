# Podcast Episode Outline

**Objective:** Give the host a one-page rundown they can talk from — segment
order, timings, the points each segment must land, and how to get from one to
the next — and give the producer show notes that can be published the day the
episode goes live.

**When to Use:**
- A hosted or co-hosted show where the hosts talk from notes, not a script.
- A recurring format that needs the same shape every week.
- You recorded an episode and need show notes with accurate timestamps.

**Not this prompt if:**
- The script *is* the product (narrated, faceless, or fully scripted audio) →
  `domain-professional-writing/content-production/content_long_form_script.md`.
- The episode is an interview and you need the question plan and guest
  research → `domain-professional-writing/content-production/content_podcast_host_interview_prep.md`.
  Use both: interview prep feeds the interview segment of this rundown.
- You need titles and descriptions optimised for search →
  `domain-professional-writing/content-production/content_seo_title_description.md`.
- You are cutting the episode into clips and posts →
  `domain-professional-writing/content-production/content_repurpose_one_to_many.md`.

## Inputs

1. **Show format**: hosts, usual length, recurring segments, sponsor slots.
2. **Episode topic and angle**: the one thing a listener should leave with.
3. **Source material**: research notes, links, the listener question, the news
   item — whatever the episode discusses.
4. **Guest**, if any, and whether interview prep exists.
5. **Call to action** for this episode.
6. **For show notes after recording**: transcript or timecoded log.

## Method

1. **State the episode's promise.** One sentence the intro can say out loud:
   what the listener gets by the end. If you cannot write it, the topic is
   still a subject area.
2. **Budget the runtime (DS-06).** Allocate minutes to each segment from the
   target length; give the core segment the most time. Put sponsor reads
   where the show's contract says, and never inside the core segment's payoff.
3. **Build the rundown (ST-03).** For each segment: purpose, 2–4 talking points
   (bullets, not sentences), the one line the host must say verbatim (if any —
   e.g. disclosures, sponsor copy), and a transition into the next segment.
4. **Constrain the content (CM-02).**
   - Talking points only from supplied material; flag anything else `[CHECK]`.
   - Sponsor copy and legal disclosures are verbatim from the sponsor brief.
   - No full sentences except verbatim lines; this is a rundown.
5. **Write show notes (OC-03).** Summary (2–3 sentences, no spoilers of the
   payoff unless the format wants it), timestamps (only from the recording or
   transcript — never estimated from the rundown), links mentioned, guest bio
   (supplied), credits, and the CTA.
6. **Self-check (QA-01).**

## Output Format

```
# [Show] — Ep [n]: [working title]
Promise: [one sentence]   Target: [mm] min   CTA: [...]

## Rundown
| # | Segment | Time budget | Purpose | Talking points | Verbatim line | Transition out |

## Host notes
Things to avoid · pronunciations · callbacks to earlier episodes

## Show notes (publish after recording)
Summary · Timestamps [from recording] · Links · Guest · Credits · CTA
```

## Verification

- [ ] The promise is one sentence and appears in the intro segment.
- [ ] Segment budgets sum to the target runtime (±2 min).
- [ ] Talking points are bullets, not scripted sentences.
- [ ] Every fact in the talking points traces to supplied material or is `[CHECK]`.
- [ ] Sponsor and disclosure lines are verbatim.
- [ ] Timestamps come from the recording, not the plan.

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **A rundown that reads as a script defeats its purpose.** Hosts reading
   sentences sound read; keep it to points.
2. **Planned timings are not timestamps.** Publishing plan times as chapter
   markers sends listeners to the wrong moment.
3. **Do not invent listener questions or anecdotes** to fill a segment.
4. **More segments is not more value.** A 30-minute show rarely carries more
   than three substantive segments.
5. **The CTA belongs once near the end** (plus a brief mention at the top if
   the show does that) — not in every transition.
6. **Show notes are not a transcript.** Summarise; link the transcript
   separately if one is published.

## Example Output

```
# Small Shop Radio — Ep 112: "Should you raise prices in a slow month?"
Promise: by the end you'll know the two numbers to check before raising
prices, and how to announce a rise without a wave of cancellations.
Target: 32 min   CTA: download the price-rise email template (link in notes)

## Rundown
| # | Segment | Time | Purpose | Talking points | Verbatim | Transition |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cold open | 0:45 | Hook | Listener Priya's question (supplied email): "sales are down 20%, do I dare raise prices?" | — | "We'll answer Priya properly — first…" |
| 2 | Intro | 1:30 | Promise | Hosts, episode promise | Promise sentence | "Let's start with the numbers." |
| 3 | Core: the two numbers | 11:00 | Teach | Gross margin per order; share of customers who bought on price (from Priya's survey data, supplied) | — | "Numbers say yes — now how to say it." |
| 4 | Sponsor | 1:00 | Read | — | Sponsor copy v2 + "paid partnership" disclosure | "Back to Priya." |
| 5 | Core: announcing the rise | 10:00 | Teach | 30 days' notice; lead with what's improved; grandfather for 60 days [CHECK: grandfathering % from host's own shop] | — | "Here's the template." |
| 6 | Answer Priya | 5:00 | Apply | Walk her numbers through segments 3 & 5 | — | — |
| 7 | Close + CTA | 2:45 | Act | Template link; next week's topic | CTA line | — |
Total: 32:00

## Host notes
Avoid naming Priya's shop (she asked). Callback: Ep 98 on discount traps.

## Show notes
Summary: Sales are down — is it the moment to raise prices? We answer a
listener's question with the two numbers to check first and a way to announce
a rise that keeps customers.
Timestamps: [fill from recording — e.g. 00:00 Cold open]
Links: Price-rise email template · Ep 98 "Discount traps"
Credits: Hosts [names] · Producer [name] · Sponsor: [name] (paid partnership)
CTA: Grab the template at the link above.
```

## Techniques Used

- **ST-03 Output Format Specification** — fixed rundown and show-notes schema.
- **CM-02 Constraint Specification** — supplied-material-only, verbatim sponsor lines, no scripting.
- **DS-06 Prioritization and Severity Guidance** — runtime budgeted toward the core segment.
- **OC-03 Markdown Table Specification** — the rundown table.
- **QA-01 Self-Verification** — timing, sourcing, and timestamp checks.
