# Somatic Symptom Disorder — Reattribution & CBT Protocol

## Objective

Generate a CBT session plan for DSM-5-TR Somatic Symptom Disorder and Illness Anxiety Disorder anchored to Barsky and Ahern's cognitive-behavioral/reattribution model for somatization and to Warwick and Salkovskis's cognitive model of health anxiety (catastrophic misinterpretation of benign bodily sensations, maintained by body-checking, reassurance-seeking, avoidance, and attentional scanning). The plan operationalizes the reattribution structure — feeling understood (validate the reality of the symptoms and the distress), broadening the agenda (introduce psychosocial and attentional factors), and making the link (collaboratively connect symptom amplification, anxiety, and behavior) — and adds behavioral experiments targeting checking, reassurance-seeking, and body scanning, attention training, reduction of illness behavior, and active coordination with the primary care/medical team. Validated measures (PHQ-15, SSD-12, Whiteley Index) baseline and track progress.

## When to Use

- DSM-5-TR Somatic Symptom Disorder or Illness Anxiety Disorder with disproportionate health-related thoughts, anxiety, or behaviors.
- Frequent reassurance-seeking, body-checking, doctor-shopping, or repeated investigations without resolution.
- Patients with real or medically explained symptoms whose distress and behavior are the treatment target.
- Collaborative care embedded with or coordinated alongside primary care.
- Not appropriate to use to dismiss symptoms as psychological when a genuine undiagnosed or evolving medical condition has not been adequately evaluated — psychologizing prematurely is a clinical error; coordinate to ensure appropriate (not excessive) medical workup first.

## Inputs / Context

- Predominant somatic complaints and the patient's feared illness/interpretation.
- Maintaining behaviors: checking, reassurance-seeking (medical and interpersonal), internet symptom searching, avoidance, scanning.
- Baseline measures: PHQ-15 (somatic symptom burden), SSD-12 (psychological criteria), Whiteley Index / Health Anxiety Inventory.
- Medical context: workup completed, treating physicians, scheduled tests. `[clinician input required: appraisal of whether medical evaluation is adequate or further workup is genuinely indicated]`
- Comorbid depression/anxiety and SI screen. `[clinician input required: current depression/SI screen result]`
- The behavioral experiment target for this session. `[clinician input required: collaboratively selected checking/reassurance behavior to test]`

## Constraints

### Must

- Validate the reality of the patient's symptoms and distress explicitly ("the symptoms are real; we work on the distress, the interpretation, and the cycle, not on proving the symptoms are imaginary").
- Follow the reattribution structure: feeling understood → broadening the agenda → making the link, in that sequence.
- Reduce reassurance-seeking and body-checking through collaborative behavioral experiments rather than abrupt prohibition; test the patient's predictions.
- Coordinate with the medical team and explicitly aim to reduce iatrogenic over-investigation (consolidate care, agree on a workup plan), not to deny care.
- Screen for a genuinely undiagnosed or evolving medical condition before attributing symptoms to the disorder; do not psychologize prematurely.
- Baseline and track using PHQ-15, SSD-12, and/or Whiteley Index.
- Screen comorbid depression and suicidal ideation; escalate per the risk hook if positive.

### Must Not

- Do not tell the patient the symptoms are "all in your head," imaginary, or "not real."
- Do not abruptly forbid reassurance-seeking without a collaborative rationale and experiment.
- Do not endorse open-ended additional testing as anxiety management, nor obstruct genuinely indicated medical care.
- Do not substitute psychological formulation for needed medical evaluation when red flags are present.
- Do not fabricate PHQ-15/SSD-12/Whiteley scores or medical workup details — all from measurement and records.

## Instructions

1. Open with measure review (PHQ-15, SSD-12, Whiteley) and homework review.
2. Begin with feeling understood: validate the symptoms and distress; summarize the patient's experience accurately.
3. Confirm medical-evaluation adequacy: if genuinely undiagnosed/evolving illness or red flags are present, prioritize appropriate coordination before reattribution work.
4. Run the depression/SI screen; if positive, branch to the risk pathway.
5. Broaden the agenda: introduce attention, stress, symptom amplification, and behavior as contributors without dismissing the body.
6. Make the link: collaboratively connect a recent episode's sensation → catastrophic interpretation → checking/reassurance → short-term relief → maintenance.
7. Design a behavioral experiment for the session's target behavior (e.g., delay/reduce checking or reassurance-seeking; test the prediction), and plan attention-training or scanning-reduction practice.
8. Coordinate with the medical team (consolidate care, agree on a reasonable workup ceiling); assign homework and document measures, medical coordination, risk, and billing.

## Verification

- [ ] Symptom reality and distress validated explicitly; no "all in your head" framing used.
- [ ] Reattribution sequence followed: feeling understood → broadening the agenda → making the link.
- [ ] Reassurance-seeking/checking reduced via a collaborative behavioral experiment that tests a prediction, not by abrupt prohibition.
- [ ] Medical-evaluation adequacy checked; genuine undiagnosed/evolving illness not psychologized prematurely.
- [ ] Medical-team coordination documented, aiming to reduce iatrogenic over-investigation without denying care.
- [ ] PHQ-15/SSD-12/Whiteley baselined and tracked.
- [ ] Depression/SI screened; risk pathway used if positive.
- [ ] CPT code and supervisor co-sign (if high acuity) recorded.
- [ ] No fabricated PHQ-15/SSD-12/Whiteley scores or medical workup details — all from measurement and records.
