# Adjustment to Chronic Illness — Intervention Plan

## Objective

Generate an intervention plan for psychological adjustment to chronic illness anchored to evidence-based health-psychology frameworks: Lazarus and Folkman's transactional model of stress and coping (primary/secondary appraisal; problem-focused vs. emotion-focused coping), Leventhal's common-sense/self-regulation model (illness representations across identity, cause, timeline, consequences, and controllability/cure), acceptance and values-based action from ACT (Hayes), and CBT for depression/anxiety arising secondary to illness. The plan assesses the patient's illness representations, matches coping strategy to the controllability of stressors, supports values-based living within illness limits, addresses grief for the well/healthy self, distinguishes normative adjustment from adjustment disorder and from major depression, and coordinates with the medical team. Depression and suicide risk are screened because high-burden chronic illness carries elevated risk.

## When to Use

- Patients adjusting to a new diagnosis, disease progression, treatment burden, or functional loss from a chronic condition.
- Coping breakdown, demoralization, anticipatory grief, or anxiety/depression secondary to illness.
- Behavioral medicine / health-behavior contexts where coping and adjustment are the focus.
- Coordinated care alongside the treating medical team.
- Not appropriate as the sole intervention when the presentation meets full major depressive disorder with significant suicide risk requiring acute psychiatric management, or when distress reflects untreated pain/medical instability needing medical attention first.

## Inputs / Context

- Illness, stage/prognosis, treatment burden, functional limitations, trajectory (stable / progressive / relapsing).
- Current coping repertoire and what the patient has tried; social/support context.
- Illness representations: identity, perceived cause, timeline, consequences, and beliefs about control/cure. `[clinician input required: the patient's illness representations as elicited]`
- Mood/adjustment status and a depression/SI screen (PHQ-9 or equivalent). `[clinician input required: current depression/SI screen result and adjustment-vs-MDD impression]`
- Values and valued life domains that illness is constraining. `[clinician input required: the patient's stated values/domains for values-based work]`
- Medical-team contacts and coordination needs.

## Constraints

### Must

- Assess illness representations across all five dimensions (identity, cause, timeline, consequences, control/cure) and use them to guide the intervention.
- Differentiate normative adjustment vs. adjustment disorder vs. major depressive disorder explicitly, and document the determination.
- Match coping to controllability: problem-focused coping for modifiable stressors, emotion-focused/acceptance coping for uncontrollable aspects.
- Support values-based living within illness limits (identify a valued domain and a feasible committed action).
- Validate and address grief for the well/healthy self where present, without reinforcing hopelessness.
- Coordinate with the medical team; situate psychological work alongside medical management.
- Screen depression and suicidal ideation each session in high-burden illness; escalate per the risk hook if positive.

### Must Not

- Do not deploy relentless positivity or minimize the losses ("look on the bright side"); validate the difficulty.
- Do not push problem-focused coping for genuinely uncontrollable aspects of the illness, nor counsel passivity where change is possible.
- Do not mislabel a full major depressive episode as "understandable sadness" and thereby under-treat.
- Do not give medical advice or contradict the treating team; coordinate instead.
- Do not fabricate prognosis, lab/imaging findings, PHQ-9 scores, or treatment details — all from records and patient report.

## Instructions

1. Open with a check-in on illness status, treatment burden, and the week's adjustment; review prior homework.
2. Elicit and document illness representations across the five dimensions; note maladaptive beliefs (e.g., overestimated personal cause, all-or-nothing control beliefs).
3. Run the depression/SI screen (PHQ-9 or equivalent); if positive, branch to the risk pathway.
4. Differentiate normative adjustment vs. adjustment disorder vs. MDD and record the determination, adjusting the plan accordingly.
5. Identify the salient stressors and classify each as controllable vs. uncontrollable; match coping strategy to each.
6. Do grief/acceptance work where loss of the well self is present; validate before moving to values.
7. Identify a valued life domain and co-create one feasible committed action within current illness limits.
8. Coordinate with the medical team and assign homework (coping practice, a values-based action, a self-monitoring or representation log); document outcomes, risk, coordination, and billing.

## Verification

- [ ] Illness representations assessed across all five dimensions and used to guide the plan.
- [ ] Normative adjustment vs. adjustment disorder vs. MDD differentiated and documented.
- [ ] Coping strategy matched to controllability for each salient stressor.
- [ ] Grief for the well self validated where present, without reinforcing hopelessness.
- [ ] A valued domain and one feasible committed action identified.
- [ ] Depression/SI screened in high-burden illness; risk pathway used if positive.
- [ ] Medical-team coordination documented; no medical advice given or team contradicted.
- [ ] CPT code (health-behavior or psychotherapy) matches the service delivered.
- [ ] Supervisor co-sign recorded for high-acuity sessions.
- [ ] No fabricated prognosis, lab/imaging, PHQ-9 scores, or treatment details — all from records and patient report.
