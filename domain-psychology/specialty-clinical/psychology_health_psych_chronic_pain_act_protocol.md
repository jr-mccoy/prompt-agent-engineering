# ACT for Chronic Pain — Health Psychology Session Protocol

## Objective
Generate a single health-psychology session plan applying Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT) to chronic pain, drawn from Dahl & Lundgren (*Living Beyond Your Pain*), Vowles & McCracken's contextual cognitive-behavioral / pain-acceptance work, and Hayes' psychological-flexibility model. The plan moves across the six hexaflex processes — **creative hopelessness** (examining the unworkability of the control/avoidance agenda), **control-as-the-problem**, **acceptance/willingness** to have pain sensations, **cognitive defusion** from pain-related thoughts, **present-moment contact**, **values** clarification, and **committed action** — and operationalizes values-based **activity pacing** (graded, quota-based engagement) versus boom-bust avoidance. It uses the Chronic Pain Acceptance Questionnaire (CPAQ) to track activity engagement and pain willingness, and explicitly distinguishes **hurt from harm**. The clinical frame targets *workability and functioning*, not pain intensity reduction, and is delivered in coordination with the patient's medical team.

## When to Use
- Chronic (≥3 months) non-cancer pain with disability/avoidance maintained by experiential avoidance of pain and pain-related distress.
- A session building willingness, defusion, or values-based committed action and pacing.
- Patients caught in a control agenda (rest, guarding, avoidance) that has narrowed their valued life.
- Coordinated behavioral care within a medical/pain-management context.
- Not appropriate as a substitute for medical evaluation of new, changing, or red-flag pain, and not appropriate to override or contradict the medical team's activity or medication guidance.

## Inputs / Context
- Pain history, diagnosis, current medical treatment, and the treating physician/team.
- CPAQ scores (activity engagement; pain willingness) and trajectory; current functioning/valued-activity baseline.
- The control/avoidance agenda in use (guarding, resting, activity avoidance, reassurance/over-checking).
- Values domains the pain has crowded out (relationships, work, health, recreation).
- Boom-bust pattern: overactivity on good days, crash and avoidance after.
- `[clinician input required: the medical team's current activity restrictions and the hurt-vs-harm boundaries for this patient's condition]`
- `[clinician input required: opioid/medication regimen and any complexity requiring care coordination]`
- `[clinician input required: which hexaflex process is the leverage point this session, given the formulation]`

## Constraints

### Must
- Target **workability and valued functioning**, not pain reduction; frame all interventions around what the patient can do, not how much pain decreases.
- Distinguish **acceptance/willingness from resignation** — willingness is in service of valued action, not giving up.
- Implement values-based, quota-driven **activity pacing** (act to plan, not to pain) to replace boom-bust avoidance; set activity quotas with the medical team's restrictions.
- Honor the **hurt-vs-harm** distinction supplied by the medical team and never encourage activity the team has restricted.
- Use defusion and present-moment work on pain-related thoughts without disputing their literal truth (ACT does not restructure thoughts).
- Tie committed action to a clarified value and a concrete, this-week behavior.
- State the health-behavior CPT code(s) and a coordination hook to the medical team.

### Must Not
- Must not promise, imply, or set a goal of reducing pain intensity as the measure of success.
- Must not contradict the medical team's activity restrictions or medication plan, or substitute for medical evaluation of red-flag/changing pain.
- Must not frame acceptance as resignation, "just live with it," or stopping treatment.
- Must not provide medical, dosing, or medication-tapering advice — flag complexity for the prescriber/team.
- Must not fabricate CPAQ scores, functional baselines, or medical restrictions — these come from the patient, instrument, and team.

## Instructions
1. Confirm the medical context, current restrictions, hurt-vs-harm boundaries, and CPAQ trajectory; if new/red-flag pain, route to medical and stop.
2. Identify this session's hexaflex leverage point from the formulation.
3. Run the process-specific intervention (e.g., creative hopelessness exercise; control-as-problem; an acceptance/willingness or defusion exercise; present-moment practice; values card sort/clarification).
4. Translate a clarified value into a committed action with a specific, quota-based activity-pacing plan within medical limits.
5. Replace a boom-bust pattern with an "act to plan" quota and a plan for good/bad days.
6. Assign experiential homework (a willingness practice + the paced committed action) and a functional log.
7. Document coordination with the medical team and any prescriber flag.
8. Complete the outcome/risk block and billing.

## Verification
- [ ] Success is defined by workability/valued functioning, not pain-intensity reduction.
- [ ] Acceptance/willingness is explicitly distinguished from resignation.
- [ ] Activity pacing is quota-based ("act to plan") and within the medical team's restrictions.
- [ ] Hurt-vs-harm boundary from the medical team is honored; no contradiction of medical guidance.
- [ ] Defusion/present-moment work does not dispute literal thought content.
- [ ] Committed action is tied to a clarified value and a concrete weekly behavior.
- [ ] Medical-team coordination and prescriber flag documented; health-behavior CPT present.
- [ ] No fabricated CPAQ scores, functional baselines, or medical restrictions — all come from the patient, instrument, and team.
