# Bipolar Disorder IPSRT — Interpersonal & Social Rhythm Therapy Protocol

## Objective

Generate an Interpersonal and Social Rhythm Therapy (IPSRT) session plan anchored to Ellen Frank's manualized protocol and social zeitgeber theory (Ehlers, Frank & Kupfer) — the model that disrupted social/circadian rhythms destabilize mood in bipolar disorder. The plan situates the session within IPSRT's four phases (initial, intermediate, maintenance, termination), uses the Social Rhythm Metric (SRM-5) to track and stabilize daily routines (sleep/wake, first contact with another person, start of work/activity, evening meal, bedtime), selects one IPT interpersonal problem area (grief, role transition, role dispute, interpersonal deficits — plus IPSRT's "grief for the lost healthy self"), monitors prodromes of mania and depression, protects sleep, and coordinates with the prescriber on mood-stabilizer adherence.

## When to Use

- DSM-5-TR Bipolar I or II, euthymic or with subsyndromal symptoms, in maintenance-oriented psychotherapy.
- Clients with rhythm-disrupting life changes (new shift work, parenthood, travel, relationship transitions).
- Adjunct to pharmacotherapy (IPSRT is designed to accompany, not replace, mood stabilizers).
- Clients ready to track daily rhythms and engage interpersonal work.
- Not appropriate as monotherapy for acute mania, mixed states with impaired judgment, or psychosis — these require psychiatric stabilization first.

## Inputs / Context

- Bipolar subtype, current phase (euthymic / depressed / subsyndromal / mixed-leaning).
- Current medications and adherence; prescriber contact.
- Most recent SRM-5 data: regularity of the five anchor activities and sleep timing/duration.
- Personal prodrome signature for mania and for depression. `[clinician input required: client's individualized early-warning signs for each pole]`
- Current interpersonal problem area and supporting detail. `[clinician input required: which IPT problem area is the session focus and why]`
- Mood-chart data (e.g., daily mood rating, sleep hours).
- Recent rhythm disruptions or upcoming events that threaten routine.
- Risk status: SI/HI, recent mood elevation, impulsivity, substance use. `[clinician input required: current risk screen]`

## Constraints

### Must

- Use SRM-5 to quantify rhythm regularity; identify the most irregular anchor and target it.
- Protect sleep explicitly — flag sleep loss as a mania-precipitating risk and build a stabilization step.
- Monitor and document prodromes for BOTH poles each session; act on early signs.
- Select and work ONE interpersonal problem area per the IPT framework; link it to rhythm disruption where relevant.
- Coordinate with the prescriber on mood-stabilizer adherence and any emerging symptom shift.
- Track mood-chart data and connect rhythm regularity to mood trajectory.
- Escalate and notify the prescriber if mania, hypomania, mixed features, or SI emerge; obtain supervisor co-sign for high-acuity sessions.
- Frame "grief for the lost healthy self" respectfully when relevant — validate the loss without reinforcing hopelessness.

### Must Not

- Do not treat IPSRT as a substitute for pharmacotherapy; do not encourage medication discontinuation.
- Do not aggressively restructure routines all at once; rhythm change is gradual and collaborative.
- Do not ignore reduced sleep need or increased goal-directed activity — these are mania prodromes, not "good weeks."
- Do not work multiple interpersonal problem areas simultaneously; maintain IPT focus.
- Do not minimize emerging elevation as positive mood improvement.
- Do not fabricate SRM-5 scores, mood-chart values, or prodrome history — all come from client self-monitoring.

## Instructions

1. Open with mood-chart and SRM-5 review; note rhythm regularity and sleep.
2. Identify the most disrupted anchor activity and the precipitating event.
3. Screen prodromes for mania and depression against the client's individualized signatures; assess SI/HI.
4. If prodromal or risk-positive, branch to stabilization (sleep protection, prescriber contact, safety plan, escalation).
5. Work the selected interpersonal problem area; connect interpersonal stress to rhythm disruption.
6. Co-develop a concrete rhythm-stabilization step (regularize one anchor; protect bedtime/wake).
7. Reinforce mood-stabilizer adherence; flag adherence barriers to the prescriber.
8. Assign homework: continued SRM-5 + mood charting, one rhythm-regularization action, one interpersonal step.
9. Document phase, outcomes, risk, prescriber coordination, and co-sign if high acuity.

## Verification

- [ ] SRM-5 data reviewed and most disrupted anchor targeted.
- [ ] Sleep protection addressed with mania-risk framing.
- [ ] Prodromes for both poles monitored and documented.
- [ ] One interpersonal problem area worked, linked to rhythm where relevant.
- [ ] Mood-stabilizer adherence reinforced; prescriber coordination noted.
- [ ] SI/HI screened; escalation pathway used if positive.
- [ ] IPSRT phase identified and consistent with content.
- [ ] Supervisor co-sign recorded for high-acuity session.
- [ ] CPT 90837 documented.
- [ ] No fabricated SRM-5 scores, mood-chart values, or prodrome history — all client-reported.
