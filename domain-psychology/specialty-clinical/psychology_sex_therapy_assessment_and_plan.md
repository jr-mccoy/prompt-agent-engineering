# Sex Therapy — Assessment and Intervention Plan (PLISSIT-Aware)

## Objective
Generate a biopsychosocial sexual assessment and a tiered intervention plan organized around Annon's **PLISSIT** model — **P**ermission, **L**imited **I**nformation, **S**pecific **S**uggestions, and **I**ntensive **T**herapy — escalating only as far up the tiers as the presentation requires. Where behavioral work is indicated, the plan structures **sensate focus** (Masters & Johnson's graded, non-demand touching progression that removes performance pressure). It accommodates the common presentations — desire discrepancy, female sexual interest/arousal and male erectile concerns, orgasmic disorders, genito-pelvic pain/penetration disorder, and premature/delayed ejaculation — and front-loads a **medical rule-out** (urology, gynecology, pelvic-floor physical therapy, endocrine) and a review of medication effects (notably SSRI-induced sexual dysfunction). The clinical frame is biopsychosocial, consent- and trauma-aware, partner-inclusive where relevant, and explicitly non-prescribing.

## When to Use
- Adult or couple presenting with a sexual concern (desire, arousal, orgasm, pain, ejaculatory) after or alongside medical evaluation.
- Initial assessment building the biopsychosocial formulation and selecting the PLISSIT entry tier.
- Planning a sensate-focus progression for arousal/performance-anxiety-maintained difficulties.
- Concerns embedded in relationship distress, medication side effects, or medical/chronic-illness context.
- Not appropriate when intake screening reveals coercion, intimate-partner violence, or non-consent — that routes to a safety response, not a sex-therapy plan — and not a substitute for the indicated medical/urologic/gynecologic evaluation.

## Inputs / Context
- Presenting sexual concern, onset (lifelong vs acquired), context (situational vs generalized), and duration.
- Relationship status/structure, partner involvement, and relational satisfaction/distress.
- Medical history, current medications (especially SSRIs/SNRIs, antihypertensives), and prior medical work-up status.
- Sexual history, beliefs/scripts, prior trauma, and cultural/religious context affecting the concern.
- `[clinician input required: medical rule-out status — what has been evaluated by urology/gynecology/pelvic-floor PT/endocrine, and what referral is still outstanding]`
- `[clinician input required: results of the coercion / IPV / consent screen and any safety routing required]`
- `[clinician input required: the appropriate PLISSIT entry tier and whether intensive therapy or referral is indicated]`

## Constraints

### Must
- Build the **biopsychosocial** formulation and confirm/initiate the **medical rule-out** before behavioral intervention; flag medication contributors (e.g., SSRI sexual side effects) for prescriber coordination.
- Conduct a **coercion/IPV/consent screen** at intake; if positive, route to safety and pause the sex-therapy plan.
- Match the intervention to the correct **PLISSIT tier** and escalate no further than needed; refer to Intensive Therapy / specialist when beyond scope.
- When sensate focus is indicated, structure it as graded, non-demand, sensation-focused (not goal/orgasm-focused) touching with the intercourse ban early, progressing by mutual readiness.
- Be partner-inclusive where the concern is dyadic; address desire discrepancy as a couple-level systemic issue, not one partner's deficit.
- Keep the work consent- and trauma-aware, with pacing and opt-out built into any touch-based homework.
- State the CPT code and a coordination hook to the medical/referral team.

### Must Not
- Must not provide medical diagnosis, prescribe, recommend dosing, or advise medication changes — flag for the prescriber/physician.
- Must not proceed with a sex-therapy plan when coercion/IPV/non-consent is present.
- Must not assign sensate-focus or other behavioral homework before the relevant medical rule-out and consent are established.
- Must not pathologize sexual orientation, identity, relationship structure, or normative variation.
- Must not fabricate medical findings, medication effects, or screen results — these come from the patient, the record, and the medical team.

## Instructions
1. Take the biopsychosocial sexual history; classify the concern (lifelong/acquired, situational/generalized).
2. Run the coercion/IPV/consent screen; if positive, switch output to a safety-routing note and stop.
3. Confirm medical rule-out status; identify outstanding referrals and medication contributors to coordinate.
4. Build the biopsychosocial formulation (biological, psychological/script, relational/systemic factors).
5. Select the PLISSIT entry tier and justify it; specify the tier-matched intervention (permission and accurate information for many concerns; specific suggestions; sensate focus; or referral to intensive/specialist care).
6. If sensate focus is indicated, lay out the staged progression with the intercourse ban and non-demand framing.
7. Address desire discrepancy or the dyadic dynamic at the couple level where relevant.
8. Assign consent- and trauma-aware homework with opt-out; document coordination and prescriber flag.
9. Complete the outcome/risk block and billing.

## Verification
- [ ] Biopsychosocial formulation built and medical rule-out confirmed/initiated before behavioral homework.
- [ ] Coercion/IPV/consent screen conducted; positive result routes to safety and pauses the plan.
- [ ] Intervention matched to the correct PLISSIT tier; escalation/referral only as needed.
- [ ] Sensate focus (if used) is graded, non-demand, non-orgasm-focused, with the early intercourse ban and opt-out.
- [ ] Desire discrepancy/dyadic concerns framed at the couple level, not as one partner's deficit.
- [ ] No medical diagnosis, prescribing, or medication-change advice; contributors flagged for prescriber.
- [ ] Identity, orientation, and relationship structure not pathologized; work is trauma-aware.
- [ ] CPT and medical/referral coordination hook present.
- [ ] No fabricated medical findings, medication effects, or screen results — all come from the patient, record, and medical team.
