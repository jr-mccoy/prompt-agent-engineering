# Hoarding Disorder CBT — Sorting/Discarding Protocol

## Objective

Generate a CBT session plan for DSM-5-TR Hoarding Disorder anchored to the Steketee & Frost manualized treatment ("Treatment for Hoarding Disorder"; the self-help companion "Buried in Treasure," Tolin, Frost & Steketee) and its cognitive-behavioral model — information-processing deficits (categorization, decision-making, attention, memory), maladaptive beliefs about possessions (identity, responsibility, memory, control, waste), strong emotional attachment, and avoidance maintained by acquiring and not-discarding. The plan specifies CIR/SI-R baselining, skills training in categorizing and decision-making, motivational enhancement, in-home sorting practice, graded exposure to discarding and to not-acquiring, cognitive work on beliefs, and — because clutter creates real life-safety hazards and may involve dependents or animals — explicit hazard screening with harm-reduction framing where insight is limited.

## When to Use

- DSM-5-TR Hoarding Disorder, with or without excessive acquisition; with good, fair, poor, or absent insight.
- Clutter that prevents use of living spaces and/or creates safety risk.
- Clients ambivalent about discarding where motivational enhancement precedes action work.
- Coordinated care involving family, housing, or community agencies.
- Not appropriate as the sole intervention when an imminent life-safety emergency (fire risk, structural collapse, unsafe conditions for a dependent child, elder, or animal) requires immediate protective action — address safety/reporting first, then resume CBT.

## Inputs / Context

- Insight level and stage of change; acquisition pattern (buying, free items, both).
- Baseline measures: Saving Inventory-Revised (SI-R) subscales (clutter, difficulty discarding, acquisition); Clutter Image Rating (CIR) room scores; Hoarding Rating Scale (HRS).
- Belief content driving saving (identity, responsibility, memory, waste, opportunity).
- Living situation: who else lives there, dependents, animals, landlord/code status. `[clinician input required: current life-safety hazard level and any duty-to-report considerations]`
- Functional impact: blocked exits, unusable kitchen/bath/bed, fall risk, sanitation.
- Comorbidity: depression, anxiety, ADHD, OCD; SI screen. `[clinician input required: current SI/depression screen result]`
- Target area for this session and the client's chosen practice space. `[clinician input required: collaboratively selected sorting target]`

## Constraints

### Must

- Screen for life-safety hazards each session (blocked egress, fire load, fall risk, sanitation, structural concern) and assess duty-to-report when a dependent child, vulnerable adult, or animal is in the home; coordinate with adult protective services, animal control, or housing where life-safety dictates.
- Baseline and re-measure using SI-R subscales, CIR room scores, and/or HRS; track change against these named instruments.
- Train decision-making and categorization skills explicitly — teach the client how to decide, not just what to remove.
- Include non-acquisition practice alongside discarding practice (acquisition maintains the disorder even when discarding occurs).
- Do cognitive work on the specific saving beliefs active for the target items (downward arrow, examining responsibility/waste/memory beliefs, behavioral experiments).
- Apply a harm-reduction frame when insight is limited or discarding readiness is low — prioritize safety and function over volume removed.
- Screen for comorbid depression and suicidal ideation; escalate per the risk hook if positive.

### Must Not

- Do not direct or endorse forced discarding, surprise clean-outs, or third parties removing items without consent — these worsen distress, damage the alliance, and predict re-accumulation.
- Do not set volume-removed as the primary success metric; decision-making skill and safety/function are the targets.
- Do not bypass the client's decision authority over their possessions except where genuine life-safety/legal duty applies.
- Do not psychologize a genuine hazard away — a real safety emergency takes precedence over the therapy agenda.
- Do not fabricate SI-R/CIR/HRS scores, room ratings, or housing/code status — these come from measurement and records.

## Instructions

1. Open with measure review (SI-R subscales, CIR for the target room, HRS if used) and homework review.
2. Conduct the life-safety hazard screen for the home; if an emergency or reportable condition exists, branch to the safety/reporting pathway before sorting work.
3. Run the depression/SI screen; if positive, branch to the risk pathway.
4. Where ambivalence is present, do brief motivational enhancement and reaffirm the collaborative goal (function and safety, not bare volume).
5. Train or reinforce categorization and decision-making skills (sorting categories, keep/discard/recycle/donate decision rules, time-limiting decisions).
6. Conduct in-session or plan in-home sorting practice on the chosen target; pair with cognitive work on the saving beliefs that arise.
7. Add a non-acquisition step (rules for not bringing items in; cope with acquisition urges).
8. Assign graded homework (one categorization/sorting block; one non-acquisition practice; one cognitive practice) and document measures, hazards, risk, coordination, and billing.

## Verification

- [ ] Life-safety hazard screen completed; duty-to-report assessed when dependents/animals present; coordination noted where indicated.
- [ ] SI-R subscales, CIR room score, and/or HRS reviewed and tracked over time.
- [ ] Decision-making and categorization skills explicitly trained — not just item removal.
- [ ] Non-acquisition practice included alongside discarding practice.
- [ ] Cognitive work targets the specific saving beliefs raised by the target items.
- [ ] Harm-reduction frame applied where insight/readiness is limited; success measured by function and safety, not volume.
- [ ] Depression/SI screened; risk pathway used if positive.
- [ ] No forced discarding, surprise clean-out, or third-party removal directed or endorsed.
- [ ] CPT 90837 and supervisor co-sign (if life-safety/high acuity) recorded.
- [ ] No fabricated SI-R/CIR/HRS scores or housing/code status — all from measurement or records.
