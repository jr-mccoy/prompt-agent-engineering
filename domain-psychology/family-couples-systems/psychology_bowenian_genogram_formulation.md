# Bowenian Three-Generation Genogram Formulation

## Objective

Produce a Bowen Family Systems formulation built on a three-generation genogram. The output renders the genogram (using standard symbols and relationship lines), then interprets the family across all eight Bowen concepts and reads the multigenerational patterns — repeated cutoffs, anxiety pathways, projection targets, transmitted differentiation levels — to a clinically usable hypothesis and a differentiation-oriented direction for treatment. This is a formulation artifact: a knowledgeable clinician should be able to recognize the eight concepts and the genogram conventions in the output.

## When to Use

- When understanding a presenting problem requires a multigenerational lens (chronic anxiety, cutoffs, repeated relational patterns across generations).
- At the start of Bowenian coaching to establish differentiation goals.
- When patterns (divorces, estrangements, over-/under-functioning) recur across generations and the clinician wants them mapped.
- For supervision/consultation requiring a genogram-anchored formulation across the eight concepts.

## Inputs / Context Required

- **Three generations of family data**: identified client, their parents and siblings, grandparents; spouses/partners and children where relevant.
- **Dates/facts**: births, marriages, separations/divorces, deaths, major moves, illnesses.
- **Relationship qualities**: close, conflictual, distant, cutoff, fused/over-involved, abusive.
- **Functioning patterns**: over-/under-functioning reciprocity, who is the family's "focus"/projection target.
- **Anxiety events**: nodal stressors (deaths, job loss, migration) and how the family reacted.
- **Cutoffs and triangles**: known estrangements; classic triangles (e.g., parent–parent–child).
- **Sibling positions** across generations.
- **Safety screen**: IPV / abuse history that the genogram surfaces. `[clinician input required: IPV/abuse screen; note any active safety concern surfaced by family history]`
- `[clinician input required: cultural/migration context affecting family structure and what counts as cutoff vs normative distance]`

## Constraints

### Must

- Render the genogram using standard symbols and relationship lines (see legend), three generations deep.
- Address all eight Bowen concepts explicitly, each with evidence from the genogram: (1) differentiation of self, (2) triangles, (3) nuclear family emotional process, (4) family projection process, (5) multigenerational transmission process, (6) emotional cutoff, (7) sibling position, (8) societal emotional process.
- Map at least the central triangle(s) and show how anxiety routes through them.
- Estimate a relative differentiation level for the index client and locate it on the multigenerational transmission gradient (how differentiation rose/fell across generations).
- Identify the family projection target(s) and the route by which parental anxiety focuses on a child.
- Read repeated patterns: recurring cutoffs, over-/under-functioning reciprocity, anxiety pathways across generations.
- Frame the treatment direction in Bowenian terms: increasing differentiation, detriangling, reconnecting cutoffs (person-to-person), managing reactivity — not symptom-removal alone.
- Distinguish characterological/cultural distance from emotional cutoff; do not over-label normative distance as cutoff.
- Include a safety hook for abuse/IPV surfaced by the history.
- Use `[clinician input required: ...]` for clinician-supplied content.

### Must Not

- Do not omit any of the eight concepts; if data is missing for one, flag it rather than skip it.
- Do not present differentiation as a fixed personality trait or moral ranking; it is a systemic, functional, and variable concept.
- Do not fabricate genogram facts (dates, deaths, relationships) not provided.
- Do not coach a client toward confrontation or reconciliation as a directive; Bowenian work supports the client's own differentiation, not prescribed reunions.
- Do not pathologize culturally normative closeness/distance.

## Instructions

1. **Render the three-generation genogram.** Use the legend.

   | Symbol / Line | Meaning |
   |---------------|---------|
   | □ Square | Male |
   | ○ Circle | Female |
   | □ or ○ with X / ⊠ ⊗ | Deceased (with year) |
   | ◇ Diamond | Pregnancy / unknown sex / other |
   | Double border (▣ / ◎) | Identified client (index person) |
   | Horizontal line between □—○ | Marriage (with year) |
   | `=//=` on marriage line | Divorce (`//`) or separation (`/`) |
   | Dashed couple line | Cohabiting / unmarried partners |
   | Vertical/branch lines down | Children (eldest to left) |
   | Single line (relationship) | Normal/close-enough |
   | Double/triple line | Close / fused (over-involved) |
   | Jagged line | Conflictual |
   | Dashed line | Distant |
   | Cutoff: line with a gap / cut bars | Emotional cutoff / estrangement |
   | Combined (e.g., double + jagged) | Fused-conflictual |

   Render at least: index client, siblings, parents, and grandparents (both sides), with marriage/divorce/death years and relationship lines.

2. **Differentiation of self.** Estimate the index client's relative level (capacity to separate intellectual from emotional process; stay a self in relationships under anxiety). Note solid-self vs pseudo-self indicators.

3. **Triangles.** Map the operative triangle(s) (e.g., father–mother–child; client–partner–parent). Show how anxiety routes to the third position when the twosome is stressed.

4. **Nuclear family emotional process.** Identify which pattern(s) absorb anxiety in the nuclear family: marital conflict, dysfunction in a spouse (over-/under-functioning reciprocity), impairment of a child (projection), or emotional distance.

5. **Family projection process.** Name the child(ren) who receive parental anxiety and the three-step route (parent focuses on child → interprets child's behavior as a problem → treats child as if the problem is real, shaping it).

6. **Multigenerational transmission process.** Trace how differentiation levels and patterns pass across the three generations; note where differentiation stepped up or down and the resulting symptom load.

7. **Emotional cutoff.** Identify cutoffs and assess whether they manage (rather than resolve) unresolved attachment/anxiety, and what they cost the system.

8. **Sibling position.** Note positions across generations (Toman) and how they shape roles and couple fit.

9. **Societal emotional process.** Note societal-level stressors (migration, discrimination, economic pressure) that raise chronic anxiety in the family.

10. **Read the patterns** (repeated cutoffs, anxiety pathways, projection targets) and write the formulation + Bowenian treatment direction.

11. **Run verification.**

## Verification

- [ ] Genogram rendered three generations deep with standard symbols and relationship lines.
- [ ] All eight Bowen concepts addressed, each with genogram-based evidence (gaps flagged, not skipped).
- [ ] Operative triangle(s) mapped with the anxiety route shown.
- [ ] Differentiation estimated relatively and located on the multigenerational gradient.
- [ ] Family projection target(s) and route identified.
- [ ] Repeated patterns and anxiety pathways read across generations.
- [ ] Emotional cutoffs distinguished from culturally normative distance.
- [ ] Treatment direction framed as differentiation/detriangling/reconnection, not symptom-removal or directive reconciliation.
- [ ] Differentiation presented as systemic/functional, not a fixed trait or moral ranking.
- [ ] IPV/abuse surfaced by history has a safety hook; missing data flagged with `[clinician input required]`.
