# IFS-Informed Couples Session Structure (Intimacy from the Inside Out)

## Objective

Produce a structured IFS-informed couples session plan faithful to Toni Herbine-Blank's Intimacy from the Inside Out (IFIO) model. The plan identifies each partner's protective parts that get activated in the couple's dynamic, sequences the U-turn (each partner turning attention inward to their own activated parts rather than at the other), facilitates unblending and access to Self-energy, and choreographs courageous communication in which partners speak *for* their parts rather than *from* them. The output is a session-level map that names the parts-detecting-parts dynamic and specifies the therapist's moves to keep both partners in (or returning to) Self while a vulnerable exchange occurs.

## When to Use

- For couples whose recurring conflict is driven by reactive protective parts (managers and firefighters) firing at each other.
- When one or both partners are chronically blended with a protector (e.g., a critic, a withdrawer, a placater) during conflict.
- When an EFT- or Gottman-informed clinician wants an internal/parts lens to deepen a softening or repair.
- When a vulnerable disclosure keeps getting hijacked by protectors and needs an unblended, Self-led container.

## Inputs / Context Required

- **The recurring dynamic / loop** the couple wants help with.
- **Each partner's known protectors**: the parts that show up in conflict (e.g., critic, defender, distancer, fixer, peacekeeper) and what each protects.
- **Exiles (if known/safe)**: the vulnerable parts (shame, fear of abandonment, not-enough) the protectors guard.
- **Each partner's capacity for the U-turn**: can they turn inward, or do they immediately externalize?
- **Self-energy markers observed**: calm, curiosity, compassion, clarity when present.
- **Safety screen**: IPV / coercive-control screen. `[clinician input required: separate IPV screen result; active IPV contraindicates conjoint vulnerable parts work]`
- `[clinician input required: trauma history that may flood an exile if accessed conjointly — pacing note]`

## Constraints

### Must

- Distinguish speaking *for* a part (Self describing the part's experience: "a part of me feels…") from speaking *from* a part (the part talking through the person: "you always…"). The session goal is to move partners from speaking-from to speaking-for.
- Name the parts-detecting-parts dynamic: each partner's protectors are reacting to the *other partner's protectors*, not to the partner's Self. Make this loop explicit.
- Sequence the U-turn: when a partner is activated/externalizing, invite them to turn attention inward to the part that just got triggered, before any further exchange with the partner.
- Facilitate unblending: help the activated partner gain enough separation from the protector (notice it, locate it, ask it to give some space) so Self-energy is available.
- Require the therapist to track and protect Self-energy in both partners; pause the exchange to do a U-turn or unblending whenever a protector reblends.
- Choreograph the vulnerable exchange (an enactment) only once both partners have enough Self-energy to speak for parts and to listen from Self.
- Protect exiles: do not expose a partner's exile to the other partner before the protectors consent and the receiving partner can witness from Self.
- Include a safety re-assessment hook; route out of conjoint vulnerable work if active IPV/coercive control is present.
- Use `[clinician input required: ...]` for clinician-supplied content.

### Must Not

- Do not let partners speak from blended protectors at each other; interrupt and U-turn.
- Do not access or expose an exile while the guarding protector is non-consenting or while the partner is flooded.
- Do not pathologize protectors; in IFS all parts are welcome and protective in intent.
- Do not stage the courageous-communication enactment before both partners have unblended enough to hold Self-energy.
- Do not proceed with conjoint vulnerable parts work under active IPV/coercive control.
- Do not fabricate parts not reported; flag with `[clinician input required]`.

## Instructions

1. **Map each partner's protector system in the dynamic.** For each partner, list the protectors that fire in conflict, what each does, and the exile each guards. Use the table.

   | Element | What it is | In-conflict example | Therapist move |
   |---------|------------|---------------------|----------------|
   | Manager (proactive protector) | Keeps system controlled/safe pre-emptively | Critic, fixer, planner, placater | Acknowledge its job; ask for space |
   | Firefighter (reactive protector) | Douses pain once an exile is touched | Stonewall, rage, leave, numb | Validate intent; slow it down |
   | Exile | Young, vulnerable, burdened part | Shame, abandonment fear, "not enough" | Protect; access only with protector consent |
   | Self | Calm/curious/compassionate core | Able to speak-for and listen | Cultivate and protect; the goal-state |

2. **Make the parts-detecting-parts loop explicit.** State the loop: "When A's [protector] fires, it triggers B's [protector], which triggers A's [protector]…" — the partners are relating protector-to-protector, not Self-to-Self.

3. **Run the U-turn.** When a partner externalizes ("you did X"), invite the U-turn: "Can we pause — what just happened *inside you* when [event]? Which part got activated?" The U-turn redirects attention from the partner to one's own activated part. This is the central IFIO move.

4. **Facilitate unblending.** Help the activated partner separate from the protector: notice it, find it in/around the body, name its fear, and ask it to relax back a little so the partner can be present. Confirm Self-energy returning (curiosity/calm).

5. **Shift to speaking-for.** Coach the partner to speak *for* the part: "Tell your partner about the part — 'a part of me feels ___ and is afraid of ___'," rather than letting the part speak *from* them. The receiving partner is coached to listen from Self.

6. **Choreograph the courageous-communication enactment.** Once both partners hold Self-energy, set up the direct, vulnerable exchange: speaker speaks for their part/need; listener witnesses from Self and reflects. Pause and U-turn whenever a protector reblends.

7. **Protect and (if appropriate) witness an exile.** Only with protector consent and a Self-led receiving partner, allow a brief, contained sharing of the vulnerable part so the partner can witness it.

8. **Close with integration:** what each partner learned about their own parts and their partner's parts; the new internal U-turn skill to practice between sessions.

9. **Re-screen safety; run verification.**

- [ ] Unblending steps specified before any vulnerable exchange.
- [ ] Speaking-for vs speaking-from distinction operationalized in the coaching.
- [ ] Courageous-communication enactment staged only with Self-energy in both partners; reblend protocol included.
