# Discernment Counseling Protocol (Doherty, Mixed-Agenda Couples)

## Objective

Produce a Discernment Counseling plan faithful to Bill Doherty's protocol for mixed-agenda couples — one partner leaning out of the marriage and one leaning in. Discernment Counseling is explicitly NOT couples therapy: its goal is to help the couple reach a clear, confident DECISION about a direction, not to solve marital problems. The plan structures the brief course (one session, up to a maximum of five), specifies the within-session format (the central individual conversations with each partner plus brief joint check-ins), elicits each partner's narrative of the marriage and their own contributions to the problems, works the leaning-out / leaning-in dynamic, and applies hard contraindication screening (active IPV/coercive control, an ongoing undisclosed affair) that routes the couple out of the protocol.

## When to Use

- When one partner wants to work on the marriage and the other has "one foot out the door" — the mixed-agenda situation that standard couples therapy handles poorly.
- When a couple is uncertain whether to divorce or to recommit to repair and needs a decision before any couples therapy.
- When a leaning-out partner would refuse traditional couples therapy but will engage in a short decision-focused process.

## Inputs / Context Required

- **Each partner's stated agenda**: who is leaning out, who is leaning in (and how strongly).
- **Marriage history (brief)** and the precipitant for seeking help now.
- **Prior couples therapy** attempts and outcomes.
- **Children / life context** affecting the decision.
- **Each partner's openness** to looking at their own contributions.
- **Contraindication screen (mandatory, separate from partner)**:
  - IPV / coercive control / fear of partner.
  - An ongoing affair (especially undisclosed) or a partner who has already privately decided to divorce and is using the process to "soften the landing."

  - `[clinician input required: clinician's read on whether an undisclosed ongoing affair or a foregone decision is present]`

## Constraints

### Must

- State clearly that the goal is a confident DECISION, not problem-resolution, and that this is not couples therapy.
- Offer the three paths and require the process to drive toward choosing one:
  - Path 1: Status quo — stay married as-is, do not decide now (off the table as an end-state; it is the default they are trying to move beyond).
  - Path 2: Move toward separation / divorce.
  - Path 3: A defined 6-month, all-out effort to restore the marriage, in couples therapy, with divorce off the table during that window.
- Bound the dose: one session, up to a maximum of five; each session can be the last; the leaning-out partner is told they can stop at any time.
- Make individual conversations the core of each session (the therapist spends most time one-on-one with each partner in turn, with brief joint segments to open and close).
- Elicit each partner's narrative of the marriage AND, crucially, each partner's view of their OWN contributions to the problems (the central growth question, especially for the leaning-in partner: "what is your part?").
- Work the leaning-out / leaning-in dynamic respectfully: validate the leaning-out partner's reasons without pushing reconciliation; help the leaning-in partner shift from pursuit/pressure to self-reflection.
- Apply hard contraindication screening: active IPV/coercive control, or an ongoing undisclosed affair / already-decided partner → route out (individual therapy, safety planning, or appropriate referral); do not run Discernment Counseling as a vehicle to manage those.
- Include a safety/risk re-assessment hook (separation talk can escalate risk).
- Use `[clinician input required: ...]` for clinician-supplied content.

### Must Not

- Do not try to fix the marriage or do couples-therapy interventions; that violates the frame.
- Do not pressure the leaning-out partner toward reconciliation, nor the leaning-in partner toward divorce.
- Do not exceed five sessions; Discernment Counseling is deliberately brief.
- Do not run the protocol when active IPV/coercive control is present, or as cover for an undisclosed ongoing affair / a decision already made — route out instead.
- Do not treat Path 1 (status quo) as a successful end-state; the aim is a confident choice between Path 2 and Path 3.
- Do not fabricate partner statements; flag missing input.

## Instructions

1. **Open with the frame (Session 1, joint, brief).** State the goal (clarity and confidence about a direction, not fixing the marriage), the three paths, and the brief dose (1–5 sessions, can stop anytime). Confirm both partners understand this is not couples therapy.

2. **Run the contraindication/safety screen separately.** With each partner alone, screen for IPV/coercive control/fear, and assess for an ongoing undisclosed affair or an already-final decision. If any are present, stop the protocol and route out (safety planning, individual therapy, referral).

3. **Conduct individual conversations (core of each session).** Spend the bulk of the session one-on-one with each partner in turn:
   - Leaning-out partner: understand what brought them to the edge; what (if anything) could make repair worth considering; their fears about each path. Do not push back toward the marriage.
   - Leaning-in partner: shift from pursuit/anxiety to self-reflection; help them see and own their contributions; build their capacity to be a different partner regardless of outcome.

4. **Elicit each partner's narrative + own contributions.** For each partner, draw out (a) their story of how the marriage got here and (b) their honest view of their own part in the problems — the growth-oriented pivot of the model.

5. **Work the leaning-out / leaning-in dynamic.** Name the dynamic without judgment; help each partner stop the pursue/distance escalation and look inward.

6. **Brief joint segment to close each session.** Each partner shares (with the therapist's help) one thing they learned about themselves; the therapist summarizes and checks whether they want to return.

7. **Move toward the decision.** Across the (≤5) sessions, help the couple choose Path 2 or Path 3 with confidence. If Path 3, define the 6-month repair effort and hand off to couples therapy with divorce off the table during that window.

8. **Re-assess safety/risk** throughout (separation talk can raise risk); run verification.
