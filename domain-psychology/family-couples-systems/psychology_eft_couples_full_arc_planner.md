# EFT Couples Full-Arc Planner (Three Stages / Nine Steps)

## Objective

Produce a complete Emotionally Focused Therapy (EFT) treatment-arc plan for a couple, organized by Sue Johnson's three stages and nine steps. The plan must (1) map the couple's negative interactional cycle and its attachment underpinnings, (2) sequence the de-escalation, restructuring, and consolidation work, (3) identify any attachment injuries requiring a structured forgiveness process, (4) specify the EFT Tango moves and enactments that drive change at each step, and (5) define markers that indicate readiness to move to the next stage. The output is an arc-level case map a supervising EFT therapist can recognize as protocol-faithful, not a single session script.

## When to Use

- At the start of an EFT episode, once intake confirms EFT is appropriate (secure-enough alliance is achievable; no active IPV, no undisclosed ongoing affair, both partners willing).
- When a couple is stuck in Stage 1 and the clinician needs to recheck cycle mapping and de-escalation markers before pushing into restructuring.
- When an attachment injury (betrayal, abandonment at a moment of need) keeps re-triggering the cycle and a structured Attachment Injury Resolution Model (AIRM) sequence must be planned.
- For EFT supervision and consultation, to externalize the case conceptualization in stage/step terms.

## Inputs / Context Required

- **Couple composition and history**: length of relationship, structure, presenting complaint in each partner's words.
- **Negative cycle data**: what each partner does when distressed; the typical escalation sequence; who pursues, who withdraws.
- **Attachment history**: each partner's attachment-relevant history (caregiving, prior losses, betrayals) to the extent known.
- **Attachment injuries**: any specific incident where one partner felt abandoned/betrayed at a moment of vulnerability and the bond was not repaired.
- **Risk screen**: results of an IPV / coercive-control screen conducted with partners seen separately; substance use; current safety.
- **Stage location**: clinician's best estimate of current stage/step, if mid-treatment.
- `[clinician input required: each partner's primary attachment strategy as observed — anxious/hyperactivating (pursue) vs avoidant/deactivating (withdraw) vs disorganized]`
- `[clinician input required: any individual trauma history that may surface during emotion-deepening work]`

## Constraints

### Must

- Conduct (or confirm) a separate-partner IPV / coercive-control screen before planning conjoint emotion-deepening work; if active IPV or coercive control is present, conjoint EFT is contraindicated — flag and route to appropriate IPV-specialist / individual services rather than producing a couples arc.
- Organize the entire plan around the three stages and nine steps in order; do not present restructuring work (Stage 2) before de-escalation markers (Stage 1) are met.
- Name and map the specific negative cycle (pursue–withdraw, withdraw–withdraw / freeze, attack–attack reactive escalations), framing it as the shared problem/"common enemy."
- Tie surface (secondary, reactive) emotions to underlying (primary, attachment) emotions for each partner.
- Reframe the problem in attachment terms (longing for accessibility, responsiveness, engagement — A.R.E.).
- Specify EFT Tango moves where change happens: (1) mirroring present process, (2) affect assembly and deepening, (3) choreographing engaged encounters/enactments, (4) processing the encounter, (5) integrating and validating.
- Plan withdrawer re-engagement before blamer softening (Step 5 precedes Steps 6–7); a softening enactment requires an accessible, re-engaged withdrawer to receive it.
- If an attachment injury is present, embed the Attachment Injury Resolution Model steps within Stage 2.
- Define explicit transition markers between stages.

### Must Not

- Do not push for a blamer-softening enactment while the withdrawer is still defended/disengaged.
- Do not relabel the cycle work as communication-skills training; EFT works experientially with emotion, not via psychoeducation alone.
- Do not pathologize either partner's attachment strategy; frame both as understandable protective moves within the cycle.
- Do not plan conjoint work in the presence of active IPV, coercive control, or an undisclosed ongoing affair.
- Do not fabricate attachment-history content the clinician has not supplied; mark gaps with `[clinician input required]`.

## Instructions

1. **Confirm appropriateness and run the safety gate.** State the IPV/coercive-control screen result. If contraindicated, stop and route out. Otherwise proceed.

2. **Map the negative cycle (Stage 1, Steps 1–2).** Build the cycle map using the structure below for each partner:

   | Element | Partner A | Partner B |
   |---------|-----------|-----------|
   | Trigger / attachment cue | [What sets it off] | [...] |
   | Perception / meaning made | [e.g., "I don't matter"] | [...] |
   | Secondary (reactive) emotion | [anger, frustration, numbness] | [...] |
   | Action tendency / move | [pursue/criticize OR withdraw/shut down] | [...] |
   | Effect on partner | [confirms partner's worst fear] | [...] |
   | Primary (attachment) emotion underneath | [fear, sadness, shame, aloneness] | [...] |
   | Unmet attachment need (A.R.E.) | [accessibility/responsiveness/engagement] | [...] |

   Name the cycle type: pursue–withdraw, withdraw–withdraw, or reactive attack–attack collapsing into pursue–withdraw.

3. **Stage 1 — Cycle De-escalation (Steps 1–4).** Plan:
   - **Step 1**: Create alliance; assess the relationship and the nature of the distress.
   - **Step 2**: Identify the negative interactional cycle and the positions each partner takes.
   - **Step 3**: Access the unacknowledged underlying (primary/attachment) emotions driving each position.
   - **Step 4**: Reframe the problem in terms of the cycle, the underlying emotions, and attachment needs — the cycle becomes the shared enemy.
   - **De-escalation markers**: the couple can name the cycle in the moment; reactivity is lower; each can see the other's underlying vulnerability; conflict no longer escalates as fast.

4. **Stage 2 — Restructuring the Bond (Steps 5–7).** Plan in order:
   - **Step 5 (Withdrawer re-engagement)**: deepen the withdrawer's underlying emotion; help them stay present and articulate needs/fears rather than deactivate. Choreograph an enactment where the withdrawer speaks from primary emotion.
   - **Step 6 (Blamer softening – accessibility)**: deepen the pursuer's attachment fears under the anger; the pursuer reaches for the now-accessible partner from a vulnerable, soft place.
   - **Step 7 (Responsiveness / bonding event)**: choreograph the key change event — pursuer asks for attachment needs to be met; re-engaged partner responds with accessibility and responsiveness; a new bonding interaction is created and processed.
   - If an **attachment injury** exists, run the AIRM within Stage 2: injured partner stays with and articulates the pain of the injury; injuring partner moves from defensiveness to taking in the impact, expressing remorse, and becoming emotionally available; injured partner risks reaching; new responsiveness allows forgiveness and re-engagement.

5. **Apply the EFT Tango at each enactment.** For each restructuring enactment, specify the five Tango moves: mirror the present process → assemble and deepen affect → choreograph the engaged encounter → process the encounter → integrate and validate.

6. **Stage 3 — Consolidation (Steps 8–9).** Plan:
   - **Step 8**: Facilitate new solutions to old problems from the secure base now available.
   - **Step 9**: Consolidate new positions and the new positive cycle; build a coherent relationship narrative of how they got stuck and how they changed; create a relapse-vulnerability plan.

7. **Define transition and termination markers**, including a relapse/early-warning plan and a re-contracting trigger.

8. **Run verification.**

## Verification

- [ ] Separate-partner IPV / coercive-control screen documented; contraindication routes out rather than producing a conjoint arc.
- [ ] Undisclosed-affair check addressed before conjoint work.
- [ ] Negative cycle named (pursue–withdraw / withdraw–withdraw / attack–attack) with per-partner secondary AND primary emotions and unmet A.R.E. need.
- [ ] Cycle framed as the shared "common enemy."
- [ ] Stage 1 steps 1–4 sequenced with explicit de-escalation markers as the gate to Stage 2.
- [ ] Stage 2 sequences withdrawer re-engagement (Step 5) BEFORE blamer softening (Steps 6–7).
- [ ] Step 7 bonding event / responsiveness enactment designed.
- [ ] Attachment-injury work, if present, embedded as an AIRM sequence inside Stage 2.
- [ ] EFT Tango five moves specified for restructuring enactments.
- [ ] Stage 3 consolidation (Steps 8–9) includes new positive cycle + relapse-vulnerability plan.
- [ ] Risk re-assessment hook and supervisor/co-sign line present.
- [ ] Unsupplied attachment-history content flagged with `[clinician input required]`.
