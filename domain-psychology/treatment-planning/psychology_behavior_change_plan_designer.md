# Behavior Change Plan Designer

**Important Disclaimer:** This tool assists in designing behavior change intervention frameworks for professional, organizational, or personal development purposes. It does NOT provide clinical treatment, therapy, or medical advice. For clinical behavior change interventions (e.g., treating addictions, eating disorders, or mental health conditions), consult qualified healthcare professionals.

**Objective:** Apply evidence-based behavioral science frameworks (COM-B, Transtheoretical Model, Social Cognitive Theory) to design structured behavior change interventions, including barrier analysis, technique selection, and measurement strategies.

**Instructions:**

1. **Define the target behavior with specificity**
   * Request the following information:
     - What is the behavior to be changed? (Be specific: "exercise more" → "30 minutes of moderate cardio 3x/week")
     - Is this behavior to be increased, decreased, started, or stopped?
     - Who is the target population? (individual, group, organization)
     - What is the context where the behavior occurs or should occur?
     - What is the timeframe for desired change?
     - What has been tried previously? What worked or didn't work?
   * Ensure the behavior is observable and measurable
   * Distinguish between single behaviors vs. behavioral patterns

2. **Conduct COM-B analysis (Capability, Opportunity, Motivation - Behavior)**
   * **Capability Assessment:**
     - *Physical Capability:* Does the person have the physical skills, strength, or stamina?
     - *Psychological Capability:* Does the person have the knowledge, psychological skills, cognitive capacity?
     - Assessment questions: "Do they know how to do it? Can they physically do it?"
   * **Opportunity Assessment:**
     - *Physical Opportunity:* Is the environment conducive? Are resources available? Is there time?
     - *Social Opportunity:* Do social norms support it? Is there social support or pressure?
     - Assessment questions: "Does the environment allow it? Do social circumstances support it?"
   * **Motivation Assessment:**
     - *Reflective Motivation:* Are there conscious beliefs, intentions, plans, and goals supporting the behavior?
     - *Automatic Motivation:* Are there habits, emotional responses, desires, impulses aligned with the behavior?
     - Assessment questions: "Do they want to do it? Do they believe they should?"
   * Rate each component (High/Medium/Low) as a barrier or enabler
   * Identify the PRIMARY barriers that most limit behavior change

3. **Assess stage of change (Transtheoretical Model)**
   * Determine current stage for the target population:
     - **Precontemplation:** Not considering change; may not recognize problem
     - **Contemplation:** Considering change but ambivalent; weighing pros and cons
     - **Preparation:** Intending to take action soon; making small steps
     - **Action:** Actively engaging in behavior change
     - **Maintenance:** Sustaining change over time; preventing relapse
   * Note: Different individuals may be at different stages
   * Stage-matched intervention principle: Interventions must match current stage
     - Precontemplation → Consciousness raising, dramatic relief
     - Contemplation → Self-reevaluation, decisional balance
     - Preparation → Self-liberation, commitment strengthening
     - Action → Counter-conditioning, stimulus control, reinforcement
     - Maintenance → Relapse prevention, helping relationships

4. **Apply Social Cognitive Theory constructs**
   * Assess key constructs:
     - **Self-Efficacy:** Confidence in ability to perform the behavior
     - **Outcome Expectations:** Beliefs about consequences of the behavior (physical, social, self-evaluative)
     - **Observational Learning:** Availability of role models and vicarious experience
     - **Behavioral Capability:** Skills and knowledge required
     - **Reinforcement:** Incentives and rewards (internal and external)
     - **Reciprocal Determinism:** How behavior, person, and environment interact
   * Identify which constructs are most relevant to target
   * Note existing strengths to leverage

5. **Conduct comprehensive barrier analysis**
   * Categorize barriers by type:
     - **Individual barriers:** Knowledge gaps, skill deficits, beliefs, competing priorities
     - **Social barriers:** Lack of support, negative social norms, peer influence
     - **Environmental barriers:** Physical access, time constraints, resource limitations
     - **Systemic barriers:** Policies, economic factors, cultural factors
   * For each barrier identified:
     - Assess modifiability (Can it be changed? How easily?)
     - Assess impact (How much does it impede the behavior?)
     - Prioritize barriers by modifiability × impact
   * Identify enablers and facilitators that already exist

6. **Select behavior change techniques (BCTs) using evidence-based taxonomy**
   * Based on COM-B analysis, select appropriate intervention functions:

   | COM-B Component | Intervention Functions | Example BCTs |
   |-----------------|----------------------|--------------|
   | Physical Capability | Training, Enablement | Instruction on how to perform, behavioral practice/rehearsal |
   | Psychological Capability | Education, Training | Information about consequences, problem solving |
   | Physical Opportunity | Environmental restructuring, Enablement | Adding objects to environment, prompts/cues |
   | Social Opportunity | Modeling, Enablement, Environmental restructuring | Social comparison, social support, restructuring social environment |
   | Reflective Motivation | Education, Persuasion, Incentivization | Information about consequences, credible source, self-monitoring |
   | Automatic Motivation | Persuasion, Modeling, Incentivization, Environmental restructuring | Habit formation, reduce negative emotions, associate with positive |

   * Select 3-7 BCTs maximum to maintain focus
   * Ensure BCTs address the primary barriers identified
   * Consider feasibility, acceptability, and cost-effectiveness
   * Specify how each BCT will be delivered

7. **Design measurement and evaluation strategy**
   * Define outcome measures:
     - **Primary outcome:** Direct measure of target behavior (frequency, duration, quality)
     - **Secondary outcomes:** Related behaviors, intermediate outcomes
     - **Process measures:** Reach, dose, fidelity, acceptability
   * Select measurement methods:
     - Self-report (diaries, surveys, recall)
     - Observation (direct, video)
     - Objective measures (sensors, records, biomarkers)
     - Informant reports (others' observations)
   * Establish measurement timeline:
     - Baseline assessment
     - During-intervention monitoring
     - Post-intervention assessment
     - Follow-up assessments (maintenance)
   * Define success criteria (What level of change indicates success?)

8. **Create implementation plan with probability-weighted scenarios**
   * **Optimistic Scenario:** Everything goes well
     - Expected outcomes and timeline
     - Probability estimate
   * **Realistic Scenario:** Typical challenges encountered
     - Expected outcomes with common setbacks
     - Probability estimate
   * **Challenging Scenario:** Significant barriers emerge
     - Minimum viable progress
     - Probability estimate
   * For each scenario:
     - Identify early warning indicators
     - Specify adaptive strategies and contingencies
   * Include relapse prevention planning:
     - High-risk situations identification
     - Coping strategies for lapses
     - Distinction between lapse and relapse

**Psychology-Specific Considerations:**
- This is an intervention design tool, not clinical treatment guidance
- Recognize that behavior change is complex and context-dependent
- Acknowledge individual differences in what works
- Consider cultural appropriateness of techniques and frameworks
- Ensure voluntary participation and informed consent principles
- Address potential unintended consequences of intervention
- Build in ethical considerations (autonomy, beneficence, non-maleficence)
- Note that population-level interventions differ from individual interventions

**Expected Output:** A comprehensive behavior change plan containing:
1. Clearly defined target behavior with measurable criteria
2. COM-B assessment summary with priority barriers
3. Stage of change assessment with stage-matched recommendations
4. Social Cognitive Theory construct analysis
5. Prioritized barrier analysis with modifiability ratings
6. Selected BCTs with delivery specifications
7. Measurement strategy with outcome definitions and timeline
8. Probability-weighted implementation scenarios with contingencies

## Behavior Change Plan: Increasing Medication Adherence in Hypertension Patients

**Target Behavior:** Take prescribed antihypertensive medication at the same time daily, achieving 90%+ adherence over 3 months.

**Target Population:** Adults aged 50-70 with newly diagnosed hypertension, prescribed once-daily medication.

---

### 1. COM-B Analysis Summary

| Component | Sub-component | Rating | Key Findings |
|-----------|---------------|--------|--------------|
| **Capability** | Physical | High | No physical barriers to taking oral medication |
| | Psychological | Medium | Some knowledge gaps about medication importance, limited understanding of asymptomatic nature of hypertension |
| **Opportunity** | Physical | Medium | Medication stored in bathroom; no consistent daily routine anchor |
| | Social | Low | Family unaware of diagnosis; no social support structure for adherence |
| **Motivation** | Reflective | Low | Does not perceive immediate benefit; competing health priorities |
| | Automatic | Low | No established habit; medication not linked to existing routine |

**Primary Barriers:**
1. Social Opportunity (lack of support and accountability)
2. Reflective Motivation (low perceived need due to no symptoms)
3. Automatic Motivation (no habit formation)

---

### 2. Stage of Change Assessment

**Current Stage:** Contemplation-Preparation borderline

**Evidence:** Patient has filled prescription (action initiation) but expresses ambivalence ("I feel fine, is this really necessary?") and inconsistent taking (contemplation characteristics).

**Stage-Matched Approach:**
- Decisional balance exploration (contemplation)
- Commitment strengthening and self-liberation (preparation)
- Stimulus control planning (preparing for action)

---

### 3. Social Cognitive Theory Construct Analysis

| Construct | Current State | Intervention Target |
|-----------|---------------|---------------------|
| Self-Efficacy | Moderate | Build confidence through small wins, simplify regimen |
| Outcome Expectations | Low (negative physical) | Education on benefits, reframe outcomes from prevention to vitality |
| Observational Learning | None | Connect with successfully adherent peers |
| Reinforcement | None | Build intrinsic monitoring, consider family involvement |

---

### 4. Barrier Analysis (Prioritized)

| Barrier | Type | Impact | Modifiability | Priority | Strategy |
|---------|------|--------|---------------|----------|----------|
| No perceived symptoms/benefit | Individual (belief) | High | Medium | 1 | Education, personalized risk communication |
| No daily routine anchor | Environmental | High | High | 2 | Habit stacking with existing behavior |
| Family unaware | Social | Medium | High | 3 | Family disclosure conversation support |
| Medication location | Environmental | Low | High | 4 | Relocate to routine-visible location |

---

### 5. Selected Behavior Change Techniques

1. **Information about health consequences** (Address reflective motivation)
   - Delivery: Personalized risk calculator showing individual 10-year cardiovascular risk with/without treatment
   - Timing: Initial session

2. **Action planning** (Enhance psychological capability)
   - Delivery: Specific when-where-how plan creation: "After I brush my teeth in the morning, I will take my medication from the kitchen counter"
   - Timing: Initial session with written plan

3. **Habit formation through routine integration** (Build automatic motivation)
   - Delivery: Stack medication with existing morning routine; visual prompt placement
   - Timing: Week 1 implementation

4. **Self-monitoring of behavior** (Support reflective motivation)
   - Delivery: Simple calendar checkbox or pill organizer with weekly review
   - Timing: Ongoing

5. **Social support (practical)** (Improve social opportunity)
   - Delivery: Enlist family member as supportive reminder partner
   - Timing: After week 1, once patient is comfortable with routine

6. **Behavioral contract** (Strengthen commitment)
   - Delivery: Written commitment to 30-day trial with self-chosen reward
   - Timing: Initial session

---

### 6. Measurement Strategy

**Primary Outcome:** Medication adherence rate
- Measurement: Pill count + self-report diary
- Target: >90% doses taken on correct day
- Timeline: Weekly for month 1, biweekly for months 2-3

**Secondary Outcomes:**
- Blood pressure readings (monthly)
- Self-efficacy score (baseline, 4 weeks, 12 weeks)
- Habit automaticity (SRBAI) at 4, 8, 12 weeks

**Process Measures:**
- Completion of action planning (Y/N)
- Family member engagement (Y/N)
- Self-monitoring compliance (days recorded/days elapsed)

**Success Criteria:**
- Minimal: 80% adherence by month 3
- Target: 90% adherence by month 3
- Optimal: 95% adherence with habit automaticity established

---

### 7. Probability-Weighted Scenarios

**Optimistic Scenario (25% probability)**
- Rapid habit formation within 2 weeks
- Family support enhances motivation
- 95%+ adherence achieved and maintained
- Early warning: Adherence >85% in week 1
- Approach: Reinforce, extend self-monitoring, fade support gradually

**Realistic Scenario (55% probability)**
- Initial adherence variable (70-80%), improving over month 1
- Some missed doses during routine disruptions
- 85-90% adherence by month 3
- Early warning: 2-3 missed doses per week in weeks 1-2
- Approach: Troubleshoot barriers, strengthen habit cues, add backup reminders

**Challenging Scenario (20% probability)**
- Persistent ambivalence about medication need
- Routine disruptions prevent habit formation
- <80% adherence despite intervention
- Early warning: <70% adherence at week 2, expressed doubts about continuing
- Contingency: Return to contemplation-focused approach, motivational interviewing, explore alternative medication timing, consider simplifying further

**Relapse Prevention:**
- High-risk situations: Travel, illness, routine changes, medication side effects
- Coping strategy: Portable pill case, temporary alarm backup, provider contact for side effects
- Lapse response: Normalize brief lapses, identify trigger, recommit without self-criticism

---

**Techniques Used:**
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis):** Systematic COM-B framework analysis examining capability, opportunity, and motivation dimensions
- **DT-01 (Task Breakdown):** Step-by-step breakdown of behavior change planning process
- **DS-02 (Metric Specification):** Clear measurement criteria, success definitions, and evaluation timeline
- **NE-10 (Probability-Weighted Scenarios):** Three scenarios with probability estimates and contingency planning
