# Dual-Relationship / Boundary Analyzer

## Objective

Analyze a potential or existing multiple/dual relationship and decide whether it is permissible, a manageable boundary crossing, or an impermissible boundary violation. The analysis is anchored to **APA Ethics Code Standard 3.05 (Multiple Relationships)** and parallel ACA/NASW provisions, and must:

1. Characterize the relationship and how it arose (sought, incidental, unavoidable, small-community).
2. Apply the 3.05 test: a multiple relationship is unethical only if it **could reasonably be expected to impair objectivity/competence/effectiveness or risks exploitation or harm**.
3. Distinguish a **boundary crossing** (a deviation from the usual frame that may be benign or even therapeutic) from a **boundary violation** (exploitative or harmful).
4. Produce a management/mitigation plan or a recusal/termination/referral decision, with documentation.

## When to Use

- A current/former client, supervisee, or their relation enters another role with the clinician (business, social, familial, employment, financial, online).
- Small-community or rural practice where overlap is hard to avoid.
- A request for a non-clinical favor, gift, barter, or social-media connection.
- Considering a romantic/sexual boundary — which is categorically prohibited with current clients and tightly restricted post-termination.
- Supervision reviewing a supervisee's boundary decisions.

## Inputs / Context Required

- **The two roles**: the clinical role and the second relationship, and the people involved (de-identified).
- **Origin**: sought by clinician/client, incidental, unavoidable, pre-existing.
- **Power differential and dependency**: the client's vulnerability, transference, current treatment intensity.
- **Temporal status**: current client / former client (and time since termination) / never a client.
- **Community context**: urban vs. small/rural community where avoidance is impractical.
- **What has happened so far**: any actions already taken.
- `[clinician input required: organizational policy, licensure-board rule, or jurisdictional statute on the boundary at issue]`

## Constraints

### Must

- Apply the APA 3.05 standard explicitly, including 3.05(a) (the impairment/exploitation/harm test), 3.05(b) (unforeseen multiple relationships — resolve with the client's best interests and code compliance), and 3.05(c) (court-ordered roles).
- Classify the situation as: **permissible (no reasonable expectation of impairment/exploitation/harm)**, **boundary crossing — manageable with safeguards**, or **boundary violation — impermissible**.
- Treat **sexual/romantic relationships with current clients as categorically prohibited**, and post-termination sexual relationships as prohibited except under the narrow, time-limited conditions the code specifies (and never to be facilitated by this analysis).
- Assess four risk vectors: **impairment of objectivity/judgment, risk of exploitation, foreseeable harm, and effect on the client's transference/treatment**.
- Evaluate **foreseeability** and **avoidability** (could this have been anticipated; can it be avoided).
- Produce a concrete management plan (informed consent, frame clarification, safeguards, consultation) OR a recusal/termination/referral when the relationship is impermissible.
- Include onward referral to licensing board/legal counsel where a violation or statute is implicated.
- Include documentation and supervisor/consultant co-sign where applicable.

### Must Not

- Do not treat all dual relationships as automatically unethical; 3.05 turns on reasonable expectation of impairment/exploitation/harm.
- Do not minimize a violation as a mere "crossing" when exploitation or foreseeable harm is present.
- Do not provide any pathway that facilitates a sexual/romantic relationship with a current client or circumvents post-termination restrictions.
- Do not ignore power differential, transference, or client dependency.
- Do not fabricate board rules or statutes; flag with `[clinician input required]` / `[verify]`.
- Do not include client-identifying detail.

## Instructions

1. **Describe both roles** and how the overlap arose (origin, avoidability).
2. **Locate the temporal/power context**: current vs. former client, time since termination, dependency, transference.
3. **Apply 3.05**: assess impairment of objectivity, exploitation risk, foreseeable harm, and treatment/transference effects.
4. **Screen categorical prohibitions** (sexual/romantic with current clients; post-termination restrictions); if implicated, stop and route appropriately.
5. **Classify**: permissible / manageable crossing / violation.
6. **If manageable**: design safeguards (informed consent, role clarity, frame, consultation, monitoring).
7. **If violation**: specify recusal/termination/referral and onward reporting/consultation obligations.
8. **Document** and run verification.

## Verification

- [ ] Both roles, origin, and avoidability described.
- [ ] Temporal/power context (current vs. former client, dependency, transference) established.
- [ ] Categorical screen run; current-client sexual/romantic relationships treated as prohibited and routed out.
- [ ] APA 3.05 applied across impairment, exploitation, foreseeable harm, and treatment effect.
- [ ] Foreseeability and avoidability assessed.
- [ ] Situation classified as permissible / manageable crossing / violation — crossing not conflated with violation.
- [ ] Management safeguards specified (if manageable) or recusal/termination/referral (if violation).
- [ ] Onward reporting/consultation obligation flagged where a violation or statute is implicated.
- [ ] No pathway facilitates a prohibited relationship.
- [ ] Documentation line present; supervisor/consultant co-sign where applicable; board rules/statutes not fabricated; client material de-identified.
