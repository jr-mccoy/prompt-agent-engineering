# Scope-of-Practice / Boundaries-of-Competence Decision Aid

## Objective

Help a clinician decide, when competence is ambiguous, whether they may ethically and legally provide a service — and if not yet, how to acquire competence or refer out. The analysis is anchored to **APA Ethics Code Standard 2.01 (Boundaries of Competence)** and parallel ACA/NASW competence provisions, and must:

1. Specify exactly what new element creates the competence question (new population, presenting problem, modality, technology, or setting).
2. Assess current competence across education, training, supervised experience, and consultation.
3. Decide among: provide now, provide with supervision/consultation, build competence before providing, or refer out — including the emergency exception.
4. Address licensure/credential and jurisdictional limits (e.g., telehealth across state lines, prescriptive authority, specialty credentials).

## When to Use

- A clinician is asked to treat a population, problem, modality, or setting at the edge of or beyond their trained competence.
- Adding a new service line (e.g., EMDR, ERP for OCD, gender-affirming care, forensic evaluation, neuropsych testing).
- Telehealth or relocation raising cross-jurisdiction licensure questions.
- Supervision where a supervisee's caseload is drifting beyond their training.
- An emergency where no qualified provider is available and the competence question must be weighed against access.

## Inputs / Context Required

- **The service in question**: population, presenting problem, modality/technique, and setting.
- **The novel element**: what specifically is outside or at the edge of established competence.
- **Competence inventory**: `[clinician input required: relevant coursework, formal training, supervised hours, prior cases, consultation access, and any specialty credentials]`.
- **Profession, license type, and jurisdiction(s)**: and whether the service crosses state/national lines.
- **Availability of alternatives**: are qualified providers/referrals available, and timeframe.
- **Emergency status**: is this an emergency where no qualified provider is available?
- `[clinician input required: organizational policy, payer, or credentialing constraints]`

## Constraints

### Must

- Anchor the analysis to APA Standard 2.01 (Boundaries of Competence), including 2.01(c) developing competence in new areas and 2.01(e) emerging areas without established standards, and the **2.01(d)/2.02 emergency exception** (services in emergencies to ensure access, discontinued once the emergency ends or appropriate services are available).
- Assess competence across all four sources: **education, training, supervised experience, professional consultation** — not self-rated comfort alone.
- Decide explicitly among: provide now / provide with supervision or consultation / build competence first / refer out / emergency-only provision.
- For "build competence," specify a concrete competence-acquisition pathway (didactics, supervised cases, consultation, credential) with a checkpoint before independent practice.
- Address licensure and credential limits, including cross-jurisdiction telehealth rules and any specialty credential or scope statute.
- Address cultural competence as part of competence, not separate from it.
- Include a referral pathway whenever the decision is refer-out, and a supervision/consultation pathway whenever provision proceeds at the edge of competence.
- Include a documentation line and supervisor/consultant co-sign where applicable.

### Must Not

- Do not equate willingness or confidence with competence.
- Do not authorize independent practice in a new area on the basis of a single workshop without supervised application.
- Do not ignore jurisdictional/licensure limits even when clinical competence exists.
- Do not over-refer reflexively when a clear competence-acquisition pathway with consultation is appropriate and access is otherwise limited.
- Do not fabricate the clinician's credentials/training; use `[clinician input required: ...]`.

## Instructions

1. **Name the novel element** and why it raises the competence question.
2. **Inventory competence** across education, training, supervised experience, and consultation; rate the gap.
3. **Check jurisdiction/licensure**: license scope, specialty statutes, cross-state telehealth rules.
4. **Check cultural competence** for the population in question.
5. **Apply 2.01**: determine which provision governs (established area, developing competence, emerging area, emergency).
6. **Choose the pathway**: provide now / provide with supervision-consultation / build competence first / refer out / emergency-only.
7. **If building competence**: specify the acquisition plan and a gate before independent practice.
8. **If referring out**: specify the referral pathway and interim coverage.
9. **Document** and run verification.

## Verification

- [ ] Novel element clearly identified.
- [ ] Competence inventoried across education, training, supervised experience, and consultation — not confidence alone.
- [ ] Cultural competence addressed as part of competence.
- [ ] Jurisdiction/licensure and cross-state telehealth limits checked.
- [ ] Governing APA 2.01 provision named (including emergency exception where relevant).
- [ ] Explicit pathway chosen among provide / provide-with-support / build-first / refer / emergency-only.
- [ ] Competence-acquisition pathway includes a gate before independent practice (if applicable).
- [ ] Referral pathway and interim coverage specified (if referring out).
- [ ] Emergency-only provision includes a termination condition.
- [ ] Documentation line present; supervisor/consultant co-sign where applicable.
- [ ] Clinician credentials not fabricated; gaps flagged with `[clinician input required]`.
