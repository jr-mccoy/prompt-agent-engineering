# Ethics Consultation Walkthrough

## Objective

Take a clinician through a structured ethics consultation on a specific dilemma, using a named ethical decision-making model so the reasoning is transparent, defensible, and documentable. The walkthrough must:

1. Define the ethical problem precisely (the actual conflict, not the surface complaint).
2. Identify the applicable ethics-code standards (e.g., APA Ethics Code, ACA Code of Ethics, NASW Code) and any law/regulation in tension with them.
3. Map stakeholders and their interests.
4. Generate and weigh options against the five core principles, then state a defensible decision with a documentation record.

## When to Use

- A clinician faces a values/standards conflict (confidentiality vs. duty to protect, competence limits, conflicting obligations, gifts, bartering, technology/social-media boundaries).
- Supervision where a supervisee needs a model for ethical reasoning, not just an answer.
- Peer consultation documenting the reasoning behind a risky judgment call.
- Preparing a defensible record before acting on an ambiguous ethical situation.

## Inputs / Context Required

- **The dilemma (de-identified)**: what is happening and why it feels ethically fraught.
- **Profession and governing code(s)**: psychology (APA), counseling (ACA), social work (NASW), or other — and licensure jurisdiction.
- **Relevant law/regulation**: any statute, board rule, mandated-reporting or duty-to-protect law in play `[clinician input required: jurisdiction-specific legal standard]`.
- **Stakeholders**: client, third parties, family, agency, profession, the clinician.
- **Time pressure**: emergent (act now) vs. deliberative.
- **Prior consultation**: any consultation already obtained.
- `[clinician input required: organizational policy that applies]`

## Constraints

### Must

- Name the decision model used (e.g., the Koocher–Keith-Spiegel / APA eight-step model, or Kitchener's principle-based approach) and follow its steps in order.
- Anchor to the five core ethical principles — **autonomy, beneficence, nonmaleficence, justice, fidelity** (Kitchener) — when balancing options.
- Cite specific, applicable ethics-code standards by section where natural (e.g., APA Standard 4.05 disclosures, 3.05 multiple relationships, 2.01 boundaries of competence) — without inventing standard numbers.
- Distinguish what the **code requires**, what the **law requires**, and where they conflict; name the conflict explicitly rather than collapsing it.
- Generate at least three options, including a do-nothing/least-restrictive option, and weigh each against the principles.
- State a decision, the rationale, and the residual risks/limits of that choice.
- Specify a documentation record (what to chart, consultation obtained, basis for the decision).
- Flag any situation requiring legal counsel or licensing-board guidance rather than peer consultation alone.

### Must Not

- Do not output a verdict without showing the model's steps.
- Do not fabricate ethics-code standard numbers or legal citations; if unsure, describe the standard and mark `[verify standard/citation]`.
- Do not treat a legal answer as automatically the ethical answer (or vice versa); preserve the distinction.
- Do not substitute this consultation for required legal advice in high-stakes legal situations.
- Do not include client-identifying detail.

## Instructions

1. **Name the model** and confirm the governing code(s) and jurisdiction.
2. **Define the ethical problem**: the precise conflict, separated from the emotional or logistical surface.
3. **Gather facts** and flag unknowns that materially affect the decision.
4. **Identify applicable standards and laws**; mark where code and law diverge.
5. **Map stakeholders** and their interests/vulnerabilities.
6. **Generate options** (≥ 3, including least-restrictive/do-nothing).
7. **Weigh options against the five principles**; note which principles each option honors or sacrifices.
8. **Decide and justify**; state residual risks and any onward referral (legal counsel, board, organizational risk).
9. **Document**: write the consultation record and run verification.

## Verification

- [ ] Named decision model followed step-by-step; no verdict without the steps.
- [ ] Ethical problem stated as the precise conflict, not the surface complaint.
- [ ] Applicable ethics-code standards cited by section where natural; uncertain citations flagged `[verify]`, not fabricated.
- [ ] Law/regulation identified and code–law conflict named where present.
- [ ] Stakeholders mapped with interests/vulnerabilities.
- [ ] ≥ 3 options including a least-restrictive/do-nothing option.
- [ ] Each option weighed against autonomy, beneficence, nonmaleficence, justice, fidelity.
- [ ] Decision stated with rationale and residual risks.
- [ ] Onward referral to legal/board flagged where the situation exceeds peer consultation.
- [ ] Documentation record specified; supervisor co-sign field present where applicable.
- [ ] Client material de-identified; nothing fabricated.
