# Military Sexual Trauma (MST)-Aware Engagement and Treatment Planning Protocol

## Objective

Provide a trauma-informed, gender-inclusive protocol for engaging a veteran or service member who screens positive for Military Sexual Trauma (MST) and translating that into a phased treatment plan that:

1. Applies the **VA definition of MST** — sexual assault or repeated, threatening sexual harassment experienced during military service — and uses the **universal two-item VA MST screen**, recognizing that MST is an experience, not a diagnosis.
2. Differentiates **harassment** from **assault**, and accounts for the unique features of MST: perpetrator and victim often in the same unit/chain of command, dependence on the institution that failed to protect, and continued service alongside the perpetrator.
3. Addresses **institutional betrayal** as a distinct, formulation-relevant injury layered on top of interpersonal trauma.
4. Is explicitly **gender-inclusive** — MST affects men and women; men are a large share of those affected and disclosure rates are low; the protocol avoids assumptions about gender, perpetrator, or reaction.
5. Documents **VA free MST-related care eligibility** — available regardless of service-connection, discharge characterization in many cases, VA enrollment status, or whether the veteran reported the event at the time.
6. Maintains a **continuous suicide-risk lens** with lethal-means (firearm) counseling and the Veterans Crisis Line pathway, given elevated risk.
7. Produces a phased, trauma-informed treatment plan referencing (not delivering) evidence-based trauma treatments (CPT, PE, EMDR).

## When to Use

- When a veteran screens positive on the VA MST screen at intake, or discloses sexual assault or repeated threatening sexual harassment during service.
- When engaging a veteran whose presentation suggests MST even if not yet disclosed, to structure a safe, paced, gender-inclusive inquiry.
- When building or revising a treatment plan for an established MST-related presentation.
- When clarifying VA MST-related care eligibility and access pathways with or for the veteran.

## When NOT to Use

- For the universal MST screen embedded in routine intake: that lives in `psychology_veteran_intake_with_service_history.md` (this protocol begins at or after a positive screen / disclosure).
- For combat-trauma or moral-injury formulation unrelated to sexual trauma: use `psychology_combat_trauma_formulation.md` (though moral-injury/institutional-betrayal concepts may cross-reference).
- For acute crisis: lead with `psychology_columbia_suicide_risk_assessment.md` and the Veterans Crisis Line, then resume this protocol when stabilized.

## Inputs / Context Required

- **Screen/disclosure status:** Result of the VA two-item MST screen and/or nature of disclosure.
- **Service context:** Branch, era, status (veteran/active/reserve), discharge characterization, VA enrollment and service-connection (to map eligibility — not to gate care).
- **Trauma parameters (paced, only as the veteran is ready):** Harassment vs. assault vs. both; single vs. repeated; relationship to perpetrator and chain of command; whether the veteran continued serving with the perpetrator.
- **Institutional response:** Whether reported; response received; retaliation, disbelief, or career consequences (institutional-betrayal indicators).
- **Current symptoms:** PCL-5; PHQ-9; AUDIT-C; sleep; dissociation; shame/self-blame; sexual-health and intimacy impact.
- **Risk data:** Columbia C-SSRS; lethal-means (firearm) status; shame-driven SI.
- **Identity and supports:** Gender identity, sexual orientation (without assumption), relational supports, prior care experiences (including prior re-traumatizing care).
- `[clinician input required: veteran's stated goals, current safety, and readiness for any trauma-focused work]`

## Constraints

### Must

- Use the VA definition of MST and state that MST is an **experience/exposure, not a diagnosis**; diagnose resulting conditions (e.g., PTSD, MDD) separately.
- Be **gender-inclusive** throughout: do not assume the veteran's gender, the perpetrator's gender, the type of act, or the veteran's emotional reaction; explicitly note that men are commonly affected and underdisclose.
- Differentiate **harassment vs. assault** and capture the MST-specific features (intra-unit perpetrator, chain-of-command dynamics, continued proximity, dependence on the institution).
- Assess and document **institutional betrayal** as a distinct injury layer and integrate it into the formulation.
- Document **VA MST-related care eligibility**: free MST-related care is available regardless of service-connection, generally regardless of VA enrollment requirements that apply to other care, and without requiring that the veteran reported the MST or has documentation — flag eligibility as a formal VA determination but do not imply ineligibility.
- Pace inquiry to the veteran's readiness; obtain only the detail needed for safety and planning — do not require a full trauma narrative at first contact.
- Maintain a continuous risk lens with firearm/lethal-means counseling, a Columbia C-SSRS anchor, the Veterans Crisis Line pathway, and a re-assessment hook.
- Reference evidence-based trauma treatments (CPT, PE, EMDR) as options to be delivered elsewhere, with stabilization prerequisites; do not deliver processing in this protocol.
- Flag all `[clinician input required: ...]` gaps; do not fabricate disclosure content, eligibility determinations, or instrument scores.

### Must Not

- Do not pressure a detailed account of the assault; demanding the narrative before safety and rapport can re-traumatize.
- Do not condition care, empathy, or eligibility messaging on whether the veteran reported the MST, has proof, or has a service-connection.
- Do not assume heterosexual or male-perpetrator/female-victim configurations; do not assume a male veteran was not affected.
- Do not treat institutional betrayal as merely "context"; it is a clinical injury that shapes trust, including trust in the treating system.
- Do not deliver a trauma-processing protocol here; this is engagement and planning.
- Do not fabricate VA eligibility outcomes, report content, or scores.

## Harassment vs. Assault and Institutional Betrayal Overlay

| Element | Sexual Harassment (threatening, repeated) | Sexual Assault |
|---------|-------------------------------------------|----------------|
| Nature | Repeated unwanted sexual remarks, advances, coercion, threats | Unwanted sexual contact obtained by force, threat, or inability to consent |
| MST-specific dynamics | Often by peers/superiors in unit; chain-of-command power; inescapable daily proximity | Perpetrator frequently known, in unit/command; victim may continue serving alongside them |
| Common sequelae | Hypervigilance, shame, anxiety, occupational/career impact, erosion of safety | PTSD, depression, dissociation, sexual-health/intimacy impact, profound self-blame |

Institutional betrayal indicators (assess and document): the institution depended upon for safety failed to prevent the harm, disbelieved or minimized the veteran, retaliated, damaged the career, or required continued proximity to the perpetrator. Institutional betrayal compounds interpersonal trauma and predicts mistrust of systems — including the treatment system.

## Instructions

1. **Confirm screen/disclosure and establish safety in the room.** Acknowledge the disclosure non-judgmentally, affirm that MST is common and that care is available, and explain confidentiality limits. Do not request a detailed narrative yet.

2. **Pace the inquiry.** Gather only what is needed for safety and planning: harassment vs. assault vs. both, single vs. repeated, relationship to perpetrator and chain of command, and whether the veteran continued serving alongside the perpetrator. Let the veteran control depth.

3. **Apply gender-inclusive framing.** Avoid all assumptions about the veteran's gender, the perpetrator, the act, or the expected reaction. Normalize for male veterans that MST affects men and that disclosure is hard.

4. **Assess institutional betrayal.** Whether reported, the institutional response, retaliation, disbelief, or career consequences; integrate as a distinct injury layer and note implications for trust in care.

5. **Run the continuous risk lens.** Administer/anchor Columbia C-SSRS; assess shame- and betrayal-driven SI; document firearm/lethal-means status and deliver lethal-means counseling; provide the Veterans Crisis Line pathway (988 then Press 1). Specify a re-assessment hook.

6. **Document VA MST-related care eligibility.** State that free MST-related care is available regardless of service-connection (and is not gated by reporting or documentation), flag enrollment/discharge nuances as formal determinations, and identify access pathways (VA MST Coordinator, Vet Center).

7. **Symptom and functional assessment.** PCL-5, PHQ-9, AUDIT-C; dissociation; sexual-health and intimacy impact; sleep; occupational functioning; prior care experiences (including re-traumatizing care).

8. **Build a phased, trauma-informed treatment plan.** Phase 1 stabilization and safety/trust-building (including repairing trust given institutional betrayal); Phase 2 trauma processing options referenced (CPT, PE, EMDR) with readiness prerequisites; Phase 3 integration, intimacy/relational, and meaning work. Address provider-gender preference and choice.

9. **Write the MST-Aware Engagement and Treatment Plan** using the output format below.

10. **Run verification.**
