# Combat Trauma Formulation with Moral Injury

## Objective

Produce an advanced, mechanism-level case formulation for a combat-exposed veteran that:

1. Maps the PTSD symptom architecture (re-experiencing, avoidance, negative alterations in cognition/mood, hyperarousal) against named index events.
2. Explicitly assesses for **moral injury** using the Litz et al. (2009) framework and the Moral Injury Events Scale (MIES), and **differentiates** transgression-based moral injury (guilt, shame, betrayal, loss of meaning) from fear-based PTSD — recognizing they frequently co-occur.
3. Distinguishes the affective signatures (fear/threat vs. guilt/shame/betrayal) because they imply different treatment levers.
4. Maps evidence-based treatment options (CPT, PE, EMDR — referenced as options, not delivered within this prompt) to the formulation, and notes where moral-injury-specific approaches (e.g., Adaptive Disclosure, Building Spiritual Strength, Acceptance/values work) augment standard protocols.
5. Maintains a continuous suicide-risk lens, since guilt/shame and moral injury are independently associated with suicidal ideation in this population.

## When to Use

- After a completed veteran intake when a combat-trauma presentation requires a detailed formulation to drive treatment planning or supervision.
- When a veteran's distress centers on guilt, shame, betrayal, or "loss of who I was" rather than (or in addition to) fear and threat — i.e., when moral injury is on the table.
- When standard trauma-focused treatment has stalled and reformulation is needed to test a moral-injury hypothesis.
- When articulating, for case consultation or supervision, why a particular treatment sequence fits this veteran's mechanism.

## When NOT to Use

- For the initial service-history intake: use `psychology_veteran_intake_with_service_history.md` first.
- For sexual-trauma presentations where MST is the index event: use `psychology_military_sexual_trauma_aware_protocol.md` (moral-injury and institutional-betrayal logic there is MST-specific).
- For final modality selection across a broad differential: pair this with `psychology_modality_selection_decision_aid.md`.
- For acute crisis: lead with `psychology_columbia_suicide_risk_assessment.md`.

## Inputs / Context Required

- **Service & exposure context:** Branch, era, MOS, deployments, Combat Exposure Scale results, role in events.
- **Index event(s):** Candidate Criterion A traumatic event(s) and candidate morally injurious event(s) — these may differ.
- **Symptom data:** PCL-5 total and cluster scores; PHQ-9; AUDIT-C; sleep; anger; dissociation indicators.
- **Moral injury data:** MIES results or structured inquiry into perpetration, witnessing, betrayal, and failure-to-prevent experiences; affective content (guilt, shame, betrayal, contempt, loss of meaning, spiritual/existential distress).
- **Risk data:** Most recent Columbia C-SSRS; lethal-means (firearm) status; guilt/shame-driven SI.
- **Function & meaning:** Pre- vs. post-event identity, values, relationships, occupational functioning, spiritual/moral framework.
- `[clinician input required: current safety status and any change since last risk screen that should reorder this work toward risk evaluation]`

## Constraints

### Must

- Distinguish, in the formulation, **fear-based PTSD** (threat/learning model; mechanism = pathological fear conditioning and avoidance) from **moral injury** (transgression/betrayal model; mechanism = unassimilated guilt/shame and shattered moral expectations), and document where they overlap.
- Anchor every symptom cluster and the moral-injury construct to specific named index events; do not formulate trauma in the abstract.
- Use the Litz et al. moral injury framework and name the MIES; do not conflate moral injury with a DSM diagnosis — it is a construct that may accompany, but is not identical to, PTSD or MDD.
- Map evidence-based treatment options (CPT, PE, EMDR) to the formulation and explicitly state these are referenced as options to be selected/delivered elsewhere, not administered by this prompt.
- Where moral injury is prominent, identify augmentation approaches (Adaptive Disclosure; Building Spiritual Strength; values/acceptance and self-forgiveness work; chaplaincy/spiritual-care collaboration) and note that standard exposure alone may be insufficient for transgression-based distress.
- Maintain a continuous risk lens: assess guilt/shame-driven and betrayal-driven suicidal ideation, document firearm/lethal-means status, and include a risk-reassessment hook.
- Flag all `[clinician input required: ...]` gaps; do not fabricate event details, MIES/PCL-5 scores, or exposure history.

### Must Not

- Do not collapse moral injury into PTSD or vice versa; treating shame-based moral injury as if it were fear conditioning (exposure-only) risks reinforcing self-condemnation.
- Do not assign moral judgment to the veteran's actions or impose the clinician's moral framework; formulate the veteran's own moral appraisal and its function.
- Do not omit the risk lens because the presentation is "about meaning" rather than threat; moral injury is independently linked to suicidality.
- Do not deliver a trauma-processing protocol within this prompt; this is formulation, not treatment delivery.
- Do not fabricate exposure narratives, deployment details, or instrument scores.

## Fear-Based PTSD vs. Moral Injury: Differentiation Overlay

| Dimension | Fear-Based PTSD | Moral Injury (Litz et al.) |
|-----------|-----------------|----------------------------|
| Core event | Life-threat / horror (Criterion A) | Perpetrating, failing to prevent, witnessing, or being betrayed in acts that transgress moral beliefs |
| Dominant affect | Fear, terror, helplessness | Guilt, shame, contempt, betrayal, anger, loss of meaning |
| Mechanism | Pathological fear conditioning, avoidance, hyperarousal | Unassimilated moral appraisal; shattered moral/just-world expectations; self-condemnation |
| Cognitions | "I am in danger"; "the world is unsafe" | "I am bad/unforgivable"; "they betrayed us"; "I no longer know who I am" |
| Avoidance target | Trauma reminders / threat cues | Self-disclosure, intimacy, spiritual/moral reckoning |
| Treatment lever | Extinction/processing (PE, CPT, EMDR) | Disclosure, moral repair, self-forgiveness, meaning/values, spiritual care; CPT addresses guilt cognitions; Adaptive Disclosure targets transgression directly |
| Suicide risk pathway | Hyperarousal, hopelessness | Shame, self-punishment, "I deserve to suffer," loss of belonging |

Note: co-occurrence is common. The formulation should specify the proportional contribution of each and which is currently most functionally impairing.

## Instructions

1. **Consolidate exposure and index events.** From intake and inputs, list candidate Criterion A events and candidate morally injurious events separately. Note role, agency, and outcome for each.

2. **Map PTSD symptom architecture.** For each PCL-5 cluster, link current symptoms to specific index events. Note severity, dissociation, and sleep/anger.

3. **Assess moral injury.** Apply the Litz et al. framework and MIES inquiry across perpetration, witnessing, failure-to-prevent, and betrayal. Characterize the dominant moral emotions (guilt, shame, betrayal, contempt) and the resulting self/world appraisals and meaning disruption.

4. **Differentiate and proportion.** Using the overlay, state how much of the presentation is fear-based vs. transgression-based, where they overlap, and which is most functionally impairing now.

5. **Run the continuous risk lens.** Assess guilt/shame- and betrayal-driven suicidal ideation; document firearm/lethal-means status and most recent Columbia C-SSRS; specify a re-assessment hook. Provide the Veterans Crisis Line pathway (988 then Press 1) if any elevation.

6. **Build the integrative formulation.** Synthesize predisposing, precipitating, perpetuating, and protective factors across both the fear and moral-injury pathways, including spiritual/existential and relational dimensions.

7. **Map treatment options to mechanism.** Match CPT / PE / EMDR (referenced as options) to the fear-based pathway, and identify moral-injury augmentations (Adaptive Disclosure, Building Spiritual Strength, self-forgiveness/values work, chaplaincy collaboration) for the transgression-based pathway. State sequencing and prerequisites.

8. **Write the Combat Trauma Formulation** using the output format below.

9. **Run verification.**

## Verification

- [ ] Index events listed separately for threat/horror vs. morally injurious events; symptom clusters anchored to named events.
- [ ] PTSD symptom architecture mapped across all four clusters with dissociation noted.
- [ ] Moral injury assessed via Litz et al. framework with MIES named; characterized by type and dominant moral emotions.
- [ ] Moral injury explicitly distinguished from PTSD diagnosis (construct, not a DSM code).
- [ ] Differentiation overlay applied; proportional contribution of fear vs. transgression stated, plus most impairing pathway.
- [ ] Continuous risk lens addresses guilt/shame- and betrayal-driven SI, firearm/lethal-means status, and a re-assessment hook.
- [ ] Veterans Crisis Line (988 then Press 1) included where any elevation exists.
- [ ] Treatment options (CPT/PE/EMDR) mapped to mechanism and labeled as referenced, not delivered; moral-injury augmentations identified.
- [ ] Sequencing/prerequisites and concurrent targets specified.
- [ ] No clinician moral judgment imposed; veteran's own moral appraisal formulated.
- [ ] Gaps flagged with `[clinician input required: ...]`; no fabricated events or scores.
