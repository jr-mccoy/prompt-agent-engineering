# Adult Autism Assessment Intake Module

## Objective

Produce a structured, masking-aware intake record for an adult presenting for autism (ASD) assessment that:

1. Gathers DSM-5-TR-aligned history across the two core domains — social communication/interaction and restricted, repetitive patterns of behavior, interests, or activities (RRBs) — with explicit attention to how camouflaging may conceal observable signs.
2. Documents retrospective childhood-onset evidence (DSM-5-TR requires symptoms present in the early developmental period, even if not fully manifest until social demands exceeded capacity).
3. Administers and interprets adult-validated screening instruments (RAADS-R, AQ, CAT-Q for camouflaging) by band, noting they are screens, not diagnostic confirmation.
4. Distinguishes autistic traits from co-occurring, separately treatable conditions (anxiety, depression, OCD, CPTSD, social anxiety, ADHD) without collapsing one into the other.
5. Screens for elevated suicide risk and autistic burnout.
6. Identifies what trained, observation-based confirmation (ADOS-2 / ADI-R) would add and whether referral is indicated.
7. Produces a CPT 90791 intake note. (Formal standardized testing batteries are billed and referred separately — see Billing Note.)

## When to Use

- An adult (18+) self-refers or is referred for autism assessment, often after self-identification, a relative's diagnosis, or burnout/breakdown of long-standing coping.
- A late-identified adult who has "passed" socially for decades and now seeks formal evaluation, accommodations, or self-understanding.
- A clinician needs to structure a masking-aware history that adult instruments alone do not capture.

## When NOT to Use

- For confirmatory diagnosis where local standards require ADOS-2 / ADI-R administration — use this module to organize history and screen, then route to a trained administrator.
- For children/adolescents — use a developmentally calibrated pediatric protocol and caregiver-informant tools.
- For acute crisis presentations — stabilize first; defer the structured assessment.

## Inputs / Context Required

- **Demographics and language preference:** Name, age, pronouns, identity-first vs. person-first language preference (ask directly; do not assume).
- **Reason for assessment:** Self-understanding, accommodation, diagnosis for services, relationship/work concerns.
- **Developmental history:** Childhood records, school reports, parent/sibling informant availability (collateral strengthens early-onset documentation but is not always available for adults).
- **Current functioning:** Work, relationships, sensory environment, executive load, daily living.
- **Masking history:** Conscious strategies used to appear non-autistic; cost of masking.
- **Co-occurring concerns:** Mood, anxiety, OCD, trauma, ADHD, eating, sleep.
- `[clinician input required: prior evaluations, neuropsychological testing, or ADHD/learning-disability history on file]`

## Constraints

### Must

- Treat camouflaging as a confounder of observation: absence of overt social difficulty in the room does not rule out ASD in a high-masking adult. Document masking explicitly.
- Document retrospective early-developmental evidence; if collateral is unavailable, state this as a limitation rather than treating adult self-report alone as confirmatory.
- Interpret RAADS-R, AQ, and CAT-Q by published cut bands and label them screens; never present a screen score as a diagnosis.
- Assess sensory profile, monotropism (sustained, narrow attentional focus), and RRBs in adult-appropriate forms (interests, routines, regulation behaviors) — not only childhood stereotypies.
- Separate neurotype from co-occurring conditions: name which presenting distress is plausibly a treatable comorbidity vs. an expression of autistic neurology.
- Screen for suicide risk (elevated in autistic adults) and autistic burnout at every intake.
- Honor the client's language preference (identity-first "autistic" vs. person-first "person with autism") throughout the record.
- Flag all `[clinician input required: ...]` gaps; do not fabricate scores, school records, or developmental milestones.

### Must Not

- Do not frame autism itself as a disorder to be cured or as the treatment target; the assessment characterizes neurotype and identifies distress, environmental fit, and co-occurring conditions.
- Do not interpret good eye contact, humor, marriage, employment, or social success as ruling out ASD — these are common in masking adults.
- Do not collapse trauma, social anxiety, or ADHD into ASD (or vice versa) without documenting the differential reasoning.
- Do not reproduce copyrighted instrument item text; reference instruments by name and interpret structure/bands only.
- Do not claim ADOS-2/ADI-R results; note when trained administration is indicated and refer.

## Instrument Reference (interpret by band; do not reproduce items)

| Instrument | Respondent | What it captures | Interpretation note |
|------------|-----------|------------------|---------------------|
| RAADS-R (Ritvo Autism Asperger Diagnostic Scale–Revised) | Self-report, clinician-supported | Social relatedness, circumscribed interests, language, sensorimotor across lifespan | Higher totals raise suspicion; designed to retain childhood-onset framing. A screen, not diagnostic. |
| AQ (Autism-Spectrum Quotient) | Self-report | Broad autistic traits across 5 areas | Screening cutoff commonly cited; sensitive to masking under-report. |
| CAT-Q (Camouflaging Autistic Traits Questionnaire) | Self-report | Compensation, masking, assimilation | High scores indicate heavy camouflaging — contextualizes low observable signs and high masking cost. |
| ADOS-2 / ADI-R | Trained administrator / caregiver interview | Observation-based and developmental-interview confirmation | Requires certified administration; refer if confirmatory diagnosis is the goal. |

## Instructions

1. **Set language and frame.** Ask the client's preferred terminology (identity-first vs. person-first) and the purpose of assessment. Use their language throughout.
2. **Take the masking history first.** Ask what strategies the client uses to appear non-autistic (scripting, mimicking, suppressing stimming, rehearsing conversations), when masking started, and its cost (exhaustion, post-social shutdown, loss of identity). This reframes everything that follows.
3. **Document the social-communication domain.** Reciprocity, nonverbal communication, relationship development/maintenance — gathered as lived experience, not only in-room observation, given masking.
4. **Document RRBs in adult form.** Repetitive behaviors/speech, insistence on sameness and routines, highly restricted/fixated interests (and monotropism), sensory hyper-/hyporeactivity. Include adult self-regulation behaviors (stimming).
5. **Establish early-developmental onset.** Gather childhood evidence from memory, records, and collateral if available. Document limitation if collateral is absent.
6. **Administer and band the screens.** RAADS-R, AQ, CAT-Q; record totals, bands, and that they are screens. Note CAT-Q's role in explaining low observable signs.
7. **Differential pass.** For each presenting distress, state whether it is plausibly a co-occurring treatable condition (social anxiety, OCD, CPTSD, depression, ADHD) vs. an autistic-neurology expression — or both.
8. **Risk and burnout screen.** Administer a suicide-risk screen (e.g., Columbia C-SSRS) and screen for autistic burnout (pervasive exhaustion, skill loss, reduced tolerance to stimuli following sustained masking/overload).
9. **Determine next step.** State whether ADOS-2/ADI-R referral is indicated for confirmation and what the screen-plus-history supports in the interim.
10. **Write the intake note** using the output format below.
11. **Run verification.**

## Verification

- [ ] Language preference (identity-first vs. person-first) elicited and used throughout.
- [ ] Masking/camouflaging history taken and used to contextualize in-room observation.
- [ ] Both DSM-5-TR domains (A social-communication, B RRBs) documented in adult-appropriate form, including sensory and monotropism.
- [ ] Early-developmental onset addressed; collateral availability or its absence documented as a limitation.
- [ ] RAADS-R, AQ, and CAT-Q banded and explicitly labeled as screens, not diagnoses.
- [ ] Differential separates autistic neurotype from co-occurring treatable conditions with reasoning.
- [ ] Suicide-risk screen completed; autistic burnout screened.
- [ ] ADOS-2/ADI-R referral decision documented; no fabricated standardized-test results.
- [ ] Autism is not framed as the treatment target; formulation targets distress, fit, and comorbidity.
- [ ] No reproduced copyrighted item text; no fabricated scores or records; gaps flagged.
- [ ] Billing note clarifies that formal testing batteries are separate referrals.
