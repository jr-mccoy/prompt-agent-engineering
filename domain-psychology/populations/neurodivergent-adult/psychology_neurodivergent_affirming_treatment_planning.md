# Neurodivergent-Affirming Treatment Planning

## Objective

Produce a treatment plan for a neurodivergent adult (autistic, ADHD, AuDHD, or demand-avoidant profile) that:

1. Targets **distress, co-occurring treatable conditions, and environmental fit** — explicitly **not the neurotype itself**. Goals are about reducing suffering and increasing functioning on the client's terms, not making the client less autistic / less ADHD.
2. Applies the **double empathy problem**: communication and relational difficulty is a **two-way mismatch** between neurotypes, not a one-sided deficit in the autistic person; the plan adjusts environments and shared understanding, not only the client.
3. Honors the client's **identity-language preference** (identity-first vs. person-first) and affirming framing throughout.
4. Chooses **accommodation over normalization**: stims, routines, special interests, sensory supports, and communication preferences are treated as valid regulation strategies and strengths, not symptoms to extinguish.
5. Addresses **autistic burnout** with a recovery-oriented plan (demand reduction, recovery, accommodation) distinct from standard depression treatment.
6. Maintains a rigorous **co-occurring-vs-neurotype distinction**, so that genuinely treatable conditions (anxiety, depression, trauma, OCD, eating disorders, sleep) are treated and the neurotype is not pathologized.
7. Builds a **golden-thread** plan (presenting problem → target → goal → objective → intervention → measure) with **elevated-risk reassessment hooks**.

## When to Use

- After intake/assessment, when building or revising a treatment plan for a neurodivergent adult.
- When a prior plan implicitly targeted the neurotype (social-skills "normalization," extinguishing stimming, eliminating special interests) and needs reframing toward distress and fit.
- When autistic burnout, masking cost, or environmental misfit is driving the presentation.
- To articulate affirming clinical reasoning for supervision or for the client's collaborative review.

## When NOT to Use

- For assessment/diagnosis — use the autism or ADHD intake modules first.
- For acute crisis stabilization — address acute risk via crisis protocols first, then plan.
- As a reason to under-treat a genuine co-occurring condition; affirming care still vigorously treats anxiety, depression, trauma, OCD, eating disorders, and suicidality.

## Inputs / Context Required

- **Neurotype and assessment summary** (autistic / ADHD / AuDHD / demand-avoidant profile; identification status).
- **Presenting distress and functional concerns** (in the client's words and priorities).
- **Co-occurring conditions** and their severity baselines (PHQ-9, GAD-7, PCL-5, etc.).
- **Masking/burnout history** and current demand load.
- **Environmental context** (work, relationships, sensory environment, supports, accommodations available).
- **Strengths and regulation resources** (special interests, routines, stims, hyperfocus/flow).
- **Identity-language preference:** `[clinician input required: confirm identity-first vs. person-first; use throughout]`
- `[clinician input required: current risk profile — suicidality is elevated in autistic and ADHD adults; record screen result]`
- `[clinician input required: client's own goals and what "better" means to them]`

## Constraints

### Must

- Write goals that target **distress, co-occurring conditions, and environmental fit** — never the neurotype itself. No goal should read "reduce stimming," "improve eye contact," "decrease special-interest time," or "become more neurotypical" as an end in itself.
- Apply the **double empathy problem**: include at least one intervention that adjusts the environment or builds mutual understanding (psychoeducation for partners/employers, communication accommodation), not only client-side change.
- Use **accommodation over normalization**: preserve and leverage stims, routines, interests, and sensory supports as regulation strengths; only modify a behavior if it causes the client harm and the client chooses to.
- Treat **autistic burnout** with a recovery model (reduced demand, rest, sensory recovery, accommodation, gradual re-engagement) and distinguish it from an MDD treatment plan.
- Maintain the **co-occurring-vs-neurotype distinction** for every problem on the plan: state whether each target is a treatable co-occurring condition, an environmental-fit issue, or a neurotype feature (which is not a target).
- Honor the client's **language preference** and write the plan collaboratively, reflecting the client's own goals.
- Use a **golden-thread** structure and include **risk-reassessment hooks**.
- Flag all `[clinician input required: ...]` gaps; do not fabricate scores or history.

### Must Not

- Do not write normalization goals (extinguishing stimming, forcing eye contact, eliminating special interests, "social skills to appear neurotypical") as treatment objectives.
- Do not frame the neurotype as the disorder/target; the neurotype is the client's way of being.
- Do not locate communication difficulty solely in the client (double empathy: it is a two-way mismatch).
- Do not under-treat a real co-occurring condition in the name of "acceptance" — affirming care treats genuine distress vigorously.
- Do not omit the risk screen or risk-reassessment hooks given elevated suicide risk.

## Affirming Reframe Table

| Pathologizing target (avoid) | Affirming target (use) |
|---|---|
| "Reduce stimming" | "Support safe self-regulation; identify stims as coping resources; reduce only self-injurious forms, by client choice" |
| "Improve eye contact / social skills to appear neurotypical" | "Build communication that works for the client; psychoeducate communication partners (double empathy)" |
| "Decrease time on special interests" | "Leverage interests for motivation, regulation, and connection; address only if displacing essential functioning, by client choice" |
| "Increase flexibility / tolerate demands" (PDA) | "Reduce demand load; build autonomy-supportive, low-demand environment; treat underlying anxiety" |
| "Resolve sensory sensitivities" | "Accommodate the sensory environment; build a sensory-regulation toolkit" |
| "Treat the autism/ADHD" | "Treat the distress, the co-occurring conditions, and the environmental misfit" |

## Instructions

1. **Set affirming frame and language.** Confirm identity-language preference (`[clinician input required: ...]`); center the client's own goals and definition of "better." Build the plan collaboratively.

2. **Screen risk and embed hooks.** Record the suicide-risk screen result (elevated in autistic/ADHD adults) and place reassessment hooks at plan creation and each review.

3. **Sort every concern into the co-occurring-vs-neurotype distinction.** For each presenting concern, label it: (a) treatable co-occurring condition, (b) environmental-fit / accommodation issue, or (c) neurotype feature (not a target). Only (a) and (b) become goals.

4. **Apply the double empathy problem.** For relational/communication concerns, add environment- and partner-directed interventions, not only client-change interventions.

5. **Apply accommodation over normalization.** Use the reframe table to convert any pathologizing target into an affirming one; preserve regulation strengths.

6. **Address burnout if present.** Write a recovery-oriented plan (demand reduction, rest, sensory recovery, gradual re-engagement) distinct from a standard depression plan.

7. **Treat genuine co-occurring conditions vigorously**, selecting modalities (route to `psychology_modality_selection_decision_aid.md`) with neurodivergent-affirming adaptations (concrete language, processing time, sensory-aware environment, interest-leveraged engagement).

8. **Write the golden-thread plan** using the output format.

9. Run verification.

## Verification

- [ ] No goal targets the neurotype itself; no normalization goals (no "reduce stimming," "improve eye contact," "eliminate special interests," "become more neurotypical").
- [ ] Every concern sorted into co-occurring / environmental-fit / neurotype-feature; only the first two become goals.
- [ ] Double empathy applied — at least one environment- or partner-directed intervention for relational/communication concerns.
- [ ] Accommodation over normalization applied; stims, routines, interests, and sensory supports preserved/leveraged as strengths.
- [ ] Autistic burnout addressed with a recovery model distinct from a depression plan (when present).
- [ ] Genuine co-occurring conditions treated vigorously with neurodivergent-affirming modality adaptations.
- [ ] Identity-language preference honored; plan co-developed with the client around their own goals.
- [ ] Golden-thread structure complete (problem → target → objective → intervention → measure).
- [ ] Suicide-risk screen recorded (elevated risk noted) and risk-reassessment hooks embedded.
- [ ] All gaps flagged with `[clinician input required: ...]`; no fabricated scores or history.
