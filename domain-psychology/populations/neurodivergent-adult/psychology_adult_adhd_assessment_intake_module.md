# Adult ADHD Assessment Intake Module

## Objective

Produce a structured adult ADHD assessment intake record that:

1. Documents a detailed **executive-function history** (attention regulation, working memory, task initiation, time management, emotional regulation, organization, response inhibition) across life domains.
2. Maps the presentation against **DSM-5-TR ADHD criteria**: the inattentive symptom set, the hyperactive-impulsive symptom set, the adult threshold (≥5 symptoms per domain), cross-setting impairment, and the **age-of-onset criterion** (several symptoms present before age 12), gathered retrospectively.
3. Administers and interprets **adult-validated rating scales** by band: ASRS v1.1 (screener), CAARS (clinician/self), DIVA-5 (diagnostic interview), and the Wender Utah Rating Scale (retrospective childhood) — referenced by name, interpreted structurally, without reproducing item text.
4. Distinguishes ADHD from **mimics and co-occurring conditions** (anxiety, depression, trauma, sleep disorders, substance use, thyroid/medical, autistic overlap "AuDHD").
5. Screens for **elevated risk** (suicide risk and accident/impulsivity risk are elevated in adults with ADHD).
6. Produces a **CPT 90791** intake record and notes when formal neuropsychological testing is a separate referral.

The clinician retains diagnostic authority; this module organizes decision-relevant history and rating-scale interpretation.

## When to Use

- At intake for an adult (18+) self-referring or referred for ADHD assessment, including adults identified late after a child's diagnosis or after escalating executive demands (new job, parenthood, college).
- When an adult in treatment for anxiety/depression has a treatment-resistant picture with a strong lifelong executive-dysfunction thread.
- When structuring a retrospective childhood-onset history and selecting/interpreting rating scales.

## When NOT to Use

- For pediatric ADHD assessment — use a developmentally calibrated child protocol with teacher/parent informants.
- As a substitute for formal neuropsychological/continuous-performance testing where the clinical question requires it (e.g., medicolegal, complex differential) — this module structures intake and refers.
- During acute crisis — stabilize first.

## Inputs / Context Required

- **Demographics:** Age, gender identity/pronouns, primary language, occupation/education.
- **Referral context and the client's question.**
- **Identity-language preference:** `[clinician input required: ask and record whether the client prefers identity-first ("ADHDer") or person-first ("person with ADHD") language; use their preference throughout]`
- **Executive-function history** across domains and across the lifespan (childhood, school, work, home, relationships, driving/finances).
- **Retrospective childhood evidence** (records, report cards, parent/sibling informant if available — collateral strengthens onset documentation but is not always available).
- **Rating scales** completed or to be administered: ASRS, CAARS, DIVA-5, Wender Utah.
- **Co-occurring / mimic history:** anxiety, depression, trauma, sleep, substance use, medical (thyroid, anemia), autistic traits.
- `[clinician input required: current substance use, prior stimulant exposure, cardiac history relevant to later medication discussion]`

## Constraints

### Must

- Anchor the assessment in a concrete **executive-function history with examples**, not a symptom checklist alone — adults narrate impairment through life consequences (lost jobs, late fees, missed deadlines, relationship friction).
- Document the **age-of-onset criterion** (several symptoms before age 12) retrospectively; use the Wender Utah Rating Scale and collateral where available; document absence of collateral as a limitation.
- Count symptoms against the **adult DSM-5-TR threshold (≥5 per domain)** and document **cross-setting impairment** (≥2 settings).
- Interpret ASRS, CAARS, DIVA-5, and Wender Utah **by band/structure**; label ASRS a screener and DIVA-5 a structured diagnostic interview; never reproduce copyrighted item text.
- Run an explicit **mimic/differential pass**: anxiety, depression, trauma, sleep deprivation, substance use, and medical causes can each produce attention complaints; document the reasoning.
- Address **AuDHD overlap** when autistic traits are present (route to the autism module where indicated).
- Screen for **elevated risk** — suicide risk and impulsivity/accident risk are elevated in adult ADHD.
- Honor the client's identity-language preference.
- Flag all `[clinician input required: ...]` gaps; do not fabricate scores, records, or milestones.

### Must Not

- Do not diagnose from a single positive ASRS screen; the screener identifies who warrants full evaluation.
- Do not frame the ADHD neurotype itself as the treatment target; the assessment characterizes the neurotype and targets distress, functional impairment, environmental fit, and co-occurring conditions.
- Do not attribute every attention complaint to ADHD without the mimic/differential pass, nor dismiss ADHD because a comorbidity is present (comorbidity is the rule).
- Do not reproduce copyrighted item text from ASRS, CAARS, DIVA-5, or Wender Utah; reference by name and interpret structurally.
- Do not omit the risk screen.
- Do not present intake findings as formal neuropsychological testing results.

## Rating-Scale Reference (interpret by band/structure; do not reproduce items)

| Instrument | Respondent | Role | Interpretation note |
|---|---|---|---|
| ASRS v1.1 (Adult ADHD Self-Report Scale, WHO) | Self-report | Screener | Part A items flag who warrants full evaluation; a positive screen is not a diagnosis. |
| CAARS (Conners' Adult ADHD Rating Scales) | Self and/or observer | Symptom severity, normed T-scores | Subscales: inattention/memory, hyperactivity/restlessness, impulsivity/lability, self-concept, DSM symptom subscales. |
| DIVA-5 (Diagnostic Interview for ADHD in adults) | Clinician-administered | Structured diagnostic interview | Walks DSM-5 criteria for both domains, childhood and adulthood, with concrete real-life examples; supports onset and cross-setting documentation. |
| Wender Utah Rating Scale (WURS) | Self-report, retrospective | Retrospective childhood symptom estimate | Supports the before-age-12 onset criterion when collateral is limited. |

## Instructions

1. **Set frame and language.** Record the client's question and identity-language preference (`[clinician input required: ...]`); use it throughout. State that intake structures history and rating scales and may lead to referral.

2. **Triage risk.** Conduct a suicide-risk screen (Columbia C-SSRS) and note impulsivity/accident risk before proceeding.

3. **Take a domain-by-domain executive-function history with examples.** Attention regulation, task initiation/procrastination, working memory, time management/time blindness, organization/clutter, response inhibition/impulsivity, and emotional regulation/rejection sensitivity — each grounded in concrete life consequences across school, work, home, finances, and driving.

4. **Map DSM-5-TR criteria.** Count inattentive and hyperactive-impulsive symptoms against the adult threshold (≥5 each), document cross-setting impairment (≥2 settings), and gather retrospective evidence of several symptoms before age 12.

5. **Administer and band rating scales.** ASRS (screen), CAARS (severity T-scores), DIVA-5 (structured criteria walk), WURS (childhood). Record results by band and label each scale's role.

6. **Run the mimic/differential pass.** For each driver of attention complaints — anxiety, depression, trauma, sleep, substance use, medical — state whether it explains, contributes to, or is comorbid with the picture.

7. **Address AuDHD overlap.** If autistic traits are present, document and route to the autism module where indicated.

8. **Write the intake note** using the output format below.

9. Run verification.

## Verification

- [ ] Identity-language preference elicited and used throughout.
- [ ] Suicide-risk screen conducted first; impulsivity/accident risk noted; elevated risk acknowledged.
- [ ] Executive-function history documented domain-by-domain with concrete life examples.
- [ ] DSM-5-TR criteria mapped: symptom counts vs. adult ≥5 threshold, cross-setting impairment, before-age-12 onset.
- [ ] Collateral availability or its absence documented as a limitation for the onset criterion.
- [ ] ASRS (screener), CAARS, DIVA-5, and WURS interpreted by band/role; no copyrighted items reproduced.
- [ ] Mimic/differential pass run across anxiety, depression, trauma, sleep, substance use, and medical causes.
- [ ] AuDHD overlap addressed; routed to autism module where indicated.
- [ ] ADHD neurotype not framed as the treatment target; formulation targets impairment, fit, and comorbidity.
- [ ] Formal-testing referral and medication-evaluation referral decisions documented; no fabricated test results.
- [ ] Billing note distinguishes 90791 intake from separate formal testing referral.
- [ ] All gaps flagged with `[clinician input required: ...]`; no fabricated scores, records, or milestones.
