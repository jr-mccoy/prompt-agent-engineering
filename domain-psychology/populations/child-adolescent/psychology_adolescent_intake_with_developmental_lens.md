# Adolescent Intake with Developmental Lens

## Objective

Produce a developmentally calibrated biopsychosocial intake for an adolescent (ages 12–17) that:

1. Centers the adolescent developmental tasks — identity, autonomy, peer belonging, intimacy, and emerging future orientation — alongside symptom assessment.
2. Manages the confidentiality structure that defines adolescent care: caregiver consent for treatment, adolescent assent, explicit limits-of-confidentiality explanation, and applicable state minor-consent statutes.
3. Assesses the four functional arenas — self/identity, peers, family, school — plus risk behaviors (substance, sexual, online, self-harm, suicidality).
4. Applies adolescent-validated screening instruments (PHQ-A, GAD-7, SCARED, CRAFFT-2, Columbia C-SSRS – Adolescent, YSR).
5. Integrates caregiver collateral while protecting the adolescent's developmentally appropriate privacy.
6. Produces a structured intake note meeting CPT 90791/90792 documentation requirements.

## When to Use

- At initial intake for any adolescent ages 12–17 entering outpatient mental health care.
- When the adolescent is the primary informant and the clinician must structure both a confidential adolescent interview and caregiver collateral.
- For re-intake of an adolescent whose developmental stage or risk profile has shifted significantly.

## When NOT to Use

- For children under 12: use `psychology_pediatric_intake_with_caregiver.md`.
- For adults 18+: use `psychology_intake_assessment_note.md`.
- For forensic, custody-evaluation, or court-ordered evaluation contexts governed by separate protocols.
- For acute crisis triage only — if presenting in active suicidal crisis, complete risk assessment and safety planning first (`psychology_pediatric_safety_planning_with_caregiver.md`).

## Inputs / Context Required

- **Demographics:** Initials/MRN, DOB, age, grade, school, gender identity, pronouns, sexual orientation (elicited respectfully, not assumed), race/ethnicity as the teen identifies, primary language.
- **Caregiver/guardian:** Name(s), relationship, legal guardianship, custody arrangement, consent status.
- **Referral:** Source, reason, accompanying documentation.
- **Prior records:** Prior evaluations, IEP/504, prior treatment, prior hospitalizations.
- **Consent/assent:** Caregiver consent obtained; adolescent assent obtained; state minor-consent statutes reviewed.
- **Interview segments:** Confidential adolescent interview and caregiver collateral conducted (or noted as deferred).
- `[clinician input required: any acute safety findings (SI/HI, self-harm, abuse) that modify the intake flow]`

## Constraints

### Must

- Explain confidentiality and its limits to the adolescent in age-appropriate language before the confidential interview; document what will and will not be shared with caregivers.
- Document legal guardian consent and custody; review and document applicable state minor-consent rights (mental health access age, substance use, reproductive/sexual health).
- Center identity, peer, family, and school as required developmental domains.
- Use adolescent-validated instruments only; interpret by band/structure without reproducing item text.
- Administer Columbia C-SSRS – Adolescent and document SI/HI explicitly (present or absent), with a risk-reassessment hook.
- Conduct a mandated-reporter screen for abuse/neglect/DV and document the determination even when negative.
- Document informant discrepancies between adolescent and caregiver with clinical weighting.
- Flag gaps with `[clinician input required: ...]`; do not fabricate scores, history, or records.

### Must Not

- Do not disclose the adolescent's confidential content (substance use, sexual behavior, identity, non-acute self-harm) to caregivers without clinical and legal analysis; document the analysis.
- Do not pathologize normative adolescent development (identity exploration, autonomy-seeking, peer orientation, experimentation) without anchoring to impairment or risk.
- Do not assume gender or sexual orientation; elicit with open, non-leading questions.
- Do not omit the mandated-reporter screen or the Columbia C-SSRS.
- Do not fabricate instrument scores, prior treatment history, or school records.

## Developmental Lens Reference

| Domain | Developmental Task | Normative Range | Clinically Significant Flag |
|--------|--------------------|-----------------|------------------------------|
| Identity | Coherent self-concept; values; gender/sexual identity exploration | Exploration, role experimentation, idealism | Identity diffusion with distress; rigid foreclosure; identity-based victimization |
| Peers | Belonging, intimacy, status navigation | Strong peer orientation, conflict, shifting groups | Isolation; bullying (victim/perpetrator); deviant peer affiliation; total withdrawal |
| Autonomy/Family | Renegotiating dependence; boundary testing | Conflict over autonomy, privacy-seeking | Enmeshment or estrangement; parentification; unsafe home dynamics |
| School/Future | Competence, planning, emerging future orientation | Variable motivation, stress, perfectionism | Functional decline; refusal; loss of any future orientation |

## Instructions

1. **Pre-intake setup.** Confirm guardian identity, custody, consent, and applicable state minor-consent statutes. Review records.
2. **Joint opening.** Briefly meet with adolescent and caregiver together; explain the intake structure and confidentiality framework to both.
3. **Confidential adolescent interview.** After explaining limits of confidentiality, interview the adolescent alone. Cover chief complaint in their words; mood/anxiety; identity (incl. gender/sexual identity, non-leading); peer relationships and belonging; family relationships and autonomy; school and future orientation; substance use (CRAFFT-2); sexual behavior and online safety (age-appropriate); trauma screen; self-harm and suicidality (Columbia C-SSRS – Adolescent).
4. **Caregiver collateral.** Gather developmental history, symptom trajectory, family history, observed functioning, and safety concerns — while protecting adolescent privacy.
5. **Administer screeners.** PHQ-A, GAD-7, SCARED, CRAFFT-2, Columbia C-SSRS – Adolescent, YSR as indicated.
6. **Mandated-reporter screen.** Document abuse/neglect/DV screen and determination.
7. **Multi-informant integration.** Reconcile adolescent and caregiver reports; document divergence and weighting.
8. **Formulate and write the note** using the output format below.
9. **Run verification.**

## Verification

- [ ] Consent, assent, confidentiality limits, and state minor-consent rights documented.
- [ ] Custody/guardianship confirmed.
- [ ] Identity, peer, family/autonomy, and school/future domains all assessed with normative-vs-clinical framing.
- [ ] Gender/sexual orientation elicited, not assumed.
- [ ] Risk behaviors (substance via CRAFFT-2, sexual, online, self-harm) assessed.
- [ ] Columbia C-SSRS – Adolescent administered; SI/HI documented present-or-absent with risk level and reassessment hook.
- [ ] Mandated-reporter screen and determination documented even when negative.
- [ ] Adolescent-validated instruments used; no item text reproduced.
- [ ] Multi-informant integration documents corroboration and divergence with weighting.
- [ ] Diagnostic impressions include ICD-10-CM; formulation developmentally framed.
- [ ] CPT matches service and duration.
- [ ] Gaps flagged with `[clinician input required: ...]`; nothing fabricated.
