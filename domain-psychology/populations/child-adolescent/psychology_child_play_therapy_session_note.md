# Child Play Therapy Session Note

## Objective

Produce a complete, developmentally framed progress note for an individual play therapy session with a child (ages 3–11) that:

1. Documents the modality and approach used (non-directive/child-centered, directive, or integrative) and the rationale for the chosen stance this session.
2. Records observed play themes, the toys/media engaged, and the relational stance the child took toward the clinician.
3. Translates play content into developmentally appropriate clinical inference without over-interpreting.
4. Links the session's play content and clinician interventions back to specific treatment plan goals (golden thread).
5. Documents risk, caregiver contact, and plan for the next session.
6. Meets CPT 90837 / 90834 documentation requirements for an individual psychotherapy session.

## When to Use

- For any individual play therapy session with a child roughly ages 3–11 where play is the primary therapeutic medium.
- When the treatment plan specifies a play-based modality (child-centered play therapy, Theraplay-informed, CBT-informed directive play, sand tray, expressive arts).
- For routine session documentation that must demonstrate medical necessity and goal linkage to payers.

## When NOT to Use

- For talk-based therapy with older children and adolescents who no longer use play as the primary medium: use `psychology_soap_progress_note.md` or an adolescent-specific note.
- For an intake/diagnostic evaluation: use `psychology_pediatric_intake_with_caregiver.md` (CPT 90791/90792).
- For family or dyadic sessions where the caregiver is an active participant the whole session (e.g., PCIT, Theraplay dyadic) — those have dyadic CPT and documentation conventions; adapt accordingly.

## Inputs / Context Required

- **Client:** Initials/MRN, DOB, age (years + months), session date and times.
- **Treatment plan goals:** The active goals/objectives this session is meant to advance.
- **Play therapy approach:** Non-directive (CCPT), directive, or integrative; theoretical orientation if relevant.
- **Session content:** Toys/media offered and chosen; play themes observed; child's verbalizations; relational stance toward clinician; transitions and regulation.
- **Caregiver context:** Pre/post-session caregiver check-in content, if any; any new caregiver-reported information.
- **Risk context:** Any disclosures or play content raising safety, abuse, or self-harm concerns.
- `[clinician input required: any new caregiver-reported behavioral change at home or school since last session]`

## Constraints

### Must

- Frame all observations against developmental norms for the child's age: aggressive, regressive, and fantasy play are developmentally common and must not be pathologized without functional context.
- Document the play therapy approach used and the rationale for directive vs. non-directive stance this session.
- Link at least one observed play theme or clinician intervention to a specific treatment plan goal.
- Screen for and document any abuse, neglect, or safety content that emerges in play; document mandated-reporter analysis if triggered.
- Distinguish observation (what occurred) from inference (what it may mean clinically); label tentative interpretations as such.
- Document caregiver contact (pre/post check-in) and any information shared, within confidentiality boundaries appropriate to the child's age.
- Flag all `[clinician input required: ...]` gaps; do not fabricate play content, child verbalizations, or caregiver reports.

### Must Not

- Do not over-interpret single play instances as definitive evidence of trauma, abuse, or diagnosis; reckless interpretation creates clinical and forensic risk.
- Do not pathologize developmentally expected play (e.g., aggressive figure play, messy/regressive play, repetitive play) without functional impairment context.
- Do not omit the safety/abuse screen when play content raises concern.
- Do not fabricate quotes, themes, or caregiver statements.
- Do not document interpretive leaps as fact in a record that may be subpoenaed.

## Developmental Play Framing Guide

| Age Band | Typical Play Mode | Developmentally Expected | Watch-For (functional concern) |
|----------|-------------------|--------------------------|--------------------------------|
| 3–5 (Preschool) | Symbolic/pretend, parallel, sensory | Magical thinking, animism, aggressive figure play, repetition, messy play | Pervasive freeze/avoidance, sexualized play beyond age, inability to engage any play |
| 6–8 (Early School-Age) | Thematic pretend, rule emergence, mastery play | Good-vs-bad themes, rescue/danger themes, testing limits | Rigid trauma re-enactment without resolution, self-directed aggression in play |
| 9–11 (Late Childhood) | Games-with-rules, sand tray, narrative/expressive arts | Competence and fairness themes, more verbal processing | Withdrawal from play into avoidance, dissociative gaps, hopelessness themes |

## Instructions

1. **Confirm goal context.** Review the active treatment plan goals this session advances. Note the play therapy approach planned.
2. **Document the approach and stance.** Record whether the session was non-directive, directive, or integrative, and why that stance was chosen today.
3. **Record play content as observation.** Document toys/media chosen, play themes, verbalizations (quote where notable), relational stance, regulation and transitions — as factual observation.
4. **Frame developmentally.** Apply the framing guide: note what is developmentally expected vs. what raises functional concern.
5. **Make calibrated inference.** Offer tentative clinical inference linked to formulation; label as hypothesis, not fact.
6. **Screen safety.** Screen play content and any disclosures for abuse/neglect/self-harm; document mandated-reporter analysis if triggered.
7. **Link to goals (golden thread).** Connect at least one theme or intervention to a specific treatment goal and document progress.
8. **Document caregiver contact and plan.** Record pre/post caregiver check-in and the plan for the next session.
9. **Write the note** using the output format below.
10. **Run verification.**

## Verification

- [ ] Play therapy approach and stance rationale documented.
- [ ] Developmental age band identified and framing guide applied (expected vs. concerning).
- [ ] Play content documented as observation separate from labeled inference.
- [ ] Inference labeled as hypothesis with confidence level and theme continuity.
- [ ] Safety/mandated-reporter screen completed even when no concern is present.
- [ ] At least one play theme or intervention linked to a specific treatment goal (golden thread).
- [ ] Caregiver contact documented within the child's confidentiality boundaries.
- [ ] CPT code matches documented session duration (90837 ≥53 min; 90834 38–52 min).
- [ ] No developmentally expected play pathologized without functional context.
- [ ] No fabricated play content, quotes, or caregiver reports.
- [ ] Gaps flagged with `[clinician input required: ...]`.
