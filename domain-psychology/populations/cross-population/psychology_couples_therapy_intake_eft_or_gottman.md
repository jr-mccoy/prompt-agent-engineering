# Couples Therapy Intake — EFT or Gottman Frame

## Objective

Produce a complete couples (conjoint) intake record for a relational unit presenting for couples therapy that:

1. Screens **each partner separately and privately** for intimate partner violence (IPV), coercive control, and safety **before** any conjoint treatment is planned, and applies the appropriate contraindication carve-out when active violence is present.
2. Captures both partners' presenting concerns, relationship history, and goals, while resisting premature alignment with either partner's narrative.
3. Maps the couple's **negative interaction cycle** (EFT) and/or assesses the **Sound Relationship House** and the **Four Horsemen** (Gottman Method).
4. Selects and justifies an EFT vs. Gottman treatment frame based on presentation features, evidence, and couple factors.
5. Documents each partner's individual mental-health and risk profile to the extent it affects conjoint work.
6. Produces a structured intake note meeting CPT 90791 documentation requirements for the identified treatment unit.

## When to Use

- At initial intake for a couple presenting for couples/marital/relationship therapy.
- When a clinician needs a defensible structure for separating IPV screening from conjoint assessment.
- When choosing between an EFT and a Gottman treatment frame for a specific couple.
- For re-intake when a couple returns after a gap, a separation, or a major relational rupture (affair, betrayal, near-divorce).

## When NOT to Use

- For individual intake of one partner who is not pursuing conjoint work: use `psychology_intake_assessment_note.md`.
- For whole-family systemic intake (parents + children, multigenerational): use `psychology_family_therapy_intake_systemic.md`.
- For forensic, custody-evaluation, or court-ordered "reconciliation" contexts: different protocols govern informant contact and evidence handling.
- When IPV screening reveals active, severe, or escalating violence — do **not** proceed to conjoint treatment planning; pivot to the safety/contraindication pathway and individual referrals.

## Inputs / Context Required

- **Both partners' identifying information:** names/initials, ages, gender identities and pronouns, relationship configuration (married, partnered, cohabiting, polyamorous/CNM — document structure), relationship length, children (ages, custody arrangement if relevant).
- **Presenting concern from each partner** (elicited individually where possible, not only as a couple's consensus narrative).
- **Relationship history:** how they met, relationship trajectory, prior separations, prior couples therapy and response.
- **Each partner's individual history relevant to conjoint work:** psychiatric history, substance use, trauma history, attachment history, current risk (SI/HI/self-harm).
- **Affair / betrayal status if applicable:** disclosure status, ongoing vs. ended, secrecy.
- **Consent:** documented understanding of confidentiality structure (no-secrets vs. limited-secrets policy) and informed consent to conjoint treatment by both partners.
- `[clinician input required: clinician's no-secrets vs. accommodating-secrets policy, stated explicitly to both partners before individual screening begins]`

## Constraints

### Must

- Conduct **IPV and safety screening with each partner alone**, out of earshot of the other partner, before planning any conjoint treatment. Document that separate screening occurred and how privacy was secured (including for telehealth: confirm each partner is alone and unmonitored).
- State the confidentiality / secrets policy to both partners **before** individual segments begin, and document which policy is in force.
- Apply the **contraindication carve-out**: if screening reveals active/severe IPV, coercive control, fear of the partner, or escalation, do not recommend conjoint couples therapy; document the safety determination and route to individual safety planning, IPV-specialized services, and separate individual treatment.
- Map the couple's interaction pattern explicitly using at least one named relational frame (EFT negative cycle and/or Gottman Sound Relationship House + Four Horsemen).
- Document each partner's individual risk screen (SI, HI, self-harm) — relational distress is a known risk amplifier.
- Document affair/betrayal status and any secret disclosed in an individual segment, and handle it per the stated secrets policy.
- Justify the selected EFT vs. Gottman frame against the couple's presentation, not modality preference alone.
- Flag all `[clinician input required: ...]` gaps; do not fabricate relationship history, screening results, or instrument scores.

### Must Not

- Do not conduct IPV screening conjointly (with both partners in the room) — this is unsafe and can elicit minimization or retaliation.
- Do not recommend conjoint behavioral exercises (e.g., communication homework, soft-startup practice) when active IPV or coercive control is present.
- Do not align the formulation with one partner's narrative; document both partners' perspectives and the clinician's systemic, non-blaming framing.
- Do not disclose a secret obtained in an individual segment in a way that violates the stated secrets policy; if a no-secrets policy is in force, document how the policy was explained and how secrets will be handled.
- Do not fabricate instrument scores (Locke-Wallace, DAS, Gottman assessment), affair timelines, or risk history.
- Do not diagnose the relationship as a billable DSM disorder; the identified clinical target may be a Z-code relational problem (e.g., Z63.0) plus each partner's individual diagnoses where applicable.

## EFT vs. Gottman Frame Selection

| Feature | Lean EFT (Johnson) | Lean Gottman Method |
|---------|--------------------|---------------------|
| Core lever | Attachment bonds; de-escalating the negative cycle; restructuring emotional engagement | Friendship/intimacy system + conflict-management skills + shared meaning (Sound Relationship House) |
| Presentation fit | Pursue–withdraw / criticize–defend cycles driven by unmet attachment needs; attachment injuries | Skill deficits in conflict, gridlocked perpetual problems, eroded fondness/admiration, flooding |
| Assessment anchor | The negative cycle map; attachment injuries; primary vs. secondary emotion | Sound Relationship House audit; Four Horsemen frequency; Gottman couples assessment / DAS / Locke-Wallace |
| Stage logic | Stage 1 de-escalation → Stage 2 restructuring bonds (withdrawer re-engagement, blamer softening) → Stage 3 consolidation (nine-step model) | Assessment → intervention across the floors of the house (build love maps, manage conflict, create shared meaning) |
| Strong fit when | Emotional disconnection, attachment insecurity, recurring reactive cycle | Concrete conflict/communication breakdown, contempt present, need for structured skills |
| Caution | Requires capacity to access vulnerable emotion; complex trauma may need pacing | Skills frame can feel mechanical if attachment wound is the true driver |

Both are evidence-supported; many clinicians integrate. Document the selected lead frame and any integration.

## Instructions

1. **Pre-intake setup.** Review intake paperwork from both partners. Confirm the confidentiality/secrets policy you will use. Plan the session structure: brief joint opening → individual segments (each alone) → joint close. For telehealth, plan how you will confirm each partner is alone.

2. **Joint opening.** Orient both partners to the structure and to confidentiality, and explicitly state the secrets policy. Obtain informed consent to conjoint treatment from both.

3. **Individual segment — Partner A (alone).** Conduct private IPV/safety screening (fear of partner, physical violence, threats, coercive control, weapons, escalation, sexual coercion), individual psychiatric/substance/trauma/attachment history, individual risk (SI/HI/self-harm), and any secret or affair disclosure. Document.

4. **Individual segment — Partner B (alone).** Repeat the same private screening and history for Partner B. Document.

5. **Safety determination.** Integrate both individual screens. Determine whether conjoint couples therapy is **appropriate**, **contraindicated**, or **conditional (pending safety steps)**. If contraindicated, stop conjoint planning and route to the safety pathway.

6. **Cycle / relationship-system mapping (if conjoint work is appropriate).** Map the EFT negative cycle and/or complete the Gottman Sound Relationship House audit and Four Horsemen assessment. Note attachment injuries and/or gridlocked perpetual problems.

7. **Frame selection.** Select EFT, Gottman, or an integrated frame and justify against the presentation using the table above.

8. **Write the Couples Intake Note** using the output format below.

9. **Run verification.**

## Verification

- [ ] IPV/safety screen conducted with **each partner alone**; separate screening and privacy method documented (including telehealth confirmation).
- [ ] Confidentiality/secrets policy stated to both partners before individual segments and recorded.
- [ ] Safety determination explicitly classifies conjoint therapy as Appropriate / Contraindicated / Conditional.
- [ ] Contraindication carve-out applied if active/severe IPV, coercive control, or fear present — no conjoint behavioral homework assigned.
- [ ] Both partners' presenting concerns documented separately; formulation is systemic and non-blaming.
- [ ] Each partner's individual risk screen (SI/HI/self-harm) documented.
- [ ] Affair/betrayal status documented and handled per stated secrets policy.
- [ ] At least one named relational frame mapped (EFT negative cycle and/or Gottman Sound Relationship House + Four Horsemen).
- [ ] EFT vs. Gottman frame selection justified against presentation features.
- [ ] Problem/diagnostic coding uses appropriate Z-code and any individual diagnoses; billing note included.
