# Grief Assessment — Prolonged Grief Disorder vs. Normal Grief

## Objective

Produce a documented differential assessment that distinguishes **Prolonged Grief Disorder (PGD)** from **normal/acute (uncomplicated) grief** and from **Major Depressive Disorder (MDD)**, by:

1. Establishing the **time elapsed since the death** and applying the correct duration threshold (≥12 months for adults; ≥6 months for children/adolescents) before PGD can be diagnosed.
2. Assessing the **DSM-5-TR PGD core feature** (intense yearning/longing for, or preoccupation with, the deceased) plus the required number of accompanying symptoms, and cross-walking to **ICD-11 PGD**.
3. Differentiating PGD from **normal grief** (which is painful but adaptive and non-disordered) and from **MDD** (pervasive low mood/anhedonia not tethered to the loss).
4. Screening **suicide risk**, which is elevated in bereavement and in PGD.
5. Referencing named grief instruments (Brief Grief Questionnaire, PG-13/PG-13-R, Inventory of Complicated Grief [ICG]) without reproducing item text.
6. Producing a diagnostic determination and treatment-direction recommendation.

## When to Use

- When a bereaved client presents with persistent, impairing grief and the clinician must determine whether it meets PGD criteria or represents normal grief.
- When grief and depressive symptoms co-occur and the differential between PGD and MDD is unclear.
- When a client requests "grief therapy" and the clinician needs to calibrate the diagnosis and modality.
- For periodic reassessment when grief has not remitted over expected time.

## When NOT to Use

- Within the duration window (before 12 months in adults / 6 months in children) when the question is acute-grief support, not PGD diagnosis — document that PGD cannot yet be diagnosed and provide normal-grief support.
- For a primary trauma presentation where PTSD is the leading question (use a trauma-focused assessment), though comorbid PTSD should be screened.
- For routine bereavement support that does not require a diagnostic determination.

## Inputs / Context Required

- **The loss:** Who died, relationship to the deceased, date of death, circumstances (anticipated/sudden/violent/by suicide/overdose), and any complicating features.
- **Time since death:** Exact elapsed time relative to the applicable threshold.
- **Grief presentation:** Yearning/preoccupation; identity disruption; disbelief; avoidance of reminders; intense emotional pain; difficulty re-engaging; emotional numbness; meaninglessness; intense loneliness.
- **Functional impairment:** Impact on work, relationships, daily functioning.
- **Depression and trauma context:** Mood, anhedonia, guilt, worthlessness; trauma symptoms if death was traumatic.
- **Risk context:** SI/HI, self-harm, hopelessness; whether the death was by suicide (a known risk amplifier).
- **Culture:** Cultural and religious mourning norms that calibrate what is expectable.
- `[clinician input required: current suicide-risk status and any active plan/intent, given elevated bereavement risk]`

## Constraints

### Must

- Establish elapsed time since the death and apply the duration threshold **before** assigning a PGD diagnosis: ≥12 months for adults, ≥6 months for children and adolescents. If the threshold is not met, state that PGD cannot be diagnosed yet and frame as acute grief.
- Assess the DSM-5-TR PGD **core symptom** (intense yearning/longing OR preoccupation with the deceased, present most days to a clinically significant degree and persistent for at least the last month) and the required **accompanying symptoms** (three or more of the listed set), plus clinically significant distress/impairment, and that the disturbance exceeds expected social/cultural/religious norms.
- Cross-walk to **ICD-11 PGD** and note convergence/divergence.
- Explicitly **differentiate from normal grief** (painful, oscillating, adaptive, no disorder) and from **MDD** (pervasive low mood/anhedonia, worthlessness, guilt not specific to the loss; grief is loss-focused, depression is self-focused).
- Conduct and document a **suicide-risk screen**; note that bereavement — and especially death by suicide — elevates risk.
- Reference named instruments (Brief Grief Questionnaire, PG-13 / PG-13-R, ICG) for severity/screening; do not reproduce copyrighted item text.
- Produce a clear determination: **Normal/Acute Grief / PGD (criteria met) / PGD threshold not yet reachable (within window) / MDD / Comorbid** with rationale.
- Flag all `[clinician input required: ...]` gaps; do not fabricate dates, scores, or symptoms.

### Must Not

- Do not diagnose PGD before the duration threshold is met; within the window, support acute grief and reassess.
- Do not pathologize normal grief; intense pain, longing, and oscillation are expectable and not, by themselves, a disorder.
- Do not collapse PGD and MDD; they can co-occur but are distinct and require distinct treatment direction.
- Do not ignore culture; what is "prolonged" or "excessive" must be judged against the person's cultural/religious mourning context.
- Do not skip the suicide-risk screen; document it even when negative.
- Do not fabricate the date of death, instrument scores, or symptom presence.

## Normal Grief vs. Prolonged Grief Disorder vs. MDD

| Feature | Normal / Acute Grief | Prolonged Grief Disorder (PGD) | Major Depressive Disorder (MDD) |
|---------|----------------------|--------------------------------|----------------------------------|
| Time course | Pain that softens and oscillates over months | Core grief persists ≥12 mo (adult) / ≥6 mo (child), most days, ≥1 month | Independent of any loss; 2-week episode |
| Predominant affect | Yearning/sadness tied to the deceased, with waves and respite | Persistent intense yearning/preoccupation, identity disruption, disbelief | Pervasive low mood/anhedonia across domains |
| Self-view | Self-esteem typically intact | Loss-related; "part of me died" — identity rupture | Worthlessness, global guilt, self-criticism |
| Positive emotion | Capacity for joy/connection returns in waves | Inability to re-engage; meaninglessness; emotional numbness | Anhedonia pervasive |
| Course toward function | Gradual re-engagement with life | Stalled; marked impairment past the threshold | Impairment across functioning |
| Suicidality | Passive wishes to join the deceased may occur; assess | Elevated risk; assess actively | Elevated risk; assess actively |
| Treatment direction | Normalize, support, monitor | Complicated Grief Therapy (Shear) / PGD-targeted CBT | Evidence-based depression treatment (CBT/IPT/meds) |

## Instructions

1. **Establish the loss and elapsed time.** Document who died, the relationship, circumstances, date of death, and elapsed time relative to the applicable threshold (12 mo adult / 6 mo child).

2. **Apply the threshold gate.** If the threshold is not met, state PGD cannot be diagnosed yet, frame as acute grief, support, and schedule reassessment. If met, proceed to criteria.

3. **Assess the PGD core symptom** (intense yearning/longing OR preoccupation), most days, clinically significant, persisting at least the last month.

4. **Count accompanying symptoms** (identity disruption, disbelief, avoidance of reminders, intense emotional pain, difficulty reintegrating, emotional numbness, life-is-meaningless, intense loneliness): require three or more.

5. **Confirm distress/impairment and the cultural-norm exceedance** clause.

6. **Cross-walk to ICD-11 PGD** and note agreement.

7. **Differentiate from MDD and normal grief** using the table; document the distinguishing features observed.

8. **Administer/score a named instrument** (Brief Grief Questionnaire / PG-13-R / ICG) and interpret.

9. **Screen suicide risk** (passive wish to join the deceased vs. active ideation/plan/intent), noting amplifiers (death by suicide/overdose, social isolation).

10. **Render the determination and treatment direction.**

11. **Run verification.**

## Verification

- [ ] Date of death and elapsed time documented; correct duration threshold applied (12 mo adult / 6 mo child) BEFORE any PGD diagnosis.
- [ ] If within the window, PGD marked not-yet-diagnosable and acute-grief framing used.
- [ ] DSM-5-TR PGD core symptom assessed; accompanying-symptom count documented (≥3 required); distress/impairment and cultural-norm-exceedance clauses addressed.
- [ ] ICD-11 PGD cross-walk completed.
- [ ] Differentiation from normal grief and from MDD documented with observed distinguishing features (grief-focused vs. self-focused).
- [ ] At least one named grief instrument referenced/scored or deferral noted; no copyrighted item text reproduced.
- [ ] Suicide-risk screen completed and documented (even if negative); amplifiers (death by suicide/overdose) noted.
- [ ] Clear determination with appropriate coding and rationale.
- [ ] Treatment direction matches the determination (CGT/PGD-CBT vs. supportive monitoring vs. depression treatment).
