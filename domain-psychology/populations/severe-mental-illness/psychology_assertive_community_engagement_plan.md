# Assertive Community Treatment (ACT) Engagement & Outreach Plan

## Objective

Produce a fidelity-aligned ACT-team engagement and outreach plan for an adult with serious mental illness who is disengaged or at risk of disengaging from care, that:

1. Assesses the client's **engagement stage** and the drivers of disengagement (mistrust, symptom-driven avoidance, prior negative care experiences, practical barriers, ambivalence).
2. Designs **assertive, in-vivo, in-community outreach** consistent with the ACT fidelity model — the team goes to the client, not the reverse.
3. Operationalizes ACT structural principles: **shared/team caseload, low staff-to-client ratio, multidisciplinary team, high contact frequency, 24/7 crisis availability, time-unlimited service**.
4. Assigns **specific team roles** (team leader, psychiatric prescriber, nurse, substance-use specialist, vocational/IPS specialist, peer specialist, housing specialist) to engagement tasks.
5. Builds an **outreach sequence** with graded, low-demand contacts that prioritize relationship before clinical demand.
6. Embeds **risk and disengagement monitoring** with re-engagement triggers and a crisis/escalation pathway.

## When to Use

- For a client newly enrolled in (or being referred to) an ACT or FACT team where engagement must be built from a low base.
- When an existing client is missing contacts, declining visits, or showing early disengagement.
- When repeated hospitalizations or crisis contacts signal that office-based care is not reaching the client.
- To structure a team huddle around a shared-caseload engagement strategy for a specific client.

## When NOT to Use

- For routine office-based outpatient therapy with an engaged client (no ACT-level intensity needed).
- For an acute emergency requiring immediate safety disposition — manage the crisis first (`psychology_civil_commitment_narrative.md`), then plan engagement.
- As the medication-adherence intervention itself — pair with `psychology_smi_med_adherence_motivational_plan.md`.

## Inputs / Context Required

- **Client status:** diagnosis, current symptoms, insight, recent hospitalizations/crisis contacts, current engagement pattern (last contact, missed contacts).
- **Disengagement drivers:** known reasons for avoidance — mistrust, paranoia toward providers, prior coercion/involuntary care, stigma, substance use, practical barriers (transport, phone, housing instability).
- **Living situation and reachability:** where the client can be found; phone/address stability; natural supports who can facilitate contact.
- **Team composition:** which ACT disciplines are available (prescriber, nurse, SUD specialist, IPS/vocational, peer specialist, housing specialist, team leader).
- **Risk profile:** suicide/self-harm, harm-to-others, grave disability, substance-related risk.
- **Legal/coordination context:** guardianship, AOT/civil commitment status, probation/parole, releases of information on file.
- `[clinician input required: current ACT fidelity context — caseload ratio, team contact-frequency capacity]`

## Constraints

### Must

- Identify the **engagement stage** and the specific **disengagement drivers** before prescribing outreach intensity.
- Design **in-vivo, in-community outreach** (home, shelter, street, community settings) rather than requiring the client to come to the clinic.
- Honor ACT fidelity: **shared team caseload** (multiple team members know and can serve the client), **low staff-to-client ratio**, **multidisciplinary roles**, **high contact frequency**, **24/7 crisis access**, **time-unlimited** service.
- Sequence outreach from **low-demand, relationship-first** contacts toward clinical tasks; do not lead with adherence demands when trust is absent.
- Assign **named roles** to outreach and engagement tasks so the shared caseload is operational, not abstract.
- Build **disengagement triggers** (e.g., N missed contacts) tied to a defined assertive re-engagement response.
- Include a **risk and crisis/escalation pathway**, including how the team responds to non-contact in a high-risk client.
- Document with collateral/care-coordination conventions; flag `[clinician input required: ...]` gaps; do not fabricate contact history or releases.
- Include a **team-leader / supervisor co-sign line** for high-acuity engagement plans.

### Must Not

- Do not default to passive "client may call to reschedule" responses for a disengaged high-risk client — ACT is assertive by design.
- Do not assign all engagement to a single worker; that violates the shared-caseload principle and creates a single point of failure.
- Do not use coercion framed as engagement; outreach is persistent and assertive, not coercive (distinguish from AOT/commitment, which is a separate legal pathway).
- Do not specify psychiatric medications or doses — route to the team prescriber.
- Do not ignore practical barriers (transport, phone, ID, benefits) that are often the real obstacle to engagement.

## ACT Fidelity Engagement Overlay

| Fidelity element | Engagement implication | Plan must specify |
|------------------|------------------------|-------------------|
| Shared team caseload | Multiple staff can engage the client | ≥2 named team members per client |
| Low staff-to-client ratio | Capacity for high-frequency contact | Target contact frequency |
| Multidisciplinary team | Match outreach to client need | Role assignments (prescriber, nurse, SUD, IPS, peer, housing) |
| In-vivo service | Meet the client where they are | Outreach locations + safety plan for staff |
| 24/7 availability | Crisis access builds trust | After-hours/crisis contact path |
| Time-unlimited | No premature discharge for non-engagement | Re-engagement (not discharge) on missed contacts |
| Assertive (not coercive) | Persistent, respectful outreach | Escalating contact ladder, distinct from legal coercion |

## Instructions

1. **Assess engagement stage and drivers.** Determine where the client sits (no contact / minimal contact / ambivalent / building alliance / engaged) and name the specific drivers of disengagement.
2. **Map reachability and natural supports.** Identify where and how the client can be reached and which supports can facilitate contact.
3. **Set fidelity parameters.** Define target contact frequency, the shared-caseload members assigned, and the in-vivo locations.
4. **Build the outreach sequence.** Start with low-demand, relationship-first contacts (practical help, peer connection, presence) and grade up to clinical tasks as trust builds.
5. **Assign multidisciplinary roles.** Match prescriber, nurse, SUD specialist, IPS/vocational, peer specialist, housing specialist, and team leader to specific engagement tasks.
6. **Define disengagement triggers and re-engagement response.** Specify missed-contact thresholds and the assertive (not discharging) response.
7. **Integrate risk and crisis pathway.** Define how the team monitors risk and responds to non-contact in a high-risk client, including 24/7 access and crisis/commitment linkage.
8. **Assemble the plan** using the output format and route to the team huddle.
9. **Run verification.**

## Verification

- [ ] Engagement stage and specific disengagement drivers identified before outreach intensity set.
- [ ] Outreach is in-vivo and community-based; clinic-only attendance not required.
- [ ] ACT fidelity honored: ≥2 shared-caseload members named, target contact frequency set, multidisciplinary roles assigned, 24/7 access defined, service time-unlimited.
- [ ] Outreach sequence is relationship-first → clinical; adherence demands not led with when alliance absent.
- [ ] Named role assignments make the shared caseload operational.
- [ ] Disengagement triggers tied to an assertive re-engagement (not discharge) response.
- [ ] Outreach is assertive but not coercive; legal coercion (AOT/commitment) kept distinct.
- [ ] Risk and crisis/escalation pathway addresses non-contact in a high-risk client, including 24/7 access.
- [ ] Practical barriers (transport, phone, ID, benefits, housing) addressed, not ignored.
- [ ] No psychiatric medications/doses specified; routed to team prescriber.
- [ ] Generic case-management/H0039 billing flagged for clinician/biller verification.
