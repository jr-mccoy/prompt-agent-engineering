# First-Episode Psychosis Intake with CHR/FEP Differentiation

## Objective

Produce a complete intake record for a young person or adult presenting with possible psychosis that:

1. Differentiates **clinical high risk (CHR)** / attenuated psychosis syndrome / prodrome from **frank first-episode psychosis (FEP)** using structured criteria (SIPS/SOPS or CAARMS framing).
2. Estimates **duration of untreated psychosis (DUP)** — a primary prognostic variable — with onset anchoring.
3. Screens for and explicitly excludes secondary causes (substance-induced, medical/organic, mood-with-psychotic-features) before anchoring a primary psychotic-disorder hypothesis.
4. Conducts a layered risk assessment (psychosis + suicide + violence + vulnerability/exploitation) with relapse-signature baselining.
5. Routes to the appropriate level of care, prioritizing **Coordinated Specialty Care (CSC)** (RAISE / NAVIGATE / OnTrackNY model) where available.
6. Produces a structured intake note meeting CPT 90791 / 90792 documentation requirements with supervisor co-sign for high-acuity content.

## When to Use

- At first contact with a person (typically adolescent through early-30s, but any age) presenting with attenuated or frank positive symptoms, functional decline, or referral for "possible psychosis."
- When a referral source (ED, school counselor, PCP, family) reports new-onset unusual thoughts, perceptual changes, suspiciousness, or marked functional decline.
- When differentiating CHR (help-seeking but sub-threshold) from FEP changes the entire care pathway (CHR monitoring/prevention vs. FEP active treatment).

## When NOT to Use

- For an established, multi-episode schizophrenia-spectrum client with a known illness course: use a recovery-oriented plan or relapse-management workflow instead.
- For acute behavioral emergencies requiring immediate containment: stabilize and use the crisis/civil-commitment pathway first, then complete this intake.
- For primary substance intoxication where psychosis cannot yet be assessed: defer formal psychosis differentiation until a window of sobriety/observation allows it (document the deferral).

## Inputs / Context Required

- **Demographics:** Age, developmental/educational stage, living situation, primary language, cultural/spiritual context (load-bearing for interpreting belief content).
- **Referral and timeline:** Referral source and reason; collateral records; first appearance of any unusual experience; first appearance of frank positive symptoms.
- **Symptom data:** Positive (delusions, hallucinations, disorganized thought/speech, disorganized behavior), negative (avolition, anhedonia, alogia, blunted affect), cognitive, and functional-decline data.
- **Substance and medical context:** Substance use history and recent use; medical history; head injury, seizures, autoimmune/metabolic, medications; need for medical workup.
- **Family history:** Psychosis, mood disorder, suicide.
- **Collateral:** Family/caregiver observations (critical — insight is often limited).
- `[clinician input required: any prior antipsychotic exposure, prior brief psychotic episodes, and results of any medical workup ordered]`

## Constraints

### Must

- Make an explicit **CHR-vs-FEP determination** and state the criteria-based rationale; do not leave the threshold ambiguous.
- Anchor and document **DUP**: onset of attenuated symptoms, onset of frank psychosis, and time-to-treatment.
- Document a **secondary-cause exclusion** (substance-induced, mood-with-psychotic-features, medical/organic, delirium) before naming a primary psychotic disorder; recommend medical workup where indicated.
- Interpret belief and perceptual content against the person's **cultural, religious, and developmental context**; do not pathologize culturally sanctioned experience.
- Conduct a **layered risk assessment**: suicide (elevated in early psychosis), violence/agitation, and vulnerability to exploitation/self-neglect; baseline a **relapse signature** (the person's earliest individualized warning signs).
- Recommend **Coordinated Specialty Care** routing where available; specify the team disciplines CSC entails.
- Obtain **supervisor/attending co-sign** for the diagnostic impression and disposition given diagnostic uncertainty and acuity.
- Flag every `[clinician input required: ...]` gap; do not fabricate workup results, instrument scores, or medication history.

### Must Not

- Do not assign a definitive schizophrenia diagnosis at first intake when criteria (including the 6-month duration for schizophrenia) are not met; use the appropriate provisional/duration-qualified diagnosis (e.g., schizophreniform, brief psychotic disorder, unspecified) and document the differential.
- Do not fabricate DUP dates; if onset cannot be anchored, document estimation method and uncertainty.
- Do not omit the secondary-cause exclusion or the medical-workup recommendation.
- Do not specify antipsychotic drugs or doses; route medication decisions to the prescriber.
- Do not minimize suicide risk because the presentation is dominated by positive symptoms.

## CHR vs. FEP Differentiation Overlay

| Dimension | Clinical High Risk (CHR) / Attenuated Psychosis Syndrome | First-Episode Psychosis (FEP) |
|-----------|----------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------|
| Positive symptoms | Attenuated — present but with preserved reality testing / insight; experienced as unusual but doubted | Frank — full conviction, reality testing lost, at threshold intensity/frequency |
| Framing instruments | SIPS/SOPS (Structured Interview for Psychosis-Risk Syndromes / Scale of Psychosis-Risk Symptoms); CAARMS | Threshold positive symptoms meeting psychotic-disorder criteria; PANSS-style anchoring |
| Syndrome types | Attenuated Positive Symptom Syndrome (APSS); Brief Intermittent Psychotic Syndrome (BIPS); Genetic Risk + Deterioration (GRD) | Schizophreniform / brief psychotic disorder / schizophrenia (by duration) / mood disorder with psychotic features / substance-induced (excluded) |
| Care pathway | Monitoring, psychoeducation, low-intensity psychosocial intervention, prevention; antipsychotics generally NOT first-line | Active CSC treatment; prescriber evaluation; DUP-minimizing engagement |
| Primary prognostic lever | Transition-risk reduction | DUP reduction; rapid engagement |

## Instructions

1. **Establish the timeline and DUP.** Anchor first appearance of any attenuated/unusual experience, first appearance of frank positive symptoms, and time elapsed before any treatment. Use collateral to corroborate self-report.
2. **Catalog positive, negative, cognitive, and functional symptoms.** For each positive symptom, rate intensity/frequency AND degree of reality testing/insight — the CHR/FEP hinge.
3. **Apply the CHR-vs-FEP overlay.** State whether the presentation meets attenuated-syndrome criteria (CHR) or threshold psychosis (FEP), with the criteria-based rationale.
4. **Run the secondary-cause exclusion.** Address substance-induced, mood-with-psychotic-features, medical/organic, and delirium. Recommend medical workup (labs, tox, imaging/neuro consult as indicated).
5. **Interpret content in context.** Evaluate belief/perceptual content against cultural, religious, linguistic, and developmental norms before labeling it psychotic.
6. **Conduct layered risk assessment.** Suicide (with C-SSRS framing), violence/agitation, vulnerability/self-neglect; baseline the **relapse signature**.
7. **Determine disposition and CSC routing.** Recommend level of care and CSC referral; specify team composition and prescriber evaluation.
8. **Write the FEP/CHR Intake Note** using the output format; route for supervisor co-sign.
9. **Run verification.**

## Verification

- [ ] DUP anchored (or estimation method + uncertainty documented).
- [ ] Explicit CHR-vs-FEP determination with criteria-based rationale.
- [ ] Each positive symptom rated for reality testing/insight (the CHR/FEP hinge).
- [ ] Secondary-cause exclusion completed (substance / mood / medical / delirium) with workup recommendation.
- [ ] Belief/perceptual content interpreted against cultural, religious, and developmental context.
- [ ] Layered risk assessment: suicide, violence, vulnerability; relapse signature baselined.
- [ ] Schizophrenia not assigned without ≥6-month criteria; duration-qualified provisional diagnosis used.
- [ ] CSC routing specified with team composition; prescriber evaluation referred.
- [ ] Crisis/civil-commitment pathway referenced where acuity warrants.
- [ ] Supervisor/attending co-sign line present.
- [ ] No fabricated workup results, instrument scores, or medication history; gaps flagged.
