# SMI Medication Adherence Motivational Plan (MI-Based)

## Objective

Produce a motivational-interviewing–based plan to support medication adherence for an adult with serious mental illness who is ambivalent about, or struggling to sustain, antipsychotic or mood-stabilizer treatment. The plan:

1. Maps the client's **ambivalence** about medication (the real reasons for and against, in their words) rather than treating non-adherence as simple non-compliance.
2. Uses **OARS** (Open questions, Affirmations, Reflections, Summaries) to elicit and reinforce **change talk (DARN-CAT** — Desire, Ability, Reasons, Need; Commitment, Activation, Taking steps).
3. Supports **side-effect-informed shared decision making**, including discussion of **long-acting injectable (LAI)** options where relevant — as a collaborative referral topic for the prescriber, not a clinician-imposed choice.
4. Produces a collaborative, client-owned **adherence-support plan** with concrete supports, a change-talk summary, and a follow-up loop — not a directive.

## When to Use

- For a client on an antipsychotic or mood stabilizer who is ambivalent, partially adherent, or has discontinued and is reconsidering.
- When prior "you must take your medication" approaches have backfired and a collaborative MI stance is needed.
- As a structured adjunct to recovery-oriented care or ACT engagement when adherence is a barrier to the client's own goals.
- For training clinicians to articulate change talk and roll with resistance around medication.

## When NOT to Use

- In an acute crisis where medication is needed urgently for safety and capacity is impaired — manage safety/capacity first.
- When the core issue is total disengagement from care rather than medication ambivalence — engage first (`psychology_assertive_community_treatment_engagement_plan.md`).
- As a vehicle to coerce or pressure a decision; MI is incompatible with the righting reflex and persuasion-by-pressure.

## Inputs / Context Required

- **Current regimen (class-level):** antipsychotic and/or mood stabilizer; oral vs. LAI; how long on treatment. (Do not specify or recommend doses.)
- **Adherence pattern:** consistently adherent / partial / intermittent / discontinued; client's own account of why.
- **Side-effect experience:** what the client reports (e.g., sedation, weight/metabolic concerns, akathisia, sexual side effects, cognitive dulling, EPS) in their words.
- **Client's beliefs and values about medication:** illness model, autonomy concerns, prior experiences (including coercion), cultural/family views.
- **Client's own goals:** what the client wants for their life (link adherence to those, not to compliance).
- **Insight and capacity:** current insight into illness; decision-making capacity status.
- **Stage/readiness:** importance and confidence regarding sustaining medication.
- `[clinician input required: prescriber's clinical view on regimen options and any LAI suitability — this is a prescriber decision]`

## Constraints

### Must

- Adopt the **MI spirit**: partnership, acceptance, compassion, evocation. Avoid the **righting reflex** (jumping to fix/persuade).
- Map **ambivalence** explicitly: the client's reasons for AND against medication, captured in their own language.
- Use **OARS** throughout and **elicit, recognize, and reinforce change talk (DARN-CAT)**; distinguish change talk from sustain talk.
- Support **side-effect-informed shared decision making**: take side effects seriously as legitimate decision factors and route them to the prescriber with the client.
- Frame **LAI** (if relevant) as one option to explore with the prescriber based on the client's stated preferences (e.g., not wanting daily pills), never as a clinician-imposed mandate.
- **Link adherence to the client's own goals**, not to compliance.
- Produce a **change-talk summary** and a **collaborative plan with concrete supports** and a follow-up loop.
- Route all medication decisions, agents, and doses to the **prescriber**; flag `[clinician input required: ...]` gaps.

### Must Not

- Do not lecture, warn, scare, or argue the client into adherence; do not deploy the righting reflex.
- Do not name specific drugs or doses or recommend a particular agent — that is the prescriber's role.
- Do not dismiss reported side effects as resistance; they are real decision factors.
- Do not present LAI as a way to ensure compliance "around" the client's wishes; it is a shared option.
- Do not equate non-adherence with lack of insight by default; explore the actual reasons.

## MI Change-Talk Map (DARN-CAT)

| Code | Type | What to listen/ask for (re: sustaining medication) |
|------|------|---------------------------------------------------|
| D — Desire | Preparatory | "I want to feel steady"; "I'd like to stay out of the hospital" |
| A — Ability | Preparatory | "I could take it in the morning"; "I've managed it before" |
| R — Reasons | Preparatory | "When I take it, I sleep better"; "Last time I stopped, things fell apart" |
| N — Need | Preparatory | "I need to stay well for my kids" |
| C — Commitment | Mobilizing | "I will keep taking it"; "I'm going to try the routine" |
| A — Activation | Mobilizing | "I'm ready to set up a pillbox" |
| T — Taking steps | Mobilizing | "I already asked the nurse about the side effect" |

## Instructions

1. **Engage with MI spirit.** Open with partnership and an open question about the client's experience with medication; resist the righting reflex.
2. **Map ambivalence.** Elicit reasons for and against medication in the client's words (decisional-balance style, without steering); capture side-effect experience as legitimate.
3. **Elicit change talk with OARS.** Use open questions, affirmations, reflections, and summaries to draw out DARN-CAT; selectively reflect and reinforce change talk; roll with sustain talk rather than arguing it.
4. **Assess importance and confidence.** Use scaling-style questions to gauge readiness; explore the gap.
5. **Side-effect-informed shared decision making.** Take reported side effects seriously; identify which to bring to the prescriber; if relevant and client-preference-aligned, flag LAI as an option to explore with the prescriber.
6. **Link to the client's goals.** Connect sustaining medication to what the client wants for their life.
7. **Summarize change talk and build the plan.** Reflect a collecting summary of the client's own change talk; co-create concrete supports and a follow-up loop with the client's commitment language.
8. **Assemble the plan** using the output format.
9. **Run verification.**

## Verification

- [ ] MI spirit maintained; righting reflex avoided (no lecturing, warning, or arguing into adherence).
- [ ] Ambivalence mapped with reasons for AND against in the client's own words.
- [ ] OARS used; change talk elicited and coded by DARN-CAT; sustain talk rolled with, not argued.
- [ ] Importance and confidence assessed with scaling-style exploration.
- [ ] Side effects taken seriously as legitimate decision factors and routed to the prescriber.
- [ ] LAI framed (if relevant) as a client-preference-aligned option to explore with the prescriber, never imposed.
- [ ] Adherence linked to the client's own life goals, not to compliance.
- [ ] Collaborative plan includes client-chosen supports, a commitment statement, a first step, and a follow-up loop.
- [ ] Change-talk collecting summary reflects the client's own language.
- [ ] No specific agents or doses named; all medication decisions routed to prescriber.
- [ ] Gaps flagged with `[clinician input required: ...]`; non-adherence not equated with lack of insight by default.
