# SMI Recovery-Oriented Treatment Plan

## Objective

Produce a person-centered, recovery-oriented treatment plan for an adult with serious mental illness (e.g., schizophrenia-spectrum, schizoaffective, bipolar I, recurrent severe MDD with psychotic features) that:

1. Centers **client-defined recovery goals** in the client's own language, not clinician-defined symptom-elimination goals.
2. Organizes objectives across the four **SAMHSA recovery domains** — Health (symptom and physical), Home (housing stability), Purpose (work, education, meaningful activity), and Community (social connection, belonging).
3. Integrates **psychiatric rehabilitation** methods, **supported employment (Individual Placement and Support, IPS)**, **Housing First** principles, and a client-authored **Wellness Recovery Action Plan (WRAP)**.
4. Builds a **relapse-signature / early-warning monitoring** plan and a crisis/escalation pathway directly into the plan.
5. Uses a recovery-oriented PROM such as the **Recovery Assessment Scale (RAS)** to measure progress beyond symptom scales.
6. Produces documentation with the golden thread (assessment → goal → objective → intervention) for payer and care-coordination requirements.

## When to Use

- For an adult with established SMI moving from acute stabilization toward longitudinal community-based care.
- At plan creation or the periodic review for clients in community mental health, ACT/FACT, clubhouse, or psych-rehab programs.
- When a symptom-reduction-only plan has stalled and a function- and goals-oriented reframe is needed.
- When integrating multiple service domains (clinical, housing, vocational, peer support) into one coherent plan.

## When NOT to Use

- During an acute psychotic, manic, or suicidal crisis requiring stabilization first — stabilize, then build the recovery plan.
- For first-presentation/early psychosis still being differentiated: use `psychology_first_episode_psychosis_intake.md` first.
- As a substitute for an ACT engagement/outreach plan when the core problem is disengagement — use `psychology_assertive_community_treatment_engagement_plan.md`.

## Inputs / Context Required

- **Diagnosis and current symptom status:** primary SMI diagnosis, current positive/negative/mood symptoms, recent hospitalizations.
- **Client's own recovery goals and values** in their words; what a good life looks like to them.
- **Housing status:** stable / at-risk / homeless / supported / institutional; preferences.
- **Work / education / role status:** employment history, current vocational interest, benefits status (SSI/SSDI considerations).
- **Social / community:** supports, isolation, family involvement, peer-support engagement, cultural/spiritual community.
- **Physical health:** chronic conditions, metabolic monitoring needs (relevant to antipsychotic treatment), PCP linkage.
- **Strengths and prior recovery capital:** skills, interests, past periods of wellness, what has worked before.
- **Relapse history:** early-warning signs that have preceded past relapses; known triggers.
- **Medication status:** current regimen and adherence pattern (route to prescriber for changes; do not specify doses).
- `[clinician input required: most recent RAS or recovery-oriented PROM baseline if available]`

## Constraints

### Must

- Write **at least one objective per SAMHSA domain** (Health, Home, Purpose, Community); state explicitly if a domain is deferred and why.
- Phrase the **overarching recovery goal in the client's own words**; clinician objectives must ladder up to it.
- Make objectives **measurable, time-bound, and function-oriented** (role functioning, environmental milestones) rather than symptom-elimination-only.
- Apply **Housing First** logic: housing stability is not made contingent on treatment compliance or sobriety.
- Apply **IPS** principles for vocational goals: zero-exclusion, rapid job search aligned to client preference, benefits counseling, integration with the clinical team.
- Include a **relapse-signature monitoring plan** with named early-warning signs, who monitors them, and the agreed early response.
- Include a **crisis/escalation pathway** with thresholds and contacts.
- Include the **WRAP** components the client has authored (daily maintenance, triggers, early warning signs, when-things-break-down plan, crisis/advance directive).
- Maintain the **golden thread** for documentation.
- Flag `[clinician input required: ...]` gaps; do not fabricate goals, scores, or housing/vocational status.

### Must Not

- Do not write a deficit-only, clinician-imposed plan; recovery goals come from the client.
- Do not make housing contingent on medication adherence or abstinence.
- Do not specify antipsychotic/mood-stabilizer agents or doses — route to prescriber.
- Do not reduce "purpose" objectives to "attend day program" when the client's stated goal is competitive employment or education.
- Do not omit physical-health/metabolic monitoring for clients on antipsychotics.

## SAMHSA Recovery Domain Overlay

| Domain | Focus | Example function-oriented objective | Linked method/resource |
|--------|-------|-------------------------------------|------------------------|
| Health | Symptom self-management + physical health | Client identifies 3 early-warning signs and a response plan within 4 weeks | WRAP, relapse signature, illness self-management, metabolic monitoring |
| Home | Stable, safe, client-preferred housing | Client secures and sustains housing for 90 days | Housing First, supported housing, benefits navigation |
| Purpose | Work, education, meaningful daily activity | Client begins a preference-matched job search and attends first interview within 8 weeks | Supported employment (IPS), supported education, benefits counseling |
| Community | Social connection and belonging | Client attends a self-chosen community/peer activity weekly for 6 weeks | Peer support, clubhouse, cultural/spiritual community, family involvement |

## Instructions

1. **Elicit the client's recovery vision.** Capture, in the client's words, what recovery and a good life mean to them. This becomes the overarching goal.
2. **Map strengths and recovery capital.** Inventory skills, interests, past wellness periods, supports, and what has worked before.
3. **Assess each SAMHSA domain.** For Health, Home, Purpose, Community, summarize current status, client priority, and one or more target objectives.
4. **Apply method overlays.** Tie Home objectives to Housing First, Purpose objectives to IPS/supported education, Health objectives to WRAP/illness self-management and metabolic monitoring, Community objectives to peer support and natural supports.
5. **Build the relapse-signature plan.** With the client, name early-warning signs from past relapses, assign monitors, and define the agreed early response.
6. **Build the crisis/escalation pathway.** Define thresholds, contacts, and any psychiatric advance directive / WRAP crisis plan.
7. **Set measurement.** Establish RAS (or comparable recovery PROM) baseline and a re-measure interval; pair with symptom measures.
8. **Assemble the plan** with golden-thread documentation using the output format.
9. **Run verification.**

## Verification

- [ ] Overarching recovery goal is stated in the client's own words; clinician objectives ladder up to it.
- [ ] At least one measurable, time-bound, function-oriented objective per SAMHSA domain (Health, Home, Purpose, Community); deferred domains explained.
- [ ] Housing First applied — housing not contingent on treatment compliance or sobriety.
- [ ] IPS principles applied to vocational/education goals; benefits counseling addressed.
- [ ] Relapse-signature monitoring plan names early-warning signs, monitors, and agreed early response.
- [ ] WRAP components are client-authored and present.
- [ ] Crisis/escalation pathway with thresholds and contacts included.
- [ ] Recovery PROM (RAS or comparable) baseline and re-measure interval set, paired with symptom measures.
- [ ] Physical-health/metabolic monitoring included for clients on antipsychotics; no agents/doses specified (routed to prescriber).
- [ ] Golden thread maintained across assessment → goal → objective → intervention → measure.
- [ ] Gaps flagged with `[clinician input required: ...]`; no fabricated goals, scores, or status.
