# Geriatric Grief and Late-Life Transitions

## Objective

Produce a structured assessment-and-treatment scaffold for an older adult (65+) navigating loss and late-life transition. The output:

1. Maps the **loss landscape** — including cumulative, sequential, and disenfranchised losses that are easily under-recognized in older adults (death of spouse and peers, loss of role/identity, functional and sensory decline, relocation from home, loss of driving/independence, pet loss, anticipatory loss).
2. **Differentiates** normative (adaptive) grief from **prolonged grief disorder (PGD)** and from **grief-related major depression**, without pathologizing culturally and developmentally expectable grief.
3. Screens **late-life suicide risk**, which is elevated in bereaved older adults — particularly recently widowed men.
4. Frames the **identity / role-loss** work: who the person is after the role that organized their life is gone, and how meaning is reconstructed.
5. Recommends a **stage-matched intervention** plan with culturally responsive, strengths-based framing and reassessment hooks.

## When to Use

- An older-adult client presents after the death of a spouse, partner, sibling, adult child, or long-standing peer network.
- The presenting issue is a non-death loss central to late life: retirement, widowhood adjustment, relocation to assisted living, loss of driving/independence, caregiving role loss (after a spouse's death or institutionalization), or functional/sensory decline.
- The clinician must distinguish ongoing adaptive grief from a grief-related disorder warranting active treatment.
- A bereavement reaction is suspected of masking, or coexisting with, depression.

## When NOT to Use

- For the differential between depression and dementia: route to `psychology_geriatric_depression_vs_dementia_differential.md`.
- For a full intake/medication-falls baseline: pair with `psychology_geriatric_intake_with_polypharmacy_review.md`.
- For the general (non-geriatric) complicated-vs-normal grief differential, or when population-general structure is sufficient: use `psychology_grief_complicated_vs_normal_assessment.md`.
- When acute suicidality or a safety crisis is present: stabilize and route to the risk-crisis prompts first; this scaffold is not a crisis protocol.

## Inputs / Context Required

- **Client demographics and living situation:** Age, current household, recent moves, sensory/functional status.
- **Loss history:** Each loss — what, when, relationship, circumstances (anticipated vs. sudden, traumatic), and whether it was socially acknowledged.
- **Time since index loss(es):** Bereavement timeline matters for PGD thresholds.
- **Current functioning:** Daily routine, social engagement, self-care, role activities pre- and post-loss.
- **Cultural / spiritual context:** Mourning practices, beliefs about death and continuing bonds, community supports. `[clinician input required: cultural and faith framework that shapes what "normal" grieving looks like for this client]`
- **Mental health and medical context:** Prior depression/anxiety, current GDS-15 if available, cognitive status, pain, medication burden.
- **Risk context:** Prior suicide attempts, current ideation, lethal-means access, isolation, recent anniversary/holiday proximity.
- **Strengths:** Surviving relationships, meaning sources, prior resilience after loss, faith/community.

## Constraints

### Must

- Map the **full loss landscape**, not only the index death; older adults commonly carry cumulative and disenfranchised losses (driving, home, peers, role, pet) that are minimized by others.
- Anchor the grief differential in named criteria: distinguish **normative grief**, **prolonged grief disorder (DSM-5-TR / ICD-11 PGD)**, and **major depressive episode**, recognizing they can co-occur.
- Apply PGD's structure correctly: persistent, pervasive yearning/preoccupation with intense grief beyond the culturally expected period (generally ≥12 months for adults), with identity disruption, avoidance, emotional numbness, meaninglessness, or difficulty re-engaging — causing functional impairment.
- Account for **cultural and spiritual variation** in mourning duration and expression; do not pathologize culturally sanctioned continuing bonds, rituals, or expressions.
- Complete a **late-life suicide-risk screen** (Columbia C-SSRS) and document risk even when negative; flag widowhood, isolation, recent loss, and male sex as amplifiers.
- Distinguish **adaptive role-transition distress** (reorganizing identity) from **clinical disorder**; normalize transition difficulty while naming when it crosses into impairment.
- Recommend **stage-matched, strengths-based intervention** (e.g., complicated grief treatment / PGD-targeted therapy, behavioral activation, meaning-centered and life-review approaches, dignity-oriented work) and name the rationale.
- Use `[clinician input required: ...]` for missing inputs that would materially change the formulation; do not fabricate loss details, dates, or scores.

### Must Not

- Do not pathologize normative or culturally expectable grief; intensity alone, early in bereavement, is not disorder.
- Do not collapse grief, depression, and PGD into one undifferentiated category; name the distinctions and the overlap.
- Do not minimize non-death and disenfranchised losses (independence, home, driving, role, pet) as "not real grief."
- Do not omit the suicide-risk screen; bereaved older adults — especially recently widowed men — are a high-risk group.
- Do not fabricate cultural norms; elicit the client's own framework rather than assuming.
- Do not recommend "moving on" or detachment as a goal; continuing-bonds and meaning-reconstruction are evidence-consistent.

## Late-Life Grief Differential Matrix

| Feature | Normative / Adaptive Grief | Prolonged Grief Disorder (PGD) | Grief-Related Major Depression |
|---------|----------------------------|--------------------------------|-------------------------------|
| Core affect | Yearning, sadness in waves; pangs triggered by reminders | Persistent, pervasive yearning/preoccupation that does not attenuate | Pervasive low mood / anhedonia not tied specifically to the deceased |
| Time course | Intensity gradually eases; oscillates (loss-vs-restoration) | Beyond culturally expected period (generally ≥12 months adults), still intense and impairing | Can onset any time; sustained ≥2 weeks |
| Self-view | Self-esteem generally intact | Identity disruption ("part of me died"); role confusion | Worthlessness, excessive guilt, self-criticism |
| Positive emotion | Capacity for joy/connection returns intermittently | Avoidance of reminders; emotional numbness; can't re-engage | Global anhedonia across domains |
| Thoughts of death | Wish to join deceased may appear transiently | Intense; meaninglessness; difficulty imagining a future | Active suicidal ideation possible; assess directly |
| Function | Gradually re-engages roles/relationships | Marked, sustained impairment in re-engagement | Marked impairment; psychomotor change, neurovegetative signs |
| Response to support | Comforted by support and reminders over time | Support insufficient; "stuck" | May reject support; mood-congruent hopelessness |

*Overlap is common: PGD and MDD can co-occur and both can coexist with adaptive grief. Use the matrix to weight, not to force a single label. Cultural and spiritual framework moderates "expected period."*

## Late-Life Loss Landscape Checklist

- [ ] Death losses: spouse/partner, sibling(s), adult child, peer/friend network attrition
- [ ] Anticipatory loss: terminal illness in self or loved one
- [ ] Role/identity loss: retirement, end of caregiving role, loss of "provider/matriarch/expert" identity
- [ ] Independence loss: driving cessation, loss of home, relocation to assisted living/SNF
- [ ] Functional/sensory loss: mobility, vision, hearing, continence, cognition
- [ ] Relational loss: estrangement, friends who have died or are now distant
- [ ] Disenfranchised/under-acknowledged loss: pet death, loss of a former life/community, ambiguous loss (spouse with dementia)
- [ ] Cumulative/sequential loss: multiple losses in a short window, bereavement overload

## Instructions

1. **Map the loss landscape.** Use the checklist to surface all losses — death and non-death, acknowledged and disenfranchised, single and cumulative — with timeline and circumstances.

2. **Elicit the cultural/spiritual framework.** Ask how the client and their community grieve, what continuing-bonds practices exist, and what duration/expression is normative. Anchor the differential to this framework, not a generic norm.

3. **Apply the grief differential matrix.** Weight the presentation across normative grief, PGD, and grief-related depression; note co-occurrence explicitly. Use bereavement timeline against PGD's generally ≥12-month threshold while honoring cultural variation.

4. **Screen risk.** Complete a Columbia C-SSRS-based suicide-risk screen; document amplifiers (widowhood, isolation, recent loss, anniversary proximity, male sex, lethal-means access). Route positives to formal assessment.

5. **Frame the identity / role-loss work.** Articulate which organizing role(s) have been lost, what identity gap that creates, and what meaning-reconstruction and continuing-bonds avenues are available.

6. **Assess strengths and supports.** Surviving relationships, prior resilience after loss, faith/community, generativity opportunities, life-review material.

7. **Recommend a stage-matched intervention plan** (see output), naming the rationale and a reassessment window. **Run verification.**

## Verification

- [ ] Full loss landscape mapped (death + non-death + disenfranchised + cumulative), not only the index death.
- [ ] Client's cultural/spiritual mourning framework elicited and used to moderate the "expected period."
- [ ] Differential weights normative grief vs. PGD vs. grief-related depression and notes co-occurrence; PGD duration applied (generally ≥12 months) with cultural moderation.
- [ ] Normative/culturally-sanctioned grief not pathologized by intensity alone.
- [ ] Late-life suicide-risk screen completed (Columbia C-SSRS); amplifiers documented; risk routed appropriately even when low.
- [ ] Identity / role-loss formulation names the lost organizing role and meaning-reconstruction avenues.
- [ ] Continuing-bonds and meaning framing used; "detachment/moving on" not set as a goal.
- [ ] Strengths and supports documented.
- [ ] Intervention plan is stage-matched with named rationale and a reassessment window.
