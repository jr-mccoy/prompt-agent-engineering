# LGBTQ+ Affirmative Intake Module

## Objective

Produce an identity-affirmative biopsychosocial intake record for a lesbian, gay, bisexual, transgender, queer, intersex, asexual, or otherwise sexually/gender-diverse client that:

1. Elicits sexual orientation, gender identity, sex assigned at birth, pronouns, and name-in-use with affirming, non-assumptive language and a two-step gender question.
2. Distinguishes orientation from gender identity from embodiment, and avoids conflating them.
3. Applies Meyer's minority-stress model to organize history: distal stressors (prejudice events, discrimination, victimization, rejection, family-of-origin response) and proximal stressors (expectations of rejection, identity concealment, internalized stigma).
4. Documents resilience, coping, and community connectedness as a required domain — not an afterthought.
5. Screens for elevated suicide and self-harm risk known to be higher in LGBTQ+ populations, with a defined escalation pathway.
6. Locates the presenting concern relative to identity: separates identity-related distress (which is not pathologized) from co-occurring mental health conditions.
7. Applies an intersectional lens (race, ethnicity, disability, immigration, faith, socioeconomic status) to minority-stress exposure.
8. Produces a structured intake note meeting CPT 90791 documentation requirements.

## When to Use

- At initial intake for any adult or older-adolescent client who identifies as LGBTQ+, is questioning, or presents with identity-related concerns.
- When a general intake has surfaced sexual-orientation or gender-identity content that warrants an affirmative, structured follow-up module.
- When a client is entering care specifically to address minority-stress sequelae (rejection, discrimination, concealment burden, internalized stigma).
- As the intake layer preceding a minority-stress case formulation or a gender-affirming-care assessment.

## When NOT to Use

- As a substitute for a gender-affirming-care assessment letter: that is a distinct deliverable — use `psychology_gender_affirming_letter_drafter.md`.
- For clients who do not identify as LGBTQ+ and have no identity-related presenting concern: use the standard `psychology_intake_assessment_note.md`.
- For acute crisis stabilization: complete a crisis assessment first, then return to the structured intake when the client is stabilized.
- For pediatric clients requiring caregiver-integrated intake: combine with `psychology_pediatric_intake_with_caregiver.md` and adapt assent/confidentiality accordingly.

## Inputs / Context Required

- **Demographics and identity:** Name-in-use, legal name (only if administratively required), pronouns, sexual orientation, gender identity, sex assigned at birth, relationship structure (monogamous, consensually non-monogamous, partnered, single).
- **Presenting concern:** In the client's own words; clinician's preliminary read on whether it is identity-related, comorbid, or both.
- **Minority-stress history inputs:** Family-of-origin response to identity; experiences of discrimination, harassment, or violence; outness across contexts (family, work, faith community, healthcare); concealment demands.
- **Intersectional context:** Race/ethnicity, disability status, immigration status, faith background, socioeconomic context — and how these intersect with identity-based stress.
- **Risk inputs:** Any history of suicidality, self-harm, or psychiatric hospitalization; current ideation; access to means.
- **Support and resilience inputs:** Chosen family, affirming relationships, community connectedness, prior affirming care experiences.
- `[clinician input required: any safety profile considerations — active suicidality, intimate partner violence, housing instability tied to identity — that modify the intake structure]`

## Constraints

### Must

- Use the client's stated name-in-use and pronouns throughout the entire record; if legal name is recorded for administrative reasons, segregate it clearly and do not use it in narrative.
- Use a two-step approach to gender (sex assigned at birth + current gender identity) and ask sexual orientation separately; do not infer one from the other.
- Frame identity itself as non-pathological. Document identity-related distress as a response to minority stress and environment, not as a disorder.
- Organize stressor history explicitly under Meyer's distal and proximal categories.
- Document resilience, coping, and community connectedness as a required domain.
- Screen explicitly for suicidal ideation, self-harm, and prior attempts; LGBTQ+ populations carry elevated risk and the screen is mandatory even when no concern is volunteered.
- Apply an intersectional lens: document how race, disability, immigration, faith, and class intersect with identity-based stress exposure.
- Flag all `[clinician input required: ...]` gaps; do not fabricate disclosure history, outness status, or screening scores.

### Must Not

- Do not assume heterosexuality, cisgender identity, monogamy, or a binary gender from any demographic or presentation cue.
- Do not conflate sexual orientation with gender identity, or either with embodiment/transition status.
- Do not pathologize identity, attraction, relationship structure, or gender expression; do not frame identity as the "cause" of a disorder.
- Do not pressure disclosure of outness, transition history, or sexual practices beyond what is clinically relevant to the presenting concern.
- Do not omit the suicide-risk screen under any circumstances; document it even when negative.
- Do not record deadname in narrative text or read it back to the client.

## Minority-Stress Framing Overlay (Meyer)

Use this overlay to organize the stressor history. Distal stressors are external events; proximal stressors are internalized psychological processes. Both are buffered by resilience and community connectedness.

| Process | Type | What to Elicit and Document |
|---------|------|------------------------------|
| Prejudice events | Distal | Discrete experiences of discrimination, harassment, hate-motivated violence, denial of services, microaggressions |
| Discrimination / structural | Distal | Employment, housing, healthcare, legal, faith-community exclusion; institutional barriers |
| Victimization / rejection | Distal | Family-of-origin rejection, peer victimization, partner violence, loss of relationships after disclosure |
| Expectations of rejection | Proximal | Vigilance, anticipating discrimination, guardedness in new settings (including healthcare) |
| Concealment | Proximal | Energy and cost of hiding identity; selective outness; "passing" demands; impact on relationships |
| Internalized stigma | Proximal | Internalized homophobia/transphobia/biphobia; shame; self-devaluation tied to identity |
| Resilience / coping | Buffer | Individual coping, identity pride, meaning-making, prior mastery of minority stress |
| Community connectedness | Buffer | Chosen family, affirming community, LGBTQ+ peer relationships, affirming faith/cultural spaces |

## Instructions

1. **Establish affirming frame.** Confirm name-in-use and pronouns before the interview proper. Explain that questions about identity are routine and that the client controls the pace and depth of disclosure.

2. **Two-step identity elicitation.** Ask sex assigned at birth and current gender identity separately; ask sexual orientation as a distinct question. Record relationship structure without assuming monogamy or partner gender.

3. **Locate the presenting concern.** With the client, determine whether the concern is identity-related, a co-occurring condition, or both. Document the client's own framing.

4. **Elicit distal minority-stress history.** Using the overlay, document prejudice events, discrimination, victimization, and family-of-origin response — paced and non-leading.

5. **Elicit proximal minority-stress processes.** Document expectations of rejection, concealment burden, and internalized stigma. Note their functional impact.

6. **Apply intersectional lens.** Document how race, disability, immigration, faith, and class intersect with and compound identity-based stress.

7. **Document resilience and community connectedness.** Identify coping strengths, chosen family, affirming relationships, and community resources.

8. **Conduct risk screen.** Administer a suicide-risk screen and document self-harm and attempt history. Apply the escalation pathway if positive.

9. **Affirmative MSE and history.** Complete medical, psychiatric, and substance history; conduct MSE without pathologizing identity, gender expression, or affect tied to minority stress.

10. **Write the LGBTQ+ Affirmative Intake Note** using the output format below.

11. **Run verification.**
