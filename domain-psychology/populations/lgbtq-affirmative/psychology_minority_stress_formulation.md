# Minority-Stress Case Formulation

## Objective

Build an explanatory case formulation for an LGBTQ+ client using Meyer's minority-stress model. The formulation:

1. Maps distal (objective, external) stressors: prejudice events, discrimination, victimization, and rejection.
2. Maps proximal (subjective, internalized) processes: expectations of rejection, identity concealment, and internalized stigma.
3. Documents the buffering factors — individual resilience/coping and community connectedness — that moderate the relationship between stress and outcomes.
4. Applies an intersectional lens so that minority stress is not modeled in isolation from race, disability, immigration, faith, and class.
5. Links the stress processes to the client's presenting symptoms and functional impairment, distinguishing minority-stress sequelae from independent psychopathology.
6. Translates the formulation into specific, affirming treatment targets and a risk-monitoring plan that accounts for the elevated suicide risk in LGBTQ+ populations.

The formulation is explanatory and treatment-guiding — it does not pathologize identity.

## When to Use

- After an affirmative intake, when the clinician needs an integrative formulation linking environment and identity-based stress to symptoms.
- When a presentation is not well-explained by a standard diagnostic formulation because minority stress is a central driver.
- When supervision or treatment planning requires explicit articulation of how distal and proximal stressors maintain the presenting problem.
- When designing minority-stress-informed interventions (e.g., affirmative CBT, ESTEEM, identity-affirming ACT).

## When NOT to Use

- As a substitute for diagnostic assessment: pair with `psychology_case_conceptualization_framework.md` for the full diagnostic picture.
- When the client does not identify as LGBTQ+ and minority stress is not a relevant explanatory frame.
- For acute risk management in isolation: complete a crisis assessment first; this formulation supports, but does not replace, a safety plan.

## Inputs / Context Required

- **Identity and presenting concern:** From the affirmative intake — orientation, gender identity, presenting symptoms.
- **Distal stressor data:** Documented prejudice events, discrimination, victimization, family-of-origin response.
- **Proximal process data:** Expectations of rejection, concealment burden, internalized stigma and their functional impact.
- **Resilience and community data:** Coping strengths, chosen family, community connectedness, prior mastery of minority stress.
- **Intersectional context:** Race/ethnicity, disability, immigration, faith, socioeconomic status.
- **Symptom and severity anchors:** PHQ-9, GAD-7, PCL-5, or equivalent; functional impairment domains.
- **Risk data:** Current and historical suicidality and self-harm.
- `[clinician input required: any data gaps that materially change the formulation or the weighting of stressors]`

## Constraints

### Must

- Use Meyer's model explicitly: separate distal stressors from proximal processes and name the buffering factors.
- Model resilience and community connectedness as active moderators, not a closing courtesy — show how they buffer specific stressors.
- Apply an intersectional lens; show how intersecting marginalizations compound or alter minority-stress exposure rather than treating identity as a single axis.
- Link each major stress process to the client's specific symptoms and functional impairment with an explicit mechanism (e.g., concealment → hypervigilance → exhaustion and social withdrawal).
- Distinguish minority-stress sequelae from independent psychopathology where both are present.
- Translate the formulation into specific, affirming treatment targets that act on the modeled mechanisms.
- Include a risk-monitoring plan reflecting elevated LGBTQ+ suicide risk and the minority-stress contributors to it.
- Flag `[clinician input required: ...]` for any data gap that changes stressor weighting; do not fabricate history or scores.

### Must Not

- Do not frame identity, attraction, or gender as the cause of symptoms; the model locates pathogenic load in stress and stigma, not in identity.
- Do not present minority stress as the explanation for all symptoms without checking for independent psychopathology.
- Do not omit resilience and community connectedness; a deficit-only formulation is incomplete and non-affirmative.
- Do not collapse intersecting identities into a single stress axis.
- Do not omit the risk dimension.

## Meyer's Minority-Stress Process Map

| Layer | Process | Definition | Typical Sequelae to Test |
|-------|---------|------------|--------------------------|
| Distal | Prejudice events | Discrete external experiences of discrimination, harassment, hate-motivated violence | Trauma symptoms, hypervigilance, mistrust |
| Distal | Discrimination / structural | Institutional exclusion (work, housing, healthcare, legal, faith) | Demoralization, access barriers, chronic stress load |
| Distal | Victimization / rejection | Family rejection, partner violence, peer victimization, relationship loss | Depression, attachment injury, isolation |
| Proximal | Expectations of rejection | Anticipatory vigilance toward future discrimination | Anxiety, avoidance, guardedness (incl. with providers) |
| Proximal | Concealment | Active hiding of identity; "passing" demands | Cognitive/emotional load, inauthenticity distress, withdrawal |
| Proximal | Internalized stigma | Internalized homophobia/transphobia/biphobia; shame | Low self-worth, self-criticism, internalized risk for SI |
| Buffer | Resilience / coping | Individual coping, identity pride, meaning-making | Moderates stress→symptom path; protective |
| Buffer | Community connectedness | Chosen family, affirming community, peer belonging | Moderates stress→symptom path; protective |

## Instructions

1. **Assemble the profile.** Compile identity, presenting symptoms, severity anchors, and the stressor/resilience data from intake.

2. **Map distal stressors.** Using the process map, document prejudice events, discrimination, and victimization/rejection with specifics.

3. **Map proximal processes.** Document expectations of rejection, concealment burden, and internalized stigma, each with its functional impact.

4. **Map buffering factors.** Document resilience/coping and community connectedness and identify which specific stressors each buffers.

5. **Apply intersectional analysis.** Show how intersecting marginalizations compound or modify the stress exposure and access to buffers.

6. **Build the mechanistic links.** For each major symptom or impairment, trace the stress process(es) that drive and maintain it; note where independent psychopathology is also operating.

7. **Derive treatment targets.** Translate each load-bearing mechanism into a specific affirming treatment target and candidate intervention.

8. **Build the risk-monitoring plan.** Identify minority-stress contributors to suicide/self-harm risk and define monitoring and escalation.

9. **Write the formulation** using the output format below.

10. **Run verification.**

## Verification

- [ ] Distal stressors and proximal processes are separated per Meyer's model and both populated.
- [ ] Resilience and community connectedness modeled as active moderators, each mapped to specific stressors.
- [ ] Intersectional analysis shows compounding/modifying effects, not a single-axis model.
- [ ] Each major symptom/impairment is linked to stress process(es) with an explicit mechanism.
- [ ] Independent psychopathology distinguished from minority-stress sequelae where both present.
- [ ] Identity not framed as the cause of symptoms; pathogenic load located in stress and stigma.
- [ ] Treatment targets are affirming and tied to the modeled mechanisms.
- [ ] Risk-monitoring plan reflects elevated LGBTQ+ suicide risk and names minority-stress contributors.
