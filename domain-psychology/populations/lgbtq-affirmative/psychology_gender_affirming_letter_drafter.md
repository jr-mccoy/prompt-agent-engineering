# Gender-Affirming Care Letter Drafter

## Objective

Draft a behavioral-health assessment / referral letter supporting a client's access to gender-affirming medical care (hormone therapy or gender-affirming surgery), structured to the content elements described in the WPATH Standards of Care Version 8 (SOC-8). The letter:

1. Documents the clinician's relationship to the client and the basis of the assessment.
2. Documents persistent and well-documented gender incongruence in the client's own narrative.
3. Documents the client's capacity to consent to the specific medical intervention and that informed consent has been discussed.
4. Documents relevant mental health context — including whether any co-occurring conditions are reasonably well-controlled and not interfering with informed decision-making.
5. States the assessment finding and the specific referral, while leaving the medical-necessity and surgical-eligibility determinations that belong to the prescribing/operating clinician as explicit `[clinician input required: ...]` slots.
6. Applies affirmative-practice principles and an intersectional lens.

The prompt produces a defensible draft for clinician review and signature — it does not make the medical determination for the clinician.

## When to Use

- When a qualified behavioral-health clinician with an established assessment relationship needs to draft a letter supporting a client's access to gender-affirming hormone therapy or surgery.
- When a surgical program or prescribing provider requests a behavioral-health assessment letter consistent with WPATH SOC-8 content.
- When updating or re-issuing a letter for a subsequent intervention (e.g., a prior hormone letter exists; client now seeks surgery).

## When NOT to Use

- To establish gender identity as valid: identity does not require justification or a letter. This letter documents an assessment, not a verdict on the client's identity.
- When the clinician lacks the competence, scope, or relationship to assess for the specific intervention: refer to a qualified assessor instead.
- For an initial affirmative intake: use `psychology_lgbtq_affirmative_intake_module.md` first; this drafter assumes the assessment is complete.
- As a gatekeeping instrument to delay access: SOC-8 emphasizes that assessment supports informed, autonomous decision-making, not unnecessary barriers.

## Inputs / Context Required

- **Clinician details:** Name, credentials, license number, scope, and the nature/length of the assessment relationship.
- **Client details:** Name-in-use, pronouns, DOB, and (only if the receiving program requires it) legal name segregated administratively.
- **Specific intervention sought:** Hormone therapy / specific gender-affirming surgical procedure(s).
- **Assessment basis:** Number of sessions, dates, instruments used, collateral if any.
- **Gender incongruence narrative:** The client's own account of persistent gender incongruence and goals.
- **Mental health context:** Co-occurring conditions and their status; whether any condition interferes with informed decision-making.
- **Capacity and informed consent:** Evidence the client understands risks, benefits, alternatives, and the reversibility/irreversibility profile of the intervention.
- `[clinician input required: medical-necessity / surgical-eligibility determination that belongs to the prescribing or operating clinician]`
- `[clinician input required: any local program-specific letter requirements that differ from SOC-8 content]`

## Constraints

### Must

- Use the client's name-in-use and pronouns throughout; segregate legal name administratively if required and never use it in narrative.
- Structure the letter to SOC-8 content: assessor qualifications and relationship; documentation of persistent gender incongruence; capacity to consent; mental health context; confirmation that informed-consent discussion occurred; the assessment finding and specific referral.
- State that the assessment supports the client's capacity for informed, autonomous decision-making — framing assessment as supporting access, not gatekeeping.
- Distinguish clearly between what the behavioral-health clinician is attesting to (capacity, mental health context, persistent incongruence) and the medical determination that belongs to the prescribing/operating clinician (`[clinician input required: ...]`).
- Document that co-occurring mental health conditions, if present, are reasonably well-controlled to the extent that they do not impair the client's capacity to make an informed decision — without requiring the absence of all mental health conditions.
- Apply affirmative-practice principles and note intersectional context relevant to the assessment.
- Flag every clinician-judgment element as `[clinician input required: ...]`; do not fabricate session counts, instrument scores, diagnoses, or attestations.

### Must Not

- Do not make or imply the medical-necessity or surgical-eligibility determination — leave it to the clinician.
- Do not require a mental health diagnosis as a precondition for the letter; gender diversity is not a mental disorder.
- Do not use "Gender Identity Disorder" or other deprecated/pathologizing terminology; if a diagnostic code is needed, reference gender incongruence / gender dysphoria per the current applicable coding system.
- Do not frame the letter as proving the client "really is" their gender; identity is self-determined.
- Do not insert delay-justifying or skeptical language; the letter documents, it does not litigate identity.
- Do not include the client's deadname in the body of the letter.

## WPATH SOC-8 Letter Content Overlay

The letter should address each element. Map clinical content to these slots.

| SOC-8 Content Element | What the Letter Documents |
|-----------------------|----------------------------|
| Assessor qualifications & relationship | Clinician's competence, license, and length/nature of the assessment relationship |
| Persistent gender incongruence | Client's own narrative of marked, persistent incongruence between experienced gender and sex assigned at birth |
| Capacity to consent | Client can understand and weigh risks, benefits, alternatives; decision-making is autonomous |
| Mental health context | Co-occurring conditions, if any, are characterized and reasonably controlled so as not to impair informed consent |
| Informed-consent discussion | Confirmation that risks/benefits/alternatives/reversibility were discussed and understood |
| Assessment finding & referral | The clinician's finding and the specific intervention referral (medical determination remains the prescriber's) |

## Instructions

1. **Confirm scope and relationship.** Establish that the clinician is qualified to assess for the specific intervention and has an adequate assessment relationship; document session count and dates.

2. **Confirm intervention.** Identify the specific intervention sought (hormones / named surgical procedure) and any program-specific letter requirements.

3. **Draft persistent gender incongruence section.** Render the client's own narrative of persistent incongruence and goals, in affirming language, without requiring proof.

4. **Draft capacity-to-consent section.** Document evidence the client understands and can weigh risks, benefits, alternatives, and reversibility, and is deciding autonomously.

5. **Draft mental health context section.** Characterize co-occurring conditions if any and document that they are reasonably controlled such that they do not impair informed consent — leaving the absence of conditions unnecessary.

6. **Document informed-consent discussion.** Confirm the risks/benefits/alternatives/reversibility conversation occurred and was understood.

7. **State assessment finding and referral.** Provide the clinician's finding and specific referral, leaving the medical-necessity determination to the prescriber as `[clinician input required: ...]`.

8. **Apply affirmative and intersectional review.** Ensure language is affirming and note relevant intersectional context.

9. **Assemble the letter** using the output format below.

10. **Run verification.**

## Output Format

```
=== GENDER-AFFIRMING CARE — BEHAVIORAL HEALTH ASSESSMENT LETTER (DRAFT) ===

[Clinic letterhead / contact block]
Date: [YYYY-MM-DD]

To: [Receiving provider / surgical program — name, address]
Re: [Client name-in-use], pronouns [.../...], DOB [YYYY-MM-DD]
[Administrative legal name (if program-required, segregated): [clinician input required]]

─────────────────────────────────────────
1. ASSESSOR QUALIFICATIONS AND RELATIONSHIP
─────────────────────────────────────────
I am [Name, credentials, license #], a [discipline] qualified to provide behavioral-health assessment for gender-affirming care. I have worked with [client name-in-use] in a [therapeutic / assessment] relationship since [date], across [N] sessions. This assessment is based on [clinical interview / collateral / instruments: ...].

─────────────────────────────────────────
2. INTERVENTION REFERENCED
─────────────────────────────────────────
This letter is provided in support of [client]'s access to: [Gender-affirming hormone therapy / Specific procedure(s): ...].

─────────────────────────────────────────
3. PERSISTENT GENDER INCONGRUENCE
─────────────────────────────────────────
[Client] describes a persistent and consistent experience of gender incongruence between [their] experienced gender and sex assigned at birth, characterized by: [client's own narrative — duration, consistency, goals]. This experience is well-documented across our work and is not transient.
[Diagnostic reference if required by payer/program: gender incongruence / gender dysphoria per applicable coding system — clinician input required: code]

─────────────────────────────────────────
4. CAPACITY TO CONSENT
─────────────────────────────────────────
In my clinical assessment, [client] demonstrates the capacity to provide informed consent for the referenced intervention: [they] understand the nature, expected effects, risks, benefits, alternatives, and the reversibility/irreversibility profile of the intervention, and are making this decision autonomously and free of undue coercion.

─────────────────────────────────────────
5. MENTAL HEALTH CONTEXT
─────────────────────────────────────────
[Client]'s mental health was assessed as part of this evaluation. [No co-occurring condition is present / The following co-occurring condition(s) are present: ...]. To the extent any condition is present, it is reasonably well-controlled and does not impair [client]'s capacity for informed decision-making regarding this intervention. The presence or absence of a mental health condition is not a barrier to affirming care; this assessment confirms that decision-making capacity is intact.

─────────────────────────────────────────
6. INFORMED-CONSENT DISCUSSION
─────────────────────────────────────────
I have confirmed that [client] has [discussed with me / been referred to discuss with the prescribing/operating clinician] the risks, benefits, alternatives, and reversibility considerations of the intervention, and [they] demonstrated understanding of this information.

─────────────────────────────────────────
7. ASSESSMENT FINDING AND REFERRAL
─────────────────────────────────────────
Based on this assessment, it is my finding that [client] meets the behavioral-health assessment elements consistent with WPATH SOC-8 for the referenced intervention, and I am pleased to support [their] referral to your care for [intervention].
Medical-necessity / surgical-eligibility determination: [clinician input required: this determination rests with the prescribing/operating clinician].

Intersectional / contextual notes relevant to care coordination: [As applicable — affirming PCP, language access, support system]

Please contact me with any questions.

Sincerely,
[Name, credentials, license #, signature line]
```

## Verification

- [ ] Client name-in-use and pronouns used throughout; deadname not in the letter body.
- [ ] All six SOC-8 content elements present: assessor qualifications/relationship, persistent gender incongruence, capacity to consent, mental health context, informed-consent discussion, assessment finding/referral.
- [ ] Letter frames assessment as supporting informed, autonomous decision-making — not gatekeeping.
- [ ] Behavioral-health attestations clearly distinguished from the medical determination, which is left as `[clinician input required: ...]`.
- [ ] Mental health context does not require absence of all conditions; documents capacity is intact.
- [ ] No deprecated/pathologizing terminology; identity not framed as requiring proof.
- [ ] No medical-necessity or surgical-eligibility determination made by the prompt.
- [ ] Session count, dates, instruments, and diagnoses are clinician-supplied, not fabricated.
- [ ] Affirmative-practice principles and relevant intersectional context applied.
- [ ] All clinician-judgment elements flagged with `[clinician input required: ...]`.
