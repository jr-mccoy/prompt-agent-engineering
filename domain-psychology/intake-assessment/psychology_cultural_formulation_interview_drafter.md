# Cultural Formulation Interview Drafter

## Objective

Compile a structured Cultural Formulation Interview (CFI) narrative following the DSM-5-TR Appendix framework. The output:

1. Covers all four DSM-5-TR CFI core domains: Cultural Definition of the Problem; Cultural Perceptions of Cause, Context, and Support; Cultural Factors Affecting Self-Coping and Past Help Seeking; and Cultural Factors Affecting Current Help Seeking.
2. Incorporates relevant supplementary modules (Social Network, Caregivers, Psychosocial Stressors, Spirituality/Religion, Cultural Identity, Coping/Help Seeking, Patient-Clinician Relationship, School-Age Children/Adolescents, Older Adults, Immigrants/Refugees) as the client's profile warrants.
3. Frames cultural data as assets and context, not deficits or exotic differences.
4. Produces a CFI narrative section and a Cultural Formulation Synthesis paragraph ready for insertion into the 90791/90792 intake note.

## When to Use

- Whenever cultural factors are clinically salient — which is always, per DSM-5-TR guidance that the CFI is recommended for all intakes, not only for clients from minority or immigrant backgrounds.
- When explanatory models of distress diverge between clinician and client, creating potential misdiagnosis or engagement barriers.
- When treatment adherence, help-seeking patterns, or family/community involvement in care is a relevant factor.
- When assessing clients from historically underserved or marginalized communities where systemic stressors may be misread as psychopathology.

## Inputs / Context Required

Provide any combination of the following gathered through interview. Items can be gathered over multiple sessions for advanced-difficulty presentations; document what was gathered in session 1 and what remains for follow-up.

- **Cultural identity:** The cultures, communities, ethnicities, national origins, languages, religions, spiritual traditions, genders, sexual orientations, and other identity dimensions the client uses to describe themselves. Their relative importance to the client and whether any create conflict.
- **Cultural definition of the problem:** The client's own words for what is wrong, what they call it in their language/community, how others in their community would describe it. Degree of agreement with clinician's framing.
- **Cultural explanatory model:** Causes the client attributes to the problem (spiritual, supernatural, interpersonal, constitutional, fate/karma, social oppression, biomedical, moral failure, ancestral/intergenerational). Level of belief in each.
- **Cultural context and social stressors:** Immigration history (generation, documentation status, acculturation stage, discrimination experiences), socioeconomic stressors with racialized or structural components, family roles and obligations, community belonging, neighborhood context.
- **Cultural perceptions of cause:** Community vs. family vs. client's own attribution. Family and community understanding of the problem.
- **Social network support:** Who in the client's cultural community provides support; what roles are expected; whether support is conditional on conforming to community norms.
- **Spirituality/religion:** Role in the client's life; how it intersects with mental-health concepts (healing prayer, confession, ancestors, karma, divine will); whether spiritual leaders are involved.
- **Prior help seeking:** Traditional healers, folk remedies, religious interventions, community elders sought before or alongside mental-health care. Reasons for those choices. Cultural barriers to prior mental-health engagement.
- **Cultural factors affecting current help seeking:** Stigma within cultural community, shame/honor dynamics, family pressure for or against treatment, financial access shaped by discrimination, language access, distrust of healthcare system based on historical or personal experiences.
- **Patient-clinician relationship:** Perceived differences in cultural background between client and clinician; whether those differences affect the client's comfort, trust, or willingness to disclose; the clinician's own cultural background and any relevant dynamics.
- **Immigration/refugee module (if applicable):** Pre-migration experiences (trauma, loss, persecution), migration journey, post-migration stressors, acculturation conflict, legal status effects on help seeking.

## Constraints

### Must

- Use the client's own words for their explanatory model and definition of the problem; do not substitute clinical terminology.
- Frame each cultural finding in terms of clinical relevance — how it shapes diagnosis, engagement, treatment planning, or the therapeutic relationship.
- Include a Cultural Formulation Synthesis paragraph that integrates the four CFI core domains into a coherent clinical narrative.
- Note clearly which supplementary modules were administered and which were deferred, with rationale.
- Acknowledge clinician positionality: the CFI includes a patient-clinician relationship domain, and the synthesis should reference any cultural distance and its clinical implications.
- Flag areas where cultural factors may alter diagnostic interpretation — e.g., spiritual experiences not equivalent to psychosis, culturally normative grief not equivalent to MDD, culturally syntonic family involvement not equivalent to enmeshment pathology.
- Where relevant, note structural/systemic stressors (housing, racism, legal status, economic precarity) as contextual factors in the formulation, distinct from psychopathology.
- Use `[clinician input required]` for any CFI domain not yet explored.

### Must Not

- Do not treat any cultural practice, explanatory model, or help-seeking pattern as inherently pathological.
- Do not project cultural attributes based on demographic category — document only what the client reports about their own identity and experience.
- Do not collapse multiple distinct identities into a single cultural descriptor (e.g., "Hispanic" obscures national origin, generation, language, urban/rural, documentation status, racial identity).
- Do not omit the patient-clinician relationship domain — it is a required CFI component.
- Do not diagnose based on cultural difference alone; document that cultural context was considered in differential reasoning.
- Do not fabricate; flag information gaps.

## Instructions

1. **Identify supplementary modules indicated.** Review inputs for immigration history, adolescent or child status, older adult concerns, significant caregiver involvement, and prominent spirituality/religion domain. Select applicable supplementary modules and note them at the top of the output.

2. **Document Cultural Identity.** List the identity dimensions the client affirms, using their language. Note relative salience of each and any identity conflicts the client experiences.

3. **Document Cultural Definition of the Problem.** Record the client's and community's term for the problem. Note agreement or divergence from clinical framing.

4. **Document the Explanatory Model.** Compile the client's causal attributions. Note whether attribution is shared by family/community. Assess implications for treatment engagement (e.g., attributing depression to spiritual testing may mean a client will prefer pastoral counseling; attributing psychosis to spirit possession changes engagement strategy).

5. **Document Cultural Perceptions of Cause, Context, and Support.** Compile social network, family role obligations, and community supports. Note conditional vs. unconditional support. Note any structural stressors that intersect with cultural identity (discrimination, legal status, housing precarity).

6. **Document Cultural Factors Affecting Self-Coping and Past Help Seeking.** Compile traditional and alternative help seeking. Note reasons for choices and any stigma or access barriers.

7. **Document Cultural Factors Affecting Current Help Seeking.** Address stigma, shame/honor norms, family pressure, financial and language access, institutional distrust.

8. **Document Patient-Clinician Relationship Domain.** Note perceived cultural distance. Record any client statements about clinician identity relevance. Note any clinician self-awareness observations relevant to the formulation.

9. **Write the Cultural Formulation Synthesis paragraph.** In one to three coherent paragraphs, integrate all four core CFI domains. Explicitly address how cultural factors should influence: (a) diagnostic interpretation, (b) treatment selection and sequencing, (c) engagement and alliance, (d) family/community involvement decisions.

10. **List Cultural Recommendations for the Initial Plan.** Translate CFI findings into concrete treatment-planning items.

11. **Run verification.**

## Verification

- [ ] All seven output domains present in order.
- [ ] Client's own words appear in Domain 2 (definition of the problem) and Domain 3 (explanatory model).
- [ ] Explanatory model itemizes each category (biomedical, psychological, spiritual, etc.) as endorsed or not.
- [ ] Structural stressors (discrimination, legal status, economic precarity) are documented separately from psychopathology.
- [ ] Patient-clinician relationship domain is present and includes clinician positionality.
- [ ] Supplementary modules administered vs. deferred are listed with rationale.
- [ ] Cultural Formulation Synthesis addresses all four CFI core domains in integrated narrative.
- [ ] Synthesis explicitly addresses diagnostic interpretation implications (what should not be pathologized, what changes differential reasoning).
- [ ] Cultural recommendations are actionable and specific (not generic "cultural sensitivity").
- [ ] No cultural attribute is presented as inherently pathological.
- [ ] No demographic projection — only client-reported identity and experience documented.
- [ ] Gaps flagged with `[clinician input required: ...]`.
