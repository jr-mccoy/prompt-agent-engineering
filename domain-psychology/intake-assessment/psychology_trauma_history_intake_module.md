# Trauma History Intake Module

## Objective

Produce a structured trauma-history section for a psychiatric or behavioral health intake that:

1. Identifies trauma categories (interpersonal, non-interpersonal, early/developmental, single-incident, complex/chronic) and approximate timeline without requiring detailed re-narration.
2. Assesses current PTSD symptom clusters using PCL-5 structure (B: re-experiencing; C: avoidance; D: negative cognitions/mood; E: hyperarousal) and, if PCL-5 was administered, incorporates the score.
3. Flags complex trauma indicators (early onset, chronic, interpersonal perpetrator, betrayal trauma, multiple types) that elevate treatment complexity.
4. Respects titration principles: gathers what is needed for clinical formulation and safety assessment without pressuring disclosure of traumatic detail the client has not offered.
5. Produces a Trauma History section ready for insertion into the 90791/90792 intake note, with a Trauma Formulation Note linking the history to diagnostic and treatment implications.

## When to Use

- At first psychiatric or therapy intake for any client where trauma history is potentially relevant — which, given base rates, means most adult intakes.
- When a PCL-5 ≥ 31 (civilian) or ≥ 38 (military) was obtained on a screening battery and the trauma history requires clinical elaboration.
- When prior records indicate trauma history but the current intake does not yet have a structured trauma section.
- When the clinician needs to assess whether to defer detailed trauma exploration to future sessions for stabilization-first treatment sequencing.
- As a standalone supplement to a biopsychosocial intake note already drafted without this module.

## Inputs / Context Required

- **Client's own disclosure:** What the client offered in response to the clinician's open-ended invitation; level of detail offered; what was not offered and appears to be withheld.
- **Trauma categories endorsed (client report):** Physical, sexual, emotional, neglect, domestic violence, community violence, natural disaster, accident/injury, medical trauma, traumatic bereavement, combat, torture, human trafficking, other.
- **Age(s) of occurrence / timeline:** Approximate ages or developmental epochs if exact dates are not known; duration for chronic/repetitive traumas.
- **Perpetrator relationship (for interpersonal traumas):** Stranger, acquaintance, family member, caregiver, intimate partner, institutional figure — without identifying the specific person unless the client offers this.
- **Disclosure history:** Whether the client has previously disclosed to anyone (therapist, family member, police, medical provider); outcome of prior disclosures if reported by client.
- **PCL-5 score** (0–80) and item-level detail if available, or PTSD symptom cluster endorsement from clinical interview.
- **Functional impact of trauma-related symptoms:** Work, relationships, daily functioning, sleep, avoidance behaviors, dissociation.
- **Prior trauma treatment:** EMDR, Prolonged Exposure, CPT, TF-CBT, other — with client's characterization of benefit.
- **Current trauma-related safety concerns:** Whether any perpetrator is currently in contact; ongoing safety threats.
- **Level of distress during this discussion:** Clinician observation of distress indicators during trauma inquiry; use of grounding by client or clinician.
- **Client's stated readiness for trauma-focused work:** Expressed interest in or reluctance toward trauma processing as a treatment component.

## Constraints

### Must

- Honor the client's chosen level of disclosure. Document the category, approximate timeline, and perpetrator relationship (if relevant) at the level the client offered — not the level a clinician might prefer for documentation completeness.
- Record distress level and pacing decisions made during the interview (e.g., "Clinician slowed inquiry after client showed visible distress; detailed narrative deferred to future session with explicit framing").
- Assess for complex trauma indicators explicitly: early onset (before age 18), chronic/repetitive, interpersonal perpetrator (especially caregiver), and betrayal trauma dimension.
- Flag current safety concerns (ongoing contact with perpetrator, threat to safety) as an action item with immediate plan.
- Note the trauma's relationship to current DSM-5-TR Criterion A event status for diagnostic purposes (Did the trauma meet Criterion A? Does the clinician need to confirm?).
- Document PCL-5 score and cluster interpretation if administered; if not administered, note which PTSD symptom clusters were endorsed in interview.
- Include a Trauma Formulation Note connecting history to diagnostic differential and treatment sequencing (e.g., stabilization-before-processing, trauma-focused modality fit).
- Use the client's own words or framing for the trauma where the client offered them; do not rename or reframe traumatic events without the client's language.

### Must Not

- Do not pressure or suggest detail beyond what the client offered, even if information gaps exist.
- Do not minimize, normalize, or contextualize the trauma in ways that could be experienced as dismissive (e.g., "many people experience this").
- Do not document detail that could re-traumatize the client if they later read their own record — chart what is clinically necessary, not a narrative re-creation.
- Do not omit the current safety assessment for ongoing threats; this is not deferrable.
- Do not assign a PTSD diagnosis from PCL-5 score alone — the Criterion A, B, C, D, E, F, G, H requirements of DSM-5-TR require clinical interview confirmation.
- Do not conflate complex PTSD (ICD-11 CPTSD) and DSM-5-TR PTSD; note both frameworks if relevant and indicate which criteria set was used.
- Do not document perpetrator names or identifying information unless legally required.
- Do not fabricate; flag gaps.

## Instructions

1. **Document the clinician's trauma inquiry approach.** Note whether a standardized screener (LEC-5, Trauma History Questionnaire, CTQ) was used or whether open clinical inquiry was employed. Document the opening question used (e.g., "I'd like to ask about difficult or overwhelming experiences that might be contributing to how you're feeling — it's fine to share only what feels comfortable").

2. **Compile trauma categories endorsed.** For each category endorsed, document: approximate age or epoch; duration (single incident, months, years); whether interpersonal or non-interpersonal; perpetrator relationship (if interpersonal, at the level the client offered); DSM-5-TR Criterion A applicability (actual or threatened death, serious injury, or sexual violence — direct experience, witnessing, indirect learning, repeated exposure as first responder).

3. **Flag complex trauma indicators.** Assess and document whether any of the following are present: onset before age 18; chronic or repetitive nature; interpersonal perpetrator; caregiver or attachment figure as perpetrator (betrayal trauma dimension); multiple trauma types co-occurring; cultural or systemic trauma (hate crimes, forced displacement, institutional violence).

4. **Compile PTSD symptom clusters.** Using PCL-5 structure:
   - Cluster B (Re-experiencing): Intrusive memories, nightmares, flashbacks, emotional reactivity to cues, physiological reactivity to cues.
   - Cluster C (Avoidance): Internal (thoughts, feelings) and external (people, places, situations) avoidance.
   - Cluster D (Negative alterations in cognitions/mood): Negative beliefs about self/others/world, blame, persistent negative affect, diminished interest, detachment, inability to experience positive emotion.
   - Cluster E (Alterations in arousal/reactivity): Hypervigilance, exaggerated startle, irritability, reckless behavior, sleep disturbance, concentration problems.
   Note which clusters are endorsed at a clinically significant level; note PCL-5 subscale scores if administered.

5. **Assess current safety.** Explicitly address whether the perpetrator (for interpersonal traumas) is currently in contact, the nature of current contact, and any ongoing threat. If threat exists, this becomes Action Item 1.

6. **Document prior trauma treatment and response.** What was tried, when, client's characterization of benefit or harm.

7. **Note pacing decisions.** Document any points where the inquiry was slowed, redirected, or deferred; the clinician's rationale; and the grounding or stabilization techniques used if any.

8. **Write the Trauma Formulation Note.** Connect the trauma history to: (a) diagnostic differential (PTSD, C-PTSD, Acute Stress Disorder, adjustment disorder, dissociative disorders, complex grief); (b) treatment sequencing decision (stabilization-first vs. can proceed to trauma processing); (c) trauma-focused modality fit (EMDR, PE, CPT, TF-CBT, DBT-PTSD); (d) trauma's role as a predisposing and perpetuating factor in other presenting problems.

9. **List immediate action items.**

10. **Run verification.**

## Verification

- [ ] Trauma categories table complete with epoch, duration, interpersonal/non-interpersonal, perpetrator relationship, and Criterion A applicability.
- [ ] Complex trauma indicators explicitly assessed (early onset, chronic, caregiver perpetrator, multiple types).
- [ ] PTSD symptom clusters documented for all four clusters (B, C, D, E) with severity estimate.
- [ ] PCL-5 score included or explicitly noted as not administered.
- [ ] Current safety assessment present — ongoing threat is never deferred.
- [ ] Pacing and distress management documented.
- [ ] Trauma Formulation Note connects history to diagnostic, sequencing, and modality decisions.
- [ ] Client's own words or framing preserved where offered; no clinician-imposed reframing.
- [ ] No traumatic narrative detail beyond what the client offered.
- [ ] No PTSD diagnosis assigned from PCL-5 alone — diagnostic criteria confirmation noted.
- [ ] Action items listed in priority order.
- [ ] Gaps flagged with `[clinician input required: ...]`.
