# Re-Intake After Lapse Protocol

## Objective

Guide and document a re-intake evaluation following a significant lapse in mental health care (typically ≥ 6 months absence, or any lapse after a high-acuity presentation) that:

1. Audits the prior record to identify what was documented, what has likely changed, and what requires full re-assessment vs. update only.
2. Covers the lapse interval: what happened, why care stopped, what changed psychiatrically and functionally.
3. Re-administers key screening instruments (PHQ-9, GAD-7, C-SSRS as minimum) and compares to prior scores for trajectory.
4. Determines whether a full 90791 re-intake is warranted or whether a comprehensive re-evaluation note (with updated sections) is sufficient.
5. Re-establishes the treatment frame and begins a new treatment plan cycle.
6. Produces a structured Re-Intake Note ready for the clinical record.

## When to Use

- Any return to care after ≥ 6 months absence from the current provider or practice.
- Return to care after any absence following high-acuity events (hospitalization, crisis, suicide attempt, major substance relapse, psychotic episode).
- When a client transfers from another provider and a substantial lapse separates the prior care from the current contact.
- When the prior note is > 12 months old regardless of stated reason for gap.
- When the client returns with materially changed circumstances (new diagnosis, major life change, new medications) that render the prior formulation substantially outdated.

## Inputs / Context Required

- **Prior record summary:** Date of last clinical contact, last diagnoses (ICD-10), last treatment plan goals, last medications, last risk stratification level, last screening instrument scores.
- **Lapse interval history:** Duration of absence from care; reason care ended (disengaged voluntarily / moved / financial / provider left / feeling better / avoidance / other); what psychiatric treatment if any occurred elsewhere during the gap.
- **Psychiatric status during lapse:** How the client fared during the interval — symptom trajectory, functional changes, major life events, crises, hospitalizations, SUD events, legal involvement.
- **Current presentation:** Chief complaint on return; what prompted re-engagement now; current symptom severity; current medications.
- **Current screening battery scores:** Re-administered PHQ-9, GAD-7, PCL-5 (if PTSD relevant), C-SSRS, AUDIT (if SUD relevant).
- **Psychosocial updates:** New or resolved stressors; changes to household, employment, relationships, legal, financial status.
- **Medical updates:** New diagnoses, new medications, hospitalizations during the lapse interval, current labs.
- **Risk history updates:** Any self-harm, suicide attempts, overdoses, or violent events during the lapse interval.

## Constraints

### Must

- Begin by auditing the prior record; document what is available and what is not. If no prior record is available (new-to-practice client claiming a lapse from a prior provider), proceed as a new intake and note the record gap.
- Apply the Re-Intake Decision Framework (see Instructions) to determine which sections require full re-assessment and which require update notation only.
- Re-administer C-SSRS at minimum; document risk continuity from prior to current contact.
- Compare current screening scores to prior scores where available; note trajectory (improved / stable / worsened / new clinical-range elevation).
- Address the lapse directly and non-punitively in the clinical framing: disengagement from care is a clinical data point, not a patient failure.
- Document a new treatment plan start or treatment plan update, with new target date for completion (typically within 1–3 sessions of the re-intake).
- Determine CPT code: full re-intake (90791/90792) is warranted when the lapse is ≥ 12 months, when the prior record does not have a complete biopsychosocial intake on file, or when clinical complexity warrants a fresh evaluation baseline.

### Must Not

- Do not simply carry forward the prior treatment plan without updating it; the re-intake must produce an updated plan with current targets.
- Do not omit the lapse interval — what occurred in the gap is clinically significant; absence from treatment does not mean absence of events.
- Do not approach the re-intake punitively or frame the lapse as non-adherence without clinical context; lapse factors are often systemic (access, cost, stigma) or illness-related (anosognosia, avoidance, symptom remission).
- Do not defer risk re-assessment to a later session if the client presents with elevated risk indicators on return.
- Do not fabricate; flag gaps.

## Instructions

1. **Prior record audit.** Retrieve the prior chart. Document: date of last contact, prior diagnoses, last medications, last risk level, prior treatment plan goals, prior screening scores. Flag any sections that are absent or outdated.

2. **Apply the Re-Intake Decision Framework.** For each intake section, determine:

   | Section | Action |
   |---------|--------|
   | Identifying Information | UPDATE: verify demographics, insurance, emergency contacts |
   | Chief Complaint | NEW: re-document in client's current words |
   | HPI | EXTEND: document lapse interval as an HPI addendum |
   | Past Psychiatric History | UPDATE: add lapse interval events |
   | Substance Use History | UPDATE: re-assess current status; re-score AUDIT/DAST if > 12 months |
   | Risk History | RE-ASSESS: C-SSRS current; update lifetime with lapse-interval events |
   | Trauma History | REVIEW: update for new events; do not re-narrate if unchanged |
   | Medical History | UPDATE: new diagnoses, medications, labs |
   | Family History | REVIEW: update for new events (bereavement, new diagnoses) |
   | Developmental History | CARRY: unchanged unless new information surfaces |
   | Social/Relational History | UPDATE: significant changes in household, relationships, employment |
   | Cultural Formulation | REVIEW: relevant if significant life changes occurred |
   | MSE | NEW: re-compile for today's contact |
   | Outcome Measures | NEW: re-administer and compare to prior |
   | Five-P Formulation | REVISE: update predisposing (stable), precipitating (lapse events), perpetuating (current), protective (current) |
   | Diagnoses | REVIEW: confirm, revise, or add based on updated picture |
   | Risk Stratification | NEW: current stratification |
   | Treatment Plan | NEW CYCLE: fresh plan with updated goals |

3. **Document the lapse interval as an HPI addendum.** What happened? Why did care end? What occurred psychiatrically during the gap? Any outside treatment? What prompted return now?

4. **Re-administer and compare screening instruments.** Present in a comparison table: prior score vs. current score, trajectory interpretation.

5. **Compile the updated MSE.** Do not carry forward the prior MSE; document today's observations.

6. **Update the Five-P Formulation.** Revise precipitating and perpetuating factors for the current episode; update protective factors.

7. **Draft updated diagnoses.** Confirm or revise ICD-10 codes based on the current picture.

8. **Complete risk stratification with current C-SSRS data.**

9. **Initiate new treatment plan cycle.** Specify new targets, modality, frequency, referrals.

10. **Run verification.**

## Verification

- [ ] Prior record audit documented with dates, prior diagnoses, medications, risk level, screening scores.
- [ ] Re-Intake Decision Framework applied — each section explicitly marked NEW / UPDATE / EXTEND / REVISE / CARRY / REVIEW.
- [ ] Lapse interval documented as HPI addendum: reason care ended, psychiatric status during gap, crises, major events, and reason for return.
- [ ] Screening instruments re-administered and compared to prior scores with trajectory interpretation.
- [ ] Risk re-assessment (C-SSRS) is NEW for today's contact — not carried from prior record.
- [ ] MSE is newly compiled for today's contact.
- [ ] Updated Five-P Formulation distinguishes stable elements from updated elements.
- [ ] Diagnoses reviewed and updated with rationale for changes.
- [ ] New treatment plan cycle initiated with updated targets and completion date.
- [ ] CPT billing code rationale documented.
- [ ] Lapse framed non-punitively in clinical documentation.
