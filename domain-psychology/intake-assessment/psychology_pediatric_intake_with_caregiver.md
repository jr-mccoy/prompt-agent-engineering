# Pediatric and Adolescent Intake with Caregiver

## Objective

Produce a complete, developmentally calibrated biopsychosocial intake record for a child or adolescent (ages 4–17) that:

1. Obtains and integrates parallel inputs from (a) the child or adolescent and (b) the caregiver(s), with explicit management of informant discrepancies.
2. Applies age-appropriate assent and consent procedures: caregiver consent for minors under 18; adolescent assent with explanation of limits of confidentiality; minor consent where state law permits (e.g., substance use, reproductive health, mental health access age).
3. Documents developmental and school functioning across academic, behavioral, and social domains.
4. Screens for trauma, abuse and neglect indicators, and mandated reporter obligations.
5. Incorporates pediatric-validated screening instruments.
6. Calibrates MSE, diagnostic impressions, and formulation to the child's developmental stage.
7. Produces a structured intake note meeting CPT 90791 / 90792 documentation requirements.

## When to Use

- At initial intake for any child or adolescent ages 4–17 presenting for outpatient psychotherapy or psychiatric evaluation.
- When caregiver is present at intake (in-person or concurrent telehealth) and the clinician needs to structure both child and caregiver interview segments.
- For re-intake after a gap in care when the child has advanced developmental stages significantly (e.g., re-intake after 2+ years; child who was 7 is now 10).
- When referral from pediatrician, school, or court requires a structured multi-informant intake record.

## When NOT to Use

- For adult clients (18+): use `psychology_intake_assessment_note.md`.
- When a child is presenting without a caregiver and caregiver interview is not possible at this encounter: document as caregiver information deferred; schedule separately.
- For forensic or custody-evaluation contexts: different protocols govern evidence handling and informant contact sequencing.

## Inputs / Context Required

- **Client demographics:** Name, DOB, age, grade level, school name, primary language, race/ethnicity (as client/family identifies), gender identity, pronouns (developmentally calibrate how these are elicited).
- **Caregiver information:** Name(s), relationship to child, legal guardianship status, custody arrangement (if applicable), contact information.
- **Referral information:** Referring provider or source, reason for referral, any referral documentation received.
- **Prior records:** Prior evaluations (psychological, neuropsychological, school), prior IEP/504 plans, prior treatment records.
- **Insurance and consent:** Caregiver consent form signed; minor assent obtained and documented per age; applicable state minor consent statutes reviewed.
- **Interview segments:** Clinician has conducted (or will document) both a caregiver-only segment and a child/adolescent interview segment.

## Constraints

### Must

- Document the child's developmental stage and calibrate all clinical language, MSE expectations, and diagnostic thresholds accordingly.
- Obtain and document legal guardian consent before the intake proceeds; document custody arrangement if parents are separated or divorced and confirm who holds legal authority to consent to mental health treatment.
- Obtain and document age-appropriate assent from children ages 7 and older; document how assent was explained and whether the child understood and agreed.
- Explain confidentiality and its limits to adolescents in age-appropriate language before the adolescent interview segment: what the clinician will share with caregivers and what will be kept private within the law.
- Screen explicitly for abuse, neglect, and safety in the child's environment; document the screen result and any mandated reporting obligation triggered.
- Document informant discrepancies explicitly: where caregiver report and child report diverge, note both data points and provide clinical weighting.
- Include school functioning as a required domain — not optional — because school is the primary functional arena for children and adolescents.
- Use pediatric-validated instruments appropriate to the child's age band; do not administer adult instruments to children under the validated age range.
- Flag all `[clinician input required]` gaps; do not fabricate developmental milestones, screening scores, or school records.

### Must Not

- Do not conduct a child MSE using adult norms without developmental calibration; note explicitly what is developmentally expected vs. clinically significant for the child's age and stage.
- Do not share the child's confidential disclosures (especially substance use, sexual behavior, self-harm) with caregivers without clinical and legal analysis — document the analysis.
- Do not omit the mandated reporter screen under any circumstances; document the screen result even when no concerns are identified.
- Do not fabricate school records, prior evaluation scores, IEP status, or developmental history.
- Do not use a single-informant report (caregiver only or child only) as sufficient for diagnosis without documenting why the second informant was unavailable.

## Age-Band Calibration Guide

The following calibrations apply across the intake. Reference the appropriate band.

| Age Band | Assent | Confidentiality Explanation | MSE Norms | Primary Instruments |
|----------|--------|----------------------------|-----------|---------------------|
| 4–6 (Preschool / Early Childhood) | Assent not yet developmentally applicable; document child's cooperation | Not applicable — full caregiver consent and report | MSE via play observation and caregiver report; limit verbal MSE to behavioral anchors | CBCL 1.5–5; Vineland-3 (caregiver form); developmental screening (ASQ-3, M-CHAT for ASD if applicable) |
| 7–11 (Middle Childhood) | Simple assent required — "Do you want to talk with me about how you're feeling?"; document child's understanding and agreement | Brief, concrete: "What you tell me stays between us unless you tell me you're being hurt or you want to hurt yourself or someone else" | MSE calibrated to concrete operational stage; assess for school-age anxiety, ADHD presentations, social functioning | CBCL 6–18 (caregiver); YSR 11–18 (self-report if ≥11); CDI-2; MASC-2; Conners-3 or SNAP-IV (ADHD); SCARED |
| 12–14 (Early Adolescence) | Assent with fuller explanation of limits of confidentiality; note emerging capacity for abstract reasoning | "What you share here is between us. I will talk with your parent(s) about general goals and progress. There are things I'm required to report: if you're being hurt, if you want to hurt yourself, or if you're planning to hurt someone else." | MSE for formal operational stage; identity and peer functioning as key domains | PHQ-A (Adolescent PHQ-9); GAD-7; SCARED; CRAFFT-2 (substance screen); Columbia C-SSRS — Adolescent; YSR 11–18 |
| 15–17 (Late Adolescence) | Full assent; review state minor consent statutes; document any applicable minor consent rights | Full explanation including minor consent rights in state: substance use, reproductive health, mental health access — document which apply | Near-adult MSE norms; identity consolidation, autonomy, intimacy as developmental tasks; risk behaviors as normative-vs-clinical distinction | PHQ-A; GAD-7; PCL-5 (if trauma history warrants; validated ≥13); CRAFFT-2; AUDIT-C; Columbia C-SSRS; YSR 11–18 |

## Instructions

1. **Pre-intake setup.** Review all available records, referral documentation, and prior evaluations. Confirm legal guardian identity and custody arrangement before the encounter. Confirm applicable state minor consent statutes.

2. **Open with joint family meeting (if applicable).** Introduce yourself, explain the structure of the intake (joint time + caregiver-only time + child/adolescent-only time), and explain confidentiality in age-appropriate terms to both the child and caregiver simultaneously.

3. **Caregiver interview segment.** Conduct the caregiver interview covering: presenting concerns, symptom onset and trajectory, developmental history, school functioning, family history, prior treatment, social history, safety concerns.

4. **Child or adolescent interview segment.** After explaining confidentiality to the child in age-appropriate terms, conduct the child interview in the absence of the caregiver (ages 7+). Calibrate interview style to developmental stage. Cover: chief complaint in child's own words, mood and worry, peer and school experience, family relationships, trauma screen (paced, age-appropriate), substance use (adolescents), safety (SI, HI, self-harm, home safety).

5. **MSE via observation.** Throughout both interview segments, conduct the MSE using age-calibrated norms. Note behavioral observations from waiting room if relevant.

6. **Mandated reporter screen.** Explicitly screen for indicators of abuse (physical, sexual, emotional), neglect, domestic violence exposure, and unsafe home environment. Document the screen result.

7. **Multi-informant integration.** Compare caregiver and child reports. Document both data points where they diverge and provide clinical weighting.

8. **Write the Pediatric Intake Note** using the output format below.

9. **Run verification.**
