# MI Decisional Balance Facilitator

## Objective

Facilitate a structured decisional balance exploration of an ambivalent change topic. Per MI-4 (Miller & Rollnick, 2023), decisional balance is appropriate when the clinician is in **equipoise** (no preferred outcome); when there is a preferred outcome (e.g., reduce harmful drinking, attend dialysis), use a **change-favoring** variant that emphasizes change-talk elicitation. Output is a structured exploration plus a summary that strengthens change talk or sustains autonomy depending on framing.

## When to Use

- Client is ambivalent about a behavior change (substance use, medication, relationship, job change, treatment).
- Pre-contemplation / contemplation stage of change.
- When the clinician has formed a hypothesis that ambivalence is the central blocker.
- Standalone or as part of an MI session.
- Not when client has already moved into planning (apply planning-stage MI techniques); not when sustain talk has been over-evoked already (shift to value-based change talk).

## Inputs / Context

- Behavior under consideration.
- Client's stage of change (Prochaska / DiClemente).
- Clinician's stance: **equipoise** vs **change-favoring**.
- Prior change-talk and sustain-talk patterns.
- Client's values from earlier work.
- Cultural and life-context constraints on change.
- Risk factors (harm reduction targets; medical urgency).
- Modality, time (15–30 min within a session).

## Constraints

### Must

- Declare the stance: equipoise or change-favoring; if change-favoring, conduct a **single-column** sustain exploration first, then a **richer** change-exploration. Standard four-quadrant decisional balance with equal time on sustain and change is appropriate only in true equipoise.
- Map four quadrants explicitly:
  - Good things about current behavior (sustain talk pulls here).
  - Less-good things about current behavior.
  - Good things about change.
  - Less-good things about change.
- Use **open questions** to elicit each quadrant; do not lead with closed yes/no.
- Use **OARS** (Open questions, Affirmations, Reflections, Summaries) throughout.
- Reflect **change talk** preferentially in summaries (DARN-CAT: Desire, Ability, Reasons, Need; Commitment, Activation, Taking steps).
- Avoid the **righting reflex** (correcting, persuading, warning).
- Conclude with a **summary** that integrates the quadrants and asks **"Where does that leave you?"** (a key question).
- Adjust direction based on client response; allow autonomy.
- For harm-reduction frames (e.g., medication, dialysis), use change-favoring variant; honor autonomy while strengthening change talk.
- Document MI-consistent vs MI-inconsistent behavior the clinician noticed in self.

### Must Not

- Do not "balance" change talk and sustain talk equally when the client is clearly at risk and the clinician is not in equipoise.
- Do not argue against sustain talk; reflect and explore.
- Do not deliver advice unless requested (and with permission).
- Do not finalize a decision in this session unless the client moves there.
- Do not allow the exercise to deflate change talk; if it does, restructure (sustain-then-change).
- Do not omit the key question ("Where does that leave you?").

## Instructions

1. Declare stance to yourself (equipoise vs change-favoring).
2. Set the focus: name the behavior with the client.
3. Sustain side first (single column if change-favoring):
   - Good things about current behavior.
   - Less-good things about current behavior.
4. Change side:
   - Good things about change.
   - Less-good things about change.
5. For each quadrant, use open questions and reflective listening; identify DARN-CAT change talk as it emerges.
6. Summarize with weighted reflection of change talk (in change-favoring stance) or balanced reflection (in equipoise).
7. Key question: "Where does that leave you?"
8. If the client moves toward change, explore planning. If not, support autonomy and revisit later.
9. Document.

## Verification

- [ ] Stance declared; rationale present.
- [ ] Four quadrants explored (with single-column sustain in change-favoring variant).
- [ ] Open questions used; OARS evident.
- [ ] DARN-CAT change talk identified verbatim.
- [ ] Summary delivered with weighted reflection per stance.
- [ ] Key question asked.
- [ ] Autonomy respected.
- [ ] No righting reflex.
- [ ] MI-adherence self-check completed.
- [ ] Risk considerations addressed when applicable.
- [ ] No fabricated client wording.
