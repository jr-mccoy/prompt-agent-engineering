# Solution-Focused Brief Therapy (SFBT) Session Structure

## Objective

Generate a Solution-Focused Brief Therapy (SFBT; Steve de Shazer & Insoo Kim Berg, Milwaukee BFTC) session structure that opens with presession change, builds well-formed goals, deploys the miracle question and scaling questions, draws out exceptions and coping, and closes with the **break** followed by an end-of-session message (compliments + bridge + task). The structure holds the **client-as-expert** stance and orients toward the **presence of solutions**, not analysis of the problem's causes.

## When to Use

- Brief, goal-directed work where the client can articulate a preferred future and identify existing strengths/resources.
- A wide range of presenting concerns (mood, adjustment, relational, behavioral goals) where solution-building is appropriate.
- First sessions (using presession change + miracle question to set well-formed goals) and follow-up sessions (using scaling + "what's better?" + exceptions).
- Telehealth: fully deliverable; scaling and the miracle question work well remotely.
- Integratable as a stance/technique set alongside other modalities.
- Not a stand-alone protocol for acute suicidality, active psychosis, or trauma requiring a trauma-focused protocol; not a substitute for risk assessment, crisis intervention, or diagnostic evaluation.

## Inputs / Context

- Presenting concern in the client's own words and what the client wants to be different.
- Presession change report (anything better between scheduling and the session).
- Current scaling baseline (0–10) on the goal, and what one point higher would look like.
- Known exceptions (times the problem was absent, smaller, or handled better) and how they happened.
- Coping resources (how the client has gotten through hard moments).
- The client's stated "miracle" picture of the preferred future, anchored in concrete, observable behavior.
- Session type (first vs. follow-up) and any prior end-of-session task and its outcome.
- Risk screen status and safety considerations.
- Cultural, developmental, and language considerations (well-formed goals must be the client's, in the client's terms).

## Constraints

### Must

- Open by eliciting **presession change** ("What's been better since you scheduled / since we last met?").
- Co-construct **well-formed goals**: stated as the **presence of something** (what the client *will* be doing), concrete, observable, small, and within the client's control — not the absence of the problem.
- Use the **miracle question** and fully **elaborate** it (who would notice first, what they would see, what the client would be doing differently) — concrete behavioral detail, not abstract feeling states.
- Use **scaling questions (0–10)** for the goal, confidence, and motivation; identify what is already in place at the current number and what one point higher looks like.
- Draw out **exception questions** (times the problem was absent or less) and amplify *how* the client made those happen.
- Use **coping questions** when the client is overwhelmed ("How have you managed to keep going?").
- Take a **break** before the end-of-session message (a brief pause to formulate feedback), consistent with the BFTC model.
- Deliver an **end-of-session message** with three parts: **compliments** (genuine, evidence-based affirmations), a **bridge** (rationale linking compliments to the task), and a **task** — typically the **first-session formula task** ("notice what's working that you want to keep happening") or **"do more of what works."**
- Hold the **client-as-expert** stance: the client defines goals and solutions; the therapist asks, amplifies, and reflects.
- Screen risk as clinically indicated; do not let solution-focus override a needed safety assessment.

### Must Not

- Do not frame goals as the absence of a problem ("stop being anxious"); reframe to the presence of a desired behavior.
- Do not pursue problem etiology / root-cause analysis / insight into "why"; SFBT builds solutions, not problem understanding.
- Do not deliver the miracle question abstractly; require concrete, observable, behavioral elaboration.
- Do not skip the break before formulating the end-of-session message.
- Do not give advice in place of compliments-bridge-task; the message is structured and strengths-based.
- Do not override risk assessment; solution-focus does not replace a safety screen when indicated.
- Do not impose the therapist's goals or interpretations over the client's stated preferred future.

## Instructions

1. **Presession change**: ask what's already better; amplify any reported change.
2. **Goal-setting**: co-construct well-formed goals (presence, concrete, small, client-controlled).
3. **Miracle question**: pose and elaborate the preferred-future picture in observable detail.
4. **Scaling**: rate the goal (0–10); identify what's already present at the current number and what +1 looks like.
5. **Exceptions**: find times the problem was absent/smaller; explore how the client made them happen.
6. **Coping** (if overwhelmed): surface how the client has been managing.
7. **Break**: pause to formulate feedback.
8. **End-of-session message**: deliver compliments + bridge + task (first-session formula task or "do more of what works").
9. Document scaling, exceptions, the message, and any risk consideration.

## Output Format

```
=== SFBT SESSION STRUCTURE / NOTE ===
Client: [Initials/MRN]    Session type: [First / Follow-up #N]    Date: [YYYY-MM-DD]
Presenting concern (client's words): [...]
Risk screen: [As indicated; SI/means; safety considerations]

PRESESSION CHANGE
- "What's been better since scheduling / last session?": [...]
- Amplification (how it happened): [...]

WELL-FORMED GOAL
- Stated as presence of: [What the client WILL be doing]
- Concrete / observable / small / client-controlled: [Y/N — refine]

MIRACLE QUESTION — ELABORATION
- Miracle picture (preferred future): [...]
- First sign the client would notice: [...]
- Who else would notice, and what they'd see: [...]
- Specific behaviors the client would be doing: [...]

SCALING SNAPSHOT (0–10)
- Goal progress today: [N/10]
- What's already in place at [N]: [...]
- One point higher ([N+1]) would look like: [...]
- Confidence to take a step: [N/10]    Motivation: [N/10]

EXCEPTIONS
- Times the problem was absent / smaller / handled better:
  1. [Exception — how the client made it happen]
  2. [...]
- Deliberate vs. spontaneous: [...]

COPING (if overwhelmed)
- How the client has kept going: [...]

--- BREAK (formulate feedback) ---

END-OF-SESSION MESSAGE
- Compliments (evidence-based): [...]
- Bridge (links compliments to task): [...]
- Task:
  - [First-session formula task: "Notice what's happening that you want to keep happening"]
  - OR ["Do more of what works": specify which exception to amplify]

DOCUMENTATION
- Note type / billing: [90834 / 90837]
- Goal movement observed: [...]
- Risk consideration: [...]
- Next session focus: ["What's better?" + scaling re-rate]
```

## Verification

- [ ] Presession change elicited and amplified.
- [ ] Goal stated as the presence of a behavior, concrete and client-controlled.
- [ ] Miracle question elaborated in observable behavioral detail.
- [ ] Scaling (0–10) captured with what's present now and what +1 looks like.
- [ ] Exception questions used and the client's agency in exceptions amplified.
- [ ] Coping questions available for overwhelm.
- [ ] Break taken before the end-of-session message.
- [ ] End-of-session message = compliments + bridge + task (formula task or "do more of what works").
- [ ] Client-as-expert stance held; no problem-etiology/root-cause analysis.
- [ ] Risk screened where clinically indicated.
- [ ] No fabricated scaling numbers, exceptions, or client content.
