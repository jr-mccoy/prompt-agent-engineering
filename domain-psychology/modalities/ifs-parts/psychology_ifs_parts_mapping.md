# IFS Parts Mapping (Managers / Firefighters / Exiles + Self)

## Objective

Map a client's internal system using Schwartz's IFS framework: identify protective **manager** parts, reactive **firefighter** parts, vulnerable **exile** parts, and the **Self**'s eight C qualities (curiosity, compassion, courage, confidence, calm, clarity, creativity, connectedness). Output is a parts diagram with each part's name (client-given), role, age, polarities, and known burdens — ready for unblending and unburdening work.

## When to Use

- Early IFS (sessions 2–5) after psychoeducation and Self-energy access.
- Reformulation when new parts emerge or when treatment is stuck.
- Adjunctive use with trauma-focused therapy as a stabilization / formulation tool.
- Couples / family therapy adapted with each person's system.
- Telehealth or in-office.
- Not for clients with severe psychosis as primary framework; not as a substitute for risk assessment.

## Inputs / Context

- Presenting concerns and recent system activations.
- Trauma / attachment history.
- Spiritual / cultural frame; some clients hold parts as metaphor, others as more literal — honor the client's frame.
- Dissociation profile (structural dissociation requires careful framing — clinician training in IFS for dissociation matters).
- Modality and time (parts-mapping typically 30–60 min).
- Prior IFS exposure.
- Whether unburdening work has been agreed to as a future direction.

## Constraints

### Must

- Use the client's **own names** for parts (e.g., "the worrier," "the planner," "the little one," "the rebel teen"). Do not impose textbook labels.
- For each part, capture: **name, age, role, what it feels / does, what it protects against, polarities with other parts, known burdens (extreme beliefs / feelings carried from past experience)**.
- Identify **managers** (proactive protectors: caretaker, perfectionist, planner, critic, pleaser, intellectualizer).
- Identify **firefighters** (reactive protectors: rage, substance, dissociator, binge, suicidal urge, self-harm).
- Identify **exiles** (vulnerable parts: shamed child, abandoned child, terrified part, lonely part).
- Identify **polarities** (e.g., critic ↔ rebellious teen; caretaker ↔ resentful).
- Assess **Self-access**: which 8 C qualities are reliably accessible, which are blended out.
- Use **unblending language**: "There's a part of you that feels …" rather than "You feel …" — track shifts in language.
- For each protector, ask its **fear**: "What would happen if you didn't do this job?" — this reveals exiles being protected.
- Do not approach exiles in this session unless protectors give permission and Self-energy is adequate.
- Document the system as a **diagram** (radial, with Self at center; protectors in inner ring; exiles in outer ring; polarities marked).
- Plan unblending homework: daily 5-minute "noticing parts" practice.

### Must Not

- Do not pathologize parts; every part has a positive intent within its frame.
- Do not bypass protectors to access exiles.
- Do not impose IFS metaphor on clients whose frame is unfit (psychosis-spectrum without training; cultural mismatch).
- Do not promise that parts will "go away"; the goal is healing burdens, not eliminating parts.
- Do not finalize the map in one session if the system reveals dissociative structures requiring IFS-for-dissociation training.
- Do not approach trauma-laden exiles without unburdening protocol and stabilization.
- Do not assign manager-style productivity homework (the manager will run the practice and exiles stay stuck).

## Instructions

1. Brief IFS psychoeducation (Self / managers / firefighters / exiles; positive-intent frame).
2. Ask the client: "What parts of you do you already notice? What do they want? What do they fear?"
3. Map each named part: name, age, role, fear, body location, polarities.
4. For each protector, ask "What would happen if you didn't do this?" — reveals exile.
5. Note exiles without engaging; mark for later unburdening.
6. Assess Self-access: which 8 C qualities show up; which are blended-out.
7. Document polarities.
8. Establish unblending language and a daily noticing practice.
9. Plan next session: protector engagement or further mapping.
10. Flag dissociation / risk considerations.

## Verification

- [ ] Client's own names used for parts.
- [ ] For each part: role, fear, body location, burden, polarities.
- [ ] Managers, firefighters, exiles distinguished.
- [ ] Protector fear questions asked (reveals exiles).
- [ ] Exiles noted but not engaged this session.
- [ ] Self-access assessed across 8 Cs.
- [ ] Unblending language installed.
- [ ] Daily noticing practice assigned.
- [ ] Dissociation / risk flagged.
- [ ] Supervision item set when warranted.
- [ ] No pathologizing of parts.
- [ ] No bypassing protectors.
- [ ] No fabricated parts.
