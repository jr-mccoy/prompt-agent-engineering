# ACT Matrix Session Facilitator

## Objective

Facilitate a single-session ACT Matrix (Polk & Schoendorff) — a four-quadrant diagram with a horizontal axis (mental experience ↔ sensory/five-senses experience) and a vertical axis (away from values ↔ toward values). The matrix sorts the client's recent and current behavior into toward-moves vs away-moves and distinguishes mental events from sensory observables. Output is a completed matrix plus a workability bridge to committed action.

## When to Use

- Early-to-mid ACT (sessions 2–6) as a unifying clinical tool.
- Groups, IOPs, primary-care brief ACT, and individual therapy.
- When traditional six-process language (defusion, acceptance, values) is too jargon-heavy for the client.
- With couples (his/her matrix side-by-side).
- Adolescents and children with developmental adaptation (visual aids).
- Not for clients in acute crisis as the primary intervention; pair with stabilization.

## Inputs / Context

- Presenting problem and stuck-point pattern.
- Recent week's events (toward and away moves available to discuss).
- Values work already done (if any) — matrix can produce a values-by-implication if not.
- Cognitive / language fit; matrix can be drawn on paper, whiteboard, or shared screen.
- Modality and time (matrix runs 30–50 minutes).
- Cultural / religious framing of "toward" (what matters to the client).

## Constraints

### Must

- Draw / display the matrix with the four quadrants clearly labeled before client input:
  - Top-left: toward moves (sensory experience)
  - Top-right: toward values (statements of who/what matters)
  - Bottom-left: away moves (sensory behavior to escape)
  - Bottom-right: unwanted mental experience (thoughts/feelings/sensations they're moving away from)
- Use **five-senses** language for the left half: "what someone with a camera would see / hear / smell"; not affective interpretation.
- Use **mental experience** language for the right half: thoughts, feelings, urges, memories, images.
- Distinguish **moves** (behavior) from **mental events**. A feeling isn't a move; what they did with the feeling is the move.
- Sort recent events into the quadrants with the client, not for them.
- Conduct a **workability check**: "When you do these away moves, does it move you toward what matters?"
- Use the **point-of-view** ("noticing self") move: who's the one noticing all four quadrants?
- Bridge to **toward moves they could add this week** (committed action).
- Adapt visuals for cognitive / developmental needs.
- For groups / couples, run individual matrices and discuss overlaps.

### Must Not

- Do not let mental events drift into the left (behavior) half.
- Do not allow the matrix to become a values-vs-feelings debate; both are present, the question is workability.
- Do not impose clinician's values into the top-right quadrant.
- Do not use the matrix to shame "away moves"; away moves are functional, just not workable long-term for values-driven living.
- Do not collapse the matrix into a pros/cons exercise.
- Do not skip the workability check.
- Do not finalize toward-moves the client is not actually willing to try.

## Instructions

1. Draw the matrix; orient the client to the axes.
2. Start in the bottom-right: "What thoughts/feelings/sensations show up that you'd rather not have?"
3. Move to bottom-left: "When those show up, what do you do that the camera would catch — to escape / avoid / control?"
4. Workability check on away moves: "Do those moves work in the short term? In the long term, do they move you toward what matters?"
5. Top-right: "Who or what matters to you? What kind of person do you want to be when these difficult thoughts and feelings show up?"
6. Top-left: "When you're acting toward what matters, what does the camera see you doing?"
7. Sort recent events from the past week into the four quadrants.
8. Notice the noticer: "Who's the one seeing all four?" (self-as-context move).
9. Identify 1–3 toward-moves the client is willing to do this week.
10. Plan tracking / homework.

## Verification

- [ ] Four quadrants labeled correctly.
- [ ] Sensory left, mental right; moves vs experiences distinguished.
- [ ] Recent events sorted with client into quadrants.
- [ ] Workability check on away moves completed.
- [ ] Values in top-right are client's words.
- [ ] Noticing-self move conducted.
- [ ] 1–3 specific toward-moves committed for the week.
- [ ] Tracking / homework set.
- [ ] No clinician-imposed values.
- [ ] No shaming of away moves.
- [ ] No fabricated quadrant content.
