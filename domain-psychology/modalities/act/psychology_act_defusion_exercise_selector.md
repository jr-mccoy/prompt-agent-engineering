# ACT Defusion Exercise Selector

## Objective

Identify a client's dominant cognitive-fusion pattern and the workability problem it produces, then select and structure a matched defusion exercise from the ACT repertoire. The output is a session plan with a single matched exercise, in-session debrief, and between-session generalization homework.

## When to Use

- Mid-ACT protocol once contextual self / mindfulness foundations are present.
- When a client is repeatedly stuck inside the literal content of a thought ("I'm a loser," "I must figure this out before I act").
- After values clarification, when committed action is blocked by a fused thought.
- Telehealth, in-office, group (adapted).
- Not when client is in acute risk and needs stabilization; not as a substitute for reality-testing in psychotic-spectrum content.

## Inputs / Context

- A specific recurring thought, image, or rule (verbatim).
- The function this thought serves (justify avoidance, control, comparison, self-attack, predict, judge, give reasons).
- The action this thought blocks or pulls (workability cost).
- Prior defusion exposure (familiar exercises and the client's reaction).
- Reading level, language, cultural fit (some exercises like "Milk, Milk, Milk" require translation choices).
- Sensory profile (silly-voice exercises can land or backfire depending on shame profile).
- Trauma history (some exercises like "thoughts as passengers on a bus" can feel intrusive if not framed carefully).
- Modality and time available (defusion runs 5–20 minutes).

## Constraints

### Must

- Identify the **fusion pattern** explicitly: rule, evaluation, comparison, reason-giving, past/future, conceptualized self.
- Identify the **workability cost**: what the client is or is not doing because of the fusion.
- Pick **one** defusion exercise matched to the pattern; do not stack three at once.
- Frame defusion as **changing relationship with the thought**, not getting rid of it; if the exercise reduces frequency that is incidental, not the goal.
- Conduct a **pre-rating**: believability (0–100) and entanglement (0–100).
- Conduct the exercise in-session with timing and procedure cues.
- **Debrief workability**: did the thought lose any of its grip on action? Did believability vs entanglement shift differently?
- Plan a **generalization homework**: when/where to practice; cue.
- For thoughts loaded with shame, trauma, or psychosis content: select a frame-neutral exercise (e.g., "thank your mind") rather than playful exercises that may invalidate.

### Must Not

- Do not "argue with" the thought (CBT cognitive restructuring); ACT defusion does not contend with content.
- Do not promise the thought will go away.
- Do not use playful exercises (silly voice, sing the thought) for trauma- or shame-loaded content without a careful frame.
- Do not stack multiple exercises in one session; one matched exercise.
- Do not use defusion as a substitute for reality testing with psychotic content (e.g., command hallucinations) — coordinate with prescriber/team.
- Do not skip the workability link to values and committed action.
- Do not let defusion become a coping technique for emotional avoidance.

## Instructions

1. Elicit the specific recurring thought verbatim and rate believability + entanglement.
2. Classify the **fusion pattern**:
   - **Rule fusion** ("I must do X before Y"): match to literalization exercises ("If I am thinking 'I have to,' I am ___").
   - **Evaluation fusion** ("I am worthless," "She is selfish"): "I'm having the evaluation that …"; "Thank your mind."
   - **Comparison fusion**: "Notice my mind comparing"; observe the comparing as a process.
   - **Reason-giving** ("I can't because…"): "List your reasons; now act anyway"; observe the reason-machine.
   - **Past/future fusion**: present-moment anchor + "thoughts as leaves on a stream."
   - **Conceptualized self** ("I'm an anxious person"): self-as-context exercises (observer self, chessboard, sky-and-weather).
3. Pick the matched exercise (see Output Format for menu).
4. Run the exercise with timing.
5. Re-rate believability and entanglement.
6. Debrief: workability check ("If your mind kept producing this thought all day but you could still do [committed action], would that be OK?").
7. Plan homework: a daily 2-minute practice + a context-cue ("When you notice this thought during meeting prep, run the exercise for 60 seconds").
8. Bridge to committed action.

## Verification

- [ ] Target thought elicited verbatim.
- [ ] Fusion pattern classified.
- [ ] Workability cost identified.
- [ ] One matched exercise selected (not stacked).
- [ ] Pre and post ratings for both believability and entanglement.
- [ ] Frame is "change relationship," not "get rid of."
- [ ] Workability debrief links to values / committed action.
- [ ] Homework specific with cue.
- [ ] Trauma / psychosis-aware exercise choice.
- [ ] No CBT-style disputation introduced.
- [ ] No fabricated client wording.
