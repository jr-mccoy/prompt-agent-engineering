# Emotion-Focused Therapy (Individual) Session — Emotion Deepening

## Objective

Structure a single session of **individual Emotion-Focused Therapy (EFT)** as developed by Leslie Greenberg and colleagues (also called process-experiential therapy) — **not** Emotionally Focused Therapy for couples (Sue Johnson). The session classifies the client's in-session emotions into Greenberg's four categories (primary adaptive, primary maladaptive, secondary reactive, instrumental), identifies the active **task markers**, selects the matching EFT intervention (empty-chair for unfinished business, two-chair for self-critical/conflict splits, focusing for an unclear felt sense, systematic evocative unfolding for problematic reactions), and moves through the emotion-coaching stages — awareness → expression → regulation → reflection → transformation ("changing emotion with emotion") — to access the underlying primary emotion and its associated need. Output is an emotion-assessment block, a marker→task map, a chair-work plan, and a transformation/meaning-making summary.

## When to Use

- Individual psychotherapy where emotional processing is the mechanism of change (depression, anxiety, complex/relational trauma, unfinished business with significant others, harsh self-criticism, unresolved grief).
- Mid-phase EFT, once a working alliance and emotional safety are established, when the client can tolerate deepening affect.
- When a clear **marker** is present in-session (a self-critical split, lingering bad feeling toward an absent other, an unclear felt sense, a puzzling overreaction).
- Not for clients outside their window of tolerance, in acute crisis, actively dissociating, or where deepening affect would destabilize without prior stabilization/resourcing; not a substitute for clinician judgment about readiness for chair work. This is individual EFT — for couples, use the couples EFT (Johnson) intake/process resource.

## Inputs / Context

- Presenting problem and EFT case formulation (core painful emotion scheme, attachment/identity themes).
- The in-session moment: what the client is feeling/saying right now and any emergent marker.
- Emotion-regulation capacity, window of tolerance, dissociation history, and prior chair-work experience.
- The significant other or self-aspect relevant to unfinished business or a split, if applicable.
- Alliance quality and the client's tolerance for experiential intensity.
- `[clinician input required: current window-of-tolerance / dissociation status and readiness to deepen affect this session]`

## Constraints

### Must

- Identify this as **individual EFT (Greenberg)** and keep interventions experiential and emotion-focused (not couples EFT, not generic CBT).
- **Classify the client's emotions** explicitly into the four categories:
  - **Primary adaptive** — the first, healthy, action-oriented response (e.g., adaptive anger at violation, sadness at loss) carrying a need.
  - **Primary maladaptive** — old, familiar, "stuck" core pain (e.g., core shame, fear/worthlessness from learning history) that no longer guides adaptively.
  - **Secondary reactive** — a feeling about a feeling that obscures the primary (e.g., anger covering hurt, anxiety covering anger).
  - **Instrumental** — emotion expressed to influence others (e.g., "crocodile tears," displayed anger to control).
- **Get under the secondary to the primary**: validate the secondary, then access the underlying primary emotion and the **need** it carries.
- **Identify the active marker** and match the EFT task:
  - **Empty-chair** for **unfinished business** (lingering unresolved feeling toward a significant other).
  - **Two-chair** for a **self-critical split** or **conflict split** (one part criticizes/coerces the other).
  - **Focusing** (Gendlin-informed) for an **unclear felt sense** / murky experience.
  - **Systematic evocative unfolding** for a **problematic reaction point** (a puzzling overreaction the client wants to understand).
- Follow the **emotion-coaching stages** in order: **awareness → expression → regulation → reflection → transformation** ("change emotion with emotion" — accessing an adaptive emotion to transform a maladaptive one).
- Work **within the window of tolerance**; titrate intensity and include regulation before deepening when needed.
- Maintain an **empathic, following, process-directive** stance (lead the process, not the content); use empathic attunement and conjecture.
- Close with **meaning-making / reflection** and consolidate the new emotional experience and any newly accessed need.

### Must Not

- Do not conflate this with couples EFT (Johnson); no de-escalation cycle / withdrawer-pursuer reframe as the frame here.
- Do not treat a **secondary** emotion as if it were the core; do not stop at "anger" when hurt or shame underlies it.
- Do not initiate chair work when the client is outside the window of tolerance, dissociating, or in acute crisis — stabilize/resource first.
- Do not interpret content over the client; follow and evoke rather than explain.
- Do not push catharsis for its own sake; intensity serves access to the primary emotion and need, then transformation.
- Do not fabricate the client's history, the significant other's words, or in-session process; mark unknowns with `[clinician input required: ...]`.

## Instructions

1. **Confirm the frame and readiness.** Individual EFT; check window of tolerance and chair-work readiness.
2. **Track the in-session emotion** and classify it: primary adaptive / primary maladaptive / secondary reactive / instrumental.
3. **Validate the secondary, reach for the primary.** Use empathic attunement to access the underlying primary emotion and the need it carries.
4. **Identify the marker** and select the matching task (empty-chair / two-chair / focusing / evocative unfolding).
5. **Stage the awareness step** — help the client contact and symbolize the emotion in words/felt sense.
6. **Stage expression and regulation** — deepen expression while titrating to stay within tolerance; add regulation/grounding if intensity rises too far.
7. **Run the chair-work or focusing plan** — set up chairs/dialogue or felt-sense focusing; track shifts (softening of the critic, accessing unmet need, self-compassion emerging).
8. **Transform** — access an adaptive emotion (e.g., adaptive anger/self-compassion) to transform the maladaptive one ("changing emotion with emotion").
9. **Reflect and make meaning** — symbolize the new experience, consolidate the need, and bridge to between-session life.
10. **Run verification.**

## Verification

- [ ] Framed as individual EFT (Greenberg), not couples EFT (Johnson).
- [ ] Emotions classified across all four categories (primary adaptive / primary maladaptive / secondary reactive / instrumental).
- [ ] Secondary validated and the underlying primary emotion + need accessed.
- [ ] Active marker identified and matched to the correct task (empty-chair / two-chair / focusing / evocative unfolding).
- [ ] Chair-work / focusing plan laid out with the shift being sought.
- [ ] Emotion-coaching stages followed (awareness → expression → regulation → reflection → transformation).
- [ ] Work kept within the window of tolerance; regulation included as needed.
- [ ] Transformation step accesses an adaptive emotion to change the maladaptive one.
- [ ] Meaning-making / consolidation and between-session bridge present.
- [ ] Readiness/dissociation and risk gated via `[clinician input required: ...]`.
- [ ] No fabricated history, significant-other words, or in-session process.
