# Narrative Therapy Externalizing Conversation Session

## Objective

Produce a session-ready plan for a Michael White & David Epston narrative-therapy **externalizing conversation**, in which the problem is linguistically and relationally separated from the person ("the person is not the problem; the problem is the problem"). The output must:

1. Name the problem as a distinct, externalized entity in the client's own language.
2. Map **relative influence** in both directions: the problem's influence on the person/relationships/life, and the person's influence on the life of the problem.
3. Surface **unique outcomes** (sparkling moments, exceptions, initiatives) where the client escaped, resisted, or contradicted the problem's account.
4. Deconstruct the dominant, problem-saturated story and the discourses that support it.
5. Build a **re-authoring bridge** toward a preferred-story development and a plan to thicken it.
6. Hold a non-pathologizing, agency-centered stance throughout.

This is a meaning-making and identity-re-authoring tool, not a risk-assessment or crisis protocol.

## When to Use

- The client describes themselves *as* the problem ("I'm an anxious person," "I'm broken") rather than relating *to* a problem.
- A single dominant, problem-saturated narrative is crowding out competing accounts of the client's life.
- The client has lost contact with their own values, skills, and acts of resistance to the problem.
- Couples / families locked into a totalizing description of one member or of "the conflict."
- Not for acute risk, active psychosis, or in-session crisis (route to a crisis / risk protocol); not a substitute for trauma-memory processing or safety planning.

## Inputs / Context

- Presenting concern in the client's own words, and any self-as-problem language ("I am ___").
- Client's preferred terms, metaphors, and cultural/linguistic context for naming experience.
- Family / relational system and who else the problem affects.
- The dominant story so far: how the problem has been described, by whom, and which discourses (gender, productivity, "normality," diagnosis) prop it up.
- Any already-mentioned exceptions, hopes, values, or moments the problem "didn't win."
- Possible outsider witnesses (people who could be invited to a definitional ceremony / re-telling).
- `[clinician input required]` — clinical judgment on pacing, cultural fit, and whether externalizing is contraindicated (e.g., where it could minimize accountability for harm to others).

## Constraints

### Must

- Output the labeled sections in order: **Problem-Saturated Story (Brief)**, **Naming and Externalizing the Problem**, **Relative Influence Map**, **Statement-of-Position Map**, **Unique Outcomes**, **Deconstruction**, **Re-Authoring Bridge**, **Thickening and Outsider Witnesses**, **Session Notes / Next Step**.
- Externalize the problem as a separate noun/entity with the client's own name for it (e.g., "the Worry," "the Anger," "Self-Doubt," "the Voice"), used grammatically as an agent acting upon the person.
- Map relative influence in BOTH directions: (a) the problem's influence on the person and their relationships/work/body/future; (b) the person's influence on the life and operations of the problem.
- Include a **statement-of-position map** stage: evaluate the effects of the problem, then ask the client to take a position and justify it ("Is this okay or not okay for you? Why?") — grounding the work in the client's values, not the clinician's.
- Surface at least the structure for eliciting **unique outcomes** (times the problem expected to win and didn't; preparations, plans, or qualities that made resistance possible) and trace them into the past and future (landscape of action and landscape of identity/meaning).
- Deconstruct the dominant story: name the discourses/expectations that recruit the client into the problem's account, without lecturing.
- Build a re-authoring bridge that links unique outcomes into an alternative, preferred story line and names how to thicken it.
- Use language that preserves the client's agency and authorship throughout.

### Must Not

- Do not collapse the person back into the problem (no "you are anxious / you are an addict"); keep the problem externalized.
- Do not pathologize, diagnose-as-identity, or use deficit/disorder language as the client's description of self.
- Do not impose the clinician's evaluation of the problem; the client takes the position (statement-of-position map).
- Do not use externalizing to minimize responsibility for abuse or harm to others — name this guardrail where relevant.
- Do not manufacture unique outcomes; elicit and verify them from the client's actual reported experience.
- Do not fabricate client quotes, history, or witnesses; mark unknowns with bracketed slots and flag missing inputs.

## Instructions

1. Briefly capture the problem-saturated story in the client's own words, including any self-as-problem statements.
2. Co-name and externalize the problem as a separate entity using the client's language; restate it grammatically as an agent.
3. Trace the problem's influence on the person and their relationships, body, work, and view of the future (first direction).
4. Trace the person's influence on the problem — moments they limited, resisted, outwitted, or starved it (second direction).
5. Run the statement-of-position map: have the client evaluate the effects and take and justify a position on the problem.
6. Elicit unique outcomes; thicken each across landscape of action (what happened, steps, skills) and landscape of identity (what it says about values, intentions, what the client gives value to).
7. Deconstruct the dominant narrative and supporting discourses.
8. Build the re-authoring bridge: weave unique outcomes into an alternative preferred story and a plan to thicken it (documents, re-tellings, outsider witnesses / definitional ceremony).
9. Capture session notes and the next-step question.
10. Run verification.

## Output Format

```
=== NARRATIVE THERAPY EXTERNALIZING CONVERSATION ===

PROBLEM-SATURATED STORY (BRIEF)
Presenting concern (client's words): "[...]"
Self-as-problem language present: [Y/N — verbatim, e.g., "I'm just a broken person"]
Who else this story affects: [...]

NAMING AND EXTERNALIZING THE PROBLEM
Externalized name (client's own): "[e.g., the Worry / Self-Doubt / the Critic / the Anger]"
Restated as agent: "When [the Problem] shows up, it tells you/does [...] to you."
Externalizing questions to use:
- "When did [the Problem] first start trying to get the upper hand?"
- "What does [the Problem] talk you into / out of?"
- "What does [the Problem] want for your life?"

RELATIVE INFLUENCE MAP
A) Influence of [the Problem] ON the person / life:
- On relationships: [...]
- On work / school / daily life: [...]
- On the body / sleep / energy: [...]
- On self-view and hopes for the future: [...]
B) Influence of the PERSON on the life of [the Problem]:
- Times the person limited / shrank / resisted [the Problem]: [...]
- Conditions under which [the Problem] loses ground: [...]
- Allies, qualities, or know-how that work against [the Problem]: [...]

STATEMENT-OF-POSITION MAP
1. Negotiate experience-near definition of [the Problem]: [...]
2. Map effects of [the Problem]: [...]
3. Client's evaluation — "Is this okay with you, or not okay?": [...]
4. Justification — "Why is that your position?" (links to values/hopes): [...]

UNIQUE OUTCOMES (sparkling moments / exceptions / initiatives)
- Moment: [Time [the Problem] expected to win and didn't]
  Landscape of action: [What happened, steps taken, who was involved]
  Landscape of identity/meaning: [What it reveals about the client's values, intentions, commitments]
- Moment: [...]
Trace forward: "Given this, what might be a next sign that [the Problem] is losing its grip?" [...]

DECONSTRUCTION
Dominant story / "truths" propping up [the Problem]: [...]
Discourses recruiting the client (e.g., productivity, normality, gendered expectation, diagnostic identity): [...]
Question to loosen them: "Where did you learn that [...]? Whose interests does that idea serve?"

RE-AUTHORING BRIDGE
Emerging preferred / alternative story line (named): "[...]"
Unique outcomes woven into it: [...]
What this says about who the client is becoming / what they give value to: [...]
Plan to thicken: [therapeutic document / letter, re-telling, ritual, evidence-gathering between sessions]

THICKENING AND OUTSIDER WITNESSES
Possible outsider witnesses / definitional-ceremony invitees: [Names, relationship]
Re-telling structure (expression → image → resonance → transport): [Plan or "[clinician input required]"]
Between-session task to gather evidence for the preferred story: [...]

SESSION NOTES / NEXT STEP
Stance check (non-pathologizing, agency preserved): [Y/N + note]
Accountability guardrail (if harm-to-others relevant): [...]
Next-step / opening question for next session: "[...]"
Missing inputs flagged: [...]

Clinician: __________________  Date: ___________
```

## Verification

- [ ] Problem is externalized as a separate, client-named entity and kept separate from the person throughout (no person-as-problem collapse).
- [ ] Relative influence mapped in BOTH directions (problem→person and person→problem).
- [ ] Statement-of-position map present: effects mapped, client takes and justifies a position grounded in their values.
- [ ] At least one unique outcome elicited and thickened across landscape of action and landscape of identity.
- [ ] Dominant story and supporting discourses deconstructed without lecturing.
- [ ] Re-authoring bridge links unique outcomes into a named preferred story with a thickening plan.
- [ ] Outsider-witness / definitional-ceremony option considered.
- [ ] Language is non-pathologizing and agency-preserving; deficit/diagnostic-identity language avoided.
- [ ] Accountability guardrail addressed where harm-to-others is in scope.
- [ ] No fabricated quotes, exceptions, or witnesses; missing inputs flagged with bracketed slots.
