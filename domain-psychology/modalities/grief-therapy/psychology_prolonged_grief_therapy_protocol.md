# Prolonged Grief Therapy (PGT) Session Protocol

## Objective

Generate a single Prolonged Grief Therapy (PGT; M. Katherine Shear — also delivered as Complicated Grief Treatment, CGT) session plan placed within the standard 16-session protocol, or a multi-session map. The plan holds the **dual focus on LOSS** (coping with and accepting the death) **and RESTORATION** (rebuilding a meaningful, purposeful life) — integrating the Dual Process Model (Stroebe & Schut) — and sequences the seven core procedures, the grief monitoring diary, aspirational-goals work, and imaginal/situational revisiting. PGT is a structured, time-limited, attachment-informed protocol — distinct from generic supportive grief counseling.

## When to Use

- DSM-5-TR **Prolonged Grief Disorder (PGD)** — persistent, intense grief with functional impairment beyond the expected timeframe (≥12 months for adults; ≥6 months for children/adolescents), distinguished from normal/integrated grief.
- Adults (and adapted versions for older adults) for whom a structured 16-session protocol is appropriate.
- Telehealth: feasible with the diary, revisiting, and aspirational-goals work delivered remotely.
- Mid-protocol sessions where revisiting and restoration work are underway.
- Not for clients in acute crisis, with active psychosis, or imminent suicide risk requiring stabilization-first (suicidality is elevated in PGD — screen each session); not a substitute for crisis intervention, bereavement support groups, or normal-grief psychoeducation alone.

## Inputs / Context

- Diagnostic confirmation of PGD vs. normal/integrated grief (e.g., PG-13 / ICG / structured assessment), with rationale for distinguishing the two.
- Deceased's identity and relationship; circumstances and timing of the death.
- Grief Intensity / grief monitoring diary data (daily ratings, highest-grief moments).
- Avoidance map: people, places, reminders, and situations the client avoids; and any "proximity-seeking" / over-engagement with reminders.
- Aspirational goals: what a meaningful life could look like; values and roles the client wants to reclaim.
- Revisiting status: imaginal revisiting (retelling the story of the death) progress; situational revisiting hierarchy.
- Connecting-with-memories material (positive and bittersweet memories; the imagined conversation).
- Risk and safety plan; suicidality screen.
- Modality and cultural / spiritual / continuing-bonds considerations.

## Constraints

### Must

- Hold the **dual focus** every session: oscillate between **LOSS-oriented** work (coping with the death) and **RESTORATION-oriented** work (rebuilding life), consistent with the Dual Process Model.
- Sequence the **seven core procedures** across the protocol: (1) understanding and accepting grief; (2) managing emotional pain (self-regulation, grief monitoring diary); (3) planning for the future / **aspirational goals**; (4) strengthening relationships (re-engaging supports); (5) telling the story of the death / **imaginal revisiting**; (6) living with reminders / **situational revisiting**; (7) connecting with memories (positive memories, imagined conversation with the deceased).
- Use the **grief monitoring diary** as the between-session tracking instrument; review it at each session.
- Conduct **imaginal revisiting** (retell the death story, eyes closed, with grief-intensity ratings and a debrief) in the designated mid-protocol sessions, then process meaning afterward.
- Build a **situational revisiting hierarchy** and assign graded steps toward avoided reminders.
- Develop and revisit **aspirational goals** throughout (restoration), not only at the end.
- **Screen suicidality at every session** (suicidality is elevated in PGD); maintain an active safety plan; include a **risk-reassessment hook**.
- Respect **continuing bonds** and cultural/spiritual framing of the relationship with the deceased.

### Must Not

- Do not deliver PGT as generic supportive grief counseling; the structured procedures, diary, revisiting, and aspirational-goals work are load-bearing.
- Do not collapse the dual focus into LOSS-only (rumination on the death) or RESTORATION-only (avoiding the grief); oscillation is the mechanism.
- Do not pathologize normal/integrated grief; confirm PGD criteria before applying the protocol.
- Do not run imaginal revisiting as exposure-for-habituation with SUDS extinction goals (that is the PE frame); PGT revisiting targets acceptance and meaning, with grief-intensity ratings and a debrief.
- Do not skip the suicidality screen or proceed without an active safety plan.
- Do not push situational revisiting steps faster than the client's hierarchy supports.

## Instructions

1. Review the grief monitoring diary; chart grief-intensity trend.
2. Screen suicidality (Columbia or comparable); confirm safety plan adequacy.
3. Confirm session number; pull the relevant core procedures from the protocol map.
4. Do **LOSS-oriented** work for the session (e.g., imaginal revisiting of the death story; processing emotional pain; connecting with memories).
5. Do **RESTORATION-oriented** work for the session (aspirational goals; situational revisiting steps; re-engaging relationships).
6. Assign between-session practice: diary, situational revisiting steps, aspirational-goal actions, memory work.
7. Document the dual-focus balance, revisiting grief-intensity ratings, restoration progress, and risk.
8. Update safety plan and flag reformulation if no movement.

## Verification

- [ ] PGD confirmed and distinguished from normal/integrated grief before applying the protocol.
- [ ] Dual focus (LOSS and RESTORATION) present every session; oscillation documented.
- [ ] Seven core procedures correctly named and sequenced.
- [ ] Grief monitoring diary reviewed and trended.
- [ ] Imaginal revisiting framed for acceptance/meaning (grief-intensity ratings + debrief), not habituation.
- [ ] Situational revisiting uses a graded hierarchy.
- [ ] Aspirational-goals work present (restoration), not deferred to the end.
- [ ] Suicidality screened every session; active safety plan in place.
- [ ] Distinguished from generic supportive grief counseling.
- [ ] Continuing bonds / cultural framing respected.
- [ ] No fabricated diary data, grief-intensity ratings, or client content.
