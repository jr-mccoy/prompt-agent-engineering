# Sensorimotor / Somatic Session Plan (Window of Tolerance)

## Objective

Produce a session-ready, body-oriented plan grounded in Pat Ogden's **Sensorimotor Psychotherapy** and Peter Levine's **Somatic Experiencing**, organized around Daniel Siegel's **Window of Tolerance**. The output must:

1. Establish the client's baseline arousal zone and the markers of hyperarousal and hypoarousal.
2. Track **sensation** (the body's narrative) as primary, distinguished from emotion and cognition (the three levels of processing).
3. Use **titration** and **pendulation** to stay within — or return to — the window of tolerance.
4. Resource and ground before and during deepening; complete **thwarted defensive responses** and incomplete actions only within the window.
5. Specify a return-to-window safety carve-out and a do-not-flood constraint.

This is a body-oriented processing and stabilization plan, not a crisis protocol or a verbatim trauma-narrative exposure.

## When to Use

- Trauma or stress responses held somatically: chronic activation, freeze/collapse, numbing, constriction, or driven mobilization.
- The client over-relies on cognitive talk and loses contact with bodily experience, or floods when approaching content top-down.
- Building bottom-up regulation capacity before or alongside narrative/meaning-based work.
- Re-establishing access to truncated fight/flight defensive responses (e.g., a protective movement that never completed).
- Not for acute risk, active suicidality, dissociative crisis, intoxication, or unstable medical conditions (route to a crisis / risk protocol); not a substitute for safety planning or trauma-memory-processing protocols where those are indicated.

## Inputs / Context

- Presenting somatic pattern (e.g., chest tightness, collapse, restlessness, numbness) and triggers.
- Client's known window-of-tolerance signs: what hyperarousal and hypoarousal look like for them.
- Existing resources (somatic, relational, environmental, imaginal) and grounding skills that work.
- Trauma history relevance at a structural level (no need for full narrative); any dissociation history.
- Contraindications / medical issues affecting breath, movement, or interoception.
- `[clinician input required]` — clinical judgment on depth, pacing, touch (if any and within scope/consent), and whether bottom-up work is indicated today.

## Constraints

### Must

- Output the labeled sections in order: **Window-of-Tolerance Baseline**, **Session Arc**, **Resourcing and Grounding**, **Sensation-Tracking Log**, **Pendulation / Titration Plan**, **Defensive-Response / Incomplete-Action Work**, **In-Session Window Shifts**, **Closing and Re-Orientation**, **Between-Session Plan**, **Session Notes**.
- Define the **three zones** explicitly: window of tolerance (optimal arousal), hyperarousal (above the window — sympathetic, fight/flight, flooding), hypoarousal (below the window — collapse, numbing, dissociation), each with this client's observable markers.
- Track the **three levels of processing** — cognitive, emotional, **sensorimotor** — and prioritize sensation ("track the body's narrative") as the entry point, naming sensation in physical descriptors (temperature, pressure, movement, tingling, density, location) rather than interpretation.
- Use **titration**: approach activation in small, tolerable amounts rather than whole-charge exposure.
- Use **pendulation**: alternate attention between activation and a resource/settled sensation so the nervous system discharges gradually and returns toward equilibrium.
- Include mindful tracking of **micro-movements** and emergent impulses, and a plan to support **completion of thwarted defensive responses** (e.g., a push, reach, or run that was interrupted) only while in the window.
- Provide a **return-to-window safety carve-out**: explicit cues for detecting departure from the window and a stepwise return (orient, ground, resource, slow) before any further processing.
- Include boundary/orienting work (e.g., establishing physical space, orienting to the room) as a resource.

### Must Not

- Do **not** flood: no large-dose activation, no pushing through escalating hyperarousal, no chasing catharsis.
- Do not deepen processing while the client is outside the window; return to the window first.
- Do not interpret sensation into story prematurely; stay with the bottom-up track until the system settles.
- Do not force movement, breath, or (if in scope) touch; all are invitations with consent and choice.
- Do not run this with acute risk, active dissociation crisis, or intoxication present — route out.
- Do not fabricate the client's history, resources, or markers; flag missing inputs with bracketed slots.

## Instructions

1. Establish the baseline zone and write this client's hyper- and hypoarousal markers.
2. Set the session arc and a felt-sense check-in; install/confirm resources and grounding first.
3. Begin sensation tracking; name sensation in physical descriptors at the sensorimotor level, distinct from emotion and cognition.
4. Titrate: introduce a small amount of activation; observe arousal direction.
5. Pendulate between activation and resource/settling; allow discharge and settling between passes.
6. Track micro-movements and emergent defensive impulses; support completion of an interrupted action only within the window.
7. Monitor in-session window shifts; if leaving the window, invoke the return-to-window carve-out before continuing.
8. Close with re-orientation, grounding, resource consolidation, and a felt-sense check-out.
9. Set a between-session plan (resources, micro-practices, what to track).
10. Run verification.

## Output Format

```
=== SENSORIMOTOR / SOMATIC SESSION PLAN (WINDOW OF TOLERANCE) ===

WINDOW-OF-TOLERANCE BASELINE
Baseline zone at start: [In window / Hyperarousal / Hypoarousal]
Hyperarousal markers (this client): [e.g., rapid breath, heat, racing thoughts, urge to flee, clenched jaw]
Window-of-tolerance markers (this client): [e.g., steady breath, present, curious, able to track]
Hypoarousal markers (this client): [e.g., heaviness, numbness, foggy, far away, slowed speech, collapse]

SESSION ARC
Intention for the session (capacity-building, not catharsis): [...]
Felt-sense check-in: "What are you noticing in your body right now?" [client report]
Depth chosen today: [Stabilization only / titrated processing / defensive-response completion] — [clinician input required]

RESOURCING AND GROUNDING
Resources confirmed (somatic / relational / environmental / imaginal): [...]
Grounding installed before deepening: [feet on floor, back support, orienting to room, longer exhale]
Boundary / orienting work: [establish personal space; orient to exits/safety; "let your eyes find something that feels okay to rest on"]

SENSATION-TRACKING LOG (the body's narrative — sensation first)
Three levels tracked:
- Cognitive (thoughts/meaning): [brief — held lightly]
- Emotional (feelings): [brief]
- Sensorimotor (sensation — PRIMARY): [physical descriptors: location / temperature / pressure / movement / density / tingling]
Sequence observed: [Sensation A → shift → Sensation B ...]
Emergent impulses / micro-movements: [e.g., slight push in hands, turn of head, urge to curl]

PENDULATION / TITRATION PLAN
Titration step (small dose of activation): [What is approached and how small]
Resource/settling anchor to pendulate to: [Specific settled sensation or resource]
Pendulation passes: [Activation ↔ resource; allow discharge/settling between; observe trembling, breath change, warmth, tears as discharge]
Stop rule: [If activation climbs toward hyperarousal or drops toward hypoarousal, pause and resource]

DEFENSIVE-RESPONSE / INCOMPLETE-ACTION WORK (within window only)
Truncated defensive response identified: [e.g., interrupted push / reach / run / protect]
Supported completion (slow, mindful, consensual): [How the movement is allowed to complete — e.g., slow push against a cushion/wall, reach, orienting turn]
Window check before/during/after: [In window? Y/N at each]

IN-SESSION WINDOW SHIFTS
Shifts observed: [Time/marker — moved to hyper/hypo and back]
RETURN-TO-WINDOW SAFETY CARVE-OUT (invoke whenever outside the window):
1. Name it: "Let's pause the processing."
2. Orient: eyes to the room, name 3 things seen/heard.
3. Ground: feet, seat, longer exhale, press into support.
4. Resource: return to a confirmed resource; co-regulate (calm prosody, pacing).
5. Slow: re-enter only if back in the window; otherwise stay in stabilization.

CLOSING AND RE-ORIENTATION
Re-orient to present time/place: [...]
Consolidate gains / new resource: [...]
Felt-sense check-out (zone at end): [In window / hyper / hypo] + [client report]

BETWEEN-SESSION PLAN
Micro-practices (small, titrated): [grounding, orienting, resource recall — short and frequent]
What to track (no pushing): [sensation noticing window; no flooding]
Return-to-window reminder for home: [...]

SESSION NOTES
Stayed within window? [Y/N + note]; any flooding? [N expected — note if occurred]
Missing inputs flagged: [...]

Clinician: __________________  Date: ___________
```

## Verification

- [ ] Three zones (window, hyperarousal, hypoarousal) defined with this client's observable markers.
- [ ] Sensation tracked as primary, with the three levels of processing (cognitive/emotional/sensorimotor) distinguished and sensation named in physical descriptors.
- [ ] Titration (small-dose approach) and pendulation (alternating activation ↔ resource with discharge/settling) both specified.
- [ ] Resourcing, grounding, and boundary/orienting work installed before deepening.
- [ ] Micro-movement tracking and consensual completion of a thwarted defensive response, done only within the window.
- [ ] Return-to-window safety carve-out present with concrete stepwise cues.
- [ ] Do-not-flood / no-catharsis-chasing constraint honored; depth choice delegated to clinician judgment.
- [ ] Closing re-orientation and felt-sense check-out with end-of-session zone recorded.
- [ ] Between-session plan uses small, titrated micro-practices, not exposure.
- [ ] No fabricated history, resources, or markers; missing inputs flagged with bracketed slots.
