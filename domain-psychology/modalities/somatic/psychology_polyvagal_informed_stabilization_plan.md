# Polyvagal-Informed Stabilization & Regulation Plan

## Objective

Build a Polyvagal-Theory-informed stabilization and regulation plan grounded in Porges's autonomic hierarchy and its clinical application by Deb Dana. The plan maps the client's three autonomic states — **ventral vagal** (social engagement / safe-and-social), **sympathetic** (mobilization / fight-flight), and **dorsal vagal** (immobilization / shutdown-collapse) — onto a personal **autonomic ladder**, identifies **neuroception** cues of safety, danger, and life-threat, distinguishes **glimmers** from triggers, and assigns per-state regulation anchors plus a daily practice plan that tones the ventral vagal system and supports state-shifting. This is a stabilization / regulation tool, not trauma-memory processing.

## When to Use

- Early-phase trauma treatment and Phase 1 stabilization (before any reprocessing).
- Clients who describe being "stuck" in shutdown, chronic anxiety/activation, or rapid state swings.
- Building regulation capacity prior to exposure, EMDR reprocessing, or sensorimotor trauma work.
- Adjunct to any modality where window-of-tolerance widening is the immediate goal.
- Telehealth or in-office; adaptable for couples (co-regulation framing).
- Not for active trauma-memory reprocessing; not a substitute for risk assessment, crisis de-escalation, or medical evaluation of autonomic/cardiac symptoms.

## Inputs / Context

- Presenting concerns and the client's described state pattern (predominant state, frequency of shifts).
- Trauma / attachment history at headline level (no detailed memory work here).
- Current safety, risk, and any dissociation profile.
- Existing supports and relationships available for co-regulation.
- Sensory preferences and contraindications (e.g., breath-holding history, vestibular issues, panic with interoception).
- Cultural/individual meaning of safety cues (eye contact, touch, prosody differ across cultures — honor the client's frame).
- Modality, session length, and the client's prior polyvagal exposure.
- `[clinician input required]` for state-shift practices that may activate (e.g., breathwork pacing) given the client's history.

## Constraints

### Must

- Frame all three states as **adaptive and protective**, not pathological — each state is the autonomic nervous system doing its survival job.
- Use the client's **own language** for each state ("the buzzing," "the fog," "the open place") alongside the technical labels.
- Build the **autonomic ladder** top-to-bottom: ventral (top, safe/social) → sympathetic (middle, mobilized) → dorsal (bottom, collapsed/shutdown).
- For each state, capture: **body cues / felt sense, behaviors, thoughts/story it tells, triggers (cues of danger), and glimmers (cues of safety) or pathways out.**
- Distinguish **neuroception** (automatic, below-awareness detection of safety/danger/life-threat) from conscious perception; name the client's reliable cues for each category.
- Distinguish **glimmers** (micro-moments of ventral/safety) from **triggers**; have the client begin noticing and collecting glimmers.
- Build a **regulation-anchor menu per state** drawing on the named pathways: breath (extended exhale), prosody/voice, orientation to the environment, social engagement (face/voice/co-regulation), and gentle movement.
- Include **both co-regulation and self-regulation** anchors; name specific safe people/relationships for co-regulation where available.
- Emphasize **toning the ventral vagal** (lengthened exhale, humming/singing, soft eye gaze, orienting, safe connection) as the system that makes safety and social engagement possible.
- Specify the **direction of travel**: from dorsal, often *up through* sympathetic mobilization before reaching ventral — plan for this rather than expecting a direct jump to calm.
- Assign a concrete **daily practice plan** with frequency and a brief glimmer-noticing log.
- Keep the plan **stabilization-only**: explicitly bound out trauma-memory content.

### Must Not

- Do not pathologize sympathetic or dorsal states or frame regulation as "fixing" a broken nervous system.
- Do not push breath-holding, intense breathwork, or interoceptive intensity that can provoke panic or destabilization; titrate and defer to `[clinician input required]`.
- Do not pair this plan with trauma-memory reprocessing in the same step.
- Do not assume eye contact, touch, or stillness are universally regulating — verify the client's neuroception of each cue.
- Do not promise the client will "stay ventral"; the goal is flexible movement and faster return, not a fixed state.
- Do not use this plan in place of crisis/risk protocols when acute risk is present.
- Do not fabricate state cues, glimmers, or practices the client did not report or agree to.

## Instructions

1. Brief polyvagal psychoeducation: three states as a protective hierarchy; neuroception; the ladder metaphor.
2. Map the **ventral vagal** state: body cues, behaviors, the story it tells, what brings the client here (glimmers/anchors).
3. Map the **sympathetic** state: activation cues, behaviors, story, triggers, and pathways that help discharge or channel mobilization.
4. Map the **dorsal vagal** state: shutdown/collapse cues, behaviors, story, triggers, and the gentle first steps that begin re-mobilization.
5. Identify **neuroception** cues per category (safety / danger / life-threat) and the client's reliable signals of each.
6. Collect **glimmers**: have the client name recent micro-moments of safety/connection and a noticing practice to gather more.
7. Build the **per-state regulation-anchor menu** (breath, prosody, orientation, movement, co-regulation), tested for fit.
8. Identify **co-regulation** resources: specific people/voices/settings that cue ventral safety.
9. Sequence **state-shifting** realistically (e.g., dorsal → sympathetic mobilization → ventral), with the first concrete step from each lower state.
10. Assign the **daily practice plan** (ventral toning + glimmer log) and set review criteria.
11. Flag risk, dissociation, or medical referral needs; mark `[clinician input required]` items.

- [ ] Client's own language captured alongside ventral/sympathetic/dorsal labels.
- [ ] Autonomic ladder built top-to-bottom (ventral → sympathetic → dorsal).
- [ ] Each state has body cues, behaviors, story, and triggers/glimmers or pathways.
- [ ] Neuroception cues distinguished across safety / danger / life-threat.
- [ ] Glimmers distinguished from triggers; glimmer-noticing practice assigned.
- [ ] Per-state regulation-anchor menu includes breath, prosody, orientation, movement.
- [ ] Both co-regulation and self-regulation anchors named.
- [ ] Ventral-vagal toning emphasized.
- [ ] State-shifting sequenced realistically (dorsal up through sympathetic).
- [ ] Daily practice plan with frequency and glimmer log assigned.
- [ ] Titration/avoidance carve-outs and return-to-safety flagged; `[clinician input required]` used.
- [ ] Stabilization scope held; no trauma-memory reprocessing included.
- [ ] Risk / dissociation / medical referral flagged.
- [ ] No fabricated states, cues, glimmers, or practices.
