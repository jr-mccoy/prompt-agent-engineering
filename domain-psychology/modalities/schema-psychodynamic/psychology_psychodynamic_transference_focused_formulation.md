# Psychodynamic Transference-Focused Psychotherapy (TFP) Formulation

## Objective

Build a Transference-Focused Psychotherapy (TFP; Kernberg, Yeomans, Clarkin) formulation: the dominant **object-relations dyads** organizing the client's experience (self-representation, other-representation, predominant affect), assessment of **identity integration** vs **identity diffusion**, **primitive defenses** in play (splitting, projective identification, omnipotent control, primitive idealization/devaluation, denial), **reality testing**, and tactical priorities (treatment frame, contracting, hierarchy of priorities in the session).

## When to Use

- Intake / first 4–6 sessions of TFP for personality-organization-level disturbance (most often borderline-level personality organization with BPD, NPD, ASPD, severe HPD, paranoid features).
- Re-formulation mid-treatment when new dyads emerge.
- Consultation / supervision presentation.
- TFP-informed psychodynamic work in other settings.
- Not appropriate as a stand-alone intervention when imminent risk is unmanaged — TFP includes a structured treatment frame, but acute risk requires stabilization concurrent with frame contracting.

## Inputs / Context

- STIPO (Structured Interview of Personality Organization) ratings if completed, or equivalent clinical assessment.
- Personality-disorder diagnoses (DSM-5-TR or AMPD).
- History of self-harm, suicidality, treatment ruptures, aggressive incidents, substance use.
- Identity-diffusion markers (inconsistent self-description across contexts; superficial / chaotic self-narrative).
- Reality-testing assessment (intact / strained under affect / impaired).
- Treatment-frame elements already discussed (frequency, fee, missed appointments, secondary gain, third-party involvement).
- Modality, session length (TFP is typically 2x/week, 45–50 min).
- Cultural / language fit.

## Constraints

### Must

- Identify the **two or three dominant object-relations dyads** organizing current experience:
  - Self-representation: [...]
  - Other-representation: [...]
  - Predominant affect: [...]
- For each dyad, note **role reversals** observed (client occupies both self and other positions at different times).
- Assess **identity integration**:
  - Stable, integrated self / other across contexts? (neurotic-level organization)
  - Inconsistent, polarized, all-good/all-bad? (borderline-level organization)
  - Reality testing strained or impaired? (psychotic-level organization → not TFP)
- Identify **primitive defenses**: splitting, projective identification, omnipotent control, primitive idealization, devaluation, denial. Provide examples.
- Specify **reality testing**: intact, strained under affect, impaired.
- Specify the **treatment frame**:
  - Frequency, fee, missed-appointment policy, between-session contact, third parties (family / prescriber / referral source), secondary gain.
  - For high-risk clients: explicit contracting around suicidality / self-harm / substance / acting out, with conditions for hospitalization or treatment termination.
- Specify **session hierarchy** for managing material:
  1. Suicidal / homicidal threats.
  2. Threats to treatment continuity.
  3. Severe acting out (in session or between sessions).
  4. Trivialization (intellectualization, silence, lying).
  5. Affect-dominant material in transference and outside.
- Identify **likely transference patterns** at this stage (idealizing, devaluing, paranoid, eroticized, contemptuous).
- Plan **interpretation strategy**: clarification → confrontation → interpretation; tact and timing.
- For NPD: account for narcissistic transferences (idealizing / mirroring / devaluing) and pacing.
- Document supervisor / consultation needs; TFP without adequate training carries risk.

### Must Not

- Do not finalize the formulation without the client's collaborative review of the treatment frame.
- Do not treat the formulation as a fixed label; dyads shift session to session.
- Do not deliver "deep" interpretations early; clarification and confrontation precede.
- Do not collude with secondary gain (e.g., disability claims contingent on staying sick).
- Do not omit the session-hierarchy plan; without it, sessions drift to defensive material.
- Do not provide TFP without adequate training and supervision.
- Do not finalize a treatment frame inconsistent with client's life realities (e.g., 2x/week when client cannot afford it) — adapt or refer.

## Instructions

1. Synthesize history, STIPO / equivalent assessment, recent sessions.
2. Identify 2–3 dominant object-relations dyads.
3. Note role reversals.
4. Rate identity integration; rate reality testing.
5. Catalogue primitive defenses with examples.
6. Specify treatment frame; contract with client.
7. Specify session hierarchy.
8. Anticipate likely transference patterns and a plan.
9. Document supervision plan.
10. Schedule reformulation review (every 8–12 sessions).
