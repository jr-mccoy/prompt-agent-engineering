# NSSI Functional Assessment and Replacement-Skill Plan

## Objective

Conduct a structured functional analysis of non-suicidal self-injury (NSSI) using a four-function frame (Nock & Prinstein), identify maintaining factors, and produce a matched replacement-skill and harm-reduction plan. The output must:

1. Operationalize the **specific NSSI behavior(s)** (method, location on body, frequency, severity, recency, history).
2. Map antecedents → behavior → consequences (ABC) for at least 2–3 recent episodes.
3. Classify function(s) using the four-function model:
   - **Automatic Negative Reinforcement** (relieves distress / numbness)
   - **Automatic Positive Reinforcement** (generates feeling / sensation)
   - **Social Negative Reinforcement** (escapes interpersonal demand)
   - **Social Positive Reinforcement** (elicits help / connection / response)
4. Identify maintaining factors at biological, psychological, social, and contextual levels.
5. Generate replacement skills matched to each identified function.
6. Build a harm-reduction plan that reduces lethality and infection while skills consolidate.
7. Integrate with safety planning and lethal-means counseling for any co-occurring SI.

## When to Use

- Adolescent or adult presenting with NSSI at intake or in ongoing therapy.
- Following an episode of self-injury, in or out of session.
- DBT or CBT-based treatment when NSSI is a target behavior.
- Pre-discharge from inpatient / IOP after NSSI-driven admission.
- Family-involved cases when parents need to understand function and response.

## Inputs / Context

- Client demographics (age, gender, pronouns).
- NSSI history: age of first episode, methods used over time, body locations, current method(s), tools used, frequency over the past month / 6 months / lifetime, most severe episode (medical lethality, sutures / hospitalization), longest interval without NSSI.
- 2–3 specific recent episodes the client can describe in detail (when, where, what happened before, during, after).
- Affective state typically preceding NSSI (numbness, dissociation, anger, shame, sadness, panic).
- Distinguishing NSSI from suicide attempts in client's framing (intent, expectation of injury vs death).
- Co-occurring SI / SA history.
- Comorbidities: depression, anxiety, PTSD, eating disorder, BPD features, substance use, autism, ADHD.
- Social context: who knows, family / partner response, school / workplace knowledge.
- Current coping repertoire and skills already learned.
- Access to means (sharp objects, lighters, etc.) and willingness to reduce access.
- Cultural / identity context relevant to NSSI (e.g., scarification practices distinct from NSSI; LGBTQ+ minority stress).

## Constraints

### Must

- Output the following labeled sections in order: **Behavior Definition**, **Episode-Level ABC Analysis**, **Function Classification (4-Function Model)**, **Maintaining Factors**, **Differentiation from SA**, **Co-Occurring Risk**, **Replacement-Skill Plan (matched to function)**, **Harm-Reduction Plan**, **Family / Support-System Plan**, **Trigger Map and Early-Warning Plan**, **Tracking Plan**, **Coordination and Documentation**, **Signatures**.
- Behavior Definition operationalizes method, location, depth/severity, tools, current frequency, longest gap.
- ABC Analysis covers 2–3 episodes with specific antecedents (cognitive, affective, situational), the behavior, and short- and long-term consequences (intra- and interpersonal).
- Function Classification can identify multiple functions; do not force a single function. Document evidence for each.
- Replacement skills are matched to function (different skills relieve numbness vs reduce shame vs solicit support).
- Harm-reduction plan addresses tool access, wound care, infection prevention, and a specific "if I'm going to use, I'll do it this way" tier — explicitly framed as transitional, not endorsement.
- Co-occurring SI must be assessed and linked to safety planning and lethal-means counseling when present. NSSI is a strong predictor of future SA risk.
- Family / support-system plan addresses how others should respond (no shaming, no surveillance escalation that increases function, supportive validation, when to escalate).
- Trigger map and early-warning plan link cues → skill choice.
- Tracking plan: diary card or equivalent with urge intensity, action, function-of-urge tag.

### Must Not

- Do not pathologize the client; functional analysis is collaborative.
- Do not promise to keep NSSI from family / partner if the client is a minor or if safety requires disclosure; document the boundary clearly.
- Do not eliminate NSSI by demanding cessation without replacement skills; this rarely works and often increases severity.
- Do not collapse NSSI and SA when the client distinguishes them; respect the client's framing while assessing both.
- Do not skip co-occurring SI assessment.
- Do not endorse any method as "safe"; harm-reduction is harm-reduction.
- Do not fabricate; flag missing inputs.

## Instructions

1. Compile behavior definition.
2. Walk 2–3 recent episodes through ABC.
3. Cross-reference each episode against the four functions; document which function(s) are evident and what evidence supports each.
4. Identify maintaining factors at biological, psychological, social, contextual levels.
5. Differentiate from SA in client's framing; assess co-occurring SI.
6. For each function identified, generate 2–4 replacement skills.
7. Build harm-reduction plan tier (tools, wound care, infection prevention, transitional "if I do" tier).
8. Build family / support-system plan.
9. Build trigger map → early-warning plan.
10. Set tracking plan (diary card or equivalent).
11. Coordinate with safety plan, lethal-means counseling, prescriber.
12. Run verification.

- [ ] Function Classification cites evidence for each function indicated.
- [ ] Maintaining factors at biological, psychological, social, contextual levels.
- [ ] NSSI vs SA differentiation present.
- [ ] Co-occurring SI assessed; safety plan and lethal-means linked.
- [ ] Replacement skills matched to each identified function (not generic).
- [ ] Harm-reduction plan addresses tool access, wound care, threshold for medical attention.
- [ ] Family / support-system plan includes do / avoid / escalation.
- [ ] Trigger map maps cues → matched skill + backup.
- [ ] Tracking plan in place.
- [ ] No promise of secrecy from family for minors when safety requires disclosure.
- [ ] No pathologizing language.
