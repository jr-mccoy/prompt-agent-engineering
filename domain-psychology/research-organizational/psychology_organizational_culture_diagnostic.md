# Organizational Culture Diagnostic

**Important Disclaimer:** This tool provides a framework for organizational culture assessment intended for organizational development professionals, I-O psychologists, HR practitioners, consultants, and organizational leaders. It does NOT provide clinical assessment, individual psychological evaluation, or guarantee organizational outcomes. Culture assessment should complement, not replace, employee feedback mechanisms and appropriate organizational governance. Implementation of culture interventions should involve qualified professionals and appropriate stakeholder engagement.

**Objective:** Apply established organizational psychology frameworks to systematically assess organizational culture and climate, identifying patterns, tensions, contradictions, and potential intervention points for organizational development initiatives.

**Instructions:**

1. **Establish assessment context and purpose**
   * Gather foundational information:
     - What is the organization type? (for-profit, nonprofit, government, healthcare, education, startup, established corporation)
     - What is the organization size? (employees, locations, divisions)
     - What is the industry context and competitive environment?
     - What prompted this culture assessment? (merger, performance issues, employee feedback, strategic change, routine assessment, leadership transition)
     - Who commissioned the assessment? What is their role and stake?
     - What decisions will this assessment inform?
     - What is the timeline and scope (whole organization, specific units, specific dimensions)?
   * Clarify assessment parameters:
     - Available data sources (surveys, interviews, observations, documents)
     - Access to different organizational levels
     - Confidentiality and anonymity considerations
     - Budget and resource constraints
   * Acknowledge limitations upfront:
     - Culture assessment is inherently interpretive
     - Point-in-time snapshot vs. dynamic reality
     - Assessor position and potential biases

2. **Select and apply culture assessment framework(s)**

   **A. Competing Values Framework (Cameron & Quinn)**
   * Assess organization along two dimensions:
     - Flexibility vs. Stability (vertical axis)
     - Internal focus vs. External focus (horizontal axis)
   * Identify quadrant emphasis:

   | Quadrant | Culture Type | Values | Leadership | Effectiveness Criteria |
   |----------|--------------|--------|------------|----------------------|
   | Clan | Collaborative | Commitment, communication, development | Facilitator, mentor, team builder | Cohesion, morale, human development |
   | Adhocracy | Creative | Innovation, transformation, agility | Innovator, entrepreneur, visionary | New outputs, creativity, cutting-edge |
   | Market | Competitive | Market share, achievement, profitability | Hard driver, competitor, producer | Market share, goal achievement, beating competitors |
   | Hierarchy | Controlling | Efficiency, consistency, uniformity | Coordinator, organizer, administrator | Efficiency, timeliness, smooth functioning |

   * Assess current state profile (percentage in each quadrant)
   * Assess preferred/desired state profile
   * Identify gaps between current and preferred states

   **B. Schein's Three Levels of Culture**
   * **Artifacts (Visible level):**
     - Physical environment, office design, dress code
     - Language, jargon, acronyms used
     - Stories, legends, myths shared
     - Rituals, ceremonies, celebrations
     - Published values and mission statements
     - Technology and tools visible
   * **Espoused Values (Stated beliefs):**
     - Official values, mission, vision
     - Stated strategies and goals
     - Philosophies articulated by leaders
     - Policies and codes of conduct
   * **Basic Underlying Assumptions (Deepest level):**
     - Beliefs about human nature
     - Beliefs about relationships (competition vs. cooperation)
     - Beliefs about truth (how it's determined)
     - Beliefs about time orientation (past, present, future)
     - Beliefs about human activity (being vs. doing)
     - Beliefs about environment (control vs. adaptation)
   * Analyze alignment and contradictions between levels

   **C. Organizational Climate Dimensions**
   * Measure climate factors:
     - **Autonomy:** Freedom to make decisions, independence
     - **Cohesion:** Team spirit, cooperation, trust among colleagues
     - **Trust:** Confidence in leadership, fairness perceptions
     - **Pressure:** Workload, time pressure, stress levels
     - **Support:** Supervisor support, resource availability, mentoring
     - **Recognition:** Acknowledgment of contributions, rewards
     - **Fairness:** Procedural justice, distributive justice, equity
     - **Innovation:** Encouragement of new ideas, tolerance for risk
     - **Clarity:** Role clarity, goal clarity, communication clarity
     - **Safety:** Psychological safety, ability to voice concerns
   * Use rating scales (1-5 or 1-7) with behavioral anchors
   * Compare across units, levels, demographics

3. **Identify culture patterns and themes**
   * Pattern Recognition Analysis:
     - What themes emerge consistently across data sources?
     - What behaviors are rewarded vs. punished (regardless of stated values)?
     - How do people describe "how things really work here"?
     - What do new employees learn quickly about surviving/thriving?
     - What topics are "undiscussable"?
     - Who has informal power and influence?
   * Consistency Analysis:
     - Where do different levels (Schein) align or conflict?
     - Where do different quadrants (CVF) show tension?
     - Where do espoused values differ from values-in-action?
     - Where do different stakeholder groups have different perceptions?
   * Strength Assessment:
     - How widely shared are cultural elements?
     - How deeply held are assumptions?
     - How resistant to change are patterns?

4. **Analyze cultural tensions and contradictions**
   * Tension Mapping:

   | Tension Type | Description | Examples | Indicators |
   |--------------|-------------|----------|------------|
   | Espoused-Enacted Gap | Stated values vs. actual behaviors | "We value work-life balance" + 60-hour weeks expected | Cynicism, disengagement, credibility loss |
   | Subculture Conflict | Different units have incompatible cultures | Sales (competitive) vs. Engineering (collaborative) | Coordination failures, us-vs-them mentality |
   | Level Discrepancy | Leadership and frontline have different cultures | Executive optimism vs. frontline frustration | Disconnection, trust erosion, feedback blockage |
   | Historical-Aspirational | Legacy culture vs. desired future state | Startup agility vs. need for structure as you scale | Growing pains, nostalgia, change resistance |
   | Strategic-Cultural | Strategy requires culture that doesn't exist | Innovation strategy + risk-averse culture | Strategy execution failures |

   * For each tension identified:
     - Describe the specific contradiction
     - Assess severity (impact on performance, engagement, retention)
     - Identify root causes
     - Note how long this tension has existed
     - Document current coping mechanisms (functional and dysfunctional)

5. **Conduct intervention point analysis**
   * Identify leverage points for culture change:

   **High Leverage Points:**
   - Leadership behavior modeling
   - Hiring and promotion criteria
   - Performance evaluation and rewards
   - Resource allocation decisions
   - Stories celebrated and shared
   - Response to crises and failures

   **Medium Leverage Points:**
   - Training and development programs
   - Communication systems and channels
   - Physical workspace design
   - Team structures and collaboration tools
   - Policies and procedures

   **Lower Leverage Points (but often attempted first):**
   - Mission/vision statement rewrites
   - Values posters and communications
   - Town halls and announcements
   - Culture workshops (without systemic change)

   * For each potential intervention:
     - Assess feasibility (resources, authority, readiness)
     - Assess potential impact (scope, depth, sustainability)
     - Identify risks and unintended consequences
     - Consider sequencing and dependencies

6. **Develop findings and recommendations framework**
   * Organize findings:
     - Key cultural strengths to preserve and leverage
     - Key cultural challenges requiring attention
     - Critical tensions requiring resolution
     - Priority intervention points
   * Recommendations structure:
     - Short-term actions (0-6 months): Quick wins, symbolic changes, communication
     - Medium-term initiatives (6-18 months): Systemic changes, process redesign
     - Long-term culture evolution (18+ months): Deep assumption shifts, sustained behavior change
   * Include measurement approach:
     - How will culture change be tracked?
     - What leading indicators suggest progress?
     - What lagging indicators confirm change?
     - Timeline for re-assessment

7. **Apply self-audit and quality verification**
   * Data quality check:
     - [ ] Multiple data sources triangulated
     - [ ] Diverse perspectives represented (levels, functions, tenure)
     - [ ] Sufficient sample for conclusions drawn
     - [ ] Potential biases acknowledged
   * Interpretation quality check:
     - [ ] Findings grounded in evidence, not assumptions
     - [ ] Alternative interpretations considered
     - [ ] Complexity and nuance preserved (not oversimplified)
     - [ ] Cultural context considered (industry, region, history)
   * Usefulness check:
     - [ ] Findings are actionable
     - [ ] Recommendations are feasible given constraints
     - [ ] Stakeholder perspectives considered
     - [ ] Ethical implications addressed
   * Communication check:
     - [ ] Findings presented with appropriate confidence levels
     - [ ] Sensitive issues handled with care
     - [ ] Report accessible to intended audience

**Psychology-Specific Considerations:**
- Culture assessment is inherently interpretive; multiple valid interpretations may exist
- Organizational culture is nested within societal culture; avoid imposing culturally-specific frameworks as universal
- Power dynamics influence what is shared in assessments; psychological safety affects data quality
- Culture change is slow and often meets resistance; unrealistic expectations harm credibility
- Assessors have their own cultural assumptions; reflexivity is essential
- Anonymous and confidential data collection increases honesty but limits follow-up
- Culture assessment can raise expectations; communicate clearly about next steps
- Consider unintended consequences of labeling and categorizing culture

**Expected Output:** A comprehensive organizational culture diagnostic containing:
1. Assessment context and methodology summary
2. Framework-based culture profile (CVF quadrants, Schein levels, climate dimensions)
3. Pattern and theme analysis with supporting evidence
4. Tension and contradiction mapping with severity ratings
5. Intervention point analysis with feasibility and impact assessment
6. Prioritized recommendations across time horizons
7. Measurement and re-assessment plan
8. Limitations and confidence level acknowledgments

## Organizational Culture Diagnostic: TechStart Inc.

**Assessment Context:**
- Mid-sized technology company (450 employees, 3 locations)
- Assessment prompted by post-merger integration challenges and engagement survey decline
- Scope: Organization-wide, with emphasis on cross-functional collaboration
- Data sources: Climate survey (78% response), 24 interviews, document review, observation

---

### 1. Competing Values Framework Profile

**Current State Assessment:**

| Quadrant | Current % | Preferred % | Gap |
|----------|-----------|-------------|-----|
| Clan (Collaborative) | 15% | 30% | +15% (increase desired) |
| Adhocracy (Creative) | 20% | 30% | +10% (increase desired) |
| Market (Competitive) | 40% | 25% | -15% (decrease desired) |
| Hierarchy (Controlling) | 25% | 15% | -10% (decrease desired) |

**Interpretation:**
- Organization is heavily weighted toward Market and Hierarchy cultures
- Employees desire more Clan (collaboration, development) and Adhocracy (innovation, flexibility)
- Gap is consistent across levels but more pronounced in engineering and product teams
- Post-merger emphasis on performance metrics has intensified Market orientation

---

### 2. Schein's Three Levels Analysis

**Artifacts Observed:**
- Open floor plan but private offices for directors+ (status signaling)
- Mission statement emphasizes innovation and collaboration (posted prominently)
- Jargon heavy in "execution," "metrics," "accountability" (competitive language)
- Few visible celebrations; quarterly awards are performance-metric focused
- Dress code relaxed but meeting culture is formal and structured

**Espoused Values:**
- "Innovation through collaboration"
- "People are our greatest asset"
- "Fail fast, learn faster"
- "Customer obsession"

**Basic Assumptions (Inferred):**
- Success comes from individual achievement and competition
- Trust must be earned through performance; new members are on probation
- Problems should be escalated to leaders for resolution
- Time is scarce; efficiency trumps relationship-building
- The external market is hostile; internal competition prepares us

**Alignment Analysis:**
| Level | Content | Alignment |
|-------|---------|-----------|
| Artifact | Competitive language, metric-focused awards | Aligned with Market culture |
| Espoused | Innovation, collaboration, people-first | Contradicts actual practices |
| Assumption | Competition, hierarchy, efficiency | Drives actual behavior |

**KEY FINDING:** Significant espoused-enacted gap. Stated values of collaboration contradict reward systems and behavioral norms that emphasize individual competition.

---

### 3. Climate Dimension Scores

| Dimension | Score (1-7) | Benchmark | Assessment |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|------------|
| Autonomy | 4.2 | 4.5 | Slightly below; micromanagement concerns |
| Cohesion | 3.4 | 5.0 | **Significantly below**; siloed teams |
| Trust | 3.8 | 4.8 | **Below**; leadership credibility concerns |
| Pressure | 5.8 | 4.2 | **Above**; unsustainable workload |
| Support | 3.9 | 4.6 | Below; resource constraints |
| Recognition | 3.5 | 4.4 | **Below**; only top performers recognized |
| Fairness | 3.6 | 4.5 | **Below**; merger integration inequity |
| Innovation | 4.0 | 4.8 | Below; risk aversion in practice |
| Clarity | 4.4 | 4.5 | Average; some role confusion post-merger |
| Psychological Safety | 3.2 | 4.5 | **Significantly below**; fear of speaking up |

**Priority Concerns:** Cohesion, Psychological Safety, and Fairness are significantly below benchmarks and most correlated with engagement decline.

---

### 4. Tension Analysis

**Tension 1: Innovation Espoused vs. Risk Aversion Enacted**
- Severity: High
- Description: "Fail fast, learn faster" is stated value, but failures are punished through performance reviews and public criticism
- Evidence: 78% of survey respondents agree "it's safer to follow proven approaches here"
- Root Cause: Post-merger pressure for predictable results; leadership fears shareholder reaction to misses
- Impact: Best ideas not surfaced; employees protect themselves; innovation theater (safe "innovations")

**Tension 2: Legacy vs. Acquired Company Subcultures**
- Severity: High
- Description: Two distinct cultures attempting to coexist; "us vs. them" mentality
- Evidence: Cross-functional collaboration ratings 2.1 points lower for cross-company teams
- Root Cause: Insufficient integration work; perceived inequity in role assignments; different working styles
- Impact: Duplicate work, coordination failures, talent attrition from acquired company

**Tension 3: People-First Espoused vs. Burnout Reality**
- Severity: High
- Description: "People are our greatest asset" contradicted by workload and work-life boundary violations
- Evidence: 65% report working evenings/weekends regularly; burnout scores elevated
- Root Cause: Headcount constraints post-merger; fear-based performance culture
- Impact: Engagement decline, retention risk, long-term productivity threat

---

### 5. Intervention Points

**High-Leverage Recommendations:**

| Intervention | Feasibility | Impact | Priority |
|--------------|-------------|--------|----------|
| Leadership behavior modeling (psychological safety) | Medium | High | 1 |
| Performance evaluation criteria revision (collaboration metrics) | Medium | High | 2 |
| Promotion criteria transparency (not just individual results) | Medium | High | 3 |
| Cross-company integration rituals and shared experiences | High | Medium-High | 4 |
| Failure-positive storytelling from leadership | High | Medium | 5 |

**Sequencing:**
1. **Immediate (0-3 months):** Leadership development on psychological safety; public commitment to collaboration
2. **Short-term (3-6 months):** Pilot revised performance criteria in two departments; integration team-building
3. **Medium-term (6-12 months):** Organization-wide performance system revision; cross-functional project incentives
4. **Long-term (12-24 months):** Sustained behavior change; culture reassessment; assumption evolution

---

### 6. Measurement Plan

**Leading Indicators (Monthly):**
- Pulse survey items on psychological safety and collaboration
- Cross-functional meeting quality ratings
- Innovation idea submission rates

**Lagging Indicators (Quarterly):**
- Employee engagement scores
- Voluntary turnover (especially from acquired company)
- Cross-company collaboration project outcomes

**Culture Reassessment:** Full diagnostic recommended at 12-month mark.

---

### 7. Limitations and Confidence

**High Confidence:**
- Climate dimension scores (strong survey response rate, consistent with interviews)
- Espoused-enacted gap in innovation (overwhelming qualitative evidence)
- Subculture tension (clearly measurable in collaboration metrics)

**Moderate Confidence:**
- Basic underlying assumptions (inferred from patterns; requires deeper inquiry)
- Root cause attributions (plausible but alternative explanations exist)

**Lower Confidence / Hypothetical:**
- Precise percentage shifts in CVF (preference data may reflect current frustration vs. true preference)
- Long-term impact predictions (organizational change is complex)

**Key Uncertainties:**
- How will leadership respond to findings?
- Is there genuine commitment to change, or will this assessment be shelved?
- Economic pressures may limit intervention feasibility

---

**Techniques Used:**
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis):** Systematic analysis using multiple frameworks (CVF, Schein's levels, climate dimensions) to build comprehensive picture
- **DS-04 (Pattern Recognition Requests):** Identification of patterns across data sources, levels, and subgroups to surface themes and contradictions
- **DT-02 (Specific Focus Areas with Examples):** Detailed enumeration of assessment dimensions with concrete indicators and behavioral examples
- **NE-06 (Self-Audit Requirements):** Built-in quality verification checklist for data quality, interpretation quality, and usefulness
