# Research Interview Protocol Designer

**Objective:** Design comprehensive qualitative research interview protocols that include appropriate question sequencing, effective probing strategies, rapport-building elements, pilot testing guidance, and warnings about common pitfalls.

**Instructions:**

1. **Gather research context and parameters**
   * Request the following information:
     - Research question(s) or aims of the study
     - Target population and sampling approach
     - Theoretical framework or approach (phenomenological, grounded theory, thematic analysis, IPA, etc.)
     - Interview format (semi-structured, unstructured, narrative)
     - Estimated interview duration
     - Data collection context (in-person, virtual, phone)
     - Any ethical considerations or sensitive topics involved
   * Clarify the level of structure desired in the protocol

2. **Design the interview structure with phased workflow**
   * **Phase 1: Opening (5-10 minutes)**
     - Welcome and introduction script
     - Consent review and recording permission
     - Demographic/background questions (if applicable)
     - Icebreaker question to establish rapport
   * **Phase 2: Core Interview (main duration)**
     - Opening question(s) that invite narrative
     - Main topic domains with key questions
     - Transition statements between domains
     - Probing question bank for each domain
   * **Phase 3: Closing (5-10 minutes)**
     - Summary and member-checking opportunity
     - Closing questions ("Is there anything else?")
     - Debrief and next steps
     - Expression of appreciation

3. **Develop question sequencing strategy**
   * Follow the funnel approach:
     - Begin with broad, open-ended questions
     - Progress to more specific, focused questions
     - Save sensitive or challenging topics for middle-to-late interview
     - End with forward-looking or empowering questions
   * Use one question at a time—avoid double-barreled questions
   * Sequence topics logically based on participant experience flow
   * Build in flexibility for participant-led tangents

4. **Create probing strategies and question bank**
   * **Clarification probes:**
     - "Can you tell me more about that?"
     - "What do you mean when you say [term]?"
     - "Could you give me an example?"
   * **Elaboration probes:**
     - "How did that make you feel?"
     - "What happened next?"
     - "What was that experience like for you?"
   * **Contrast probes:**
     - "How was that different from [previous experience]?"
     - "What would it have looked like if [alternative]?"
   * **Reflection probes:**
     - "Looking back, how do you make sense of that now?"
     - "What would you say to someone in a similar situation?"
   * **Silent probes:**
     - Allow pauses for participant reflection
     - Nod and maintain engaged body language
   * Include specific probes for each main question in the protocol

5. **Incorporate rapport-building elements**
   * **Pre-interview:**
     - Brief personal introduction (humanizing the researcher)
     - Clarify participant's preferred name/pronouns
     - Explain the purpose in accessible, non-jargon language
     - Emphasize there are no right or wrong answers
   * **During interview:**
     - Active listening indicators (verbal and non-verbal)
     - Validation statements: "That makes sense," "Thank you for sharing that"
     - Appropriate self-disclosure when it facilitates openness
     - Flexibility in question order based on participant flow
   * **Recovery strategies:**
     - If participant becomes distressed: pause, offer break, remind of right to skip
     - If off-topic: gentle redirection with acknowledgment
     - If brief responses: try alternative question framing

6. **Include pilot testing guidance**
   * Recommend piloting with 2-3 participants from similar population
   * Provide pilot evaluation questions:
     - Were questions understood as intended?
     - Did question order flow naturally?
     - Were any questions redundant or missing?
     - How long did the interview actually take?
     - Did participants seem comfortable/engaged?
   * Suggest recording reflective notes immediately after each pilot
   * Include protocol revision checklist post-pilot

7. **Warn about common pitfalls and provide mitigations**
   * **Leading questions:** Avoid "Don't you think..." or questions that suggest answers
   * **Jargon and assumptions:** Use participant-accessible language
   * **Rushing:** Allow time for reflection and elaboration
   * **Over-structure:** Balance protocol adherence with responsiveness
   * **Missing follow-up:** Probe for depth, not just breadth
   * **Recording issues:** Test equipment, have backup plan
   * **Power dynamics:** Acknowledge researcher position, create psychological safety
   * **Sensitive topics:** Have support resources available, know referral pathways

**Psychology-Specific Considerations:**
- Frame the protocol within appropriate research paradigm (positivist, constructivist, critical)
- Consider reflexivity—include researcher positioning statement guidance
- Ensure protocol aligns with IRB/ethics requirements (informed consent, confidentiality)
- For clinical populations: include appropriate safeguards and exclusion criteria
- Consider cultural and linguistic appropriateness of questions
- Plan for unexpected disclosures (abuse, suicidality, illegal activity)

**Expected Output:** A complete interview protocol document containing:
1. Introduction script and consent procedures
2. Sequenced interview questions organized by phase and domain
3. Probing question bank linked to main questions
4. Rapport-building and recovery strategies
5. Pilot testing plan and evaluation criteria
6. Pitfall warnings with prevention strategies
7. Post-interview checklist and reflection prompts

## Research Interview Protocol: Experience of Career Transition in Mid-Life Adults

**Study Aim:** Explore the lived experience of voluntary career changes for adults aged 40-55

**Theoretical Framework:** Interpretative Phenomenological Analysis (IPA)

**Format:** Semi-structured, 60-75 minutes, virtual (video call)

---

### Phase 1: Opening (5-8 minutes)

**Welcome Script:**
"Thank you so much for taking the time to speak with me today. My name is [Researcher], and I'm a [position] at [institution] studying how people experience major career changes in mid-life. I'm really interested in hearing your story and understanding your experience in your own words."

**Consent Review:**
"Before we begin, I want to make sure you're comfortable with everything. You received the consent form—do you have any questions about it? [Address questions] As a reminder, you can stop at any time, skip any questions, and everything you share will be kept confidential with a pseudonym used in any publications."

**Recording Permission:**
"I'd like to record our conversation so I can focus on listening to you rather than taking notes. Is that okay with you? [If yes] Great, I'm starting the recording now."

**Icebreaker/Context:**
"To start, could you tell me a little about yourself—what you're doing now professionally and how long you've been in this role?"

---

### Phase 2: Core Interview (50-60 minutes)

**Domain 1: The Decision to Change (15-20 min)**

*Opening Question:*
"I'd like to hear about when you decided to make this career change. Can you take me back to that time and tell me what was happening for you?"

*Probes:*
- "What was going on in your life at that point?"
- "How long had you been thinking about making a change?"
- "What was the moment when you knew you were going to do it?"
- "How did it feel to make that decision?"

*Transition:* "Thank you for sharing that. I'd now like to hear more about what the transition itself was like..."

**Domain 2: The Transition Experience (15-20 min)**

*Opening Question:*
"Tell me about the experience of actually making the transition—what was that period like for you?"

*Probes:*
- "What were the biggest challenges you faced?"
- "What supported you during that time?"
- "How did others in your life respond?"
- "Were there any surprises—things you didn't expect?"
- "What emotions stand out when you think back to that time?"

*Transition:* "I really appreciate you sharing those experiences. Now I'd like to look at where you are today..."

**Domain 3: Current Reflections and Meaning (15-20 min)**

*Opening Question:*
"Looking back now, how do you make sense of this career change in the context of your life?"

*Probes:*
- "How has this change affected your sense of who you are?"
- "What have you learned about yourself through this experience?"
- "Is there anything you would do differently?"
- "How do you think this experience might shape your future?"

---

### Phase 3: Closing (5-8 minutes)

**Summary and Member Check:**
"I've really appreciated hearing your story. If I were to summarize, it sounds like [brief summary]. Does that capture it, or is there anything you'd add or correct?"

**Catch-All Question:**
"Is there anything else about your experience that we haven't touched on that feels important to share?"

**Forward-Looking Close:**
"What advice would you give to someone who is considering a similar change?"

**Debrief:**
"Thank you so much for your time and openness. Your insights will be really valuable for this research. As mentioned, I'll be analyzing interviews over the next few months. If you're interested, I can share a summary of the findings when complete. Do you have any questions for me?"

**Expression of Appreciation:**
"Thank you again—this conversation has been really meaningful."

---

### Pilot Testing Plan

**Participants:** 2-3 adults aged 40-55 who have made a career change (can be from researcher's network)

**Evaluation Criteria:**
- [ ] Questions understood without clarification needed
- [ ] Natural flow between domains
- [ ] Interview completed within 60-75 minutes
- [ ] Participants reported feeling comfortable and heard
- [ ] Rich, descriptive data generated

**Post-Pilot Reflection Questions:**
1. Which questions generated the richest responses?
2. Where did participants seem confused or hesitant?
3. What important topics emerged that aren't in the protocol?
4. Did the sequencing feel natural or forced?
5. Were probes sufficient or did new ones emerge?

---

### Common Pitfalls Checklist

| Pitfall | Prevention Strategy |
|---------|---------------------|
| Leading questions | Review questions for neutrality; avoid "Did you feel..." |
| Rushing to next question | Count to 3 after participant stops before speaking |
| Missing depth | Use "Can you tell me more?" at least once per domain |
| Over-adherence to script | Allow participant tangents if relevant; return gently |
| Power imbalance | Use first name, sit at same level, share appropriate context |
| Distress without support | Have crisis resources ready; know when to pause or stop |

**Techniques Used:**
- **NE-01 (Single-Question Pacing):** Protocol uses one question at a time with dedicated probing before moving on
- **NE-02 (Phased Workflow Architecture):** Clear Phase 1/2/3 structure with distinct purposes and transitions
- **DT-02 (Specific Focus Areas with Examples):** Detailed domains with example probes and specific guidance
- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** Step-by-step protocol structure from opening to closing
