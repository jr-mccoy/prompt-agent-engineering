# Anxiety vs Depression vs Burnout — Self-Differentiator

## Objective

Help a client think through whether what they're experiencing is anxiety, depression, burnout, grief, or some combination. The goal is not to diagnose — only a clinician does that — but to give the client clearer language for naming what's going on and matching it to action.

## When to Use

- "I don't know what's wrong with me but something is off."
- Trying to decide whether to see a therapist, see a primary-care doc, change a job, or wait it out.
- Trying to describe what's happening to a partner, friend, or doctor.

## Inputs / Context

- What's been off, in the client's own words.
- How long it's been going on (days / weeks / months / longer).
- Sleep, appetite, energy, motivation, ability to feel pleasure.
- Worry / racing thoughts / physical anxiety symptoms.
- Recent losses, work pressure, life transitions.
- Whether things lift on weekends or vacation.
- Whether the client has been here before; what helped then.

## Constraints

### Must

- Output sections in order: **What I'm Noticing**, **Pattern Match (each condition with overlap notes)**, **The Question That Best Sorts These**, **What This Suggests**, **When to Get Professional Input**.
- For each condition (anxiety, depression, burnout, grief), give a short signature pattern + overlap notes + the diagnostic question that best discriminates.
- Acknowledge that combinations are common — burnout often co-presents with depression; anxiety often masks depression; grief looks like depression but is functionally different.
- Always include "when to get professional input" with concrete triggers (duration, severity, function, safety).

### Must Not

- Don't issue a diagnosis. Use "this looks like" language, not "you have."
- Don't dismiss with "everyone feels that way sometimes."
- Don't recommend medication.
- Don't talk the client out of seeking help.
- Don't pathologize ordinary stress responses.

## Instructions

1. Hear the client's description in their own words.
2. Walk through each pattern: signature features, what makes it that vs something else.
3. Surface the highest-discriminating question (e.g., "On a vacation week with nothing to do, do you feel better?").
4. Reflect back which patterns seem to fit best and which don't.
5. Specify when to get professional input.

## Output Format

```
=== WHAT'S GOING ON — A SORT ===

What I'm Noticing (in my own words):
- [...]
Duration: [...]
Things that make it better: [...]
Things that make it worse: [...]

Pattern Match:

Anxiety
- Signature: future-oriented; what-if loops; physical (chest, jaw, gut, sleep onset); often gets worse with rest because rest gives thinking room.
- Overlap with depression: both can disrupt sleep and concentration.
- Discriminating question: "When I imagine a future event, do I feel charged-up worry — or do I feel flat and pre-defeated?"

Depression
- Signature: anhedonia (loss of pleasure in things that used to feel good); flatness; effort cost on basic tasks; rumination on past; sometimes diurnal pattern (worse mornings).
- Overlap with burnout: both can flatten motivation. Depression flattens broadly; burnout flattens specifically toward the source.
- Discriminating question: "When I imagine doing the things I used to love, do I feel 'I just can't bring myself to' (depression) or 'I want to but don't have the bandwidth' (burnout)?"

Burnout
- Signature: chronic depletion tied to a specific demand (work, caregiving, school); cynicism about the demand; reduced sense of efficacy; lifts on real time off (and often crashes on Sunday night).
- Overlap with depression: long-term burnout can become depression.
- Discriminating question: "If I had a real two-week break with no obligations, would I bounce back?"

Grief
- Signature: tied to a loss (person, role, relationship, identity, place, future); waves rather than constant; intrusive memories; physical heaviness.
- Overlap with depression: can look identical from outside; a clinician differentiates by tying to loss and trajectory.
- Discriminating question: "When I think about what's been hard, is there a specific loss at the center?"

The Question That Best Sorts What I'm Noticing: 
[The question chosen as most discriminating for this client.]

What This Suggests:
- Looks most like: [...]
- May also be present: [...]
- Doesn't fit well: [...]

When to Get Professional Input:
- It's been ≥ 2 weeks and isn't lifting.
- It's getting in the way of work, sleep, or relationships.
- I'm having thoughts of being better off dead or hurting myself — talk to someone now (988 in the US, or my PCP / crisis line).
- I'm using more substances to cope.
- I've been here before and it got worse without help.
- I'm unsure — that's already a reason to get one consultation.
```

## Verification

- [ ] Sections present in order.
- [ ] Each pattern has signature + overlap + discriminating question.
- [ ] No diagnosis issued.
- [ ] Combination presentations honored.
- [ ] Concrete triggers for professional input.
- [ ] Suicidal ideation handled with clear next step (no medication advice, just contact).
- [ ] No dismissal or pathologizing language.
