# Panic Attack vs Heart Attack — Reasoning Through It

## Objective

Help a client think clearly about whether what they're feeling — chest tightness, racing heart, shortness of breath, dread — is more likely panic or a medical emergency, and what to do about it. The clear default when in doubt: get evaluated medically. This is not a substitute for an evaluation; it's help with the reasoning.

## When to Use

- During or right after an episode of intense chest / breath / heart symptoms.
- When trying to decide whether to call 911, drive to the ED, call a friend, or wait.
- When a client has had this before and wants to think it through again.

## Inputs / Context

- Current symptoms in body terms.
- How long they've lasted.
- What was happening just before they started.
- Personal cardiac risk factors (age, family history, hypertension, diabetes, smoking, prior cardiac events, current cardiac medications).
- History of panic attacks (yes / no / suspected).
- Whether the symptoms are familiar or new.

## Constraints

### Must

- Output sections in order: **Symptoms Right Now**, **Comparison Table (Panic vs Cardiac vs Other)**, **What This Episode Looks Like**, **Action**, **If In Doubt — Get Evaluated**.
- The Action section must always include the line: **If you have any meaningful uncertainty, treat it as cardiac and get evaluated. Panic doesn't kill; missed cardiac events can.**
- Specifically flag features that demand immediate medical evaluation regardless of panic history (crushing chest pressure, pain radiating to jaw / left arm / back, severe shortness of breath, syncope, new onset in someone with cardiac risk factors, symptoms not improving with grounding within ~20 min).
- Honor that even a known panic-prone person can have a cardiac event.

### Must Not

- Don't reassure into delay. "Probably just panic" without checking the must-evaluate features can be harmful.
- Don't offer cardiac diagnosis.
- Don't stop the client from calling 911 if they're considering it.
- Don't shame past ED visits that turned out to be panic — those visits were the right call.

## Instructions

1. Hear the symptoms in body terms.
2. Walk the comparison table.
3. Match this episode to features.
4. Recommend action with the always-include line about uncertainty.
5. End by emphasizing medical evaluation is the right move when in doubt.

## Output Format

```
=== PANIC OR HEART — A REASONING WALK-THROUGH ===

Symptoms Right Now:
- [In body terms — pressure, sharp, radiating, breath, heart rate, sweat, nausea, lightheaded, tingling, sense of doom]
- Duration so far: [...]
- Started [N min] ago
- What was happening just before: [...]

Comparison Table:

| Feature | More like panic | More like cardiac | More like other |
|---------|-----------------|-------------------|-----------------|
| Onset | Sudden, often after stress / caffeine / poor sleep / triggering thought | Sudden or gradually building; sometimes with exertion | Varies (GI, asthma, vestibular, low blood sugar) |
| Chest sensation | Tight, squeezing, sometimes sharp; usually central or shifting | Heavy pressure ("elephant"), often left or central; radiates to jaw / left arm / back / between shoulder blades | Sharp localized (musculoskeletal); burning (GERD) |
| Breath | Air hunger, hyperventilating | Shortness of breath with exertion; sometimes at rest | Wheezing (asthma) |
| Heart rate | Fast, regular, palpitations | Can be fast, slow, irregular; sometimes "skipping" | Varies |
| Trembling / tingling | Common, especially hands and around mouth (hyperventilation) | Less common | — |
| Sense of doom | Common — "I'm dying" / "going crazy" | Possible | — |
| Time course | Peaks within ~10 min, eases with grounding within ~20–30 min | Doesn't ease with grounding; may worsen | — |
| Position effect | Usually no improvement with sitting / standing | Sometimes improves with rest, worsens with exertion | Pleuritic worsens with breath; GERD worsens lying down |

What This Episode Looks Like:
- [Pattern match]
- Doesn't look like: [...]
- Worrying features (if any): [...]

Action:

If any of these are present, get medical evaluation now (911 or ED):
- Crushing chest pressure
- Pain radiating to jaw, left arm, back, between shoulder blades
- Severe shortness of breath
- Syncope (passing out) or near-syncope
- New symptoms in someone with cardiac risk factors (older age, family history, hypertension, diabetes, smoking, prior cardiac event)
- Symptoms not improving with grounding within ~20 minutes
- It feels different from prior panic attacks
- You're alone and uncertain

If this looks clearly like a known panic pattern AND none of the above are present:
- Slow exhale longer than inhale (4 in, 6–8 out) for 2 minutes
- Cold water on the face / cold pack on chest (mammalian dive reflex)
- Name 5 things you see, 4 you hear, 3 you can touch
- Reassure self: this peaks within 10 minutes and will pass; it has every other time
- Reach out to a person you've identified for these moments

If In Doubt — Get Evaluated:
**If you have any meaningful uncertainty, treat it as cardiac and get evaluated. Panic doesn't kill; missed cardiac events can.** Going to the ED for what turns out to be panic is not a failure; it's correct decision-making under uncertainty.

After This Episode:
- Tell your therapist or PCP what happened.
- If this was panic and it's recurring, this is treatable; CBT-panic protocols and / or medication are highly effective.
- If this was cardiac, follow medical guidance.
```

## Verification

- [ ] Symptoms captured in body terms.
- [ ] Comparison table present.
- [ ] Always-include uncertainty line present in Action.
- [ ] Must-evaluate features explicit and unambiguous.
- [ ] Grounding sequence given as alternative only when clearly panic AND no must-evaluate features.
- [ ] No reassurance that delays evaluation.
- [ ] No diagnosis.
- [ ] Post-episode follow-up included.
