# Hypomania Self-Check

## Objective

Help a client examine whether a recent stretch of unusually high energy, mood, productivity, or sociability might be hypomania (or, more rarely, mania), and what to do about it. Hypomania often feels good and goes unnoticed; missed hypomania is a common reason bipolar disorder gets diagnosed late.

## When to Use

- "I've been on fire lately and I want to check that this is actually fine."
- After a stretch of less sleep but feeling great.
- After someone close ("Are you okay? You seem...") names it.
- A client previously diagnosed with depression who's wondering if they've been misdiagnosed.
- Someone with family history of bipolar wondering about themselves.

## Inputs / Context

- How long the stretch has been (≥ 4 days is the typical threshold for hypomania; ≥ 7 days or hospitalization for mania).
- Sleep — hours per night and whether the client feels rested.
- Energy, motor activity, talking speed.
- Mood — elevated, irritable, expansive, both.
- Risk-taking, spending, sexual behavior, substance use changes.
- Productivity — and whether the productivity is actually finishing things vs starting many.
- Pressured speech, racing thoughts, distractibility.
- Whether others have noticed.
- Any psychosis features (paranoia, special abilities, hearing things).
- Personal and family psychiatric history.
- Substance use, including stimulants and steroids.

## Constraints

### Must

- Output sections in order: **What I've Noticed**, **DSM-Style Symptom Check (informal)**, **Duration and Function Check**, **What This Could Be (and What It Could Be Other Than Hypomania)**, **Action**, **When to Reach a Clinician Today**.
- Use a structured-but-non-diagnostic version of the DSM hypomania criteria: distinct period of elevated/irritable mood + at least 3 (or 4 if mood is irritable only) of: inflated self-esteem / decreased need for sleep / pressured speech / racing thoughts or flight of ideas / distractibility / increased goal-directed activity or psychomotor agitation / risky behavior.
- Address "could be other than hypomania": substance effects (stimulants, steroids), thyroid, post-depression rebound, bereavement-relief, structural change in life.
- For any psychosis feature, escalate immediately to clinician contact.
- For mania-level symptoms (severe impairment, psychosis, hospitalization-territory), recommend reaching prescriber or going to ED today.

### Must Not

- Don't diagnose bipolar.
- Don't recommend a specific medication.
- Don't pathologize a genuinely good week.
- Don't moralize about risk-taking that's already happened; focus on next step.
- Don't tell the client what they're feeling is "fake" if they say they feel great.

## Instructions

1. Hear the description.
2. Walk the symptom check.
3. Check duration and functional impact.
4. Generate the differential including non-hypomania possibilities.
5. Recommend action proportional to severity.
6. Specify same-day clinician contact if certain features are present.

## Output Format

```
=== HYPOMANIA SELF-CHECK ===

What I've Noticed:
- [Stretch duration: N days]
- Mood quality: [Elevated / irritable / expansive / both]
- Sleep: [hours / night, feel rested?]
- Energy / activity: [...]
- Speech / thinking: [Pressured / racing / fine]
- Behavior changes: [Spending / sexual / substances / risk-taking / driving / business / social]
- Productivity: [Finishing or just starting many things]
- What others have said: [...]
- Substances: [Including stimulants, ADHD meds, steroids]
- Personal/family psychiatric history: [...]

DSM-Style Symptom Check (informal — not a diagnostic instrument):
A distinct period of elevated / irritable / expansive mood AND increased energy / activity, lasting at least 4 days, most of the day nearly every day, AND at least 3 of the following (4 if mood is only irritable):

- [ ] Inflated self-esteem or grandiosity
- [ ] Decreased need for sleep (e.g., feels rested after 3 hours)
- [ ] More talkative than usual / pressure to keep talking
- [ ] Racing thoughts or flight of ideas
- [ ] Distractibility
- [ ] Increased goal-directed activity or psychomotor agitation
- [ ] Excessive involvement in activities with high potential for painful consequences (spending, sex, business ventures, driving)

Count: [N of 7]

Duration and Function Check:
- Has lasted [N days] (4+ days for hypomania, 7+ or hospitalization for mania).
- Others have noticed and said something: [Yes / No]
- Causes problems with work / relationships / safety: [Yes / No / Not yet but trending]
- Has involved psychotic features (paranoia, hearing/seeing things, special powers): [Yes — escalate / No]

What This Could Be:
- Consistent with hypomania: [Yes / Possibly / Doesn't match]
- Consistent with mania: [Yes / Possibly / No]
- Could also be:
  - Substance-induced (stimulants, steroids, certain medications)
  - Hyperthyroidism (would benefit from a TSH check via PCP)
  - Post-depression rebound (often a normalization, not hypomania)
  - Genuinely high-functioning week from a structural change (new job, lifted constraint, recovered sleep)
  - SSRI- or stimulant-induced activation in someone with bipolar substrate

Action:
- If 0–2 symptoms and short duration: probably a great week. Track sleep and energy for the next 2 weeks; revisit if pattern continues.
- If 3+ symptoms with duration ≥ 4 days: reach out to your clinician (therapist or prescriber) within the week. If you don't have one, this is a reason to start.
- If significant impairment, risky behavior already happening, or you're losing sleep without feeling tired: reach out today.
- If psychosis features, severe impairment, or someone close is alarmed: reach prescriber today or go to ED. Mania can escalate.

When to Reach a Clinician Today:
- Psychosis features (paranoia, hallucinations, delusional certainty)
- Sleeping ≤ 3 hours and feeling rested for several days running
- Major financial / sexual / legal decisions being made impulsively this week
- Driving recklessly
- Family or partner is alarmed and asking you to get help
- Substance use has spiked

What This Walk-Through Is Not:
A diagnosis. The MDQ (Mood Disorder Questionnaire) is a self-screen worth doing with a clinician; only a clinician can confirm bipolar spectrum.
```

## Verification

- [ ] Symptom check uses informal version of DSM hypomania criteria.
- [ ] Duration / function check explicit.
- [ ] Differential includes non-bipolar possibilities (substance, thyroid, rebound, structural).
- [ ] Psychosis features → same-day clinician contact.
- [ ] Risky-behavior present → same-day or this-week clinician contact.
- [ ] No diagnosis.
- [ ] No medication recommendation.
- [ ] Genuinely good weeks not pathologized.
- [ ] MDQ named as the standard self-screen, used with clinician.
