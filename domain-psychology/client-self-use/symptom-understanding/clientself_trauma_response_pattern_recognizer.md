# Trauma Response Pattern Recognizer

## Objective

Help a client recognize trauma-response patterns in their day-to-day experience and understand which kinds of help are matched to them. Many people recognize the event was hard but don't realize that years-later experiences are connected.

## When to Use

- "Something happened a long time ago and I think I'm fine but [pattern] keeps showing up."
- After a recent traumatic event when symptoms are starting to organize.
- Before deciding whether to start trauma-focused therapy.
- After someone (partner / friend / clinician) said "that sounds like trauma" and the client wants to think about it.

## Inputs / Context

- Whether the client wants to share what happened (not required).
- When the event(s) occurred (recent / long ago / ongoing).
- Symptoms in their own words.
- What triggers them.
- What they do to cope (including substances, work, isolation, dissociation, hypervigilance).
- Whether they've sought trauma-specific help before.

## Constraints

### Must

- Output sections in order: **What I'm Noticing**, **The Four Trauma-Response Clusters**, **My Pattern**, **Important Distinctions (PTSD vs Complex Trauma vs Acute Stress)**, **What Kinds of Help Are Matched**, **When to Reach Out Now**.
- The Four Clusters are: **Re-experiencing** (intrusive memories, nightmares, flashbacks, body memories); **Avoidance** (of reminders, places, people, thoughts); **Negative Mood / Cognition** (numbing, distorted blame, anhedonia, "I'm broken," disconnection); **Hyperarousal** (startle, sleep disturbance, irritability, hypervigilance, concentration).
- Acknowledge complex / developmental trauma which adds: difficulty with affect regulation, self-organization, relationships, and meaning.
- Name evidence-based trauma treatments: trauma-focused CBT, EMDR, prolonged exposure, cognitive processing therapy, narrative exposure, somatic experiencing, IFS-informed trauma work; for complex trauma, often a phase-based approach starting with stabilization.
- Honor that some people aren't ready to do trauma work and that stabilization-only is a legitimate choice.

### Must Not

- Don't ask for traumatic content the client hasn't offered.
- Don't recommend self-administered exposure to traumatic memories.
- Don't dismiss something as "not really trauma" because it doesn't match a stereotype.
- Don't push the client toward therapy if they're not ready; surface readiness as part of the work.
- Don't diagnose PTSD.

## Instructions

1. Receive whatever the client shares; never push for content.
2. Walk the four clusters.
3. Reflect the client's pattern.
4. Distinguish PTSD / complex trauma / acute stress.
5. Map matched treatments.
6. Specify when to reach a clinician now.

## Output Format

```
=== TRAUMA RESPONSE PATTERN — A WALK-THROUGH ===

What I'm Noticing (you don't have to say what happened):
- Symptoms in my own words: [...]
- Common triggers: [...]
- How I cope (including any way that's costing me): [...]
- When the event(s) occurred: [Recent / years ago / ongoing / unclear]

The Four Trauma-Response Clusters:

1. Re-experiencing
   - Intrusive memories that arrive uninvited
   - Nightmares, including ones that don't replay the event but match its emotional tone
   - Flashbacks (sensory, somatic, or full reliving)
   - Body memories (chest tightening, bracing, pelvic tension, jaw clamp) without conscious memory
   - Strong reaction to reminders (sound, smell, calendar date, body posture)

2. Avoidance
   - Avoiding people, places, conversations, news, certain types of media
   - Avoiding thoughts and feelings about it (including by overworking, scrolling, drinking, sleeping)
   - "I just don't go there"

3. Negative Mood and Cognition
   - Numb / blank / disconnected from people you love
   - Persistent distorted blame ("It was my fault," "I should have known")
   - "I'm broken / unsafe / unlovable" as a backdrop belief
   - Anhedonia
   - Memory gaps around the event(s)
   - Hopelessness about the future

4. Hyperarousal
   - Startled by ordinary noise
   - Hard time falling or staying asleep
   - Irritable, snappish, low fuse
   - Hypervigilant (always scanning, knowing the exits, sitting facing the door)
   - Difficulty concentrating
   - Anger surges

My Pattern:
- Re-experiencing: [Yes / No / Sometimes — examples]
- Avoidance: [Yes / No / Sometimes — examples]
- Negative mood / cognition: [Yes / No / Sometimes — examples]
- Hyperarousal: [Yes / No / Sometimes — examples]
- Functional impact: [Significantly / Some / Minimal]
- Duration: [<1 month / 1–3 months / 3+ months]

Important Distinctions:

- **Acute Stress** — many of the above in the first month after a recent traumatic event. Often resolves; sometimes doesn't.
- **PTSD** — the pattern persists past 1 month and meets criteria across the four clusters with functional impact.
- **Complex (Developmental) Trauma** — usually arising from prolonged or repeated trauma, especially in childhood or in interpersonal contexts. Adds: difficulty regulating affect, sense of self, relationships, and meaning. Treatment usually starts with stabilization (Phase 1) before processing memory directly.
- **Single-event trauma in an otherwise resourced adult** is often very treatable in 8–20 sessions of focused trauma work.

What Kinds of Help Are Matched:

For PTSD-pattern (single-event, resourced):
- Trauma-focused CBT
- EMDR (Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing)
- Prolonged Exposure (PE)
- Cognitive Processing Therapy (CPT)
- Narrative Exposure Therapy

For Complex Trauma (developmental, repeated):
- Phase-based approach: stabilization → processing → integration
- Often longer relational therapy with trauma-trained clinician
- IFS-informed trauma work
- Somatic Experiencing / Sensorimotor Psychotherapy
- DBT for affect regulation if dysregulation is high
- Sometimes med support for sleep / arousal, decided with prescriber

For Acute Stress (within first month):
- Watchful waiting plus support; many people self-resolve
- Skills for sleep, grounding, social connection
- Avoid "debriefing" (single-session retelling shortly after event has not been shown helpful and may be harmful)
- Reach out for trauma-focused help if not improving by 4–6 weeks

When to Reach Out Now:
- SI / NSSI / wanting to numb with substances escalating
- Flashbacks intense enough to lose track of present
- Dissociation that's affecting safety (driving, parenting, work)
- Functional shutdown
- Pattern lasting > 1 month with no improvement
- You don't know if you're okay

What This Walk-Through Is Not:
A diagnosis. The PCL-5 is a self-screen worth doing with a clinician.
```

## Verification

- [ ] No traumatic content elicited beyond what client offered.
- [ ] Four clusters with body-memory and somatic options included.
- [ ] PTSD vs Complex Trauma vs Acute Stress distinguished.
- [ ] Phase-based approach named for complex trauma.
- [ ] Evidence-based modalities listed by category.
- [ ] No self-exposure recommended.
- [ ] No diagnosis.
- [ ] PCL-5 named as standard self-screen used with clinician.
- [ ] Same-day reach-out triggers explicit.
