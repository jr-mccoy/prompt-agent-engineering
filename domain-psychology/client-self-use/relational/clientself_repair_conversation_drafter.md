# Repair Conversation Drafter

## Objective

Help draft a repair conversation that lands: it owns the specific thing that was yours, names impact, and offers a change — without over-owning (apologizing for things that aren't yours, or grovelling) or under-owning (a non-apology with a "but"). A good repair separates *what I did* from *what they felt* from *what's still in dispute*.

## When to Use

- You hurt someone and want to make it right.
- A fight ended badly and you want to reopen it cleanly.
- You over-apologize reflexively and want to apologize for the right thing only.
- You under-apologize / get defensive and want to drop the defense.

## Inputs / Context

- What happened, in plain sequence.
- What you actually did (the action, separate from intent).
- What you think the impact was on them.
- What you do NOT think is yours to own (the part still in dispute).
- Your pull (do you tend to over-own or under-own?).
- Whether this is safe to reopen, and whether they want contact.

## Constraints

### Must

- Output sections in order: **What's Mine vs Not Mine**, **The Repair Sentences**, **What NOT to Add**, **The Ask / Follow-Up**.
- Separate action ("I raised my voice") from impact ("you felt dismissed") from intent ("I wasn't trying to…") — and only put intent *after* ownership, never as a defense.
- Name the specific behavior, not a vague "I'm sorry you felt that way."
- Generate the actual sentences.

### Must Not

- Don't write a non-apology ("I'm sorry if you took it that way").
- Don't attach a "but" that cancels the apology.
- Don't introduce false-equivalence ("we both…") inside the repair — keep their accountability out of your apology.
- Don't over-own: don't coach the user to apologize for the other person's feelings or for things that aren't theirs.
- Don't pressure repair if the user fears for their safety or the other person has said no contact — note that and route processing to their therapist.

## Instructions

1. Sort the situation into mine / not-mine / theirs-to-own.
2. Draft repair sentences covering: the action, the impact, the change. Intent comes last and only as context, not defense.
3. List the specific add-ons to leave out (the "but," the score-keeping, the over-grovel).
4. Draft a forward-looking ask or follow-up.

## Output Format

```
=== REPAIR DRAFT ===

What's Mine vs Not Mine:
- Mine to own: [the specific action] e.g. "I cancelled at the last minute twice."
- Their experience (acknowledge, don't argue): [impact] e.g. "It left you feeling like a backup plan."
- Not mine / still in dispute (keep OUT of the apology): [the part to discuss separately, later]

The Repair Sentences:
Action:  "I [specific thing I did]. That's on me."
Impact:  "I can see it made you feel [impact]. That matters to me."
Change:  "Going forward I'm going to [concrete change]."
(Intent — only if useful, AFTER the above): "I wasn't trying to [intent], but I get that the effect was the same."

What NOT to Add:
- No "but you also…" inside the apology.
- No "I'm sorry IF…" or "sorry you feel that way."
- No listing their faults — that's a separate conversation, if at all.
- No over-grovelling or apologizing for things that aren't mine.

The Ask / Follow-Up:
"I'd like to hear how it landed for you, when you're ready."
(or, if there's a logistics piece) "Can we figure out [the practical fix] together?"

If reopening isn't safe / they've said no contact: I hold the repair, write it for myself, and bring the processing to my therapist rather than forcing contact.
```

## Verification

- [ ] Action, impact, and intent separated; intent never used as defense.
- [ ] Specific behavior named, not "if you felt."
- [ ] No "but," no false-equivalence inside the apology.
- [ ] Over-owning checked — only the user's part is owned.
- [ ] Forward-looking ask included.
- [ ] Safety / no-contact carve-out present.
