# Navigating Family Estrangement

## Objective

Support someone working through estrangement from a family member or parent — without pushing toward reconciliation OR toward cutoff. Hold all three stances neutrally (initiating distance, maintaining existing distance, attempting repair), name the trade-offs of each in the user's own situation, screen for safety, and hand the grief work to a therapist.

## When to Use

- Considering going low- or no-contact with a parent or family member.
- Already estranged and second-guessing, grieving, or facing pressure from others.
- Considering reaching back out and unsure whether/how.

## Inputs / Context

- Who, and the current state (in contact, low-contact, no-contact, considering a change).
- What's driving the distance, in plain terms.
- The stance the user is leaning toward, if any.
- Pressure from others (holidays, mutual family, "but they're your mother").
- Whether there's any history of abuse, danger, or fear.

## Constraints

### Must

- Run a **Safety Screen first**.
- Output sections in order: **Safety Screen**, **The Three Stances (held neutrally)**, **What Each Stance Costs and Protects for Me**, **What's Mine to Decide vs Others' Pressure**, **The Grief Underneath (for my therapist)**, **If/How I Set the Terms**.
- Present initiate / hold / repair as legitimate options; describe trade-offs without steering.
- Separate the user's own decision from external pressure and from the family member's wishes.

### Must Not

- Don't push reconciliation as the healthy/mature default, and don't push cutoff as the empowered default.
- Don't tell the user what their relationship "should" be.
- Don't minimize abuse or pressure contact where there's fear — route to safety resources.
- Don't try to resolve the grief here; that belongs with a therapist.

## Instructions

1. Screen for abuse/danger; if present, prioritize safety and remove any pressure toward contact.
2. Lay out the three stances neutrally.
3. Map what each stance costs and protects in the user's specific situation.
4. Separate the user's decision from others' pressure and the family member's preferences.
5. Name the grief and explicitly hand it to therapy.
6. If the user chooses a stance, help set the terms (contact level, channels, topics off-limits).

## Output Format

```
=== ESTRANGEMENT MAP ===

Safety Screen (first):
- Is there a history of abuse, or do I fear harm if I make contact? [Y/N]
- If yes: contact is not owed. Route any contact decision through safety planning.
  U.S.: National DV Hotline 1-800-799-7233; 988 for crisis; 911 if in danger now.

The Three Stances (all legitimate):
- Initiate distance: choosing to step back / reduce or end contact.
- Hold the current distance: keeping things as they are.
- Attempt repair: opening a limited, conditional door.

What Each Stance Costs and Protects for Me (in my situation):
- Initiate: protects [...]; costs [...].
- Hold: protects [...]; costs [...].
- Repair: protects [...]; costs [...].

What's Mine to Decide vs Others' Pressure:
- My actual wish (setting aside what others say): [...]
- The pressure I'm feeling from [people]: [...] — noted, but not the deciding voice.
- "They're family" is information, not an obligation that overrides my safety or limits.

The Grief Underneath (for my therapist):
- What I'm actually grieving: [the relationship I wanted / the parent I didn't have / the ambiguous loss of someone still alive].
- This is heavy and ongoing — I'll bring this part to my therapist rather than try to "solve" it here.

If / How I Set the Terms (only if I choose contact):
- Contact level: [none / occasional / limited to X].
- Channel: [...]. Topics off the table: [...].
- The one line I'd open with: "[...]"
```

## Verification

- [ ] Safety screen first; abuse/fear routes to resources, no pressure to contact.
- [ ] All three stances presented neutrally with trade-offs.
- [ ] User's decision separated from external pressure.
- [ ] Grief explicitly handed to a therapist.
- [ ] Terms-setting only offered if the user chooses contact.
- [ ] No steering toward reconciliation or cutoff.
