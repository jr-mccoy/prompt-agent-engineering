# Screening a Therapist for Cultural & Identity Fit

## Objective

Help me prepare to screen a prospective therapist for cultural and identity fit: questions I can ask, and a guide to *reading* their answers — what a thoughtful response sounds like versus a dismissive or performative one. The point is to help me trust my own read, not to hand me a "correct answer" the therapist is supposed to recite.

## When to Use

- An aspect of my identity (race, ethnicity, religion, immigration story, language, LGBTQ+, disability, body size, neurodivergence, class) is central to what I want to work on, or to feeling safe enough to talk.
- I've had a past therapist miss, minimize, or fumble an identity issue and I want to screen better.
- I want a therapist who either shares part of my background or can work skillfully across difference — and I'm trying to tell which is true for me.

## Inputs / Context

- The identity factor(s) that matter most to me in this work, in my own words.
- Whether I'm looking for shared identity, demonstrated competence across difference, or either.
- A specific worry (e.g., "I don't want to have to educate my therapist," "I need someone who won't pathologize my faith / my queerness / my culture").
- The format I'll screen in (consult call, intake, first session).

## Constraints

### Must

- Output sections in order: **What Identity Fit Means for Me**, **Screening Questions to Ask**, **How to Read the Answers (not scripts)**, **Green Signals / Caution Signals**, **Sharing Identity vs Working Across Difference**, **If the Fit Isn't There**.
- Generate open questions that invite the therapist to describe their actual practice (e.g., "How do you approach working with clients whose [identity factor] is central to their experience?") rather than yes/no loyalty tests.
- For reading answers, describe the *qualities* of a strong response (curiosity, humility, concrete examples, willingness to be corrected, naming limits) without supplying the words I should hope to hear.
- Treat both options as legitimate: a therapist who shares my identity, and one who works skillfully across difference — help me notice which I actually want.
- Include a graceful exit script for when fit isn't there, and route bigger in-session concerns to the "saying hard things to a therapist" rehearsal.

### Must Not

- Don't supply model "right answers" the therapist should give — that lets a polished performance pass.
- Don't tell me a particular identity match is required or forbidden; that's mine to decide.
- Don't stereotype any group or assume what my identity means to me.
- Don't pathologize wanting identity fit, and don't promise a perfect match exists.

## Instructions

1. Reflect back which identity factors matter most and what I'm screening for.
2. Generate open screening questions tied to those factors.
3. Describe how to read the *quality* of answers, not the content I should want.
4. Lay out green and caution signals as observable behaviors.
5. Help me weigh shared identity versus competence across difference.
6. Give a graceful exit and a handoff to in-session rehearsal for harder conversations.

## Output Format

```
=== CULTURAL & IDENTITY FIT SCREEN ===

What Identity Fit Means for Me:
- Factor(s) that matter most: [...]
- I'm screening for: [shared identity / competence across difference / either]
- My specific worry: [...]

Screening Questions to Ask:
- "How do you approach working with clients for whom [my identity factor] is central?"
- "Have you worked with people from [my community/background]? What did you learn?"
- "How do you handle it when you don't share or fully understand a client's experience?"
- "What do you do if I tell you something you said didn't land right?"
- "How do you keep from assuming my experience based on my background?"

How to Read the Answers (qualities, not scripts):
- Strong responses tend to show: curiosity over claims of expertise, concrete examples,
  comfort naming what they don't know, openness to being corrected.
- Weaker responses tend to: deflect, overclaim ("I treat everyone the same"),
  get defensive, or put the work of educating them back on me.
(These are patterns to feel for — not exact words to wait for.)

Green Signals:
- Asks what my identity means *to me* rather than assuming
- Names a limit and how they'd address it (consultation, learning, referral)
- Stays warm and non-defensive when I push

Caution Signals:
- "I don't see [race/gender/etc.], I just see the person"
- Treats my identity as the whole problem, or ignores it entirely
- Gets defensive or makes me feel like I'm being difficult for asking

Sharing Identity vs Working Across Difference:
- Shared identity can mean less translating — but isn't a guarantee of fit.
- Skilled work across difference can be excellent — the test is how they handle not-knowing.
- What I actually want here: [my read]

If the Fit Isn't There:
- Exit script: "Thank you — I don't think we're the right fit, but I appreciate your time."
- For concerns I'd want to raise *in* therapy rather than leave over,
  see the "saying hard things to a therapist" rehearsal.
```

## Verification

- [ ] Screening questions are open and invite description of actual practice, not loyalty tests.
- [ ] Answer-reading guidance describes qualities, not scripted "right answers."
- [ ] Both shared-identity and across-difference paths treated as legitimate.
- [ ] Green/caution signals are observable behaviors.
- [ ] Graceful exit script included; harder concerns routed to in-session rehearsal.
- [ ] No stereotyping, no required/forbidden match, no perfect-fit promise.
