# Personal Panic Plan Builder

## Objective

Generate a one-page personal panic plan covering: early warning, in-the-moment steps, post-attack debrief, and the medical-rule-out checkpoint. Designed to be carried, not memorized.

## When to Use

- After medical evaluation has ruled out cardiac / pulmonary cause and panic has been identified.
- After a first panic attack when the client wants a plan.
- For someone in CBT-panic protocol building scaffolding between sessions.

## Inputs / Context

- Typical body symptoms of a panic attack (which ones for this client).
- Typical settings (home, driving, work, public).
- Catastrophic thoughts that show up ("I'm dying," "I'm losing it").
- Current safety / coping behaviors that may be maintaining panic (escape, avoidance, calling someone every time).
- Whether medical rule-out has been completed.
- Substances that may amplify (caffeine, stimulants, cannabis withdrawal).

## Constraints

### Must

- Output a one-page plan with sections: **Medical Rule-Out Status**, **Early Warning Signs**, **In-the-Moment Steps (3 minutes)**, **Catastrophic Thought Reframes**, **Post-Attack Debrief**, **What I Won't Do (avoidance traps)**, **When to Get Evaluated Anyway**.
- "What I Won't Do" explicitly names the behaviors that maintain panic (avoidance of place, escape mid-attack, repeated reassurance-seeking, safety behaviors that prevent learning).
- Include a "When to Get Evaluated Anyway" — even with confirmed panic history, new or different symptoms warrant medical evaluation.

### Must Not

- Don't recommend exposure planning without therapist; that belongs in CBT-panic protocol with clinician.
- Don't dismiss medical features.
- Don't recommend medication.

## Instructions

1. Confirm medical rule-out is in place.
2. Capture the client's specific early warnings.
3. Build the 3-minute in-the-moment script.
4. Reframe catastrophic thoughts in the client's words.
5. Build the post-attack debrief.
6. List avoidance traps to break.
7. Set medical-evaluate-anyway triggers.

## Output Format

```
=== MY PANIC PLAN — ONE PAGE ===

Medical rule-out: [Completed by [provider, date] / Pending — schedule before relying on this plan]

Early Warning Signs (catch it earlier next time):
- [Body cue #1: ...]
- [Body cue #2: ...]
- [Cognitive cue: ...]
- [Situational cue: ...]

In-the-Moment Steps (3 minutes):
1. Name it: "This is panic. It will peak within 10 min and pass."
2. Slow exhale: 4 in, 6–8 out, x 10 cycles.
3. Cold or hard sensory: cold water on wrists / face; ice in hand; cold can on chest.
4. Anchor: feet to floor; name 5 things I see; 4 I hear; 3 I touch.
5. Stay where I am if safe. Do not flee the situation; that teaches my brain the situation was the threat.

Catastrophic Thought Reframes (in my words):
- "I'm dying" → [Client's reframe: e.g., "I've thought this every time, and every time it has passed. My heart is doing what it does in panic; if I had a real cardiac issue, the steps would not change it."]
- "I'm losing it / going crazy" → [...]
- "Everyone can tell" → [...]
- "I have to leave" → [...]

Post-Attack Debrief:
- It peaked at: [Min ___]
- It ended by: [Min ___]
- I stayed in the situation: [Yes / No — if no, why]
- I did the steps: [All / partial / none]
- What I learned: [...]
- What I'll do differently next time: [...]

What I Won't Do (avoidance traps that keep panic alive):
- [ ] I won't avoid [the place / person / activity] from now on
- [ ] I won't escape the next attack mid-way
- [ ] I won't ask [person] for repeated reassurance after; one debrief is enough
- [ ] I won't rely on [safety behavior — water bottle, exit-tracking, phone in hand] that prevents me from learning the situation is safe

When to Get Evaluated Anyway (medical):
- New chest pain that's different in quality
- Pain radiating to jaw / left arm / back
- Severe shortness of breath at rest
- Syncope or near-syncope
- Symptoms not resolving with the steps within ~20 minutes
- It feels different from prior panic attacks
```

## Verification

- [ ] Medical rule-out status documented.
- [ ] Specific (not generic) early warnings.
- [ ] 3-minute in-the-moment steps.
- [ ] Reframes in client's own words.
- [ ] Post-attack debrief structure.
- [ ] Avoidance traps explicit.
- [ ] Medical-evaluate-anyway triggers.
- [ ] No exposure planning beyond therapist guidance.
