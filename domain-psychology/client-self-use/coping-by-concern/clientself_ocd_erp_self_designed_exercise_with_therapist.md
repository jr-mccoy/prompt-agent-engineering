# OCD ERP Exercise — Drafting With Your Therapist

## Objective

Help a client *prepare* an ERP exposure to **bring to their OCD-trained clinician**. ERP is the gold-standard OCD treatment but requires specific calibration: too easy = no learning; too hard = sensitization and dropout; uncovered compulsions = no learning regardless of exposure.

## When to Use

- Active CBT/ERP for OCD; client wants to come prepared.
- Working a hierarchy and ready to design the next exposure.
- Stuck and wanting to think through why the current exposure isn't working.

## When Not to Use

- Without an OCD-trained clinician — solo ERP for severe OCD often goes wrong; find ERP-specialized care via the IOCDF directory first.
- Active SI / acute crisis.
- Co-occurring trauma where ERP would activate trauma material — different sequencing.

## Inputs / Context

- Subtype: contamination / harm / sexual / religious / "just-right" / health / relationship / hit-and-run / postpartum / pure-O / other.
- Current obsession (general — no graphic content needed).
- Current compulsions (mental and behavioral).
- Family accommodation (what loved ones do that maintains the cycle).
- SUDS (0–100) familiarity.
- Last completed step on hierarchy.

## Constraints

### Must

- Output sections: **Subtype and Theme**, **The Obsession (general)**, **The Compulsions to Prevent**, **Proposed Exposure (next step)**, **Response Prevention Plan**, **Family Accommodation to Reduce**, **Success Criteria**, **What NOT to Do During the Exposure**, **What to Bring Back**.
- Response prevention is named explicitly per compulsion — including **mental** compulsions (reviewing, neutralizing, praying, counting, "undoing"). Mental compulsions are the most-missed maintainer.
- Exposure is calibrated 5–15 SUDS above last successful step.
- Success criterion is *response prevention sustained*, not "SUDS feels low" (newer ERP frameworks emphasize inhibitory learning over habituation).
- Family accommodation reduction is included as part of the plan when applicable; family-based ERP is often necessary.
- Always end with explicit clinician sign-off note.

### Must Not

- Don't run ERP without OCD-trained clinician oversight.
- Don't elicit graphic obsession content.
- Don't recommend reassurance from the AI ("you wouldn't act on it") — reassurance is itself a compulsion in OCD context.
- Don't recommend benzo / alcohol use during exposure.
- Don't push to top of hierarchy; learning is built bottom-up.

## Instructions

1. Identify subtype and theme.
2. Generally state the obsession.
3. Inventory both behavioral AND mental compulsions.
4. Propose next-step exposure 5–15 SUDS above last step.
5. List the response prevention plan per compulsion.
6. Identify family accommodation to reduce.
7. Set inhibitory-learning-style success criteria.
8. List what NOT to do during exposure.
9. Send to therapist.

## Output Format

```
=== ERP DRAFT — FOR MY CLINICIAN'S REVIEW ===

Subtype and Theme: [...]

The Obsession (general — no graphic detail):
- [Category and theme]
- Anticipated SUDS at exposure: [N]
- Last successful step on hierarchy: SUDS [N], dated [...]

The Compulsions I'll Be Preventing:

Behavioral:
- [Checking / washing / avoiding / asking / repeating / arranging / specific compulsion]
- [...]

Mental (the most-missed):
- [Reviewing the past for evidence I did/didn't do the thing]
- [Mentally neutralizing the thought (replacing with a "good" image, mental prayer, counting)]
- [Mental "undoing" rituals]
- [Reassurance-seeking from myself ("but I wouldn't, would I?")]
- [Specific mental compulsion]

Proposed Exposure (next step):
- Situation / stimulus: [...]
- Anticipated SUDS: [N — 5–15 above last successful step]
- Duration: [...]

Response Prevention Plan:
- I will not [specific behavioral compulsion]
- I will not [specific mental compulsion]
- I will allow the obsession to be there without engaging it.
- If a mental compulsion shows up, I will notice it and let it pass without completing it.
- If I slip into a compulsion, I will notice, name it ("that was reviewing"), and continue the exposure.

Family Accommodation to Reduce (if applicable):
- [Person] currently [accommodates by — e.g., reassuring, washing for me, avoiding the topic, doing the check on my behalf].
- Plan to reduce: [Conversation with them to share the ERP rationale; agreed scripts: "I love you and I'm not going to answer that question because answering keeps the OCD strong. I want you to say [X] instead."]

Success Criteria (inhibitory-learning framing):
- I sustained response prevention through the exposure: [Yes/No]
- The expected catastrophic outcome did not occur: [Yes/No]
- I learned my brain can hold the obsession without doing the compulsion and not catastrophe-out: [Yes/No]
- (SUDS may or may not drop within the exposure; that's not the criterion. The criterion is inhibitory learning — that the feared outcome didn't happen and I tolerated the not-knowing.)

What NOT to Do During the Exposure:
- [No reassurance from AI / partner / google]
- [No mental neutralizing]
- [No "checking" mid-exposure to see if the obsession is "still there"]
- [No alcohol / benzo / cannabis pre-medication]
- [No leaving when SUDS spikes — that teaches my brain the situation was the threat]

What to Bring Back to Therapist:
- This worksheet
- Actual SUDS log
- Whether response prevention held
- What mental compulsions surfaced
- Family accommodation status

⚠️ Don't run this exposure until my OCD-trained clinician has reviewed it. If you don't have an ERP-trained clinician, find one first — see iocdf.org clinician directory.
```

## Verification

- [ ] Subtype identified.
- [ ] Obsession general, not graphic.
- [ ] Both behavioral AND mental compulsions inventoried.
- [ ] Exposure step 5–15 SUDS above last successful.
- [ ] Response prevention plan per compulsion type.
- [ ] Mental compulsions explicitly named and prevented.
- [ ] Family accommodation addressed if applicable.
- [ ] Success criteria use inhibitory-learning framing (not just SUDS reduction).
- [ ] No reassurance offered.
- [ ] Therapist sign-off note prominent.
