# Family Accommodation Reduction Plan (OCD)

## Objective

Help an OCD client and their household identify family accommodations and build a reduction plan that supports rather than undermines ERP. Accommodations come from love and feel like helping; they actually keep OCD strong.

## When to Use

- Anyone in active ERP whose household has been accommodating.
- A partner / parent who's exhausted from accommodating and wants to stop.
- An OCD client whose progress in session isn't generalizing because home keeps the loop alive.

## Inputs / Context

- Who is in the household.
- Specific accommodations (provide reassurance, perform the check, avoid trigger, wash items, drive to avoid, take over a task, modify routine, answer the OCD question repeatedly).
- Length of time they've been accommodating.
- The ERP plan (what's being targeted in therapy).
- Family member's own bandwidth and willingness.
- Children in the home (their accommodations are different — schools and family-based ERP).

## Constraints

### Must

- Output sections: **The Household and Roles**, **Accommodation Inventory**, **Why Reducing Helps (the rationale to share)**, **Reduction Sequence (graded, not all-at-once)**, **Scripts for Each Reduction**, **What to Do When OCD Pushes Back**, **Coordination With Clinician**.
- Reduction is graded. Stopping all accommodations at once usually backfires; a 2–4 week stepped reduction works better.
- Each reduction has a script the family member can use ("I love you. I'm not going to answer that question because answering keeps the OCD louder. What I will do is...").
- "What to do when OCD pushes back" is critical — escalation is normal and the family member needs to expect and not flinch from it.
- Children's accommodations must be coordinated with their pediatric OCD clinician.

### Must Not

- Don't ask family to drop accommodations without OCD-trained clinician oversight.
- Don't recommend punishing the OCD client for compulsions.
- Don't substitute family accommodation for clinical ERP.
- Don't push family members past their bandwidth; they need their own resources.

## Instructions

1. List the household roles.
2. Inventory accommodations honestly.
3. Share the rationale for reduction.
4. Sequence reductions from least to most disruptive over 2–4 weeks.
5. Generate per-accommodation scripts.
6. Anticipate escalation and prepare.
7. Coordinate with the clinician.

## Output Format

```
=== FAMILY ACCOMMODATION REDUCTION PLAN ===

The Household:
- Person with OCD: [...]
- Household members involved: [partner / parent / child / roommate]

Accommodation Inventory:
- [Family member] currently:
  - Reassurance: [Yes — about [topic, frequency]]
  - Performs the check: [Yes — does [check] for the person]
  - Avoids triggers: [Yes — avoids [thing] in shared spaces]
  - Washes items / hands for the person: [Yes / No]
  - Drives to avoid the trigger: [Yes / No]
  - Takes over tasks the person can't do due to OCD: [Yes — specific tasks]
  - Modifies routines: [...]
  - Answers OCD questions repeatedly: [Yes — questions about [topic]]
  - Other: [...]

Why Reducing Helps (rationale to share with family):
"OCD works like a loop: the obsession → distress → compulsion (or accommodation) → brief relief → loop continues, often louder next time. When we accommodate — answer the question, do the check, avoid the trigger — we give the loop the relief it's seeking. We feel like we're helping, but the loop gets stronger, the OCD gets bigger, and the person we love becomes more tied to the cycle. Reducing accommodation is not withholding love; it's stopping feeding the loop. We will reduce gradually, with scripts, and the OCD will get louder before it quiets."

Reduction Sequence (over 2–4 weeks):

Week 1 — Easiest:
- Reduce: [Single, lowest-impact accommodation]
- Script: "[exact words]"
- What to expect: [escalation 2–7 days]

Week 2:
- Reduce: [...]
- Script: "[...]"

Week 3:
- Reduce: [...]
- Script: "[...]"

Week 4:
- Reduce: [hardest of the planned set]
- Script: "[...]"

Scripts for Each Reduction:

For "answer the OCD question":
"I love you. The answer to that question feeds the OCD loop, so I'm not going to answer it. What I will do is sit here with you while you sit with not knowing."

For "perform the check":
"I see you're asking me to check the door. The OCD plan we agreed on says I won't do that for you. I'll be here when you've done what you can to sit with the uncertainty."

For "avoid the trigger":
"I'm going to keep [object/setting] available in our shared space because the OCD plan says we don't avoid. I love you. We can be in this together while it's hard."

For "do the wash for them":
"I won't wash [item] for you. I will hug you when you're ready."

For "drive the long way":
"We're taking the regular route today. I know it's hard. I'll be present with you while it is."

What to Do When OCD Pushes Back:
- Expect escalation in days 2–7 of each reduction. The person may beg, threaten to leave, sob, get angry, or have a worse week. This is the loop fighting reduction; it's a sign reduction is working.
- Family member's job: stay loving AND firm. Both matter.
- Do not return to accommodation to soothe escalation. That teaches the loop that escalation works.
- Family member needs their own support: their own therapy or an OCD family-support group (NAMI / IOCDF resources).
- If escalation includes risk (SI, NSSI, self-harm intent), contact the OCD clinician same day; this is a clinical decision moment, not a "be firmer" moment.

Coordination With Clinician:
- Send this plan to the OCD clinician for sign-off before starting.
- Schedule check-in 1 week after Week 1 reduction.
- Bring family member to a session if the clinician offers family-based ERP.
- For pediatric cases: this is family-based ERP territory and must be clinician-led.

⚠️ Don't reduce accommodations without coordinating with the OCD clinician.
```

## Verification

- [ ] Household roles identified.
- [ ] Accommodations inventoried specifically.
- [ ] Rationale to share generated.
- [ ] Reduction sequenced over 2–4 weeks, easiest first.
- [ ] Per-accommodation scripts in actual words.
- [ ] Escalation expectations explicit.
- [ ] Family-member-needs-support note included.
- [ ] Risk escalation triggers separated from "be firmer."
- [ ] Clinician sign-off note prominent.
