# Journal Prompt Generator (Anti-Toxic-Positivity)

## Objective

Generate journaling prompts matched to the client's current state — without sliding into either toxic positivity (forced gratitude) or rumination (looping in pain). Output 5–10 prompts the client can choose from.

## When to Use

- Building or refreshing a journaling practice.
- Tired of "what are you grateful for" prompts.
- Wanting to journal but not knowing where to start.
- Coming out of a hard day and wanting structure.

## Inputs / Context

- Current state (high distress, neutral, processing, content, numb).
- Time available (5 min, 15 min, 30 min).
- Format preference (sentence completion, open-ended, structured).
- Whether the client is in active therapy (some prompts pair better with therapeutic processing).
- Recent themes the client is sitting with.

## Constraints

### Must

- Output 5–10 prompts matched to state and time.
- Mix prompt types: emotional-noticing, body-scanning, value-checking, observational, gratitude-with-honesty, future-self, parts-noticing.
- Avoid generic gratitude lists; if gratitude is included, pair with the harder edge ("what I'm grateful for AND what I'm sitting with about it").
- For high-distress states, lean toward sensory and body-anchoring prompts; avoid prompts that invite rumination (e.g., "what's the worst that could happen?").
- For numb states, lean toward observational and curiosity prompts; avoid forced emotion-naming.
- Include a "permission line" reminding the client they can set the prompt down if it doesn't land.

### Must Not

- Don't use generic positivity prompts ("3 things I'm grateful for").
- Don't generate prompts that invite rumination in distressed clients.
- Don't push trauma processing in journaling without therapist.
- Don't issue advice in prompt form.

## Instructions

1. Identify state, time, format preference.
2. Generate 5–10 prompts matched.
3. Include a mix of emotional, body, observational, value, and parts prompts.
4. Add the permission line.

## Output Format

```
=== JOURNAL PROMPTS — PICK ANY ===

State: [High distress / Neutral / Processing / Content / Numb]
Time: [5 / 15 / 30 min]
Permission line: Pick one. If it doesn't land in the first sentence, set it down and pick another. Not every prompt fits every day.

PROMPTS:

1. [Body-anchoring]
"What is my body holding right now? I'm not going to fix it; I'm going to notice it. Where is it tight, soft, hot, cold, neutral?"

2. [Emotional noticing — without forcing a name]
"Three things I noticed today. (Not three feelings — three noticings. They can be sensations, sights, sounds, thoughts, brief flashes of emotion.)"

3. [Honesty alongside gratitude]
"One thing I'm grateful for today AND one thing I'm sitting with about it. (Most things have both edges.)"

4. [Value check]
"In the last 24 hours, what did I do that lined up with what matters to me? What pulled me away from it?"

5. [Observational distance]
"Write about today's most difficult moment as if I'm a friendly observer. What did I see this person do? What would I want to say to them?"

6. [Parts-noticing — light]
"What part of me showed up loudest today? What part stayed quiet?"

7. [Future-self]
"If I read this entry in 6 months, what would I want past me to know I noticed today?"

8. [Sensory anchor — high-distress safe]
"Five things I see right now. Four things I hear. Three things I touch. Two things I smell. One thing I taste."

9. [Capacity check]
"What is my current emotional capacity, on a 0–10 scale? What is one thing I can do, and one thing I will not try to do today?"

10. [Letter-to-self]
"Write a 3-sentence letter to past me about today. Not advice — just what I want them to know."

If a prompt cracks something open that needs more than journaling:
- Bring it to therapy.
- Set the journal down.
- Use a grounding skill if needed.

If today is a high-distress day:
- Stay with prompts 1, 2, 8, 9.
- Skip the "go deeper" prompts (3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 10).
```

## Verification

- [ ] 5–10 prompts.
- [ ] State-matched.
- [ ] Mix of body, emotional, observational, value, parts.
- [ ] No generic gratitude lists.
- [ ] Permission line.
- [ ] No rumination-inducing prompts in distressed states.
- [ ] No trauma-processing prompts.
- [ ] Escalation if cracks open.
