# Religious Deconstruction Support

## Objective

Support someone working through a shift in their religious faith — questioning, leaving, or reconstructing — with room for grief and intellectual honesty, and without pushing a destination. Output should:

1. Name what is actually being lost or changing, across belief, belonging, meaning, and certainty.
2. Make room to grieve those losses without rushing to replace them.
3. Separate questions of belief (what's true) from questions of belonging (community, family, identity).
4. Hold ambiguity as a livable state, not a failure.
5. Offer a handoff for the grief, anxiety, or relational rupture that often comes with this.

## When to Use

- Questions about a faith one was raised in, or chose, that no longer fit.
- Already left or leaving and feeling unmoored, grieving, or guilty.
- Wanting to stay connected to community or family while one's belief changes.
- Feeling intense fear (of hell, of being wrong, of judgment) while questioning.

## Inputs / Context

- The tradition and the person's relationship to it (raised in it, converted, devout, cultural).
- What's prompting the questioning, in their own words.
- What the faith currently provides (community, ritual, meaning, moral framework, family ties).
- What they fear losing, and what they fear about leaving or staying.
- Whether family / community membership is conditional on belief.

## Constraints

### Must

- Output sections in order: **Where I Am Right Now**, **What's Actually Changing (belief / belonging / meaning / certainty)**, **The Losses I Can Name and Grieve**, **Belief vs. Belonging (separated)**, **Holding the Open Questions**, **Fears, Named and Held**, **Bring to a Therapist**.
- Stay tradition-neutral: do not argue for or against the faith, validate or invalidate any belief, or steer toward staying, leaving, or reconstructing.
- Treat deconstruction as a grief process — name the specific losses (a worldview, a future imagined, a community, certainty itself).
- Explicitly separate "Is it true?" from "Do I belong / who am I without it?" — they can move at different speeds and have different answers.
- Normalize ambiguity, doubt, and a long, non-linear timeline.

### Must Not

- Don't push the person toward atheism, agnosticism, reconstruction, or return.
- Don't dismiss the faith as foolish or the doubt as rebellion.
- Don't promise relief or resolution.
- Don't minimize fear responses (e.g., fear of hell) — these are real and can be intense; route them to support, not argument.

## Instructions

1. Locate where the person is now without naming a destination.
2. Map what's changing across belief, belonging, meaning, and certainty.
3. Name the losses concretely and give them room to be grieved.
4. Separate belief questions from belonging questions.
5. Frame the open questions as livable, not as emergencies to resolve.
6. Hold the fears without arguing them away; offer the therapist handoff.

## Output Format

```
=== DECONSTRUCTION SUPPORT ===

Where I Am Right Now:
"[In my own words — questioning / leaving / left / rebuilding / don't know]"

What's Actually Changing:
- Belief (what I think is true): [...]
- Belonging (community, family, identity): [...]
- Meaning (the story that made things make sense): [...]
- Certainty (having a clear framework for right/wrong/why): [...]

The Losses I Can Name and Grieve:
- I'm losing: [...]  → It mattered because: [...]
- I'm losing: [...]  → It mattered because: [...]
> These deserve grief. I don't have to replace them on a schedule.

Belief vs. Belonging (they're not the same question):
- The belief question I'm sitting with: [...]
- The belonging question I'm sitting with: [...]
- These can have different answers and move at different speeds. I don't have to solve both at once.

Holding the Open Questions:
- Questions I'm carrying without an answer: [...]
- What it would mean to live with these unresolved for a while: [...]

Fears, Named and Held:
- A fear that's loud right now: [...]
- This fear makes sense given where I came from. I don't have to argue it away today.
- If a fear (hell, punishment, being unforgivable) becomes overwhelming: this is worth bringing
  to a therapist, ideally one experienced with religious trauma.

Bring to a Therapist:
- What I'd most want help with: [grief / anxiety / family rupture / identity]
- If I feel hopeless or can't function: contact a clinician this week. If I'm thinking about
  ending my life: call or text 988 (US) now.
```

## Verification

- [ ] Tradition-neutral; no push toward staying, leaving, or any belief.
- [ ] Change mapped across belief, belonging, meaning, certainty.
- [ ] Specific losses named and framed as grievable.
- [ ] Belief and belonging questions explicitly separated.
- [ ] Open questions framed as livable, not emergencies.
- [ ] Fears held, not argued; religious-trauma-aware handoff offered.
- [ ] 988 escalation included for hopelessness / SI.
