# Gender Identity Exploration Journal

## Objective

Provide a structured, open-ended journal for someone exploring their gender — without rushing toward a label, a transition decision, or a conclusion. Output should:

1. Offer non-leading reflection prompts that invite noticing, not deciding.
2. Help map moments of euphoria and dysphoria as data, not verdicts.
3. Hold social questions (name, pronouns, presentation) and medical questions (if any) lightly and separately.
4. Make explicit that there is no required timeline and no right answer.
5. Offer a handoff to an affirming, gender-competent therapist for deeper work.

## When to Use

- Wondering about one's gender and wanting space to explore without committing.
- Feeling something is "off" but not having words for it yet.
- Already exploring and wanting a structure to track the felt sense over time.
- Feeling pressure (from self, partner, community, or online discourse) to "figure it out" and wanting to slow down.

## Inputs / Context

- Whatever the person already notices about their gender, in their own words (can be very little).
- Moments — recent or old — that felt right, wrong, freeing, or distressing around gender.
- Current questions they're sitting with (none required).
- How much pressure they feel to reach an answer, and from where.

## Constraints

### Must

- Output sections in order: **Note Before I Start**, **Felt-Sense Reflection (open prompts)**, **Euphoria Map**, **Dysphoria Map**, **Social Questions (held lightly)**, **Medical Questions (held lightly, only if relevant)**, **What I'm Noticing Over Time**, **Bring to an Affirming Therapist**.
- Keep reflection prompts open and non-leading ("When did you feel most at home in your body?" not "Do you think you're trans?").
- Treat euphoria and dysphoria as observations to notice, not proof of a conclusion.
- State plainly: there is no timeline, no required endpoint, and exploring is not the same as deciding. Stopping, pausing, or landing anywhere is valid.
- Affirm all outcomes equally, including cis, nonbinary, questioning indefinitely, or fluid.

### Must Not

- Don't push toward a label, a transition, or a decision.
- Don't interpret the person's gender for them or tell them what their feelings "mean."
- Don't frame uncertainty as a problem; uncertainty is part of exploration.
- Don't medicalize; medical questions are theirs to raise, and they route to an affirming clinician.

## Instructions

1. Open with a low-pressure note normalizing exploration and not-knowing.
2. Offer open felt-sense prompts; invite, don't quiz.
3. Help the person log euphoria and dysphoria moments as neutral data.
4. Separate social questions from medical questions; hold both lightly.
5. Mirror back patterns observed over time without drawing conclusions.
6. Offer the affirming-therapist handoff for deeper processing.

## Output Format

```
=== GENDER EXPLORATION JOURNAL ===

Note Before I Start:
There's nothing to solve today. I'm allowed to not know. Whatever I notice is allowed,
and I can pause this any time. There's no finish line.

Felt-Sense Reflection (answer any, skip any):
- When have I felt most at home in my body or how I'm seen?
- When have I felt most like a stranger to myself?
- If no one would react, how would I want to be seen / named / referred to?
- What do I feel when I imagine that? (curiosity / relief / fear / longing / nothing)

Euphoria Map (moments that felt right — just notice):
- Moment: [...]  →  What felt good: [...]
- Moment: [...]  →  What felt good: [...]

Dysphoria / Discomfort Map (moments that felt off — just notice):
- Moment: [...]  →  What felt off: [body / language / others' perception / role]
- Moment: [...]  →  What felt off: [...]

Social Questions (held lightly — no commitment):
- A name I'd want to try, even just in my head: [...]
- Pronouns I'm curious about: [...]
- Presentation I'd like to experiment with: [...]
> These are experiments, not declarations. I can try one privately.

Medical Questions (only if they're mine to ask):
- Questions I'm holding: [...]
> I don't have to answer these now. They belong in a conversation with an affirming clinician.

What I'm Noticing Over Time:
- Patterns so far (no conclusions): [...]
- What I want to keep watching: [...]

Bring to an Affirming Therapist:
- The thread I'd most want help exploring: [...]
- Finding someone: ask for a gender-affirming / gender-competent therapist; WPATH or local
  LGBTQ center directories can help.
```

## Verification

- [ ] Opening normalizes not-knowing and no-timeline.
- [ ] Reflection prompts are open and non-leading.
- [ ] Euphoria and dysphoria mapped as data, not verdicts.
- [ ] Social and medical questions held separately and lightly.
- [ ] Patterns mirrored without drawing a conclusion or assigning a label.
- [ ] All outcomes affirmed equally.
- [ ] Affirming-therapist handoff included.
