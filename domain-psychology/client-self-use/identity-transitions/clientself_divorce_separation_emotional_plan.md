# Divorce / Separation Emotional Plan

## Objective

Help someone build an emotional plan for going through a divorce or separation — kept deliberately separate from the legal and logistical work. Output should:

1. Draw a clear line between the emotional track and the legal/logistical track, routing legal questions to an attorney.
2. Name the grief of the relationship and the identity shift it brings.
3. Address the co-parenting emotional load if children are involved.
4. Build day-to-day emotional support and steadiers.
5. Set escalation triggers for depression and suicidal thinking.

## When to Use

- Deciding to separate, going through it, or in the raw aftermath.
- Feeling flooded and unable to separate "how I feel" from "what I have to do."
- Struggling with who they are now that the relationship is ending.
- Carrying the emotional weight of co-parenting with someone they're divorcing.

## Inputs / Context

- Where they are in the process (considering / separated / filing / finalized).
- Who initiated, and how they feel about that.
- Whether children are involved and the co-parenting picture.
- What they're grieving most (the person, the future they imagined, the family unit, the identity).
- Current support, and current functioning (sleep, eating, work, hopelessness).

## Constraints

### Must

- Output sections in order: **The Two Tracks (emotional vs. legal/logistical)**, **What I'm Grieving**, **Who I Am Now (identity shift)**, **Co-Parenting Emotional Load (if applicable)**, **Daily Steadiers**, **When to Get More Support**, **Bring to a Therapist**.
- Explicitly separate the emotional track from the legal/logistical track; route any legal, financial, or custody questions to a family-law attorney and state this plan does not give legal advice.
- Name the grief specifically — including the loss of a future and an identity ("wife/husband," "the couple," "the family we were").
- For co-parenting, focus on managing one's own emotions and not routing them through the children; keep it emotional, not legal.
- Include escalation: clinician this week for persistent depression; today / 988 for suicidal thoughts.

### Must Not

- Don't give legal, custody, or financial advice, or estimate outcomes — route to an attorney.
- Don't take sides, assign blame, or characterize the ex.
- Don't rush the person to "move on," date, or feel grateful.
- Don't treat reconciliation or finality as the "right" outcome.

## Instructions

1. Draw the two-track line and offload legal items to a separate list bound for the attorney.
2. Name what's being grieved, beyond the person.
3. Explore the identity shift without prescribing a new identity.
4. If children: address the emotional load and protecting them from the adult conflict.
5. Build daily steadiers and a support map.
6. Set escalation triggers and the therapist handoff.

## Output Format

```
=== DIVORCE / SEPARATION — EMOTIONAL PLAN ===

The Two Tracks (keep these apart):
- EMOTIONAL (this plan): grief, identity, support, regulation.
- LEGAL / LOGISTICAL (NOT this plan): filing, custody, finances, property.
  → These go on a separate list for my attorney. This plan gives no legal advice.
  My one-line reminder when they blur: "That's a lawyer question. I'll write it down and feel my feelings here."

What I'm Grieving:
- The person: [...]
- The future I imagined: [...]
- The daily life / identity (being a spouse, the family unit): [...]
- What surprises me about the grief: [...]

Who I Am Now (identity shift):
- A role that's ending: [...]
- Something true about me that isn't ending: [...]
- A small thing that's mine again: [...]
> I don't have to know who I am next yet.

Co-Parenting Emotional Load (if children):
- The hardest emotional part: [...]
- How I'll keep my feelings about my ex off the kids: [...]
- I won't use the kids as messengers or confidants.
- (Custody/schedule details: those go to the attorney list, not here.)

Daily Steadiers:
- Three non-negotiables (sleep / eat / move): [...]
- One person I can call on a hard night: [...]
- One thing that reminds me I'm still me: [...]

When to Get More Support:
- If low mood, hopelessness, or not-functioning lasts 2+ weeks → contact a clinician this week.
- If I'm thinking about ending my life or that others would be better off without me →
  reach out today: call or text 988 (US), or go to the nearest ER.

Bring to a Therapist:
- What I'd most want help carrying: [...]
- A divorce-experienced therapist or a support group could help me not do this alone.
```

## Verification

- [ ] Emotional and legal/logistical tracks explicitly separated; legal items routed to attorney.
- [ ] States it gives no legal advice.
- [ ] Grief named specifically, including future and identity loss.
- [ ] Identity shift explored without prescribing a new identity.
- [ ] Co-parenting load addressed emotionally, kids protected from adult conflict.
- [ ] No blame, no rush to move on, no "right" outcome.
- [ ] Escalation: clinician this week for depression; 988 / today for SI.
- [ ] Therapist handoff included.
