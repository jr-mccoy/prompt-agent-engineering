# Coming-Out Conversation Planner

## Objective

Help a person plan a specific coming-out conversation about their gender, sexuality, or identity. Output should:

1. Run a safety check first and, if any safety risk is present, plan for safety before disclosure.
2. Decide who, when, and how — matched to this person and this relationship.
3. Generate the actual opening sentences at varying directness.
4. Anticipate likely reactions and prepare a response to each.
5. Build aftercare so the person isn't alone with whatever follows.

## When to Use

- Wanting to come out to a parent, partner, friend, sibling, employer, or community.
- Deciding whether now is the right time, or whether to wait.
- Already decided to come out and wanting to rehearse the actual words.
- Feeling pressure (internal or external) to disclose and wanting to slow down and choose deliberately.

## Inputs / Context

- What the person wants to share, in their own words, and to whom.
- The relationship and its history (close, strained, religious, conditional support, financial/housing dependence).
- Any signals about how this person may react.
- Whether the person is physically, financially, or housing-dependent on the listener.
- The person's support network and where they could go if it goes badly.

## Constraints

### Must

- Output sections in order: **Safety Check First**, **Who / When / Where**, **What I Want Them to Know**, **Three Ways to Open It (low / medium / high directness)**, **Likely Reactions and How I'll Respond**, **My Boundaries**, **Aftercare**.
- Generate the actual opening sentences, not "you could mention that..."
- Run the safety check before anything else. If there is risk of physical harm, being outed against their will, losing housing, or losing financial support, advise planning for safety first — securing support, resources, or independence — and explicitly do **not** pressure disclosure on any timeline.
- Affirm that coming out is the person's choice, including the choice to wait, to come out selectively, or not at all.
- Include where to turn if the conversation goes badly (a named friend, a community line such as the Trevor Project or a local LGBTQ center, the person's therapist).

### Must Not

- Don't pressure disclosure, set a timeline, or imply that not coming out is avoidance or cowardice.
- Don't promise how the listener will react.
- Don't treat being closeted in an unsafe situation as a problem to fix — treat safety as the priority.
- Don't assume the relationship's values; ask.

## Instructions

1. Run the safety check. If unsafe, shift the whole plan toward safety and support, not disclosure.
2. Clarify who this conversation is with and why this person, now.
3. Help the person name what they want the listener to know and what they want from the conversation.
4. Generate 3 openings at low / medium / high directness.
5. For each likely reaction, prepare the person's response and a boundary.
6. Build aftercare: who they'll talk to right after, regardless of outcome.

## Output Format

```
=== COMING-OUT PLAN ===

Safety Check First:
- Could this person react with physical harm, threats, or kicking me out?   [Yes / No / Unsure]
- Do I depend on them for housing or money right now?                       [Yes / No]
- Could they out me to others against my will?                              [Yes / No]
> If any "Yes" or "Unsure": this plan pauses. The next move is SAFETY, not disclosure.
  - Where I could stay if needed: [...]
  - Money / documents / meds I'd want secured first: [...]
  - Who already knows and has my back: [...]
  - Support line: Trevor Project 1-866-488-7386 / Trans Lifeline / local LGBTQ center
  Coming out can wait until I'm safe. There is no deadline.

Who / When / Where:
- Person: [...]
- Why them, why now: [...]
- Setting that gives me an exit and privacy: [...]
- A time that isn't rushed or right before something big: [...]

What I Want Them to Know:
"[In my own words]"
What I want from this conversation: [to be heard / acceptance / nothing to change / a specific ask]

Three Ways to Open It:
Low directness — testing the water:
"There's something about me I've been wanting to share with you. Is now an okay time to talk?"
Medium directness — naming it gently:
"I want you to know something that's important to me. [Identity, named.] I've known for a while and I trust you with it."
High directness — saying it plainly:
"I'm [identity]. I'm telling you because you matter to me."

Likely Reactions and How I'll Respond:
If they're warm: I'll say: [...]
If they're quiet or need time: I'll say: "You don't have to react right now. I just wanted you to know." I'll remember: silence isn't rejection.
If they ask intrusive questions: I'll say: "I'm happy to talk, but some of that is private for now."
If they react badly: I'll say: [...]  Then I'll leave / call [name]. I won't argue my identity.

My Boundaries:
- I won't debate whether this is real.
- I won't accept being outed to others — that's my choice: [...]
- I can end the conversation any time.

Aftercare:
- Right after, I'll contact: [name / line]
- Something kind I'll do for myself: [...]
- If I'm reeling: bring this to my therapist or call [support line].
```

## Verification

- [ ] Safety check runs first; any risk routes to safety-first, no disclosure pressure.
- [ ] Who / when / where chosen deliberately, with an exit.
- [ ] What the person wants them to know is in their own words.
- [ ] Three openings at varying directness, each as actual sentences.
- [ ] At least 3 reactions anticipated with prepared responses and a boundary.
- [ ] Aftercare names a person or line plus the therapist handoff.
- [ ] No promise of reaction, no timeline pressure, choice to wait affirmed.
