# Post-Diagnosis Adjustment

## Objective

Help someone adjust to a recent diagnosis — a chronic illness or a neurodivergence (e.g., ADHD, autism, a lifelong condition newly named). Output should:

1. Make room for both grief and relief, often present at once.
2. Help integrate the diagnosis as one part of them, not the whole of them.
3. Reframe past experiences through the new lens — with compassion, not rewriting blame onto themselves.
4. Seed early self-advocacy (language, needs, accommodations) without pressure.
5. Offer a therapist handoff for grief, anxiety, or identity disruption.

## When to Use

- Recently diagnosed and feeling some mix of grief, relief, anger, or disorientation.
- Re-reading their whole life story through the diagnosis and not sure what to do with it.
- Wondering if they're "still themselves" or now "a diagnosis."
- Wanting language to explain their needs to others.

## Inputs / Context

- The diagnosis (in their words) and how recently it came.
- Their first reaction (relief, grief, denial, anger, numbness, all of it).
- What they fear it means about them or their future.
- What it might explain about their past.
- Their current support and where the diagnosis lands in their life right now.

## Constraints

### Must

- Output sections in order: **Both/And: Grief and Relief**, **It's Part of Me, Not All of Me**, **Reframing the Past (with compassion)**, **What Stays True About Me**, **Self-Advocacy Seeds**, **Bring to a Therapist**.
- Hold grief and relief together — many people feel both, and that's coherent.
- Frame the diagnosis as one part of a whole person; resist a "now I'm defined by this" narrative.
- When reframing the past, do so with self-compassion ("I wasn't lazy/broken; I was unsupported") — never to assign new self-blame.
- Keep self-advocacy as optional seeds (language for needs, possible accommodations), not a to-do list with pressure.
- Stay non-pathologizing and affirming, especially for neurodivergence — describe difference, not deficiency, in the user's own framing.

### Must Not

- Don't give medical advice, treatment recommendations, or prognosis.
- Don't define the person by the diagnosis or speak as though it's their whole identity.
- Don't push acceptance, gratitude, or a positive spin before the grief is allowed.
- Don't contradict or second-guess the diagnosis itself; that's between them and their clinician.

## Instructions

1. Validate the mix of grief and relief without resolving the tension.
2. Frame the diagnosis as part, not whole.
3. Reframe the past compassionately, lifting self-blame.
4. Name what stays true about the person regardless.
5. Offer optional self-advocacy seeds.
6. Offer the therapist handoff and escalation.

## Output Format

```
=== POST-DIAGNOSIS ADJUSTMENT ===

Both/And: Grief and Relief:
- What I'm grieving (a version of myself, a hoped-for ease, certainty): [...]
- What's a relief (an explanation, a name, "it wasn't just me failing"): [...]
> I can feel both at once. Neither cancels the other out.

It's Part of Me, Not All of Me:
- This diagnosis explains some things. It is not the whole of who I am.
- One sentence I'll keep: "I have [diagnosis]; I am [a whole person who also is ___]."

Reframing the Past (with compassion, not new blame):
- A struggle this might explain: [...]
- The kinder read: "I wasn't [lazy / broken / too much]; I was [unsupported / undiagnosed / working harder than anyone saw]."
- One thing I can forgive my past self for: [...]

What Stays True About Me:
- Strengths and traits the diagnosis doesn't erase: [...]
- What I still want for my life: [...]

Self-Advocacy Seeds (optional — no pressure):
- A need I now have language for: [...]
- An accommodation I could explore (work / school / home): [...]
- One person I might explain this to, in my own words: [...]

Bring to a Therapist:
- What I'd want help processing: [grief / anxiety / identity / how to tell people]
- Medical or treatment questions go to my diagnosing clinician.
- If I feel hopeless or unsafe → call or text 988 (US) today, or go to the ER.
```

## Verification

- [ ] Grief and relief held together, not resolved away.
- [ ] Diagnosis framed as part, not whole of identity.
- [ ] Past reframed with self-compassion, no new self-blame.
- [ ] What stays true about the person named.
- [ ] Self-advocacy kept optional, non-pressuring.
- [ ] Non-pathologizing / affirming stance, especially for neurodivergence.
- [ ] No medical advice or prognosis; diagnosis not second-guessed.
- [ ] Therapist handoff and 988 escalation included.
