# Caregiver Burnout — Self-Check and Sustainable-Support Plan

> **IF YOU ARE HAVING THOUGHTS OF SUICIDE, OR YOU FEEL YOU MIGHT HURT OR SERIOUSLY NEGLECT THE PERSON YOU CARE FOR: call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) now, or call 911 if anyone is in immediate danger.** Those feelings are a sign of overload that needs help today — not proof you are a bad person. Outside the US, use your local emergency number or find a local crisis line at findahelpline.com. This is a self-support aid — it is **not** a diagnosis, therapy, or a substitute for your own doctor.

## Objective

Help you see honestly how caregiving is affecting you — body, mood, relationships, and patience — and turn that into a plan you can sustain for months or years, not just the next crisis. The self-check is a conversation starter, not a score; the plan puts a floor under your own health and makes asking for help concrete.

## When to Use

- You are the main unpaid carer for a parent, partner, child with high needs, or relative (dementia, disability, serious illness, mental illness, addiction).
- You're running on empty, snapping at people, dropping your own appointments, or feeling resentful and then guilty about it.
- Someone said "you need to look after yourself" and you don't know what that would look like in practice.

**Distinct from:**
- `domain-productivity/home-life/home_caregiver_respite_plan.md` — schedules breaks and cover. This prompt is the **emotional and health check** that says whether you need them; it hands the logistics to that plan.
- `domain-personal-development/major-decisions/personal_caring_for_aging_parent.md` — decides the care **arrangement** for the parent; this is about the carer.
- `symptom-understanding/clientself_anxiety_depression_burnout_differentiator.md` — general burnout vs depression sorting; this is caregiver-specific (guilt, anticipatory grief, role strain).

## Inputs / Context

- Who you care for, their condition, and how long you've been caring.
- Hours per week, night duties, and who else helps (or doesn't).
- Your own health: sleep, appointments you've missed, alcohol or other substances, physical symptoms.
- Mood: irritability, resentment, hopelessness, crying, numbness, withdrawal.
- Anything that scares you — thoughts of suicide, of wishing it were over, or moments of being rough or neglectful.

## Constraints

### Must

- Lead with validation (NE-07): caregiving strain is common and heavy; resentment and "wishing it were over" are frequent and are not the same as wanting harm.
- **Self-check (DS-02):** six areas — energy/sleep, own health, mood, patience/irritability, connection, substance use — each rated 0 (fine) to 3 (severe). Label it **not a validated instrument**; mention that clinicians use tools such as the Zarit Burden Interview if the person wants a formal measure.
- Map the pattern to action: any 3 in mood or thoughts of death → clinician this week (and 988 if suicidal); alcohol/substances at 3 → GP or clinician this week, and name the drinking or use at that appointment (never advise stopping heavy daily drinking abruptly without medical advice — withdrawal can be dangerous); several 2s → plan plus a GP/therapist appointment; mostly 0–1 → maintenance plan.
- Plan sections: **Self-Care Floor** (3 non-negotiables), **Specific Asks** (named person + named task), **Respite** (hand-off to respite plan), **Boundaries** (one "no" or "not me"), **Guilt & Grief** (anticipatory grief named), **Warning Signs to Act On**.
- Include a script for one specific ask.

### Must Not

- Do not diagnose depression or burnout.
- Do not shame the person for resentment, relief fantasies, or considering residential care.
- Do not tell them to "just take time off" without naming who covers.
- Do not give legal or financial advice about care arrangements — point to the relevant planner instead.

## Instructions

1. Run the safety check; route if needed.
2. Validate the load.
3. Walk the six-area self-check and summarise the pattern.
4. Map pattern to level of help.
5. Build the six plan sections with personalised details (ED-04).
6. Write one ask script and the warning signs.

## Output Format

```
=== CAREGIVER CHECK & SUPPORT PLAN ===

Safety: thoughts of suicide, or fear I might hurt/neglect [name]? → 988 now; 911 if immediate danger.

What I'm carrying: [who, condition, hours, how long]. This is a lot.

SELF-CHECK (0 fine – 3 severe; not a diagnosis)
Energy/sleep [ ]  Own health [ ]  Mood [ ]  Patience [ ]  Connection [ ]  Alcohol/substances [ ]
Pattern: [...] → Level of help: [maintenance / GP or therapist appointment / clinician this week]

SELF-CARE FLOOR (non-negotiable):
1. [e.g., my own GP appointment on (date)]
2. [e.g., 30 min outside, 4×/week]
3. [e.g., 7 hours in bed on non-night-duty nights]

SPECIFIC ASKS:
- [Sibling] → [Tuesday evening visits]
- [Friend] → [pharmacy pick-up]
Script: "I'm at my limit and I need a specific thing: could you [task] on [day]? It would really help."

RESPITE: see respite plan → [weekly block / monthly day / annual break]

BOUNDARY: I will stop [...] / I will say "not me" to [...]

GUILT & GRIEF: I can love them and resent the load. I may be grieving someone who is still here.

WARNING SIGNS TO ACT ON: [mood 3 for 2 weeks / drinking more / snapping physically / hopelessness] → [call GP / therapist / 988]
```

## Verification

- [ ] Safety banner covers suicide and harm/neglect of the care recipient.
- [ ] Six-area self-check labeled not validated; pattern mapped to a level of help.
- [ ] All six plan sections present, with named people and tasks.
- [ ] One ask script included.
- [ ] No diagnosis, no shaming, no legal/financial advice.

## False-Positive Prevention

- "Sometimes I wish it were over" is common in long-term caregiving and usually means wishing the suffering or the load would end — ask what it means; do not treat it as intent to harm, and do not ignore it if it includes the carer's own death.
- High scores in energy and sleep alone may reflect night duties, not depression; route to practical cover first, clinician if mood is also high.
- Considering residential care is not abandonment; do not frame it as a failure.

## Example

**Input:** "I look after my mum with dementia, 60+ hours a week, my brother does nothing. I'm snapping at her and hate myself after. Not sleeping. No thoughts of suicide, I'd never hurt her, but I'm drinking a bottle of wine most nights."

**Output (abbreviated):** Validation; self-check energy 3, own health 2, mood 2, patience 3, connection 2, alcohol 3 → GP appointment this week, mention the drinking. Floor: GP visit (ask how to cut down on the wine safely — stopping heavy daily drinking suddenly can cause withdrawal), a 20-minute walk daily, a set bedtime on non-night-duty nights. Asks: brother → every Saturday afternoon, with script. Respite: hand to the respite planner for a day programme. Boundary: stop doing mum's paperwork alone. Grief: naming anticipatory grief. Warning signs: drinking more, rough handling → call GP / 988.
