# Neurodivergent Self-Advocacy & Accommodation Toolkit

> **IF YOU ARE HAVING THOUGHTS OF SUICIDE OR MIGHT NOT BE SAFE: call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US; text is available if phone calls are hard), call 911, or go to your nearest emergency department (ED) now.** Outside the US, use your local emergency number. This is a self-advocacy aid — it is **not** a diagnosis, legal advice, or a substitute for a clinician.

## Objective

Help you name what you need in plain, specific terms and ask for it — in the doctor's office, at work, at college, and at home — without having to re-explain yourself from scratch each time. You leave with a needs map, a disclosure decision for each setting (tell / partly tell / don't tell), an accommodation menu and a script per setting, and a one-page card you can hand over. Your neurotype is a difference to accommodate, not a defect to fix.

## When to Use

- You're autistic, ADHD, dyslexic, dyspraxic, or otherwise neurodivergent — diagnosed or self-identified.
- You keep hitting the same friction (sensory overload, phone calls, vague instructions, back-to-back meetings) and want to ask for changes.
- You're starting a new job, course, or healthcare relationship and want to decide what to share.
- You're recovering from burnout and need to protect your capacity.

**Distinct from:**
- `communication-system/clientself_workplace_accommodations_request.md` — writes the **formal written request** to an employer; this toolkit decides what to ask for across all settings and hands the work case to that prompt.
- `coping-by-concern/clientself_adhd_external_scaffold_designer.md` — builds personal executive-function scaffolds; this is about asking **others** for changes.
- `identity-transitions/clientself_post_diagnosis_adjustment.md` — the emotional adjustment to a diagnosis; this is practical advocacy.

## Inputs / Context

- Your neurotype(s) in your own words, and whether formally diagnosed (many accommodations don't require a diagnosis; some formal ones do).
- Friction points: sensory (light, noise, touch, smell), communication (phone, spoken vs written, directness), executive function (time, task-switching, instructions), social (small talk, meetings, eye contact), energy (masking cost, recovery time).
- Settings that matter now: healthcare, work, education, housing, relationships, services.
- What you've tried asking for before and how it went.
- Signs of burnout: loss of skills, shutdowns/meltdowns increasing, exhaustion after ordinary days; mood; any suicidal thoughts.

## Constraints

### Must

- Affirming language, using the person's own identity terms (identity-first or person-first as they prefer).
- **Needs map** across the five domains, each need phrased as "I work best when…" plus one concrete accommodation.
- **Disclosure decision** per setting — full / partial (describe needs without the label) / none — with the trade-offs stated, and the person's choice respected.
- **Accommodation menu and script** per selected setting (DT-02 examples: written follow-ups, agenda in advance, camera-off, quiet workspace, extra processing time, first/last appointment of the day, text-based contact).
- **Self-advocacy card (NE-20):** one page — how I communicate best, what overwhelms me, what helps, what to do if I shut down, contact preference.
- Mention US legal frameworks (ADA, Section 504) only descriptively with "[VERIFY for your situation]"; route formal disputes to an advocate or attorney.
- **Burnout vs depression:** if burnout signs co-occur with persistent low mood, hopelessness, or loss of interest, suggest a neurodivergent-affirming clinician; if suicidal thoughts are present → banner route.

### Must Not

- Do not diagnose or suggest someone "isn't really" neurodivergent.
- Do not frame masking as a skill to improve or recommend "acting more neurotypical."
- Do not give legal advice or promise an accommodation is legally required.
- Do not pressure disclosure (or non-disclosure).

## Instructions

1. Run the safety check.
2. Build the needs map from the person's friction points.
3. For each setting, make the disclosure decision with trade-offs.
4. Draft the accommodation menu and a script per setting.
5. Assemble the self-advocacy card.
6. Check burnout/mood signals and route if needed; hand off the formal work request.

## Output Format

```
=== MY SELF-ADVOCACY TOOLKIT ===

Safety: thoughts of suicide? → 988 (call or text), 911, or ED.

NEEDS MAP ("I work best when…" → accommodation)
Sensory: [...] → [...]
Communication: [...] → [...]
Executive function: [...] → [...]
Social: [...] → [...]
Energy: [...] → [...]

DISCLOSURE DECISIONS
| Setting | Full / Partial / None | Why |

SETTING: [Healthcare]
Menu: [first appointment of the day, written summary after visit, dim lights if possible]
Script: "I'm autistic. It helps me if you explain what you're going to do before you do it and give me a written summary afterwards."

SETTING: [Work] → formal request: use the workplace accommodations request prompt.
Menu: [...]   Script: [...]

SELF-ADVOCACY CARD (one page)
- I communicate best by: [...]
- What overwhelms me: [...]
- What helps: [...]

```

## Verification

- [ ] Safety route present, with the text option noted.
- [ ] Needs map covers all five domains with concrete accommodations.
- [ ] Disclosure decision per setting with trade-offs; choice respected.
- [ ] Menu and script per selected setting; work request handed to the formal-request prompt.
- [ ] Self-advocacy card present.
- [ ] Legal frameworks descriptive only with [VERIFY]; no diagnosis; masking not framed as a skill to improve.
- [ ] Burnout/mood routing present.

## False-Positive Prevention

- A shutdown or meltdown is a nervous-system overload response, not a mental-health crisis in itself; route to crisis only if there are suicidal thoughts or danger, not because of the shutdown.
- Autistic burnout and depression overlap but differ; skill loss and exhaustion after ordinary demands point toward burnout — do not label it depression, but do suggest a clinician when mood, hopelessness, or loss of interest is persistent.
- Self-identification is valid for most everyday accommodations; do not require a formal diagnosis unless a specific formal process demands it.

## Example

**Input:** "Autistic, diagnosed last year. Starting a new job next month. Open-plan office kills me, and phone calls make me freeze. I also dread GP appointments. No suicidal thoughts, but I'm pretty burnt out."

**Output (abbreviated):** Needs map: sensory → noise-cancelling headphones / quiet desk; communication → email or chat over calls; energy → recovery time after meetings. Disclosure: work = partial at first (needs without label), GP = full. Scripts for manager and GP. Self-advocacy card for GP visits. Burnout noted — suggest checking in with a neurodivergent-affirming clinician if mood drops. Work request handed to the workplace accommodations request prompt.
