# Supporting a Partner Through Pregnancy and Postpartum — Recognition & Action

> **IF YOUR PARTNER IS SEEING OR HEARING THINGS OTHERS DON'T, IS CONFUSED OR EXTREMELY SUSPICIOUS, HASN'T SLEPT FOR DAYS EVEN WHEN THEY COULD, SAYS THINGS ABOUT THE BABY THAT DON'T MAKE SENSE, OR TALKS ABOUT HARMING THEMSELVES OR THE BABY: call 911 or take them to the nearest emergency department (ED) now, and do not leave them alone — or alone with the baby.** For suicidal thoughts, call or text **988** (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US). Support any time: **National Maternal Mental Health Hotline 1-833-TLC-MAMA (1-833-852-6262)** — it also supports partners. Outside the US, use your local emergency number or find a local crisis line at findahelpline.com. This is a support guide — it is **not** a diagnosis or a substitute for their care team.

## Objective

Help you tell the difference between the normal upheaval of a new baby and signs that your partner needs a clinician — or emergency help — and then act: say something in a way that lands, take real load off, get them connected to care, and keep an eye on your own mental health too. You don't need to diagnose anything. Your job is to notice, speak up, share the load, and connect them to help.

## When to Use

- Your partner is pregnant or within the first year after birth (or loss), and something seems off beyond exhaustion.
- You're not sure whether what you're seeing is "normal."
- You've noticed changes but don't know how to bring it up without upsetting them.
- You're struggling yourself — partners can develop perinatal depression and anxiety too.

## Inputs / Context

- Where things are: weeks pregnant or weeks/months postpartum; any loss.
- What you've noticed and for how long: tearfulness, withdrawal, anger, constant worry, checking, not sleeping when able, not eating, disinterest in or fear around the baby, statements of hopelessness, unusual beliefs or behavior.
- History: prior depression, anxiety, bipolar disorder, or postpartum episodes.
- What help is available (family, leave, money for support).
- How you are doing.

## Constraints

### Must

- **Gate (QA-08):** psychosis signs, suicidal statements, or talk of harming the baby → 911/ED or 988 as appropriate, and not left alone (or alone with the baby); stop the rest.
- Three recognition tiers: **Usually expected** (baby blues: tearful, up-and-down mood in the first ~2 weeks that eases), **Call their clinician this week** (lasting 2+ weeks, affecting functioning, constant worry, intrusive scary thoughts, not bonding, rage, can't sleep when able), **Emergency now** (gate signs).
- Flag **bipolar or prior postpartum history** → contact their care team proactively.
- A conversation script that leads with care and specifics, not blame (NE-07); includes "you're not a bad parent; this is common and treatable."
- Concrete load-sharing: a protected 4–5-hour sleep block, taking a night feed (expressed milk or formula if that's their choice), managing visitors, meals, one household task owned outright.
- Getting-to-care steps: offer to book, attend, and speak at the appointment if they want.
- **Partner's own check:** name that non-birthing partners can develop depression/anxiety; give the same hotline and a "talk to your own GP" step.
- Close with one action today (NE-17).

### Must Not

- Do not diagnose PPD, PPA, OCD, bipolar disorder, or psychosis.
- Do not advise confronting delusional beliefs or arguing in an emergency — stay calm and get help.
- Do not advise medication decisions, or pressure about feeding method.
- Do not suggest waiting to "see if it passes" when any week-tier sign has lasted 2+ weeks.

## Instructions

1. Run the emergency gate.
2. Sort what the partner has noticed into the three tiers.
3. Flag history risk.
4. Write the conversation script.
5. Build the load-sharing plan.
6. Lay out getting-to-care steps.
7. Do the partner's own check; set today's action.

## Output Format

```
=== SUPPORTING MY PARTNER — WHAT I'M SEEING & WHAT I'LL DO ===

>>> EMERGENCY NOW <<<
Seeing/hearing things, confused, very suspicious, no sleep for days despite chances, strange ideas about the baby,
or talk of harming self/baby → 911 or ED. Don't leave them alone or alone with the baby.
Suicidal statements → 988 (call/text) now.

What I've noticed: [...] for [how long]
Tier: [Usually expected / Call their clinician this week / Emergency]
History flag: [bipolar / previous postpartum episode → contact care team now]

What I'll say:
"I love you and I've noticed you've seemed [specific] for [time]. You're doing so much, and this isn't your fault —
it's really common after a baby and it's treatable. Can I help you call [OB/midwife/GP]? I'll come with you."

Load I'm taking:
- Protected sleep: [you sleep 10pm–3am; I take the feed]
- Visitors: [I'll manage/limit]
- Meals / household: [I own ...]

Getting to care: [book by (day) / I attend / I'll help describe what I've seen]

Me too: partners can get perinatal depression/anxiety. How I'm doing: [...]
  → 1-833-TLC-MAMA supports partners; my own GP: [...]

TODAY'S ACTION: [...]
```

## Verification

- [ ] Emergency gate with 911/ED, 988, and not-alone / not-alone-with-baby rule.
- [ ] Three recognition tiers with time thresholds.
- [ ] Bipolar/prior-episode flag present.
- [ ] Blame-free script with specifics.
- [ ] Concrete load-sharing including protected sleep.
- [ ] Getting-to-care steps and partner's own check present.
- [ ] No diagnosis, no medication or feeding pressure.

## False-Positive Prevention

- Tearfulness and mood swings in the first two weeks are common baby blues — reassure and support, but re-check at two weeks; do not escalate these alone to emergency.
- Being anxious and protective is normal new-parent behavior; intrusive fears become a "call this week" sign when they are constant, drive avoidance or checking, or stop sleep.
- Sleep deprivation explains a lot, but not everything: if symptoms persist after a protected sleep block or two, that points toward a clinician, not more waiting.

## Example

**Input:** "My wife had our son 5 weeks ago. She cries most days, says she's a terrible mum, and won't let anyone else hold him. She says she's fine. No weird beliefs or anything, and she hasn't mentioned hurting herself."

**Output (abbreviated):** Gate clear. Tier: call her clinician this week (5 weeks, persistent crying, harsh self-judgement, reluctance to let others hold the baby). Script built around what he's noticed. Load: he takes the 2 a.m. feed with expressed milk if she wants, manages visitors. Getting to care: he offers to book and attend the GP or midwife visit. Partner check: how he's sleeping and coping; hotline covers partners. Today: have the conversation after dinner.
