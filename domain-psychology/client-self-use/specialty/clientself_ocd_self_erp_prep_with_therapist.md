# OCD Self-ERP Preparation — For Therapist Review, Not Self-Administration

> **IF YOU ARE HAVING THOUGHTS OF SUICIDE OR MIGHT ACT ON THEM: call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US), call 911, or go to your nearest emergency department (ED) now.** OCD can be exhausting and hopelessness is common — reaching out is the right move. Outside the US, use your local emergency number or find a local crisis line at findahelpline.com. This is preparation for treatment with an OCD-trained clinician — it is **not** ERP, a diagnosis, or reassurance.

## Objective

Help you arrive at your first ERP sessions (or at a new ERP therapist) with the raw material already organised: what triggers the obsessions, every compulsion you can name — especially the mental ones — what you avoid, what other people do to accommodate the OCD, a *draft* hierarchy with SUDS estimates, and what you want from treatment. Your therapist uses this to build the real plan. You do **not** start exposures from this document.

## When to Use

- You're starting ERP, on a waitlist, or switching to an ERP-trained therapist and want to use the time well.
- Your therapist asked you to "think about your triggers and compulsions" before building a hierarchy.
- You've tried to do exposure alone, it went badly, and you want to bring a clearer picture to a professional.

**Distinct from:**
- `coping-by-concern/clientself_ocd_erp_exercise_with_therapist.md` — drafts the **next single exposure** once a hierarchy exists and ERP is under way. This prompt comes **before** that: it builds the inventory and first-draft hierarchy.
- `symptom-understanding/clientself_intrusive_thoughts_vs_ocd_signal.md` — helps decide whether a pattern looks like OCD at all. Use that first if you are unsure.
- `specialty-clinical/psychology_ocd_erp_provider_protocol.md` — the clinician's protocol that consumes this prep.

## When NOT to Use (safety carve-outs)

- Active suicidal thoughts or crisis → banner routes first.
- No plan to see an OCD-trained clinician → the first step is finding one (the IOCDF clinician directory is a common starting point), not this worksheet.
- Co-occurring trauma, an eating disorder, or psychosis → tell the clinician first; sequencing differs.
- If writing the inventory itself turns into reviewing, checking, or seeking certainty → stop there and bring what you have.

## Inputs / Context

- OCD theme(s) in general terms (contamination, harm, sexual, religious/scrupulosity, just-right, relationship, health, postpartum, other). No graphic detail needed.
- Situations, objects, places, or thoughts that set it off.
- Behavioural compulsions and mental compulsions; reassurance-seeking; avoidance.
- What family, partners, or coworkers do to accommodate.
- Who your therapist is and when you see them.

## Constraints

### Must

- Sections: **Themes**, **Trigger Map**, **Compulsion Inventory (behavioral / mental / reassurance)**, **Avoidance List**, **Accommodation List**, **Draft Hierarchy (SUDS estimates)**, **What I Want From Treatment**, **Questions for My Therapist**.
- Prompt explicitly for **mental compulsions** (reviewing, neutralising, counting, praying to undo, mentally checking feelings) — they are the most often missed.
- Hierarchy items carry **estimated** SUDS (0–100) and are labeled as guesses (QA-04) for the therapist to recalibrate.
- Aim for 10–15 hierarchy items spread across the SUDS range, without ordering them into a schedule.
- Mark the document **"Draft — do not start exposures until my therapist reviews this"** at top and bottom.
- End with a handoff block (NE-20).

### Must Not

- Do not give reassurance about the obsession's content ("you'd never do that," "that's not who you are") — reassurance is a compulsion; say so neutrally if asked.
- Do not schedule, sequence, or instruct any exposure or response prevention.
- Do not elicit graphic detail of intrusive thoughts.
- Do not diagnose OCD or rate severity (e.g., do not produce a Y-BOCS score).
- Do not suggest alcohol, cannabis, or as-needed medication to cope with anxiety.

## Instructions

1. Run the safety check; route if needed.
2. Name the themes in general language.
3. Walk the trigger map (situations → thoughts → urge).
4. Build the compulsion inventory with explicit mental-compulsion prompts.
5. List avoidance and accommodation.
6. Draft 10–15 hierarchy items with estimated SUDS; label them as estimates.
7. Capture goals ("what I'd do if OCD had less say") and questions.
8. Add the do-not-start marker and the handoff.

## Output Format

```
=== ERP PREP — DRAFT FOR MY THERAPIST (do not start exposures from this) ===

Safety: suicidal thoughts? → 988 / 911 / ED now.

Themes (general): [...]

Trigger Map:
| Situation / cue | Thought or doubt (general) | Urge | SUDS estimate |

Compulsion Inventory:
- Behavioural: [...]
- Mental (most missed): [reviewing / neutralising / counting / mental checking / praying to undo]
- Reassurance-seeking: [asking people / googling / asking AI / confessing]

Avoidance List: [places, objects, people, words, media]
Accommodation List: [who does what — e.g., partner answers "are you sure?" questions]

Draft Hierarchy (my ESTIMATES — therapist will recalibrate):
| # | Situation | Est. SUDS |
(10–15 items across the range; unordered for scheduling)

What I Want From Treatment: [what I'd do more of if OCD had less say]

Questions for My Therapist:
1. [...]
2. [...]

--- HANDOFF ---
Bring to: [therapist] on [date]. Draft only — no exposures until reviewed.
```

## Verification

- [ ] Safety routing present at top and in output.
- [ ] All eight sections present.
- [ ] Mental compulsions and reassurance-seeking explicitly inventoried.
- [ ] Hierarchy has 10–15 items, SUDS labeled as estimates, no schedule or instructions.
- [ ] No reassurance, no graphic content, no diagnosis or severity score.
- [ ] "Do not start exposures" marker at top and bottom; handoff block present.

## False-Positive Prevention

- A user asking "is this thought normal?" mid-worksheet is often seeking reassurance; the right response is to note it as a possible reassurance compulsion for the inventory, not to answer the content.
- Not every avoidance is OCD-driven (disliking hospitals after a bereavement is not contamination OCD). Record it and let the therapist classify; do not force items into the OCD frame.
- A high SUDS estimate is not a warning sign in itself — it is a hierarchy data point, not a crisis signal. Crisis routing is triggered by suicidal thoughts, not by anxiety intensity.

## Example

**Input:** "Starting ERP next month. Contamination stuff — door handles, public bathrooms. I wash a lot and I ask my partner if things are clean. I also replay whether I touched something."

**Output (abbreviated):** Themes: contamination. Trigger map rows for door handles, public restrooms, shopping carts. Compulsions — behavioral: repeated handwashing, using sleeves on handles; mental: replaying contact; reassurance: asking partner "is this clean?". Accommodation: partner opens doors, answers cleanliness questions. Draft hierarchy of 12 items from "touch own front-door handle" (est. 20) to "use public restroom without washing beyond once" (est. 90), labeled as estimates. Questions: "How do we handle my partner's reassurance?" Marker: do not start exposures until reviewed.
