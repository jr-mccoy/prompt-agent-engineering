# What to Expect at My First Psychiatry Appointment

## Objective

Demystify a first appointment with a psychiatrist or psychiatric prescriber (PMHNP, etc.) so I walk in prepared and less anxious. This explains what these appointments **generally** look like — it is not a substitute for the evaluation itself, and it doesn't tell me what they'll decide.

## When to Use

- I have a first psychiatry appointment coming up and don't know what happens.
- I'm nervous about being judged, "committed," or put on medication on the spot.
- I want to bring the right information so I don't forget half of it.
- A therapist or doctor referred me and I'm not sure why.

## Inputs / Context

- Why I'm going / what was referred (anxiety, mood, sleep, ADHD eval, med review, etc.).
- The format (in person or telehealth) and how long it's scheduled for, if I know.
- My biggest worry about the visit.
- Whether I'm already on any medications or have records.

## Constraints

### Must

- Walk through the appointment as a **general** sequence, with an everyday framing (RT-04) (e.g., "like a thorough intake interview, not a test you can fail").
- List concretely **what to bring** (medication list, past treatment history, key dates, questions, insurance/ID).
- State plainly that medication **may or may not** be started at the first visit, and that it's a conversation, not an automatic prescription.
- Address common fears directly and neutrally (being judged, being hospitalized, being dismissed).
- Include questions I can ask, and an escalation pathway if I'm in crisis before the appointment.

### Must Not

- Don't predict what they'll diagnose, prescribe, or decide.
- Don't recommend any specific medication or dosing.
- Don't tell me to start, stop, or change any medication before the visit.
- Don't promise the visit will go a particular way.
- Don't substitute this for the clinical evaluation.

## Instructions

1. Reassure and frame what kind of appointment this is.
2. Walk through the typical flow (intake questions, history, mental-status conversation, plan discussion).
3. List what to bring.
4. Set expectations about medication being optional and a shared decision.
5. Address the common fears.
6. Provide questions to ask and a crisis pathway.

## Output Format

```
=== YOUR FIRST PSYCHIATRY APPOINTMENT — WHAT TO EXPECT ===

What this appointment is (general):
A structured conversation where the prescriber gets to know you and your history so they can make a plan with you. Think of it like a thorough intake interview — there's no way to "fail" it. Honesty helps them help you.

What they'll typically ask about:
- Why you're coming in / what you've been experiencing and for how long
- Sleep, appetite, energy, concentration, mood
- Past mental-health treatment, therapy, medications (what worked / didn't)
- Medical history and current meds/supplements
- Family history
- Alcohol, cannabis, other substances, caffeine
- Safety: thoughts of harming yourself or others (a routine question — not a trap)

How long: often 45–90 minutes for a first visit (shorter for telehealth follow-ups later).

What to bring:
- [ ] A list of all current medications & supplements (with doses if you know them)
- [ ] A short history: past diagnoses, past meds, hospitalizations, therapists
- [ ] Key dates / timeline of what's been going on
- [ ] Your top 2–3 goals or questions, written down
- [ ] Insurance card / ID; pharmacy name & location
- [ ] (Telehealth) a quiet, private space and a charged device

About medication:
- They MAY suggest a medication, or they may not — sometimes the first visit is just assessment.
- It's a conversation: you can ask why, what to expect, and what the alternatives are.
- You don't have to decide everything in the room. It's okay to say "I'd like to think about it."

Common worries, addressed:
- "Will I be judged?" — Prescribers hear all of this every day; candor is the point.
- "Will they hospitalize me?" — Only if there's an immediate safety risk; routine visits are not about that.
- "What if they don't take me seriously?" — Bring written notes; if you feel unheard, you can say so or seek a second opinion.

Questions I can ask:
1. "What do you think is going on, and what are my options?"
2. "If we try medication, what should I expect and when?"
3. "What are the non-medication options?"
4. "How and when do we follow up?"

If I'm in crisis before the appointment:
- Worsening / can't wait → call the office; ask about an earlier slot.
- Thoughts of suicide / can't stay safe → 988 (call or text, US) now, or go to the nearest ER.

Reminder: This is what these visits generally look like — the actual assessment and plan are up to you and the prescriber.
```

## Verification

- [ ] Appointment described as a general sequence with reassuring framing.
- [ ] Concrete "what to bring" checklist included.
- [ ] States medication may or may not be started; framed as shared decision.
- [ ] Common fears addressed neutrally.
- [ ] No specific medication or dosing advice; no instruction to change meds pre-visit.
- [ ] Questions to ask included.
- [ ] Crisis pathway (office / 988 / ER) included.
- [ ] Closing reminder defers the actual assessment to the prescriber.
