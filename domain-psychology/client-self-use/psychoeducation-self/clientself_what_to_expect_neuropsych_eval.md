# What to Expect From a Neuropsychological Evaluation

## Objective

Demystify a neuropsychological evaluation for a client who has one scheduled or is considering one. Explain, in plain language: what it's for, the typical multi-session structure, the general categories of testing (without revealing test content), how to prepare, and the usual timeline to results and a feedback session. This is orientation, not the evaluation.

## When to Use

- A neuropsych evaluation has been referred or scheduled (for memory concerns, ADHD/learning questions, post-concussion, post-stroke, baseline before treatment, etc.).
- The client is anxious about being "tested," worried about cost/time, or unsure what they'll get out of it.
- The client wants to prepare well so the results are accurate.

## Inputs / Context

- The reason for the referral, if known (general — e.g., "to understand attention/memory concerns").
- Who referred it and what question they're trying to answer.
- Practical worries (length, fatigue, what to tell work/school).
- Any conditions that affect testing (vision/hearing aids, medications, anxiety about performance).

## Constraints

### Must

- Explain the **purpose** in plain terms: a structured picture of how thinking skills work, to answer a referral question and guide a plan.
- Describe the **typical multi-session structure** (interview → testing session(s) → scoring → feedback), noting it varies by clinic.
- Describe testing in **general categories only** (e.g., attention, memory, language, problem-solving, processing speed) — **never reveal specific test items, questions, or content**, and explain that preparing answers in advance would distort results.
- Use an analogy to set expectations (RT-04) — e.g., it's more like a thorough check-up of different "systems," not a pass/fail exam.
- Give concrete prep tips: good sleep, eat normally, bring glasses/hearing aids, and ask the **referrer** whether to take usual medications (e.g., stimulants) on testing day — route the medication question to the referrer, not answer it here.
- Set the timeline expectation: results usually come at a later **feedback session**, often a few weeks out, with a written report.

### Must Not

- Do not reveal, hint at, or let the client "study" specific test content.
- Do not predict the client's results or what diagnosis might emerge.
- Do not advise whether to take or hold a medication on testing day — route to the referrer/prescriber.
- Do not interpret any prior scores the client mentions.

## Instructions

1. State the purpose plainly and give one analogy.
2. Walk through the typical session-by-session structure.
3. Describe the general testing categories, with the no-studying note.
4. Give the prep checklist, including the route-to-referrer medication question.
5. Set the results/feedback timeline expectation.

## Output Format

```
=== WHAT A NEUROPSYCH EVALUATION IS LIKE ===
(General overview — clinics differ)

What it's for:
A structured way to see how different thinking skills are working, to answer the question your referrer is asking (e.g., about attention, memory, or learning) and help guide a plan.
Picture it: less like a pass/fail exam, more like a thorough check-up of several different "systems" so the picture is complete.

Typical structure (often spread over more than one day):
1. Interview — your history, concerns, daily life, goals.
2. Testing session(s) — working through a variety of tasks with the examiner; can run several hours, often with breaks.
3. Scoring & report — the neuropsychologist scores and writes up results afterward.
4. Feedback session — you come back to hear and discuss the results.

What the testing covers (general categories only):
- Attention and concentration
- Learning and memory
- Language
- Visual/spatial skills
- Problem-solving and reasoning
- Processing speed
Note: there's nothing to study, and you shouldn't try to. The point is to measure how you naturally do — preparing answers would actually make the results less useful to you.

How to prepare:
- [ ] Get a normal night's sleep beforehand
- [ ] Eat as you usually would
- [ ] Bring glasses, hearing aids, and any device you normally use
- [ ] Bring a list of your medications
- [ ] Plan for it to be long and a bit tiring — that's expected
- [ ] Ask your REFERRER whether to take your usual medications on testing day (especially attention/stimulant meds). This varies by purpose — let them decide, not me.

Getting results:
- Results usually come at the later feedback session, commonly a few weeks after testing, along with a written report.
- Bring questions to that session and ask for plain-language explanations of anything unclear.
```

## Verification

- [ ] Purpose explained plainly with an analogy.
- [ ] Multi-session structure described with a "clinics differ" note.
- [ ] Testing described in general categories only — no item content.
- [ ] Explicit "nothing to study / don't prepare answers" note.
- [ ] Prep checklist included.
- [ ] Medication-on-testing-day question routed to the referrer.
- [ ] Results/feedback timeline set.
- [ ] No predicted results; no interpretation of prior scores.
