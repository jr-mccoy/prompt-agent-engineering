# Understanding My Partner's Diagnosis — Support Without Taking It On

## Objective

Help me understand, in general terms, a diagnosis my partner (or close family member / friend) was given, so I can be supportive **without** becoming their therapist, absorbing the condition as my own, or burning myself out. This explains the diagnosis generally — it does not interpret my partner's specific situation, and it does not put me in charge of their treatment.

## When to Use

- My partner was diagnosed and I want to understand what they're dealing with.
- I keep trying to "fix" them and it's straining us both.
- I don't know where supporting ends and over-functioning begins.
- I've lost track of my own needs since their diagnosis.

## Inputs / Context

- The diagnosis as it was described to me (general name).
- My role (partner, parent, adult child, close friend).
- What I'm currently doing to help, and whether it's sustainable.
- Where I feel resentment, exhaustion, or that I'm walking on eggshells.
- Whether there's any safety concern (self-harm, suicide talk, substance crisis).

## Constraints

### Must

- Explain the diagnosis **in general** terms with an everyday analogy (RT-04), framed as "people with this often…," never "your partner is…".
- Distinguish **supporting** (alongside) from **over-functioning / caretaking** (carrying it for them) with concrete contrasting examples.
- Give boundary language I can actually say out loud.
- Include a **"My own self-care is not optional"** section — supporting someone is not the same as dissolving into their condition.
- Include a clear safety-escalation pathway for *their* safety (988 / ER) and note I'm not responsible for being their crisis service.

### Must Not

- Don't tell me what my partner "has" or "really" feels — only their clinician and they know that.
- Don't coach me to diagnose, monitor, or "manage" my partner's treatment or medication.
- Don't recommend medication or dosing for anyone.
- Don't tell me to stay in or leave the relationship — that's mine to decide.
- Don't frame the diagnosis as something I caused or can cure.

## Instructions

1. Explain the diagnosis in general, with an analogy.
2. Describe what kinds of support actually help people with this, in general.
3. Contrast helpful support vs. over-functioning, with examples.
4. Offer boundary scripts.
5. Build a short self-care / refuel section for me.
6. Lay out the safety pathway and the limit of my role.

## Output Format

```
=== SUPPORTING SOMEONE WITH: [diagnosis] (general) ===

What this diagnosis generally involves:
[Plain explanation + everyday analogy.]

What tends to actually help (general):
- [Support that helps people with this — e.g., steadiness, low-pressure presence, encouraging them to use their own treatment]
- [Another]

Support vs. over-functioning:
| Supporting (alongside)            | Over-functioning (carrying it for them) |
|-----------------------------------|------------------------------------------|
| "I'm here. What do you need?"     | Canceling my own life to monitor them    |
| Encouraging them to call their    | Becoming their unpaid therapist          |
|   clinician                       |                                          |
| Letting them have hard feelings   | Trying to talk them out of every feeling |

Boundaries I can say out loud:
- "I love you and I want to support you. I can't be your therapist — that's what [their clinician] is for."
- "I need [a night off / quiet time / my own appointment]. That's not me abandoning you."
- "I'll listen, and I'm not able to fix this for you."

My own self-care is not optional:
- One thing I'll keep doing for me this week: [____]
- One person I can talk to about MY load: [____]
- A sign I'm running on empty: [____] → when I see it, I [____]

Their safety (and the limit of my role):
- If they're in crisis but safe-ish → encourage them to contact their clinician.
- If they talk about suicide or can't stay safe → 988 (call/text, US) now, or go to / call for the ER. I can help them get there; I am not their crisis service.
- If there's danger to me → my safety comes first.

Reminder: This is general information. Their clinician — and they — are the only ones who know their actual situation and treatment.
```

## Verification

- [ ] Diagnosis explained generally with an analogy; not applied to the partner.
- [ ] Support vs. over-functioning contrasted with examples.
- [ ] Boundary scripts provided.
- [ ] Self-care section present and framed as not optional.
- [ ] Safety escalation (988 / ER) included with role limits.
- [ ] No medication or dosing advice.
- [ ] No stay/leave directive.
- [ ] Closing reminder routes specifics to the partner and their clinician.
