# Saying Hard Things to a Therapist — Rehearsal

## Objective

Help a client rehearse a high-stakes disclosure or piece of feedback they want to bring to their therapist. Output should:

1. Name what the client wants to say in their own words.
2. Surface the specific fear about saying it.
3. Generate 2–3 ways to open the topic (varying directness).
4. Anticipate the therapist's likely response and how the client wants to handle each branch.
5. Reduce avoidance by making the actual sentence concrete.

## When to Use

- Wanting to disclose SI, NSSI, substance use, an attempt, or harm content the therapist doesn't know about.
- Wanting to say the therapy isn't helping or a particular technique isn't working.
- Wanting to end therapy or take a break.
- Wanting to address something the therapist said or did that hurt or felt off.
- Wanting to admit not having done homework / having lied / having omitted.
- Wanting to ask about pricing, scheduling, modality change.

## Inputs / Context

- The hard thing in the client's own words.
- What the client most fears will happen if they say it.
- How long they've been holding it.
- Their read of the therapist's typical style (warm, direct, clinical, formal).
- How much time they have left in the upcoming session.

## Constraints

### Must

- Output sections in order: **What I Want to Say**, **What I'm Afraid Of**, **Three Ways to Open It (low / medium / high directness)**, **What Their Likely Responses Are and How I Want to Handle Each**, **What Outcome I'm Hoping For**, **Backup if I Freeze in the Room**.
- Generate the actual opening sentences, not "you could mention that..."
- Include a freeze backup: a single sentence the client can read off a phone if they can't get the words out.
- For safety disclosures (SI / attempts / NSSI / substance escalation / abuse), normalize that therapists are equipped for this and that omission has been the heavier weight; frame disclosure as relief work, not punishment.

### Must Not

- Don't promise how the therapist will react.
- Don't talk the client out of the disclosure if they want to make it.
- Don't substitute therapeutic processing for rehearsal — this is rehearsal.
- Don't moralize about prior omissions or "you should have told them earlier."

## Instructions

1. Ask the client what they want to say, in their own words.
2. Ask what they're most afraid of (judgment, abandonment, being committed, having care escalated, being seen differently, being told they're "fine").
3. Generate 3 opening sentences at low / medium / high directness.
4. For each likely therapist response, generate the client's prepared response.
5. Provide a freeze backup the client can read off a phone or note.

## Output Format

```
=== REHEARSAL ===

What I Want to Say:
"[In my own words, the actual content]"

What I'm Afraid Of:
- Most likely fear: [...]
- Underneath that: [...]

Three Ways to Open It:

Low directness — soft entry:
"I want to bring something up that I've been sitting with. It's been hard to know how to say it. Can I just start?"

Medium directness — naming the topic:
"Before we get started today, I need to tell you something I've been holding. It's about [topic, named generally]."

High directness — saying the thing:
"I need to tell you that [the hard thing, said directly]."

Likely Therapist Responses and How I Want to Handle Each:

If they say: "Thank you for telling me. Can you say more?"
   I'll say: [...]

If they say: "We need to talk about safety / I need to ask you some questions about that"
   I'll say: [...]
   I'll remember: this is them doing their job; it's not punishment.

If they pause for a long time:
   I'll say: [...]
   I'll remember: pauses don't mean disapproval; they often mean they're with me.

If they don't seem to track how big this is for me:
   I'll say: "I want to make sure you hear how heavy this has been to carry. Can we slow down here?"

What Outcome I'm Hoping For:
- [Being heard / being asked questions / a plan being made / nothing changing / something specific being adjusted]

Backup If I Freeze in the Room:
[Single sentence I can read off my phone, exactly as I'll say it]
"[The exact sentence]"

Bring my phone with this written down: ☐
```

## Verification

- [ ] Hard thing in client's own words preserved.
- [ ] Specific fear named.
- [ ] Three openings at varying directness, each as actual sentences.
- [ ] Branch responses anticipated for at least 3 likely therapist reactions.
- [ ] Freeze backup is one readable sentence.
- [ ] No promise of therapist's response.
- [ ] No moralizing about delay; safety disclosures framed as relief.
- [ ] Outcome the client wants is named.
