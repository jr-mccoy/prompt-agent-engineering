# Exercise-as-Antidepressant Plan

## Objective

Help you build a movement plan aimed at its mood benefit — not at fitness goals, weight, or looks. Output a plan with evidence-based dose framing (described as general guidance, not a personal prescription), a too-small-to-fail start, mood-paired tracking so you can see whether it's helping, and explicit overlap with behavioral activation. Movement is one tool among several, and it sits alongside — not instead of — therapy and medical care.

## When to Use

- Low mood or mild-to-moderate depression, and you want to use movement as part of feeling better.
- You've heard "exercise helps depression" and want a realistic plan that survives low energy and low motivation.
- You're already in therapy and want a between-session structure that pairs with behavioral activation.

## Inputs / Context

- Your current activity level and what kinds of movement you don't hate (walking, dancing, cycling, swimming, lifting, yard work).
- Energy and motivation right now (honest — depression flattens both).
- Any health conditions, injuries, chest symptoms, pregnancy, or being new to exercise (see the clearance note).
- When in your day movement could realistically fit.
- How your mood tends to move and what "a good day" looks like.

## Constraints

### Must

- **Medical-clearance carve-out (required):** If the user is new to exercise, older, pregnant, or has any heart/lung condition, chest pain, dizziness/fainting, or an injury, advise a quick check with a doctor before starting or intensifying — phrased plainly, not as a scare.
- Frame "dose" as **general evidence-based guidance, not a prescription** — note that even light, below-guideline movement is associated with mood benefit, and that *something beats nothing*; reference general public-health activity guidance descriptively, with uncertainty acknowledged.
- Start too-small-to-fail (e.g., a 5–10 minute walk), explicitly designed for low-energy days.
- Pair each session with a quick before/after mood rating so the person gathers their own evidence.
- Make the behavioral-activation overlap explicit: action precedes motivation; you don't wait to feel like it.
- **Severity / safety route (required):** If depression is severe, functioning is collapsing, or there are any suicidal thoughts, state that exercise is not a substitute for care — contact a clinician now, and in the US call or text **988** (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline). Do not treat movement as the fix in that case.

### Must Not

- Don't prescribe specific intensities, heart-rate zones, or a rigid weekly mileage as medical advice.
- Don't frame exercise as weight loss, body change, or "earning" food.
- Don't moralize about being sedentary or imply low motivation is a character flaw.
- Don't position exercise as a replacement for therapy or medication.

## Instructions

1. Run the medical-clearance check; advise a doctor's check if any flag is present.
2. Run the severity/safety check; if depression is severe or SI is present, route to a clinician / 988 before proceeding.
3. Pick a kind of movement the person doesn't dread and a realistic slot.
4. Set a too-small-to-fail start with a low-energy-day fallback.
5. Frame dose as general guidance with a "something beats nothing" floor.
6. Build mood-paired tracking (before/after rating).
7. Make the behavioral-activation logic explicit and set a gentle scale-up.

## Output Format

```
=== MOVEMENT-FOR-MOOD PLAN ===

>>> CHECK FIRST <<<
Medical clearance: New to exercise / older / pregnant / heart or lung condition / chest pain / dizziness / injury?
  → Quick check with a doctor before starting or pushing harder. (Routine, not a scare.)
Safety: If my depression feels severe, I can't function, or I'm having thoughts of suicide —
  exercise is NOT the fix. Contact a clinician now. US: call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline).

The point of this plan: movement for MOOD — not weight, not looks, not "earning" anything.

Movement I don't dread: [walking / dancing / cycling / swimming / lifting / yard work / ...]
Realistic slot: [when, where]

Dose (general guidance, not a prescription):
- The "more is somewhat better" research points toward regular moderate activity, but the honest headline is:
  SOMETHING BEATS NOTHING. Even short, light movement is linked to better mood.
- I'm not chasing a number. I'm building a floor I can keep.

Too-small-to-fail start:
- Standard day: [e.g., a 10-minute walk]
- Low-energy day (depression days count too): [e.g., step outside and around the block once]

Mood-paired tracking (gather my own evidence):
- Before (0–10): ___   After (0–10): ___   Note: [...]
- Over a week, I'll look for: even a small, repeated lift.

Behavioral-activation logic (the key mindset):
- I move FIRST; motivation usually shows up DURING or after, not before.
- "I don't feel like it" is expected on low days — that's exactly when the tiny version is for.

Scale-up (only once the tiny version feels easy):
- I'll gently add: [a few more minutes / one more day], no pressure, no streak-worship.

Bring to my therapist / doctor:
- "I'm using movement for my mood. Here's what I tried and what my before/after ratings showed."
```

## Verification

- [ ] Medical-clearance carve-out present and plainly worded.
- [ ] Severity/SI safety route present, with 988 and a clinician handoff.
- [ ] Dose framed as general guidance, not a prescription; "something beats nothing" floor stated.
- [ ] Start is too-small-to-fail with a low-energy fallback.
- [ ] Mood-paired before/after tracking included.
- [ ] Behavioral-activation logic (action before motivation) made explicit.
- [ ] Framed for mood, not weight/looks; not positioned as a replacement for therapy/medication.
- [ ] No rigid intensity prescriptions, no moralizing about being sedentary.
