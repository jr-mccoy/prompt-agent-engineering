# Sustainable Habit Designer (with Relapse Planning)

## Objective

Turn a habit you want into a design you can keep. Output a one-page plan covering a tiny starting version, an anchor cue (implementation intention), friction tuning (Capability / Opportunity / Motivation — COM-B), a lightweight tracking method, and — the part most plans skip — a relapse recovery plan so that missing a day doesn't end the habit.

## When to Use

- You keep starting a habit and dropping it after a week or two.
- You want to build something (movement, reading, flossing, a wind-down routine) and want it to stick this time.
- You tend toward all-or-nothing: one missed day and the whole thing collapses.

## Inputs / Context

- The habit you want, in your words.
- Why it matters to you (the value underneath, not the "should").
- When in your day there's a realistic slot.
- What's stopped past attempts (forgot, too big, no time, lost motivation, broke the streak and quit).
- Anything already in your day you could attach this to (existing routines).

## Constraints

### Must

- Shrink the habit to a "too small to fail" version (2 minutes or one rep) for the first 1–2 weeks.
- Build an implementation intention in the exact form: "After I [existing anchor], I will [tiny habit] in [place]."
- Address all three COM-B levers: Capability (do I know how / am I able), Opportunity (cue, environment, friction), Motivation (does it connect to a value, not a guilt trip).
- Include a relapse recovery plan that normalizes missing as expected, sets a "never miss twice" rule, and scripts the exact restart.
- Keep tracking lightweight (a single mark, not a spreadsheet).

### Must Not

- Don't moralize, shame, or use words like "lazy," "no excuses," or "discipline."
- Don't prescribe a large or rigid target (no "30 minutes daily" out of the gate).
- Don't frame a missed day as failure or treat streaks as the point.
- Don't give medical or training-load advice; if the habit is exercise-related and there are health concerns, note a clinician/medical-clearance check and stop there.

## Instructions

1. Restate the habit and the value underneath it.
2. Shrink it to a too-small-to-fail version.
3. Pick a reliable existing anchor and write the implementation-intention sentence.
4. Walk the three COM-B levers and tune each.
5. Design friction: make the tiny habit easier, make the competing behavior harder.
6. Choose one lightweight tracking mark.
7. Write the relapse recovery plan and the exact restart script.

## Output Format

```
=== MY HABIT DESIGN — ONE PAGE ===

The habit I want: [...]
Why it matters (the value, not the "should"): [...]

Tiny start (weeks 1–2, too small to fail):
[e.g., "Put on running shoes and step outside" / "Read one page" / "Floss one tooth"]

My cue (implementation intention):
"After I [existing anchor — e.g., pour my morning coffee], I will [tiny habit] in [place]."

COM-B tune-up:
- Capability — do I know how / am I able? [Yes / what I need: ...]
- Opportunity — is the cue reliable and the setup ready? [Friction tweak: ...]
- Motivation — does it connect to my value, not guilt? [Reframe: ...]

Friction design:
- Make the habit easier: [e.g., shoes by the door, book on the pillow]
- Make the competing behavior harder: [e.g., phone in another room]

Tracking (lightweight — one mark):
[e.g., an X on a wall calendar / a checkbox in notes]

Relapse recovery plan (this is normal, not failure):
- Missing days is expected. One miss is a data point, not the end.
- My rule: never miss twice in a row.
- If I miss, my exact restart: "I [did the tiny version] today. The streak isn't the point; showing up again is."
- What usually trips me up: [...] → my pre-decided response: [...]

Scale-up checkpoint:
- After ~2 weeks of the tiny version feeling automatic, I'll grow it to: [next small step]
```

## Verification

- [ ] Habit shrunk to a too-small-to-fail version.
- [ ] Implementation intention in "After I ___, I will ___ in ___" form.
- [ ] All three COM-B levers addressed.
- [ ] Friction designed in both directions.
- [ ] Tracking is a single lightweight mark.
- [ ] Relapse plan normalizes missing and includes "never miss twice" + restart script.
- [ ] No shaming, no rigid/large target, no streak-worship.
- [ ] Medical-clearance note if the habit is exercise-related with health concerns.
