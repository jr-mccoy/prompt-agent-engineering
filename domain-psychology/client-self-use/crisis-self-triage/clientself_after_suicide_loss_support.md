# After Losing Someone to Suicide — Initial Support

> **IF YOU ARE HAVING THOUGHTS OF SUICIDE YOURSELF, OR FEEL YOU MIGHT NOT BE SAFE: call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US) now, call 911, or go to your nearest emergency department (ED).** Suicide loss raises your own risk, and reaching out is the strong thing to do. This is a support aid for the early days of grief — it is **not** an emergency service, therapy, or a substitute for a clinician or a survivor-of-suicide-loss group.

## Objective

Offer an initial, gentle framework for the first weeks after losing someone to suicide: validate the specific, tangled grief of this loss; organize practical support; help you decide what to tell people; point toward survivor-of-suicide-loss resources; build a check on your own safety; and hand off to a therapist. The tool supports and organizes — it does not provide grief therapy or assess your risk.

## When to Use

- You've recently lost someone to suicide and don't know where to begin.
- The guilt, the "why," or the stigma feels like too much to hold.
- People are asking what happened and you don't know what to say.
- You want to find support and aren't sure it exists for this.

## Inputs / Context

- Your relationship to the person and roughly how recent the loss is (no details required).
- What's hitting hardest right now (guilt, "why," anger, numbness, replaying).
- What practical things are pressing (people asking, arrangements, work).
- Whether you've had any thoughts about your own safety.
- Who, if anyone, is supporting you right now.

## Constraints

### Must

- Open the output with the 988/911/ED escalation block, visible before anything else.
- Cover: **What You're Feeling Is Normal for This Loss**, **First Weeks — Practical Support**, **What to Tell People (your choice)**, **Finding Survivor-of-Suicide-Loss Resources**, **Watching My Own Safety**, **Bring This to a Therapist**.
- Validate (NE-07) the suicide-specific grief: guilt and "what if I'd...", the unanswerable "why," anger at the person, relief mixed with shame, and stigma — name these as common, not as failings.
- "Watching My Own Safety" must include the fact that suicide-loss survivors are at elevated risk, with explicit 988/clinician routing.
- Make clear "what to tell people" is the survivor's choice — they owe no one the details, and "died by suicide" / "I'd rather not go into it" are both okay.
- End with a handoff line: "Bring this to a therapist," ideally one familiar with grief or suicide loss.

### Must Not

- Do not push the person to find meaning, "move on," or follow grief stages on a timeline.
- Do not assign blame, speculate on the deceased's reasons, or answer the "why."
- Do not provide grief therapy or assess the survivor's suicide risk.
- Do not promise that specific feelings will pass by a certain time; do not recommend medication.

## Instructions

1. Lead with the escalation block and a validating opening.
2. Normalize the specific, contradictory feelings of suicide loss.
3. Offer light, concrete first-weeks practical support.
4. Give language options for what to tell people, framed as their choice.
5. Point to survivor-of-suicide-loss resource types (groups, lines, organizations).
6. Build the personal-safety check with 988/clinician routing.
7. Close with the therapist handoff.

## Output Format

```
=== AFTER A SUICIDE LOSS — FIRST SUPPORT ===

⚠️ IF YOU'RE THINKING ABOUT SUICIDE OR DON'T FEEL SAFE:
   call or text 988 now · call 911 · go to the nearest ED.
Losing someone this way raises your own risk. Reaching out is strength, not weakness.
This is a support aid — not therapy, not an emergency service.

I'm so sorry. This is one of the hardest losses there is. Let's go gently.

1) WHAT YOU'RE FEELING IS NORMAL FOR THIS LOSS:
- Guilt / "what if I'd...": almost universal, and it doesn't mean you failed them.
- The unanswerable "why": you may never fully know, and that is not yours to solve.
- Anger at them — and then guilt for the anger. Both can be true.
- Numbness, relief, shame, replaying the last contact. None of this makes you bad.
- Stigma from others: their discomfort is about them, not about your grief.

2) FIRST WEEKS — PRACTICAL SUPPORT:
- One small thing today: [eat / water / sleep / step outside].
- Let one trusted person carry a logistics task: ____________
- Lower the bar: you don't have to do grief "right" or fast.

3) WHAT TO TELL PEOPLE (your choice — you owe no one the details):
- Direct: "They died by suicide."
- Boundaried: "I'd rather not go into how. Thank you for caring."
- Whatever I choose can change day to day. Both are okay.

4) FINDING SURVIVOR-OF-SUICIDE-LOSS RESOURCES:
- Survivor-of-suicide-loss support groups (in person / online) — this grief has its own communities.
- 988 (call/text) for support, including for grief and your own safety.
- A national suicide-prevention/survivor organization for group locators and guides.
- A therapist experienced in grief or suicide loss.

5) WATCHING MY OWN SAFETY:
- If thoughts of suicide show up for me: call/text 988 or tell my clinician today.
- My early warning signs: ____________
- One person I'll tell if I'm not okay: ____________

HANDOFF: Bring this page to a therapist — ideally one who knows grief or suicide loss. ☐
```
