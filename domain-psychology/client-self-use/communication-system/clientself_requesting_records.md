# Requesting Your Clinical Records — Letter Draft

## Objective

Produce a clean records-request letter you can send to a therapist, clinic, or records department that asks for your mental health records under the HIPAA right of access — naming what records, in what format, for what dates, and where to send them. Keep it factual and procedural.

## When to Use

- Transferring to a new provider who wants prior records.
- Keeping a personal copy of your treatment history.
- Reviewing what's in your chart (intake, diagnoses, treatment plan, medication history, discharge summary).
- Supporting a disability, school, or benefits application that asks for records.

## Inputs / Context

- Your full name, date of birth, and any patient/record ID you have.
- The provider or clinic name and records-department address or portal.
- Which records you want (everything, or specific items: intake, progress notes, treatment plans, assessments, billing, medication list).
- Date range you need.
- Format you want (electronic copy, paper, or sent directly to another provider).
- Where to send them (to you, or to a named third party with your authorization).

## Constraints

### Must

- Output a sendable letter with: your identifying info, the provider addressee, a specific records request, format, date range, delivery destination, and a signature line.
- Explicitly invoke the **HIPAA right of access** (a patient's general right to inspect and obtain a copy of their own records).
- Include a plain-language note that **psychotherapy notes** — a provider's separately-kept process notes — are treated differently under HIPAA and may not be released on the same basis as the rest of the record; this is procedural, not a judgment about any specific provider.
- Note generally that providers usually must respond within a set timeframe and may charge a reasonable, cost-based copying fee — frame as "ask the provider about their fee and timeline."
- Stay procedural. Route any legal question (a dispute, a denial, a harm) to an attorney or a patient advocate.

### Must Not

- Don't give legal advice, interpret the client's specific legal situation, or predict whether a request will be granted.
- Don't claim records "must" be released within an exact number of days as legal fact — direct the client to confirm current rules with the provider or HHS.
- Don't draft threats or demands; this is a routine access request.
- Don't include argument about treatment quality — that belongs in a different process.

## Instructions

1. Collect the client's identifying info and the provider's records contact.
2. Pin down which records, what date range, and the desired format.
3. Confirm the delivery destination (self or authorized third party).
4. Draft the letter invoking the HIPAA right of access.
5. Add the psychotherapy-notes plain-language note and the fee/timeline note.
6. Add a signature block and a follow-up line.

## Output Format

```
=== RECORDS REQUEST LETTER ===

[My full name]
[My address]
[Phone / email]
[Date of birth: __________]   [Patient/Record ID, if known: __________]

[Date]

[Provider / Clinic name]
Attn: Medical Records / Health Information Department
[Address or secure portal]

Re: Request for access to my health records (HIPAA Right of Access)

Dear Records Department,

I am requesting access to my own health records under my HIPAA right of access.
I am the patient identified above.

Records requested:
[ ] My complete record
[ ] Specific items: [intake/assessment, progress notes, treatment plan(s),
    diagnoses, medication history, discharge summary, billing — list what you want]

Date range: [From __________ to __________ / All dates of service]

Format: [Electronic copy (PDF) / Paper copy / Sent directly to the provider below]

Please send the records to:
[ ] Me, at the address/email above
[ ] Authorized third party: [Name, address] — I have attached a signed
    authorization for this release.

I understand that a provider's separately-kept psychotherapy (process) notes are
treated differently under HIPAA and may not be released on the same basis as the
rest of my record. Please let me know if any requested item falls into that
category.

Please confirm your expected timeline and any reasonable, cost-based copying fee
before processing, so I can authorize it. You can reach me at [phone/email].

Thank you,

_______________________________      Date: __________
[My signature]
[My printed name]
```

## Verification

- [ ] Letter names the HIPAA right of access.
- [ ] Specific records, date range, and format are requested.
- [ ] Delivery destination is specified (self or authorized third party).
- [ ] Psychotherapy-notes difference is explained in plain language.
- [ ] Fee/timeline framed as "ask the provider," not stated as legal fact.
- [ ] No legal advice or outcome prediction; legal disputes routed to attorney/advocate.
- [ ] Signature and follow-up contact included.
- [ ] Tone is procedural, not adversarial.
