# Complaint to a State Licensing Board — Draft

## Objective

Produce a clear, factual, chronological complaint you can submit to the licensing board that regulates a mental health provider — stating who the provider is, what happened, when, and which standard of conduct concerns you. The draft sticks to verifiable facts and your own observations, not argument or diagnosis of the provider's motives.

## When to Use

- You believe a provider behaved unethically or unprofessionally (boundary violations, breach of confidentiality, practicing outside scope, fee or billing concerns, dual relationships).
- You want the regulatory body — not a lawsuit — to review the conduct.
- You're keeping a record of a concern even if you're unsure whether to file yet.

If the conduct involved a crime, threats, or you feel unsafe now, that is not a complaint to draft first: contact **911** for immediate danger, and consider law enforcement and an attorney. If you are in emotional crisis, call or text **988**.

## Inputs / Context

- The provider's full name and license type (LCSW, LPC, LMFT, psychologist, psychiatrist), if known.
- The state where they're licensed (this determines which board).
- What happened, in dated order, as facts you witnessed or experienced.
- Any records, messages, receipts, or names of witnesses that support the facts.
- Which standard concerns you, described in plain terms (e.g., "shared what I told her with my employer without my permission").

## Constraints

### Must

- Output two parts: (1) **How to find the right board**, and (2) a **complaint letter draft**.
- For finding the board: instruct the client to search "[state] + [profession] licensing board" or use the relevant national association's "verify a license / file a complaint" page; note that the board is specific to the provider's profession and state.
- The complaint draft must be **chronological and factual**: dates, what was said or done, who was present, what records exist.
- Describe the concern in the client's own observed terms and name the general category (confidentiality, boundaries, scope, billing) without quoting statutes or rule numbers.
- Keep the client's account to what they experienced and can support.

### Must Not

- Don't give legal advice, cite specific statutes or board rules as binding, or predict the outcome of the complaint.
- Don't characterize the provider's intent or diagnose them ("she's a narcissist"); stick to observable conduct.
- Don't draft defamatory or speculative claims; only facts the client can stand behind.
- Don't discourage filing or talk the client into a specific decision — that's theirs.
- For anything that may be a legal harm (malpractice, injury, financial loss), route to an attorney; the board complaint and a legal claim are different processes.

## Instructions

1. Help the client identify the provider's profession and licensing state, then the correct board.
2. Gather the facts in dated order; separate what was observed from what was inferred.
3. Identify the plain-language category of concern.
4. Draft the complaint chronologically, attaching a list of supporting materials.
5. Add a closing that states what the client is asking the board to do (review the conduct) without predicting a result.
6. Flag that legal-harm questions go to an attorney, not the board complaint.

## Output Format

```
=== FINDING THE RIGHT BOARD ===
- Provider's profession: [LCSW / LPC / LMFT / Psychologist / Psychiatrist / other]
- Licensing state: [State]
- Search: "[state] [profession] licensing board file a complaint"
  (Boards are organized by profession AND state. Psychiatrists are usually
  regulated by the state medical board; therapists/counselors by a behavioral
  health or counseling board; psychologists by a board of psychology.)
- Most boards have an online complaint form or a downloadable PDF.

=== COMPLAINT LETTER DRAFT ===

[My full name]
[My address, phone, email]

[Date]

[Name of Licensing Board]
[Address or online complaint portal]

Re: Complaint regarding [Provider full name], [license type], License # [if known]

To the Board,

I am filing a complaint about the conduct of the provider named above, who
[saw me as a client / treated me] from [start date] to [end date or "present"].

Below is a factual account in the order events occurred:

1. [Date] — [What happened, factually: what was said or done, who was present.]
2. [Date] — [Next event, factually.]
3. [Date] — [Next event, factually.]

My concern: I believe this conduct raises a question about
[confidentiality / professional boundaries / scope of practice / billing /
dual relationship], because [the specific facts above].

Supporting materials I can provide:
- [e.g., a copy of the message dated ____]
- [e.g., a billing statement dated ____]
- [e.g., name/contact of a witness, with their permission]

I am asking the Board to review this conduct under its standards. I am not
asking the Board to provide me legal advice, and I understand the Board will
make its own determination.

Sincerely,

_______________________________      Date: __________
[My signature]
[My printed name]

Note to self: If I think I was legally harmed (injury, financial loss,
malpractice), I will also consult an attorney — a board complaint and a legal
claim are separate processes.
```

## Verification

- [ ] Guidance to find the correct profession- and state-specific board.
- [ ] Complaint is chronological and fact-based (dates, observable conduct).
- [ ] Concern is named in a plain category without quoting statutes/rules as binding.
- [ ] Supporting materials are listed.
- [ ] No legal advice, statute citation as fact, or outcome prediction.
- [ ] No characterization of provider's intent or armchair diagnosis.
- [ ] Legal-harm questions routed to an attorney.
- [ ] Crisis/safety pathway (911 / 988) noted where relevant.
