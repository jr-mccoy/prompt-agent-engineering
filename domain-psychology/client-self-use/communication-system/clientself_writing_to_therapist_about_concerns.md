# Writing to a Therapist About a Concern — Message Draft

## Objective

Produce a short, clear between-session message to your therapist (portal, email, or text per their policy) that names one concern, says what you actually need, and signals whether it can wait until the next session. The draft should be easy to send and hard to misread.

## When to Use

- Something came up between sessions that you want on record before you meet next.
- You want to flag a reaction to the last session (a technique that didn't sit right, something that stung) without waiting two weeks.
- You need a logistics or care change (frequency, scheduling, a referral question).
- You want to give a heads-up about a topic you want to cover next time.

Do **not** use a between-session message as a crisis line. If you are in danger now — thinking about ending your life, about to harm yourself or someone else, or unsafe — that is not a message to draft. Call or text **988** (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline), go to the nearest **ED**, or call **911**. Your therapist's inbox is not monitored in real time.

## Inputs / Context

- The one concern you want to raise (in your own words).
- What you want to happen as a result (acknowledgement, an answer, a schedule change, a topic flagged for next time).
- How urgent it honestly is: can wait until next session / would like a reply this week / time-sensitive logistics.
- Your therapist's stated policy for between-session contact (if you know it).
- Whether any safety risk is present (if yes, route to the escalation pathway above, not a message).

## Constraints

### Must

- Output **one** sendable message, plus a one-line subject if a portal/email is used.
- Open by naming this is a between-session note and is **not** an emergency.
- State the concern in one or two sentences, then state the specific ask.
- Set an explicit urgency level and an expectation about reply timing ("no rush — happy to discuss next session").
- Keep it boundaried and brief: one concern per message.
- Include a short crisis-pathway line so the channel isn't mistaken for emergency contact.

### Must Not

- Don't draft a message that relies on a quick reply for safety.
- Don't pile multiple unrelated concerns into one message.
- Don't write a venting essay; this is a signal, not a session.
- Don't predict how the therapist will respond or promise a turnaround time on their behalf.

## Instructions

1. Capture the single concern in the client's words.
2. Pin down the specific ask and the honest urgency level.
3. Screen for safety risk; if present, redirect to 988/ED/911 instead of a message.
4. Draft the subject line and message in plain, calm language.
5. Add the not-an-emergency framing and the crisis-pathway line.
6. Offer one shorter variant for a text-length channel.

## Output Format

```
=== BETWEEN-SESSION MESSAGE ===

Subject: Quick note before our next session — not urgent

Message:
"Hi [Therapist name], this is a between-session note — not an emergency.

I wanted to flag something while it's fresh: [the concern, in one or two
sentences — e.g., 'the breathing exercise we tried last week made my anxiety
spike instead of settling, and I've been avoiding it since'].

What I'm hoping for: [the specific ask — e.g., 'I'd like to spend the start of
our next session figuring out whether to adjust it. No reply needed before
then unless you want to.']

Urgency: [Can wait until our next session / I'd appreciate a brief reply this
week / This is a time-sensitive scheduling question].

If something becomes urgent for me before then, I know to call or text 988 or
go to the nearest ER rather than message here.

Thank you,
[My name]"

--- Shorter version (for text-length channels) ---
"Hi [Therapist name] — between-session note, not urgent/not an emergency.
[One-sentence concern.] Can we cover it next session? No reply needed.
(If I'm ever in crisis I'll call/text 988 or go to the ER.) — [My name]"
```

## Verification

- [ ] One concern, stated in the client's own words.
- [ ] A specific ask is named.
- [ ] Urgency level is explicit.
- [ ] "Not an emergency" framing is present up top.
- [ ] Crisis pathway (988 / ED / 911) is included.
- [ ] Message is short and boundaried — no multi-topic dump.
- [ ] A shorter text-length variant is provided.
- [ ] No promise about the therapist's reply timing.
