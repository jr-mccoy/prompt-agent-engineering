# Workplace Accommodations Request for Mental Health — Draft

## Objective

Produce a clear, professional accommodations-request message (to a manager or HR) for a mental health condition, structured in the ADA-style interactive-process pattern: a functional limitation framed *without over-disclosing the diagnosis*, one or more specific accommodations tied to that limitation, and an explicit invitation to discuss alternatives. This is a request structure, not legal advice.

## When to Use

- A mental health condition is affecting work and the person wants reasonable adjustments.
- Wanting to ask before things reach a performance conversation.
- Adjusting an existing arrangement that isn't working.
- Wanting language that focuses on what they *need to do the job*, not on diagnostic detail they'd rather keep private.

## Inputs / Context

- The functional limitation, in plain terms (e.g., "mornings are hard to predict," "open-plan noise makes it hard to concentrate," "I need predictable breaks").
- The specific accommodation(s) being requested (flexible start time, quiet space or noise-canceling headphones, written instructions, a private space for a brief reset, intermittent leave for appointments).
- How much the person wants to disclose (most people can request accommodations by describing the limitation and need, not the diagnosis).
- Who to send it to (manager, HR, or both) and any company process for accommodation requests.
- Whether the person already has documentation from a clinician, or would need to request it.

## Constraints

### Must

- Output a sendable request that follows the structure: **opening + reason for writing**, **functional limitation (no required diagnosis)**, **specific accommodation(s) requested**, **interactive-process invitation**, **documentation offer**, **close**.
- Frame the limitation in terms of job functions and what would help — not clinical detail. Make clear the person can choose how much to disclose.
- Use interactive-process language explicitly ("I'd welcome the chance to discuss what works on your end," "open to alternatives that meet the same need").
- Note plainly that an employer may request documentation that a condition exists and supports the need, and that the level of detail required is usually limited — frame as "they may ask for documentation; here's how to handle it," not as a legal rule.
- Route legal questions (denial, retaliation, whether something is "reasonable" under the law) to an attorney, the EEOC, or a disability-rights organization.

### Must Not

- Don't give legal advice, interpret whether a specific accommodation is legally required, or predict the employer's decision.
- Don't tell the person they must disclose their diagnosis — the default is to disclose the limitation and need, not the label.
- Don't draft a demand or a threat; this is a collaborative request.
- Don't promise that the request will be granted.

## Instructions

1. Capture the functional limitation in job-relevant, non-diagnostic terms.
2. Pin down the specific accommodation(s) and tie each to the limitation.
3. Decide the disclosure level with the person (limitation-and-need is the usual default).
4. Draft the request in the interactive-process structure.
5. Add the documentation note and a close that invites discussion.
6. Add a self-note routing legal questions to the EEOC / a disability-rights org / an attorney.

## Output Format

```
=== ACCOMMODATIONS REQUEST DRAFT ===

To: [Manager / HR contact]
Subject: Request for a workplace accommodation

Hi [Name],

I'm writing to request a reasonable accommodation related to a health condition.
I want to keep doing strong work, and a few adjustments would help me do that
consistently.

The functional limitation (no diagnosis needed):
"[Describe the work-relevant difficulty without the clinical label — e.g.,
'I have a health condition that, at times, makes early-morning focus and
predictability difficult,' or 'a condition that makes a high-noise, open-plan
environment hard to concentrate in.']"

What I'm requesting:
- [Specific accommodation #1 — e.g., "A flexible start time within a 90-minute
  window, with the same total hours."]
- [Specific accommodation #2 — e.g., "Permission to use noise-canceling
  headphones / access to a quiet space when available."]
- [Specific accommodation #3 — e.g., "Written follow-ups after verbal
  instructions," or "intermittent time off for scheduled appointments."]

I'd welcome a conversation:
"I understand this is an interactive process, and I'm open to discussing
alternatives that meet the same need if any of these don't work on your end.
My goal is to find something that works for both of us."

Documentation:
"If you need documentation that supports this request, please let me know what
you need and I'll arrange it with my provider. I'd appreciate keeping the detail
limited to what's necessary to support the accommodation."

Thank you for considering this. I'm happy to set up a time to talk.

Best,
[My name]
[Role / team]

=== NOTE TO SELF (not part of the message) ===
- I can choose how much to disclose; I don't have to share my diagnosis to
  request an accommodation.
- If my request is denied, I'm treated differently afterward, or I have a legal
  question about what's "reasonable," I'll contact the EEOC, a disability-rights
  organization, or an employment attorney — those are legal questions, not ones
  to settle in this message.
```

## Verification

- [ ] Functional limitation is framed in job terms, not diagnosis.
- [ ] Disclosure is the person's choice (label not required).
- [ ] Specific accommodation(s) named and tied to the limitation.
- [ ] Interactive-process language is explicit.
- [ ] Documentation note frames it as "they may ask," with detail kept limited.
- [ ] No legal advice or prediction of the employer's decision.
- [ ] Legal questions routed to EEOC / disability-rights org / attorney.
- [ ] Tone is collaborative; nothing promised.
