# Continuing-Bonds Grief Journaling

## Objective

Generate a personalized set of journaling prompts built on the **continuing-bonds** model of grief: the goal is not to "let go" or "reach acceptance" and be done, but to find an ongoing, livable relationship with the person who died and a place for them in your life now. Output prompts the user can return to over weeks, with a calibration note that grief moves in waves, not stages.

## When to Use

- Someone the user loved has died and they want a way to stay connected without "moving on" from them.
- The user feels pressure (from others or themselves) to "get over it" and wants a different frame.
- Between grief-counseling sessions, or for someone not in counseling who wants a reflective practice.

## Inputs / Context

- Who died and the user's relationship to them.
- How long ago (recent loss and older loss are both valid here).
- A few specific things about the person — phrases they used, things they valued, shared rituals.
- What the user is afraid of (forgetting them, "doing grief wrong," being stuck).
- Whether the user has any current thoughts of not wanting to be here (see escalation).

## Constraints

### Must

- Open by validating the loss and the love behind the grief before giving any prompts (NE-07).
- Build prompts around the continuing-bonds frame: memory, ongoing relationship, integration — not "stages" or "closure."
- State explicitly that grief is non-linear: waves, anniversaries, and ambushes are normal and do not mean regression.
- Personalize at least two prompts using the specific details the user gave (ED-04).
- Output prompts in named categories: **Letters**, **Conversations ("what would they say")**, **Rituals & Memory**, **Carrying Them Forward**.
- Include an escalation note: if grief comes with thoughts of dying, joining them, or not wanting to be here, route to a clinician this week, and to 988 (call/text, US) or local emergency services today if there is any intent or plan.

### Must Not

- Don't impose a five-stages model or any fixed sequence.
- Don't frame the goal as detachment, "letting go," or "closure."
- Don't pathologize tears, talking to the person, or keeping their belongings — these are normal continuing bonds.
- Don't set timelines for when grief "should" ease.

## Instructions

1. Reflect back the relationship and validate the grief in one or two sentences.
2. Explain the continuing-bonds frame plainly (one short paragraph).
3. Generate prompts in the four categories, personalizing at least two with the user's details.
4. Add the non-linearity calibration.
5. Add the escalation note.

## Output Format

```
=== STAYING CONNECTED: A GRIEF JOURNAL ===

First, this: [One-two sentences naming the relationship and validating the grief —
e.g., "You lost your mother, and the size of this grief is the size of the love."]

How this works (continuing bonds):
Grief isn't a staircase you climb and finish. The aim isn't to let go of [name] —
it's to find a way to carry them with you that you can live with. These prompts help
you keep the relationship alive in a new form. Use one when it calls to you; skip any
that don't fit today.

--- Letters ---
- Write [name] a letter about something that happened this week that you wish they knew.
- Tell [name] one thing you never got to say.
- [Personalized: e.g., "Write to [name] about [specific shared thing]."]

--- Conversations ("what would they say") ---
- Ask [name] a question you're stuck on right now. Write what you imagine they'd say.
- [Personalized: e.g., "When you hear [their phrase: '...'], what were they really
  telling you? Write it back to yourself."]

--- Rituals & Memory ---
- Describe one ritual, big or small, that lets you spend time with their memory
  (a candle, a walk, their recipe, a song).
- Write down a memory you're afraid of losing, in as much sensory detail as you can.

--- Carrying Them Forward ---
- Name one value or habit of theirs you want to keep alive through how you live.
- Finish: "A part of [name] that lives on in me is ___."

Calibration — grief comes in waves:
You may feel almost okay one day and flattened the next. Anniversaries, smells, songs,
and ordinary Tuesdays can ambush you. That is grief working, not grief failing.
There is no schedule and no "behind."

If the grief turns toward not wanting to be here:
If you find yourself thinking you'd rather not be here, wanting to join [name], or
making any plan — that's a signal to reach out. Tell your clinician or doctor this
week. If there is any intent or you don't feel safe today, call or text 988 (US Suicide
& Crisis Lifeline) or contact local emergency services now. You shouldn't carry that part alone.
```

## Verification

- [ ] Loss validated before prompts (NE-07).
- [ ] Continuing-bonds frame stated; no five-stages / closure framing.
- [ ] Four prompt categories present.
- [ ] At least two prompts personalized with user's details.
- [ ] Non-linearity (waves) calibration included.
- [ ] Escalation note: clinician this week / 988 / emergency services today.
- [ ] No timelines for when grief "should" ease.
