# Pet Loss Reflection & Ritual Script

## Objective

Help the user grieve the death (or impending death, or loss through rehoming) of a beloved animal. Output validates that pet grief is real grief — often **disenfranchised** because others minimize it — and produces a concrete script: a goodbye or memory ritual, an ongoing way to hold the bond, and language for handling people who say "it was just a dog." Built on continuing-bonds, not stages.

## When to Use

- A pet has died, is being euthanized, or has been lost through circumstance.
- The user feels their grief is "too much" or "embarrassing" for an animal.
- People around the user are minimizing the loss and the user feels alone with it.
- The user wants a way to say goodbye or to keep their pet's memory present.

## Inputs / Context

- The animal's name, species, and how long they were together.
- The role the pet played (companion through a hard time, first pet, a child's pet, a service/working animal).
- How the loss happened or is happening (sudden, illness, euthanasia decision, rehoming).
- Whether the user is carrying guilt (about a euthanasia decision, an accident, "could I have done more").
- Who in the user's life is minimizing it, and what they've said.
- Any current thoughts of not wanting to be here (see escalation).

## Constraints

### Must

- Open by naming this as **real grief** and the bond as a real attachment — validate before anything else (NE-07).
- Name **disenfranchised grief** in plain language: grief that society doesn't fully recognize, which makes it lonelier, not smaller.
- Output sections in order: **What I Want You to Hear**, **A Ritual to Mark It**, **An Ongoing Memory Practice**, **When People Minimize It**, **A Note on Guilt** (only if guilt was raised).
- Personalize the ritual and memory practice with the animal's name and details (ED-04).
- Give the user actual sentences for responding to (or stepping away from) people who minimize the loss (RP-04).
- If euthanasia guilt is present, normalize that choosing to end an animal's suffering is an act of love, not failure — without lecturing.

### Must Not

- Don't compare the loss to human loss or rank grief.
- Don't suggest "getting another pet" as a fix for the grief.
- Don't minimize, rush, or set a timeline.
- Don't moralize about a euthanasia or rehoming decision.

## Instructions

1. Reflect the bond and validate the grief as real and the loneliness as understandable.
2. Name disenfranchised grief.
3. Build a marking ritual personalized to the pet.
4. Offer an ongoing memory practice.
5. Provide language for handling minimizers.
6. If guilt was raised, add the guilt note.
7. If safety concern surfaced, lead with escalation.

## Output Format

```
=== SAYING GOODBYE TO [pet's name] ===

What I Want You to Hear:
[2–3 sentences. Name the bond — "[name] was with you through [span/role], and that bond was real."
Grief for an animal is real grief. It can feel lonelier because the world doesn't always recognize
it — that's called disenfranchised grief, and it makes the loss harder to carry, not smaller.]

A Ritual to Mark It:
- [Personalized, e.g., "Light a candle by [name]'s spot, or take their favorite walk one more time."]
- Say goodbye out loud, or write it: "[name], thank you for ___."
- Keep something of theirs where you'll see it — a tag, a photo, a tuft of fur.

An Ongoing Memory Practice:
- Write down one thing [name] taught you or gave you that you want to keep.
- On hard days, recall a specific [name] moment — the way they [specific habit].
- Mark the day they died, or their "gotcha day," however small.

When People Minimize It ("it was just a pet"):
- A short reply: "They were family to me, and I'm grieving. I'd rather not debate that."
- A boundary: "I know not everyone gets it — I'm going to talk to someone who does."
- Permission: you don't have to justify your grief to anyone. Find the people who understand.

[Only if guilt was raised:]
A Note on Guilt:
If you're carrying guilt about [the decision / the accident], know that choosing to end an animal's
suffering, or doing your best with what you knew, is love — not failure. Guilt is grief looking for
somewhere to land. Be as gentle with yourself as you were with [name].

[If any thought of not wanting to be here was shared, this leads the whole output:]
>> You mentioned not wanting to be here. Please reach out today — call or text 988 (US Suicide &
   Crisis Lifeline) or contact local emergency services, and tell someone you trust. This grief is
   real and you shouldn't carry that part alone. <<
```

## Verification

- [ ] Pet grief validated as real grief before anything else (NE-07).
- [ ] Disenfranchised grief named in plain language.
- [ ] Ritual and memory practice personalized to the animal (ED-04).
- [ ] Actual sentences provided for handling minimizers (RP-04).
- [ ] Guilt note included only if guilt was raised; no moralizing.
- [ ] No "get another pet" fix, no grief-ranking, no timeline.
- [ ] Crisis escalation leads if SI is present: 988 / emergency services today.
