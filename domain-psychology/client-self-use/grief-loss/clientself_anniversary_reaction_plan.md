# Anniversary Reaction Plan

## Objective

Build a personal plan for an upcoming grief anniversary — the death-day, the person's birthday, a wedding anniversary, a holiday, or a season that carries the loss. Output anticipates the **anniversary reaction** (the predictable resurgence of grief around these dates), shapes the day deliberately, gives permission for what the user wants to feel and do, and includes a check for when grief is tipping into crisis.

## When to Use

- A date connected to the loss is coming up and the user already feels the dread building.
- The user got blindsided by a previous anniversary and wants to prepare this time.
- A first "year of firsts" (first birthday, first holiday season, first death-day) is approaching.

## Inputs / Context

- Who died and the date/occasion that's coming.
- What past anniversaries or hard dates have been like for the user.
- Who, if anyone, the user wants around — and who they want to avoid.
- Practical constraints (work, kids, travel, other people's expectations).
- What has helped before, even a little.
- Any current thoughts of not wanting to be here (see escalation).

## Constraints

### Must

- Validate the anticipatory dread first — anniversary reactions are normal and expected (NE-07).
- Output sections: **The Wave I'm Expecting**, **Shape of the Day**, **With Others / Alone**, **What I'm Allowing Myself**, **Anchors If It Gets Heavy**, **Crisis Check & Who I'll Call**.
- "Shape of the Day" gives a loose plan with room to change — not a rigid schedule the user must perform.
- "What I'm Allowing Myself" explicitly permits both grieving openly and having an ordinary or even good day — neither is a betrayal.
- "Crisis Check" names the difference between a hard grief day and a safety concern, and gives an escalation path: clinician this week if dread is unmanageable; 988 (call/text, US) or emergency services today if there are thoughts of self-harm, intent, or a plan.

### Must Not

- Don't prescribe how the user "should" spend the day or how they "should" feel.
- Don't frame avoiding the date or marking the date as the wrong choice — either can be valid.
- Don't promise the day will be easier than they fear.
- Don't treat a normal surge of grief as a crisis, or a real safety concern as "just grief."

## Instructions

1. Reflect back the occasion and validate the build-up.
2. Help the user name the wave they expect (what it tends to feel and look like).
3. Sketch a flexible shape for the day.
4. Decide on company vs. solitude, and how to handle others' expectations.
5. Grant explicit permission for the range of feelings.
6. List anchors for the heavy moments.
7. Add the crisis check and contacts.

## Output Format

```
=== MY ANNIVERSARY PLAN: [Occasion / Date] ===

The Wave I'm Expecting:
- It usually shows up as: [tears / numbness / irritability / exhaustion / dread the
  week before]
- It tends to peak: [the morning of / the night before / a specific moment]
- Knowing this, I won't be surprised by: [...]

Shape of the Day (loose — I can change any of it):
- Morning: [...]
- Middle: [...]
- Evening: [...]
- One thing I'll do to mark it (or not): [a visit / a meal / their song / nothing
  planned — and that's allowed]

With Others / Alone:
- I want around me: [name(s)] — because [...]
- I want to skip: [event / person] — and my line is: "[Short thing I'll say to bow out]"
- If someone pushes: I'll remember I don't owe anyone a performance of grief.

What I'm Allowing Myself:
- [ ] To cry / fall apart for a while
- [ ] To NOT cry, and to have an ordinary day — this is not forgetting them
- [ ] To laugh or enjoy something — this is not a betrayal
- [ ] To change my mind about the whole plan

Anchors If It Gets Heavy:
- A person I can text: [name]
- A small action that grounds me: [walk / cold water / a specific photo or letter]
- Words for myself: "[Their loss and my love are both real. I can feel this and be okay.]"

Crisis Check & Who I'll Call:
A hard grief day looks like deep sadness, longing, tears, and tiredness that eases
when supported. A safety concern looks like thoughts of not wanting to be here,
wanting to join them, or any plan to hurt myself.
- If the dread feels unmanageable or won't lift: tell my clinician/doctor this week.
- If I have thoughts of self-harm, intent, or a plan: call or text 988 (US Suicide &
  Crisis Lifeline) or contact local emergency services TODAY.
- Person I'll reach first: [name + number]
```

## Verification

- [ ] Anticipatory dread validated first (NE-07).
- [ ] All six sections present.
- [ ] Day plan is flexible, not a rigid performance.
- [ ] Permission given for both grieving openly and an ordinary/good day.
- [ ] Crisis check distinguishes hard grief from safety concern.
- [ ] Escalation path: clinician this week / 988 / emergency services today.
- [ ] No prescription of "right" way to spend the day.
