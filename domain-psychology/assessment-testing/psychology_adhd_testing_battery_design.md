# ADHD Testing Battery Design (Adult / Child, Multi-Source)

## Objective

Design an evidence-aligned ADHD assessment battery for an adult or child referral that:

1. Anchors the assessment in a **developmental history** establishing childhood onset and cross-setting impairment, not just current symptoms.
2. Gathers **multi-source, multi-informant** data (self-report plus at least one observer; for children, parent and teacher) using named rating scales — without resting the conclusion on any single instrument.
3. Frames the role and **interpretive caveats of continuous performance tests (CPTs)** so they support rather than overdetermine the picture.
4. Builds a structured **differential** (anxiety, mood, sleep, learning disorder, trauma, substance use, medical) so that ADHD-mimicking conditions are actively considered.
5. Specifies what the battery can and cannot establish, and the **referral thresholds** that route the case elsewhere (learning disorder evaluation, neuropsychology, neurology, medical/sleep workup).

This is a battery-design scaffold. It does not interpret scores or diagnose; it assembles the data sources and the differential frame. All selections require clinician confirmation.

## When to Use

- An ADHD referral arrives for an adult or child and the clinician must assemble the data sources before testing.
- A request for "an ADHD test" needs reframing toward multi-method assessment (DSM-5-TR ADHD has no single confirmatory test).
- Differential-heavy presentations ("is this ADHD, anxiety, a learning disorder, or sleep?") requiring a structured rule-in/rule-out plan.
- Re-evaluation where prior assessment rested on a single rating scale or a single CPT and needs broadening.

Not appropriate as a stand-alone diagnostic instrument or as a substitute for clinical interview and developmental history.

## Inputs / Context Required

- **Age band and developmental stage:** adult vs. child/adolescent (drives informant set and norms) `[clinician input required]`
- **Referral question and source** (self, parent, school, treating clinician, accommodations/disability) `[clinician input required]`
- **Presenting concerns** and the setting(s) where impairment shows (home, school, work, relationships)
- **Developmental history availability:** report cards, prior evaluations, parent/caregiver recall of childhood symptoms before age 12 `[clinician input required]`
- **Available informants:** self, partner/parent, teacher(s), supervisor; for children, at least two settings `[clinician input required]`
- **Differential history:** mood, anxiety, trauma/ACEs, sleep, substance use, learning history, medical/thyroid/vision-hearing, head injury `[clinician input required]`
- **Constraints:** time, authorization, language, prior testing and dates
- **High-stakes flag:** accommodations, disability, or external-incentive context (raises validity considerations) `[clinician input required]`

## Constraints

### Must

- Require a **developmental history** component establishing (per DSM-5-TR) several symptoms present before age 12 and impairment in two or more settings; flag when childhood-onset evidence is unavailable and how that limits conclusions.
- Build a **multi-informant** design: self-report plus at least one observer for adults; parent and teacher (or two settings) for children. Name candidate scales (e.g., Conners family, CAARS, ASRS, Vanderbilt, BRIEF-2) by name only.
- Treat **CPT/performance measures (e.g., TOVA/CPT family)** as supplementary: state that a normal CPT does not rule out ADHD and an abnormal CPT is not specific to ADHD; they index attention/inhibition performance, not the disorder.
- Include a structured **differential** that actively tests anxiety, mood, sleep, learning disorder, trauma, and substance/medical contributors as alternative or co-occurring explanations.
- Address **rating-scale validity/response style** (over- or under-reporting), especially in accommodation/disability/external-incentive contexts, and recommend symptom-validity consideration where appropriate.
- Note **norm, age, language, and cultural fit** for each scale; flag where norms may not apply.
- State **what the battery can and cannot establish** and the **referral thresholds** (e.g., suspected learning disorder → psychoeducational/achievement testing; possible neurological cause → neurology; suspected sleep disorder → sleep medicine).

### Must Not

- Do not reproduce copyrighted item content, verbatim scale questions, proprietary scoring keys, or copyrighted normative tables. Reference instruments by name, subscale/index structure, and score-range bands only.
- Do not base an ADHD conclusion on a single instrument, a single informant, or a single CPT.
- Do not present a CPT as a confirmatory or rule-out test for ADHD.
- Do not skip the developmental-history requirement or the differential.
- Do not interpret scores or assign a diagnosis — the output stops at battery design.
- Do not fabricate informant data, developmental history, or scores; use `[clinician input required: ___]`.

## Instructions

1. **Set the frame by age band.** Determine adult vs. child/adolescent; this fixes the informant set, the norm-referenced scales, and which settings must be sampled.

2. **Plan the developmental-history component.** Specify how childhood-onset (before age 12) and cross-setting impairment will be established: collateral records (report cards, prior evals), parent/caregiver recall, retrospective self-report. Flag if unavailable and state the limitation.

3. **Design the multi-informant rating-scale layer.** Select named scales for each informant:
   - Adult: self-report (e.g., CAARS, ASRS) + observer (e.g., CAARS observer form); executive function (e.g., BRIEF-2 adult).
   - Child/adolescent: parent + teacher (e.g., Conners, Vanderbilt) + executive function (e.g., BRIEF-2).
   Mark cross-informant agreement as a key interpretive signal.

4. **Position the performance-test layer with caveats.** If a CPT (e.g., TOVA/CPT family) is included, state its supplementary role and the rule-in/rule-out caveats explicitly.

5. **Construct the differential plan.** For each alternative/co-occurring condition, specify how it will be screened or assessed:

   | Differential | Why it mimics/co-occurs with ADHD | How this battery addresses it |
   |--------------|-----------------------------------|-------------------------------|
   | Anxiety | Restlessness, concentration difficulty | [screener/interview] |
   | Mood (depression/bipolar) | Inattention, energy/motivation change | [screener/interview] |
   | Sleep disorder | Daytime inattention, irritability | [screen + referral] |
   | Learning disorder | Task avoidance, "inattention" in academics | [achievement/psychoed testing or referral] |
   | Trauma / ACEs | Hypervigilance, concentration, dysregulation | [trauma screen] |
   | Substance use | Attention/executive effects | [substance screen] |
   | Medical (thyroid, vision/hearing, TBI) | Cognitive/attention change | [history + medical referral] |

6. **Add validity/response-style layer.** Specify how over/under-reporting will be evaluated; escalate in accommodation/disability/external-incentive contexts.

7. **State scope and referral thresholds.** Write what the battery can/cannot establish and the conditions that route to learning-disorder evaluation, neuropsychology, neurology, or sleep/medical workup.

8. **Run verification.**

## Verification

- [ ] A developmental-history component establishing onset before age 12 and cross-setting impairment is required (and limitations flagged if unavailable).
- [ ] The design is multi-informant: self + observer for adults, parent + teacher (or two settings) for children.
- [ ] At least two named rating scales from different sources are included; cross-informant agreement is identified as an interpretive signal.
- [ ] CPT/performance measures, if used, carry explicit rule-in/rule-out caveats and a supplementary role.
- [ ] A structured differential (anxiety, mood, sleep, learning disorder, trauma, substance, medical) is included with a resolution plan for each.
- [ ] A validity/response-style layer is present and escalated for accommodation/disability/external-incentive contexts.
- [ ] Norm, age, language, and cultural fit are flagged per scale.
- [ ] Scope and referral thresholds (psychoeducational, neuropsychology, neurology, sleep/medical) are stated.
- [ ] No copyrighted item content, verbatim questions, scoring keys, or norm tables were reproduced — instruments referenced by name and structure only.
- [ ] No scores interpreted and no diagnosis assigned; output stops at battery design.
- [ ] No informant data, developmental history, or scores fabricated; gaps carry `[clinician input required]`.
