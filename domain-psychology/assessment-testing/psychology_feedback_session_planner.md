# Therapeutic Assessment Feedback-Session Planner

## Objective

Plan a collaborative, client-centered assessment feedback session in the tradition of Finn's **Therapeutic Assessment (TA)** model. The planner organizes assessment findings into **Level 1, Level 2, and Level 3 information** — findings that **confirm** the client's existing self-view (Level 1), findings that **extend or modify** it (Level 2), and findings that **challenge or contradict** it (Level 3) — then sequences delivery so the session moves from the most assimilable material to the most discrepant. It ties each finding back to the **client's original assessment questions**, anticipates likely emotional reactions and how to hold them, and produces **usable, collaboratively-framed next steps**. The output is a session plan and script scaffold, not the assessment findings themselves, which the clinician supplies.

## When to Use

- Preparing to deliver assessment results to a client in a collaborative/therapeutic manner (vs. a purely informational results readout).
- After a personality, psychoeducational, neuropsychological, or integrated assessment, when the feedback session is itself intended to be therapeutic.
- When the client posed their own assessment questions at intake and the feedback should answer them directly.
- When findings include discrepant or potentially destabilizing material that needs careful sequencing and containment.
- Supervision/training on therapeutic feedback delivery.

This planner structures the conversation; it does not generate or interpret the test data, assign diagnoses, or substitute for the integrated report.

## Inputs / Context Required

Provide what is available; flag the rest. Do not invent findings or client statements.

- **The client's original assessment questions** (their words, from intake/contracting) `[clinician input required]`.
- **Key assessment findings to convey**, supplied by the clinician (already interpreted) `[clinician input required]`.
- **The client's stated self-view** on relevant dimensions — how they currently understand themselves on the issues assessed `[clinician input required]`.
- **Client factors:** readiness, fragility/defensiveness, cognitive level, language, support system, prior reactions to feedback `[clinician input required]`.
- **Relationship context:** is the assessor also the treating clinician, a referral source's evaluator, or a one-time consultant (affects framing and follow-through).
- **Constraints on the session:** time available, whether a support person is present, whether a written report will follow, and any results that carry safety implications.
- **Setting / referral purpose:** clinical, educational, vocational, or self-referred — shapes what "usable next steps" means.

## Constraints

### Must

- Classify each finding to be delivered as **Level 1 (confirms self-view)**, **Level 2 (extends/modifies self-view)**, or **Level 3 (challenges/contradicts self-view)**, with the rationale for the classification tied to the client's stated self-view.
- **Sequence** delivery from Level 1 → Level 2 → Level 3, so the client first hears confirming material (builds alliance/credibility) before assimilable extensions, and discrepant material last with the most support.
- **Anchor every finding to a client assessment question** wherever possible; feedback exists to answer the client's questions, not to recite the data.
- **Anticipate client reactions** for Level 2 and especially Level 3 findings (e.g., relief, surprise, shame, defensiveness, grief) and specify how the clinician will hold/contain each and check the client's experience in the room.
- Use **collaborative, non-pathologizing language** and check understanding throughout (e.g., "does that fit your experience?"), inviting the client to co-author meaning.
- Produce **usable next steps** that connect findings to action (treatment, accommodations, self-management, referrals) and are framed collaboratively, not prescriptively.
- State **referral/limits**: what the feedback session can and cannot do, when findings warrant a separate risk or disclosure pathway, and when to refer (e.g., destabilizing reactions, safety concerns, findings outside the assessor's scope).

### Must Not

- Do not reproduce copyrighted instrument item content, exact item wording, scoring keys, or normative tables. Reference instruments **by name and structure only**; convey findings as interpreted statements, not raw scale outputs.
- Do not lead with Level 3 (most discrepant) findings or dump all findings at once without sequencing or in-room checking.
- Do not deliver findings as fixed labels ("you are a narcissist"); use functional, collaborative framing the client can engage with.
- Do not fabricate findings, scores, or client questions/statements; gaps carry `[clinician input required]`.
- Do not ignore safety-relevant findings — if a finding implicates risk, route it to the appropriate risk-assessment and response pathway rather than treating it as ordinary feedback.

## Instructions

1. **Restate the client's assessment questions.** List them verbatim; these organize the session. If none were captured, flag and reconstruct collaboratively with the clinician.

2. **Intake the findings.** Take the clinician's interpreted findings as input. Do not re-interpret data; work from the supplied conclusions.

3. **Classify each finding by level.** For each finding, judge against the client's stated self-view: Level 1 (confirms), Level 2 (extends/modifies), or Level 3 (challenges/contradicts). Record the rationale.

4. **Map findings to questions.** Tie each finding to the assessment question(s) it helps answer. Flag findings that answer no client question and decide whether/how to include them.

5. **Sequence the session.** Order delivery Level 1 → Level 2 → Level 3. Within Level 3, prioritize the most clinically important discrepant finding and plan the most support around it.

6. **Plan reactions and containment.** For each Level 2/3 finding, anticipate likely emotional reactions and specify the clinician's holding response and an in-room check of the client's experience.

7. **Draft collaborative framing.** For each finding, write a brief, non-pathologizing way to present it and an invitation for the client to react and co-author meaning.

8. **Build usable next steps.** Translate findings into collaborative next steps tied to the client's questions and goals; identify any referral or safety pathway.

9. **State limits and run verification.**

## Output Format

### Session Frame

```
CLIENT ASSESSMENT QUESTIONS (verbatim):
  Q1: [ ... ]   Q2: [ ... ]   Q3: [ ... ]
RELATIONSHIP / SETTING: [assessor-as-therapist / evaluator / consultant; clinical/educational/vocational]
CLIENT FACTORS: [readiness / fragility / support / prior reactions]
SESSION CONSTRAINTS: [time / support person / report to follow / safety-relevant findings?]
OUTPUT STATUS: Feedback-session plan — findings supplied by clinician; not re-interpreted here
```

### Findings Organized by Level

| Finding (interpreted, by-name instruments only) | Level (1/2/3) | Rationale vs. Self-View | Answers Which Question | Anticipated Reaction | Containment / In-Room Check |
|-------------------------------------------------|---------------|-------------------------|------------------------|----------------------|-----------------------------|
| [finding] | 1 — confirms | [fits client's view of self] | [Q#] | [relief / validation] | [ "does this match how you see it?" ] |
| [finding] | 2 — extends | [adds nuance to self-view] | [Q#] | [curiosity / mild surprise] | [check fit, invite examples] |
| [finding] | 3 — challenges | [contradicts self-view] | [Q#] | [shame / defensiveness / grief] | [normalize, slow down, hold, check experience] |

### Delivery Sequence (script scaffold)

```
1. OPEN — reconnect to the client's questions: "You came in wanting to understand [Q]. Here's what we found."
2. LEVEL 1 (confirming) — deliver first to build credibility and alliance.
   Framing: [ ... ]   In-room check: [ ... ]
3. LEVEL 2 (extending) — present as additions/refinements.
   Framing: [ ... ]   Invite co-authoring: [ ... ]
4. LEVEL 3 (challenging) — deliver last, most support; pace to the client's reactions.
   Framing: [ ... ]   Containment plan: [ ... ]   Safety route if applicable: [ ... ]
5. INTEGRATE — return to the client's questions and summarize collaboratively.
```

### Usable Next Steps & Limits

```
NEXT STEPS (collaborative, tied to questions/goals):
  - [step → which finding/question it serves]
WHAT THIS SESSION CANNOT DO: [resolve everything; substitute for ongoing treatment; render legal/forensic opinion]
SAFETY / DISCLOSURE ROUTE: [if any finding implicates risk → risk-assessment + response pathway]
REFERRAL TRIGGERS: [destabilizing reaction / findings outside scope / specialist follow-up]
```

## Verification

- [ ] The client's original assessment questions are restated and organize the session.
- [ ] Every finding is classified Level 1 / 2 / 3 against the client's stated self-view, with rationale.
- [ ] Delivery is sequenced Level 1 → Level 2 → Level 3, not dumped or led by discrepant material.
- [ ] Each finding is tied to the client question(s) it answers (or flagged if it answers none).
- [ ] Likely reactions and in-room containment/checks are planned for Level 2 and Level 3 findings.
- [ ] Findings are framed collaboratively and non-pathologizingly, with invitations to co-author meaning.
- [ ] Usable, collaboratively-framed next steps are produced and tied to the client's questions/goals.
- [ ] Limits are stated and safety-relevant findings are routed to a risk-assessment/response pathway; referral triggers are named.
- [ ] No copyrighted item content, exact item wording, scoring keys, or normative tables are reproduced — instruments referenced by name only and findings conveyed as interpreted statements.
