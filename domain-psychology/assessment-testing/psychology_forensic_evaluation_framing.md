# Forensic Evaluation Framing (Referral Question, Role, Limits)

## Objective

Frame a forensic psychological evaluation **before it begins** so the evaluator's role, scope, data requirements, and the boundaries of opinion are explicit from the outset. The scaffold operationalizes the foundational forensic-assessment principles (in the tradition of Heilbrun, Grisso, and the AP-LS / specialty guidelines): translate a referral into a **specific psycholegal question**, identify the **retaining party** and legal context, hold the **forensic-vs-therapeutic role boundary** (no dual role), conduct **informed-notification** (not therapeutic consent) with explicit limits of confidentiality, plan **third-party/collateral data** collection, build in **response-style/validity** assessment, and define where the evaluator's opinion stops — including whether the **ultimate legal issue** should be addressed. This is a framing/planning tool, not a determination of the legal question itself.

## When to Use

- A referral for a forensic evaluation arrives (competency to stand trial, criminal responsibility, fitness, risk, custody, fitness-for-duty, disability, civil tort/psychological injury, capacity in a legal proceeding) and the evaluation must be structured before contact with the examinee.
- A clinician is asked to "evaluate" someone for a legal purpose and needs to determine whether the role is forensic vs. therapeutic and whether they can accept it.
- An evaluator needs to draft the notification, data-collection plan, and scope-of-opinion before the first interview.
- Consultation/supervision on forensic role boundaries, retaining-party clarity, or ultimate-issue questions.

Not for delivering a forensic opinion, scoring forensic instruments, or providing therapy to a forensic examinee. Framing precedes — and is kept separate from — both.

## Inputs / Context Required

Provide what is known; flag the rest. Do not infer the legal standard.

- **Referral source and retaining party:** who is requesting the evaluation (court order, retaining attorney — which side, agency, employer) and the legal matter type `[clinician input required]`.
- **The legal/psycholegal question as posed**, in the referrer's words `[clinician input required]`.
- **Jurisdiction and applicable legal standard** for the question (the standard is load-bearing and jurisdiction-specific) `[clinician input required]`.
- **Examinee context:** identifying information, the proceeding's stage, deadlines, and any prior evaluations.
- **Existing relationship:** whether the evaluator has any prior or current therapeutic relationship with the examinee (a dual-role red flag).
- **Available third-party information:** records, collateral interviews, prior testing, charging documents, depositions — and what is still needed.
- **Notification requirements:** who must be informed of purpose, non-confidentiality, and use of findings, per jurisdiction and referral type.
- **Scope of requested opinion:** is the ultimate legal issue being asked, and is addressing it permitted/appropriate in this jurisdiction and context?

## Constraints

### Must

- Translate the referral into a **specific, answerable psycholegal question** tied to the named legal standard; flag vague referrals ("just evaluate him") for clarification before proceeding.
- Identify the **retaining party** and the legal context explicitly, and frame all subsequent role/communication decisions around it.
- Enforce the **forensic-vs-therapeutic role boundary**: a single clinician should not occupy both roles for the same person; flag any dual-role conflict and recommend declining or referring.
- Specify **informed-notification** content (purpose, who retained the evaluator, who receives the report, the **limits of confidentiality**, and that this is an evaluation, not treatment) — distinguishing it from **therapeutic informed consent**.
- Require a **third-party / collateral information** plan: forensic opinions rest on multiple data sources, not self-report alone; list records and collaterals to obtain.
- Build in **response-style / validity** assessment (consideration of over-reporting, under-reporting, and inconsistent responding via named validity/response-style measures and convergent data) **without reproducing any item content**.
- Define the **boundary of opinion**: state which questions the data can support, and address the **ultimate legal issue** only if asked and appropriate in the jurisdiction; otherwise describe functional capacities and let the trier of fact decide.
- State the evaluator's **role and limits**, when to **decline** (insufficient competence, dual role, inadequate data), and that the report informs but does not replace the legal decision-maker.

### Must Not

- Do not reproduce copyrighted instrument item content — no forensic-instrument items, no response-style/validity-measure items, no verbatim probes, no scoring keys or normative tables. Reference instruments **by name and structure only**.
- Do not adopt a dual forensic/therapeutic role for the same person, nor convert an existing therapy relationship into a forensic evaluation.
- Do not offer therapeutic informed-consent framing (confidentiality protections, treatment alliance) in a forensic context — the examinee must be notified of non-confidentiality and the evaluation purpose.
- Do not state the legal standard, outcome, or ultimate-issue opinion as fact; the standard is jurisdiction-specific `[clinician input required]` and the determination belongs to the court/trier of fact.
- Do not base a forensic opinion on examinee self-report alone, or proceed without a third-party-data plan.
- Do not fabricate records, collateral content, or examinee statements; gaps carry `[clinician input required]`.

## Instructions

1. **Clarify the referral and retaining party.** Record who is requesting the evaluation, on whose behalf, the matter type, and the proceeding's stage and deadline. Flag if the retaining party or purpose is ambiguous.

2. **Translate to a psycholegal question.** Convert the referral into a specific question tied to the named legal standard in the relevant jurisdiction. If the standard is unstated, mark `[clinician input required]` and request it before proceeding.

3. **Screen the role boundary.** Determine whether the evaluator has any therapeutic relationship with the examinee. If so, flag the dual-role conflict and recommend declining/referring. Confirm the role is purely forensic.

4. **Draft the notification.** Specify what the examinee must be told: purpose, retaining party, who receives the report, the limits of confidentiality, and that this is an evaluation and not treatment. Distinguish this notification from therapeutic informed consent.

5. **Plan third-party / collateral data.** List records to obtain (legal, medical, prior evaluations) and collateral interviews to conduct. Note what is missing and how its absence limits the opinion.

6. **Plan response-style / validity assessment.** Identify, by name only, the response-style and validity strategies/measures to be used (over-reporting, under-reporting, inconsistency) and the convergent data that will cross-check self-report. Do not reproduce any items.

7. **Define the scope of opinion.** State which questions the planned data can support. Determine whether the ultimate legal issue is being asked and whether addressing it is appropriate in this jurisdiction; if not, plan to describe functional capacities only.

8. **State role, limits, and decline triggers.** Note the evaluator's role and its limits, conditions under which the referral should be declined, and that the report informs — does not replace — the legal decision-maker.

9. **Run verification.**

## Output Format

### Referral & Role Frame

```
RETAINING PARTY / REFERRAL SOURCE: [court order / attorney (which side) / agency / employer]
MATTER TYPE: [competency / responsibility / risk / custody / fitness-for-duty / civil injury / ...]
JURISDICTION + LEGAL STANDARD: [clinician input required — standard is jurisdiction-specific]
PSYCHOLEGAL QUESTION (specific, answerable): "[ ... ]"
ROLE: Forensic evaluator (NOT treating clinician)
DUAL-ROLE CHECK: [no prior/current therapy relationship CONFIRMED / CONFLICT — decline/refer]
DEADLINE / PROCEEDING STAGE: [ ... ]
OUTPUT STATUS: Pre-evaluation framing scaffold — not an opinion on the legal question
```

### Notification (not therapeutic consent)

```
The examinee will be informed of:
  - Purpose of the evaluation: [ ... ]
  - Who retained the evaluator and who receives the report: [ ... ]
  - LIMITS OF CONFIDENTIALITY: findings are NOT confidential; they will be reported to [recipient].
  - This is an EVALUATION, not treatment; no therapeutic relationship is formed.
  - [Jurisdiction-specific notification requirements — clinician input required]
```

### Data-Collection & Validity Plan

| Data Source | Status | What It Informs | If Missing |
|-------------|--------|-----------------|-----------|
| Examinee interview | [planned] | [self-report, mental status] | n/a |
| Legal records / charging docs / depositions | [obtain] | [context, consistency] | [limits opinion] |
| Medical / psychiatric records | [obtain] | [history, diagnoses] | [limits opinion] |
| Prior evaluations / testing | [obtain] | [baseline, consistency] | [limits opinion] |
| Collateral interviews | [planned] | [corroboration] | [limits opinion] |
| Response-style / validity strategy (by name only) | [planned] | [over/under-reporting, inconsistency] | [opinion qualified] |

### Scope of Opinion & Limits

```
QUESTIONS THE DATA CAN SUPPORT: [functional-capacity findings the plan can address]
ULTIMATE LEGAL ISSUE: [addressed only if asked AND appropriate in jurisdiction — else describe
  functional capacities and defer the determination to the trier of fact]
EVALUATOR ROLE/LIMITS: Forensic opinion informs, does not replace, the legal decision-maker.
DECLINE TRIGGERS: [dual role / outside competence / inadequate data / no legal standard provided]
REFERRAL: [specialist forensic evaluator / different examiner where conflict exists]
```

## Verification

- [ ] The referral is translated into a specific psycholegal question tied to a named, jurisdiction-specific legal standard (or flagged for input).
- [ ] The retaining party and legal context are identified and frame the role/communication plan.
- [ ] The forensic-vs-therapeutic role boundary is enforced; any dual-role conflict is flagged with a decline/refer recommendation.
- [ ] Informed-notification content (purpose, recipients, limits of confidentiality, non-treatment status) is specified and distinguished from therapeutic consent.
- [ ] A third-party / collateral data plan is present; the opinion does not rest on self-report alone.
- [ ] Response-style / validity assessment is planned by name only, with convergent data, and no items reproduced.
- [ ] The scope of opinion is defined, and the ultimate legal issue is addressed only if asked and appropriate — otherwise functional capacities are described.
- [ ] The evaluator's role, limits, and decline/referral triggers are stated.
- [ ] No copyrighted instrument items, probe wording, scoring keys, or normative tables are reproduced — instruments referenced by name/structure only.
- [ ] No legal standard, outcome, records, or examinee statements are fabricated; gaps carry `[clinician input required]`.
