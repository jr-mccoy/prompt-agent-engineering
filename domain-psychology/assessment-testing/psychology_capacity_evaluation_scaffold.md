# Decisional-Capacity Evaluation Scaffold (Medical / Financial)

## Objective

Scaffold a structured, **decision-specific** capacity evaluation for either a medical-treatment-consent decision or a discrete financial decision, organized around Appelbaum and Grisso's four legally relevant abilities — **Understanding**, **Appreciation**, **Reasoning**, and **Expressing a Choice** — operationalized in the tradition of the MacArthur Competence Assessment Tools (MacCAT-T for treatment; MacCAT-CR framework adapted for the decision at hand). The scaffold applies a **sliding scale**: the threshold of demonstrated ability rises with the risk/complexity and irreversibility of the decision. It integrates a brief cognitive screen as context (not as a capacity verdict), keeps the **capacity (clinical, decision-specific) vs. competency (legal/judicial determination)** distinction explicit, and states the evaluator's role and limits. All capacity conclusions require the evaluating clinician's judgment.

## When to Use

- A treating team requests a clinical opinion on whether a patient can give informed consent to (or refuse) a specific treatment.
- A question arises about capacity to make a discrete financial decision (e.g., a specific transaction, designating a power of attorney for a defined act).
- Cognitive impairment, acute illness, psychiatric symptoms, or fluctuating mental status raises doubt about a specific decision.
- A second opinion or structured documentation is needed before proceeding with consent, refusal, or surrogate decision-making.

Not for: global "is this person competent?" determinations (that is a legal, judicial question), and not for decisions other than the specific one evaluated. Capacity does not transfer across decisions of different risk or complexity.

## Inputs / Context Required

Provide what is available; flag the rest. Never infer responses the patient did not give.

- **The specific decision at issue**, stated precisely (the exact treatment/refusal or the exact financial act), and its **risk/benefit/alternatives** profile `[clinician input required]`
- **Decision risk and reversibility:** low-risk/clearly beneficial vs. high-risk/serious/irreversible — this sets the sliding-scale threshold.
- **Referral source and question** (treating physician, surgeon, social work, attorney, family) and the decision deadline.
- **Relevant medical/psychiatric context:** diagnoses, current mental status, delirium screen status, medications affecting cognition, fluctuation pattern.
- **Cognitive screening data** if available (e.g., MoCA, MMSE, or a brief screen) — used as context only `[clinician input required]`
- **Patient's stated understanding** of the decision, in the patient's own words `[clinician input required]`
- **Values/goals context** relevant to appreciation (how the patient relates the decision to their own situation and values).
- **Communication considerations:** language, sensory deficits, education, the need for an interpreter or augmentative communication.
- **Jurisdictional/role context:** is this a clinical capacity consult or part of a legal proceeding? (Determines documentation and who renders the ultimate determination.)

## Constraints

### Must

- Anchor the evaluation to **one specific decision**; state it verbatim. If multiple decisions are in question, scaffold each separately.
- Evaluate all four Appelbaum abilities and document evidence for each: **Understanding** (the disclosed information), **Appreciation** (relevance to one's own situation, including the consequences of choosing or refusing), **Reasoning** (comparing options, manipulating information, drawing a logically consistent conclusion), **Expressing a Choice** (communicating a stable, consistent choice).
- Apply the **sliding scale explicitly**: state that the threshold of required ability rises with the decision's risk and irreversibility, and note which threshold this decision warrants.
- Keep **capacity vs. competency distinct**: capacity is a clinical, decision-specific judgment by a clinician; competency is a legal status determined by a court. State which the evaluator is rendering and which they are not.
- Treat **cognitive screening as context, not verdict**: a low screen score raises concern but does not by itself establish incapacity; a normal score does not by itself establish capacity for a complex decision.
- Account for **reversible/fluctuating contributors** (delirium, intoxication, acute psychiatric state, pain, medication): recommend re-evaluation when capacity may be temporarily impaired rather than declaring stable incapacity.
- State **referral/limits**: when the question exceeds the evaluator's scope (e.g., a legal guardianship determination, a forensic question), or when re-evaluation, specialist input, or judicial referral is indicated.
- Tag every ability rating and the overall opinion as `[clinician judgment required]`.

### Must Not

- Do not reproduce copyrighted instrument item content, exact MacCAT-T/MacCAT-CR probe wording, scoring keys, or normative tables. Reference instruments and the four-ability framework **by name and structure only**.
- Do not render a global competency or guardianship determination — that is a judicial function; the evaluator provides a clinical capacity opinion that may inform the court.
- Do not treat a psychiatric diagnosis, a cognitive-screen score, or a "bad" decision as equivalent to incapacity; capacity turns on the four abilities for the specific decision, not on the content of the choice.
- Do not generalize a capacity finding to other decisions of different risk or complexity.
- Do not fabricate patient responses, cognitive scores, or understanding; missing data carries `[clinician input required]`.

## Instructions

1. **State the decision and its risk profile.** Record the exact decision, the disclosed risks/benefits/alternatives, and classify the decision risk/reversibility. Use this to set the sliding-scale threshold (low-risk/beneficial → lower threshold; high-risk/serious/irreversible → higher threshold).

2. **Establish the evaluation context.** Note referral source/question, mental-status and reversible contributors (delirium, intoxication, acute symptoms, pain, sedating medications), communication needs, and whether the consult is clinical or legal.

3. **Place cognitive screening as context.** If a screen exists, record the band/level only and explicitly mark it as supporting context — not the capacity determination. If no screen exists and cognition is in question, recommend one.

4. **Evaluate the four abilities** for this decision, capturing evidence for each:
   - **Understanding** — Can the patient paraphrase the nature of the condition, the proposed decision, and the disclosed risks/benefits/alternatives in their own words?
   - **Appreciation** — Does the patient acknowledge that the information applies to *them* and grasp the likely consequences of consenting vs. refusing, including for their own situation and values?
   - **Reasoning** — Can the patient compare options, weigh consequences, and reach a conclusion through a logically consistent process (process, not the specific outcome chosen)?
   - **Expressing a Choice** — Can the patient communicate a clear, stable choice that does not vacillate to the point of being uninterpretable?

5. **Apply the sliding scale.** Judge each ability against the threshold set by the decision's risk. A patient may have capacity for a low-risk decision but not for a high-risk, complex one.

6. **Identify reversible factors and re-evaluation triggers.** If impairment appears state-dependent (delirium, intoxication, acute crisis), recommend timing of re-evaluation rather than a stable incapacity opinion.

7. **Form the capacity opinion and state limits.** Render a decision-specific capacity opinion (has / lacks / indeterminate-pending-re-eval) framed as a clinical judgment. State what the opinion does not decide (competency, guardianship, other decisions) and when to refer (legal proceeding, specialist, ethics consult, surrogate pathway).

8. **Run verification.**

## Output Format

### Evaluation Context

```
SPECIFIC DECISION EVALUATED: "[verbatim decision]"
DECISION RISK / REVERSIBILITY: [low-beneficial / moderate / high-serious-irreversible]
SLIDING-SCALE THRESHOLD APPLIED: [lower / moderate / higher] — rationale: [ ... ]
REFERRAL SOURCE / QUESTION: [ ... ]    CONTEXT: [clinical capacity consult / legal proceeding]
REVERSIBLE CONTRIBUTORS SCREENED: [delirium / intoxication / acute symptoms / pain / sedation — present?]
COGNITIVE SCREEN (context only): [instrument name — band/level, or none]
COMMUNICATION SUPPORTS: [interpreter / AAC / sensory aids / none]
OUTPUT STATUS: Capacity-evaluation scaffold — all ability ratings and the opinion require clinician judgment
```

### Capacity Opinion and Limits

```
CAPACITY OPINION (this decision, this risk level): [Has capacity / Lacks capacity / Indeterminate — re-evaluate]
REVERSIBLE/STATE FACTORS: [present? recommended re-evaluation timing if so]
CAPACITY vs. COMPETENCY: This is a clinical, decision-specific CAPACITY opinion. It does NOT
  constitute a legal competency or guardianship determination, which is a judicial function.
SCOPE — THIS OPINION DOES NOT EXTEND TO: [other decisions of different risk/complexity]
REFERRAL / NEXT STEPS: [ethics consult / surrogate or POA pathway / legal referral / specialist / re-eval]
```
