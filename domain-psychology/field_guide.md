# Psychology & Behavioral Science Field Guide

**Purpose:** Prompt engineering techniques and ideas specifically curated for psychologists, therapists, counselors, behavioral researchers, organizational psychologists, UX researchers, and behavioral science practitioners.

**Important Disclaimer:** AI cannot provide therapy, diagnosis, or clinical treatment. These prompts are designed for professional assistance, research support, education, and self-reflection tools—not clinical intervention. Mental health crises require human professionals.

---

## Top 10 Prompt Ideas for Psychology & Behavioral Science

### 1. Cognitive Distortion Identifier
Analyze thought patterns to identify common cognitive distortions (catastrophizing, black-and-white thinking, mind reading, etc.). Provide psychoeducation about the distortion and gentle reframing suggestions without providing therapy.

### 2. Research Interview Protocol Designer
Design qualitative research interview protocols with appropriate question sequencing, probing strategies, and rapport-building elements. Include pilot testing suggestions and common pitfall warnings.

### 3. Behavioral Observation Framework
Create structured observation protocols for behavioral assessment, including operationalized behavior definitions, recording methods, inter-rater reliability considerations, and context documentation.

### 4. Psychological Assessment Report Structurer
Help structure psychological assessment reports with appropriate sections, professional language, and interpretation guidelines. Ensure proper caveats, limitations, and recommendations format.

### 5. Therapeutic Technique Explainer
Explain evidence-based therapeutic techniques (CBT interventions, motivational interviewing, DBT skills, etc.) for training and educational purposes, including theoretical rationale, implementation steps, and common adaptations.

### 6. Psychometric Instrument Evaluator
Analyze psychological measures for reliability, validity, normative data appropriateness, cultural considerations, and practical utility. Compare alternative instruments for specific assessment needs.

### 7. Behavior Change Plan Designer
Apply behavioral science frameworks (COM-B, Transtheoretical Model, Social Cognitive Theory) to design behavior change interventions. Include barrier analysis, technique selection, and measurement strategies.

### 8. Qualitative Data Theme Analyzer
Support thematic analysis of qualitative data (interviews, focus groups, open-ended responses) with systematic coding suggestions, theme development, and reflexivity prompts.

### 9. Case Conceptualization Framework
Guide clinical case conceptualization using specific theoretical frameworks (CBT, psychodynamic, systemic, etc.). Structure presenting problems, predisposing/precipitating/perpetuating factors, and treatment implications.

### 10. Organizational Culture Diagnostic
Assess organizational culture and climate using established frameworks. Identify patterns, tensions, and intervention points for organizational development initiatives.

## Relevant Prompt Engineering Techniques

### Tier 1: Essential Techniques for Psychology

#### NE-07: Emotional Validation First
**Relevance:** Core to psychological practice
**Pattern:** Acknowledge and validate emotional impact before proceeding to analysis
**Psychology Application:** Reflective listening, building rapport, discussing sensitive topics
**Why Essential:** Emotional safety enables deeper exploration; validation is therapeutic in itself

#### NE-01: Single-Question Pacing Protocol
**Relevance:** Clinical interviewing and assessment
**Pattern:** Ask one question at a time, wait for response before proceeding
**Psychology Application:** Intake interviews, assessments, therapeutic conversations, research interviews
**Why Essential:** Prevents overwhelm; allows depth; enables adaptive follow-up

#### RT-02: Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework
**Relevance:** Biopsychosocial-cultural assessment
**Pattern:** Analyze from multiple perspectives systematically
**Psychology Application:** Case conceptualization, assessment, holistic understanding of behavior
**Why Essential:** Human behavior is multi-determined; single-dimension analysis is incomplete

#### QA-04: Uncertainty Acknowledgment
**Relevance:** Diagnostic humility and ethical practice
**Pattern:** State confidence levels, acknowledge limitations, suggest verification
**Psychology Application:** Assessment interpretations, treatment recommendations, behavioral predictions
**Why Essential:** Psychological constructs have inherent uncertainty; overconfidence is harmful

#### NE-08: Catchall Context Gathering
**Relevance:** Client-centered intake
**Pattern:** Open-ended collection of unstructured information before systematic questioning
**Psychology Application:** "Tell me what brings you here," initial exploration, phenomenological inquiry
**Why Essential:** Honors client's frame; reveals what they consider important; builds alliance

### Tier 2: Highly Valuable Techniques

#### ED-03: Guided Discovery
**Relevance:** Therapeutic technique (especially CBT)
**Pattern:** Ask guiding questions instead of giving answers
**Psychology Application:** Socratic questioning, insight facilitation, collaborative empiricism
**Why Valuable:** Core to many therapeutic approaches; develops client autonomy

#### RP-04: Socratic Dialogue
**Relevance:** Cognitive restructuring and exploration
**Pattern:** Question-and-answer format for exploring beliefs and assumptions
**Psychology Application:** Examining automatic thoughts, exploring core beliefs, values clarification
**Why Valuable:** Facilitates insight without being directive or lecturing

#### NE-02: Phased Workflow Architecture
**Relevance:** Structured assessment and intervention
**Pattern:** Explicit Phase 1 → Phase 2 → Phase 3 structure with handoff logic
**Psychology Application:** Assessment phases, treatment stages, research protocols
**Why Valuable:** Provides structure while allowing flexibility within phases

#### DT-02: Specific Focus Areas with Examples
**Relevance:** Comprehensive assessment domains
**Pattern:** Detailed enumeration of areas to explore with examples
**Psychology Application:** Mental status exam components, assessment domains, interview topics
**Why Valuable:** Ensures thorough coverage without rigid scripting

#### RT-04: Analogical Reasoning
**Relevance:** Psychoeducation and interpretation
**Pattern:** Explain concepts through analogies from familiar domains
**Psychology Application:** Explaining psychological concepts, metaphors in therapy, normalizing experiences
**Why Valuable:** Makes abstract psychological concepts accessible and memorable

### Tier 3: Valuable Supporting Techniques

#### QA-01: Self-Verification
**Relevance:** Reflexivity in practice and research
**Pattern:** Built-in review asking "What assumptions did I make?"
**Psychology Application:** Countertransference awareness, researcher positionality, bias checking
**Supporting Role:** Essential for ethical practice and research quality

#### CM-01: Explicit Context Framing
**Relevance:** Cultural and situational context
**Pattern:** Provide all relevant background information upfront
**Psychology Application:** Cultural context, life circumstances, systemic factors
**Supporting Role:** Prevents decontextualized interpretation

#### DS-04: Pattern Recognition Requests
**Relevance:** Identifying behavioral patterns
**Pattern:** Identify trends, patterns, and recurring themes
**Psychology Application:** Behavioral patterns, relationship dynamics, cognitive patterns
**Supporting Role:** Helps identify targets for intervention

#### NE-04: Good vs Bad Example Calibration
**Relevance:** Training and supervision
**Pattern:** Show contrasting examples to illustrate quality
**Psychology Application:** Training examples, distinguishing skilled from unskilled responses
**Supporting Role:** Concrete demonstration for learning

#### ST-04: Delimited Sections
**Relevance:** Report and documentation structure
**Pattern:** Clear sections for multi-part responses
**Psychology Application:** Assessment reports, case notes, research summaries
**Supporting Role:** Organizes complex psychological information

### Tier 4: Specialized Applications

#### RP-03: Multi-Persona Debate
**Relevance:** Internal family systems and parts work
**Pattern:** Simulate perspectives with different priorities
**Psychology Application:** Exploring internal conflicts, understanding ambivalence, systemic perspectives
**Specialized Use:** Externalizing internal conflicts for exploration

#### NE-09: Scope Reduction Pressure
**Relevance:** Problem definition and treatment focus
**Pattern:** Challenge and reduce scope to essential elements
**Psychology Application:** Prioritizing treatment targets, defining research questions, focusing assessment
**Specialized Use:** Moving from diffuse concerns to actionable focus

#### RT-03: Tree of Thoughts
**Relevance:** Treatment planning alternatives
**Pattern:** Generate multiple approaches, compare systematically
**Psychology Application:** Treatment options, intervention selection, case formulation alternatives
**Specialized Use:** Considering multiple valid approaches to complex cases

#### NE-06: Self-Audit Requirements
**Relevance:** Quality assurance in assessment
**Pattern:** Verify output meets specific criteria
**Psychology Application:** Report completeness, ethical considerations, cultural responsiveness
**Specialized Use:** Ensuring professional standards are met

#### ED-04: Personalization Hooks
**Relevance:** Client-centered adaptation
**Pattern:** Incorporate individual interests and background
**Psychology Application:** Culturally adapted interventions, values-consistent goal setting
**Specialized Use:** Tailoring approaches to individual clients

### Tier 5: Quality Assurance for Psychological Content

#### QA-01: Chain-of-Verification
**Relevance:** Assessment accuracy
**Pattern:** Self-critique, verify interpretations, revise
**Quality Application:** Checking assessment interpretations against evidence

#### AG-02: Skeptical Default Stance
**Relevance:** Diagnostic caution
**Pattern:** Default to uncertainty, require strong evidence for conclusions
**Quality Application:** Avoiding premature diagnosis or labeling

#### QA-05: Citation Requirements
**Relevance:** Evidence-based practice
**Pattern:** Cite sources for claims and techniques
**Quality Application:** Linking recommendations to evidence base

#### CM-02: Constraint Specification
**Relevance:** Ethical boundaries
**Pattern:** Explicit must/must-not requirements
**Quality Application:** Scope of practice, confidentiality, duty to warn

#### RT-05: Evidence-Based Reasoning
**Relevance:** Treatment selection
**Pattern:** Require evidence for recommendations
**Quality Application:** Ensuring recommendations have empirical support

## Ethical Considerations for Psychology Prompts

When building psychology prompts, ensure:

1. **Clear scope boundaries** - AI is not therapy; distinguish support from treatment
2. **Crisis protocols** - Clear escalation paths for safety concerns
3. **Confidentiality framing** - Appropriate privacy expectations set
4. **Cultural humility** - Avoid universal assumptions; acknowledge cultural context
5. **Diagnostic caution** - Labels have consequences; uncertainty appropriate
6. **Power awareness** - Recognize inherent power dynamics in helping relationships
7. **Evidence linkage** - Connect techniques to empirical support
8. **Reflexivity prompts** - Build in awareness of biases and assumptions

### What AI Can and Cannot Do in Psychology

**Appropriate Uses:**
- Psychoeducation and information
- Self-reflection prompts and journaling
- Research assistance and literature review
- Training scenarios and case examples
- Assessment report structuring (not interpretation)
- Behavior tracking and pattern identification
- Administrative and documentation support

**Inappropriate Uses:**
- Diagnosis or diagnostic impressions
- Therapy or clinical treatment
- Crisis intervention or safety assessment
- Interpretation of individual assessment results
- Mandatory reporting decisions
- Competency or forensic opinions
- Medication recommendations

### Key Psychological Frameworks Reference

When prompts involve psychological frameworks, consider:

**Assessment Models:** Biopsychosocial, Mental Status Exam, Functional Behavioral Assessment
**Therapeutic Orientations:** CBT, DBT, ACT, Psychodynamic, Humanistic, Systemic
**Behavior Change:** COM-B, Transtheoretical Model, Social Cognitive Theory, Nudge
**Research Approaches:** Phenomenological, Grounded Theory, Thematic Analysis, IPA
**Organizational:** PERMA, Job Demands-Resources, Psychological Safety
