# Informed Consent Template Builder

**Important Disclaimer:** This tool provides a FRAMEWORK for structuring informed consent documents. It does NOT provide legal advice, IRB approval, or guarantee regulatory compliance. Informed consent documents must be reviewed by appropriate institutional review boards (IRBs), ethics committees, legal counsel, and/or licensing bodies as applicable to your jurisdiction and context. Requirements vary by institution, jurisdiction, population, and type of activity. Always verify current regulatory requirements with appropriate authorities.

**Objective:** Guide the creation of comprehensive informed consent documents for research or clinical settings by providing required elements, language accessibility recommendations, context-specific adaptations, and compliance considerations—while maintaining clear boundaries that final documents must be reviewed by appropriate oversight bodies.

**Instructions:**

1. **Determine consent context and requirements**
   * Identify the consent context:
     - **Research consent:** Studies involving human participants
     - **Clinical consent:** Therapeutic services, treatment, assessment
     - **Dual-role consent:** Research conducted in clinical settings
     - **Educational consent:** Training, teaching, or supervision contexts
   * Gather essential parameters:
     - What is the specific activity requiring consent? (study, treatment type, assessment)
     - Who is the target population? (adults, minors, vulnerable populations, specific conditions)
     - What is the setting? (university, hospital, private practice, community, online)
     - What jurisdiction(s) apply? (federal, state/province, international)
     - What regulatory bodies have oversight? (IRB, ethics committee, licensing board)
     - What is the anticipated reading level of participants?
     - Are there language translation needs?
     - Are there capacity concerns requiring additional safeguards?
   * Identify special considerations:
     - Vulnerable populations (children, prisoners, cognitively impaired, economically disadvantaged)
     - Power differentials (students, employees, patients)
     - Sensitive topics (trauma, illegal behavior, stigmatized conditions)
     - Deception or incomplete disclosure requirements

2. **Structure required consent elements**

   **A. For RESEARCH consent, include all applicable elements:**

   | Element | Description | Required? |
   |---------|-------------|-----------|
   | Study title | Official title of the research | Yes |
   | Principal investigator | Name, credentials, contact information | Yes |
   | Institutional affiliation | Organization conducting/sponsoring research | Yes |
   | Purpose of research | Why the study is being conducted | Yes |
   | Procedures | What participants will be asked to do, in sequence | Yes |
   | Duration | Time commitment required (per session and total) | Yes |
   | Number of participants | Approximate enrollment target | Recommended |
   | Risks | Potential harms (physical, psychological, social, economic, legal) | Yes |
   | Benefits | Potential benefits to participant and/or society | Yes |
   | Alternatives | Options other than participating | Yes |
   | Confidentiality | How data will be protected, stored, shared | Yes |
   | Compensation | Payment, reimbursement, incentives | If applicable |
   | Costs | Any costs to participant | If applicable |
   | Voluntary participation | Right to refuse or withdraw without penalty | Yes |
   | Withdrawal procedures | How to withdraw, what happens to data | Yes |
   | New findings | How participants will be informed of relevant new information | If applicable |
   | Contact information | Who to contact for questions, concerns, complaints | Yes |
   | Signature lines | Participant, researcher, date | Yes |

   **B. For CLINICAL consent, include all applicable elements:**

   | Element | Description | Required? |
   |---------|-------------|-----------|
   | Provider information | Name, credentials, license, contact | Yes |
   | Nature of services | Type of therapy, assessment, or treatment | Yes |
   | Approach/methods | Therapeutic orientation, techniques used | Yes |
   | Expected duration | Frequency, length of sessions, estimated treatment duration | Recommended |
   | Risks | Potential discomfort, worsening of symptoms, relationship impacts | Yes |
   | Benefits | Expected outcomes, what therapy can and cannot address | Yes |
   | Alternatives | Other treatment options available | Yes |
   | Confidentiality | What is protected, limits to confidentiality | Yes |
   | Limits to confidentiality | Mandated reporting, duty to warn, legal processes | Yes (critical) |
   | Fees and payment | Session fees, insurance, payment policies, missed appointments | Yes |
   | Cancellation policy | Notice required, fees for missed sessions | Yes |
   | Emergency procedures | What to do in crisis, after-hours contact | Yes |
   | Communication policies | Email, text, phone, social media boundaries | Recommended |
   | Records | How records are kept, retention period, access rights | Yes |
   | Termination | How therapy ends, referral process | Recommended |
   | Complaints | How to file complaints with licensing board | Yes |
   | Signature lines | Client, provider, date | Yes |

3. **Apply language accessibility guidelines**
   * Readability standards:
     - Target 6th-8th grade reading level for general adult populations
     - Use Flesch-Kincaid or SMOG readability measures
     - Aim for Flesch Reading Ease score of 60+ (plain English)
   * Language recommendations:

   | Instead of... | Use... |
   |---------------|--------|
   | Utilize | Use |
   | Participate | Take part |
   | Compensation | Payment |
   | Confidentiality | Privacy |
   | Voluntary | Your choice |
   | Terminate | End |
   | Subsequently | Later, after |
   | Approximately | About |
   | Regarding | About |
   | In the event that | If |
   | Methodology | Method |
   | Disseminate | Share |
   | Ascertain | Find out |
   | Adverse events | Bad things that could happen |

   * Formatting recommendations:
     - Use short sentences (average 15-20 words)
     - Use short paragraphs (3-5 sentences)
     - Use bullet points and numbered lists
     - Use headers and subheaders to organize sections
     - Use white space generously
     - Use 12-point font minimum
     - Avoid all-caps except for brief emphasis
     - Define technical terms when they must be used
   * Structure recommendations:
     - Lead with most important information
     - Use the inverted pyramid (key points first)
     - Include a summary section for long documents
     - Provide a "key points" box for essential information
   * Special population adaptations:
     - **Children/adolescents:** Use age-appropriate language, include assent forms
     - **Non-native speakers:** Provide translated versions, use simple sentence structures
     - **Low literacy:** Consider verbal consent with witness, use visual aids
     - **Cognitive impairment:** Use simplified versions, involve legally authorized representatives

4. **Provide context-specific template sections**

   **A. RESEARCH CONSENT TEMPLATE:**

   ```
   INFORMED CONSENT FOR RESEARCH PARTICIPATION

   STUDY TITLE: [Full title in plain language]

   PRINCIPAL INVESTIGATOR: [Name, Degree(s)], [Title]
   INSTITUTION: [University/Organization]
   CONTACT: [Phone] | [Email]

   ---

   KEY INFORMATION (Summary)

   You are being asked to take part in a research study. Here is what you need to know:

   - PURPOSE: We are studying [plain language description of what and why]
   - WHAT YOU WILL DO: [Brief overview of procedures]
   - TIME: This will take about [duration]
   - RISKS: [Most significant risks in plain language]
   - BENEFITS: [What participant and/or science may gain]
   - YOUR CHOICE: You do not have to participate. You can stop at any time.

   Please read this form carefully. Ask any questions before you decide.

   ---

   WHY IS THIS STUDY BEING DONE?

   [2-3 paragraphs explaining the purpose in accessible language]

   ---

   WHAT WILL HAPPEN IF I TAKE PART?

   If you agree to be in this study, you will:

   1. [First procedure with time estimate]
   2. [Second procedure with time estimate]
   3. [Continue numbering...]

   Total time: [Total duration]

   ---

   WHAT ARE THE RISKS?

   [Describe risks organized by likelihood and severity]

   - [Risk 1]: [Description and likelihood]
   - [Risk 2]: [Description and likelihood]

   [Include statement about unknown risks if applicable]

   ---

   WHAT ARE THE BENEFITS?

   To you: [Direct benefits or "This study may not benefit you directly"]
   To others: [How this research may help others or advance knowledge]

   ---

   WHAT OTHER OPTIONS ARE THERE?

   You do not have to be in this study. [Describe alternatives if any]

   ---

   HOW WILL MY INFORMATION BE PROTECTED?

   [Describe confidentiality measures]

   - Your name will not be [...]
   - Data will be stored [...]
   - Only [who] will have access to [...]
   - Results may be published but [...]

   [Include limits to confidentiality if any]

   ---

   WILL I BE PAID?

   [Compensation details or "You will not be paid for this study"]

   ---

   WHAT IF I HAVE QUESTIONS OR CONCERNS?

   Questions about the study: Contact [PI name] at [contact info]

   Questions about your rights as a research participant:
   Contact [IRB/Ethics Board name] at [contact info]

   ---

   VOLUNTARY PARTICIPATION AND WITHDRAWAL

   Your participation is completely voluntary. You may:
   - Decide not to participate
   - Stop participating at any time
   - Skip any questions you don't want to answer

   If you withdraw, [describe what happens to data, no penalty statement]

   Participant Signature         Date

   ___________________________ _______________
   Researcher Signature          Date
   ```

5. **Address special populations and circumstances**
   * **Minors (children and adolescents):**
     - Parental/guardian consent required (from legally authorized representative)
     - Child assent required (age-appropriate explanation and agreement)
     - Consider mature minor doctrine if applicable
     - Age-appropriate language in assent forms
     - Address confidentiality between parent and minor
   * **Cognitively impaired individuals:**
     - Assess capacity to consent
     - Use legally authorized representative if lacking capacity
     - Maximize participation in decision-making
     - Use simplified language and visual aids
     - Consider ongoing consent (not just one-time)
   * **Non-English speakers:**
     - Provide consent in participant's primary language
     - Use certified translators (not family members)
     - Back-translate to verify accuracy
   * **Online/remote participants:**
     - Electronic signature considerations
     - Data security in online environment
     - Verify identity and age
     - Screen capture and recording policies
   * **Deception research:**
     - Justify necessity of deception
     - Minimize deception
     - Plan for debriefing
     - Ensure debriefing occurs promptly
     - Consider option to withdraw data after debriefing

6. **Provide compliance verification checklist**
   * Regulatory compliance:
     - [ ] Reviewed applicable federal regulations (e.g., Common Rule for US research)
     - [ ] Reviewed state/provincial requirements
     - [ ] Reviewed institutional policies
     - [ ] Reviewed professional ethics codes
     - [ ] Considered HIPAA/health privacy regulations if applicable
   * Content completeness:
     - [ ] All required elements included
     - [ ] Risks described completely and accurately
     - [ ] Benefits described without exaggeration
     - [ ] Confidentiality and its limits clearly explained
     - [ ] Voluntary nature emphasized
     - [ ] Withdrawal process explained
     - [ ] Contact information complete
   * Accessibility:
     - [ ] Reading level appropriate for population
     - [ ] Technical terms defined or avoided
     - [ ] Format aids comprehension
     - [ ] Translations provided if needed
   * Process considerations:
     - [ ] Adequate time for review provided
     - [ ] Opportunity for questions ensured
     - [ ] Coercion and undue influence minimized
     - [ ] Copy provided to participant
     - [ ] Signed copy retained appropriately
   * Documentation:
     - [ ] Version number and date on form
     - [ ] IRB/ethics approval number (for research)
     - [ ] Appropriate signatures and dates
     - [ ] Witness signature if required

**Psychology-Specific Considerations:**
- Informed consent is an ongoing process, not a one-time event; understanding should be verified throughout
- Consent documents are the minimum; the consent conversation is equally important
- Power differentials can undermine voluntariness; take extra care with vulnerable populations
- Cultural factors influence understanding of consent; adapt approach appropriately
- Capacity to consent may fluctuate; reassess as needed
- Consent for one thing does not imply consent for another (e.g., treatment vs. research vs. training)
- Some populations cannot consent directly (minors, incapacitated); follow surrogate consent procedures
- Professional ethics codes may have stricter requirements than legal minimums

**Expected Output:** A context-appropriate informed consent document containing:
1. All required elements for the specific context (research or clinical)
2. Language at appropriate reading level for target population
3. Clear organization with headers, formatting, and summaries
4. Context-specific adaptations as needed
5. Compliance verification checklist completed
6. Notes on any special considerations or required reviews

## Informed Consent Document: Research Study

**Context:** Online survey study on workplace stress with adult employees
**Population:** General adult population, English-speaking, online recruitment
**Key Considerations:** Electronic consent, minimal risk, anonymous data collection

### Consent Form (Formatted for Online Presentation)

**CONSENT TO PARTICIPATE IN RESEARCH**

**Study Title:** Understanding Workplace Stress and Coping Strategies

**Researcher:** Dr. Jane Smith, PhD
Department of Psychology, University of Example
Email: jsmith@example.edu | Phone: (555) 123-4567

---

**KEY POINTS**

- This is a research study about stress at work
- You will complete an online survey (about 15 minutes)
- Your answers are anonymous
- You may stop at any time
- There is no payment for participating

---

**WHAT IS THIS STUDY ABOUT?**

We are studying how people experience and cope with stress at work. We want to understand what makes work stressful and what helps people manage that stress. This information may help develop better workplace wellness programs.

**WHO CAN PARTICIPATE?**

You can participate if you:
- Are 18 years or older
- Are currently employed (part-time or full-time)
- Have been in your current job for at least 3 months

**WHAT WILL I DO?**

If you agree to participate, you will:
1. Answer questions about your job (5 minutes)
2. Answer questions about stress you experience (5 minutes)
3. Answer questions about how you cope with stress (5 minutes)

Total time: About 15 minutes

**ARE THERE RISKS?**

This study has minimal risks. Some questions ask about stressful experiences, which may cause temporary discomfort. You may skip any question you don't want to answer.

If you experience distress, you may contact:
- National Alliance on Mental Illness (NAMI): 1-800-950-6264
- Your employee assistance program (if available)

**ARE THERE BENEFITS?**

You may not benefit directly from this study. Your answers will help researchers understand workplace stress, which may lead to better support programs in the future.

**WILL MY ANSWERS BE PRIVATE?**

Yes. This survey is anonymous. We do not collect your name, email, or any information that identifies you. Your responses cannot be linked back to you.

Data will be stored securely and only the research team will access it. Published results will describe groups, not individuals.

**WILL I BE PAID?**

No. There is no payment for this study.

**WHAT IF I HAVE QUESTIONS?**

About the study: Contact Dr. Jane Smith at jsmith@example.edu

About your rights as a participant: Contact the University of Example IRB at irb@example.edu or (555) 987-6543

**DO I HAVE TO PARTICIPATE?**

No. Participation is completely voluntary. You may close this survey at any time. There is no penalty for not participating or stopping early.
