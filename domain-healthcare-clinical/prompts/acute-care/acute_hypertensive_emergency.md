## Objective

Distinguish hypertensive emergency (severe HTN with acute end-organ damage) from urgency (severe HTN without end-organ damage), choose the appropriate IV antihypertensive based on the affected organ, set the right BP reduction target and rate, and avoid over-correction.

## Inputs

- BP measurement (verify in both arms; manual confirmation)
- Symptoms: headache, vision changes, chest pain, dyspnea, neurologic deficit, oliguria, abdominal/back pain, pregnancy
- End-organ assessment:
  - Neurologic (stroke, encephalopathy, ICH): exam, CT head
  - Cardiac (acute MI, acute pulmonary edema, aortic dissection): ECG, troponin, CXR
  - Renal (AKI): BMP, UA
  - Eye (papilledema, retinal hemorrhages, exudates): fundoscopy
  - Pregnancy (eclampsia, preeclampsia): obstetric assessment
- Baseline BP if known (chronic HTN tolerates higher BPs and over-correction is dangerous)

## Reasoning Steps

1. **Define and classify.**
   - **Hypertensive emergency:** severe BP elevation (typically SBP >180 or DBP >120) WITH acute end-organ damage. Requires IV therapy in monitored setting.
   - **Hypertensive urgency:** severe BP elevation WITHOUT acute end-organ damage. Treated with PO therapy over hours-to-days; aggressive IV reduction harmful.
   - The number itself does not define emergency — the end-organ damage does.

2. **Confirm with end-organ workup.**
   - ECG, troponin, CXR.
   - BMP, UA (proteinuria, hematuria).
   - CT head if any neurologic symptoms.
   - Fundoscopy for retinopathy.
   - Aortic CTA if dissection suspected (asymmetric pulses, severe chest/back pain, widened mediastinum on CXR).
   - Pregnancy test in any woman of reproductive age; full preeclampsia workup if pregnant or postpartum.

3. **General BP reduction rule.**
   - Reduce MAP by ~10–20% in the first hour, then another 5–15% over next 23 hours.
   - Goal in first 24 h: typically MAP not below ~110, BP not below ~160/100 (in chronic HTN) — except specific scenarios (dissection, eclampsia, ICH thresholds).
   - Faster correction → watershed ischemia (brain, kidney, retina) in patients chronically autoregulated to higher BP.

4. **Tailored targets and agents by end-organ damage.**

   - **Aortic dissection:**
     - Target SBP <120 and HR <60 within 20 minutes — aggressive.
     - **Esmolol or labetalol FIRST** to control HR (to prevent dP/dt → propagation of dissection), then add nitroprusside or nicardipine for BP.
     - Avoid pure vasodilator first — reflex tachycardia worsens dissection.
     - Surgical consult immediate for type A; medical management for uncomplicated type B with surveillance.

   - **Acute MI / unstable angina:**
     - Target SBP <140 (to reduce myocardial O2 demand).
     - Nitroglycerin IV infusion 5–200 mcg/min — direct coronary vasodilation, preload reduction.
     - Beta-blocker (metoprolol IV or PO) once not in shock.
     - Avoid hydralazine (reflex tachycardia worsens ischemia).

   - **Acute pulmonary edema (cardiogenic):**
     - Nitroglycerin IV 5–200 mcg/min (preload reduction, modest afterload reduction).
     - Loop diuretic (furosemide 40–80 mg IV).
     - Non-invasive ventilation (CPAP/BiPAP) reduces work of breathing and improves oxygenation.
     - Avoid beta-blockers acutely in decompensated HF.

   - **Acute ischemic stroke:**
     - If candidate for tPA: lower BP to ≤185/110 before thrombolysis with labetalol or nicardipine. Do not delay tPA for BP control.
     - If not candidate for tPA: permissive hypertension up to 220/120 unless other indication to lower (BP often falls spontaneously after stroke).
     - Post-thrombectomy: lower target (140/90) if successful recanalization.

   - **Acute hemorrhagic stroke (ICH):**
     - INTERACT2 / ATACH-II: lowering SBP to ~140 (acute target 140–160) is safe and may improve hematoma expansion outcomes.
     - Nicardipine or clevidipine infusion (titratable, smooth control).
     - Avoid nitroprusside in ICH (raises ICP).

   - **Hypertensive encephalopathy:**
     - Reduce MAP 20–25% in first hour. Then more gradual.
     - Nicardipine, clevidipine, or labetalol.
     - Distinguish from PRES (similar imaging) — same management.

   - **Acute kidney injury / scleroderma renal crisis:**
     - ACE inhibitor (captopril, IV enalaprilat) is first-line for scleroderma renal crisis (life-saving).
     - For other AKI from severe HTN, gradual reduction with nicardipine.

   - **Eclampsia / severe preeclampsia:**
     - Magnesium sulfate 4 g IV over 15–20 min then 1–2 g/h infusion (seizure prophylaxis and treatment).
     - Labetalol 20 mg IV bolus, may repeat 40 mg q10 min (max 300 mg). Or hydralazine 5–10 mg IV q15–30 min. Or nifedipine 10 mg PO.
     - Target SBP 140–160, DBP 90–100. Avoid abrupt drops (placental hypoperfusion).
     - Definitive treatment: delivery.

   - **Pheochromocytoma crisis:**
     - **Phentolamine** (alpha-blocker) first; do NOT give beta-blocker first (unopposed alpha causes hypertensive crisis).
     - Or nicardipine.
     - Urgent surgical consultation; alpha-block before beta-block before surgery.

   - **Cocaine / sympathomimetic-associated:**
     - Benzodiazepines first (sedates the sympathetic surge).
     - Phentolamine for refractory.
     - Avoid pure beta-blockers (unopposed alpha).
     - Labetalol controversial — has alpha and beta activity; some use, others avoid.

5. **Agent selection summary.**

   | Agent | Mechanism | Onset | Duration | Best for | Avoid in |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | Labetalol | alpha + beta block | 5–10 min | 3–6 h | most emergencies, eclampsia | severe asthma, decomp HF, bradyarrhythmia |
   | Esmolol | beta-1 selective | 1–2 min | 10–20 min | dissection, perioperative | severe asthma, decomp HF, AV block |
   | Nicardipine | DHP CCB | 5–10 min | 1–4 h | most emergencies, hemorrhagic stroke, encephalopathy | severe AS, hypotension |
   | Clevidipine | DHP CCB (ultra-short) | 1–2 min | 5–15 min | rapid titration needed | egg/soy allergy (lipid emulsion), severe AS |
   | Nitroglycerin | venous > arterial vasodilator | 1–2 min | 5 min | acute pulmonary edema, MI | RV infarct, recent PDE5 inhibitor |
   | Nitroprusside | arterial + venous vasodilator | seconds | 1–2 min | last resort, careful with cyanide tox | renal failure (thiocyanate accumulation), ICH (raises ICP), prolonged use |
   | Hydralazine | direct arterial vasodilator | 10–20 min | 4–6 h | eclampsia (older agent) | aortic dissection, ischemia (reflex tachy) |
   | Phentolamine | alpha-block | 1–2 min | 10–30 min | pheo, sympathomimetic | hypotension |
   | Enalaprilat | ACEi (IV) | 15 min | 6 h | scleroderma renal crisis | pregnancy, bilateral RAS |

6. **Hypertensive urgency (no end-organ damage).**
   - Asymptomatic SBP 180+ in clinic does not require ED admission.
   - Restart or intensify oral antihypertensives. Common choice: amlodipine, lisinopril, or hydrochlorothiazide added or up-titrated.
   - Rapid IV reduction is harmful in this scenario.
   - Identify cause: medication non-adherence (most common), volume overload, NSAIDs, drug use, OSA, secondary HTN.
   - Outpatient follow-up within 1–7 days.

7. **Disposition.**
   - True emergency: ICU or step-down with arterial line monitoring and titratable IV infusion.
   - Urgency: discharge with oral medication adjustment if asymptomatic and reliable follow-up.

## Worked Example

**Input:** 64 y/o M, sudden onset 10/10 tearing chest pain radiating to back. BP 218/112 right arm, 178/96 left arm. HR 102 sinus. PMH: HTN. CXR: widened mediastinum. CTA chest: type B aortic dissection, descending aorta, no end-organ malperfusion.

**Output:**

```
CLASSIFICATION: Hypertensive emergency.
END-ORGAN DAMAGE: Aortic dissection (type B, descending).
KEY SCENARIO: Aortic dissection — most aggressive BP control scenario.

BP REDUCTION TARGET:
- Initial 20 minutes: HR <60 AND SBP <120.
- This is the most aggressive BP target in any hypertensive emergency. The goal is to reduce shear stress (dP/dt) on the aortic wall to prevent propagation.
- Maintain target until imaging stable and patient transferred to vascular surgery / cardiothoracic care.

AGENT SELECTION:
- FIRST: control HR before adding pure vasodilator. Esmolol bolus 500 mcg/kg over 1 min, then infusion 50–300 mcg/kg/min titrated to HR <60. Or labetalol 20 mg IV q10 min titrated.
- SECOND (after HR controlled): add nicardipine infusion 5 mg/h, titrate to SBP <120. Clevidipine 1–2 mg/h titrated is alternative.
- AVOID:
  - Pure vasodilators first (hydralazine, nitroprusside without beta-blocker) → reflex tachycardia → propagation of dissection.
  - Nitroprusside as monotherapy in dissection.
  - Calcium channel blockers (nicardipine) before HR control — same reflex tachy concern.

ADJUNCTS:
- Pain control: morphine 4 mg IV, may repeat. Pain drives sympathetic surge.
- Beta-blocker continuation oral once stable (atenolol or metoprolol succinate).
- Anti-platelet, anticoagulation: not routine in dissection (avoid).
- Surgical consult: type B dissection without malperfusion or rapid expansion is medically managed with strict BP/HR control. Type A would require emergent cardiothoracic surgery.

DISPOSITION:
- ICU with arterial line for continuous BP monitoring.
- Vascular surgery (or cardiothoracic) consultation immediately for type B dissection management; transfer to vascular surgery service or center if needed.
- Serial imaging for surveillance: follow-up CTA at 48–72 h, then per protocol (1 week, 1 month, 3 month, 6 month, annually).

WORKUP:
- Already: CTA confirmed dissection.
- Pending: troponin (rule out concurrent MI from aortic root involvement — this is type B so coronary involvement less likely but check), BMP (renal artery involvement → AKI), CBC, coags, type and screen.
- Repeat exam: peripheral pulses (femoral, popliteal, DP, PT) for malperfusion. Mental status (cerebral malperfusion). Abdominal exam (mesenteric malperfusion).
- ECG to look for ischemic changes.
- TTE/TEE for proximal extension into ascending aorta (would change to type A and require surgery).

PITFALLS TO AVOID:
- Starting nicardipine first without rate control → reflex tachycardia → propagation.
- Failing to address pain — sympathetic activation undoes BP control.
- Not measuring BP in BOTH arms (here noted asymmetric, supports diagnosis; if missed, could miss subclavian compromise).
- Failing to transfer or consult vascular/cardiothoracic in a timely manner; type B can progress to require surgical intervention if malperfusion or rapid expansion develops.
- Lowering BP too fast in a chronically hypertensive patient: brain, kidney, coronary watershed areas have right-shifted autoregulation. Aortic dissection is the exception that justifies aggressive lowering, but watch for malperfusion signs (deteriorating mental status, oliguria, lactic acidosis, abdominal pain, leg ischemia).
```
