## Objective

Manage a patient with acute coronary syndrome (STEMI, NSTEMI, or unstable angina): triage to reperfusion (primary PCI vs fibrinolysis vs early invasive), name antiplatelet and anticoagulant choices and doses, manage anti-ischemic therapy, and risk-stratify.

## Inputs

- Presentation: chest pain duration, quality, associated symptoms, time from onset
- ECG (with serial ECGs if dynamic)
- Troponin (initial and serial; high-sensitivity if available)
- Vitals: BP, HR, SpO2, signs of heart failure or shock
- Comorbidities: diabetes, CKD (eGFR), prior CABG/PCI, bleeding risk factors, recent stroke/surgery, anticoagulant use
- Local PCI capability and door-to-balloon time

## Reasoning Steps

1. **Classify ACS subtype.**
   - **STEMI:** ST elevation ≥1 mm in ≥2 contiguous leads (≥2 mm in V2-V3 for men <40, ≥1.5 mm women, ≥2.5 mm men <40). New LBBB with concerning clinical picture (Sgarbossa criteria for STEMI-equivalent in pre-existing LBBB or paced rhythm: concordant ST elevation ≥1 mm, concordant ST depression V1–V3, or excessively discordant ST elevation ≥5 mm). Posterior MI: ST depression V1–V3 with tall R waves, confirm with V7–V9.
   - **NSTEMI:** elevated troponin without persistent ST elevation. ECG may show ST depression, T-wave inversion, or be normal.
   - **Unstable angina:** ischemic symptoms without troponin elevation. Increasingly rare with high-sensitivity troponin (hsTn detects nearly all true ischemia).

2. **STEMI: reperfusion is the priority.**
   - **Primary PCI is preferred** if can be performed within 120 minutes of first medical contact (FMC).
   - **Fibrinolysis** if PCI unavailable within that window; door-to-needle ≤30 min. Tenecteplase 0.5 mg/kg single weight-based bolus (max 50 mg). Then transfer to PCI center for routine post-fibrinolysis angiography 3–24 hours later (pharmacoinvasive strategy).
   - Cath lab activation within 10 minutes of STEMI ECG.
   - Goal door-to-balloon ≤90 min (PCI center) or ≤120 min (transfer).

3. **STEMI: pharmacotherapy in parallel with cath activation.**
   - **Aspirin 162–325 mg chewed PO** immediately.
   - **P2Y12 inhibitor:**
     - **Ticagrelor 180 mg load** (preferred over clopidogrel; CV death/MI/stroke benefit) — caution in dyspnea history, bradyarrhythmia.
     - **Prasugrel 60 mg load** — avoid in age ≥75, weight <60 kg, prior stroke/TIA. Often given after coronary anatomy known.
     - **Clopidogrel 600 mg load** — alternative; lower potency. Used if ticagrelor/prasugrel contraindicated, or in fibrinolysis era.
   - **Anticoagulant:**
     - **Heparin (UFH) 60 units/kg IV bolus (max 4000), then infusion 12 units/kg/h (max 1000/h)** to aPTT 1.5–2× control.
     - Bivalirudin 0.75 mg/kg bolus + 1.75 mg/kg/h infusion alternative, often used in cath lab.
     - Enoxaparin 1 mg/kg SC q12h alternative; 30 mg IV bolus + 1 mg/kg SC for STEMI fibrinolysis (per ExTRACT-TIMI 25).
   - **Statin: atorvastatin 80 mg PO** load on arrival (high-intensity).
   - **Beta-blocker** within 24 h if not contraindicated (no shock, no acute heart failure, no second-degree or higher AV block, no severe asthma): metoprolol tartrate 12.5–25 mg PO BID, titrated. Avoid IV beta-blocker in acute STEMI with hemodynamic instability (COMMIT trial).
   - **Nitrates:** sublingual NTG 0.4 mg q5 min × 3 for ongoing pain. IV NTG infusion 5–200 mcg/min for HTN, heart failure, or persistent ischemic pain. AVOID in inferior MI with RV involvement (preload-dependent — drops BP), severe AS, recent PDE-5 inhibitor.
   - **Oxygen** only if SpO2 <90% — no benefit and possible harm with routine supplemental O2 (DETO2X-AMI).
   - **Morphine** for pain only if uncontrolled by NTG. Use cautiously: morphine slows P2Y12 absorption (delayed antiplatelet effect). Fentanyl is alternative.

4. **NSTEMI / UA: invasive vs conservative timing.**
   - **Immediate invasive (within 2 h)** for ongoing ischemia despite therapy, hemodynamic instability, mechanical complications, life-threatening arrhythmia.
   - **Early invasive (within 24 h)** for high-risk: GRACE >140, dynamic ST/T changes, troponin rise. Most NSTEMI fits here.
   - **Delayed invasive (24–72 h)** for intermediate-risk: GRACE 109–140, diabetes, CKD, EF <40%, prior CABG/PCI, post-MI angina.
   - **Ischemia-guided / conservative** for low-risk, stable, troponin-negative: stress testing or CT coronary angiography after symptom resolution.

5. **NSTEMI / UA pharmacotherapy.**
   - Aspirin 162–325 mg load, then 81 mg daily.
   - P2Y12 inhibitor — typically held until coronary anatomy known if angiography planned within 24 h (clopidogrel can be loaded earlier; ticagrelor/prasugrel timing depends on local practice). If not going to cath, load with ticagrelor or clopidogrel.
   - Anticoagulant: enoxaparin 1 mg/kg SC q12h (adjust for renal function — 1 mg/kg q24h if CrCl <30) or fondaparinux 2.5 mg SC daily (preferred in increased bleeding risk; avoid as sole agent in PCI due to catheter thrombosis without UFH supplementation), or UFH infusion if invasive imminent.
   - Statin atorvastatin 80 mg.
   - Beta-blocker as above.
   - Anti-ischemic: NTG, morphine sparingly.

6. **Cardiogenic shock.**
   - Activate cath lab regardless of subtype; primary PCI improves survival in STEMI cardiogenic shock (SHOCK trial).
   - Norepinephrine first vasopressor.
   - Dobutamine for inotropy if MAP rescued but CO low.
   - Mechanical support: IABP no longer routinely recommended (IABP-SHOCK II); Impella, VA-ECMO selectively for severe shock.

7. **Special situations.**
   - **Right ventricular MI** (in inferior STEMI): preload-dependent. Avoid nitrates and aggressive diuresis. Volume load (NS bolus). Norepinephrine for pressure. PCI of culprit is therapy.
   - **Posterior MI** (ST depression V1–V3, tall R waves V1): often misread. Confirm with V7–V9. Treat as STEMI.
   - **Wellens syndrome:** biphasic or deeply inverted T waves V2–V3 in pain-free state with prior ischemic chest pain — proximal LAD lesion. Urgent angiography; do NOT stress.
   - **De Winter T waves:** upsloping ST depression at J point with tall T waves in precordial leads — proximal LAD occlusion, treat as STEMI-equivalent.
   - **Pregnancy:** PCI preferred over fibrinolysis. Radiation shielding; consider radial access.
   - **Type 2 MI** (demand ischemia from sepsis, anemia, tachycardia, hypotension without thrombus): treat the trigger. Do not always proceed to cath; depends on clinical picture and ischemic burden.

8. **Secondary prevention initiated by 24–48 h.**
   - Aspirin 81 mg daily indefinitely.
   - P2Y12 inhibitor (ticagrelor 90 mg BID, prasugrel 10 mg daily, or clopidogrel 75 mg daily) for 12 months minimum (DAPT). Longer durations weighed by ischemic vs bleeding risk.
   - Statin: high-intensity (atorva 80 or rosuva 40) regardless of baseline LDL.
   - ACE/ARB if EF <40%, HTN, DM, CKD.
   - MRA (spironolactone, eplerenone) if EF <40% with HF symptoms or DM.
   - Beta-blocker continued.
   - Smoking cessation, BP control, lipid management, diabetes management, cardiac rehab referral.
   - SGLT2 inhibitor if T2DM, CKD, or HF.
   - Eplerenone after STEMI with EF <40% + HF or DM (EPHESUS).

## Worked Example

**Input:** 58 y/o M, smoker, presenting with 45 minutes of crushing substernal chest pain. BP 138/82, HR 92, SpO2 98%. ECG: ST elevation 3 mm V1-V4 with reciprocal ST depression in II, III, aVF. Troponin pending. PCI center available, door-to-balloon achievable in 60 minutes. Aspirin allergy: none. PMH: HTN. Cr 0.9.

**Output:**

```
SUBTYPE: Anterior STEMI (ST elevation V1–V4 with reciprocal inferior depression).
LOCATION: Anterior — likely proximal LAD occlusion.
TIME FROM ONSET: 45 minutes — very early, large salvageable myocardium.

REPERFUSION DECISION:
- Primary PCI. Activate cath lab now (door-to-balloon goal ≤90 min, achievable in 60 min).
- No fibrinolysis — PCI available within window.

ANTIPLATELETS:
- Aspirin 325 mg chewed PO now.
- Ticagrelor 180 mg PO load now (preferred over clopidogrel given STEMI presentation; preserved renal function; no relative contraindications listed).
  - If concerns about CABG (unlikely given STEMI plan for PCI), some centers defer P2Y12 until anatomy known.

ANTICOAGULATION:
- Heparin 60 units/kg IV bolus = 4200, capped at 4000 IV. Then infusion 12 units/kg/h ≈ 840 units/h, titrated to aPTT 1.5–2× control. Cath lab will continue or switch to bivalirudin per their protocol.
- Bivalirudin alternative if used.

ANTI-ISCHEMIC:
- Sublingual NTG 0.4 mg q5 min × 3 for ongoing pain.
- IV NTG infusion 5–10 mcg/min titrated for persistent pain or HTN, after sublingual response (caution: large anterior MI without RV involvement — fine for IV NTG, but keep BP monitored).
- Beta-blocker: metoprolol tartrate 12.5–25 mg PO once stable (within 24 h). Hold IV beta-blocker — patient is stable, no acute hypertension, no shock, but COMMIT showed harm with early IV beta-blocker.
- Avoid morphine if possible (delays P2Y12 effect). Fentanyl 25–50 mcg IV alternative if pain persists despite NTG.

OTHER ACUTE:
- Atorvastatin 80 mg PO load now.
- Oxygen only if SpO2 <90% (currently 98%, no O2 needed).
- Continuous telemetry, frequent vitals.
- Two large-bore PIVs.
- Foley if going to cath and clinically warranted.

SHOCK MANAGEMENT: not applicable currently.

SPECIAL CONSIDERATIONS:
- Anterior STEMI with proximal LAD occlusion has highest risk of cardiogenic shock, mechanical complications (papillary muscle, VSD, free wall rupture days 3–5), and death. Watch closely for new murmur, hypotension, pulmonary edema.
- Watch for early reperfusion arrhythmia (accelerated idioventricular rhythm common, often benign and self-limited).
- Anticipate post-PCI: groin or radial access site care, monitoring for retroperitoneal hematoma if femoral.

POST-ACUTE / SECONDARY PREVENTION (initiated within 24–48 h):
- Aspirin 81 mg daily indefinitely.
- Ticagrelor 90 mg BID for 12 months minimum (DAPT).
- Atorvastatin 80 mg daily.
- Beta-blocker (metoprolol succinate 25–50 mg daily, titrate up).
- ACE inhibitor (lisinopril 5–10 mg daily, titrate) for anterior MI / EF assessment pending — most anterior STEMIs have reduced EF and benefit from ACE/ARB.
- Echocardiogram before discharge for EF assessment; if EF <40% with HF features → eplerenone 25 mg daily; SGLT2 inhibitor (empagliflozin 10 mg daily) for HF benefit regardless of diabetes.
- Smoking cessation counseling and pharmacotherapy.
- Cardiac rehab referral.
- BP and lipid follow-up.
- Document need for ICD reassessment at 40 days post-MI if EF <30–35% with persistent symptoms.
```
