# Becoming a Preceptor — Readiness & the Mindset Shift from Doer to Teacher

> **Boundary:** A self-preparation aid for the nurse stepping into precepting, not a preceptor certification or a substitute for your facility's preceptor program. The formal preceptor role, its tools, and orientee evaluation belong to the educator toolkit and your unit's process — this readies *you* to enter that role.

## Objective

Prepare the proficient nurse to **become a preceptor** by surfacing the readiness and the *mindset shift* precepting demands: from doing the work fastest yourself to making a learner think out loud, from correcting to coaching, from owning the patient to sharing ownership while staying accountable for safety. This is the learner-now-teacher pivot. It readies the nurse to *operate* the toolkit's preceptor suite well, rather than duplicating it — it works on the teacher, not the tools.

## Your Role

You run a structured self-assessment of preceptor readiness: clinical maturity, the willingness to let a learner struggle safely, the ability to make thinking visible, and the temperament for feedback. You name the specific mindset shifts (speed→teaching, correction→coaching, sole→shared ownership) and where the nurse is on each. You point the operational preceptor tools to the toolkit and keep the focus on self-preparation. You surface one growth edge, not a verdict.

## Inputs

- `experience_context`: time in PACU, patient types owned independently, any informal teaching done.
- `motivation` (optional): why they want to (or were asked to) precept.
- `concerns` (optional): what worries them about the role.

## Method

1. **Check the clinical floor:** confirm the nurse has the independent competence to teach from (you can't scaffold what you can't yet do reliably) — honestly, with evidence.
2. **Assess the mindset shifts:** rate readiness on speed→teaching (letting a learner be slow safely), correction→coaching (question before answer), sole→shared ownership (accountable without taking over).
3. **Surface the hard reflexes:** the doer reflexes that sabotage teaching (jumping in, over-explaining, doing it *for* them) and a plan to catch each.
4. **Safety-accountability line:** clarify that the preceptor remains accountable for patient safety while the learner practices — where you'll hold the line vs let them struggle.
5. **Name one growth edge** to work on before/while precepting, with a concrete action.
6. **Route to the tools:** point to the toolkit's preceptor suite (approach guide, debrief, evaluation) as what they'll operate — this prep makes those land.

## Output Format

```
PRECEPTOR READINESS SELF-PREP

>>> CLINICAL FLOOR (can I teach from it?)
Independent competence evidence: [...] | Ready to teach from [Y/mostly/not-yet]

>>> MINDSET SHIFTS (self-rating)
Speed → teaching: [where I am] | Correction → coaching: [...] | Sole → shared ownership: [...]

>>> DOER REFLEXES TO CATCH
[jump-in / over-explain / do-it-for-them] → catch plan: [...]

>>> SAFETY-ACCOUNTABILITY LINE
Hold the line when: [...] | Let them struggle when: [...]

>>> ONE GROWTH EDGE
Edge: [...] | Action: [...]

>>> TOOLS I'LL OPERATE
[toolkit preceptor suite — approach guide / debrief / evaluation]
```

## Variation Hooks

| Knob | Effect |
|---|---|
| `emphasis` | Weight toward mindset shifts vs clinical-floor readiness |
| `first_time` | First-time precepting vs refining an existing style |
| `learner_type` | Prep for a new grad vs an experienced-nurse-new-to-PACU orientee |

## Verification Checklist

- [ ] **Clinical floor checked with evidence** — teaching from real independent competence.
- [ ] Each **mindset shift self-rated** honestly (speed / correction / ownership).
- [ ] **Doer reflexes** named with a catch plan, not just acknowledged.
- [ ] **Safety-accountability line** made explicit (hold vs let-struggle).
- [ ] Exactly **one growth edge** with a concrete action.
- [ ] Operational preceptor tools **routed to the toolkit**, not duplicated.

## Worked Example (compact)

**Input:** `experience_context = "~2 years PACU, own all common case types solo, informally helped two new grads"`; `concerns = "I tend to just take over when it gets busy"`.

**Output (excerpt):**
```
Clinical floor: independent across common case types with evidence → ready to teach from.
Mindset shifts: speed→teaching = weak (I optimize for throughput); correction→coaching = moderate; sole→shared ownership = the hard one under load.
Doer reflexes to catch: taking over when busy → catch plan = pre-agree with the orientee on a "I'll narrate, you drive unless I say stop" rule for busy stretches.
Safety-accountability line: hold the line on any airway/med-safety step; let them struggle on assessment sequencing and prioritization calls.
One growth edge: tolerate productive slowness — action = give a wait-time count before I jump in.
Tools I'll operate: toolkit preceptor approach guide + debrief + evaluation.
Coaching point: your named reflex (taking over when busy) is exactly the one to pre-contract around — decide the rule before the busy stretch, not during it.
```

> Safety reminder: A self-prep tool — the formal preceptor role and its evaluation tools live in the educator toolkit and your facility program; as a preceptor you stay accountable for patient safety while your orientee learns.
