# Preceptor Difficult Conversation Guide — "You're Not On Track"

> Safety reminder: This prepares the preceptor to deliver the message well. It does not substitute for facility HR processes, EAP referral for well-being concerns, or patient-safety event reporting.

## Objective

Prepare a PACU preceptor to deliver a difficult message to an orientee — that they are not on track for sign-off at the current phase, that orientation may be extended, or that a remediation plan is coming. Output is a conversation plan: opener drafts, the hardest sentence verbatim, reaction-handling notes, and boundaries on what is and is not negotiable.

## When to use

- The preceptor's evidence grid (from `pacu_preceptor_approach_guide.md`) supports an Extend or Remediation disposition, and the orientee has not yet heard this framed as at-risk for sign-off.
- A calibration session concluded the orientee is off-track and the primary preceptor needs to communicate the change.
- A specific patient-safety or competency concern needs to be surfaced in 1:1 **before** the written evaluation lands.

## When not to use

- For general end-of-shift debriefs — use `pacu_preceptor_debrief.md`.
- To avoid writing the remediation plan — this conversation precedes or accompanies `pacu_orientee_remediation_plan.md`; it does not replace it.
- For personal or well-being concerns — refer the orientee to the facility EAP; do not diagnose or document personal circumstances.

## Inputs

Ask one at a time.

- **Orientee identifier:** {{initials}}
- **Phase / context:** {{...}}
- **Core message in one sentence** (the preceptor drafts this first): {{e.g., "You are not on track to sign off at the end of Week 10, and we are going to extend orientation by 2 weeks with a focused plan."}}
- **Evidence base:** reference from `pacu_preceptor_approach_guide.md`.
- **Has the orientee heard any of this before?** Confirm in prior debrief(s). Note dates.
- **Relationship dynamics to account for:** {{new preceptor/orientee pair, prior friction, strong rapport that may make message harder to land, language considerations}}
- **Time and setting planned for the conversation:** private, off-unit, uninterrupted, ≥20 minutes.
- **Support available to orientee:** educator, charge, EAP contact (by role, per facility protocol).

## Audience / Scope

- **Primary:** Preceptor preparing the conversation.
- **Secondary:** Educator or nurse manager validating the preceptor's readiness.
- **Scope:** Phase 1 PACU orientation difficult conversations tied to competency trajectory. Not a substitute for HR disciplinary conversations or clinical supervision conversations — those follow facility protocol.

## Output requirements

```markdown
# Conversation Plan — {Initials} — {Phase / Type} — {Date}

> Safety reminder: Conversation prep only. File patient-safety events through facility channels separately. Refer well-being concerns to EAP.

## 1. Core Message (one sentence, delivered in the first 30 seconds)
"{Exact wording. Short. Direct. Not apologetic. Not softened into meaninglessness.}"

Example: "I want to be direct — based on what I've observed over the last 6 weeks, you are not on track to sign off at the end of Week 10, and we're going to put a focused plan in place and extend orientation by 2 weeks."

## 2. Openers (2–3 options to choose from)
- Option A (direct): "{wording}"
- Option B (setup + direct): "{wording}"
- Option C (reference prior debriefs + direct): "{wording}"

Pick the option that matches the orientee's communication style — then rehearse it aloud.

## 3. Evidence Summary (what the orientee will hear after the opener)
- 2–3 specific observations, each anchored to a shift date and observable behavior.
- State each as: "On {date}, during {case}, I observed {behavior}. The impact was {...}. We talked about it in debrief on {date}."
- **No new critical feedback.** Every observation should be one the orientee has already heard in a debrief.

## 4. The Hardest Sentence (verbatim)
"{The one sentence the preceptor is dreading. Write it exactly. Say it out loud before the conversation.}"

Do not let this sentence get dropped, softened, or walked back during the conversation.

## 5. What Happens Next (the plan)
- **Extension / remediation framing:** "{number of additional shifts, reassessment date, scaffold criteria}"
- **Link to the written remediation plan:** "I'll have a written plan to you by {date}, using `pacu_orientee_remediation_plan.md`, so you have it in hand before signatures."
- **What stays the same:** escalation expectations, patient-safety requirements, facility policy.
- **Orientee's role in the plan:** concrete things they'll practice on each shift.
- **Support available:** educator, charge, EAP (by role).

## 6. Reaction Planning
Anticipate and plan for each likely reaction:

| Reaction | What you'll say | What you won't do |
|---|---|---|
| Defensive / argumentative | "{wording that acknowledges and returns to the evidence}" | Argue ratings, renegotiate the disposition, walk back the core message |
| Emotional / tearful | "{wording — offer a pause, reaffirm the plan, not the decision}" | Retract the message, promise outcomes the org hasn't approved |
| Dismissive / flat | "{wording — name the pattern and invite them back}" | Force agreement, over-explain, fill silence with softening |
| Blindsided (hopefully none) | "{wording — acknowledge and own the gap}" | Pretend the gap doesn't exist; this is a preceptor error to own |
| Grateful / receptive | "{wording — accept, then move to the plan}" | Assume receptivity means retention — follow through with the written plan anyway |

## 7. What Is Not Up for Negotiation
- The disposition (Extend / Remediation).
- Patient-safety expectations and facility escalation pathways.
- Scaffold criteria for sign-off.
- Timeline of the written remediation plan.
- Documentation requirements.

## 8. What Is Open for Input
- Which specific shifts the orientee wants to focus practice on.
- Which second preceptor they'd request for the reassessment period (subject to facility scheduling).
- Additional simulated practice topics (case scenarios, quizzes, flashcards from the toolkit).
- Orientee's written response to attach to the remediation plan.

## 9. Closing
- Restate the core message + plan in 2 sentences.
- Confirm next touch point (when the written remediation plan arrives, when the next debrief is).
- Offer EAP referral by role if the conversation surfaced well-being concerns (do not document the reason).

## Self-check after the conversation (for the preceptor)
- [ ] Core message was delivered in the first 30 seconds.
- [ ] Hardest sentence was said verbatim; not softened.
- [ ] No new critical feedback was introduced that hadn't been in a prior debrief.
- [ ] Orientee was offered space to respond.
- [ ] Plan was stated; timeline was named.
- [ ] Conversation was documented (summary only; not verbatim) in the preceptor's notes, not the orientee's chart.

## Sources / reference
- ASPAN *Standards of Perianesthesia Nursing Practice*, {relevant section (if anchoring patient-safety expectations)}
- Facility orientation program difficult-conversation / remediation policy: {{per facility protocol}}
- Facility EAP contact pathway (by role): {{per facility protocol}}
```

## Must / Must not

**Must:**
- Deliver the core message in the first 30 seconds.
- Anchor every observation to a specific shift and observable behavior.
- Verify each observation has previously landed in a debrief (no-surprises).
- State what is and is not negotiable.
- Offer EAP by role if well-being concerns surface — do not diagnose or document.
- Follow through in writing via `pacu_orientee_remediation_plan.md` within the named timeline.
- Rehearse the hardest sentence aloud before the conversation.
- Hold a private, off-unit, uninterrupted setting for ≥20 minutes.

**Must not:**
- Open with softeners that obscure the message ("I just wanted to check in on how you're feeling…" before the core message).
- Argue ratings, renegotiate the disposition, or walk back the core message during the conversation.
- Introduce new critical feedback that wasn't in a prior debrief.
- Use personality labels ("you're shy," "you're arrogant," "you're not a good fit"). Describe observable behavior.
- Reference age, race, sex, disability, religion, national origin, pregnancy, or other protected characteristics.
- Speculate about medical, mental-health, or family circumstances. Refer to EAP by role; do not document.
- Reference license pathway (BSN/ASN/LPN-bridge) or prior unit as a cause.
- Compare the orientee by name to other orientees or staff.
- Discuss the orientee's situation with anyone not in the chain of supervision (educator, nurse manager, HR per facility protocol).
- Document the conversation verbatim in the orientee's personnel or training chart — summary only, per facility policy.
- Include patient-identifying information (MRN, full name, date of birth, room number) in the conversation notes.
- Skip writing the remediation plan after this conversation — the conversation does not replace the plan.

## Quality signals

- The orientee can summarize the core message in their own words afterward.
- Nothing said in the conversation is new information to the orientee.
- The written remediation plan arrives within the named timeline.
- The preceptor delivered the hardest sentence verbatim and did not walk it back.
- EAP was offered if well-being concerns surfaced, without diagnosis or documentation of the reason.

## Self-check (before the conversation)

- [ ] Core message written as a single sentence and rehearsed aloud.
- [ ] Evidence summary anchored to specific shifts and debrief dates.
- [ ] Hardest sentence drafted verbatim.
- [ ] Reaction plan covers defensive / emotional / dismissive / blindsided / grateful.
- [ ] Negotiable vs non-negotiable clearly listed.
- [ ] Private, off-unit, uninterrupted setting reserved for ≥20 minutes.
- [ ] Written remediation plan timeline committed to.
- [ ] No protected-characteristic references; no medical / family speculation.
- [ ] EAP referral pathway (by role) identified in case it's needed.
- [ ] Safety reminder acknowledged.

## Verification

Before the conversation and the written record:

- [ ] Core message is one sentence, direct, and will land in the first 30 seconds.
- [ ] Every observation in the Evidence Summary section was delivered in a prior debrief (dates recorded).
- [ ] Hardest Sentence is written verbatim (not paraphrased) and has been spoken aloud.
- [ ] Reaction matrix covers all five reaction types with both "what you'll say" and "what you won't do."
- [ ] Non-negotiable list explicitly names disposition, patient-safety expectations, scaffold criteria, timeline, and documentation requirements.
- [ ] Plan links to `pacu_orientee_remediation_plan.md` with a concrete delivery date.

## False-Positive Prevention

Do **not** fabricate:

- **No invented shift dates, times, or observations.** Every observation in the Evidence Summary must be something the preceptor actually saw and already raised in a debrief.
- **No invented facility EAP contact, phone number, or pathway.** Use "per facility protocol" / "by role."
- **No speculation about the orientee's medical, mental-health, or family circumstances.** EAP referral by role only; do not document a reason.
- **No references to age, race, sex, disability, religion, national origin, pregnancy, license pathway, or prior unit as a cause.**
- **No personality labels in the core message or reaction-plan wording.**
- **No invented facility HR process specifics** (extension length limits, termination pathways, PIP thresholds).
- **No documentation of patient-safety events** in the conversation plan — those file separately through the facility's event-reporting system.
- **No patient-identifying information** (MRN, full name, full DOB, room number).

## Worked Example

<details>
<summary>Example: Core message + hardest sentence for a Week 8 orientee whose BP-trend cue-recognition hasn't advanced (click to expand)</summary>

```markdown
## 1. Core Message (one sentence, delivered in the first 30 seconds)
"I want to be direct — based on what I've seen across the last 6 weeks, you are not on track to sign off at the end of Week 10, and we're going to put a focused plan in place and extend orientation by 2 weeks."

## 3. Evidence Summary (what the orientee will hear after the opener)
- "On 03/18, during the post-spinal case in bay 4, I cued you on the BP trend at the second cycle. In debrief that day we talked about verbalizing a differential before completing the checklist."
- "On 03/25, similar pattern on the post-lap-chole case in bay 2 — cue from me before you verbalized. Debrief notes from 03/25."
- "On 04/02, same pattern on a post-regional case. At this point the trajectory isn't closing."

## 4. The Hardest Sentence (verbatim)
"Signing you off at Week 10 with this cue-recognition gap would put patients at risk, and I'm not willing to do that."

Do not let this sentence get dropped, softened, or walked back during the conversation.
```

Notes: core message lands in the opener, every observation is anchored to a dated shift AND a debrief date (no-surprises principle), hardest sentence names the patient-safety stake without personality labels or protected-characteristic references.
