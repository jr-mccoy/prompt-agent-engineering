# PACU Orientee Reflective Journal Prompts

> Safety reminder: Reflective journaling is a personal learning tool. It is not a clinical record, not a performance review document, and not shared without your consent.

## Objective

Provide a PACU orientee with **end-of-shift, end-of-week, and end-of-month reflective journal prompts** — designed to surface real learning (including confusion, near-misses, and changes in self-perception), not toxic-positivity gratitude lists.

## Inputs

- **Orientee orientation week:** {{e.g., Wk 3}}
- **Reflection cadence:** {{end-of-shift / end-of-week / end-of-month — generate the set asked for, or all three}}
- **Any specific event the orientee wants prompts about:** {{optional — e.g., "first time I led an admission alone"}}

## Audience / Scope

- **Primary:** PACU orientee (self-use, off-shift).
- **Secondary:** Optionally shared with primary preceptor at orientee's discretion.
- **Scope:** Reflection only. Not an HR record. Not a self-evaluation (for that, see `pacu_competency_self_assessment.md`).

## Output requirements

```markdown
# Reflective Journal Prompts — Wk {n}

> These are your prompts. You decide what to do with the writing. Nothing here is for HR or your evaluation unless you choose to share it.

## End-of-shift (5–10 min)

Pick 3 of the following. Write 2–3 sentences each. Resist the urge to write only good things.

1. What's one thing I did today that I'm still thinking about — for any reason?
2. What's one moment today where I was about to do something and someone or a thought stopped me? What was that signal?
3. What did I say to a patient today that I want to say differently next time?
4. What did I miss today that someone caught? (If "nothing" is the honest answer, write "nothing" — then sit with it.)
5. What did I think I understood that turned out to be more complicated than I thought?
6. When did I cue myself today, and when did my preceptor cue me?
7. What's one thing I want to ask my preceptor about that I didn't have time for today?

## End-of-week (15 min, weekend)

Write 4–6 sentences total.

1. What's the through-line of this week? If I had to name the week in one sentence, what would it be?
2. Where did I notice myself getting more efficient or surer? Where did I notice myself slowing down or losing confidence?
3. What's one thing my preceptor said this week that I want to come back to next week?
4. What's one thing about PACU that I'm seeing differently now than I did at the start of the week?
5. If next week were a continuation of this one — same patterns — what would worry me?

## End-of-month (30 min, monthly)

Longer reflection. ~1 page.

1. Where am I now compared to a month ago — concretely, not in feelings? What can I do without cue that I couldn't do before? What can I still not do?
2. What kind of patient do I feel most prepared for? Which kind least?
3. What has changed in how I think about my role in PACU?
4. What's a piece of feedback I've gotten more than once? What's my honest read on it?
5. What's a question I had at the start of orientation that I now answer differently?
6. If I were giving a future orientee one piece of advice about this month, what would it be?
7. What am I avoiding looking at in my own practice?

## Anti-toxic-positivity reminder

Reflection isn't a gratitude journal. The most useful writing is about confusion, near-misses, and things I haven't figured out. If a prompt makes me uncomfortable, that's often the one worth writing about.

## Optional: bring to preceptor

You can share one or two journal entries with your primary preceptor if you want a particular topic discussed. You are not obligated to share. The journal is yours.

## Sources / reference

- ASPAN *Standards* — orientation reflection is part of competency development.
```

## Must / Must not

**Must:**
- Anchor in concrete observable moments, not "feelings" abstractions.
- Include prompts that surface missed events and confusion, not just positive learning.
- Explicitly disclaim toxic-positivity and "gratitude journal" framing.
- Make the journal owned by the orientee.
- Surface "what am I avoiding looking at" as an end-of-month prompt.

**Must not:**
- Treat the journal as an HR or evaluation record.
- Direct the orientee to share specific entries with the preceptor or educator.
- Project feelings ("you must be feeling …").
- Require completion ("must write every shift").
- Reference protected characteristics in prompts.
- Generate prompts that assume a specific emotional state.

## Quality signals

- An orientee writes 3 prompts in 10 minutes and has surfaced something real.
- The end-of-month prompt produces self-perception shifts, not just "skills inventory."
- The "what am I avoiding" prompt actually gets used.

## Verification

- [ ] End-of-shift prompts ≤ 10 min total.
- [ ] At least 2 prompts in each set surface missed-events or confusion.
- [ ] Anti-toxic-positivity reminder included.
- [ ] Orientee ownership explicit.
- [ ] No required sharing.
- [ ] Safety + FPP sections present.

## False-Positive Prevention

- **No invented clinical content** in prompts (no doses, no thresholds).
- **No invented evaluation criteria** that reflection writing would be graded against.
- **No invented "research shows X about journaling"** claims.
- **No invented therapeutic framing** — this is a learning tool, not therapy.
- **No protected-characteristic prompts.**
- **No license-pathway prompts.**
- **No required-frequency assertions** ("you must journal every shift to graduate orientation").

<summary>Example: Wk 3 orientee, end-of-shift + end-of-week set (click to expand)</summary>

```markdown
# Reflective Journal Prompts — Wk 3

## End-of-shift

Pick 3:
1. What's one thing I did today that I'm still thinking about?
2. What did I miss today that someone caught?
3. When did I cue myself, when did my preceptor cue me?

(The orientee writes:
1. "I led the admission for the spinal patient. Preceptor offstage. I got through it but I didn't catch that the BP was drifting on the second cycle — preceptor noticed first."
2. "Above. The drift on the second cycle.")
…

## End-of-week

1. Through-line: "I'm starting to see the workflow but I'm still alarm-driven on hemodynamics, not trend-driven."
2. "I felt sure on admission documentation. I felt slow on PONV recognition pre-symptom."
…
```

Notes: prompts surface real misses ("preceptor noticed first"), anti-toxic-positivity ("alarm-driven not trend-driven"), no required positivity.
