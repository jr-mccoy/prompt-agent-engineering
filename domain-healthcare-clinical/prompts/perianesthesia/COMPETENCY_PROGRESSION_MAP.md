# PACU RN Competency Progression Map

**The spine of the library.** Every prompt declares a `journey_stage` and one or more `competency_domains`; this map is where those two axes meet. It states, for each of the **14 ASPAN perianesthesia competency domains** across the **5 journey stages**, the **expected performance level**, the **waypoint** (what "arriving" at that cell looks like), and the **classic misconception** that stalls a learner there.

> **What this map is.** A learner-facing *orientation and self-location* tool: "where am I, where am I going, and what does the next cell demand?" It is **not** an evaluation instrument and **not** a sign-off checklist — the official sign-off and competency validation belong to the preceptor, educator, and facility (see the educator toolkit's `pacu_orientee_evaluation_meta_prompt` and `pacu_competency_self_assessment`). Use it to read your own trajectory, not to grade yourself into or out of a role.
>
> **Number-free & scope-safe.** No cell states a dose, threshold, rate, or facility protocol — those are always `per order` / `per facility protocol` / learner-pasted. Every clinical action is framed at nurse scope: *recognize, assess, prepare, support-in-scope, assist the provider, escalate by role*.

---

## The performance-level scale

The four in-orientation tokens are the same scale the educator toolkit's v2/v3 competency artifacts use, extended past sign-off into the growth tail:

| Token | Meaning | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| **Not Yet** | Has not performed it; may not yet recognize when it is needed | Pre-orientation / early Stage 1 |
| **With Direction** | Performs when told what to do and when | Early Stage 1 |
| **With Cues** | Performs correctly when prompted or reminded; not yet self-initiating | Mid–late Stage 1 |
| **Independent** | Recognizes the need and performs correctly, unaided and safely | Sign-off bar (Stage 2 → 3) |
| **Proficient** | Sees the pattern early, anticipates, adapts to the atypical | Stage 3 → 4 |
| **Expert** | Fluid, intuitive grasp; teaches it and improves the system around it | Stage 4 |

**The sign-off bar is `Independent + safe`** on the safety-critical domains — not confidence, not attendance, not "With Cues." A domain sitting at *With Cues* is honest, useful information, not a failure; it just names the next rep.

**Benner anchor per stage:** Stage 0 novice · Stage 1 advanced beginner · Stage 2 advanced beginner → competent · Stage 3 competent · Stage 4 proficient → expert.

## Master grid — expected level per cell

Columns are the five journey stages; each cell is the *expected* level a typical learner reaches **by the end of that stage** (individual pacing varies; this is a trajectory, not a deadline). Safety-critical domains are marked ⚠ — these are the ones the sign-off bar holds to `Independent + safe`.

| Competency domain | S0 Foundations | S1 Orientation | S2 Independence | S3 Independent+Cert | S4 Growth/Advanced |

**How to read a row:** a domain that is *Not Yet* at S0 and *Independent* at S2 has to travel three levels in orientation — those are the domains to spend cues on. A domain already *Independent* by S1 (thermoregulation, PONV) is a place to *bank* attention, not neglect it.

## Per-domain detail — waypoint + classic misconception, by stage

Each domain below gives, per stage: the **waypoint** (what arriving looks like) and the **misconception** (the belief or habit that most often stalls a learner in that cell). Waypoints are behavioral and number-free; misconceptions are the thing a preceptor most often has to correct.

### ⚠ Airway / Respiratory
- **S0 — With Direction.** *Waypoint:* can name why the emergence airway is fragile (residual NMB, obstruction, hypoventilation, opioid effect) from the physiology primer. *Misconception:* "an awake patient has a safe airway" — emergence obstruction and renarcotization come **after** apparent wakefulness.
- **S1 — With Cues.** *Waypoint:* recognizes stridor/obstruction/desaturation trend and initiates in-scope support (position, stimulate, oxygen per order) when cued; discriminates laryngospasm vs bronchospasm vs NPPE vs OIRD in a drill. *Misconception:* treating the number (SpO₂) instead of the pattern; waiting for a "classic" sign before acting.
- **S2 — Independent.** *Waypoint:* self-initiates recognition→support-in-scope→escalate on a real respiratory deviation, unaided; rehearses the nurse role in a provider-led airway rescue. *Misconception:* "escalation means I failed" — early escalation on a trend is the competent move.
- **S3 — Proficient.** *Waypoint:* anticipates the at-risk airway (OSA, difficult intubation history, long case) before it declares. *Misconception:* comfort breeds under-monitoring of the "easy" case.
- **S4 — Expert.** *Waypoint:* manages post-difficult-airway surveillance and re-obstruction risk; teaches airway recognition to juniors. *Misconception:* assuming a stable first hour means the risk window has closed.

### ⚠ Cardiovascular / Hemodynamic
- **S0 — With Direction.** *Waypoint:* explains the physiology behind post-op BP/HR swings and rhythm changes. *Misconception:* "high BP is always pain" — hypertension has many reversible causes (bladder, hypoxia, hypercarbia, hypothermia-shivering).
- **S1 — With Cues.** *Waypoint:* recognizes hypotension/hypertension/dysrhythmia patterns and works reversible-cause-first when prompted. *Misconception:* anchoring on the first plausible cause instead of scanning reversibles.
- **S2 — Independent.** *Waypoint:* self-initiates the hemodynamic recognition→support→escalate chain unaided. *Misconception:* treating a trend as noise until it becomes an event.
- **S3 — Proficient.** *Waypoint:* reads the trajectory early and pre-sets escalation triggers. *Misconception:* over-trusting a single reassuring vital in a deteriorating trend.
- **S4 — Expert.** *Waypoint:* reasons through shock differentiation and vasoactive-support awareness within facility scope; recovers cardiac/high-acuity cases. *Misconception:* conflating "I understand the pressor" with "I order the pressor" — the nurse supports and monitors; the provider prescribes.

### Neurologic / Emergence
- **S0 — With Direction.** *Waypoint:* describes the expected emergence arc and what "normal" looks like. *Misconception:* agitation always means pain (vs hypoxia, full bladder, emergence delirium).
- **S1 — With Cues.** *Waypoint:* distinguishes normal emergence from a deviation (delayed emergence, agitation) when cued. *Misconception:* rushing a slow-to-wake patient rather than scanning for a cause.
- **S2 — Independent.** *Waypoint:* self-initiates the normal-vs-deviation call and the in-scope response. *Misconception:* under-weighting subtle neuro change in a "comfortable" patient.
- **S3 — Proficient.** *Waypoint:* anticipates emergence risk by agent/case/population. *Misconception:* pattern-matching so fast that the atypical presentation is missed.
- **S4 — Expert.** *Waypoint:* reasons through complex emergence in high-comorbidity patients; teaches the arc. *Misconception:* assuming experience removes the need to reassess the baseline.

### Thermoregulation
- **S0 — With Direction.** *Waypoint:* names why post-op hypothermia and shivering matter. *Misconception:* "shivering is just cold" — it drives oxygen demand and can mask/mimic other events.
- **S1 — Independent.** *Waypoint:* recognizes and manages the thermoregulation domain in-scope, mostly unaided. *Misconception:* warming as comfort-only, not as a physiologic intervention.
- **S2 — Independent.** *Waypoint:* handles thermoregulation independently under load. *Misconception:* de-prioritizing temperature when other systems are busy.
- **S3 — Proficient.** *Waypoint:* anticipates at-risk patients (long case, peds, geri, large fluid volumes). *Misconception:* treating it as fully "solved" and stopping the scan.
- **S4 — Proficient → Expert.** *Waypoint:* integrates thermoregulation into complex-case reasoning; teaches it. *Misconception:* forgetting MH sits in the thermoregulation-adjacent differential.

### Pain & Comfort
- **S0 — With Direction.** *Waypoint:* names the comfort domains and how they interact. *Misconception:* pain is a single number to be zeroed, not a multi-signal assessment.
- **S1 — With Cues → Independent.** *Waypoint:* assesses and supports comfort in-scope (interventions **per order**), reassessing at a named interval. *Misconception:* stacking interventions without reassessing between them.
- **S2 — Independent.** *Waypoint:* manages comfort independently, integrating it with airway/sedation risk. *Misconception:* treating pain in isolation from respiratory risk (the OIRD link).
- **S3 — Proficient.** *Waypoint:* anticipates the hard-to-control pattern and multimodal considerations. *Misconception:* assuming yesterday's regimen fits today's patient.
- **S4 — Expert.** *Waypoint:* reasons through complex/opioid-tolerant/regional-adjunct comfort; teaches it. *Misconception:* losing the comfort-vs-safety balance in the tolerant patient.

### Nausea / PONV
- **S0 — With Direction.** *Waypoint:* names PONV risk factors and why it matters for discharge. *Misconception:* PONV is a nuisance, not a discharge- and aspiration-relevant problem.
- **S1 — Independent.** *Waypoint:* recognizes and supports PONV in-scope (agents **per order**), mostly unaided. *Misconception:* treating without scanning for a reversible driver (pain, hypotension, opioids).
- **S2 — Independent.** *Waypoint:* manages PONV independently under load. *Misconception:* under-documenting refractory PONV as a discharge barrier.
- **S3 — Proficient.** *Waypoint:* anticipates high-risk patients and pre-empts. *Misconception:* one-size-fits-all antiemetic thinking.
- **S4 — Proficient → Expert.** *Waypoint:* integrates PONV into fast-track/discharge judgment; may drive a PONV QI question. *Misconception:* stopping at the number instead of the trajectory to discharge.

### Fluid / Electrolyte / Renal
- **S0 — With Direction.** *Waypoint:* explains post-op fluid shifts and urine-output relevance at a beginner level. *Misconception:* oliguria is always hypovolemia (vs retention, obstruction).
- **S1 — With Cues.** *Waypoint:* recognizes oliguria/retention patterns and escalates when cued; reads post-op labs at nurse scope (recognize + escalate, **not diagnose**). *Misconception:* interpreting a lab as a diagnosis rather than a signal to escalate.
- **S2 — Independent.** *Waypoint:* self-initiates the fluid/renal recognition→escalate chain. *Misconception:* charting an abnormal value without acting on the trend.
- **S3 — Proficient.** *Waypoint:* anticipates at-risk renal/fluid patients. *Misconception:* over-reading a single value out of clinical context.
- **S4 — Proficient → Expert.** *Waypoint:* integrates fluid/electrolyte reasoning into complex-case management. *Misconception:* treating the number instead of the whole patient.

### Regional / Neuraxial
- **S0 — Not Yet.** *Waypoint:* knows the categories (spinal/epidural/peripheral block) and that recovery differs. *Misconception:* a working block means "less to watch."
- **S1 — With Cues.** *Waypoint:* assesses block level and motor/sensory return when cued; recognizes red flags to escalate. *Misconception:* mistaking normal regression timing for a problem, or missing a rising block.
- **S2 — Independent.** *Waypoint:* performs serial block assessment unaided and escalates red flags (rising level, hematoma signs). *Misconception:* "escalate" delayed because the sign seemed subtle.
- **S3 — Proficient.** *Waypoint:* anticipates block trajectory and complications by type. *Misconception:* assuming all blocks regress on the same clock.
- **S4 — Expert.** *Waypoint:* reasons through advanced block assessment (hematoma red flags, atypical regression); teaches it. *Misconception:* under-weighting the neurologic-emergency window for neuraxial hematoma.

### ⚠ Pharmacology / Reversal
- **S0 — With Direction.** *Waypoint:* maps agent classes (volatiles, IV, opioids, NMBs, reversal) to their recovery signatures — **no doses**. *Misconception:* "reversal means the drug is gone" — renarcotization and recurarization can follow.
- **S1 — With Cues.** *Waypoint:* rehearses the med-admin safety process in the PACU context (no dose math); links agents to expected/adverse recovery effects. *Misconception:* focusing on giving the drug over monitoring its effect and duration.
- **S2 — Independent.** *Waypoint:* self-initiates the safe med process and monitors for reversal-agent pitfalls (e.g., renarcotization watch), all **per order**. *Misconception:* trusting a reversal agent's peak effect to outlast the opioid/NMB.
- **S3 — Proficient.** *Waypoint:* anticipates pharmacologic risk by agent + patient. *Misconception:* over-generalizing one patient's response to the next.
- **S4 — Expert.** *Waypoint:* reasons through complex pharmacology (tolerance, interactions) within scope; teaches the mechanism map. *Misconception:* drifting from "I understand it" toward provider-scope decisions.

### Assessment & Scoring (Aldrete / PADSS)
- **S0 — With Direction.** *Waypoint:* explains what monitors mean and the Aldrete/PADSS categories (**thresholds per facility**). *Misconception:* the score is the goal, rather than a summary of the assessment.
- **S1 — With Cues → Independent.** *Waypoint:* scores and trends accurately when prompted; builds a recovery one-liner + problem list. *Misconception:* scoring to a number without integrating the whole picture.
- **S2 — Independent.** *Waypoint:* scores, trends, and acts on the trend unaided. *Misconception:* discharging "to the number" past a clinical red flag.
- **S3 — Proficient.** *Waypoint:* uses the score as one input to a richer judgment. *Misconception:* letting the score override clinical concern.
- **S4 — Expert.** *Waypoint:* exercises discharge judgment beyond the number (convert-to-admit triggers); teaches scoring. *Misconception:* treating a passing score as sufficient in an atypical patient.

### ⚠ Handoff & Communication
- **S0 — With Direction.** *Waypoint:* knows the OR→PACU→floor pipeline and who's who. *Misconception:* handoff is a formality, not a safety-critical information transfer.
- **S1 — With Cues.** *Waypoint:* receives the anesthesia handoff (read-back, gap-catch) and gives SBAR report when cued. *Misconception:* accepting a handoff passively instead of confirming and closing gaps.
- **S2 — Independent.** *Waypoint:* gives and receives report unaided, escalation vs routine distinguished. *Misconception:* over- or under-including detail under time pressure.
- **S3 — Proficient.** *Waypoint:* tailors communication to context and urgency reliably. *Misconception:* assuming the receiver shares your mental model.
- **S4 — Expert.** *Waypoint:* models and teaches high-quality handoff; may drive a handoff-quality QI question. *Misconception:* efficiency at the cost of the safety read-back.

### Patient / Family Education
- **S0 — Not Yet.** *Waypoint:* aware that education is part of the PACU role. *Misconception:* education is "later," a floor problem.
- **S1 — With Cues.** *Waypoint:* delivers simple recovery/discharge teaching when cued. *Misconception:* volume of information over teach-back comprehension.
- **S2 — Independent.** *Waypoint:* educates patient/family in-scope unaided (medical facts routed to provider). *Misconception:* answering questions outside nurse scope instead of routing them.
- **S3 — Proficient.** *Waypoint:* adapts teaching to literacy, anxiety, and situation. *Misconception:* one script for every family.
- **S4 — Expert.** *Waypoint:* teaches teaching (bedside micro-teach with teach-back); mentors juniors. *Misconception:* assuming clarity to the expert equals clarity to the patient.

### ⚠ Safety & Escalation
- **S0 — With Direction.** *Waypoint:* knows the nurse recognizes/assists/escalates and that escalation is by role. *Misconception:* escalation is a last resort or a sign of weakness.
- **S1 — With Cues.** *Waypoint:* runs recognize→act-in-scope→escalate-by-role when cued; states the chain. *Misconception:* watch-and-wait when the trend already warrants escalation.
- **S2 — Independent.** *Waypoint:* self-initiates escalation on real deterioration unaided, with flip-triggers named. *Misconception:* needing certainty before escalating — escalate on the trend.
- **S3 — Proficient.** *Waypoint:* calibrates escalate-now vs watch-and-reassess reliably; catches near-misses. *Misconception:* normalization of deviance ("it's usually fine").
- **S4 — Expert.** *Waypoint:* models escalation, mentors it, participates confidently in codes/RRT; may drive a safety QI. *Misconception:* experience lowering the escalation threshold's vigilance.

### Professional Role / Leadership
- **S0 — With Direction.** *Waypoint:* understands the PACU nurse's role and realistic week-1 expectations. *Misconception:* "I should already be fast" — novice normalization is protective.
- **S1 — With Cues.** *Waypoint:* runs the shift-structure flow, keeps a question log, preps debriefs (learner side). *Misconception:* importing prior-unit habits uncritically (pattern-import risk).
- **S2 — Independent.** *Waypoint:* carries the bay's professional load unaided; preps the sign-off conversation with evidence. *Misconception:* conflating confidence with demonstrated competence.
- **S3 — Independent → Proficient.** *Waypoint:* consolidates solo practice, self-reviews monthly, plans certification. *Misconception:* stalling growth after sign-off ("I'm done learning").
- **S4 — Expert.** *Waypoint:* precepts, takes charge/resource, appraises evidence, drives QI, plans professional development. *Misconception:* teaching by telling instead of by structured, blameless debrief.

## How the map is used across the library

| Use | Where |
|---|---|
| Declare each prompt's stage + domain(s) | Every prompt's frontmatter (`journey_stage`, `competency_domains`) reconciles to this map (per `BUILD_PLAN.md` §8) |
| Locate yourself and pick the next drill | `README.md` "find your stage" flow → this map → the stage directory |
| Self-assess against evidence, not confidence | `spine/pacu_self_assessment_blueprint.md` reads each cell's waypoint as the evidence bar |
| Recover a stalled domain non-punitively | `spine/pacu_growth_remediation_pathway.md` uses the misconception column to name the likely block |
| Calibrate a stage's objectives | `spine/pacu_learning_objectives_by_stage.md` maps waypoints to Bloom's-calibrated objectives |
| Cross into clinical content for a domain | `TOOLKIT_CROSSWALK.md` maps each domain/drill to the toolkit artifact that supplies the clinical facts |

> **One source of truth.** This map names *levels and behaviors*; it never states clinical numbers. When a cell references a clinical fact (an agent's recovery signature, a complication's cues), the fact lives in the educator toolkit or in facility material — see `TOOLKIT_CROSSWALK.md`. The map points; it does not restate.
