## Objective

Work up the dyspneic patient: triage acute respiratory failure, separate cardiac from pulmonary from non-cardiopulmonary causes, order targeted testing, and dispose.

## Inputs

- Tempo: minutes/hours/days/weeks
- Quality: at rest, exertional, orthopnea, PND, positional (platypnea, trepopnea)
- Associated: chest pain, cough (productive vs dry), hemoptysis, fever, wheezing, leg swelling
- Vitals: RR, SpO2, work of breathing (accessory muscles, retractions, paradoxical abdominal movement), HR, BP, temp
- ABG/VBG with lactate
- Risk factors: smoking, occupational/inhalation exposures, recent surgery/immobility/malignancy, anticoagulant use, immunocompromise, heart failure history, asthma/COPD history, recent illness
- Exam: JVD, S3, peripheral edema, lung exam (crackles, wheeze, rhonchi, decreased breath sounds, dullness), leg swelling/asymmetry
- ECG, CXR, POCUS (B-lines, pleural effusion, cardiac function, IVC)

## Reasoning Steps

1. **Stabilize first.** RR >30, SpO2 <90 on room air, accessory muscle use, mental status change, paradoxical breathing → resuscitate concurrently with workup.
   - Supplemental O2 to SpO2 92–96 (88–92 in COPD). Escalate: NC → simple mask → NRB → HFNC → NIPPV → intubation.
   - NIPPV (BiPAP) for COPD exacerbation, cardiogenic pulmonary edema, immunosuppressed pneumonia. Avoid if vomiting, altered mental status (relative), severe agitation, facial trauma.
   - Intubate for fatigue, severe acidosis, refractory hypoxemia, AMS unable to protect airway, hemodynamic collapse.

2. **POCUS first-pass differential.**
   - **Diffuse B-lines** (≥3 per intercostal space bilaterally) → pulmonary edema (cardiogenic or non-cardiogenic).
   - **Focal B-lines / consolidation** → pneumonia.
   - **Absent lung sliding + lung point** → pneumothorax.
   - **Pleural effusion** with dependent lung consolidation → effusion ± atelectasis.
   - **RV dilation, McConnell sign** (RV free wall hypokinesis with apical sparing), septal D-shape → consider PE.
   - **Reduced LV function, dilated chambers, mitral regurgitation** → HFrEF.
   - **Plethoric IVC** without respiratory variation → volume overload, RV strain, tamponade.

3. **CXR pattern.**
   - Cardiomegaly, cephalization, Kerley B lines, perihilar haziness, pleural effusions → cardiogenic pulmonary edema.
   - Lobar consolidation → bacterial pneumonia.
   - Diffuse bilateral patchy infiltrates without cardiomegaly → ARDS (PaO2/FiO2 ≤300 on PEEP ≥5), PJP, viral pneumonia.
   - Hyperinflation, flattened diaphragms → COPD/asthma.
   - Pneumothorax: visceral pleural line, absent lung markings.
   - Mediastinal widening → consider dissection, malignancy.
   - Often normal in PE.

4. **Cardiac ischemia/heart failure workup.**
   - **BNP/NT-proBNP:** BNP <100 pg/mL or NT-proBNP age-adjusted (<450 if <50, <900 if 50–75, <1800 if >75) makes acute heart failure unlikely; high values confirm. Obesity lowers BNP; renal failure and afib raise it.
   - Echocardiogram for EF, valvular function, RV strain, pericardial effusion.
   - ECG: ischemic changes, RV strain pattern, AFib with RVR (precipitant of HF), low voltage with electrical alternans (effusion/tamponade).
   - Troponin if any concern for ACS-driven HF.

5. **PE workup if suspected.**
   - Wells / PERC / age-adjusted d-dimer / YEARS algorithm (see chest pain prompt).
   - CTPA if d-dimer positive or clinical pretest high. V/Q scan if pregnancy or contrast contraindicated.
   - Massive PE (hemodynamic compromise) → systemic thrombolysis (alteplase 100 mg IV over 2 h) or catheter-directed therapy. Submassive (RV strain, troponin elevation, normotensive) → consider catheter-directed thrombolysis or anticoagulation alone with close monitoring.

6. **COPD vs asthma exacerbation.**
   - Wheeze, tripod, accessory muscles, prolonged expiratory phase. Peak flow if asthma. ABG for hypercapnia.
   - Asthma: SABA (albuterol nebulized 5 mg q20 min × 3 or continuous), ipratropium 0.5 mg q20 min × 3, systemic corticosteroids (prednisone 40–60 mg PO or methylprednisolone 60–125 mg IV), magnesium 2 g IV for severe. Heliox, IV epinephrine for refractory.
   - COPD: SABA + ipratropium, systemic steroids (prednisone 40 mg × 5 days), antibiotics if increased sputum purulence/volume + dyspnea (Anthonisen criteria) — typical: doxycycline, azithromycin, or amox-clav. NIPPV for hypercapnic respiratory failure.

7. **Pneumonia.**
   - CURB-65, PSI for severity. CRP, procalcitonin can support.
   - Empiric CAP: outpatient — amoxicillin 1 g TID + macrolide (or doxycycline if macrolide not appropriate), or respiratory fluoroquinolone monotherapy (levofloxacin 750 mg daily). Inpatient non-ICU: ceftriaxone 1–2 g IV daily + azithromycin 500 mg IV daily. ICU: ceftriaxone + azithromycin, or beta-lactam + respiratory fluoroquinolone. Add MRSA coverage (vancomycin or linezolid) and Pseudomonas coverage (piperacillin-tazobactam or cefepime) if severe pneumonia, recent hospitalization, or risk factors.
   - HCAP/HAP/VAP: broaden empirically per local antibiogram.
   - Atypicals: Legionella urinary antigen, Mycoplasma PCR.
   - Viral: respiratory pathogen panel (influenza, RSV, SARS-CoV-2). Oseltamivir 75 mg BID × 5 days if influenza confirmed or high suspicion.

8. **Pneumothorax / hemothorax / pleural effusion.**
   - Tension pneumothorax: clinical diagnosis → needle decompression (5th ICS midaxillary now preferred for adults; 2nd ICS midclavicular alternative) → tube thoracostomy.
   - Simple pneumothorax: small primary may resolve with O2 and observation; large or symptomatic → chest tube or pigtail.
   - Pleural effusion: thoracentesis for new effusion, especially if febrile or symptomatic. Light criteria differentiate exudate from transudate. Cell count, gram stain/culture, glucose, LDH, pH, cytology, ADA if TB suspected.

9. **Other causes.**
   - **Anemia** → Hb < ~8 with cardiac comorbidity may cause exertional dyspnea. CBC, retic, iron studies, B12/folate.
   - **Metabolic acidosis** (DKA, sepsis, salicylate toxicity, uremia) → Kussmaul respiration. ABG, anion gap, lactate.
   - **Pulmonary hypertension** → exertional dyspnea, RV strain on ECG/echo, prominent P2. Right heart catheterization for diagnosis.
   - **Interstitial lung disease** → chronic dry cough, fine end-inspiratory crackles, clubbing, restrictive PFTs, HRCT pattern (UIP, NSIP).
   - **Neuromuscular weakness** (Guillain-Barré, MG crisis, ALS): paradoxical breathing, NIF (>−30 cmH2O concerning), VC <20 mL/kg or <1 L → intubate.
   - **Anxiety/hyperventilation:** diagnosis of exclusion. Respiratory alkalosis without metabolic acidosis. Treat underlying anxiety.
   - **Upper airway obstruction:** stridor, drooling, voice change, neck swelling — angioedema, anaphylaxis, foreign body, epiglottitis. Anaphylaxis → IM epinephrine 0.3–0.5 mg q5–15 min, fluids, H1/H2 blockers, steroids.

10. **Disposition.**
    - Respiratory failure or hemodynamic instability → ICU.
    - Stable hypoxia requiring O2, IV diuresis, IV antibiotics → ward.
    - Resolved symptoms with treatable outpatient cause → discharge with close follow-up.
    - Always document oxygen requirement at discharge and ambulation SpO2 if applicable.

```
ACUITY:

- Stabilization actions (O2, NIPPV, intubation)

- Cardiac: EF, RV, IVC, pericardial fluid

DIFFERENTIAL (ranked):
```

## Worked Example

**Input:** 78 y/o M with HFrEF (EF 30%), CAD s/p CABG, AFib on warfarin, presents with 3 days of progressive dyspnea, orthopnea (3 pillows), bilateral leg swelling. RR 28, SpO2 87% on RA, 95% on 4L NC. BP 148/92, HR 102 AFib. Bilateral crackles to mid-lung. JVD 12 cm. 2+ pitting edema. CXR: cardiomegaly, cephalization, bilateral pleural effusions, no consolidation. ECG: AFib, no acute ischemia. BNP 2400 pg/mL. Troponin negative. Cr 1.6 (baseline 1.2). K 4.2.

**Output:**

```
ACUITY:
- RR 28, SpO2 87% RA → 95% on 4L NC. Moderate respiratory distress. No accessory muscle use described, but borderline; reassess after diuresis.
- Stabilization: O2 4L NC titrate to SpO2 92–96. If worsening or fatigue, NIPPV (BiPAP 10/5 starting, titrate). Avoid intubation if achievable with NIPPV.

POCUS / CXR FINDINGS:
- Lung: bilateral crackles, B-lines expected on POCUS, bilateral pleural effusions on CXR, cardiomegaly, cephalization → cardiogenic pulmonary edema.
- Cardiac: known EF 30%; assess for new wall motion abnormality, valvular change, RV strain. Plethoric IVC expected.

DIFFERENTIAL (ranked):
1. Acute decompensated HFrEF — orthopnea, JVD, S3 likely, BNP 2400, classic CXR, known EF 30%.
2. Concomitant AFib with RVR contributing — HR 102 controlled enough but rate impacts forward flow.
3. Worsening renal function from cardiorenal syndrome (Cr 1.6 from 1.2).

KEY TESTS:
- BNP 2400 confirms HF.
- Troponin negative — no concomitant ischemia.
- Echo to reassess EF, valves, RV, look for new regional wall motion abnormality.
- BMP, magnesium, CBC, coags (INR on warfarin), TSH (AFib precipitants).
- ABG if worsening — currently SpO2 acceptable on NC.
- D-dimer / CTPA not indicated unless clinical course atypical.

WORKING DIAGNOSIS / TREATMENT:
- Acute decompensated heart failure with congestion, AFib with RVR contribution, cardiorenal syndrome.
- Furosemide IV: 2.5× home oral dose IV; if naive, 40–80 mg IV bolus then drip 5–20 mg/h or repeat boluses. Monitor urine output (target 1 L/h initially, then ≥3 L net negative/day). Per DOSE trial, high-dose IV bolus and continuous infusion equivalent.
- Add metolazone 5–10 mg PO 30 min before furosemide if diuretic resistance; replete K and Mg aggressively (target K 4–5, Mg >2).
- Hold home BB if frank decompensation or hypotension; otherwise continue at reduced dose (do not stop abruptly per OPTIMIZE-HF).
- ACE/ARB/ARNI: continue or restart once euvolemic and renal function stable; sacubitril-valsartan preferred per PARADIGM-HF if tolerated.
- SGLT2i (empagliflozin or dapagliflozin) if not on already — DAPA-HF / EMPEROR-Reduced; can start in hospital.
- MRA (spironolactone or eplerenone) if K and Cr permit.
- AFib rate control: continue or up-titrate metoprolol succinate or digoxin add-on if HR remains uncontrolled despite euvolemia. Avoid IV diltiazem given EF 30%.
- Anticoagulation: continue warfarin, verify INR therapeutic.
- Daily weights, strict I/O, telemetry, daily BMP, magnesium.

DISPOSITION:
- Admit to step-down/telemetry. ICU if NIPPV required or hemodynamic instability.
- Ambulation SpO2 prior to discharge.
- Discharge criteria: euvolemic, stable on oral diuretics, GDMT optimized, ambulation SpO2 acceptable.
- Follow-up: HF clinic within 7 days; cardiology within 2 weeks. Cardiac rehab referral. Patient education on daily weights, low-sodium diet, fluid restriction, when to call.
```
