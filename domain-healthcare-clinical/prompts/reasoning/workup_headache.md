## Objective

Work up the patient presenting with headache: identify red-flag (SNNOOP10) features warranting emergent imaging and CSF analysis, recognize primary headache patterns, and provide acute and preventive treatment.

## Inputs

- Onset (sudden/thunderclap vs gradual), peak time, duration, frequency, prior similar
- Quality (throbbing, pressing, stabbing), location, severity (NRS), aggravating (Valsalva, position, exertion, food, sleep), relieving
- Associated: aura, photophobia, phonophobia, nausea/vomiting, neck stiffness, fever, vision changes, jaw claudication, weakness, paresthesia, seizure, AMS, focal neuro deficit
- Risk: age, pregnancy/postpartum, immunosuppression, cancer history, anticoagulants, hypercoagulable state, recent trauma, recent LP/epidural, recent SARS-CoV-2 vaccine, IV drug use, family history of aneurysm, hormonal contraceptive
- Vitals (HTN, fever), neuro exam (CN, motor, sensory, cerebellar, gait, fundoscopy), neck stiffness, temporal artery exam, sinus exam
- Labs: CBC, BMP, ESR/CRP if GCA suspected, coags, beta-hCG if reproductive age
- Imaging: non-contrast CT head (or CTA/CTV), MRI with/without contrast and venogram if indicated; LP with opening pressure if SAH or meningitis suspected

## Reasoning Steps

1. **Apply SNNOOP10 red-flag screen.**
   - **S**ystemic symptoms (fever, weight loss)
   - **N**eoplasm history
   - **N**eurologic deficit (focal, AMS, seizure)
   - **O**nset sudden/thunderclap (≤1 minute to peak)
   - **O**lder age (>50, especially >65 — GCA, mass)
   - **P**attern change (progressive, different from prior)
   - **P**ositional headache (CSF leak, ICP)
   - **P**recipitated by Valsalva (Chiari, posterior fossa lesion)
   - **P**apilledema
   - **P**rogressive
   - **P**regnancy/postpartum (preeclampsia, CVT, RCVS, PRES, pituitary apoplexy)
   - **P**ainful eye with autonomic features (cluster, dissection)
   - **P**ost-traumatic
   - **P**athology of immune system (HIV, immunosuppressed)
   - **P**ainkiller overuse (medication-overuse headache)
   - Any positive feature → secondary cause workup.

2. **Thunderclap headache → rule out subarachnoid hemorrhage.**
   - Non-contrast CT head within 6 hours of onset has ~98–100% sensitivity for SAH (Perry rules; Ottawa SAH rule).
   - **Ottawa SAH rule** (alert ≥15 y/o, new severe non-traumatic headache peaking within 1 hour): any of age ≥40, neck pain/stiffness, witnessed LOC, onset during exertion, thunderclap (peak instantly), limited neck flexion → further workup (CT, then LP if CT negative and presentation >6 h or atypical).
   - LP if CT negative and presentation >6 h: xanthochromia (yellow CSF supernatant), elevated RBC count not clearing tube 1 to tube 4. Opening pressure.
   - CT angiogram if CT positive or LP positive — identify aneurysm, AVM, vasoconstriction (RCVS).
   - Differential of thunderclap: SAH, sentinel bleed, RCVS, cerebral venous thrombosis, pituitary apoplexy, cervical artery dissection, hypertensive emergency, ischemic stroke, intracranial hypotension (CSF leak).

3. **Suspected meningitis/encephalitis.**
   - Fever + headache + neck stiffness ± AMS ± photophobia. Brudzinski/Kernig insensitive.
   - **Treat empirically before LP** if delay anticipated. Adults <50 or 50+ with comorbidities: ceftriaxone 2 g IV q12h + vancomycin 15–20 mg/kg IV q8–12h (target trough 15–20 or AUC 400–600) + dexamethasone 0.15 mg/kg IV q6h × 4 days (give before/with first antibiotic dose). Add ampicillin 2 g IV q4h for Listeria coverage in age >50, immunocompromised, alcoholism.
   - Add acyclovir 10 mg/kg IV q8h if HSV encephalitis suspected (temporal lobe focality, AMS, seizure).
   - CT before LP if focal deficit, AMS, immunocompromised, papilledema, seizure, age >60. Otherwise LP can be done first.
   - LP: opening pressure, cell count with diff, glucose (CSF/serum ratio), protein, gram stain, culture, bacterial PCR (FilmArray meningitis panel), viral PCR (HSV, VZV, enterovirus), cryptococcal antigen if immunocompromised.
   - Bacterial pattern: PMN-predominant pleocytosis (>1000 typical), low glucose (<40, ratio <0.4), elevated protein (>200 typical).
   - Viral: lymphocyte-predominant, normal glucose, mildly elevated protein.

4. **Giant cell arteritis (age >50 with new headache).**
   - Temporal headache, scalp tenderness, jaw claudication, vision changes (transient or permanent), polymyalgia symptoms, fever.
   - ESR >50, CRP elevated. Temporal artery biopsy gold standard. Temporal artery US showing halo sign supportive.
   - **Start prednisone immediately if suspicion is high** (do not wait for biopsy; biopsy yield preserved 1–2 weeks): prednisone 40–60 mg PO daily; if vision threatened/lost, methylprednisolone 1 g IV daily × 3 days then transition to oral. Add tocilizumab 162 mg SC weekly per GiACTA for steroid-sparing.
   - Aspirin 81 mg daily. PPI for steroid-related GI risk. Bone health (calcium, vit D, bisphosphonate).

5. **Cerebral venous sinus thrombosis (CVT).**
   - Headache (95%), often progressive over days. Risk: pregnancy/postpartum, OCP, malignancy, dehydration, thrombophilia, head/neck infection, recent LP.
   - May have papilledema, seizure, focal deficit, AMS.
   - CT may show empty delta sign in superior sagittal sinus; MRV or CTV is diagnostic.
   - Anticoagulate (heparin then warfarin or DOAC) even with hemorrhagic transformation. Treat underlying cause.

6. **Hypertensive emergency / PRES / RCVS.**
   - Severe HTN with end-organ damage (encephalopathy, headache, vision changes). MRI shows posterior reversible encephalopathy syndrome (vasogenic edema parieto-occipital).
   - RCVS: thunderclap headache, often recurrent, postpartum or with serotonergic drugs/cocaine. CTA shows segmental string-of-beads vasoconstriction.
   - BP control: nicardipine or clevidipine titrated infusions; avoid sudden drops.

7. **Mass lesion / increased ICP.**
   - Progressive headache, worse in morning or with Valsalva, persistent vomiting, papilledema, focal deficit, seizure, AMS.
   - MRI brain with and without contrast (CT screen if MRI not feasible). MRV if venous cause suspected.
   - Idiopathic intracranial hypertension (IIH): young obese woman, pulsatile tinnitus, transient visual obscurations. LP opening pressure >25 cmH2O after MRI/MRV exclude mass and venous thrombosis. Acetazolamide 250 mg BID titrate to 1 g BID; weight loss; serial LPs or VP shunt or optic nerve fenestration if vision threatened.

8. **Primary headache patterns (after secondary excluded).**
   - **Migraine without aura (ICHD-3):** ≥5 attacks, lasting 4–72 h, with ≥2 of (unilateral, pulsating, moderate-severe, aggravated by activity) and ≥1 of (nausea/vomiting, photophobia + phonophobia).
   - **Migraine with aura:** transient focal neurologic symptoms (visual scotoma/scintillation most common, or sensory, language, motor) developing over 5–60 min before/with headache.
   - **Tension-type:** bilateral, pressing/tightening, mild-moderate, no nausea, photophobia or phonophobia (one allowed).
   - **Cluster:** unilateral severe orbital/temporal, 15–180 min, with autonomic features (lacrimation, conjunctival injection, ptosis, nasal congestion, miosis), occurring in clusters 1–8/day for weeks then remission. M:F 4:1.
   - **Trigeminal autonomic cephalalgias** (cluster, paroxysmal hemicrania, SUNCT/SUNA, hemicrania continua) — distinguish by attack duration and frequency.
   - **Medication-overuse headache:** chronic daily headache in patient using analgesics ≥10 days/month (triptans, opioids, combination) or simple analgesics ≥15 days/month.

9. **Acute treatment of primary headache.**
   - **Migraine:** sumatriptan 6 mg SC, 50–100 mg PO, 5–20 mg intranasal; ketorolac 30 mg IV; metoclopramide 10 mg IV (with diphenhydramine 25 mg IV to prevent akathisia/dystonia); IV fluids; dexamethasone 4–10 mg IV to reduce recurrence. CGRP receptor antagonists (rimegepant, ubrogepant) and ditans (lasmiditan) for triptan-contraindicated patients.
   - **Cluster:** 100% O2 12–15 L/min via NRB × 15–20 min; sumatriptan 6 mg SC; verapamil 240–960 mg/day for prophylaxis; prednisone bridge or galcanezumab.
   - **Tension-type:** NSAIDs, acetaminophen.
   - Avoid opioids and butalbital — promote MOH.

10. **Preventive therapy for migraine.**
    - First-line orals: propranolol 80–240 mg/day, topiramate 50–100 mg/day (avoid in pregnancy), amitriptyline 25–100 mg HS, candesartan 16 mg daily, valproate (avoid in pregnancy/childbearing potential without contraception).
    - CGRP monoclonal antibodies (erenumab 70/140 mg SC monthly, fremanezumab, galcanezumab, eptinezumab IV) for ≥4 migraine days/month with disability or failed orals.
    - OnabotulinumtoxinA 155 units q12 wks for chronic migraine (≥15 days/month with ≥8 migrainous).
    - Lifestyle: sleep regularity, hydration, regular meals, identifying triggers, exercise, magnesium 400 mg, riboflavin 400 mg, CoQ10 100 mg TID.

## Output Format

```
RED FLAG SCREEN (SNNOOP10):
- [positive features and implication, or none]

ACUITY / STABILIZATION:
- Vital signs, neuro exam, immediate actions

DIFFERENTIAL (ranked):
1. [most likely with supporting features]
2. [second]
3. [third — include can't-miss diagnoses]

WORKUP:
- Imaging: [CT / CTA / MRI / MRV with rationale]
- Labs: [ESR/CRP, BMP, beta-hCG, etc.]
- LP: [indication, what to send]

WORKING DIAGNOSIS / TREATMENT:
- Acute: [drugs, doses, route]
- Empiric coverage if infection considered
- Specialty consult (neurology, neurosurgery, IR)

PREVENTIVE / FOLLOW-UP:
- Headache diary
- Trigger avoidance
- Preventive medication if indicated
- Return precautions

DISPOSITION:
- Admit / observe / discharge
```

## Worked Example

**Input:** 38 y/o F, postpartum day 8 (uncomplicated vaginal delivery), presents with worst headache of life starting suddenly while lifting baby 3 hours ago, peak in <1 minute, holocephalic, with one episode of vomiting and mild neck stiffness. BP 152/96, HR 88, T 37.0. Neuro exam non-focal. No papilledema. No prior similar headaches. On no medications. Non-contrast CT head: no acute hemorrhage.

**Output:**

```
RED FLAG SCREEN (SNNOOP10):
- Sudden / thunderclap onset (peak <1 min)
- Pregnancy/postpartum
- Pattern change (no prior similar)
- Vomiting
- Multiple red flags → secondary headache workup mandatory.

ACUITY / STABILIZATION:
- Stable. Mild HTN. IV access. Pain control without sedation that would obscure exam.

DIFFERENTIAL (ranked):
1. Subarachnoid hemorrhage — thunderclap, vomiting, neck stiffness; CT negative but at 3 h post-onset CT is highly sensitive — still need LP if any uncertainty, or CTA.
2. Cerebral venous sinus thrombosis — postpartum hypercoagulable state, classic risk window. CT often misses; needs CTV or MRV.
3. Reversible cerebral vasoconstriction syndrome (RCVS) — postpartum, thunderclap, often recurrent. CTA shows segmental vasoconstriction.
4. Postpartum preeclampsia / PRES — BP 152/96 elevated; check for proteinuria, transaminitis, low platelets; consider MRI for PRES.
5. Pituitary apoplexy — sudden headache postpartum (Sheehan in obstetric hemorrhage); MRI pituitary if visual changes or hormonal abnormality.
6. Cervical artery dissection — neck pain or trauma history.

WORKUP:
- Imaging: CT done — negative. Order CTA head and neck + CTV in same study to evaluate aneurysm, RCVS, dissection, and venous thrombosis simultaneously. MRI brain with and without contrast + MRV if CTA non-diagnostic or if PRES/pituitary suspected.
- Labs: CBC, BMP, LFTs, urine protein/Cr ratio (postpartum preeclampsia screen), coags, magnesium, CRP, beta-hCG (recent delivery). Consider thrombophilia panel if CVT confirmed.
- LP: if CTA/CTV/MRV negative, perform LP for opening pressure, xanthochromia, RBC tube 1 vs tube 4. CTA can replace LP if it shows a clear positive finding (aneurysm, vasoconstriction).

WORKING DIAGNOSIS / TREATMENT:
- Working: thunderclap headache with broad differential, prioritizing SAH, CVT, RCVS, postpartum preeclampsia.
- Empiric BP control if MAP rising — labetalol 10–20 mg IV (preeclampsia-friendly) or hydralazine 5–10 mg IV. Magnesium sulfate 4–6 g IV load + 1–2 g/h infusion if preeclampsia confirmed.
- Analgesia: acetaminophen 1 g IV/PO. Avoid NSAIDs until SAH excluded and bleeding risk clarified. Avoid opioids if possible.
- Antiemetic: ondansetron 4 mg IV.
- Neurology consult.
- If SAH confirmed: neurosurgery, nimodipine 60 mg PO q4h × 21 days, BP control, ICU.
- If CVT confirmed: heparin or LMWH, then warfarin or DOAC; treat for 3–6 months; thrombophilia evaluation; OB-GYN coordination.
- If RCVS: nimodipine 60 mg PO q4h, BP control, avoid serotonergic agents/triggers; expect resolution over weeks.
- If postpartum preeclampsia/PRES: BP control, magnesium, OB-GYN involvement.

PREVENTIVE / FOLLOW-UP:
- Document warning bleed concern with neurosurgery if dimensional features suggest aneurysm.
- Headache diary, blood pressure log if hypertensive disorder.
- Future contraception counseling — avoid combined OCPs if CVT or hypercoagulable.

DISPOSITION:
- Admit to neuro service or ICU pending diagnosis. Even if all imaging and LP negative, admit for observation and serial neuro exams given high pretest for serious secondary cause.
```
