## Objective

Work up altered mental status (AMS) in the adult: stabilize airway/circulation, screen for reversible causes (AEIOU TIPS), localize structural vs metabolic vs toxic vs psychiatric, and dispose.

## Inputs

- Tempo (minutes vs hours vs days), waxing/waning vs progressive
- Baseline cognition (collateral history mandatory)
- Prior episodes, dementia, psychiatric history, recent hospitalization, recent procedures
- Medications (anticholinergics, opioids, benzos, sedatives, antiepileptics, lithium, insulin, antihypertensives, dopaminergic, serotonergic, antibiotics with neurotoxicity)
- Substance use (alcohol, opioids, sedatives, stimulants, hallucinogens, GHB, synthetic cannabinoids), withdrawal timing
- Comorbidities: diabetes, CKD, ESLD, malignancy, immunocompromise, recent infection, seizure disorder, dementia, psychiatric
- Vitals (T, BP, HR, RR, SpO2, glucose), GCS, focal deficit, pupils, asterixis, hyperreflexia, clonus, rigidity, neck stiffness, tongue bite, signs of trauma
- Labs (CBC, BMP, Mg, Ca, phosphorus, LFTs, TSH, B12, ammonia, lactate, blood gas, troponin, CK), urinalysis, urine drug screen, blood/urine cultures, lumbar puncture as indicated
- Imaging: non-contrast CT head; MRI if focal deficit unexplained; EEG if NCSE suspected

## Reasoning Steps

1. **Stabilize.**
   - ABC: airway protection if GCS ≤8 or unable to protect; supplemental O2 to SpO2 92–96.
   - **DONT** mnemonic for the obtunded patient: **D**extrose 25 g IV (after thiamine), **O**xygen, **N**aloxone 0.04–0.4 mg IV titrate, **T**hiamine 100–500 mg IV before glucose in chronic alcohol use or malnutrition.
   - Fingerstick glucose immediately; treat hypoglycemia <70 with D50 25 g IV (or D10 100 mL IV).
   - Treat opioid overdose: naloxone 0.04–0.4 mg IV q2 min titrated to respiratory rate; higher doses (2 mg) in arrest. Recurrent dosing or infusion for long-acting opioids (methadone, ER formulations, fentanyl analogues).
   - Benzodiazepine reversal (flumazenil) generally avoided due to seizure risk in chronic users / unknown coingestants.

2. **AEIOU TIPS systematic differential.**
   - **A**lcohol (intox, withdrawal, Wernicke), **A**bscess
   - **E**lectrolytes (Na, Ca, Mg, glucose), **E**ncephalopathy (hepatic, uremic, hypertensive, PRES)
   - **I**nsulin / hypoglycemia / hyperglycemia (DKA, HHS)
   - **O**xygen (hypoxia), **O**piates (and other drugs), **O**verdose
   - **U**remia
   - **T**rauma (head, subdural especially in elderly/anticoag), **T**emperature (hyper/hypothermia), **T**umor, **T**hyroid (myxedema, thyroid storm)
   - **I**nfection (sepsis, meningitis/encephalitis, UTI in elderly, pneumonia)
   - **P**sychiatric (after exclusion), **P**orphyria, **P**oisoning
   - **S**troke / **S**eizure (postictal, NCSE) / **S**yncope-related / **S**hock

3. **Distinguish delirium from dementia from primary psychiatric.**
   - Delirium: acute onset, fluctuating, inattention, altered consciousness, disorganized thinking. CAM-positive: (1) acute onset/fluctuation + (2) inattention + (3) disorganized thinking OR (4) altered level of consciousness. Hyperactive (agitation), hypoactive (lethargy, often missed), or mixed.
   - Dementia: chronic, progressive over months-years, level of consciousness preserved early.
   - Primary psychiatric: history of psych disorder, intact orientation often, response patterns; diagnosis of exclusion in AMS workup.

4. **Targeted labs for every undifferentiated AMS.**
   - CBC, BMP including glucose, Mg, Ca, phosphorus, LFTs (ammonia if cirrhosis), TSH, troponin if cardiac concern, lactate, ABG/VBG (CO2 narcosis, acidosis), CK (rhabdo), UA + urine culture, blood cultures × 2, COVID/respiratory pathogen panel, urine drug screen, ETOH level, salicylate and acetaminophen levels (if intentional ingestion), beta-hCG in reproductive-age women.
   - Vitamin B12, folate if subacute presentation.
   - HIV testing, RPR if risk factors and chronic AMS.

5. **Imaging.**
   - Non-contrast CT head: any focal deficit, head trauma, anticoag use, immunocompromised, age ≥60 with new AMS, suspicion of mass/hemorrhage, before LP if focal/papilledema/AMS/seizure/immunocompromised.
   - MRI brain: if CT non-diagnostic and clinical concern persists; better for stroke (especially posterior circulation), encephalitis, tumor, PRES, autoimmune encephalitis.
   - MRA/MRV/CTV: if vascular cause suspected (stroke, dissection, CVT).

6. **LP indications.**
   - Suspected meningitis/encephalitis (fever, headache, neck stiffness, photophobia, focal deficit unexplained).
   - Unexplained AMS in immunocompromised.
   - First seizure in adult with AMS persisting beyond expected postictal period.
   - Subacute AMS with normal imaging and negative metabolic workup.
   - Send: opening pressure, cell count + diff, glucose, protein, gram stain + culture, viral PCR (HSV, VZV, enterovirus), bacterial PCR panel, autoimmune encephalitis panel (NMDA, LGI1, CASPR2, GAD65) if subacute, cryptococcal antigen if immunocompromised, cytology and flow if malignancy concern.

7. **EEG indications.**
   - Persistent AMS after presumed seizure (rule out non-convulsive status epilepticus, present in up to 20% of unexplained AMS in critically ill).
   - Subtle motor signs (eye deviation, twitching, automatisms) without overt convulsion.
   - Refractory altered mentation in ICU.
   - Continuous EEG (cEEG) if 24-h surveillance needed.

8. **Specific syndromes.**
   - **Hepatic encephalopathy:** asterixis, fetor, known cirrhosis, ammonia elevated (not always). Treat with lactulose 30–45 mL PO/NG q1–2 h titrated to 3–4 BMs/day, rifaximin 550 mg BID; identify precipitant (infection, GI bleed, electrolytes, constipation, sedatives).
   - **Uremic encephalopathy:** known CKD/AKI, asterixis, myoclonus; dialysis is the treatment.
   - **Wernicke encephalopathy:** triad ophthalmoplegia, ataxia, confusion (only ~10% have full triad). Thiamine 500 mg IV TID × 2–3 days then 250 mg daily × 5 days, then oral. Always before glucose.
   - **Serotonin syndrome:** mental status change + autonomic instability + neuromuscular hyperactivity (clonus prominent, lower-extremity, tremor, hyperreflexia). Recent serotonergic agent. Treat: discontinue offender, supportive (IV fluids, cooling), benzos for agitation, cyproheptadine 12 mg PO load then 2 mg q2h up to 32 mg/day for severe.
   - **Neuroleptic malignant syndrome:** mental status change + lead-pipe rigidity + hyperthermia + autonomic instability + elevated CK. Neuroleptic exposure or dopaminergic withdrawal. Discontinue offender; supportive, benzos, dantrolene 1–2.5 mg/kg IV q6h, bromocriptine 2.5 mg PO TID.
   - **Anticholinergic toxidrome:** "mad as a hatter, hot as a hare, dry as a bone, red as a beet, blind as a bat." Physostigmine 1–2 mg IV slowly for severe (use cautiously, contraindicated with TCA, wide QRS).
   - **Sympathomimetic toxidrome** (cocaine, amphetamines): agitation, tachycardia, hypertension, mydriasis, diaphoresis. Benzos, cooling, IV fluids; avoid beta-blockers in cocaine for cardiac symptoms (unopposed alpha).
   - **Alcohol withdrawal:** tremor, tachycardia, hypertension, hallucinations, seizure (12–48 h), DTs (48–96 h). CIWA-Ar protocol with diazepam 10–20 mg PO/IV or lorazepam 2–4 mg q15 min, phenobarbital alternative. Thiamine, folate, multivitamin.
   - **Diabetic ketoacidosis / HHS** with AMS: per acute care prompts.
   - **Sepsis-associated encephalopathy:** sepsis with AMS often diagnosis of exclusion; treat infection.

9. **Postictal vs status.**
   - Postictal state typically resolves within 30 minutes to several hours. Persistent AMS beyond this → consider non-convulsive status, ongoing seizure, structural lesion, encephalitis. Get EEG.

10. **Disposition.**
    - Severe AMS, airway compromise, ongoing seizure, severe metabolic derangement, hypoperfusion → ICU.
    - Stable AMS with identified treatable cause → ward.
    - Resolved AMS with clear etiology and outpatient follow-up plan → discharge with caregiver and warning signs.
    - Document baseline mental status comparison and decision-maker involvement.

## Output Format

```
ACUITY / STABILIZATION:
- ABC, glucose, naloxone/thiamine if indicated
- Airway plan if GCS ≤8

HISTORY / COLLATERAL:
- Tempo, baseline cognition, meds, substances

EXAM:
- Vitals (T, BP, HR, RR, glucose), GCS, focal neuro
- Asterixis, rigidity, clonus, pupils, signs of trauma

DIFFERENTIAL VIA AEIOU TIPS:
- Top causes ranked with supporting features

WORKUP:
- Labs (panel listed)
- Imaging (CT/MRI rationale)
- LP (indication, what to send)
- EEG (indication)

WORKING DIAGNOSIS / TREATMENT:
- Etiology-specific therapy with drug, dose, route
- Empiric coverage if appropriate (e.g., meningitis dose stack)

DISPOSITION:
- ICU / step-down / ward / discharge
- Reorientation, sleep-wake cycle, sitter, fall precautions
```

## Worked Example

**Input:** 78 y/o M with mild dementia (baseline MoCA 22 last year), HTN, BPH, presents from nursing home with 2 days of worsening confusion and agitation. Caregiver reports decreased oral intake and dysuria. T 38.2, BP 102/64, HR 108, RR 22, SpO2 94% RA, glucose 142. Disoriented to time and place, picking at sheets, inattentive (cannot list months backward). No focal deficit. Mild suprapubic tenderness. No nuchal rigidity. Recent course of trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole 5 days ago for UTI, finished. Cr 1.6 (baseline 1.0). UA: large LE, positive nitrites, WBC >100. Lactate 2.4. WBC 14.

**Output:**

```
ACUITY / STABILIZATION:
- Airway intact; SpO2 94 RA, give O2 to 92–96.
- Glucose normal — no action.
- IV access × 2; LR 30 mL/kg (∼1.8 L for 60 kg) over 1 h for sepsis with hypotension/lactate elevation; reassess.

HISTORY / COLLATERAL:
- Acute on chronic mental status change (subacute over 2 days).
- Recent UTI treated; possible recurrence or pyelonephritis.
- Decreased PO intake → likely volume depletion contributing to AKI.
- Polypharmacy review for anticholinergics (e.g., oxybutynin for BPH/incontinence — common cause of delirium).

EXAM:
- Febrile, tachycardic, mildly hypotensive — meets sepsis criteria (qSOFA: AMS + RR 22 = 2; consider organ dysfunction).
- No focal neuro deficit, no nuchal rigidity → meningitis less likely but cannot exclude in elderly without LP if no source emerges.
- Suprapubic tenderness consistent with cystitis; check for CVA tenderness for pyelonephritis.

DIFFERENTIAL VIA AEIOU TIPS:
1. Infection — UTI/pyelonephritis with sepsis-associated delirium (most likely).
2. Electrolyte / dehydration / AKI contributing.
3. Medications — recent TMP-SMX (hyperK, AKI, contributes); confirm stopped.
4. CNS infection — possible but less likely without focal deficit, headache, or neck stiffness; LP if not improving with antibiotic.
5. Stroke — non-focal exam; CT to exclude given anticoag use and age threshold.
6. Hypoxia — borderline SpO2; treat.

WORKUP:
- Labs: CBC, BMP, Mg, Ca, phosphorus, LFTs, troponin, lactate (already 2.4), TSH, B12, blood cultures × 2, urine culture (sensitivities critical given recent abx), CRP/procalcitonin.
- UA already done — pyuria, nitrites; obtain post-void residual to rule out retention given BPH (relieve obstruction if present).
- CXR (consider pneumonia in elderly with AMS).
- Non-contrast CT head: indicated given age >60 + new AMS + on no anticoag but to exclude subdural; low threshold.
- LP: defer initially; reassess if not improving in 24–48 h or if focal deficit, severe AMS.
- ECG (sepsis can unmask cardiac issue).

WORKING DIAGNOSIS / TREATMENT:
- Working: urosepsis with sepsis-associated delirium and AKI in patient with baseline mild cognitive impairment.
- Antibiotics within 1 hour: ceftriaxone 1–2 g IV daily for empiric pyelonephritis; broaden to piperacillin-tazobactam 4.5 g IV q8h if recent abx within 90 days, prior resistant organism, or septic shock. Adjust to culture sensitivities.
- IV fluids LR continued, MAP target ≥65; norepinephrine 0.03–0.5 mcg/kg/min if not responsive.
- Hold offending meds (anticholinergics if any). Avoid benzos and antipsychotics initially; treat agitation with non-pharmacologic measures.
- Foley only if obstruction or close I/O monitoring; remove ASAP to reduce CAUTI risk.
- Delirium care: orient frequently, lighting/day-night cues, hearing aids/glasses, family at bedside, sitter, fall precautions. If severe agitation threatening safety: low-dose haloperidol 0.25–0.5 mg PO/IV (avoid in QT prolongation, Parkinson, Lewy body) or quetiapine 12.5–25 mg PO BID; reassess daily for taper.
- Repeat BMP q12h (AKI, K, Na).
- DVT prophylaxis (heparin 5000 units SC q8h).

DISPOSITION:
- Admit to medicine; step-down or ICU if pressors needed.
- Goals of care discussion with family/HCP given baseline dementia.
- Communicate baseline cognition target to plan discharge.
- PT/OT mobility once stable to prevent further functional decline.
- Discharge with medication reconciliation eliminating delirium-promoting drugs; close PCP follow-up; consider geriatrics consultation.
```
