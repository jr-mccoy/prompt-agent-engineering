## Objective

Work up the anemic adult: classify by MCV (microcytic, normocytic, macrocytic) and reticulocyte response (hypoproliferative vs hyperproliferative), identify and treat the cause, and decide on transfusion.

## Inputs

- CBC with indices (Hgb, MCV, MCH, MCHC, RDW), reticulocyte count and reticulocyte index, peripheral smear
- Tempo (acute drop vs chronic), prior baseline
- Symptoms: fatigue, dyspnea, pallor, palpitations, exertional intolerance, postural lightheadedness, signs of hemolysis (jaundice, dark urine), signs of bleeding (melena, hematochezia, hematemesis, hematuria, menorrhagia, hemoptysis), neurologic (B12)
- Diet (vegetarian/vegan, malnutrition, alcohol), GI surgery (gastrectomy, bariatric), malabsorption, autoimmune (pernicious anemia, celiac), pregnancy, NSAID/aspirin/anticoagulant use
- Family history: hemoglobinopathy, hereditary spherocytosis, G6PD, hereditary HHT
- Comorbidities: CKD, malignancy, HIV, chronic inflammation
- Medications: chemotherapy, antiretrovirals, immunosuppressants, methotrexate, sulfa, dapsone, oxidative drugs in G6PD
- Vitals (orthostasis suggests acute blood loss), exam (jaundice, glossitis, koilonychia, splenomegaly, lymphadenopathy, petechiae)
- Iron studies, B12, folate, LDH, haptoglobin, total/direct bilirubin, retic, smear; targeted: hemoglobin electrophoresis, G6PD, DAT, flow cytometry, marrow if indicated

## Reasoning Steps

1. **Confirm anemia and severity.** WHO definitions: Hgb <13 men, <12 non-pregnant women, <11 pregnant. Severe Hgb <7 (or <8 with cardiac comorbidity) — consider transfusion.

2. **Acute vs chronic.** Acute drop with hemodynamic compromise → resuscitate, find source. Chronic anemia tolerated better — work up systematically.

3. **Classify by MCV.**

   - **Microcytic (MCV <80):**
     - Iron deficiency — most common.
     - Anemia of chronic disease (often normocytic; can be microcytic).
     - Thalassemia (alpha or beta) — MCV very low for degree of anemia, target cells, basophilic stippling, family history, normal iron.
     - Sideroblastic anemia (lead, alcohol, copper deficiency, MDS, congenital) — ringed sideroblasts on marrow.
     - Lead poisoning — basophilic stippling.

   - **Normocytic (MCV 80–100):**
     - Acute blood loss before retic response.
     - Anemia of chronic disease/inflammation.
     - CKD (low erythropoietin).
     - Hypothyroidism (can be normocytic or macrocytic).
     - Mixed deficiency (B12 + iron).
     - Aplastic anemia (pancytopenia).
     - Marrow infiltration (malignancy, fibrosis, granuloma).
     - Hemolysis (with high retic).
     - Pure red cell aplasia (parvovirus B19, thymoma, drugs).

   - **Macrocytic (MCV >100):**
     - **Megaloblastic:** B12 deficiency (pernicious anemia, malabsorption, vegan, metformin), folate deficiency (poor diet, alcoholism, methotrexate, phenytoin, sulfasalazine, pregnancy, hemolysis with high turnover). Hypersegmented neutrophils, large red cells.
     - **Non-megaloblastic:** alcohol, liver disease, hypothyroidism, MDS, reticulocytosis (the retic itself is large).
     - Drug-induced (hydroxyurea, zidovudine, methotrexate, antiepileptics).

4. **Reticulocyte index (corrected retic).**
   Retic % × (Hct/45) ÷ maturation factor (1.0 if Hct ≥35, 1.5 if 25–34, 2.0 if 20–24, 2.5 if <20).
   - **Index <2:** hypoproliferative — marrow not responding (deficiency, marrow failure, chronic disease, suppression).
   - **Index >2 (or absolute retic >100,000):** hyperproliferative — bleeding or hemolysis.

5. **Iron deficiency anemia (IDA).**
   - **Diagnostics:** ferritin <30 ng/mL is diagnostic (acute-phase reactant — interpret carefully if inflammation; <100 with chronic disease still suggests deficiency). Low transferrin saturation <16%, high TIBC, low serum iron. Smear: hypochromic microcytic, anisocytosis, pencil cells.
   - **Cause finding mandatory:**
     - Premenopausal women: menstrual loss, pregnancy, diet — but still consider GI source if asymptomatic.
     - Postmenopausal women and men: GI source until proven otherwise → EGD and colonoscopy. Capsule endoscopy if both negative. Celiac serology (anti-tTG IgA + total IgA) in iron-refractory IDA.
     - Athletes, blood donors, malabsorption (post-bariatric, atrophic gastritis, H. pylori).
   - **Treatment:**
     - **Oral iron**: ferrous sulfate 325 mg (65 mg elemental) every other day or daily on empty stomach with vitamin C. Every-other-day dosing (per IRONOUT and other studies) is at least as effective and better tolerated due to hepcidin physiology.
     - Reticulocytosis at 5–10 days; Hgb increase ~1 g/dL/2–3 weeks. Continue 3–6 months after Hgb normalized to replete stores.
     - **IV iron** (ferric carboxymaltose 750 mg ×2, ferric derisomaltose 1000 mg single dose, iron sucrose) for: oral intolerance, malabsorption, ongoing loss, CKD, IBD, post-bariatric, severe anemia needing rapid replacement, anemia of pregnancy (2nd/3rd trimester).
     - Treat underlying cause (lesion, H. pylori eradication, celiac diet).

6. **B12 deficiency.**
   - **Diagnostics:** B12 <200 pg/mL diagnostic; 200–400 borderline → check methylmalonic acid (MMA) and homocysteine (both elevated in B12 deficiency; only homocysteine in folate deficiency). Pernicious anemia: anti-intrinsic factor and anti-parietal cell antibodies; gastrin elevated.
   - **Causes:** pernicious anemia (autoimmune), strict vegan, ileal disease/resection, bariatric surgery, atrophic gastritis, metformin, PPIs/H2 blockers, fish tapeworm, achlorhydria.
   - **Treatment:**
     - Severe / neurologic / pernicious anemia: cyanocobalamin 1000 mcg IM daily × 1 week, weekly × 4 weeks, then monthly for life. Or hydroxocobalamin alternative.
     - Mild dietary deficiency or stable: oral cyanocobalamin 1000–2000 mcg daily.
     - Sublingual or nasal alternatives.
     - Reticulocyte response in 5–7 days; Hgb correction in 6–8 weeks; neurologic symptoms may take months.
     - Replete folate and iron concurrently if deficient. Always rule out B12 deficiency before treating folate alone (folate alone may worsen neuro disease).

7. **Folate deficiency.**
   - Folate <2 ng/mL (RBC folate more sensitive).
   - Causes: poor diet, alcoholism, increased turnover (hemolysis, pregnancy), drugs (methotrexate, trimethoprim, sulfasalazine, phenytoin), malabsorption.
   - Treat: folic acid 1 mg PO daily; 5 mg in pregnancy or hemolysis; 5 mg with methotrexate.

8. **Hemolytic anemia.**
   - **Findings:** elevated retic, elevated indirect bilirubin, elevated LDH, decreased haptoglobin (best for intravascular), schistocytes/spherocytes/sickle cells on smear, hemoglobinuria.
   - **Direct antiglobulin test (Coombs):**
     - Warm autoimmune (IgG positive): idiopathic, lymphoproliferative, autoimmune (SLE), drug-induced (penicillin, methyldopa, fludarabine). Treat: prednisone 1 mg/kg daily, rituximab, splenectomy, immunosuppressants for refractory.
     - Cold agglutinin (C3 positive): Mycoplasma, EBV, lymphoma. Avoid cold; rituximab.
   - **Coombs negative:**
     - Microangiopathic: TTP (low ADAMTS13, MAHA, thrombocytopenia, neuro/renal/fever), HUS (post-diarrheal Shiga toxin, atypical complement-mediated), DIC, malignant HTN, HELLP, mechanical valve hemolysis, scleroderma renal crisis.
     - Hereditary: spherocytosis (osmotic fragility, EMA flow), elliptocytosis, pyruvate kinase deficiency.
     - Hemoglobinopathies: sickle cell, sickle/beta-thal, unstable hemoglobin.
     - G6PD deficiency: oxidative stress (sulfa, dapsone, primaquine, fava beans, infection); bite cells, Heinz bodies. Quantitative G6PD (false-normal during acute episode due to reticulocytes).
     - PNH: flow cytometry CD55/CD59. Eculizumab/ravulizumab.
     - March hemoglobinuria, prosthetic valve.
     - Drug-induced (immune or oxidant).

9. **Bone marrow assessment.**
   - Indications: pancytopenia, unexplained anemia after non-invasive workup, suspected myelodysplastic syndrome, suspected lymphoma/leukemia infiltration, fevers/B-symptoms with cytopenias.
   - Send aspirate (cytology, flow, cytogenetics, FISH, NGS), biopsy (cellularity, fibrosis, infiltrate), and culture if febrile.

10. **Anemia of chronic disease / inflammation.**
    - Mechanism: hepcidin elevation by IL-6 sequesters iron in macrophages; suppression of EPO and erythroid response.
    - Pattern: low serum iron, low TIBC (vs IDA where TIBC high), normal-high ferritin, low retic.
    - Soluble transferrin receptor (sTfR) and sTfR/log-ferritin index help differentiate from concurrent IDA.
    - Treat underlying inflammation; ESAs in selected (CKD, chemo-induced anemia per FDA black box risk discussion); IV iron when iron-restricted erythropoiesis.

11. **Chronic kidney disease anemia.**
    - EPO insufficiency. Target Hgb 10–11 g/dL.
    - Iron repletion: ferritin 100–500, TSAT >20%; IV iron preferred in dialysis.
    - ESA (epoetin, darbepoetin) once iron repleted; HIF-PHI (roxadustat, daprodustat) emerging oral options.

12. **Transfusion thresholds.**
    - General hospitalized patient: Hgb <7.
    - Cardiac disease, ACS, cardiac surgery: <8 (some <7 per trials including FOCUS, MINT debated).
    - Active bleeding, hemodynamic instability: clinical, transfuse to symptoms and ongoing loss.
    - Premedication for known reactions; ABO type and screen mandatory; informed consent.

```
SEVERITY:
- Hgb, hemodynamic state, transfusion threshold

CLASSIFICATION:
- MCV (micro / normo / macro)
- Retic index (hypo / hyper-proliferative)
- Pertinent smear findings

ETIOLOGY HYPOTHESIS (ranked):
1. [most likely with reasoning]
2. [second]
3. [third]

WORKUP:
- Iron studies, B12/folate, hemolysis labs, electrophoresis, Coombs, etc.
- Endoscopy / imaging / marrow if indicated

WORKING DIAGNOSIS / TREATMENT:
- Specific therapy with drug, dose, route, duration
- Source identification (GI, GU, gyn) if blood loss

- Outpatient / admit
- Follow-up labs in [time]
- Specialty referral
```

## Worked Example

**Input:** 58 y/o postmenopausal F, presents with progressive fatigue and dyspnea on exertion over 3 months. No overt bleeding. Vegetarian for 20 years. Last colonoscopy 8 years ago — normal. Exam: pale conjunctivae, mild glossitis, no jaundice, no organomegaly. CBC: Hgb 8.4, MCV 76, MCH 25, RDW 18%, platelets 480, WBC 6.8. Retic 1.2% (low for degree of anemia). Smear: hypochromic microcytic, anisopoikilocytosis, pencil cells. Iron 28, TIBC 480, transferrin saturation 6%, ferritin 8 ng/mL. B12 320, folate 9. LDH 180, total bilirubin 0.6. BMP normal. FIT positive.

**Output:**

```
SEVERITY:
- Hgb 8.4 — moderate. No hemodynamic instability. Symptomatic. Below transfusion threshold for cardiac comorbidity (none here); not yet at general threshold of 7.

CLASSIFICATION:
- Microcytic (MCV 76), elevated RDW.
- Hypoproliferative (retic 1.2%, low).
- Smear: hypochromic microcytic, pencil cells — classic IDA.
- Iron studies: ferritin 8 (diagnostic), TSAT 6%, low Fe, high TIBC — IRON DEFICIENCY ANEMIA confirmed.

ETIOLOGY HYPOTHESIS (ranked):
1. GI blood loss — postmenopausal, FIT positive, suggests right or left colon source vs upper GI source. MUST source.
2. Diet contributes — long-standing vegetarian, but on its own would not give ferritin 8 and FIT positive.
3. Less likely: malabsorption (celiac), occult genitourinary loss.

WORKUP:
- EGD AND colonoscopy (combined session). Biopsies of duodenum to evaluate celiac disease and H. pylori.
- Anti-tTG IgA + total IgA (celiac).
- If both endoscopies negative: capsule endoscopy for small bowel.
- UA to rule out hematuria.
- Repeat CBC/iron studies in 4 weeks after starting therapy.

WORKING DIAGNOSIS / TREATMENT:
- Iron deficiency anemia from suspected GI blood loss (postmenopausal, FIT positive).
- Iron repletion:
  - Option A: oral ferrous sulfate 325 mg (65 mg elemental) every other day on empty stomach with vitamin C 250 mg or orange juice. Avoid calcium, dairy, tea, coffee within 2 h. Continue 3–6 months after Hgb normalized.
  - Option B: IV iron if poor oral tolerance, malabsorption, or rapid response needed. Ferric carboxymaltose 750 mg IV × 2 doses 7 days apart. Or ferric derisomaltose 1000 mg IV single dose.
- Reticulocyte rise expected in 7–10 days; Hgb increase ~1 g/2–3 weeks.
- Vitamin B12 320 borderline-low for vegetarian — check methylmalonic acid; if elevated, repletion. Even if normal MMA, advise oral B12 1000 mcg daily (vegetarian risk) and dietary fortification.
- No transfusion indicated currently (asymptomatic at rest, no cardiac disease, no active bleed).
- Address dietary iron intake: counseling on iron-rich plant sources (lentils, beans, fortified cereals, dark leafy greens) with vitamin C; counsel against calcium/tea with iron-rich meals.

DISPOSITION:
- Outpatient. Schedule EGD/colonoscopy within 1–2 weeks.
- Repeat CBC and iron studies in 4 weeks.
- If endoscopies reveal malignancy → oncology; ulcer with H. pylori → triple therapy; celiac → gluten-free diet; angioectasia → consider IV iron + endoscopic therapy.
- Patient education on warning signs (overt bleeding, severe symptoms) requiring urgent care.
```
