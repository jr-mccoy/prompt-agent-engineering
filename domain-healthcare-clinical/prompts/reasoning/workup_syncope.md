## Objective

Work up the syncopal patient: confirm transient loss of consciousness with spontaneous recovery, classify by mechanism (reflex/vasovagal, orthostatic, cardiac), apply Canadian Syncope Risk Score and San Francisco Syncope Rule, and determine disposition.

## Inputs

- Event: prodrome (warmth, lightheadedness, nausea, visual graying), trigger (standing, micturition, defecation, cough, blood draw, emotion), position at onset, duration of LOC, post-event symptoms (confusion, fatigue, focal deficit), witness account, tongue biting/incontinence
- Associated cardiac symptoms: palpitations preceding, exertional onset, chest pain, dyspnea
- Family history: sudden cardiac death <50, channelopathy, cardiomyopathy
- Medications: antihypertensives, diuretics, QT-prolonging drugs, alpha-blockers, vasodilators, insulin, alcohol, illicit drugs
- Vitals: orthostatic BP and HR (supine, standing 1 and 3 min), bilateral arm BP if dissection considered
- Exam: cardiac (murmurs, S3/S4, JVD), neuro (focal deficits), volume status, signs of anemia or GI bleed (rectal exam), hydration
- ECG (mandatory), basic labs (CBC, BMP, Mg, glucose, troponin, beta-hCG if indicated)

## Reasoning Steps

1. **Confirm syncope.** Transient LOC due to global cerebral hypoperfusion with rapid onset, short duration, spontaneous complete recovery. Mimics to exclude: seizure (postictal confusion >5 min, tongue biting lateral, sustained convulsion), stroke/TIA (focal deficit), hypoglycemia, intoxication, psychogenic pseudosyncope.

2. **Classify by mechanism.**

   - **Reflex (neurally mediated):** vasovagal (prolonged standing, emotional, painful stimulus, with prodrome), situational (cough, micturition, defecation, swallow), carotid sinus (older, head turning, tight collar). Most common, generally benign. Tilt-table testing if recurrent and uncertain.
   - **Orthostatic hypotension:** drop ≥20 mmHg SBP or ≥10 mmHg DBP within 3 minutes of standing (or HUTT). Causes: volume depletion (GI loss, diuretics, hemorrhage), drugs (alpha-blockers, vasodilators, TCAs, antipsychotics), autonomic failure (diabetes, Parkinson, MSA, amyloid).
   - **Cardiac:**
     - **Arrhythmic:** brady (high-grade AV block, sinus pause), tachy (VT, SVT, AFib with RVR), inherited channelopathy (long QT, Brugada, CPVT, ARVC).
     - **Structural:** AS (exertional syncope = aortic stenosis until proven otherwise), HCM (exertional, with murmur intensifying on Valsalva), pulmonary embolism, tamponade, aortic dissection, atrial myxoma.

3. **Red flags suggesting cardiac syncope (high-risk).**
   - Syncope during exertion (not after)
   - Syncope while supine
   - Palpitations preceding LOC
   - Chest pain or dyspnea around event
   - Family history sudden death <50
   - Known structural heart disease, prior MI, low EF, HF
   - Abnormal ECG (any of: bifascicular block, QRS ≥120, sinus brady <50, second/third degree AV block, prolonged QTc >500, Brugada pattern, epsilon waves/ARVC features, ischemic changes, Q waves, LVH consistent with HCM)
   - Age >65 with new syncope

4. **Apply Canadian Syncope Risk Score (CSRS) — for ED disposition.**
   Variables (predicting 30-day serious outcome):
   - Predisposition to vasovagal symptoms (−1)
   - History of heart disease (+1)
   - Any SBP <90 or >180 in ED (+2)
   - Troponin >99th percentile (+2)
   - Abnormal QRS axis (+1)
   - QRS duration >130 ms (+1)
   - Corrected QT >480 ms (+2)
   - ED diagnosis of vasovagal syncope (−2)
   - ED diagnosis of cardiac syncope (+2)
   Score range −3 to +11. Risk tiers: very low (≤−2), low (−1 to 0), medium (1 to 3), high (4 to 5), very high (≥6). Discharge with very low-low; admit/observation for medium-very high.

5. **San Francisco Syncope Rule (SFSR) — predicts 7-day serious outcomes.** Any of:
   - History of CHF
   - Hematocrit <30
   - Abnormal ECG
   - Shortness of breath
   - SBP <90 mmHg in triage
   Any positive → admit / observe.

6. **ECG findings to flag immediately.**
   - WPW (delta wave, short PR), prolonged QTc, Brugada (RBBB-like with coved ST elevation V1–V3), epsilon waves (ARVC), pathologic Q waves (prior MI), AV blocks, bifascicular block, atrial flutter/fibrillation, sinus pause >3 s, ventricular ectopy, ischemic changes.

7. **Targeted workup based on suspicion.**
   - Suspected arrhythmia, normal ECG: telemetry; if intermittent and concerning, 24–48 h Holter, 14–30 day patch monitor (Zio), or implantable loop recorder for unexplained syncope with high recurrence risk.
   - Suspected structural: echocardiogram (AS, HCM, EF, RV strain).
   - Exertional syncope: stress test, echo, cardiology — consider HCM, AS, ischemia.
   - Suspected PE: Wells/PERC, d-dimer, CTPA.
   - Suspected dissection: CTA chest/abdomen/pelvis.
   - Suspected vascular brain event: CT/MRI brain — but pure syncope without focal deficit rarely needs head imaging; reserve for trauma, focal deficit, or atypical features.
   - Hypoglycemia: fingerstick glucose immediately.
   - Electrolytes, especially hypoMg/hypoK with QTc prolongation.

8. **Trigger and medication review.**
   - Hold or dose-adjust offending agents: diuretics, alpha-blockers, BB excess, nitrates, anticholinergics, alcohol.
   - Review for QT-prolonging drugs (azoles, macrolides, fluoroquinolones, antipsychotics, ondansetron, methadone, antiarrhythmics, SSRIs).

9. **Treatment by etiology.**
   - **Vasovagal:** education, hydration (≥2 L/day), sodium (∼10 g/day if no contraindication), counter-pressure maneuvers (leg crossing, hand grip, arm tensing), abort with supine + leg elevation. Midodrine 5–10 mg TID, fludrocortisone 0.1–0.3 mg daily for refractory.
   - **Orthostatic:** review meds, compression stockings, slow positional change, increase salt and fluids, midodrine, droxidopa (in autonomic failure).
   - **Bradyarrhythmia / AV block:** pacemaker (sinus node dysfunction with symptoms; second-degree Mobitz II or third-degree AV block).
   - **VT/VF or high-risk SCD substrate:** ICD (per indication), antiarrhythmic, ablation; cardiology/EP.
   - **AS / HCM:** valvular intervention (TAVR/SAVR), septal myectomy/alcohol septal ablation, myosin inhibitor (mavacamten) for obstructive HCM.

10. **Driving and activity restrictions.** Counsel per local jurisdiction; common: no driving for 1 month after unexplained syncope, longer if cardiac cause without intervention. Document the conversation.

## Worked Example

**Input:** 72 y/o M, HTN, hyperlipidemia, presents after collapsing while climbing stairs at home. No prodrome. Brief LOC, witness reports ~30 sec, no convulsion, no incontinence, immediate full recovery. Reports occasional exertional dyspnea over last 6 months. PE: harsh systolic crescendo-decrescendo murmur 3/6 at right upper sternal border radiating to carotids; soft S2. BP 132/86 supine, 128/82 standing 3 min. HR 76 regular. ECG: sinus, LVH with strain pattern, QTc 440. Troponin negative. Hgb 13.4. CSRS: heart disease ?+1, SBP within range 0, troponin normal 0, QRS axis normal 0, QRS 96 ms 0, QTc 440 0, ED dx cardiac suspected +2 = +3.

**Output:**

```
HISTORICAL FEATURES:
- Exertional syncope without prodrome — high-risk feature.
- Witnessed brief LOC with full recovery; no postictal confusion → not seizure.
- Prior exertional dyspnea over 6 months → progressive exertional cardiac symptom.

EXAM:
- BP / orthostatics not contributory (no orthostatic drop).
- Murmur consistent with severe aortic stenosis: harsh, late-peaking, radiation to carotids, soft S2.
- No JVD, no gallop noted; reassess for HF.

ECG:
- Sinus, LVH with strain — supports chronic pressure overload (AS).
- No bifascicular block, QTc normal, no ischemia.

RISK SCORES:
- CSRS: predisposition vasovagal 0, heart disease (presumed AS) +1, SBP normal 0, troponin neg 0, axis 0, QRS 0, QTc 0, ED diagnosis cardiac +2 = +3 → MEDIUM-HIGH risk.
- SFSR: abnormal ECG (LVH with strain) → positive → admit/observe.

DIFFERENTIAL (ranked):
1. Severe aortic stenosis with exertional syncope — classic triad of AS (angina, syncope, HF) signals critical disease; mortality high without intervention.
2. Hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy — would expect murmur intensifying on Valsalva; less likely at this age but check echo.
3. Exertional arrhythmia (VT in setting of LVH/cardiomyopathy substrate).
4. Less likely: vasovagal (no prodrome, exertional onset), orthostatic (no drop).

WORKUP:
- Urgent transthoracic echocardiogram: aortic valve area, mean and peak gradients, LVOT velocity, LV function, septal thickness.
- Telemetry monitoring during admission.
- BNP, lipid panel, HbA1c.
- If echo confirms severe AS (AVA <1.0, mean gradient ≥40, peak velocity ≥4), proceed with structural heart team and CTA for TAVR planning, coronary angiography for CAD evaluation, dental clearance.
- If exertional arrhythmia substrate suspected → cardiac MRI, EP consult.

WORKING DIAGNOSIS / TREATMENT:
- Working: severe symptomatic aortic stenosis presenting with exertional syncope.
- Activity restriction: no exertion or driving until evaluated.
- Avoid afterload reducers (high-dose ACE/ARB, vasodilators) until afterload-dependent severe AS excluded.
- Continue antihypertensives at conservative doses; hold any that produced orthostatic drop.
- Pain or HF medications adjusted to maintain preload.
- Antibiotic prophylaxis for dental work only if prosthetic valve in future; not indicated for native AS.

DISPOSITION:
- Admit to telemetry. Cardiology / structural heart consult.
- Plan: TAVR vs SAVR per heart team after echo and CTA.
- Patient counseling on driving cessation pending intervention; document.
- Discharge with prompt cardiology follow-up if intervention deferred; otherwise hospital course continues to definitive therapy.
```
