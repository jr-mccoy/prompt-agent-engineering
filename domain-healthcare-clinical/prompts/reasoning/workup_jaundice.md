## Objective

Work up the jaundiced patient: classify hyperbilirubinemia (indirect vs direct), differentiate hepatocellular from cholestatic patterns, identify biliary obstruction with imaging, and triage to drainage, hepatology, or hematology.

## Inputs

- Total and direct bilirubin
- Liver enzymes: ALT, AST, alkaline phosphatase, GGT
- Synthetic function: albumin, INR/PT
- CBC, retic, LDH, haptoglobin (if hemolysis suspected)
- Symptom onset: acute (days) vs subacute (weeks) vs chronic
- Associated: pain (RUQ/epigastric — biliary, hepatic), pruritus (cholestasis), dark urine, pale stools (cholestatic), fever, weight loss, anorexia, abdominal distension, encephalopathy, hematemesis/melena (varices), arthralgia, rash, sicca, IBD
- Exposure: alcohol, hepatotoxic drugs (acetaminophen, isoniazid, nitrofurantoin, methotrexate, statins, herbals, augmentin, anabolic steroids, anti-TB drugs, valproate, amiodarone), IV drugs, sexual history, transfusion history, tattoos, travel, raw shellfish, mushroom ingestion, occupational
- Family history: Wilson, hemochromatosis, alpha-1 antitrypsin
- Pregnancy
- Vitals; exam (icterus, scleral, sublingual; spider angiomata, palmar erythema, gynecomastia, caput medusae, ascites, splenomegaly, asterixis, fetor, Kayser-Fleischer rings, Murphy, palpable gallbladder — Courvoisier, lymphadenopathy, skin xanthelasma)
- Imaging: RUQ ultrasound first; MRCP, CT abdomen, ERCP, EUS as guided

## Reasoning Steps

1. **Classify bilirubin pattern.**
   - **Indirect (unconjugated) hyperbilirubinemia (≥85% indirect):**
     - Hemolysis (CBC, retic, LDH, haptoglobin, smear) — see anemia workup; intravascular vs extravascular.
     - Ineffective erythropoiesis.
     - Gilbert syndrome — common (~5%), benign, mild fluctuating elevations with fasting/illness/exertion; otherwise normal LFTs.
     - Crigler-Najjar (rare, severe).
     - Resorption of large hematoma.

   - **Direct (conjugated) hyperbilirubinemia (>50% direct), with abnormal LFTs:** classify by enzyme pattern.

2. **Liver enzyme pattern.**

   - **Hepatocellular** (ALT/AST elevation > ALP elevation):
     - Acute viral hepatitis (A, B, C, D, E; EBV, CMV, HSV in immunocompromised, COVID).
     - Drug-induced liver injury (acetaminophen, INH, nitrofurantoin, methotrexate, augmentin pattern can be cholestatic, herbals — green tea extract, kava, tea, supplements).
     - Ischemic hepatitis ("shock liver"): ALT/AST often >1000–10,000, abrupt rise then fall after hypoperfusion.
     - Autoimmune hepatitis: positive ANA, ASMA, anti-LKM, IgG; hepatology + biopsy.
     - Wilson disease (young, neuropsych, low ALP/bilirubin ratio <4, low ceruloplasmin, KF rings, high urinary copper).
     - Hemochromatosis (high ferritin, high TSAT >45%, HFE gene).
     - Alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency.
     - Alcoholic hepatitis: AST:ALT ratio >2 with both <500; GGT elevated; macrocytosis; clinical picture (heavy use, fever, leukocytosis, jaundice). Maddrey discriminant function ≥32 → consider prednisolone 40 mg PO daily × 28 days with Lille score reassessment at day 7.
     - NAFLD/NASH: usually mild, BMI/metabolic syndrome.
     - Budd-Chiari (hepatic vein thrombosis): pain, ascites, hepatomegaly; Doppler US, MRV.

   - **Cholestatic** (ALP elevation > ALT/AST elevation, GGT confirms hepatic origin of ALP):
     - **Extrahepatic obstruction:**
       - Choledocholithiasis (RUQ pain, intermittent jaundice, fluctuating LFTs).
       - Cholangitis (Charcot triad — fever, RUQ pain, jaundice; Reynolds adds AMS, shock).
       - Pancreatic head cancer (Courvoisier — palpable nontender gallbladder with painless jaundice in older patient).
       - Cholangiocarcinoma.
       - Strictures (PSC, post-surgical).
       - Mirizzi syndrome (gallstone in cystic duct compressing CBD).
       - Ampullary tumor.
     - **Intrahepatic cholestasis:**
       - Primary biliary cholangitis (middle-aged women, pruritus, AMA positive, IgM elevated).
       - Primary sclerosing cholangitis (often with IBD; MRCP shows beaded ducts; pANCA).
       - Drug-induced (augmentin, anabolic steroids, OCPs, total parenteral nutrition).
       - Sepsis, severe systemic illness.
       - Pregnancy (intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy — itching, elevated bile acids).
       - Granulomatous diseases (sarcoid, TB, lymphoma).
       - Infiltrative (amyloid, malignancy).
     - **Mixed pattern** common in cholestatic injury with hepatocyte involvement.

3. **Initial labs — every jaundice workup.**
   - Total and direct bilirubin, ALT, AST, ALP, GGT, albumin, INR/PT.
   - CBC with smear, retic, LDH, haptoglobin (hemolysis screen if indirect).
   - BMP, glucose, ammonia (if encephalopathy).
   - Hepatitis A IgM, hepatitis B surface antigen, hepatitis B core IgM, hepatitis C antibody (with PCR if positive or immunocompromised), hepatitis E in endemic regions.
   - HIV.
   - For chronic / unexplained: ANA, ASMA, anti-LKM, IgG/IgM/IgA, AMA, ceruloplasmin, ferritin/iron studies, alpha-1 antitrypsin level/phenotype.
   - Acetaminophen level if ingestion concern.
   - Pregnancy test in reproductive-age women.

4. **Imaging strategy.**
   - **RUQ ultrasound first:** sensitive for biliary dilation, gallstones, gallbladder wall, hepatic mass; assesses portal vein flow.
   - **MRCP** for non-invasive biliary tree visualization (sensitive for stones, strictures, PSC).
   - **CT abdomen with contrast** for parenchymal disease, masses, vascular evaluation.
   - **ERCP** for therapeutic intervention (stone extraction, stent placement, sphincterotomy, biopsy/brushings) — not first-line diagnostic given pancreatitis risk.
   - **EUS** for distal CBD evaluation, ampullary lesions, pancreatic mass biopsy.
   - **Cholangitis** with sepsis or shock → ERCP urgently (within 24 h ideally) for biliary decompression.
   - **Liver biopsy** for unexplained chronic hepatocellular disease, autoimmune hepatitis, suspected infiltrative disease.

5. **Specific clinical syndromes.**

   - **Acute liver failure:** jaundice + INR ≥1.5 + encephalopathy without preexisting cirrhosis, within 26 weeks of onset. Causes: acetaminophen (most common in US), drug-induced, viral, autoimmune, Wilson, mushroom (Amanita), HSV, ischemic, Budd-Chiari, pregnancy-related (AFLP, HELLP).
     - Transfer to liver transplant center early. King's College criteria for transplant referral.
     - Acetaminophen: NAC even if late presentation. ICU.
     - Avoid sedatives. Manage cerebral edema (HOB 30°, hypertonic saline, mannitol; avoid hypotonic fluids).

   - **Acute viral hepatitis:** generally supportive; hepatitis B: monitor for fulminant or chronic; tenofovir or entecavir if severe acute or fulminant. Hepatitis C: direct-acting antivirals once chronic confirmed (≥6 months). Hepatitis A and E: supportive.

   - **Cholangitis:** sepsis bundle, IV broad-spectrum antibiotics (piperacillin-tazobactam 4.5 g IV q8h or ceftriaxone + metronidazole), urgent ERCP for decompression. Mortality drops with timely drainage.

   - **Choledocholithiasis:** stratify by ASGE criteria into low / intermediate / high probability. High → ERCP. Intermediate → MRCP or EUS first.

   - **Pancreatic head cancer:** painless jaundice, weight loss, palpable gallbladder. CT pancreas protocol, EUS with biopsy, CA 19-9. Surgical (Whipple) candidacy assessment by MDT; preoperative biliary stenting selectively.

   - **PSC:** MRCP with characteristic beading; colonoscopy for IBD; surveillance for cholangiocarcinoma (MRI/CA 19-9 q6–12 mo). UDCA controversial. Liver transplant for advanced disease.

   - **PBC:** AMA positive in 95%; UDCA 13–15 mg/kg/day; obeticholic acid second-line; pruritus management (cholestyramine, rifampin, naltrexone).

   - **Pregnancy-related:** intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy (UDCA, delivery at 36–37 weeks); HELLP, AFLP — emergent delivery.

6. **Treatment principles.**
   - Identify and treat underlying cause.
   - Pruritus: cholestyramine 4 g BID-QID, rifampin 150–300 mg BID, naltrexone 50 mg daily; sertraline; UV phototherapy.
   - Coagulopathy: vitamin K 10 mg IV/SC × 3 days for cholestatic; FFP for active bleeding only.
   - Nutrition: fat-soluble vitamin (A, D, E, K) supplementation in chronic cholestasis.
   - Avoid hepatotoxins; cap acetaminophen at ≤2 g/day in chronic liver disease.
   - Vaccinate hepatitis A and B in non-immune patients with chronic liver disease.

## Output Format

```
BILIRUBIN PATTERN:
- Total / direct / indirect
- Indirect-dominant vs direct-dominant

ENZYME PATTERN:
- Hepatocellular / cholestatic / mixed
- R-factor: (ALT/ULN) / (ALP/ULN) — >5 hepatocellular, <2 cholestatic

DIFFERENTIAL (ranked):
1. [most likely with reasoning]
2. [second]
3. [third]

INITIAL LABS:
- LFT panel, INR, albumin, hepatitis screen, CBC, retic, hemolysis, autoimmune
- Acetaminophen level if relevant

IMAGING:
- RUQ US first → MRCP / CT / ERCP / EUS based on finding

WORKING DIAGNOSIS / TREATMENT:
- Targeted therapy with drug, dose, route
- Decompression (ERCP) timing if cholangitis / obstruction
- Hepatology / surgery / oncology referral

DISPOSITION:
- Admit if cholangitis, ALF, severe alcoholic hepatitis, encephalopathy, ascites
- Outpatient if stable, identifiable benign cause
```

## Worked Example

**Input:** 68 y/o M with 4 weeks of progressive painless jaundice, dark urine, pale stools, pruritus, 6 kg weight loss, mild back pain. PMH: smoker 30 pack-years. No alcohol use disorder. Exam: scleral icterus, mildly distended abdomen, palpable nontender gallbladder, no ascites, no asterixis. Labs: total bili 14.2 (direct 9.6), ALT 142, AST 110, ALP 612, GGT 850, albumin 3.4, INR 1.2. CBC normal. Hepatitis serologies pending. RUQ US: dilated intra- and extra-hepatic ducts, CBD 14 mm, no stones visualized, hypoechoic mass at pancreatic head suspected.

**Output:**

```
BILIRUBIN PATTERN:
- Total 14.2, direct 9.6 (~68% direct) → conjugated hyperbilirubinemia.

ENZYME PATTERN:
- ALP 612 (4× ULN), ALT 142 (~3× ULN), R-factor (ALT/ULN)/(ALP/ULN) ≈ 0.8 → cholestatic pattern.
- GGT 850 confirms hepatic origin of ALP.

DIFFERENTIAL (ranked):
1. Pancreatic head adenocarcinoma — painless jaundice, weight loss, palpable nontender gallbladder (Courvoisier sign), age, smoking, ductal dilation, mass on US. Most likely.
2. Cholangiocarcinoma (distal or perihilar) — same presentation pattern; imaging will differentiate.
3. Ampullary or duodenal mass.
4. Choledocholithiasis with stricture — possible but less likely given mass on US.
5. PSC with stricture — would expect IBD history, beaded ducts on MRCP; less likely as new presentation at 68.
6. Drug-induced cholestasis — review meds; less likely with mass on US.

INITIAL LABS:
- Already done. Add CA 19-9, CEA, AFP. Hepatitis B and C serologies (any chronic liver disease impact on plan).
- Type & screen, INR (1.2 mild prolongation — likely from vit K malabsorption in cholestasis).
- Vitamin K 10 mg SC × 3 days to correct coagulopathy.

IMAGING:
- **CT pancreas protocol** (multiphase) immediately — characterize mass, evaluate vascular involvement (SMA, SMV, portal vein, celiac), assess resectability, identify metastases.
- **MRCP** complements for biliary anatomy if CT inadequate.
- **EUS with FNA biopsy** of pancreatic mass for tissue diagnosis.
- ERCP for biliary decompression with stent placement (often plastic stent first, replaced with metal stent if unresectable; resectable patients may not always require pre-operative stenting unless cholangitis or significant jaundice with delayed surgery).
- Consider PET-CT for staging if no contraindications.

WORKING DIAGNOSIS / TREATMENT:
- Working: pancreatic head adenocarcinoma with biliary obstruction.
- Biliary decompression via ERCP with metal/plastic stent — relieves jaundice, pruritus, normalizes LFTs over 1–2 weeks before further treatment.
- Pruritus management: cholestyramine 4 g BID, naltrexone 25–50 mg daily as needed.
- Nutritional support; pancreatic enzyme supplementation if exocrine insufficiency suspected.
- Multidisciplinary tumor board for staging and resectability assessment (Whipple for resectable; neoadjuvant for borderline; chemotherapy/RT for unresectable).
- Genetic counseling — pancreatic cancer family history risk.
- Diabetic monitoring (new-onset DM in pancreatic cancer common).
- Vitamin K, fat-soluble vitamin support; avoid hepatotoxins.

DISPOSITION:
- Admit for ERCP decompression, biopsy, and staging.
- Surgical and oncology consults.
- Goals of care discussion early.
- Discharge once jaundice trending down, oral intake adequate, pain controlled, with planned outpatient MDT/oncology follow-up.
- Patient and family education about diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment pathway in clear, structured manner.
```
