## Objective

Work up palpable lymphadenopathy: characterize node properties (location, size, consistency, mobility, tenderness), classify localized vs generalized, identify need for biopsy, and pursue station-appropriate differential.

## Inputs

- Node characteristics: location, size (greatest dimension), number, consistency (soft, firm, rubbery, hard), mobility, tenderness, matting, overlying skin changes
- Duration and tempo: rapid (days) suggests infectious; gradual (weeks-months) and persistent suggests malignancy
- Constitutional symptoms: fever, night sweats, ≥10% weight loss in 6 months (B-symptoms of lymphoma), pruritus, alcohol-induced node pain (Hodgkin)
- Local symptoms: site-specific (sore throat, dental, skin lesion, breast mass, GI/GU symptoms)
- Exposure: cat scratch, tick, recent travel, sexual history, IV drugs, TB exposure, occupational, raw food, animal contact
- Immunization, immunosuppression, malignancy history
- Medications (phenytoin, allopurinol, others can cause pseudolymphoma / DRESS)
- Vaccination history (recent vaccines reactive nodes)
- Examination of all node stations (cervical anterior/posterior, supraclavicular, axillary, epitrochlear, inguinal, popliteal), liver and spleen, skin, oral cavity, breast, testicular, anorectal
- Labs: CBC with diff and smear, BMP, LFTs, LDH, uric acid, ESR/CRP, HIV, hepatitis serologies, RPR, EBV/CMV, IGRA, ANA
- Imaging: ultrasound for nodes (vascularity, hilum), CT chest/abdomen/pelvis if generalized or B-symptoms or supraclavicular
- Biopsy: excisional preferred for suspected lymphoma; core needle for solid tumor mets

## Reasoning Steps

1. **Determine localized vs generalized.**
   - **Localized:** one anatomic region.
   - **Generalized:** ≥2 non-contiguous regions. Generalized lymphadenopathy more often signals systemic disease (lymphoma, leukemia, HIV, infectious mono, autoimmune, drug reaction, sarcoidosis, syphilis, miliary TB).

2. **Apply 1 cm rule with caveats.**
   - Inguinal nodes up to 1.5 cm and submandibular up to 2 cm can be normal.
   - Supraclavicular node of any size = abnormal until proven otherwise (Virchow node = left supraclavicular often gastric/abdominal malignancy; right supraclavicular often thoracic malignancy).
   - Cervical, axillary, others >1 cm in adult is abnormal.
   - Epitrochlear >0.5 cm is abnormal.

3. **Concerning features for malignancy.**
   - Hard, fixed, painless, matted nodes.
   - Supraclavicular location.
   - Size ≥2 cm and persistent.
   - Progressive enlargement over weeks.
   - Generalized lymphadenopathy with hepatosplenomegaly or B-symptoms.
   - LDH elevated, uric acid elevated, abnormal smear (blasts, atypical lymphocytes).
   - Immunocompromised host.

4. **Station-specific differential.**

   - **Anterior cervical:** drainage from oropharynx, thyroid, salivary glands. Most often reactive (URI, pharyngitis, dental). Persistent or hard → head/neck malignancy (especially with smoking, alcohol, HPV), thyroid cancer, lymphoma.
   - **Posterior cervical:** EBV (mononucleosis), toxoplasmosis, lymphoma, scalp infection, NPC (Asian endemic).
   - **Supraclavicular:**
     - Right: mediastinum, lung, esophagus.
     - Left (Virchow): abdominal (gastric, pancreatic, ovarian, testicular), thoracic.
     - Always pursue biopsy for persistent supraclavicular adenopathy.
   - **Axillary:** breast (consider in women — breast exam, mammography, MRI), upper extremity infection (cat scratch, sporotrichosis), melanoma of arm/hand, lymphoma.
   - **Epitrochlear:** distal upper extremity infection, secondary syphilis, sarcoidosis, lymphoma, HIV.
   - **Mediastinal/hilar (CT):** lymphoma, sarcoidosis, TB, fungal, lung cancer, metastatic disease.
   - **Inguinal:** lower extremity, perineal, anorectal, GU infections; STIs (syphilis, chancroid, LGV); melanoma; lymphoma less common here.
   - **Mesenteric/retroperitoneal (CT):** lymphoma, GI malignancy, TB, mesenteric adenitis (Yersinia in younger patients mimicking appendicitis).

5. **Major causes by mechanism.**

   - **Infection:**
     - Viral: EBV, CMV (mono syndrome), HIV (acute or chronic), measles, rubella, parvovirus B19.
     - Bacterial: streptococcal/staph (acute pyogenic, often tender), cat scratch (Bartonella henselae), tularemia, brucellosis, syphilis (primary inguinal, secondary generalized), Lyme, leptospirosis.
     - Mycobacterial: TB (scrofula, often cervical, cold abscess), atypical mycobacterial.
     - Fungal: histo, cocci (endemic regions), cryptococcal in immunocompromised.
     - Parasitic: toxoplasmosis (posterior cervical), filariasis.
     - Spirochetal: syphilis as above.

   - **Malignancy:**
     - Lymphoma (Hodgkin and non-Hodgkin) — typically painless, rubbery, may grow over weeks. Hodgkin: contiguous spread, mediastinal involvement, alcohol-induced node pain. NHL: variable.
     - Leukemia (CLL especially in older adults).
     - Metastatic solid tumor — head/neck, thyroid, breast, lung, GI, GU, melanoma.

   - **Autoimmune / inflammatory:**
     - SLE, RA, dermatomyositis.
     - Sarcoidosis (bilateral hilar, often with erythema nodosum).
     - Castleman disease.
     - IgG4-related disease.
     - Kikuchi-Fujimoto disease (young women, cervical, fever, self-limiting).
     - Adult-onset Still disease.

   - **Drug-induced:** phenytoin, carbamazepine, allopurinol, sulfonamides — DRESS; pseudolymphoma.

   - **Storage diseases:** Gaucher, Niemann-Pick (rare).

6. **Initial testing tier.**
   - CBC with diff and peripheral smear (atypical lymphocytes — mono; blasts — leukemia; smudge cells — CLL).
   - LDH (lymphoma marker), uric acid (high turnover).
   - LFTs, BMP, ESR/CRP.
   - HIV (Ag/Ab + viral load if positive), monospot or EBV serology, CMV IgM, RPR, IGRA or PPD, hepatitis B/C serologies.
   - ANA if autoimmune features.
   - Bartonella titers if cat scratch suspected.
   - Throat culture if pharyngitis.
   - Toxoplasma IgM if posterior cervical.

7. **Imaging.**
   - **Ultrasound** of node: features favoring reactive (preserved fatty hilum, oval shape, hilar vascularity), features favoring malignancy (loss of hilum, round shape, peripheral vascularity, microcalcifications, cortical thickening, irregular margin).
   - **CT chest/abdomen/pelvis** if generalized adenopathy, supraclavicular node, B-symptoms, suspected lymphoma, or to identify primary tumor.
   - **PET-CT** for staging known lymphoma or for FUO with adenopathy.
   - **MRI / mammography** as primary site directed.

8. **Biopsy decision.**
   - **Observe** localized cervical/axillary/inguinal nodes <2 cm, soft, mobile, recently developed in setting of clear infection, in young patient, without B-symptoms — for 4–6 weeks. If not resolving or worsening → biopsy.
   - **Biopsy promptly** (excisional preferred for lymphoma):
     - Supraclavicular node any size.
     - Node ≥2 cm persisting >4 weeks without explanation.
     - Hard, fixed, matted nodes.
     - Generalized adenopathy without infectious cause.
     - B-symptoms with adenopathy.
     - Hepatosplenomegaly with adenopathy.
     - Cytopenia or hyperlymphocytosis.
     - Immunocompromised host.
   - **FNA / core needle** acceptable for suspected metastatic solid tumor or recurrence; but excisional biopsy preferred for primary lymphoma diagnosis (architecture matters).

9. **Pediatric considerations.**
   - Reactive cervical nodes very common in children; observe most.
   - Concerning features in children: supraclavicular, fixed, hard, >2 cm, growing despite antibiotics, B-symptoms, hepatosplenomegaly, cytopenia.
   - Common infections: EBV, CMV, cat scratch, mycobacterial.

## Output Format

```
NODE CHARACTERIZATION:
- Location, size, consistency, mobility, tenderness, duration

LOCALIZED vs GENERALIZED:
- Stations involved

CONSTITUTIONAL / B-SYMPTOMS:
- Fevers, night sweats, weight loss, pruritus

EXPOSURES / RISK FACTORS:
- Animal, sexual, travel, occupational, drugs

EXAM (full station + organomegaly):
- Spleen, liver, skin, primary site

DIFFERENTIAL (ranked, station-specific):
1. [most likely with reasoning]
2. [second]
3. [third]

STAGE 1 WORKUP:
- Labs (specific tests by station/exposure)
- Smear review
- Ultrasound

STAGE 2 / BIOPSY:
- Excisional vs core needle vs FNA
- CT/PET-CT if indicated

WORKING DIAGNOSIS / TREATMENT:
- Targeted therapy by cause
- Empirical antibiotic only if infection clear (avoid empiric for unclear node)

DISPOSITION:
- Observe with reassessment in [time]
- Specialty referral (heme/onc, surgery, ID)
```

## Worked Example

**Input:** 32 y/o F, no significant PMH, presents with painless right supraclavicular node first noticed 3 weeks ago, now ~2.5 cm, firm, mobile but rubbery. Reports drenching night sweats × 6 weeks, 5 kg weight loss, occasional low-grade fevers. No URI, no exposures, sexually monogamous, no IV drugs. Exam: 2.5 cm right supraclavicular node, firm-rubbery; smaller ~1 cm right cervical and left axillary nodes; spleen tip palpable 2 cm below costal margin; no skin findings; no other abnormalities. Labs: WBC 9.8, Hgb 12.5, platelets 320, LDH 425 (ULN 220), ALT 32, ESR 64, HIV negative, EBV IgG positive IgM negative, CMV IgM negative.

**Output:**

```
NODE CHARACTERIZATION:
- 2.5 cm right supraclavicular, firm-rubbery, painless, persistent 3 weeks. Multiple smaller nodes elsewhere.

LOCALIZED vs GENERALIZED:
- Generalized (right supraclavicular + right cervical + left axillary).

CONSTITUTIONAL / B-SYMPTOMS:
- All three present: drenching night sweats, weight loss ≥10%, low-grade fevers (B-symptoms).

EXPOSURES / RISK FACTORS:
- None concerning. EBV remote past infection (IgG+, IgM−). HIV negative.

EXAM:
- Splenomegaly. No skin lesions. No primary site identified.

DIFFERENTIAL (ranked):
1. Hodgkin lymphoma — classic age, supraclavicular and contiguous nodes, B-symptoms, splenomegaly, elevated LDH and ESR. Most likely.
2. Non-Hodgkin lymphoma — generalized adenopathy with B-symptoms, splenomegaly. Possible.
3. Metastatic solid tumor — supraclavicular node concerning; less likely at 32 without other risk factors.
4. Sarcoidosis with peripheral involvement — possible but less classic with B-symptoms and LDH elevation.
5. TB / atypical mycobacterial — less likely without exposure but obtain IGRA.

STAGE 1 WORKUP:
- CBC with diff and smear (already done — no blasts/atypical reported); add reticulocyte count, beta-2 microglobulin.
- LDH 425 elevated (already obtained).
- Uric acid, calcium, phosphorus.
- HIV (already neg), hepatitis B/C, RPR.
- EBV serologies (already showed remote exposure), monospot.
- IGRA.
- ESR 64 (elevated).

STAGE 2 / BIOPSY:
- **Excisional biopsy of the right supraclavicular node** — required for lymphoma diagnosis (architecture, immunohistochemistry, flow cytometry, FISH). Avoid FNA-only for first lymphoma diagnosis.
- CT chest/abdomen/pelvis with contrast for staging.
- PET-CT for staging once lymphoma confirmed (also evaluates marrow involvement).
- Bone marrow biopsy after pathology results if lymphoma confirmed (selectively per modern guidelines: PET-CT may obviate marrow in HL).
- Echocardiogram pre-chemotherapy (anthracyclines).
- Pulmonary function tests pre-chemotherapy (bleomycin in ABVD).
- Pregnancy test before staging imaging.
- Fertility preservation discussion before chemotherapy (oncofertility consult).

WORKING DIAGNOSIS / TREATMENT:
- Working: lymphoma (Hodgkin most likely) with constitutional symptoms — needs tissue diagnosis.
- Hold any empiric steroids until tissue obtained (steroids alter pathology).
- Maintain hydration; allopurinol 300 mg daily before treatment to reduce tumor lysis risk.
- Hematology/oncology referral within 1 week.

DISPOSITION:
- Expedite excisional biopsy and staging imaging this week.
- Patient counseled on disease likelihood (without giving definitive diagnosis pre-biopsy), staging, treatment paradigm, fertility considerations.
- Outpatient management; admit only if SVC syndrome, large mediastinal mass, airway compromise, severe symptoms, or tumor lysis risk.
- Follow-up in 1 week or sooner with biopsy results to plan therapy.
```
