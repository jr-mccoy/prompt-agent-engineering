## Objective

Work up the patient with rash: prioritize identification of life-threatening dermatoses, classify the eruption morphologically (primary lesion type, distribution, mucosa, systemic features), and reach a working diagnosis with treatment.

## Inputs

- Onset (acute hours to days vs subacute vs chronic), tempo, evolution, prior similar
- Distribution: localized (dermatomal, photo, intertriginous, acral, scalp, palms/soles, mucosa) vs generalized; symmetry; sun-exposed; flexural/extensor; sparing patterns
- Primary lesion morphology: macule (flat, color change), papule (raised <1 cm), nodule (>1 cm raised), plaque (raised flat-topped >1 cm), vesicle (clear fluid <1 cm), bulla (>1 cm), pustule, wheal (urticaria), petechiae/purpura
- Secondary lesions: scale, crust, erosion, ulcer, lichenification, atrophy, scar
- Color: erythematous, violaceous, blanching vs non-blanching (purpura), hyperpigmented, hypopigmented
- Mucosa: oral, conjunctival, genital
- Symptoms: pruritus, pain, burning, fever, malaise, myalgia, joint pain
- Triggers: new medication (timing days-weeks), infection (fever, sore throat), travel, tick exposure, sexual exposure, occupational, allergens (food, plant, contact), sun exposure, stress
- Drug history with start/stop dates within 8 weeks; herbal/supplement use
- Comorbidities: immunosuppression, malignancy, autoimmune disease, atopy, diabetes
- Systemic features: hemodynamic instability, mucosal involvement, lymphadenopathy, hepatosplenomegaly, eosinophilia, organ dysfunction
- Vitals (fever, hypotension), full skin/mucosal/nail/hair exam
- Labs as indicated: CBC with diff and eosinophils, BMP, LFTs, ESR/CRP, blood cultures, ANA, complement, hepatitis serologies, HIV, syphilis, KOH, tzanck smear, biopsy

## Reasoning Steps

1. **First sweep — life-threatening dermatoses.**

   - **Anaphylaxis:** urticaria + angioedema + airway/respiratory/cardiovascular involvement after exposure. **Epinephrine IM 0.3–0.5 mg (0.01 mg/kg pediatric) into anterolateral thigh; repeat q5–15 min** as needed; IVF, supplemental O2, H1 (diphenhydramine 25–50 mg) and H2 (famotidine 20 mg) blockers, glucocorticoid (methylprednisolone 125 mg IV), continuous monitoring for biphasic reaction. ICU admission for severe.

   - **Stevens-Johnson syndrome / toxic epidermal necrolysis (SJS/TEN):** flu-like prodrome 1–3 days → painful red macules, target lesions, dusky erythema progressing to vesicles/bullae and skin sloughing; mucosal involvement (oral, ocular, genital) ≥2 sites in nearly all. SJS <10% BSA, TEN >30%, overlap 10–30%. Most common drug triggers: allopurinol, sulfa, anticonvulsants (lamotrigine, carbamazepine, phenytoin), NSAIDs (oxicams), antibiotics (penicillins, cephalosporins, fluoroquinolones), nevirapine. SCORTEN to predict mortality. **Stop offending drug immediately**; transfer to burn unit/ICU; supportive care (fluids, electrolytes, wound care, nutrition, infection prevention, ophthalmology); IVIG, cyclosporine, etanercept evidence varied.

   - **DRESS (drug reaction with eosinophilia and systemic symptoms) / DIHS:** 2–8 weeks after drug start. Diffuse morbilliform eruption + facial edema + fever + lymphadenopathy + organ involvement (hepatitis most common, also kidney, lung, heart) + eosinophilia + atypical lymphocytes. Triggers: anticonvulsants (carbamazepine, phenytoin, lamotrigine, phenobarbital), allopurinol, sulfasalazine, dapsone, vancomycin, minocycline, abacavir. HHV-6 reactivation. **Stop drug**, systemic corticosteroids prednisone 1 mg/kg/day with slow taper, organ support; risk of late autoimmune sequelae (thyroiditis, T1DM, AIHA).

   - **Acute generalized exanthematous pustulosis (AGEP):** sudden widespread non-follicular sterile pustules on erythematous background with fever; days after drug (commonly antibiotics — beta-lactams, macrolides, terbinafine, diltiazem, hydroxychloroquine). Resolves in 2 weeks after stopping drug; supportive care.

   - **Meningococcemia:** non-blanching purpura/petechiae rapidly evolving in patient with fever, hypotension, AMS. **Empiric ceftriaxone 2 g IV, plus dexamethasone if meningitis suspected**, droplet precautions, contact prophylaxis (rifampin, ciprofloxacin, ceftriaxone) for close contacts. Notify public health.

   - **Necrotizing fasciitis:** rapidly progressing severe pain out of proportion to skin findings, dusky discoloration, bullae, crepitus, systemic toxicity. **Surgical emergency — immediate debridement**, broad-spectrum antibiotics (vancomycin + piperacillin-tazobactam OR vancomycin + meropenem + clindamycin), supportive care; LRINEC score supports but does not exclude.

   - **Toxic shock syndrome (staphylococcal or streptococcal):** fever, hypotension, diffuse macular erythroderma (sunburn-like) with later desquamation (palms/soles), multiorgan involvement. Streptococcal often with deep tissue infection. Source control (remove tampon, packing; debride necrotizing soft tissue infection); vancomycin + clindamycin (toxin suppression) + piperacillin-tazobactam; IVIG for severe streptococcal TSS.

   - **Rocky Mountain spotted fever:** fever, headache, myalgia followed by petechial rash starting on wrists/ankles spreading centripetally to palms/soles. Tick exposure in endemic areas. Mortality high if untreated. **Empiric doxycycline 100 mg PO/IV BID** at any age (including children) — do not wait for confirmation. Other rickettsial illnesses similar approach.

   - **Disseminated intravascular coagulation / purpura fulminans:** widespread purpura with sepsis. Treat underlying sepsis; supportive (FFP, cryo, platelets) if bleeding.

   - **Pemphigus vulgaris / paraneoplastic pemphigus / bullous pemphigoid (severe):** mucosal involvement, fragile blisters, large body surface; can be life-threatening; dermatology urgently.

   - **Erythroderma:** generalized erythema involving >90% BSA from any cause (psoriasis, drug, eczema, CTCL, idiopathic); fluid/electrolyte/temperature dysregulation; admit, supportive, identify cause.

   - **Eczema herpeticum / herpes vegetans:** disseminated HSV in atopic/immunocompromised host; punched-out monomorphic erosions. Acyclovir 10 mg/kg IV q8h; ophthalmology if periocular.

   - **Smallpox / monkeypox / VZV in pregnancy / disseminated zoster in immunocompromised:** isolation, infectious disease and public health.

2. **Pattern recognition by primary lesion.**

   - **Macular / morbilliform** (drug eruption, viral exanthem, syphilis secondary, toxic erythema):
     - Simple morbilliform drug eruption: 7–14 days after new drug, generalized blanching macules/papules, mild pruritus, no systemic features → stop drug, antihistamines, topical steroid; observe for evolution (DRESS, SJS).
     - Viral exanthem: measles, rubella, parvovirus B19 (slapped cheek), HHV-6 (roseola), enterovirus, COVID, EBV — supportive; vaccination history matters.
     - Secondary syphilis: copper-colored macules including palms/soles with constitutional symptoms, condyloma lata, mucous patches; RPR/VDRL; benzathine penicillin G 2.4 million units IM.

   - **Urticaria / wheals:** transient (<24 h per lesion), pruritic, blanching. Acute (<6 weeks) often viral, food, drug; chronic (≥6 weeks) often idiopathic. H1 antihistamine (cetirizine 10 mg, fexofenadine 180 mg, loratadine 10 mg) up to 4× daily; H2 add-on; omalizumab for refractory chronic spontaneous urticaria.
     - **Urticarial vasculitis:** lasts >24 h, leaves bruising, painful — biopsy.

   - **Papular / papulosquamous:**
     - Psoriasis: well-demarcated erythematous plaques with silvery scale, extensor surfaces (elbows, knees), scalp, intergluteal, nails (pitting, oil drops, onycholysis); psoriatic arthritis association. Topical steroids, vitamin D analogs, phototherapy, methotrexate, biologics (IL-17, IL-23, TNF inhibitors).
     - Lichen planus: pruritic violaceous polygonal flat-topped papules, Wickham striae, mucosal involvement; consider hepatitis C; topical steroid.
     - Pityriasis rosea: herald patch + Christmas-tree distribution; self-limited.
     - Tinea corporis: annular scaly plaques with central clearing; KOH; topical or oral antifungal.
     - Eczema/atopic dermatitis: pruritic flexural lichenified plaques; topical steroid, emollients, dupilumab for severe.

   - **Vesicular / bullous:**
     - Herpes zoster: dermatomal vesicles on erythematous base; pain often precedes; valacyclovir 1 g PO TID × 7 days within 72 h. Postherpetic neuralgia management. Disseminated in immunocompromised.
     - Herpes simplex: grouped vesicles, recurrent.
     - Varicella: generalized in different stages of evolution; immunocompromised treat; otherwise supportive in immunocompetent.
     - Bullous pemphigoid: tense bullae, elderly; immunofluorescence (linear IgG/C3 along basement membrane).
     - Pemphigus vulgaris: flaccid bullae, mucosal; intercellular IgG; immunosuppression.
     - Bullous impetigo (Staph), SSSS in young children.
     - Contact dermatitis: linear/geometric distribution by exposure (poison ivy).

   - **Pustular:**
     - Acne, folliculitis, hidradenitis suppurativa.
     - Acute generalized exanthematous pustulosis (above).
     - Pustular psoriasis.

   - **Petechiae / purpura:**
     - Non-palpable: thrombocytopenia (ITP, TTP, HIT, leukemia, drug), trauma, vascular fragility, scurvy.
     - Palpable: vasculitis (IgA vasculitis/HSP, ANCA, hypersensitivity), embolic (endocarditis, atheroemboli), DIC.
     - Retiform purpura: vascular occlusion (calciphylaxis, antiphospholipid, warfarin necrosis, cryoglobulinemia, levamisole-tainted cocaine).

   - **Erythematous patches/plaques with photosensitivity:**
     - SLE (malar rash sparing nasolabial folds), subacute cutaneous LE, dermatomyositis (Gottron papules, heliotrope rash, shawl sign), porphyria cutanea tarda, drug photosensitivity.

   - **Targetoid lesions:**
     - Erythema multiforme: typical 3-zone target, often HSV-triggered; minor mucosal in EM major.
     - Distinguish from SJS by pattern (atypical targets, mucosa, sloughing).

3. **Targeted workup.**
   - CBC with diff (eosinophilia in DRESS, atypical lymphs in viral, leukocytosis in infection), BMP, LFTs (DRESS hepatitis), ESR/CRP, blood cultures (sepsis), urinalysis (vasculitis), coags + d-dimer (DIC), HIV/syphilis, hepatitis serologies, ANA reflex (lupus, dermatomyositis), complement, ANCA, cryoglobulins, beta-2 GP1/anticardiolipin (vascular).
   - KOH for fungal scrapings.
   - Tzanck smear (multinucleated giant cells in HSV/VZV) — outdated where PCR available; viral PCR direct.
   - Skin biopsy (4 mm punch typical) for unknown serious eruption — H&E and DIF (direct immunofluorescence) for autoimmune blistering.
   - Patch testing for chronic contact dermatitis.
   - Imaging if necrotizing fasciitis or septic complications.

4. **Disposition.**
   - Life-threatening (anaphylaxis, SJS/TEN, DRESS, meningococcemia, necrotizing fasciitis, severe TSS) → ICU / burn unit / surgery / ID.
   - Severe non-life-threatening with systemic features → admission, dermatology consult.
   - Stable rash with reassuring exam → outpatient with dermatology follow-up.
   - Provide patient with photographs of expected evolution, return precautions (mucosal, skin sloughing, fever, hemodynamic symptoms).

## Output Format

```
LIFE-THREAT SCREEN:
- SJS/TEN, DRESS, AGEP, anaphylaxis, meningococcemia, necrotizing fasciitis, RMSF, TSS, erythroderma, eczema herpeticum
- Findings supporting / excluding each

PRIMARY LESION + DISTRIBUTION:
- Morphology, sites, mucosa, systemic features

DRUG / EXPOSURE TIMELINE:
- Drugs in past 8 weeks with start dates
- Infection, travel, contact, occupation

DIFFERENTIAL (ranked):
1. [most likely with reasoning]
2. [second]
3. [third]

WORKUP:
- Labs (CBC, eos, LFTs, BMP, etc.)
- Cultures (blood, skin, viral PCR)
- Biopsy (H&E, DIF)
- Imaging if soft tissue concern

WORKING DIAGNOSIS / TREATMENT:
- Stop offending agent
- Empiric / targeted therapy with drug, dose, duration
- Supportive care (wound, mucosal, ophthalmology)
- Specialty consult (derm, ID, burn, ophtho, surgery)

DISPOSITION:
- Discharge / floor / ICU / burn unit
- Photographs + warning signs
```

## Worked Example

**Input:** 42 y/o F, started lamotrigine 4 weeks ago for bipolar disorder maintenance, presents with 5 days of progressive fever to 38.8°C, diffuse morbilliform rash starting on chest and spreading to extremities, facial edema, malaise, sore throat. No skin sloughing. Mild oral mucositis without erosion. Lymphadenopathy in cervical and axillary chains. Exam: confluent erythematous papular rash 80% BSA, no Nikolsky sign, no target lesions, no purpura. Labs: WBC 14 with 22% eosinophils and 5% atypical lymphocytes, ALT 285, AST 240, Cr 1.4, BMP otherwise normal.

**Output:**

```
LIFE-THREAT SCREEN:
- DRESS / DIHS — strongly supported: timing 4 weeks after lamotrigine, fever, morbilliform eruption, facial edema, lymphadenopathy, eosinophilia, atypical lymphocytes, hepatitis (ALT/AST elevated), mild renal involvement.
- SJS/TEN — less likely (no skin sloughing, no Nikolsky, no significant mucosal erosion, no targetoid lesions).
- AGEP — less likely (no pustules).
- Meningococcemia — no purpura, no hemodynamic instability.
- Anaphylaxis — no airway/circulatory.
- Erythroderma — significant BSA but pattern more morbilliform with systemic features → DRESS.

PRIMARY LESION + DISTRIBUTION:
- Confluent morbilliform/maculopapular eruption ~80% BSA, facial edema, mild oral mucositis without erosion, no bullae or sloughing, generalized lymphadenopathy.

DRUG / EXPOSURE TIMELINE:
- Lamotrigine started 4 weeks ago — classic DRESS window. Highest-yield culprit.
- No other new drugs reported.

DIFFERENTIAL (ranked):
1. DRESS (drug reaction with eosinophilia and systemic symptoms) from lamotrigine — most likely.
2. Viral exanthem with hepatitis (e.g., EBV, CMV, HIV seroconversion) — possible mimic; eosinophilia favors DRESS.
3. SJS/TEN evolving — currently no sloughing but monitor closely.

WORKUP:
- CBC with diff (eosinophil count, atypical lymph), peripheral smear.
- BMP, LFTs (already abnormal), CK, lipase (organ involvement screen), TSH (autoimmune sequelae baseline), urinalysis, troponin and ECG (myocarditis screen).
- HHV-6 PCR (reactivation supports DRESS).
- HIV (Ag/Ab + viral load if positive), hepatitis A/B/C serologies, EBV/CMV PCR.
- Blood cultures × 2 if febrile and ill-appearing.
- Skin biopsy (H&E + DIF) for confirmation.
- Re-evaluate skin daily for evolution toward SJS/TEN.

WORKING DIAGNOSIS / TREATMENT:
- DRESS due to lamotrigine.
- **Stop lamotrigine immediately** and place in patient's allergy list with severity ("severe — DRESS"). Avoid all aromatic anticonvulsants (carbamazepine, phenytoin, phenobarbital) given cross-reactivity (~75%). Coordinate alternative bipolar maintenance with psychiatry.
- **Systemic corticosteroids:** prednisone 1 mg/kg/day (or methylprednisolone IV equivalent if oral intolerance), slow taper over 8–12 weeks to prevent relapse. Watch for HHV-6 reactivation and infection during steroid taper.
- Topical mid-potency steroids for skin symptoms, emollients.
- Supportive: IV fluids, analgesia, antihistamine for pruritus, gentle skin care.
- Hepatology consult for hepatitis monitoring; LFTs daily.
- Nephrology if renal worsens.
- Ophthalmology if any ocular involvement.
- Daily skin exam for SJS/TEN evolution; if appearing, escalate care.
- Long-term follow-up for autoimmune sequelae (thyroiditis, T1DM, AIHA, hepatitis flare) for at least 6–12 months — TSH, glucose, CBC at intervals.

DISPOSITION:
- Admit to ward (ICU/burn unit if hemodynamic instability, severe organ involvement, or evolving SJS/TEN).
- Dermatology consultation immediately.
- Patient education: severity of reaction, lifelong avoidance of lamotrigine and cross-reactive drugs, MedicAlert bracelet, family/caregiver awareness.
- Follow-up in dermatology, hepatology, and primary care after discharge.
```
