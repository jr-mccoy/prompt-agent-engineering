## Objective

Work up joint pain: classify inflammatory vs mechanical, monoarticular vs oligo/polyarticular, identify and rule out septic arthritis, sample synovial fluid when indicated, and reach a working rheumatologic diagnosis.

## Inputs

- Pattern: monoarticular vs oligoarticular (2–4) vs polyarticular (≥5); symmetric vs asymmetric; small vs large joint distribution; axial vs peripheral; migratory vs additive
- Inflammatory features: morning stiffness ≥30–60 min that improves with activity, swelling, warmth, erythema, systemic symptoms
- Mechanical features: pain worse with use, brief stiffness on starting (gel phenomenon), no systemic features
- Onset and tempo: acute (hours-days) vs subacute (weeks) vs chronic (months)
- Triggers: trauma, recent infection (GI, GU, URI), tick bite, IV drug use, recent procedure, sexual exposure (gonococcal), travel
- Associated: rash, fever, oral/genital ulcers, nail changes, dactylitis, enthesitis, uveitis/conjunctivitis/iritis, low back pain (inflammatory), Raynaud, sicca, alopecia, pleuritic pain, hematuria, GI symptoms (IBD), psoriasis, family history
- Vitals (fever), exam (joint count with swelling/tenderness, range of motion, effusion, deformity, nail pits, skin, mucosa, eye, lymph nodes)
- Labs: CBC, BMP, CRP, ESR, uric acid, RF, anti-CCP, ANA (with reflex if positive: anti-dsDNA, anti-Sm, anti-Ro/La, anti-RNP, anti-Scl70, anti-Jo1, anti-centromere), HLA-B27, complement, hepatitis B/C, HIV, parvovirus, TSH, Lyme, gonococcal NAAT, urinalysis
- Imaging: plain X-rays of involved joints; ultrasound (effusion, synovitis, erosions); MRI (early erosions, sacroiliitis, soft tissue)
- **Arthrocentesis** for any monoarthritis with suspicion of infection or crystal disease; send cell count + diff, gram stain + culture, crystals (compensated polarized microscopy)

## Reasoning Steps

1. **Inflammatory vs mechanical.**
   - **Inflammatory:** morning stiffness ≥30–60 min, improves with use, swelling, warmth, redness, systemic features, elevated CRP/ESR, polyarticular often.
   - **Mechanical (OA, internal derangement):** worse with use, end-of-day pain, brief gel stiffness, crepitus, normal inflammatory markers.

2. **Acute monoarthritis — septic until proven otherwise.**
   - **Septic arthritis:** medical emergency. Most common organism Staph aureus (including MRSA), then streptococci. Risk factors: prosthetic joint, immunosuppression, IV drug use, diabetes, RA on biologics, recent joint procedure, advanced age, prior septic joint, skin breakdown. Synovial WBC >50,000 with PMN predominance is suggestive (>100,000 highly so, but <50,000 does not exclude).
     - **Arthrocentesis must precede antibiotics** unless patient is in shock and cannot wait.
     - Empiric antibiotics: vancomycin 15–20 mg/kg IV q8–12h (target trough 15–20 or AUC 400–600) + ceftriaxone 1–2 g IV daily. Tailor to gram stain / culture / patient factors. Consider gonococcal coverage in young, sexually active patients.
     - **Surgical drainage** (arthroscopic washout or open) with orthopedics consult.
     - Prosthetic joint: orthopedics for surgical management; consider DAIR, single-stage or two-stage exchange.
   - **Gonococcal arthritis:** young, sexually active; classic triad of tenosynovitis, dermatitis, polyarthralgia (disseminated form), or purulent monoarthritis. Synovial culture often negative; NAAT of urethra/cervix/throat/rectum sensitive. Ceftriaxone 1 g IV/IM daily × 7+ days; co-treat for chlamydia (azithromycin 1 g once or doxycycline 100 mg BID × 7 days).
   - **Crystal arthropathy:**
     - **Gout:** monosodium urate crystals — needle-shaped, negatively birefringent (yellow when parallel to compensator). First MTP (podagra) classic; ankle, knee, midfoot. Risk: men >women (post-menopausal women catch up), alcohol (especially beer), purine-rich diet, fructose, diuretics (thiazide, loop), chemo / tumor lysis, CKD, obesity, transplant, cyclosporine.
       - Acute treatment options:
         - **NSAIDs** (naproxen 500 mg BID, indomethacin 50 mg TID; avoid in CKD, HF, GI bleed risk).
         - **Colchicine 1.2 mg PO once, then 0.6 mg one hour later, then 0.6 mg BID until resolution** (within 36 h of attack onset).
         - **Glucocorticoids:** prednisone 30–40 mg PO × 5 days (good for CKD or NSAID/colchicine intolerance); intra-articular triamcinolone for monoarthritis.
         - **IL-1 antagonist (anakinra, canakinumab)** for refractory.
       - Urate-lowering therapy (after acute attack settled, with anti-inflammatory bridging):
         - **Allopurinol** start 100 mg daily, titrate every 2–5 weeks to serum urate <6 mg/dL (<5 in tophaceous). Caution HLA-B*5801 (Han Chinese, Korean, Thai) — test before starting; SCAR risk. Reduce starting dose in CKD.
         - **Febuxostat** 40–80 mg daily (cardiac safety signals — CARES, FAST; use if intolerant or refractory to allopurinol).
         - **Probenecid** uricosuric for under-excretors with normal renal function and no nephrolithiasis.
         - **Pegloticase** for refractory tophaceous gout.
         - Bridge with colchicine 0.6 mg daily-BID or NSAID for 3–6 months when starting urate-lowering therapy.
     - **Calcium pyrophosphate (CPPD / pseudogout):** rhomboid crystals, weakly positively birefringent. Knees, wrists, MCP commonly. Chondrocalcinosis on imaging. Acute attacks treated like gout (NSAIDs, colchicine, steroids). Triggers: trauma, surgery, illness; consider hemochromatosis, hyperPTH, hypoMg, hypothyroidism in patients <60 with CPPD.
     - **Basic calcium phosphate (Milwaukee shoulder)** large joint hemorrhagic effusions in elderly women.

3. **Polyarticular inflammatory arthritis.**

   - **Rheumatoid arthritis:** symmetric, small joints (MCPs, PIPs, MTPs, wrists), morning stiffness >1 h, RF and/or anti-CCP positive in ~70%, ESR/CRP elevated. Erosions, juxta-articular osteopenia, ulnar deviation, swan-neck, boutonnière. ACR/EULAR 2010 criteria.
     - Treat-to-target with DMARDs: methotrexate 7.5–25 mg weekly with folic acid; combination (HCQ, sulfasalazine — triple therapy) if needed; biologics (TNF inhibitors, abatacept, tocilizumab, rituximab) and JAK inhibitors for inadequate response.
     - Bridge with low-dose prednisone or NSAIDs while DMARDs take effect.
     - Vaccinate (pneumococcal, influenza, zoster, HBV) before immunosuppression; screen TB and HBV.

   - **Seronegative spondyloarthritis:** axial involvement, asymmetric oligoarthritis (lower extremity), enthesitis (Achilles, plantar fascia), dactylitis ("sausage digit"), uveitis, IBD, psoriasis, HLA-B27.
     - **Ankylosing spondylitis:** inflammatory low back pain, sacroiliitis on imaging, restricted spine motion. NSAIDs first-line; TNF inhibitors and IL-17 inhibitors for inadequate response.
     - **Psoriatic arthritis:** psoriasis, nail changes (pitting, onycholysis), DIP involvement, dactylitis, enthesitis. Methotrexate; TNFi, IL-17i, IL-23i, JAK inhibitors.
     - **Reactive arthritis:** post-infectious (Chlamydia, Salmonella, Shigella, Campylobacter, Yersinia), oligoarticular lower extremity, may have urethritis, conjunctivitis ("can't see, can't pee, can't climb a tree"). Self-limited often; NSAIDs; treat triggering infection.
     - **Enteropathic arthritis** (with IBD): peripheral parallels disease activity; axial independent.

   - **Connective tissue disease:**
     - **SLE:** ACR/EULAR criteria; ANA positive nearly always; dsDNA, Sm specific; complement low; multiorgan. Hydroxychloroquine cornerstone; steroids; immunosuppressants (mycophenolate, azathioprine, methotrexate, cyclophosphamide for severe organ involvement, belimumab, anifrolumab).
     - **Sjögren:** sicca symptoms, anti-Ro/La, lip biopsy.
     - **Systemic sclerosis:** Raynaud, skin tightening, ILD, GI dysmotility; anti-Scl70, anti-centromere, anti-RNA polymerase III; manage HTN crisis with ACE inhibitors.
     - **Mixed connective tissue disease:** anti-RNP.
     - **Polymyositis / dermatomyositis:** weakness, elevated CK, anti-Jo1, anti-Mi-2; cancer screening in dermatomyositis adult.
     - **Vasculitis:** see specific vasculitis prompts; ANCA, complement, eosinophil count.

   - **Polymyalgia rheumatica / GCA:** age >50, proximal stiffness, ESR very high, prompt steroid response.

   - **Viral arthritis:** parvovirus B19 (symmetric small-joint, often resolves), hepatitis B/C, HIV, CMV, EBV, alphavirus (chikungunya).

   - **Lyme arthritis:** large joint (knee), endemic exposure, oligoarticular, antibodies; treat doxycycline 100 mg BID × 28 days (or amoxicillin) for early; later disease may need IV ceftriaxone.

   - **Bacterial endocarditis with arthritis:** consider in IV drug user with arthralgia and fever.

   - **Sarcoid (Lofgren syndrome):** bilateral hilar adenopathy, erythema nodosum, ankle arthritis; often self-limited.

4. **Synovial fluid interpretation.**
   - **Non-inflammatory** (e.g., OA, trauma): WBC <2,000, PMN <25%, clear/yellow.
   - **Inflammatory** (RA, gout, CPPD, SpA, autoimmune): WBC 2,000–50,000 (often 10,000–50,000), PMN often >50%.
   - **Septic:** WBC typically >50,000 (>100,000 highly suggestive), PMN >75%, organisms on gram stain (50–60% sensitivity), positive culture (often >90%).
   - **Hemorrhagic:** trauma, anticoagulant, hemophilia, PVNS, charcot.
   - Always send: cell count + diff, gram stain + culture (aerobic; anaerobic if abscess), crystals.

5. **Imaging.**
   - X-rays: erosions (RA), chondrocalcinosis (CPPD), osteophytes/joint-space narrowing/sclerosis (OA), syndesmophytes/bamboo spine (AS), pencil-in-cup (PsA).
   - Ultrasound: synovitis, effusion, erosions, dynamic eval; can guide arthrocentesis.
   - MRI: early erosions, sacroiliitis (best modality for early SpA), soft-tissue/cartilage detail.

6. **Treatment principles.**
   - Treat to target in inflammatory arthritides; control disease activity with DMARDs or biologics rather than chronic steroids alone.
   - Pre-immunosuppression screening (TB, HBV, HCV, HIV).
   - Vaccinate.
   - Address comorbidities (cardiovascular risk elevated in RA, SLE, SpA).
   - Physical therapy, joint protection, weight management.
   - For OA: weight loss, exercise, physical therapy, topical NSAIDs preferred over oral, intra-articular steroid for flare, joint replacement for severe.

## Worked Example

**Input:** 56 y/o M, hypertension on hydrochlorothiazide, BMI 32, presents with sudden onset severe pain, swelling, redness of right first MTP joint overnight; cannot bear weight. Reports two prior similar episodes in same joint resolving in 5–7 days. T 37.6, BP 142/86, HR 88. Exam: right first MTP swollen, erythematous, exquisitely tender; no other joint involvement, no skin lesions, no nail changes, no lymphadenopathy. CBC: WBC 11.2, otherwise unremarkable. Cr 1.2, uric acid 9.2 mg/dL. ESR 38, CRP 22. Arthrocentesis attempted: 0.5 mL clear yellow fluid, WBC 28,000 with 78% PMN, gram stain negative, polarized microscopy: needle-shaped, negatively birefringent crystals. Culture pending.

**Output:**

```
PATTERN:
- Acute monoarthritis, first MTP, recurrent.
- Inflammatory (warm, swollen, erythematous, recurrent classic for crystal disease).

ASSOCIATED FEATURES:
- No skin/nail/eye/mucosal/GI/GU.
- Risk factors: HTN on thiazide (hyperuricemia inducer), BMI 32, age 56 male, recurrent typical podagra.

SEPTIC SCREEN:
- Low pretest given recurrent prior gout, classic location (podagra), no immunosuppression.
- Synovial WBC 28,000 (inflammatory range, not typical septic >50,000 but cannot exclude solely on count), gram stain negative.
- Cover empirically only if any clinical concern (toxic, fevers, broken skin, prosthetic, IDU). In this case hold antibiotics and await culture; treat as gout.

DIFFERENTIAL (ranked):
1. Acute gout (monosodium urate) — confirmed by needle-shaped, negatively birefringent crystals; classic podagra; recurrent; risk factors.
2. Septic arthritis — keep on radar pending culture; low pretest.
3. CPPD / pseudogout — crystal morphology is wrong (rhomboid, positively birefringent); excluded.

WORKUP:
- Arthrocentesis done — confirmed gout.
- Cultures pending; will reassess in 24–48 h.
- Imaging not necessary acutely; baseline X-ray of foot for tophi or erosions if recurrent.
- Comprehensive labs: lipids, HbA1c, BMP (already done), LFT, urinalysis (urate stones).

WORKING DIAGNOSIS / TREATMENT:
- Acute gout flare.
- **Acute therapy:**
  - Colchicine 1.2 mg PO once now, 0.6 mg in 1 hour, then 0.6 mg BID until resolution (avoid combining with strong CYP3A4/P-gp inhibitors; reduce dose in CKD; max 7 days).
  - Or naproxen 500 mg PO BID × 5–7 days with PPI (caution with HTN, normal renal function; ensure no contraindication).
  - Or prednisone 30–40 mg PO daily × 5 days, taper if longer.
  - Intra-articular triamcinolone 10–20 mg also acceptable for monoarthritis.
- **Discontinue HCTZ** if alternative antihypertensive feasible (losartan also has mild uricosuric effect — good substitute).
- **Lifestyle:** weight loss, limit alcohol (especially beer), reduce purine-rich foods (organ meats, shellfish), reduce fructose-sweetened beverages, hydration.
- **Urate-lowering therapy (ULT):** indicated given ≥2 prior flares plus recurrent presentation.
  - Wait until acute flare resolved (or start during flare with anti-inflammatory bridge per recent ACR guidance).
  - Allopurinol 100 mg daily, titrate every 2–5 weeks (by 100 mg increments) to serum urate <6 mg/dL (<5 if tophi). Reduce starting dose to 50 mg in CKD.
  - HLA-B*5801 testing in high-risk ethnicities before starting.
  - Bridge anti-inflammatory: colchicine 0.6 mg daily-BID or low-dose NSAID for 3–6 months.
- Patient education: continue ULT lifelong; do not stop during a flare; expect possible early flare frequency.

DISPOSITION:
- Outpatient.
- Follow-up in 2 weeks for response and to start ULT once flare resolved.
- Repeat uric acid in 2–4 weeks after ULT initiation; titrate.
- Cardiovascular risk reduction (HTN, BMI, lipids) is part of comprehensive gout management.
- Return precautions: fevers, persistent unresolved symptoms, multiple joint involvement, signs of systemic illness → reassess for septic arthritis or alternative.
```
