## Objective

Work up acute kidney injury: confirm and stage by KDIGO, classify into pre-renal, intrinsic renal, or post-renal, identify specific etiology with urine microscopy and indices, manage cause-specific therapy, and identify indications for renal replacement therapy.

## Inputs

- Serum creatinine: current and baseline; calculate change and time course
- Urine output (accurate I/O for ≥6–24 h)
- KDIGO staging:
  - Stage 1: Cr ↑ ≥0.3 mg/dL within 48 h, or 1.5–1.9× baseline; UOP <0.5 mL/kg/h × 6–12 h
  - Stage 2: Cr 2.0–2.9× baseline; UOP <0.5 mL/kg/h × ≥12 h
  - Stage 3: Cr ≥3× baseline, or ≥4.0 mg/dL, or initiation of RRT, or eGFR <35 in <18 y; UOP <0.3 mL/kg/h × ≥24 h or anuria ≥12 h
- Symptoms: oliguria/anuria, edema, dyspnea, AMS, asterixis, uremic frost (rare/late), pruritus, nausea
- Volume status: orthostatics, JVD, edema, mucous membranes, IVC US
- Recent precipitants: hypotension/shock, sepsis, bleeding, GI losses, contrast exposure, surgery, nephrotoxins (NSAIDs, ACE/ARB, aminoglycosides, vancomycin, amphotericin, contrast, cisplatin, cyclosporine/tacrolimus, foscarnet, IV bisphosphonates), recent procedures, rhabdo trigger
- Comorbidities: CKD, HF, cirrhosis, malignancy, autoimmune (SLE, vasculitis, scleroderma), HTN, DM, multiple myeloma, BPH, prior stones
- Labs: BMP, BUN/Cr ratio, urinalysis with microscopy, urine Na, urine creatinine, urine osmolality, fractional excretion of sodium (FENa) and urea (FEUrea), CBC, LFTs, lactate, CK, urine protein/Cr ratio, coags, calcium, phos, uric acid, LDH, complement, ANCA, ANA, anti-GBM, hepatitis, HIV, SPEP/UPEP/free light chains
- Imaging: renal ultrasound (kidney size, hydronephrosis, echogenicity), bladder scan, CT non-contrast for obstruction or stones

## Reasoning Steps

1. **Confirm AKI and stage.** Compare to baseline; if baseline unknown, KDIGO Cr criteria apply with most recent value as referent. Record UOP carefully; oliguria changes prognosis and management.

2. **Classify pre/intra/post.**

   - **Pre-renal (most common, ~60%):**
     - Hypovolemia (GI losses, hemorrhage, burns, third-spacing).
     - Decreased effective circulating volume: HF, cirrhosis, sepsis, distributive shock, hepatorenal syndrome.
     - Renal artery / efferent arteriole vasoconstriction: NSAIDs (afferent constriction), ACE/ARB (efferent dilation — drops GFR in low-flow states), calcineurin inhibitors, contrast, sepsis-related.
     - Bilateral renal artery stenosis (or unilateral in solitary kidney) + ACE/ARB.
     - Urinalysis bland; FENa <1%, FEUrea <35%; urine osm >500; BUN/Cr ratio >20.
     - Reverses rapidly with restoration of perfusion (within 24–72 h).

   - **Intrinsic (~30%):**
     - **Acute tubular necrosis (ATN):** the most common intrinsic. Causes — prolonged pre-renal, sepsis, ischemia, nephrotoxins (aminoglycosides, vancomycin, amphotericin, cisplatin, contrast), pigment (myoglobin in rhabdo, hemoglobin). Urine: muddy brown granular casts, renal tubular epithelial cells. FENa >2%, urine osm <350. Time course days-weeks.
     - **Acute interstitial nephritis (AIN):** drug hypersensitivity (PPIs, NSAIDs, antibiotics — penicillins, cephalosporins, sulfonamides, fluoroquinolones; checkpoint inhibitors), infections (Legionella, hanta), autoimmune (SLE, sarcoid). Eosinophilia, eosinophiluria, sterile pyuria, WBC casts, rash, fever, arthralgias. Hold offending drug; steroids in selected cases.
     - **Glomerulonephritis (RPGN, nephritic):** hematuria with dysmorphic RBCs, RBC casts, proteinuria, HTN, edema. Causes — anti-GBM, ANCA-associated (granulomatosis with polyangiitis, microscopic polyangiitis), immune complex (post-strep, IgA, lupus, cryoglobulinemic, MPGN). Renal biopsy and immunosuppression urgently.
     - **Vascular:** thrombotic microangiopathy (TTP, HUS, atypical HUS, scleroderma renal crisis, malignant HTN, APS), atheroembolic (post-cath, livedo, blue toes, eosinophilia), renal vein thrombosis, renal infarct.
     - **Tubular obstruction (intrarenal):** crystal nephropathy (uric acid in tumor lysis, oxalate from ethylene glycol, acyclovir, methotrexate, sulfonamides, indinavir, vitamin C megadoses), light chains in myeloma (cast nephropathy).
     - **Pigment nephropathy:** rhabdomyolysis (CK >5000 typical; usually >15,000 for AKI), hemolysis.

   - **Post-renal (~10%):**
     - BPH, neurogenic bladder, bilateral ureteral obstruction (stones, malignancy, retroperitoneal fibrosis), bladder outlet, indwelling Foley clogged. Single-kidney patient with unilateral obstruction.
     - Hydronephrosis on ultrasound; relieve obstruction (Foley, percutaneous nephrostomy, ureteral stent).

3. **Order targeted workup.**
   - Urinalysis with microscopy is highest yield; obtain fresh, examine yourself or in lab.
   - Urine sediment patterns:
     - Bland → pre-renal or post-renal.
     - Muddy brown granular casts + RTE cells → ATN.
     - WBC casts, eosinophiluria → AIN.
     - RBC casts, dysmorphic RBCs → GN.
     - Crystals → crystal nephropathy.
   - **FENa = (UNa × PCr) / (PNa × UCr) × 100.** <1% = pre-renal; >2% = ATN. Confounded by diuretics, CKD, contrast.
   - **FEUrea** more reliable in patients on diuretics: <35% pre-renal, >50% ATN.
   - Urine protein/Cr ratio; if >3 g/g — nephrotic; nephritic patterns + hematuria suggest GN.
   - CK if rhabdo features (muscle pain, dark urine, immobilization, statin use, severe exertion, drug-related, trauma).
   - Renal ultrasound: hydronephrosis, kidney size (small = chronic), echogenicity (increased = chronic), stones.
   - If GN suspected: complement (C3, C4 — low in lupus, post-strep, MPGN, cryoglobulin), ANCA, anti-GBM, ANA, dsDNA, hepatitis B/C, HIV, ASO/anti-DNase B, cryoglobulins, blood cultures.
   - If myeloma suspected: SPEP/UPEP, serum free light chains, total protein, albumin (anion gap discordance).
   - Renal biopsy — for GN, AIN unresponsive to drug withdrawal, unexplained intrinsic AKI; coordinate with nephrology.

4. **Specific syndromes — rapid recognition.**
   - **Hepatorenal syndrome:** advanced cirrhosis, oliguria, urine Na <10, no improvement after 2 days of albumin 1 g/kg + diuretic withdrawal. Type 1 (rapid, Cr doubles to >2.5 in 2 weeks) requires terlipressin (or norepinephrine + albumin) and transplant referral. Type 2 (slower) — outpatient management with vasoconstrictors and volume.
   - **Cardiorenal syndrome:** worsening renal function in setting of HF; diuresis often improves; do not under-treat decompensated HF for fear of Cr rise.
   - **Abdominal compartment syndrome:** measured intra-abdominal pressure ≥20 mmHg; decompression may be needed.
   - **Contrast-associated AKI:** Cr rise within 48–72 h of iodinated contrast; usually self-limited; pre-hydrate (NS or LR 1 mL/kg/h × 12 h before/after) high-risk patients.
   - **Vancomycin-associated AKI:** ATN-like, hold or reduce dose, use AUC-based dosing.
   - **Aminoglycoside-associated:** non-oliguric ATN, renal tubular dysfunction (hypoMg, hypoK); avoid in elderly, baseline CKD, prolonged use.

5. **Acute management.**
   - Identify and treat reversible cause: stop nephrotoxins, restore perfusion (volume, vasopressors, address sepsis), relieve obstruction.
   - Volume resuscitation guided by exam, IVC US, dynamic measures (passive leg raise) — avoid fluid overload that worsens AKI ("congestive nephropathy").
   - Hold ACE/ARB temporarily; restart when stable.
   - Adjust drug doses for renal function; pharmacy assistance.
   - Avoid further nephrotoxins.
   - Treat hyperkalemia, acidosis, hyperphosphatemia, uremia symptomatically.
   - Nutrition: avoid protein restriction; provide adequate calories.

6. **Indications for emergent renal replacement therapy ("AEIOU"):**
   - **A**cidosis refractory (pH <7.1 not responsive to bicarb).
   - **E**lectrolyte refractory hyperkalemia.
   - **I**ngestion / intoxication (lithium, salicylate, methanol, ethylene glycol, valproate, severe metformin lactic acidosis, theophylline).
   - **O**verload (volume) refractory to diuresis with respiratory compromise.
   - **U**remia (pericarditis, encephalopathy, bleeding, severe symptoms).
   - Modality: intermittent HD (rapid), CRRT (hemodynamically unstable), SLED (intermediate). Decisions per nephrology.

7. **Disposition.**
   - Stage 3, oliguria, hemodynamic instability, RRT need → ICU.
   - Stage 1–2 with stable cause and improving → ward.
   - Outpatient AKI: rare, requires close follow-up with nephrology and labs in 24–72 h.

## Output Format

```
STAGING:
- KDIGO stage by Cr and UOP

CATEGORIZATION:
- Pre / intra / post-renal with supporting features

URINE ANALYSIS:
- Sediment, FENa/FEUrea, protein/Cr

ETIOLOGY (ranked):
1. [most likely with supporting data]
2. [second]
3. [third]

WORKUP NEXT STEPS:
- Renal US, biopsy, GN serologies, myeloma panel as indicated

ACUTE MANAGEMENT:
- Volume / vasopressor / nephrotoxin removal / obstruction relief
- Specific therapy (pulse steroids, plasmapheresis, antibiotics) if indicated
- K, acid-base, phos correction

RRT INDICATION:
- AEIOU evaluation
- Modality

DISPOSITION:
- ICU / ward
- Q-frequency labs and UOP
- Follow-up plan
```

## Worked Example

**Input:** 72 y/o M, HTN, T2DM, baseline Cr 1.4, on lisinopril 20 mg, metformin 1000 BID, ibuprofen for chronic back pain. Admitted 2 days ago for pneumonia, received CT chest with contrast yesterday and IV ceftriaxone, has had 2 days of low PO intake. Today: oliguria (200 mL over 12 h), Cr 2.8, K 5.4, BUN 58, HCO3 18, lactate 1.4, no melena. Exam: BP 102/64, HR 96, dry mucous membranes, no edema, lungs improving. UA: trace protein, 5–10 WBC, no casts, FeNa 0.6%, FeUrea 28%. Renal US: normal-sized kidneys, no hydronephrosis.

**Output:**

```
STAGING:
- Cr 1.4 → 2.8 (2× baseline) and oliguria <0.5 mL/kg/h × >12 h.
- KDIGO Stage 2 AKI.

CATEGORIZATION:
- Predominantly pre-renal (FENa 0.6%, FEUrea 28%, BUN/Cr ratio 21, dry exam, low PO intake) plus likely contributions from contrast, NSAID, and ACE inhibitor.

URINE ANALYSIS:
- Bland sediment — no muddy casts, no eosinophiluria, no RBC casts.
- Trace protein (mild, likely from CKD/diabetes baseline).
- FENa <1% favors pre-renal.

ETIOLOGY (ranked):
1. Pre-renal AKI from volume depletion (poor PO intake, fevers/sepsis from pneumonia) compounded by NSAID (afferent constriction) and lisinopril (efferent dilation in low-flow state).
2. Contrast-associated AKI (within 48 h of iodinated contrast, baseline CKD, diabetic).
3. Less likely: ATN (sediment bland) — but pre-renal can progress to ATN if not corrected.
4. Less likely: AIN from ceftriaxone (no eosinophilia, no rash, no WBC casts).
5. Post-renal excluded by US.
6. No suggestion of GN.

WORKUP NEXT STEPS:
- Hold and discontinue ibuprofen and lisinopril.
- Hold metformin (lactic acidosis risk in AKI; resume when Cr <1.5 stable).
- Recheck UA, retain sediment if Cr fails to improve (re-evaluate for ATN).
- BMP q6–12 h, strict I/O, daily weights.
- Serial renal US not needed unless new oliguria.
- Consider checking urine eosinophils and CK if no improvement.

ACUTE MANAGEMENT:
- Volume resuscitation: LR 1 L bolus, reassess. Continue maintenance LR ~75–100 mL/h until volume restored, then taper.
- Avoid further contrast, NSAIDs, aminoglycosides, vancomycin if possible.
- Ceftriaxone: continue for pneumonia (low nephrotoxic potential, but monitor).
- Hyperkalemia K 5.4: dietary restriction, recheck after volume; if rising, treat. Acidosis HCO3 18 — consider sodium bicarbonate 50 mEq IV if pH <7.2 or bicarb <16 not improving.
- Pain control without NSAIDs: acetaminophen scheduled, low-dose opioid if needed; avoid opioid if AMS risk.
- Continue antihypertensives that do not affect renal hemodynamics (amlodipine OK).
- DVT prophylaxis with reduced-dose enoxaparin per renal function or UFH.

RRT INDICATION:
- No emergent indication currently (no severe acidosis refractory, no refractory hyperK, no overload, no uremic symptoms, no toxic ingestion).
- Reassess if oliguria persists, Cr continues to climb, electrolytes uncontrolled, or volume overload develops.

DISPOSITION:
- Continue ward admission with close monitoring.
- Cr expected to peak 3–7 days; recovery if pre-renal predominates.
- Nephrology consultation if not improving in 48–72 h or progresses to Stage 3.
- Discharge planning: renal-friendly med list (no NSAIDs, careful re-introduction of ACE inhibitor only after Cr stable), patient education on hydration and avoidance of OTC NSAIDs, primary care follow-up within 1 week with repeat BMP.
```
