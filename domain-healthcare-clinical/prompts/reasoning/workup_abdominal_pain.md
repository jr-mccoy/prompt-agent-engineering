## Objective

Work up acute abdominal pain in the adult: identify surgical abdomen and vascular emergencies, localize by quadrant, order targeted imaging and labs, and disposition.

## Inputs

- Pain: location, quality, onset (sudden vs gradual), radiation, migration, exacerbating/relieving (food, position, defecation), duration
- Associated: nausea/vomiting, hematemesis, melena, hematochezia, fever, jaundice, anorexia, weight loss, urinary symptoms, vaginal bleeding/discharge, last menstrual period
- Vitals; orthostatics if concern for hypovolemia
- Surgical history, alcohol use, NSAID/anticoagulant use, vascular disease history, recent procedures, immunosuppression
- Exam: distension, bowel sounds, peritoneal signs (rebound, guarding, rigidity), Murphy, McBurney, psoas/obturator, costovertebral tenderness, hernia, pelvic exam if indicated, rectal exam (occult blood, mass)
- Labs: CBC, BMP, lipase, LFTs, lactate, urinalysis, urine pregnancy in reproductive-age women, type & screen, coags
- Imaging: upright and supine abdomen if perforation suspected, US, CT abdomen/pelvis with contrast (or without if AKI), CTA mesenteric if vascular suspected

## Reasoning Steps

1. **Identify the surgical / vascular abdomen first.**
   - **Peritonitis** (rigid abdomen, rebound, severe focal tenderness with guarding): perforated viscus until proven otherwise → emergency surgery consult, broad-spectrum antibiotics (piperacillin-tazobactam 4.5 g IV q8h, or ceftriaxone + metronidazole), NPO, NGT decompression, CT once stabilized.
   - **AAA rupture:** sudden severe back/flank/abdominal pain, hypotension, pulsatile mass — bedside US, immediate vascular surgery, type & cross 6 units, permissive hypotension.
   - **Mesenteric ischemia:** "pain out of proportion to exam" in atrial fibrillation (embolic), atherosclerotic disease (thrombotic), or hypotensive states (non-occlusive). Lactate elevated late. CTA mesenteric vessels — do not wait for lactate. Heparin, vascular/surgery, possible thrombolysis or surgical revascularization.
   - **Ectopic pregnancy rupture** in any reproductive-age woman with abdominal pain — beta-hCG, transvaginal US, OB consult.
   - **Aortic dissection** (thoracoabdominal): sudden tearing pain, BP differential, pulse deficit; CTA chest/abdomen/pelvis.

2. **Quadrant-based pattern recognition.**

   - **RUQ:**
     - Biliary colic, acute cholecystitis (Murphy positive, fever, leukocytosis): RUQ US first; HIDA if equivocal. Cholecystectomy within 24–72 h for acute cholecystitis. Antibiotics (ceftriaxone + metronidazole, or piperacillin-tazobactam if severe).
     - Choledocholithiasis with cholangitis: Charcot triad (fever, RUQ pain, jaundice), Reynolds pentad adds altered mentation and shock. ALT/AST/ALP/bilirubin all elevated. ERCP urgently for biliary decompression.
     - Hepatitis: ALT/AST much greater than ALP; hepatitis serologies, drug history.
     - Hepatic abscess: CT, drainage, broad antibiotics.
     - Right lower lobe pneumonia presenting as abdominal pain — CXR.

   - **Epigastric:**
     - Peptic ulcer disease: NSAID/H. pylori; PPI, treat H. pylori, endoscopy if alarm features.
     - Perforated ulcer: free air on upright CXR or CT. Surgical emergency.
     - Pancreatitis: lipase >3× ULN + characteristic pain. Causes: gallstones, alcohol, hypertriglyceridemia, hypercalcemia, drugs, ERCP, autoimmune. Severity: BISAP, APACHE II, modified Marshall. CT not needed initially unless severe or diagnosis unclear; obtain at 72 h if not improving to assess for necrosis.
     - MI presenting as epigastric pain — ECG, troponin if any concern (especially older, diabetic, posterior MI).
     - Aortic dissection or AAA.

   - **LUQ:**
     - Splenic infarct or rupture (trauma, mononucleosis, hematologic disease): CT, surgical evaluation.
     - Gastritis, gastric ulcer.

   - **RLQ:**
     - Appendicitis: periumbilical pain migrating to RLQ, anorexia, low-grade fever, McBurney tenderness. **Alvarado score** (0–10): symptoms (migration 1, anorexia 1, nausea/vomiting 1), signs (RLQ tenderness 2, rebound 1, fever 1), labs (leukocytosis 2, left shift 1). Alvarado ≥7 likely; CT confirms (or US in pediatric/pregnancy).
     - **Adult Appendicitis Score (AAS)** has higher specificity in adults.
     - Cecal volvulus, terminal ileitis (Crohn's), Meckel diverticulum.
     - Right ovarian cyst/torsion, ectopic — pelvic US.

   - **LLQ:**
     - Diverticulitis: CT abdomen/pelvis with contrast. Hinchey classification: I (pericolic abscess), II (pelvic/distant abscess), III (purulent peritonitis), IV (feculent peritonitis). Uncomplicated (no abscess/perforation): outpatient antibiotics often unnecessary in healthy patients (per AGA), or amox-clav 875/125 mg BID × 7–10 days, or cipro + metronidazole. Complicated → IV antibiotics, drainage of abscess >4 cm, surgery for III/IV.
     - Sigmoid volvulus, ischemic colitis.
     - Left ovarian cyst/torsion, ectopic.

   - **Suprapubic / pelvic:**
     - UTI, pyelonephritis (CVA tenderness, fever).
     - Cystitis.
     - Urinary retention.
     - PID: cervical motion tenderness, adnexal tenderness, fever; doxycycline + ceftriaxone + metronidazole.
     - Endometriosis, ovarian torsion (whirlpool sign), tubo-ovarian abscess.

   - **Flank:**
     - Nephrolithiasis: CT non-contrast (low-dose). Hydration, analgesia (ketorolac 30 mg IV or 15 mg if elderly), tamsulosin 0.4 mg daily for distal stones <10 mm. Stones >10 mm or with infection/AKI → urology.
     - Pyelonephritis: ceftriaxone 1 g IV; oral options ciprofloxacin 500 mg BID or trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole DS BID per local resistance.
     - Renal infarct (afib, hypercoagulability), AAA, retroperitoneal hematoma.

3. **Diffuse abdominal pain.**
   - Gastroenteritis: viral most common, supportive care.
   - Bowel obstruction: distension, vomiting, obstipation, prior abdominal surgery (adhesions), hernia. CT shows transition point. NGT, NPO, IVF, electrolyte correction, surgery if strangulation/closed-loop or failed non-op.
   - Mesenteric ischemia: see above.
   - Spontaneous bacterial peritonitis in cirrhotic with ascites: paracentesis. PMN >250 → ceftriaxone 2 g IV daily × 5 days.
   - DKA/HHS, adrenal crisis, sickle cell crisis, porphyria, lead toxicity, familial Mediterranean fever — consider when classic abdominal causes excluded.

4. **Special populations.**
   - **Reproductive-age women:** always urine hCG. Ectopic until excluded. Pelvic US. Ovarian torsion: sudden severe unilateral pelvic pain with N/V; whirlpool sign on US, OB-GYN urgent.
   - **Elderly:** present atypically. Higher index of suspicion for mesenteric ischemia, AAA, sigmoid volvulus, malignancy. Avoid premature anchoring on "constipation" or "gas."
   - **Immunocompromised:** neutropenic enterocolitis (typhlitis), CMV colitis, opportunistic infections. Atypical presentations of common processes.
   - **Pregnancy:** appendix migrates upward; appendicitis still most common surgical emergency. US first; MRI without gadolinium preferred over CT when imaging needed.
   - **Cirrhotic:** SBP, variceal bleed, hepatic encephalopathy, portal vein thrombosis.
   - **Postoperative:** anastomotic leak, abscess, ileus, mesenteric ischemia (especially after AAA repair).

5. **Imaging selection.**
   - RUQ pain → US first.
   - Suspected appendicitis adult → CT with contrast (US or MRI in pregnancy/pediatrics).
   - Suspected diverticulitis or bowel obstruction → CT abdomen/pelvis with contrast.
   - Suspected nephrolithiasis → CT abdomen/pelvis without contrast.
   - Suspected mesenteric ischemia → CTA mesenteric (do not delay for lactate).
   - Suspected AAA → bedside US (≥3 cm) → CTA if stable.
   - Pelvic pain reproductive-age → transvaginal US.
   - Suspected perforation → upright CXR or CT.

6. **Disposition.**
   - Surgical abdomen / vascular emergency / hemodynamic instability → OR / IR / ICU.
   - Mild diverticulitis, controlled biliary colic, resolved nephrolithiasis, treated UTI → discharge with follow-up.
   - Indeterminate workup, persistent pain, abnormal labs → observation / admission with serial exams.

## Output Format

```
ACUITY:
- Vital signs, peritoneal signs, hemodynamic state
- Resuscitation actions

LOCATION / PATTERN:
- Quadrant, radiation, migration
- Diffuse vs focal

DIFFERENTIAL (ranked):
1. [most likely with supporting features]
2. [second]
3. [third — include can't-miss diagnoses]

KEY TESTS / IMAGING:
- Labs ordered with rationale
- Imaging modality and rationale

WORKING DIAGNOSIS / TREATMENT:
- Antibiotics if indicated (drug, dose, route)
- Analgesia
- NPO / NGT / IVF
- Specialty consult (surgery, GI, urology, OB-GYN, vascular)

DISPOSITION:
- OR / IR / ICU / ward / observation / discharge
- Specific return precautions if discharged
```

## Worked Example

**Input:** 56 y/o F with hypertension, presents with 6 hours of severe RUQ pain radiating to right shoulder, started after dinner, with nausea, vomiting × 3, fever 38.4°C. T 38.4, BP 132/78, HR 102, SpO2 99%. Tender RUQ with positive Murphy. WBC 14.2 with left shift. ALT 48, AST 52, ALP 142, total bilirubin 1.4, lipase 38. RUQ US: gallbladder wall 5 mm, pericholecystic fluid, multiple gallstones, sonographic Murphy positive. CBD 5 mm.

**Output:**

```
ACUITY:
- Stable. Febrile, tachycardic — likely from infection/inflammation, not shock.
- IV access, IVF resuscitation (LR 1–2 L bolus, then maintenance), antiemetics (ondansetron 4 mg IV).

LOCATION / PATTERN:
- RUQ pain, postprandial onset, radiation to right shoulder.

DIFFERENTIAL (ranked):
1. Acute calculous cholecystitis — Murphy positive, gallbladder wall 5 mm, pericholecystic fluid, stones, fever, leukocytosis, classic post-prandial RUQ.
2. Choledocholithiasis or early cholangitis — mildly elevated bilirubin and ALP, but CBD 5 mm (upper normal). Watch for rising bilirubin, mental status changes (Reynolds), more severe LFT abnormality.
3. Ascending cholangitis — would expect higher fever, more jaundice, rigors. Less likely now but reassess.
4. Acute pancreatitis — lipase 38 (normal), unlikely. But gallstone pancreatitis can coexist.

KEY TESTS / IMAGING:
- CBC, BMP, LFTs, lipase done. Add CRP, lactate, type & screen, coags.
- Blood cultures × 2 prior to antibiotics.
- HIDA scan only if US equivocal — not needed here.
- MRCP if rising LFTs / CBD dilation suspected.

WORKING DIAGNOSIS / TREATMENT:
- Acute calculous cholecystitis. Tokyo Guidelines Grade I (mild) given no organ dysfunction.
- NPO, IV fluids LR maintenance.
- Analgesia: hydromorphone 0.5–1 mg IV q4h PRN or morphine 2–4 mg IV q4h PRN. Acetaminophen 1 g IV/PO scheduled.
- Antibiotics: ceftriaxone 1 g IV daily + metronidazole 500 mg IV q8h (Grade I-II). Pip-tazo 3.375–4.5 g IV q8h if Grade III or healthcare-associated.
- Antiemetic: ondansetron 4 mg IV q6h PRN.
- Surgery consult for laparoscopic cholecystectomy within 24–72 h (early cholecystectomy is standard of care; CHOCOLATE / ACDC trials).
- If high surgical risk: percutaneous cholecystostomy by IR.
- Trend LFTs and bilirubin q12–24h. If rising or CBD enlarges → MRCP, GI consult for ERCP if choledocholithiasis confirmed.

DISPOSITION:
- Admit to surgery service.
- Pre-op evaluation, anesthesia consult.
- VTE prophylaxis (heparin 5000 units SC q8h or enoxaparin 40 mg SC daily).
- Reassess hemodynamics and pain control q4h.
- Return precautions if downgraded: worsening pain, fever, jaundice, mental status change.
```
