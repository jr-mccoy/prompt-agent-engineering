## Objective

Work up unintentional weight loss in adults: confirm true weight loss, screen the high-yield differential (malignancy, GI disease, endocrine, psychiatric, social), conduct age-appropriate cancer screening, and dispose with treatment plan.

## Inputs

- Quantification: pounds/kg lost, time course, prior weights documented in chart
- Threshold: ≥5% body weight in 6–12 months is significant
- Appetite: anorexia vs preserved appetite (with malabsorption or hypermetabolism)
- Associated: night sweats, fevers, GI symptoms (dysphagia, early satiety, abdominal pain, change in bowel habits, blood in stool, hematemesis), urinary symptoms, hematuria, cough, hemoptysis, lymphadenopathy, joint pain, mood, sleep
- Diet: 24-hour recall, access to food, dental/swallowing issues, alcohol, caloric beverages
- Social: living situation, financial security, depression, isolation, dementia, substance use
- Medications: SSRIs, SGLT2 inhibitors, metformin, bupropion, topiramate, stimulants, GLP-1 agonists, chemotherapy, opioids (constipation/anorexia), digoxin (anorexia at high levels), levothyroxine over-replacement
- Comorbidities: HF, COPD, CKD, ESLD, HIV, malignancy history, autoimmune
- Exam: BMI, vital signs, oral cavity, thyroid, lymph nodes (all stations), heart, lungs, abdomen (HSM, mass, ascites), rectal (occult blood, mass), skin (jaundice, rash, café-au-lait, neurofibromas, signs of malnutrition), neuro
- Labs: CBC, BMP, LFTs, TSH, HbA1c/glucose, calcium, phosphorus, albumin, prealbumin, LDH, ESR/CRP, ferritin, B12, folate, HIV, hepatitis serologies, urinalysis, age-appropriate cancer screening status, fecal occult blood/FIT
- Imaging: CXR; CT chest/abdomen/pelvis with contrast if labs/exam suggest malignancy
- Endoscopy: colonoscopy and EGD as indicated

## Reasoning Steps

1. **Confirm objective weight loss.** Pull historical weights from chart. Many patients overestimate or underestimate. ≥5% in 6–12 months is the threshold for workup.

2. **Categorize differential into four buckets and screen each:**

   - **Malignancy (~25%):**
     - GI cancers (esophagus, gastric, pancreatic, colorectal, hepatobiliary).
     - Lung cancer.
     - Lymphoma, leukemia.
     - Renal cell, urothelial.
     - Hematologic malignancy.
     - Workup: thorough exam, age-appropriate screening (colonoscopy if not current; mammography; cervical screening; lung CT if smoking history meeting USPSTF criteria; PSA shared decision in men).

   - **GI / malabsorption (~15–20%):**
     - Peptic ulcer disease, GERD with dysphagia.
     - Inflammatory bowel disease.
     - Celiac disease (anti-tTG IgA + total IgA), tropical sprue.
     - Pancreatic insufficiency (chronic pancreatitis, post-pancreatic surgery; fecal elastase <200 mcg/g).
     - SIBO, lactose intolerance.
     - Microscopic colitis.
     - Liver disease (cirrhosis), Whipple disease.
     - Workup: stool studies, EGD ± colonoscopy with biopsies, celiac serologies, fecal elastase, calprotectin.

   - **Endocrine / metabolic (~10–15%):**
     - Hyperthyroidism (TSH suppressed, free T4 high).
     - Diabetes mellitus uncontrolled (polyuria, polydipsia + weight loss).
     - Adrenal insufficiency (orthostasis, hyperpigmentation, hyperK, hypoNa).
     - Pheochromocytoma (paroxysmal HTN, headache, palpitations).
     - Hypercalcemia of malignancy.
     - Workup: TSH, glucose/HbA1c, AM cortisol ± ACTH stim if low, plasma metanephrines if pheo features, calcium.

   - **Non-malignant chronic disease / cachexia:**
     - Heart failure, COPD, CKD, ESLD — cachexia of disease.
     - HIV/AIDS, chronic infection (TB, endocarditis, abscess).
     - Autoimmune (SLE, RA, GCA/PMR, vasculitis).
     - Workup tailored to suspected underlying disease.

   - **Psychiatric / social / iatrogenic:**
     - Depression, anxiety, dementia (forgetting to eat).
     - Eating disorders (anorexia nervosa, ARFID).
     - Substance use (stimulants, alcohol).
     - Food insecurity, social isolation, abuse/neglect.
     - Medication-induced.
     - Dental/swallowing impairment.
     - Workup: PHQ-9, GAD-7, MoCA if cognitive concern, social work, dietitian, dental and swallow evaluation as needed.

3. **Initial laboratory and imaging panel.**
   - CBC, BMP, LFTs, TSH, HbA1c, calcium, phosphorus, albumin, prealbumin, LDH, ESR/CRP, ferritin, B12, folate, HIV, hepatitis serologies, urinalysis, FIT or fecal occult blood, anti-tTG IgA + total IgA (celiac).
   - CXR.
   - Age-appropriate cancer screening if not current.

4. **Targeted second-tier workup based on clues.**
   - Iron deficiency anemia or positive FIT → colonoscopy and EGD.
   - Dysphagia, odynophagia, early satiety → EGD.
   - Lymphadenopathy → excisional biopsy.
   - HSM, abnormal LFTs → ultrasound or CT abdomen.
   - Pulmonary symptoms or smoking history → CT chest.
   - Hematuria → CT urogram, cystoscopy.
   - PSA elevated → urology evaluation.
   - Diffuse symptoms + age + ESR elevation → consider CT chest/abdomen/pelvis ± PET-CT.

5. **When initial workup is negative.**
   - Reassess weight loss accuracy and tempo.
   - Reconsider psychiatric (depression most common missed cause), social (food insecurity, elder mistreatment), and medication causes.
   - Watchful waiting with monthly follow-up if patient stable; many cases resolve or self-limit (~25% remain unexplained).
   - Reconsider PET-CT for occult malignancy if high suspicion.

6. **Older adults — additional considerations.**
   - Apply Meals on Wheels mnemonic: **M**edications, **E**motional (depression), **A**lcoholism/elder abuse, **L**ate-life paranoia, **S**wallowing problems, **O**ral problems (dentures, gum disease), **N**osocomial infection (TB, C diff), **W**andering (dementia), **H**yperthyroidism/HyperCa/Hypoadrenalism, **E**nteric (malabsorption), **E**ating problems (frailty, can't shop/cook), **L**ow-salt/cholesterol diets too restrictive, **S**ocial (isolation, finances).
   - Failure to thrive in elderly: cluster of weight loss + decreased appetite + poor nutrition + inactivity + dehydration + depression + impaired immunity. Multidisciplinary approach.

7. **Treatment principles regardless of cause.**
   - Address underlying disease first.
   - Liberalize diet (small frequent meals, high-calorie/high-protein supplements, oral nutrition supplements 240–360 mL between meals).
   - Treat depression aggressively if present (mirtazapine 15–45 mg HS — appetite-stimulating, sleep-aid for elderly with depression and insomnia).
   - Megestrol acetate 400–800 mg daily can stimulate appetite but increases VTE risk and adrenal suppression — limited use.
   - Dronabinol or cannabinoid for AIDS or cancer cachexia.
   - Avoid restrictive diets (low-salt, low-fat) in malnourished elderly unless strongly indicated.
   - Dietitian referral, swallowing evaluation if dysphagia.
   - Social work for food insecurity, home meal delivery, financial assistance.
   - Dental evaluation.
   - Physical therapy for sarcopenia.

```
WEIGHT LOSS QUANTIFIED:
- [kg/lbs over time interval]
- % body weight lost
- Confirmed by chart weights

HISTORY:
- Appetite, GI symptoms, B-symptoms
- Medications, social, mood, dental/swallow

EXAM:
- BMI, oral cavity, thyroid, nodes, abdomen, rectal, skin, neuro

STAGE 1 LABS / IMAGING:
- Panel listed
- Cancer screening status

DIFFERENTIAL (ranked):
1. [most likely with reasoning]
2. [second]
3. [third]

STAGE 2 WORKUP:
- Endoscopy / imaging / biopsy as indicated

WORKING DIAGNOSIS / TREATMENT:
- Etiology-specific therapy
- Nutritional intervention
- Mood/social support
- Dietitian, social work referrals

DISPOSITION:
- Follow-up cadence
- Repeat weight and labs in 4–6 weeks
```

## Worked Example

**Input:** 64 y/o M, smoker (40 pack-years), presents with 8 kg weight loss over 4 months (∼9% of baseline weight 88 kg). Reports decreased appetite, mild epigastric discomfort, and nonspecific fatigue. No melena or hematemesis. No fever, no night sweats. Last colonoscopy 5 years ago — normal. Has not had screening lung CT. No depression on PHQ-9 (score 4). Exam: thin, no jaundice, no lymphadenopathy, mild epigastric tenderness, no HSM. Initial labs: Hgb 11.6 (microcytic, MCV 78), iron studies pending, BMP normal, LFTs ALT 36 AST 32 ALP 158 total bili 0.9, albumin 3.4, HbA1c 5.6, TSH 1.4, ESR 38, CRP 12, FIT positive.

**Output:**

```
WEIGHT LOSS QUANTIFIED:
- 8 kg over 4 months = ~9% of baseline. Significant.

HISTORY:
- Anorexia, epigastric discomfort, fatigue. No B-symptoms but elevated ESR/CRP.
- Smoker — high lung cancer risk.
- FIT positive — high concern for GI source.

EXAM:
- Mild epigastric tenderness; no HSM, no lymphadenopathy.

STAGE 1 LABS / IMAGING:
- Microcytic anemia + positive FIT → GI bleed source likely.
- Mildly elevated ALP — possible cholestatic or bone process.
- Mildly elevated inflammatory markers.
- Albumin 3.4 — early protein-energy malnutrition.

DIFFERENTIAL (ranked):
1. GI malignancy (gastric, colorectal, pancreatic, esophageal) — anemia + positive FIT + epigastric pain + smoking + age. Most likely.
2. Lung malignancy — smoker, weight loss; CXR may be falsely reassuring; CT chest indicated.
3. Other malignancy (lymphoma) — less likely without lymphadenopathy or B-symptoms but possible.
4. Peptic ulcer disease with chronic blood loss + H. pylori.
5. Other less likely: chronic infection, hyperthyroidism (TSH normal), depression (PHQ-9 4).

STAGE 2 WORKUP:
- Iron studies, ferritin, retic count.
- EGD AND colonoscopy on same day (comprehensive endoscopy in any patient with iron deficiency anemia or positive FIT). Biopsies as indicated; H. pylori testing on biopsy.
- CT chest/abdomen/pelvis with contrast (combines lung cancer screening + abdominal evaluation given anemia, weight loss, ALP elevation).
- Tumor markers (CEA, CA 19-9) per endoscopy/CT findings.
- If endoscopy and CT non-diagnostic: capsule endoscopy for small bowel, PET-CT for occult malignancy.

WORKING DIAGNOSIS / TREATMENT:
- Working: occult GI malignancy until proven otherwise.
- Pending endoscopy and imaging: nutritional support — protein-calorie supplements between meals, dietitian referral.
- Iron supplementation (ferrous sulfate 325 mg PO daily on empty stomach with vitamin C, or IV iron if oral intolerant or if rapid replacement needed) once cause identified to avoid masking.
- Repeat Hgb in 2–4 weeks.
- Address smoking cessation (nicotine replacement, varenicline, counseling).

DISPOSITION:
- Outpatient: schedule EGD/colonoscopy and CT chest/abdomen/pelvis within 1–2 weeks.
- Follow-up clinic within 1 week of endoscopy results.
- If malignancy confirmed: oncology referral.
- Counsel patient on alarm symptoms (overt bleeding, severe pain, vomiting) and need for emergency evaluation.
```
