## Objective

Work up classic fever of unknown origin (FUO): document fever per definition, stratify into infection / malignancy / non-infectious inflammatory disease (NIID) / miscellaneous, and pursue tiered investigation including PET-CT and targeted biopsy.

## Inputs

- Fever pattern: temperature documented, frequency, daily curve, intermittent vs sustained vs relapsing
- Duration: classic FUO definition = ≥38.3°C (101°F) on multiple occasions for ≥3 weeks with no diagnosis after appropriate inpatient (3 days) or outpatient (3 visits/2 weeks) workup
- Localizing symptoms (often subtle): rashes, joint pain, night sweats, weight loss, lymphadenopathy, abdominal pain, hematuria, headache, neuro changes
- Exposure: travel, animals/pets, occupation, sexual history, IV drug use, food (unpasteurized dairy, undercooked meat), tick exposures, recent procedures/surgery, dental work, hospitalizations
- Comorbidities: malignancy, autoimmune disease, immunosuppression (HIV, transplant, chemo, biologics)
- Medications: drug fever (beta-lactams, sulfa, anticonvulsants, allopurinol, isoniazid, methyldopa, antiarrhythmics)
- Detailed exam serially (find new clue daily): skin, oral mucosa, lymph nodes (all stations), thyroid, heart (new murmur), abdomen (HSM), genitourinary, joints, fundoscopy
- Labs: CBC with diff (smear review), BMP, LFTs, ESR, CRP, ferritin, LDH, urinalysis, blood cultures × 3 from separate sites without antibiotics for ≥48 h prior, HIV, hepatitis serologies, TB screening (IGRA), procalcitonin
- Imaging: CT chest/abdomen/pelvis with contrast; echocardiogram (TTE then TEE if endocarditis suspected); PET-CT in stage 2

## Reasoning Steps

1. **Confirm and classify FUO type.**
   - **Classic FUO:** ≥3 weeks, no diagnosis after standard workup. Most common categories: NIID > infection > malignancy > miscellaneous (in modern series).
   - **Nosocomial FUO:** in hospitalized patient ≥48 h, no source identified after 3 days. Think C. diff, drug fever, line infection, sinusitis, DVT, transfusion reaction, post-surgical abscess.
   - **Neutropenic FUO:** ANC <500 with fever; presumed bacterial / fungal until proven otherwise; broad-spectrum coverage immediately.
   - **HIV-associated FUO:** opportunistic infections (TB, MAC, PJP, CMV), lymphoma, IRIS.

2. **Re-take history and re-examine daily.** Classic FUO often diagnosed by a clue missed initially: travel detail, animal exposure, evanescent rash, transient joint swelling, new murmur, retinal lesion. Document temperature pattern and any pattern (Pel-Ebstein, daily double quotidian).

3. **Stage 1 testing — broad screen.**
   - CBC with smear review (parasites, blasts, atypical lymphs), BMP, LFTs, LDH, ESR, CRP, ferritin (extreme elevation >10,000 → adult-onset Still, MAS, lymphoma).
   - Urinalysis with microscopy and culture.
   - Three sets of blood cultures from separate sites at separate times, ideally before antibiotics, off antibiotics for at least 48 hours.
   - HIV (Ag/Ab + viral load if positive), hepatitis B and C, RPR, IGRA (T-SPOT or QuantiFERON), procalcitonin.
   - Antinuclear antibody, rheumatoid factor, anti-CCP, complement (C3, C4), ANCA, cryoglobulins if vasculitic features.
   - SPEP/UPEP, free light chains if older or back pain or anemia.
   - TSH (subacute thyroiditis).
   - Consider heterophile (mono), CMV/EBV PCR, parvovirus B19, Brucella, Q fever (Coxiella burnetii) per exposure.
   - Stool studies if GI symptoms.
   - CT chest/abdomen/pelvis with contrast.
   - TTE if any murmur, embolic phenomena, IV line, valve risk; TEE if TTE non-diagnostic and endocarditis suspected (Duke criteria).

4. **Stage 2 — if Stage 1 unrevealing.**
   - **18F-FDG PET-CT** (whole body) is the highest-yield single investigation, identifying focal inflammation, malignancy, vasculitis (large-vessel), abscess.
   - Targeted biopsy of any abnormal node, mass, or PET-avid lesion.
   - Bone marrow biopsy with culture (mycobacterial, fungal) and flow cytometry — yield highest in unexplained cytopenias, hepatosplenomegaly, malignancy concern.
   - Temporal artery biopsy in patient ≥50 with elevated ESR + headache, jaw claudication, vision changes.
   - Liver biopsy if persistently abnormal LFTs without clear cause.
   - Lymph node excisional biopsy (not FNA) for suspected lymphoma.
   - MRI/CT brain with contrast if neuro symptoms.

5. **High-yield specific entities to actively consider.**
   - **Tuberculosis (especially extrapulmonary):** miliary, pleural, pericardial, peritoneal, GU, meningitic. IGRA, sputum, urine AFB, LP if neuro, biopsy.
   - **Endocarditis:** culture-negative organisms (HACEK, Coxiella, Bartonella, Brucella, Tropheryma whipplei, fungi). Modified Duke criteria. TEE.
   - **Intra-abdominal abscess** (post-surgical, diverticular, hepatic, splenic, perinephric).
   - **Osteomyelitis / vertebral / discitis** — back pain, ESR very high, MRI.
   - **Subacute thyroiditis** — neck tenderness, transient hyperthyroidism then hypo.
   - **Adult-onset Still disease:** quotidian fever, evanescent salmon rash, arthralgia, sore throat, hyperferritinemia, leukocytosis. Yamaguchi criteria.
   - **Giant cell arteritis / large-vessel vasculitis (Takayasu, isolated aortitis):** PET-CT or MRA aorta, temporal artery biopsy.
   - **SLE, dermatomyositis, polyarteritis nodosa, granulomatosis with polyangiitis** — autoimmune serologies, biopsy.
   - **Familial Mediterranean fever** and other periodic fevers — relevant ethnicity, recurrent serositis, family history; MEFV gene testing; trial of colchicine.
   - **Hematologic malignancy:** lymphoma (Pel-Ebstein in Hodgkin), leukemia, myelodysplastic syndromes.
   - **Solid tumors:** renal cell carcinoma, hepatocellular carcinoma, atrial myxoma, sarcoma.
   - **Drug fever:** stop suspect agents one at a time; resolution within 72–96 h.
   - **Factitious fever:** rare; thermometer manipulation, simultaneous oral and rectal/urine temperatures discordant.
   - **Hypersensitivity pneumonitis, sarcoidosis** — pulmonary findings, ACE level, biopsy.
   - **Hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (HLH):** fever + cytopenias + splenomegaly + hyperferritinemia (>10,000) + hypertriglyceridemia + low fibrinogen + hemophagocytosis on marrow + sIL-2R elevation. HLH-94 protocol; consult hematology urgently.

6. **Empiric therapy decisions.**
   - Generally **avoid empirical antibiotics, antitubercular, or steroids during workup** (obscures cultures and biopsies). Exceptions:
     - Hemodynamic instability or strong clinical suspicion of bacterial sepsis: broad-spectrum coverage after cultures.
     - Strongly suspected GCA with vision threat: prednisone 60 mg PO before biopsy (yield preserved 1–2 weeks).
     - Severe disease with high suspicion of TB despite negative AFB: empirical RIPE in consultation with ID, after biopsies.
     - Suspected HLH or autoimmune flare with organ failure: targeted immunosuppression with rheumatology / hematology.

7. **Documentation and follow-up.**
   - Most FUO eventually diagnoses (60–80%); ~10–20% remain undiagnosed and often resolve spontaneously.
   - If undiagnosed and stable: stop workup, monitor outpatient, repeat history/exam every visit.

## Output Format

```
DEFINITION CHECK:
- Fever ≥38.3°C, ≥3 weeks, prior workup negative

CLASSIFICATION:
- Classic / nosocomial / neutropenic / HIV-associated

KEY HISTORICAL CLUES:
- Travel, animal, food, sexual, occupational, drug, family

EXAM CLUES (serial):
- Skin, lymph nodes, heart, abdomen, joints, eyes

STAGE 1 RESULTS:
- Labs, cultures, imaging, echocardiography

WORKING DIFFERENTIAL:
1. Infection candidates
2. Malignancy candidates
3. NIID candidates
4. Miscellaneous (drug fever, factitious, periodic fever)

STAGE 2 PLAN:
- PET-CT, targeted biopsy, bone marrow

EMPIRIC TRIAL JUSTIFICATION (if any):
- Why now, what threshold for stopping

DISPOSITION:
- Inpatient vs outpatient continuation
- Re-eval cadence
```

## Worked Example

**Input:** 67 y/o F, no travel, presents with 5 weeks of intermittent fevers to 39.0°C, fatigue, 4 kg weight loss, mild bilateral shoulder and hip stiffness in the morning. Exam: temporal arteries non-tender, no scalp tenderness; bilateral shoulder limited by pain, no synovitis; no rash. Labs: WBC 11, mild normocytic anemia Hgb 11.2, platelets 480, ESR 96, CRP 14, normal LFTs, BMP normal, LDH normal, ferritin 380. UA, blood cultures × 3, HIV, hepatitis serologies, IGRA, ANA, RF, anti-CCP, ANCA, SPEP all negative. CT chest/abdomen/pelvis: no malignancy, no abscess. TTE: no vegetations.

**Output:**

```
DEFINITION CHECK:
- Fever ≥38.3°C, ≥3 weeks, Stage 1 workup negative → classic FUO.

CLASSIFICATION:
- Classic FUO.

KEY HISTORICAL CLUES:
- Age 67, B-symptoms (fevers, weight loss, fatigue), proximal stiffness (shoulders/hips morning).
- ESR 96 markedly elevated.

EXAM CLUES (serial):
- No temporal artery tenderness or scalp findings, but absence does not exclude GCA.
- Proximal myalgia/stiffness consistent with polymyalgia rheumatica.
- No synovitis, no skin findings, no lymphadenopathy palpated; recheck temporal arteries and re-palpate axillary and supraclavicular nodes daily.
- Fundoscopy and visual acuity each visit.

STAGE 1 RESULTS:
- Markedly elevated ESR, mild CRP elevation, mild anemia of inflammation, thrombocytosis, normal LFTs/LDH.
- No infection, no malignancy on CT, no endocarditis.

WORKING DIFFERENTIAL:
1. Polymyalgia rheumatica with subclinical / large-vessel giant cell arteritis (PMR/GCA spectrum) — most likely given age >50, ESR >50, proximal stiffness, B-symptoms, and frequent association of GCA with PMR.
2. Other large-vessel vasculitis (Takayasu less likely at this age; isolated aortitis possible).
3. Lymphoma — must keep on list; PET-CT covers both.
4. Indolent infection (TB, endocarditis with culture-negative organism) — Stage 1 negative; reconsider if PET non-diagnostic.

STAGE 2 PLAN:
- 18F-FDG PET-CT whole body — to evaluate for large-vessel vasculitis (high-grade FDG uptake in aortic wall and great vessels) AND occult lymphoma/solid tumor.
- Temporal artery biopsy bilaterally if PET shows large-vessel pattern or clinical suspicion increases (segmental disease — long sample, ≥1.5–2 cm).
- Imaging-guided biopsy of any FDG-avid lesion outside vasculitic pattern.
- Repeat ANCA, anti-cardiolipin, complement.
- Audiometry / IL-6 if available.

EMPIRIC TRIAL JUSTIFICATION:
- High suspicion of GCA at age 67 with ESR 96 + B-symptoms + PMR features. If any visual change reported, start prednisone 60 mg PO daily IMMEDIATELY (do not wait for biopsy; biopsy yield preserved 1–2 weeks). Without vision threat, schedule biopsy within days and start prednisone if biopsy delayed.
- Tocilizumab 162 mg SC weekly per GiACTA for steroid-sparing if GCA confirmed.
- Aspirin 81 mg daily if GCA.
- PPI for steroid-related GI risk; calcium 1000–1200 mg, vitamin D 800–1000 IU; bone density and bisphosphonate per FRAX.

DISPOSITION:
- Outpatient acceleration: PET-CT and TAB this week; rheumatology consult.
- Patient instructed to seek immediate care for any visual change, severe new headache, jaw claudication.
- Follow-up clinic within 1 week with PET results.
- Re-evaluate diagnosis in 4 weeks with response to steroids; if response inadequate, broaden differential and pursue additional biopsy/marrow.
```
