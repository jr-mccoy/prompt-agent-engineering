## Objective

Work up hyperkalemia: confirm the value, identify ECG manifestations, treat acutely with the membrane-stabilization → shift → removal sequence using named doses, identify cause (decreased excretion, transcellular shift, increased load), and prevent recurrence.

## Inputs

- Serum potassium with repeat to confirm; consider hemolysis (pseudohyperkalemia)
- ECG: peaked T waves, PR prolongation, QRS widening, sine wave, asystole, bradyarrhythmia, AV block
- Vitals (bradycardia, hypotension)
- Comorbidities: CKD, AKI, diabetes, adrenal insufficiency, heart failure, malignancy with tumor lysis, rhabdomyolysis, hemolysis, tissue ischemia, recent transfusion (especially old units)
- Medications: ACE inhibitors, ARBs, ARNI, MRAs (spironolactone, eplerenone), potassium-sparing diuretics (amiloride, triamterene), trimethoprim, heparin, NSAIDs, beta-blockers, digoxin, succinylcholine, calcineurin inhibitors (tacrolimus, cyclosporine), pentamidine, K supplements, salt substitutes (KCl)
- Diet (potassium-rich, salt substitutes)
- Acid-base (acidosis shifts K out of cells), serum osmolality, glucose (insulin deficiency in DKA), creatinine, magnesium, calcium
- Urine K, urine Na (transtubular K gradient TTKG), urine output

## Reasoning Steps

1. **Confirm value and exclude pseudohyperkalemia.**
   - Repeat plasma K (lithium-heparin tube) if hemolysis suspected, prolonged tourniquet, fist clenching, severe leukocytosis (>70,000) or thrombocytosis (>500,000).
   - Look at hemolysis index on the lab report.

2. **ECG immediately for any K >6.0 (or any symptomatic / changing).**
   - **Sequence (not always present):** peaked T waves (often >5.5) → PR prolongation, P-wave flattening (>6.5) → QRS widening (>7.0) → sine wave / VT / asystole.
   - Wide complex tachycardia with PEA in unstable patient — consider hyperK as reversible cause.
   - **Treat clinical hyperkalemia (any ECG change or K ≥6.5) immediately**, even before final cause determined.

3. **Acute treatment sequence.**

   - **Step 1: Cardiac membrane stabilization** (any ECG change, severe hyperK):
     - **Calcium gluconate 1–2 g IV (10–20 mL of 10%) over 5–10 min** via peripheral line. Or calcium chloride 1 g IV via central line (3× more elemental calcium; tissue necrosis risk peripherally).
     - Onset minutes; duration 30–60 min — repeat if ECG changes persist.
     - Avoid in digoxin toxicity (relative; theoretical "stone heart" risk overstated; use cautiously in slow infusion).

   - **Step 2: Intracellular shift** (lowers serum K by 0.5–1 mEq/L):
     - **Insulin 10 units regular IV + dextrose 25 g IV** (D50 50 mL or D10 250 mL). Onset 15 min, duration 4–6 h. Recheck glucose hourly × 6 — hypoglycemia is common, especially in CKD and elderly.
     - **Albuterol 10–20 mg nebulized** (5–10× standard asthma dose). Additive to insulin.
     - **Sodium bicarbonate 50–150 mEq IV** in acidemic patients (less effective if not acidotic; do not bolus into peripheral line at high concentration).

   - **Step 3: Removal:**
     - **Loop diuretic (furosemide 40–80 mg IV)** if patient produces urine and has volume; avoid in volume depletion.
     - **Sodium polystyrene sulfonate (Kayexalate) 15–60 g PO** — slow onset hours; concerns about colonic necrosis especially in postop and bowel disease; many institutions avoid.
     - **Patiromer 8.4–25.2 g PO** or **sodium zirconium cyclosilicate (SZC) 5–10 g PO TID up to 48 h** — newer agents, faster onset (1–2 h for SZC), better tolerated.
     - **Hemodialysis** for severe / refractory / oliguric / ESRD: definitive removal. Indications: K ≥6.5 with refractory ECG changes, anuric/oliguric AKI, severe acidosis, ESRD without urine output. Notify nephrology immediately.

4. **Cause categorization.**

   - **Decreased excretion (most common):**
     - **AKI / CKD** — assess GFR; oliguria amplifies.
     - **Adrenal insufficiency** (hypoaldosteronism) — Addison, hyporeninemic hypoaldosteronism (diabetic nephropathy), heparin-induced hypoaldosteronism, drugs (ACE/ARB, spironolactone, trimethoprim, calcineurin inhibitors, NSAIDs).
     - **Type IV RTA** (hyporeninemic hypoaldosteronism, often diabetic).
     - **Obstructive uropathy.**

   - **Transcellular shift (out of cells):**
     - **Acidosis** (especially mineral acidosis; organic acidoses like lactic and ketoacidosis cause less direct K shift but DKA shifts due to insulin deficiency).
     - **Insulin deficiency** (DKA, HHS).
     - **Hypertonicity** (hyperglycemia, mannitol).
     - **Cell lysis** — tumor lysis (uric acid, phos elevated; LDH high), rhabdomyolysis (CK high; AKI risk), massive hemolysis, ischemic tissue (e.g., compartment syndrome, mesenteric ischemia), succinylcholine in burn/denervation.
     - **Beta-blockers**, digoxin toxicity (Na/K-ATPase inhibition).
     - **Hyperkalemic periodic paralysis** (rare).

   - **Increased load:**
     - Oral KCl, salt substitutes, IV K, banana/orange overload only in setting of impaired excretion.
     - Massive transfusion of stored RBCs (K leaks from old red cells).

5. **Workup labs.**
   - BMP with creatinine, glucose, magnesium, calcium, phosphorus.
   - VBG/ABG for acid-base.
   - CK if rhabdo suspected.
   - LDH, uric acid if tumor lysis.
   - Urinalysis, urine K, urine Na, urine osmolality.
   - **TTKG = (UK × Sosm) / (SK × Uosm).** Useful when urine osm > serum osm. TTKG <5 in hyperkalemia suggests hypoaldosteronism. (Now considered less reliable; clinical interpretation supplements.)
   - Fractional excretion of K may be more useful in modern practice.
   - Aldosterone and renin if adrenal cause suspected.
   - Cortisol, ACTH stim if adrenal insufficiency suspected.

6. **Medication review and removal.**
   - Hold ACE/ARB/ARNI, MRA, K-sparing diuretic, K supplements.
   - Switch trimethoprim-containing antibiotic if alternative.
   - Hold NSAIDs.
   - Reduce or hold beta-blockers if shift contribution.
   - Counsel against salt substitutes (KCl content).

7. **Longer-term management.**
   - In HFrEF / CKD on RAASi: do not abandon RAASi for transient hyperK. Use **patiromer or SZC** as enabler to maintain RAASi at guideline doses (PEARL-HF, AMETHYST-DN, HARMONIZE).
   - Treat metabolic acidosis (sodium bicarbonate, baking soda 650 mg BID, or veverimer in trials).
   - Dietary counseling (low-K) including labels of salt substitutes; involve dietitian.
   - Monitor K periodically after RAASi or MRA initiation (within 1–2 weeks then every 3 months).

8. **Disposition.**
   - K ≥6.5 with ECG changes → ICU monitoring, treat aggressively, often dialysis if oliguric.
   - K 5.5–6.5 without ECG changes — depends on cause; admission for cause workup if unstable; outpatient management for known stable CKD with adjustment.
   - Discharge with K-binder if applicable, repeat K in 24–48 h, follow-up.

```
CONFIRMATION:
- Repeat K, hemolysis check

ECG:
- Findings, decision to treat now

ACUTE MANAGEMENT (sequence):
- Calcium [agent, dose, route]
- Insulin/glucose [doses, glucose monitoring plan]
- Albuterol [dose]
- Bicarb [if acidotic]
- Diuretic [if volume]
- K-binder [agent, dose]
- Dialysis [indication, urgency]

CAUSE WORKUP:
- Decreased excretion / shift / increased load
- Specific etiology with supporting labs

- Hold / discontinue / substitute

- Treat underlying disease
- Long-term K management plan

DISPOSITION:
- ICU / step-down / floor
- Repeat K cadence
```

## Worked Example

**Input:** 68 y/o M with CKD stage 4 (baseline Cr 2.6, eGFR 23), HFrEF (EF 30%) on lisinopril 20 mg daily, spironolactone 25 mg daily, metoprolol succinate 50 mg daily, presents with weakness and palpitations. T 36.7, BP 138/82, HR 52. Labs: Na 138, K 7.2, Cl 105, HCO3 19, BUN 64, Cr 3.4 (worsened from baseline), glucose 102, Mg 2.0, Ca 8.6. ECG: bradycardia 52, peaked T waves V2–V5, PR prolonged 220 ms, QRS 110 (mildly widened). VBG pH 7.31.

**Output:**

```
CONFIRMATION:
- K 7.2, no hemolysis flag, ECG changes corroborate true hyperkalemia.

ECG:
- Peaked T waves, PR prolongation, mild QRS widening, bradycardia → severe hyperK with cardiac involvement. Treat now.

ACUTE MANAGEMENT (sequence):
1. Continuous telemetry, 12-lead ECG monitoring, IV access × 2.
2. **Calcium gluconate 2 g IV (20 mL of 10%) over 5–10 min** for membrane stabilization. Repeat in 5 min if ECG unchanged.
3. **Insulin 10 units regular IV + D50 25 g IV bolus** (or D10 infusion to maintain glucose). Recheck glucose in 30 min then hourly × 6.
4. **Albuterol 10–20 mg nebulized** (additive to insulin).
5. **Sodium bicarbonate 50–100 mEq IV** over 30 min (acidemic with HCO3 19, pH 7.31).
6. **Loop diuretic furosemide 80 mg IV** if making urine; volume status not depleted, BP adequate. Reassess.
7. **Sodium zirconium cyclosilicate 10 g PO** TID up to 48 h, or patiromer 25.2 g PO once. Onset 1–2 h with SZC.
8. **Nephrology consult — emergent dialysis** if K not falling, ECG changes persisting, oliguria, or worsening AKI. Plan for HD if needed.

CAUSE WORKUP:
- Decreased excretion: AKI superimposed on CKD 4, plus drug-induced hypoaldosteronism from lisinopril and spironolactone.
- Acidosis (HCO3 19, pH 7.31) shifting K out of cells.
- No tumor lysis features (LDH/uric acid/phos to confirm).
- No rhabdomyolysis suggested (check CK).
- No hemolysis.

MEDICATION REVIEW:
- HOLD lisinopril.
- HOLD spironolactone.
- Continue metoprolol succinate (HFrEF benefit; minor K contribution).
- Avoid NSAIDs, salt substitutes.

DEFINITIVE TREATMENT:
- Treat AKI: identify trigger (volume status, NSAIDs, contrast, infection, obstruction). Renal ultrasound to rule out obstruction.
- Once K stabilizes <5.5 and Cr improving, restart RAASi cautiously at lower dose, with K-binder (patiromer or SZC) to enable continued GDMT in HFrEF. Goal: maintain GDMT — RAASi/MRA improves mortality in HFrEF (do not abandon).
- Sodium bicarbonate 650 mg BID PO long-term to address chronic metabolic acidosis of CKD.
- Renal-K dietary counseling (avoid bananas, oranges, potatoes large servings, salt substitutes).
- Repeat K in 6 h then q12 h; daily after stable.

DISPOSITION:
- ICU monitoring until K <6.0 with stable ECG and no further interventions.
- Step-down or floor once stable.
- Nephrology, cardiology follow-up. Outpatient K-binder, diet counseling.
- Patient and family education on early recognition (palpitations, weakness, syncope) and to avoid OTC K-containing products.
```
