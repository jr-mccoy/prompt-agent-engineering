## Objective

Work up hematuria: confirm true blood, distinguish glomerular vs urologic source, apply AUA microhematuria risk stratification, and select urinary tract imaging and cystoscopy.

## Inputs

- Visible (gross) vs microscopic; quantify (≥3 RBC/hpf on properly collected specimen for microhematuria)
- Timing in stream (initial = urethral; total = bladder/upper tract; terminal = bladder neck/prostate); blood clots (suggest urologic, not glomerular)
- Associated symptoms: pain (flank — stone, infection; suprapubic — cystitis; painless — malignancy until proven), dysuria, frequency, urgency, fever, weight loss, edema, HTN, hemoptysis (anti-GBM, GPA)
- Risk for malignancy: age ≥35, smoking (current/former), occupational exposures (aniline dyes, aromatic amines, benzene, solvents, leather, paint, petrochemicals, rubber), pelvic radiation, cyclophosphamide, prior bladder cancer, irritative voiding symptoms in older adult, gross hematuria
- Risk for glomerular disease: family history, recent infection (post-strep), rash, joint pain, hemoptysis, edema, HTN
- Medications: anticoagulants, antiplatelets, rifampin (red urine, not blood)
- Comorbidities: stones, polycystic kidney disease, sickle cell trait/disease (papillary necrosis)
- Vitals, exam (CVA tenderness, suprapubic, prostate, gyn, skin)
- Labs: CBC, BMP, coags, urinalysis with microscopy, urine culture, urine protein/Cr ratio, UPEP, complement, ANCA, anti-GBM if rapidly progressive
- Imaging: CT urography (multiphase) for upper tract; renal US in pregnancy/young/CKD/CT-contraindicated
- Cystoscopy for lower tract evaluation in indicated patients

## Reasoning Steps

1. **Confirm true hematuria.**
   - Urinalysis dipstick positive but microscopic <3 RBC/hpf — consider hemoglobinuria (intravascular hemolysis) or myoglobinuria (rhabdo, CK, brown urine, no RBC).
   - Pseudohematuria: beets, rifampin, phenazopyridine, porphyria (port-wine urine), nitrofurantoin, deferoxamine, foods.
   - Menstrual contamination (collect after menses or use catheter).
   - Recent vigorous exercise, sex, urinary catheter — repeat after 48 h with abstention.
   - Proper specimen collection: clean-catch midstream; properly examined.

2. **Distinguish glomerular vs urologic.**
   - **Glomerular features:** dysmorphic RBCs (acanthocytes especially), RBC casts, proteinuria (≥500 mg/day or UPCR ≥0.5), HTN, edema, AKI. Tea-colored or cola-colored urine without clots. Painless.
   - **Urologic features:** isomorphic RBCs, blood clots (clots indicate non-glomerular), bright red blood, lateralization, lower urinary tract symptoms, flank pain.
   - Mixed picture possible — both workups may proceed.

3. **Workup glomerular hematuria.**
   - Repeat UA, UPCR, BMP, ANA, ANCA, anti-GBM, complement (C3, C4), hepatitis B/C, HIV, ASO/anti-DNase B, cryoglobulins, IgA level (IgAN — most common in adults; usually mild proteinuria, possible synpharyngitic gross hematuria with URI).
   - Renal US.
   - **Refer to nephrology** for renal biopsy if proteinuria ≥1 g/day, eGFR declining, hypertension worsening, RPGN suspected, or unexplained persistent glomerular hematuria.
   - Common etiologies: IgA nephropathy, thin basement membrane disease (familial, generally benign), Alport syndrome (hearing loss, family history), post-streptococcal GN, lupus nephritis, ANCA vasculitis, anti-GBM disease.

4. **Workup urologic hematuria — apply AUA microhematuria risk stratification.**

   - **Low risk:** female <50, male <40, never smoker, ≤25 pack-years, 3–10 RBC/hpf on a single UA, no risk factors. Recommend repeat UA in 6 months OR cystoscopy + renal US (shared decision).
   - **Intermediate risk:** female 50–59, male 40–59, 10–30 pack-years, 11–25 RBC/hpf on single UA OR 3–25 RBC/hpf on a repeat UA after a low-risk negative initial. Recommend cystoscopy AND renal US.
   - **High risk:** female ≥60, male ≥60, >30 pack-years, >25 RBC/hpf, gross hematuria, prior gross hematuria, irritative voiding symptoms with risk factors. Recommend cystoscopy AND CT urography (multiphase CT with contrast).

   - **Gross hematuria** in any adult ≥35 (or with risk factors at any age): always cystoscopy and CT urography. Lower threshold given malignancy risk approaches 20% in gross painless hematuria.

5. **Cystoscopy and imaging.**
   - **CT urography** (non-contrast, contrast, excretory phases) is the imaging of choice for upper tract evaluation: detects renal masses, ureteral lesions, stones, structural anomalies.
   - **Renal US + KUB** alternative when CT contraindicated (pregnancy, contrast allergy, severe AKI/CKD without dialysis access). MR urography in selected.
   - **Cystoscopy** for lower tract; flexible cysto in office setting.
   - **Urine cytology** has low sensitivity especially for low-grade tumors; not routinely recommended in initial workup of microhematuria per recent AUA guidelines unless cystoscopy negative and high suspicion persists.

6. **Common urologic causes.**
   - **Stones** (CT urography sensitive). Tamsulosin 0.4 mg daily medical expulsion for distal stones <10 mm; urology for larger or obstructed.
   - **UTI / pyelonephritis:** urine culture; antibiotics; recheck UA after treatment to confirm resolution.
   - **Bladder cancer:** painless gross hematuria, smoker, older — high pretest. Biopsy / TUR-BT.
   - **Renal cell carcinoma:** classic triad (flank pain, mass, hematuria) only ~10%; often incidental.
   - **Upper tract urothelial carcinoma:** smoker, aristolochic acid exposure (Balkan, Chinese herbs).
   - **BPH / prostate cancer:** older men, terminal hematuria.
   - **Trauma:** gross hematuria after blunt or penetrating injury — CT with delayed phases.
   - **Sickle cell trait/disease:** papillary necrosis; manage hydration.
   - **Loin pain hematuria syndrome:** rare, diagnosis of exclusion.
   - **AVM, fistula** (rare, post-trauma or biopsy).
   - **Schistosomiasis** (returning traveler, freshwater exposure in endemic regions): squamous cell bladder cancer risk; praziquantel.
   - **Exercise-induced hematuria, sexual activity, recent catheterization** — benign, transient.
   - **Anticoagulation:** does NOT cause hematuria from a normal urinary tract; presence of anticoagulation should not stop workup. Continue evaluation.

7. **Pediatric / young adult considerations.**
   - Thin basement membrane disease (benign familial), Alport syndrome (X-linked, hearing loss), IgA nephropathy after URI, post-strep GN, hypercalciuria, hyperuricosuria, exercise-induced.
   - Avoid unnecessary CT in young patients; renal US first; nephrology if persistent or features of glomerular disease.
   - Sexual abuse / trauma must be considered in age-appropriate context.

8. **Gross hematuria with clot retention.**
   - Three-way Foley catheter and continuous bladder irrigation with NS until clear. Treat anemia with transfusion if symptomatic. Cystoscopy for clot evacuation and source identification. Hold anticoagulants if able; reverse if life-threatening hemorrhage.

9. **Surveillance.**
   - Persistent unexplained microhematuria after negative workup: repeat UA in 12 months; if recurrent or progressive, repeat workup, especially if new risk factors or transition to gross.
   - Document workup completion and recommendations clearly.

## Output Format

```
CONFIRMATION:
- Dipstick + microscopy
- Pseudohematuria excluded
- Repeat collection if needed

CLASSIFICATION:
- Gross vs microscopic
- Glomerular vs urologic features
- Pain vs painless

RISK STRATIFICATION (AUA):
- Low / intermediate / high

GLOMERULAR WORKUP (if indicated):
- UPCR, BMP, complement, ANCA, anti-GBM, IgA, hepatitis, HIV
- Nephrology referral / biopsy threshold

UROLOGIC WORKUP:
- Cystoscopy
- CT urography vs renal US
- Urine culture if symptoms

DIFFERENTIAL (ranked):
1. [most likely with reasoning]
2. [second]
3. [third]

WORKING DIAGNOSIS / TREATMENT:
- Specific therapy
- Anticoagulation review

DISPOSITION:
- Outpatient with urology / nephrology
- Surveillance plan if negative workup
```

## Worked Example

**Input:** 64 y/o M, 30 pack-year smoker (quit 5 years ago), no other comorbidities, presents with two episodes of painless gross hematuria over the past week, most recent today with small clots. No fever, no flank pain, no irritative voiding symptoms. Not on anticoagulants. Exam unremarkable; prostate mildly enlarged, not nodular. UA: too-numerous-to-count RBCs, no WBC, no nitrites, no casts; trace protein. UPCR 0.2. BMP normal. CBC: Hgb 13.6.

**Output:**

```
CONFIRMATION:
- True gross hematuria with clots — non-glomerular pattern by clot presence and absence of dysmorphic RBCs/RBC casts.
- No phenazopyridine, rifampin, beets, no menstrual contamination.

CLASSIFICATION:
- Gross, painless, with clots → urologic source.
- No flank pain, no LUTS prominent → consider bladder source highest priority.

RISK STRATIFICATION (AUA):
- HIGH RISK: gross hematuria, age ≥60, ≥30 pack-year smoking history. Mandates cystoscopy + CT urography regardless.

GLOMERULAR WORKUP:
- Trace protein, no casts, no dysmorphic RBC; UPCR 0.2 — minimal proteinuria.
- BP not given; check.
- Glomerular cause unlikely given presentation; no further serologies needed unless additional features emerge.

UROLOGIC WORKUP:
- Urine culture (pending) to rule out UTI; if positive treat first then reassess hematuria.
- **CT urography** (multiphase): identifies renal masses, upper tract urothelial lesions, stones.
- **Cystoscopy** by urology: gold standard for bladder lesions; biopsy/TUR-BT if mass identified.
- Urine cytology (per institutional practice; AUA does not require routinely; may be done concurrently).
- If cystoscopy reveals bladder mass: TUR-BT for diagnosis and staging.
- Prostate evaluation: PSA (already due at this age); rectal exam findings.

DIFFERENTIAL (ranked):
1. Bladder cancer (urothelial carcinoma) — painless gross hematuria + heavy smoking history + age. Highest pretest probability (~20% in this risk profile).
2. Upper tract urothelial carcinoma — same risk factors.
3. Renal cell carcinoma — possible.
4. BPH-related hematuria — common but diagnosis of exclusion in this risk profile.
5. Stone disease — possible despite painless presentation; CT urography evaluates.
6. Prostate cancer — terminal hematuria possible.

WORKING DIAGNOSIS / TREATMENT:
- Working: gross painless hematuria, malignancy until proven otherwise.
- Manage clot retention if obstructing: 3-way Foley with continuous bladder irrigation if patient develops retention.
- Avoid antiplatelet/anticoagulant if possible until source clarified; he is not on any.
- Hydration; correct anemia if symptomatic (currently Hgb 13.6 — adequate).
- Refer to urology for cystoscopy this week.
- Order CT urography concurrently or before cystoscopy per local practice.

DISPOSITION:
- Outpatient urology referral within 1–2 weeks.
- Patient education: any heavy gross hematuria with clot retention, fever, or hemodynamic symptoms → ED evaluation.
- After diagnostic workup completion, results-based plan (TUR-BT, intravesical therapy, partial/radical cystectomy, etc.).
- If workup entirely negative: repeat UA in 6–12 months and reassess if persistent or recurrent gross hematuria.
```
