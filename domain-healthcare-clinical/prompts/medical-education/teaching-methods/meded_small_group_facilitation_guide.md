# Small-Group Facilitation Guide for Clinical Educators

**Objective:** Design a complete, ready-to-run facilitation guide for a small-group educational session, including an opening hook, discussion question sequence, scripted facilitation moves, group dynamics management strategies, and a closing synthesis.

## When to Use
- ✅ Faculty designing a problem-based learning (PBL), case-based learning (CBL), or structured discussion session for 6-12 learners
- ✅ Clerkship directors building a rotation-wide library of small-group facilitation guides
- ✅ New facilitators who need explicit scripted moves before their first session
- ✅ Faculty development programs demonstrating what high-quality facilitation looks like vs. lecturing
- ❌ Do NOT use for large-group lecture settings (> 20 learners)—the dynamics management and facilitation moves assume intimate group sizes where every learner can contribute
- ❌ Do NOT use when the educational goal is information delivery, not learner reasoning generation—if the objective is "know the facts," a well-designed asynchronous resource is more efficient than small-group discussion

## Inputs Required
- **Learner level:** M1 / M2 / M3 / M4 / Resident PGY-X / Fellow / Interprofessional mix
- **Topic or case:** (e.g., undifferentiated chest pain, approach to acute kidney injury, management of septic shock)
- **Group size:** Ideal 6-10; note if larger or smaller
- **Session duration:** (e.g., 60 minutes, 90 minutes)
- **Learning objectives:** 2-3 maximum; must be learner-action oriented (e.g., "Apply the HEART score to risk-stratify a chest pain presentation")
- **Pre-session preparation assigned:** Yes / No (if yes, what)

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Structure discussion questions at ascending cognitive complexity using Bloom's Taxonomy (Understand → Apply → Analyze → Evaluate)
- Include scripted facilitation moves for at least four scenarios: dominant learner, silent member, wrong answer in group, and group derailment
- Design an opening hook that creates cognitive dissonance—a provocative vignette, counter-intuitive statistic, or controversial statement
- Include a formative closing activity (exit ticket or muddiest point) that is brief and anonymous

**Must Not:**
- Design questions that telegraph the correct answer (leading questions)
- Plan more than 5 discussion questions for a 60-minute session (depth over breadth)
- Allow the facilitation guide to default into a lecture outline—if the educator speaks > 20% of session time, the session is a lecture, not a small group
- Generate a facilitation guide without at least one Bloom's Analyze-level or higher question

## Instructions

### Step 1: Collect Session Inputs
Before generating any content, confirm all six inputs above. If learning objectives are vague (e.g., "understand chest pain"), push back with: "Learning objectives must specify what the learner will DO. Revise to: 'Apply the HEART score to risk-stratify three chest pain vignettes' or 'Analyze the clinical features that distinguish ACS from PE in an undifferentiated chest pain presentation.'"

### Step 2: Draft the Pre-Session Preparation Task
Design one focused pre-session task for learners. Keep total time commitment to 15-20 minutes maximum.

Format:
- **What to do:** (e.g., "Read the first 4 pages of the attached case summary" / "Watch the 12-minute ECG interpretation video" / "Complete the 5-question pre-session quiz on the LMS")
- **Focus questions:** 2-3 questions learners should be able to answer after completing the task. These prime schemas without teaching the session content. Example: "After reading the case, write down: (1) your leading diagnosis, (2) one finding that supports it, (3) one finding that doesn't fit."
- **Access method:** Specify where the material lives (LMS link, QR code, email, shared folder).

### Step 3: Design the Opening Hook
Create one of the following to open the session and generate cognitive dissonance before content delivery:

**Option A — Provocative Case Vignette (recommended):**
A 1-paragraph case with a twist that challenges assumptions. The case should be unresolvable with pre-existing knowledge alone—it should make learners uncomfortable with their current mental models.
- Example: A 28-year-old with no cardiac history, atypical chest pain, and a troponin of 0.15. No ECG changes. "What do you do?"

**Option B — Counter-Intuitive Statistic:**
A real finding that contradicts common clinical intuition.
- Example: "In a 2022 NEJM study, clinical gestalt was wrong in 30% of 'low-risk' chest pain presentations that later required intervention. Does that change how you'd approach the next case?"

**Option C — Controversial Statement:**
A provocation the group must defend or refute.
- Example: "The troponin has made clinical reasoning in chest pain obsolete. Agree or disagree?"

State which option was chosen and present it as a read-aloud script for the facilitator.

### Step 4: Build the Discussion Question Sequence
Generate 3-5 questions at ascending Bloom's levels. For each question, provide:
- The question text
- Bloom's level label
- Facilitator note (what a good learner answer contains; what to probe if the group gets stuck)
- Estimated time allocation

**Bloom's Question Design Reference:**
| Bloom's Level | Sample Stem | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Understand | "What is the mechanism behind..." | Surface prior knowledge; identify gaps |
| Apply | "Using [framework], how would you approach..." | Transfer concept to case |
| Analyze | "What feature best distinguishes X from Y in this patient?" | Discrimination and differential reasoning |
| Evaluate | "Given the tradeoffs, which management option would you choose and why?" | Judgment, values, uncertainty |

Design questions so each one builds on the previous. The sequence should feel like a controlled descent into complexity, not a random list.

### Step 5: Build the Facilitation Moves Toolkit
Provide the following scripted facilitation moves for the facilitator to deploy in-session:

**Move 1 — Wait Time:**
After posing a question, wait 15-30 seconds before speaking. Script: "[Facilitator asks question. Counts silently to 20. Does not fill the silence. If no response at 20 seconds:] 'Take 30 more seconds—write down one word or phrase that comes to mind, then we'll share.'"

**Move 2 — Redirecting:**
When one learner dominates: "Thank you, Jordan—let's get one more perspective. Priya, what would you add or push back on?"
When discussion stalls on one person: "We've heard from a few voices—I want to make sure we get the whole room. What does someone think who hasn't spoken yet?"

**Move 3 — Think-Pair-Share:**
Protocol: (1) Pose a question. (2) "Take 90 seconds individually—write your answer, not discuss." (3) "Now turn to the person next to you and compare for 90 seconds." (4) "Let's hear what came out of those conversations." Use for higher-complexity questions where individual generation before group discussion improves output quality.

**Move 4 — Devil's Advocate:**
When the group reaches premature consensus: "Before we move on—what would a skeptic say about that conclusion?" / "What if I told you the opposite were true—how would that change your reasoning?" / "Let's try to break our own argument. What's the strongest counterargument?"

**Move 5 — Socratic Probing:**
When an answer lacks evidence: "What's your evidence for that?" / "Where does that come from—is that something you've seen or something you've read?" / "How confident are you? What would change your mind?"

**Move 6 — Synthesis Redirect:**
When discussion wanders off-topic: "That's an interesting thread—let's park it in the 'parking lot' and come back if we have time. I want to bring us back to [learning objective X]. Given what we've discussed, how does [topic] connect to that?"

### Step 6: Design Group Dynamics Management Scripts
Provide a ready-to-use response for each of the four common difficult dynamics:

**Dominant Learner:**
Prevention: use Think-Pair-Share and cold calling by name to distribute participation structurally.
In-session script: "Jordan, I really appreciate your thinking—I'm going to pause you there and ask: does anyone want to add to what Jordan said, or take it in a different direction?" [After session, privately:] "I notice you have a lot of strong thinking to contribute. I'd love to ask you to try something—wait for three other people to speak before you respond. It'll make the group stronger and you'll hear things you wouldn't have said yourself."

**Silent Member:**
Prevention: use structured activities (think-pair-share, written reflection) that require every person to generate before anyone speaks aloud.
In-session script: "Sam, I want to make sure we get your thinking—you've been taking notes. What's one thing from your notes you'd push back on or add to?" (Note: do not cold-call silent learners on the hardest question in the room; start with a lower-stakes prompt.)

**Wrong Answer in Group:**
Do not correct directly in the moment if it will embarrass. Options:
- "Interesting—what does the group think about that?" (let peers provide gentle correction)
- "Tell me more about your reasoning—walk us through how you got there." (learner often self-corrects)
- "There's something in there I want to come back to. Let's hold that and see if the next question clarifies it." (then return with the correct concept embedded in the next question)

**Group Derailment (side conversation, tangent, energy collapse):**
Energy collapse: use Think-Pair-Share immediately to re-engage individual generation.
Tangent: use Synthesis Redirect move above.
Side conversation: make brief, direct eye contact with involved learners; pause; resume. If it continues: "Let's make sure everyone's in the same conversation—[question] was where we were. Thoughts?"

### Step 7: Design the Summary and Synthesis Close
Generate 2-3 synthesis prompts for the final 5-7 minutes of the session. These should require learners to consolidate—not the facilitator to summarize.

Examples:
- "In one sentence: what's the single most important clinical principle from today's session that will change how you approach [clinical scenario]?"
- "If you had to teach what we covered today to a colleague in 60 seconds, what would you say?"
- "What's one thing you were confident about at the start of the session that you're now less sure of?"

Facilitator note: Resist the urge to summarize yourself. If you do, learners stop generating. Your job at the close is to organize and affirm what the group produced, not to re-teach it.

### Step 8: Design the Formative Closing Activity
Select one:
- **Exit ticket (2 minutes):** One written question learners answer individually and hand in anonymously. Examples: "What's the one thing you're taking from today's session?" / "What's still confusing about [topic]?"
- **Muddiest point (2 minutes):** "Write down the muddiest point from today—the thing that's least clear. Anonymous." Collect and address at the next session opening.
- **1-2-1 reflection:** "Write one thing you learned, two questions you still have, one thing you want to apply."

Specify how the facilitator will use the data from the closing activity (e.g., "Review before next session; address top muddy points in a 2-minute opening next time").

### Step 9: Assemble the Complete Facilitation Guide
Compile the outputs of Steps 1-8 into a single ready-to-use document the facilitator can print or reference on-screen. Include timing notations for each section.

## Worked Example

**Inputs:** PGY1 residents / Undifferentiated chest pain / 8 learners / 60 minutes / Objectives: (1) Apply HEART score to risk-stratify a chest pain presentation; (2) Analyze clinical features distinguishing ACS from PE

**Pre-Session Task:** Complete 5 LMS quiz questions on HEART score and Wells criteria (15 min). Focus question: "Which HEART score components are most influenced by the history, and which require test results?"

**Opening Hook (Option A — Provocative Vignette):**
"Read this: A 35-year-old woman presents with 2 hours of sharp left-sided chest pain, worse with deep breathing. She's on oral contraceptives. Vitals normal. ECG shows sinus tachycardia only. Her HEART score is 2. The intern discharges her. Thirty-six hours later she arrests in the parking lot of her outpatient clinic. What happened?" [Allow 30 seconds of silence.] "What do you know about this case that the intern didn't act on?"

**Discussion Question Sequence:**

| # | Question | Bloom's Level | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Walk me through the components of the HEART score. Which three do you find hardest to score reliably, and why?" | Understand/Apply | 10 min |
| 2 | "Apply the HEART score to the case we opened with. What score do you get, and what does that recommend?" | Apply | 10 min |
| 3 | "What clinical features in this case make you uncomfortable with a HEART score of 2?" | Analyze | 15 min |
| 4 | "At what point—if ever—should clinical gestalt override a validated risk score? How do you know when you've crossed that line?" | Evaluate | 15 min |

**Facilitation Moves flagged:** Think-Pair-Share on Q3 (individual generation before group discussion); Devil's Advocate if group too quickly agrees HEART score should have been followed; Wait time on Q4 (20 seconds minimum).

**Closing Synthesis:** "In one sentence: what's the one lesson from today's case that you'll carry into your next overnight call?" [Facilitate brief round-robin, not facilitator summary.]

**Exit Ticket:** "Write your muddiest point about risk stratification in chest pain. Anonymous. I'll address the top two at the start of next session."

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Common Mistake | ✅ Correct Approach |
|---|---|
| Confusing facilitation guide with lecture outline | A facilitation guide specifies what the LEARNERS will say and do. If your guide contains more facilitator content than learner question prompts, it's a lecture outline. Flip the ratio: facilitator speaks < 20% of session time |
| Leading questions that telegraph the answer ("Wouldn't you agree that the HEART score is the most reliable tool?") | Use genuinely open questions: "What are the limitations of validated risk scores in chest pain?" A question with one obvious correct answer generates no discussion |
| Planning more than 5 questions for 60 minutes | One deeply unpacked question with real disagreement teaches more than five surface questions. Plan fewer; go deeper |
| No management plan for difficult group dynamics | Every facilitation guide needs a dominant-learner script and a silent-member strategy. Without them, the same two people speak every session and others disengage |
| Facilitator summarizes instead of learners synthesizing at close | When the facilitator summarizes, it rewards passive listening. Design the close so learners generate the synthesis. Facilitator role: organize, affirm, name what was most important from what the group produced |

## Output Format

**Section 1 — Session Overview**
- Learner level, topic, group size, duration, learning objectives

**Section 2 — Pre-Session Preparation Task**
- What to do, focus questions, access method, time estimate

**Section 3 — Opening Hook**
- Read-aloud script with option type labeled

**Section 4 — Discussion Question Sequence**
- Table: question text / Bloom's level / facilitator note / time allocation

**Section 5 — Facilitation Moves Toolkit**
- 4-6 scripted moves with verbatim language

**Section 6 — Group Dynamics Management**
- Scripted responses for dominant learner, silent member, wrong answer, derailment

**Section 7 — Summary and Synthesis Close**
- 2-3 synthesis prompts; facilitator role note

**Section 8 — Formative Closing Activity**
- Activity type, instructions, how data will be used

**Section 9 — Timing Master Table**
- Session timeline by segment (facilitator-facing)

## Verification Checklist
- [ ] Learning objectives are learner-action oriented (not "understand" or "know")
- [ ] Discussion questions span at least two Bloom's levels, including one at Analyze or higher
- [ ] No discussion question has a single obvious correct answer (leading questions eliminated)
- [ ] Facilitator moves toolkit includes scripts for all four common difficult dynamics
- [ ] Facilitator speaking time is planned at < 20% of total session time
- [ ] Closing activity is brief, formative, and anonymous (not graded)
- [ ] Total number of discussion questions is ≤ 5 for a 60-minute session
