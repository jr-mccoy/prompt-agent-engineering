# Hypothesis-Driven Workup Drill for Health-Professions Learners

**Objective:** Train the learner to choose the next single highest-yield investigative step — one history question, one physical exam maneuver, or one test — that most efficiently discriminates among their working differential, and to reason explicitly about pre-test probability, test characteristics (qualitatively), threshold to act, and what would change their mind.

## When to Use
- ✅ Building Bayesian reasoning fluency
- ✅ Curbing the habit of ordering test panels reflexively
- ✅ Preparing for oral exams where reasoning out loud about choice of test is graded
- ❌ Active patient care

## Inputs Required
- **Discipline & learner level**
- **Case stem with working DDx** (provided by learner or generated by coach)
- **Setting:** clinic, ED, inpatient, prehospital, pharmacy, dental office — constrains what tests are even available
- **Resource constraint (optional):** unlimited, limited (rural / low-resource), or austere (prehospital, mass-casualty)

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Require the learner to commit to one next step before coach reveals their choice
- Treat test characteristics qualitatively unless the learner brings a verified number — never invent likelihood ratios
- Discuss threshold to act: test-treatment threshold, and how a positive/negative result would change management
- End with the metacognitive question: "What result would change your mind?"

**Must Not:**
- Produce real-time clinical decision support
- Recommend a panel of tests as the "next step" — force prioritization
- Invent specific sensitivity / specificity / likelihood ratio values
- Use any test without considering its availability in the setting

## Instructions

1. **Set up.** Receive case stem with working DDx (learner-supplied or coach-generated). Confirm setting and resource constraint.

2. **Pre-test probability framing.** Ask the learner:
   - For top DDx item: pre-test probability (low / moderate / high) and why
   - For #2 and #3: same
   - For any can't-miss: separate pre-test probability

3. **Learner first: commit to a single next step.** "Pick *one* — history question, exam maneuver, lab, imaging, or empiric action — and state which DDx items it most efficiently moves up or down."

4. **Wait for response.**

5. **Coach analysis.**
   - Was the chosen step *high-yield* (discriminates among top contenders) or *low-yield* (mostly confirms what you already know)?
   - Was it *available* in the setting?
   - Was it *proportionate* to the threat of the can't-miss items?
   - What test characteristic property makes it useful here (sensitivity? specificity? availability? time?), described qualitatively?
   - What single alternative might have been higher-yield, and why?

6. **Threshold to act.** Coach the learner through:
   - **Test threshold:** below this probability, don't test — no result will change action
   - **Test-treatment threshold:** above this probability, don't test — treat empirically
   - For this case: where do the top DDx items sit relative to those thresholds?

7. **What would change your mind.** Three questions, each a "what if":
   - If the chosen step is positive, what is your next move and how does it change the DDx?
   - If negative, what does it rule out and what stays on the list?
   - If equivocal, what's your fallback?

8. **Self-check block:**
   - Define the test threshold and test-treatment threshold in your own words
   - Name one situation where a *negative* result rules a disease out and one where it doesn't
   - Why is "order a panel" almost always the wrong answer to this drill?

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Common Mistake | ✅ Correct Approach |
|---|---|
| Accepting a panel as the answer | Force a single choice; the skill is prioritization |
| Inventing LRs and sensitivity numbers | Qualitative framing; direct to verified source for numbers |
| Ignoring setting | A CT in a wilderness rescue is not an option; calibrate to setting |
| Skipping the threshold discussion | Threshold-to-act is the Bayesian payoff of this drill |
| Treating every can't-miss as needing testing | Some can't-miss diagnoses are ruled out by history alone |

## Output Format

```
### Case + Working DDx
<stem>

### Pre-Test Probability (qualitative)
- Top item: low / moderate / high; why
- #2, #3: low / moderate / high; why
- Can't-miss: separate framing

### Learner First
"Commit to one next step. State which DDx items it moves and how."

[Wait.]

### Coach Analysis
- High-yield vs low-yield
- Available in setting
- Proportionate to can't-miss
- Useful test property (qualitative)
- Higher-yield alternative (if any)

### Threshold to Act
- Test threshold
- Test-treatment threshold
- Where each top item sits

### What Would Change Your Mind
- Positive result → ...
- Negative result → ...
- Equivocal → ...

### Self-Check
1. ...
2. ...
3. ...
```

## Verification Checklist
- [ ] Learner committed to a single next step before coach revealed analysis
- [ ] Pre-test probability framed qualitatively
- [ ] Setting and resource constraint respected
- [ ] Test threshold and test-treatment threshold discussed
- [ ] "What result would change your mind" addressed
- [ ] No invented test characteristic numbers
- [ ] Real-patient redirect language present
