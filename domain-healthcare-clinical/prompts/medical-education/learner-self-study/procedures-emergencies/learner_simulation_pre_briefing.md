# Simulation Pre-Briefing for Health-Professions Learners

**Objective:** Prepare the learner mentally and emotionally for an upcoming simulation session. Surface learning objectives, set the fiction contract (the simulation is a safe space to practice and make mistakes), rehearse likely critical decisions, and design a post-sim reflection plan. This is the learner's *own* pre-brief — distinct from the educator-led pre-brief that opens the actual sim session.

## When to Use
- ✅ Before a sim lab session, mock code, OSCE-with-sim, or skills assessment
- ✅ Calming anxiety and focusing attention before a high-stakes rehearsal
- ✅ Reviewing past sim performance to set targeted goals for the upcoming session
- ❌ Real-patient encounters

## Inputs Required
- **Discipline & learner level**
- **Sim type:** mock code / mass casualty / OSCE with sim / procedural skills / communication / team training / simulated patient encounter
- **Stated learning objectives** (from course / instructor)
- **Past sim performance notes** (if any): what went well, what to work on
- **Time until sim**

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Affirm the fiction contract: simulation is for learning, not judgment; mistakes are expected and welcome
- Orient to the *learner's* objectives within the broader course objectives
- Rehearse likely critical decisions mentally
- Address physiologic state (sleep, eating, hydration before sim)
- End with a post-sim reflection plan

**Must Not:**
- Provide real-patient guidance
- Promise specific outcomes
- Pile on additional content review (this is pre-brief, not cram)
- Increase anxiety with worst-case dwelling

## Instructions

1. **Orient to the sim.** State the sim type and the stated course objectives.

2. **Personal objectives within the broader frame.** Have the learner write 2-3 *personal* objectives for this session (e.g., "use closed-loop communication," "explicitly name a working diagnosis before ordering labs," "slow down on the airway exam"). These are the things they will reflect on after.

3. **Fiction contract reminder.** Brief script:
   > "Simulation is designed for me to practice, surface gaps, and make mistakes I might otherwise make on a real patient. The point is the learning, not the performance. I'll engage as if it were real, and I'll let the debrief teach me. Mistakes are expected."

4. **Likely critical decisions.** Based on sim type, name 3-5 decisions that are likely to come up. The learner mentally rehearses each:
   - For a mock code: rhythm interpretation, shock decision, drug timing, team-leader call for help, reversible-cause search
   - For a sim communication: opening, ICE, summarization, addressing emotion
   - For a procedural skills station: indication confirmation, time-out, sterile setup, technique, complication recognition

5. **Mental rehearsal.** Have the learner imagine themselves at the first 60 seconds of the sim, calling the first action out loud (in their head or quietly). This primes confident initiation.

6. **Physiologic check.** Briefly: hydration, last meal, sleep, restroom — small details that affect performance.

7. **Post-sim reflection plan.** Set up the debrief structure now so reflection happens reliably afterward:
   - **What did I notice in myself?** (technique, emotion, decision-making)
   - **What did I do well that surprised me?**
   - **What would I do differently?**
   - **What single behavior do I want to carry forward?**

8. **Worry parking.** Acknowledge nervousness without dwelling. One sentence each:
   - "What's the worst that could happen *in the sim*?" → "It's a learning opportunity."
   - "What if I freeze?" → "Then I will name it and call for help — the instructor expects that."

9. **Self-check block:**
   - State your three personal objectives
   - State the fiction contract in your own words
   - State your first action when the sim begins

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Common Mistake | ✅ Correct Approach |
|---|---|
| Cram content review | Pre-brief is mental prep, not content review |
| Skip fiction contract | Anxiety reduction requires the contract |
| Generic objectives | Personal, behavioral, observable |
| Dwell on worst-case | Acknowledge briefly; redirect to learning frame |
| No post-sim plan | Without a plan, reflection doesn't happen |
| Ignore physiologic state | Sleep and hydration shape performance |

## Output Format

```
### Sim Type / Discipline / Learner Level / Time Until Sim

### Course Objectives + My Personal Objectives (2-3)

### Fiction Contract (script)

### Likely Critical Decisions
1-5

### Mental Rehearsal Prompt
"Imagine your first 60 seconds. State your first action."

### Physiologic Check
- Hydration / food / sleep / restroom

### Post-Sim Reflection Plan
1. What did I notice in myself?
2. What did I do well that surprised me?
3. What would I do differently?
4. What single behavior to carry forward?

### Worry Parking
- Two short reframes

### Self-Check
1. Three personal objectives
2. Fiction contract in your words
3. First action at sim start
```

## Verification Checklist
- [ ] Fiction contract present
- [ ] 2-3 personal objectives (behavioral, observable)
- [ ] Likely critical decisions named
- [ ] Mental rehearsal prompt included
- [ ] Physiologic check addressed
- [ ] Post-sim reflection plan set up
- [ ] Worry parking present without dwelling
- [ ] Self-check uses retrieval
- [ ] Real-patient redirect language present (in spirit; sim is the practice space)
