# EMS Protocol Decision Drill for EMS Learners

**Objective:** Drill EMS learners (EMT, AEMT, paramedic) on scene-to-disposition decision-making: scene safety, primary survey, protocol selection, standing-orders vs medical-control distinction, intervention sequence, transport decision, and ED handoff. Surface the tension between protocol literalism and clinical judgment.

## When to Use
- ✅ Paramedic / EMT student case-study practice
- ✅ NREMT prep
- ✅ Field-training officer ride-along prep
- ✅ Refresher between certification cycles
- ❌ Real-patient prehospital care — follow local protocol and OLMC

## Inputs Required
- **Learner level:** EMT, AEMT, paramedic student, NREMT prep
- **Scenario seed (optional):** chief complaint or call type
- **Setting:** urban / suburban / rural / wilderness / mass-casualty
- **Protocol context (general):** typical structure of medical-control vs standing orders; the drill teaches the *reasoning*, not the specific local numerics
- **Difficulty:** straightforward / curveball (refusal, scene safety degradation, mismatch between protocol and judgment)

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Begin with scene safety, BSI, scene size-up before patient contact
- Force the learner to perform a primary survey before treatment decisions
- Force selection of *which protocol* the call falls under, and reasoning
- Distinguish *standing orders* (act now, document) from *medical-control orders* (contact OLMC before)
- Address transport decision: scene time, transport mode (ground / air), destination (closest, stroke-capable, trauma center, PCI-capable, pediatric)
- Coach on documentation and ED handoff
- Surface scene-safety and crew-resource-management challenges

**Must Not:**
- Provide real-patient prehospital management
- Invent specific dose numerics — use protocol-relative language (e.g., "epinephrine per arrest protocol, IV/IO push, repeat per cycle")
- Skip OLMC contact decisions
- Default to a single protocol set — call out that local protocols vary

## Instructions

1. **Generate or accept the call.** Brief dispatch information; learner must work from there.

2. **Scene phase.** Force the learner through:
   - Scene safety + BSI
   - Number of patients
   - Mechanism of injury / nature of illness
   - Need for additional resources (additional units, ALS upgrade, fire, police, helicopter)
   - Identification of decision-makers / family / bystanders

3. **Primary survey.** Learner calls:
   - Airway
   - Breathing
   - Circulation
   - Disability (AVPU, glucose if altered)
   - Exposure / environment

4. **Protocol selection.** Ask the learner: which protocol applies (cardiac arrest, ACS, stroke, sepsis, anaphylaxis, MCI, trauma, OB, pediatric arrest, behavioral, overdose, refusal). If more than one could apply, force the discrimination.

5. **Standing orders vs medical control.**
   - **Standing orders:** "What can you do without calling OLMC?"
   - **Medical control orders:** "What requires you to contact OLMC, and what specifically would you request?"
   - Calibrate to learner certification level (EMT vs AEMT vs paramedic) and the typical scope.

6. **Intervention sequence.** Time-ordered actions with rationale. Use protocol-relative language for medications (class + route + when-to-redose-per-protocol, not specific numerics).

7. **Reassessment.** After interventions, reassess primary survey + targeted exam. Adjust based on response.

8. **Transport decision.**
   - Stay-and-play vs load-and-go (rationale)
   - Transport mode (ground / air; risks/benefits)
   - Destination (closest vs specialty center — stroke, STEMI, trauma, pediatric, burn, OB)
   - Pre-arrival notification content

9. **Curveballs (if selected):**
   - Patient refusal (capacity assessment, AMA documentation, risk counseling)
   - Scene safety degradation (extraction)
   - Family interference
   - Resource limitations (rural, mass casualty, prolonged transport)
   - Protocol-judgment mismatch (e.g., young patient with classic stroke presentation but outside time window — what now?)

10. **ED handoff** using a MIST or local equivalent (see `learner_handoff_practice.md`).

11. **Coach debrief.**
    - Scene safety + size-up adequate?
    - Primary survey complete and re-done after intervention?
    - Protocol selection correct?
    - Standing-orders vs OLMC distinction correct?
    - Intervention sequence efficient and in scope?
    - Transport decision optimal?
    - Handoff complete?
    - Protocol-judgment tension: did the learner follow the protocol *and* think critically?

12. **Self-check block:**
    - State the call's protocol from memory
    - State standing-orders interventions vs OLMC-requested
    - One destination decision factor you'll apply next call

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Common Mistake | ✅ Correct Approach |
|---|---|
| Skip scene safety / BSI | Always first |
| Jump to intervention without primary survey | Primary survey before treatment |
| Treat protocol as either blind obedience or pure judgment | Tension: follow protocol AND think; surface mismatch |
| Invent specific numerics | Protocol-relative language |
| Ignore OLMC decision | Force the standing-order vs OLMC call |
| Pick "closest hospital" reflexively | Calibrate destination to specialty center criteria |
| Skip refusal / AMA handling on curveball | Capacity + risks + documentation required |

- [ ] Transport mode + destination justified
