## Objective

Take a patient with suspected interstitial lung disease from first abnormal finding to a working diagnosis and treatment plan: characterize the physiologic and radiologic pattern, search for a cause (exposure, drug, connective-tissue disease), decide whether BAL or biopsy is needed, and start antifibrotic, immunosuppressive, or supportive therapy. Distinct from `reasoning/workup_dyspnea.md`, which reaches ILD as one branch of a dyspnea differential — this prompt starts once ILD is suspected and owns the ILD-specific workup and treatment.

## Inputs

- Symptoms: dyspnea tempo, cough, constitutional symptoms; duration
- Exposures: occupational (asbestos, silica, coal, metal dust, wood dust, farming), home (birds, feather bedding, mold, humidifiers, hot tubs), smoking (pack-years, current)
- Drugs: amiodarone, nitrofurantoin, methotrexate, bleomycin, immune checkpoint inhibitors, other chemotherapy, radiation
- CTD features: Raynaud, inflammatory arthritis, skin thickening, rash (Gottron, heliotrope, mechanic's hands), proximal weakness, dysphagia, sicca, photosensitivity
- Family history of pulmonary fibrosis
- Exam: bibasilar "Velcro" crackles, clubbing, inspiratory squeaks, skin and joint findings
- PFTs: spirometry, lung volumes (TLC), DLCO; 6-minute walk distance and nadir SpO₂
- HRCT: thin-section, inspiratory supine and prone, and expiratory images; distribution and pattern description
- Labs available; prior imaging for trend
- Echocardiogram if pulmonary hypertension suspected

## Role

Senior attending pulmonologist in an ILD clinic preparing a case for multidisciplinary discussion and writing the plan.

## Reasoning Steps

1. **Confirm ILD and grade the physiology.** Restrictive pattern (reduced FVC and TLC) with reduced DLCO is typical; DLCO may fall first. Combined emphysema can pseudo-normalize volumes with a disproportionately low DLCO. Record 6MWT desaturation — it predicts outcome and oxygen need.

2. **Get the right CT.** HRCT with thin sections, prone images (to separate dependent atelectasis from early fibrosis), and expiratory images (air trapping). Contrast-enhanced routine CT is not adequate for pattern assignment.

3. **Assign the HRCT pattern.**
   - **UIP:** subpleural, basal-predominant reticulation with honeycombing, with or without traction bronchiectasis; heterogeneous.
   - **Probable UIP:** same distribution with reticulation and peripheral traction bronchiectasis, without honeycombing.
   - **Indeterminate for UIP:** fibrosis with features not specific for UIP or an alternative.
   - **Alternative-diagnosis features:** NSIP (lower-lobe ground-glass and fine reticulation, subpleural sparing, homogeneous); organizing pneumonia (patchy peribronchovascular or peripheral consolidation, reversed halo); fibrotic hypersensitivity pneumonitis (upper/mid-zone or diffuse fibrosis, centrilobular nodules, mosaic attenuation with air trapping — the "three-density" pattern); sarcoidosis (perilymphatic nodules, upper-zone predominance, symmetric hilar/mediastinal adenopathy); smoking-related (RB-ILD, DIP, Langerhans cell histiocytosis with nodules and irregular cysts); LAM (thin-walled cysts in women).

4. **Search for a cause — always, even with a UIP pattern.**
   - Exposure history in detail — an inciting HP antigen is often not identified, and many are missed without direct questions.
   - Medication review; stop suspected drugs.
   - Serologies: ANA (HEp-2 IIF), RF, anti-CCP, ENA (SSA/Ro, SSB/La, Sm, RNP, Scl-70), CK and aldolase, myositis-specific and associated panel (Jo-1 and other antisynthetase antibodies such as PL-7 and PL-12, MDA5, Ro52, PM/Scl), ANCA (MPO-ANCA associates with fibrosis). Add anti-centromere and RNA polymerase III with SSc features.
   - HP serum precipitin/IgG panels support exposure but do not diagnose HP on their own.

5. **Decide on BAL and biopsy.**
   - A UIP pattern on HRCT in the right clinical context (older patient, no exposure, no CTD features, negative serologies) is sufficient for IPF after multidisciplinary discussion — no biopsy.
   - Probable UIP, indeterminate, or suspected alternative diagnosis: BAL cellular analysis (lymphocytosis supports HP, sarcoidosis, NSIP, drug reaction; eosinophilia supports eosinophilic pneumonia), then transbronchial lung cryobiopsy or surgical lung biopsy if the result would change management and the patient is fit. Probable UIP in an appropriate clinical setting may be diagnosed as IPF at MDD without biopsy.
   - Suspected sarcoidosis: EBUS-TBNA of nodes.

6. **Hold a multidisciplinary discussion** (pulmonology, radiology, pathology, rheumatology as needed) and record a working diagnosis with a confidence level: confident, provisional high confidence, or unclassifiable ILD.

7. **Treat by diagnosis.**
   - **IPF:** antifibrotic — nintedanib 150 mg PO BID (reduce to 100 mg BID for intolerance; LFTs monthly for 3 months then every 3 months; manage diarrhea) or pirfenidone titrated to 801 mg PO TID with food (photosensitivity, nausea, LFT monitoring). Do not use prednisone-azathioprine-N-acetylcysteine combinations (harm in PANTHER-IPF). Newer antifibrotics (e.g., nerandomilast) have phase 3 data — check current approval and labeling before substituting.
   - **Progressive pulmonary fibrosis (non-IPF fibrotic ILD):** progression defined by at least 2 of 3 within the past year without another explanation — worsening symptoms; physiologic decline (absolute FVC decline ≥5% predicted or DLCO decline ≥10% predicted); radiologic progression. Add nintedanib (INBUILD) to disease-specific therapy.
   - **CTD-ILD:** mycophenolate mofetil titrated to 1.5 g PO BID is the usual first-line for SSc-ILD and many CTD-ILDs; tocilizumab and nintedanib have evidence in SSc-ILD; rituximab or cyclophosphamide for severe or refractory disease. Myositis-associated ILD, especially MDA5-positive rapidly progressive, needs urgent combination immunosuppression.
   - **Fibrotic HP:** antigen identification and complete avoidance; mycophenolate or glucocorticoids for progressive disease; antifibrotic if progressive fibrosis.
   - **Drug-induced:** stop the drug; glucocorticoids for severe or progressive disease.
   - **Organizing pneumonia:** glucocorticoids (e.g., prednisone 0.5–1 mg/kg/day with taper over months); watch for relapse on taper.

8. **Supportive and longitudinal care.** Ambulatory and nocturnal oxygen assessment; pulmonary rehabilitation; vaccination (influenza, pneumococcal, COVID, RSV where eligible); smoking cessation; screen for pulmonary hypertension (echo) when DLCO is disproportionately low or with exertional hypoxemia; treat symptomatic GERD; early lung transplant referral for fibrotic ILD — referral thresholds are deliberately low (reduced FVC or DLCO, oxygen need, or any progression); palliative care for symptoms. PFTs every 3–6 months.

9. **Acute exacerbation.** Acute worsening over <1 month with new bilateral ground-glass on a fibrotic background: exclude infection, PE, heart failure, pneumothorax; supportive care; glucocorticoids are commonly used though evidence is weak; mechanical ventilation carries very high mortality — clarify goals early.

10. **Verify.** Confirm the HRCT protocol was adequate before assigning a pattern, a cause search was done before labeling IPF, serologies were sent, the diagnosis came through MDD, and immunosuppression is not being used alone for IPF.

## Output Format

```
PHYSIOLOGY: [FVC, TLC, DLCO % predicted; 6MWT distance and nadir SpO₂]

HRCT PATTERN: [UIP / probable UIP / indeterminate / alternative — descriptors]

CAUSE SEARCH:
- Exposures: [ ]
- Drugs: [ ]
- CTD features and serologies: [sent / results]

TISSUE / BAL DECISION: [none needed — why / BAL / cryobiopsy / SLB / EBUS]

MDD DIAGNOSIS: [diagnosis + confidence]

TREATMENT:
- Disease-directed: [drug, dose, titration, monitoring]
- Supportive: [oxygen, rehab, vaccines, PH/GERD screening]
- Transplant / palliative: [referral timing]

MONITORING: [PFT interval, progression criteria]

PITFALLS:
- [no cause search before IPF, missed HP exposure, steroids for IPF, inadequate CT protocol, late transplant referral]
```

## Worked Example

**Input:** 71 y/o M, 18 months of progressive exertional dyspnea and dry cough. Former smoker (30 pack-years, quit 15 years ago), retired accountant, no birds, no mold, no feather bedding, no relevant drugs. No Raynaud, rash, arthritis, weakness, or dysphagia. Exam: bibasilar Velcro crackles, digital clubbing. PFTs: FVC 68%, TLC 64%, DLCO 42% predicted. 6MWT 360 m, nadir SpO₂ 86%. HRCT (prone and expiratory): subpleural basal reticulation with honeycombing and traction bronchiectasis, no ground-glass predominance, no mosaic attenuation or air trapping, no nodules. ANA negative, RF negative, anti-CCP negative, myositis panel negative, ANCA negative, CK normal.

**Output:**

```
PHYSIOLOGY: Moderate restriction (FVC 68%, TLC 64%), DLCO 42%; exertional desaturation to 86%.

HRCT PATTERN: UIP — subpleural basal reticulation with honeycombing and traction bronchiectasis; no features of an alternative diagnosis.

CAUSE SEARCH:
- Exposures: none identified after directed questioning (birds, mold, feather bedding, occupation).
- Drugs: none implicated.
- CTD: no clinical features; ANA, RF, anti-CCP, myositis panel, ANCA negative.

TISSUE / BAL DECISION: None needed. UIP pattern + typical context + negative cause search.

MDD DIAGNOSIS: Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, confident.

TREATMENT:
- Disease-directed: nintedanib 150 mg PO BID with food. LFTs monthly ×3 months then every 3 months; loperamide plan for diarrhea; reduce to 100 mg BID if not tolerated. Pirfenidone 801 mg TID is the alternative if nintedanib fails on tolerability.
- No prednisone, azathioprine, or NAC.
- Supportive: ambulatory oxygen titrated to SpO₂ ≥88–90% on exertion; nocturnal oximetry; pulmonary rehabilitation; influenza, pneumococcal, COVID, RSV vaccines; echocardiogram (DLCO 42% with exertional hypoxemia — PH screening); GERD treatment only if symptomatic.
- Transplant: refer now (FVC <80%, DLCO 42%, exertional oxygen need); age 71 is a relative barrier — eligibility is center-dependent.
- Palliative care for cough and dyspnea alongside.

MONITORING: PFTs and 6MWT every 3–6 months; FVC decline ≥5% absolute or DLCO decline ≥10% absolute marks progression.

PITFALLS:
- Do not skip the exposure history because the CT "looks like IPF" — fibrotic HP mimics UIP.
- No steroid trial for IPF.
- Refer for transplant early; waitlist evaluation takes months.
```
