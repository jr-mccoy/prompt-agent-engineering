## Objective

Convert a patient from IV to oral therapy at the right time and with the right dose. Confirm clinical readiness (hemodynamic stability, GI tolerance, ability to swallow and absorb, source-controlled infection if applicable), select a PO agent that achieves equivalent or near-equivalent target exposure based on bioavailability, write the conversion order with correct dose and frequency, and integrate with discharge planning and outpatient follow-up.

## Inputs

- Patient: vitals, hemodynamic status, mental status, ability to swallow / NPO status / functional GI tract, current GI medications (PPIs, antacids, sucralfate, divalent cations), enteral access (oral, NG, J-tube)
- Current IV regimen: drug, dose, frequency, day of therapy
- Indication: infection (site, severity, source control achieved?), pain, anticoagulation, antiepileptic, cardiac, antifungal, antiviral
- Cultures and sensitivities (for antimicrobials)
- Renal and hepatic function
- Total planned duration of therapy
- Discharge plan (home, SNF, rehab) and outpatient infusion availability if PO is not feasible

## Role

Senior hospitalist / antimicrobial stewardship pharmacist writing the IV-to-PO conversion order with rationale and discharge instructions.

## Reasoning Steps

1. **Apply readiness criteria for any IV-to-PO switch.**
   - Clinically improving (afebrile ≥24 h or trending down; WBC trending down; vital signs stable; not on vasopressors; SaO2 adequate).
   - Tolerating oral intake (eating ≥50% of meals, not vomiting, not severe ileus); functional GI tract.
   - Able to swallow safely (no severe dysphagia; cleared by SLP if concern).
   - No malabsorption (severe short bowel, active GVHD, severe Crohn's, recent gastric bypass with concern about specific drug absorption).
   - For infections: appropriate source control achieved (drained abscess, removed infected device, etc.).
   - No contraindication to PO route for the specific agent (e.g., active GI bleed limits oral anticoagulant; severe gastroparesis).

2. **Choose an oral agent with comparable spectrum and exposure.**
   - **Highly bioavailable antimicrobials (PO and IV bioavailability ~ equivalent):**
     - **Fluoroquinolones:** ciprofloxacin (F ~70–80%), levofloxacin (F ~99%), moxifloxacin (F ~90%) — IV-to-PO dose 1:1.
     - **Linezolid** (F ~100%) — 600 mg PO BID = 600 mg IV BID.
     - **Metronidazole** (F ~100%) — 500 mg PO BID-TID = 500 mg IV q8h.
     - **TMP-SMX** (F ~90%) — 5 mg/kg/d TMP IV → equivalent PO.
     - **Doxycycline** (F ~95%) — 100 mg PO BID = 100 mg IV BID.
     - **Fluconazole** (F ~90%) — 1:1 conversion.
     - **Voriconazole** (F ~96% PO empty stomach) — 4 mg/kg IV BID → 200 mg PO BID; weight-based PO dose 200 mg or 300 mg depending on weight; monitor levels.
     - **Isavuconazole** (F ~98%) — 1:1.
     - **Rifampin** (F ~90% but absorption ↓ with food) — 1:1.
     - **Clindamycin** (F ~90%) — 600 mg IV q8h ≈ 450 mg PO q8h (or 300 mg PO q6h).
     - **Azithromycin** (F ~38% PO, but works because of long tissue half-life) — 500 mg IV daily = 500 mg PO daily.
   - **Moderately bioavailable (require dose adjustment):**
     - **Cefuroxime** axetil PO (F ~50%) — limited use beyond minor infections.
     - **Cefpodoxime** (F ~50%) — community-acquired infections.
     - **Cefdinir** (F ~25%) — limited spectrum.
   - **β-lactams generally have lower PO bioavailability** (amoxicillin F ~80%, but most cephalosporins much less); for serious gram-negative infection or pseudomonas, oral options are limited (ciprofloxacin and levofloxacin among few PO antipseudomonals).
   - **Drugs with no equivalent PO formulation:** vancomycin (PO not absorbed, only used for C. difficile colitis), daptomycin, aminoglycosides (gentamicin, tobramycin), ertapenem, meropenem, ceftriaxone, anti-pseudomonal β-lactams (cefepime, pip-tazo) — for these, switch is to a different class with appropriate spectrum, or transition to outpatient parenteral antimicrobial therapy (OPAT).
   - **Non-antimicrobial common conversions:**
     - **PPIs:** pantoprazole 40 mg IV = 40 mg PO daily.
     - **Furosemide:** PO bioavailability ~50%; IV 20 mg ≈ PO 40 mg.
     - **Levothyroxine:** PO dose ~25% higher than IV.
     - **Phenytoin:** fosphenytoin IV / phenytoin PO 1:1 in PE units; monitor levels.
     - **Levetiracetam, valproic acid, lacosamide:** 1:1 IV:PO.
     - **β-blockers:** metoprolol IV 5 mg ≈ PO 25 mg (rough).
     - **Diltiazem:** IV drip → PO conversion = (rate mg/h × 3 + 3) × 10 (approximate); titrate to HR/BP.
     - **Opioids:** see opioid conversion prompt.
     - **Corticosteroids:** methylprednisolone IV 4 mg ≈ prednisone PO 5 mg.
   - **Anticoagulants:** transition off heparin/IV to LMWH or DOAC/warfarin as appropriate; see anticoag transition.

3. **Account for drug–drug and drug–food interactions of the oral formulation.**
   - **Fluoroquinolones, doxycycline, tetracycline:** chelated by polyvalent cations (Mg, Al, Ca, Fe, Zn) — must space ≥2 h before or 6 h after antacids, sucralfate, oral iron, calcium, multivitamins. PPI does not chelate but altered gastric pH may affect some agents (cefdinir, itraconazole capsule). PPI does not affect fluoroquinolones meaningfully.
   - **Azole antifungals:** itraconazole capsule needs acid; PPI use reduces absorption — switch to itraconazole solution (better F, doesn't need acid). Voriconazole take on empty stomach (food reduces F by ~30%). Posaconazole tablet doesn't need food; oral suspension needs food.
   - **Linezolid:** monoamine oxidase inhibition — serotonergic drugs (SSRIs, SNRIs, tramadol, meperidine) → serotonin syndrome risk; tyramine-rich foods → hypertensive crisis (less clinically problematic than older MAOIs, but warn).
   - **TMP-SMX:** raises warfarin INR (CYP2C9 inhibition); raises K (TMP); reduces methotrexate clearance.
   - **Rifampin:** potent CYP3A4 / 2C inducer — reduces warfarin, OCP, methadone, calcineurin inhibitors, DOACs, HIV ART.
   - **Metronidazole + alcohol:** disulfiram-like reaction.

4. **Patient-specific considerations.**
   - **Renal function:** PO doses still need renal adjustment (TMP-SMX, fluconazole, levetiracetam, lacosamide for severe CKD, dabigatran etc.).
   - **Hepatic:** voriconazole, isoniazid, rifampin — toxicity and dose considerations.
   - **Pregnancy:** confirm category and clinician preference (avoid fluoroquinolones, tetracyclines, TMP-SMX 1st trimester / late 3rd trimester).
   - **Drug levels / TDM:** voriconazole, posaconazole, sirolimus, tacrolimus, phenytoin, valproic acid — confirm therapeutic levels after switch.

5. **Verify total planned duration and discharge plan.**
   - Total course depends on indication; IV-to-PO switch typically occurs after 48–72 h of IV (or earlier in mild infections) and continues PO for the remaining duration.
   - For deep infections needing prolonged therapy (osteomyelitis, endocarditis, intra-abdominal abscess with poor drainage), OPAT may be required if no good PO option; some recent data (POET, OVIVA) support PO therapy in osteomyelitis and even endocarditis with highly bioavailable agents in selected stable patients.
   - Ensure prescription, follow-up labs (CBC, CMP, drug levels), and outpatient ID or PCP follow-up are arranged.

6. **Write the order.**
   - Hold IV order. Start PO order with specific drug, dose, frequency. Plan duration. Specify monitoring. Document rationale.

```

- Source control / clinical trajectory: [stable, improving, etc.]
- GI / swallow / absorption status: [tolerating PO, no malabsorption]

READINESS CHECK:
- Hemodynamically stable: [yes/no]
- Afebrile ≥24 h or trending: [yes/no]
- WBC trend: [...]
- Tolerating PO: [yes/no]
- Source control adequate: [yes/no]
- All readiness criteria met: [yes/no — if no, defer switch]

PO AGENT SELECTION:
- Spectrum match for organism and indication: [...]
- Bioavailability: [%]
- Dose conversion: [IV dose × adjustment = PO dose]
- Frequency: [PO frequency, often matches IV]
- Renal / hepatic adjustment applied: [...]

PO ORDER:
- Drug: [name] [dose] PO [frequency] for [N more days to complete total duration]
- Take with / without food, spacing from cations / PPI: [...]
- Discontinue IV order

DRUG INTERACTIONS / TIMING:
- [Specific interactions identified and managed]

MONITORING:
- Clinical: [vitals, fever curve, leukocytosis, symptom resolution]
- Labs: [CBC, CMP, drug levels, INR if applicable]
- Adverse effects: [class-specific]

- PO regimen confirmed in discharge medications
- Outpatient labs: [day Y, content]

- [Endovascular infection requiring IV throughout; CNS infection requiring high-penetration IV; bacteremia day <X; persistent positive cultures; inability to absorb PO]

```

## Worked Example

**Input:** 64-year-old man admitted 4 days ago for community-acquired pneumonia (CAP). On IV ceftriaxone 1 g daily + IV azithromycin 500 mg daily. Now afebrile ×30 h, WBC down from 17 to 9, eating, ambulating, SaO2 95% RA. Sputum and blood cultures negative. Convert to PO regimen and finalize discharge plan.

**Output:**

```
PATIENT SNAPSHOT:
- 64 y M, CAP day 4. Empiric ceftriaxone 1 g IV q24h + azithromycin 500 mg IV q24h.
- Now afebrile ×30 h, WBC normalized, tolerating PO, ambulating, satting well on RA.
- Cultures negative. Indication for empiric atypical coverage met by initial macrolide. CrCl normal. No drug allergies. No QTc concern reported.

READINESS CHECK:
- Hemodynamically stable: yes
- Afebrile ≥24 h: yes (30 h)
- WBC trending down: yes
- Tolerating PO: yes
- Source control: not applicable; CAP — clinical resolution
- All readiness criteria met: **yes — proceed with switch**

PO AGENT SELECTION:
- Ceftriaxone PO equivalent: not available (β-lactams generally poor PO bioavailability beyond amoxicillin). Common PO substitutes for CAP: amoxicillin-clavulanate, cefpodoxime, cefuroxime axetil, or an oral fluoroquinolone (levofloxacin / moxifloxacin) which alone covers both typical and atypical organisms.
- Azithromycin already highly tissue-distributed; PO 500 mg = IV 500 mg.
- Two practical paths:
  1. **Continue β-lactam + macrolide PO** (mirrors inpatient regimen): amoxicillin-clavulanate 875/125 mg PO BID **+** azithromycin 500 mg PO daily (×total 5 days; he has had 4 days of macrolide; one more day completes typical 5-day course).
  2. **Switch to respiratory fluoroquinolone monotherapy:** levofloxacin 750 mg PO daily ×5 days from initiation (he has had 4 days IV, so 1 more day, OR if no IV fluoroquinolone given, full 5 days PO; here recommended 5 days total of effective antibiotic — he's had 4 days IV combination; complete with 1 more day to 5 days total may be reasonable; some institutions extend to 7 days).
- For this patient, choose **path 1**: continue β-lactam + macrolide → fewer drug-interaction concerns, avoids fluoroquinolone class adverse-effect profile (tendinopathy, QT, dysglycemia, aortic), preserves stewardship.

PO ORDER:
- **Amoxicillin-clavulanate 875/125 mg PO BID for 3 more days** (total CAP course 7 days; he has had 4 days of IV β-lactam — total 7 days achieved).
- **Azithromycin 500 mg PO daily × 1 more day** (total 5 days; standard atypical coverage course).
- Discontinue IV ceftriaxone and IV azithromycin.

DRUG INTERACTIONS / TIMING:
- Amoxicillin-clavulanate: take with food to reduce GI upset.
- Azithromycin: avoid with antacids (interferes with absorption; space ≥2 h).
- No significant QT concern at standard doses; no contraindicated medications listed.

MONITORING:
- Clinical: fever, dyspnea, cough, sputum, SaO2 — patient and family to monitor.
- No routine labs needed for a typical CAP completion in a stable patient.
- Watch for C. difficile-associated diarrhea (recent IV β-lactam + macrolide exposure).

DISCHARGE PLANNING:
- Discharge today (day 4 of admission).
- Prescriptions:
  - Amoxicillin-clavulanate 875/125 mg PO BID × 3 days
  - Azithromycin 500 mg PO daily × 1 day
- Inhaler review if applicable.
- Vaccinate: confirm influenza, pneumococcal (PCV20 or PCV15+PPSV23 per age and risk) status — administer or schedule.
- Smoking cessation counseling if smoker.

OUTPATIENT FOLLOW-UP:
- PCP visit within 1 week; counsel to call for persistent or worsening symptoms.
- Chest X-ray repeat at 6 weeks if lobar consolidation initially or risk for lung malignancy (smoker, age >50): rule out post-obstructive pneumonia.
- Return precautions: fever recurrence, increasing dyspnea, hemoptysis, pleuritic chest pain.

WHEN TO NOT CONVERT (didn't apply here):
- Endovascular infection, meningitis, deep abscess without source control, persistent bacteremia, severe immunocompromise.

ALTERNATIVE PATH:
- Not applicable — PO feasible.

SELF-CHECK:
- Total antimicrobial course: 7 days β-lactam + 5 days macrolide → standard CAP duration.
- No drug-drug interactions of concern.
- Discharge medications, follow-up, vaccination, and return precautions all addressed.
```
