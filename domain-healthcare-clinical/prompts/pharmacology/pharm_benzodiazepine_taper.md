## Objective

Design a benzodiazepine taper that minimizes withdrawal symptoms (anxiety, insomnia, autonomic instability, perceptual disturbances, seizures) and supports successful discontinuation: convert from short-half-life agent (alprazolam, lorazepam, oxazepam) to a long-half-life agent (diazepam or clonazepam) when feasible, design a graduated reduction schedule, anticipate when hyperbolic micro-tapering is needed (long-term users), use adjuncts (gabapentin, propranolol, CBT-I), and prevent withdrawal seizure. Output a written schedule with explicit reductions and monitoring.

## Inputs

- Current benzodiazepine (drug, dose, frequency, duration of use)
- Prescribed indication (anxiety, insomnia, seizure disorder, alcohol withdrawal, muscle spasm, procedural)
- Use pattern: stable on prescribed dose vs escalation; episodic vs daily
- Prior taper attempts and outcomes
- Concomitant substances: alcohol, opioids, gabapentinoids, sedative antidepressants
- Comorbid psychiatric (anxiety, PTSD, panic, depression), substance use, sleep, seizure history
- Age, frailty, cognitive status, falls
- Goals: complete discontinuation vs dose minimization; outpatient vs inpatient setting
- Concurrent active therapy (CBT-I, exposure therapy, mood stabilizer)

## Role

Senior psychiatrist / addiction-medicine specialist / primary care physician with experience writing the benzo taper.

## Reasoning Steps

1. **Confirm taper appropriateness and setting.**
   - **Outpatient taper:** stable patient, motivated, no severe abuse, no concurrent active alcohol or opioid misuse, no seizure history requiring control.
   - **Inpatient detox:** very high doses (e.g., >100 mg diazepam equivalent), uncontrolled escalation, prior withdrawal seizure, concurrent severe SUD requiring detox, medically frail.
   - **Indication revisit:** is the original indication still appropriate? If insomnia, substitute CBT-I + non-benzo agent. If anxiety, optimize SSRI/SNRI + therapy. If seizure disorder, do NOT taper if benzo is providing seizure control without alternative.

2. **Convert to equivalent diazepam (or clonazepam) for stable taper.**
   - **Approximate equivalents to diazepam 10 mg:**
     - Alprazolam 1 mg
     - Lorazepam 2 mg
     - Oxazepam 30 mg
     - Temazepam 20 mg
     - Clonazepam 0.5 mg
     - Chlordiazepoxide 25 mg
     - Triazolam 0.5 mg
     - Midazolam 5 mg
     - Flurazepam 30 mg
   - **Why convert:** longer half-life smoothes withdrawal (less interdose dip → fewer breakthrough symptoms); easier to adjust small increments; diazepam available in many strengths and is liquid-soluble for micro-tapers.
   - **Equivalency caveats:** clinical equivalence varies between individuals and references; start with conservative conversion and titrate.
   - Some clinicians taper in the original agent if the patient is stable and the agent's PK supports tapering — particularly clonazepam (relatively long half-life ~30–40 h).

3. **Choose taper rate based on duration and pattern.**
   - **Short duration (<3 months) on therapeutic doses:** taper 10–25% of dose every 1–2 weeks; complete in 1–3 months.
   - **Long-term use (>1 year), high dose, or prior failed taper:** much slower — 5–10% every 2–4 weeks; some patients require months to years for successful discontinuation.
   - **Hyperbolic / micro-tapering:** at lower doses, equal percentage reductions become smaller absolute amounts (reflecting receptor occupancy curve nonlinearity). Some patients especially sensitive — use liquid diazepam (1 mg/mL or 5 mg/mL) or compounded smaller doses; reduce by 0.25–0.5 mg increments at the low end.
   - **Slow down or hold** at any point if withdrawal symptoms become intolerable; do not race to complete.

4. **Anticipate withdrawal symptoms and their pattern.**
   - **Onset:** depends on half-life of agent. Short half-life (alprazolam): symptoms within 6–24 h of missed dose. Long half-life (diazepam): symptoms 2–7 days after dose reduction.
   - **Symptom spectrum:**
     - Anxiety / panic / restlessness
     - Insomnia, nightmares, vivid dreams
     - Autonomic: tachycardia, sweating, tremor, hypertension
     - Perceptual: tinnitus, dysmorphopsia, hypersensitivity to light/sound
     - GI: nausea, vomiting, abdominal cramps
     - Muscle: stiffness, twitching, cramping
     - Severe (especially rapid taper of high dose): hallucinations, paranoia, seizures, delirium
   - Differentiate from recurrence of original anxiety (which can also occur).

5. **Adjunct therapies during taper.**
   - **Gabapentin** 100–300 mg TID, titrate to 1200–1800 mg/day: anxiolytic, helps withdrawal symptoms, may reduce craving. Watch sedation, dizziness; renal-adjust.
   - **Pregabalin** similar role; less commonly used.
   - **Propranolol** 10–40 mg TID for autonomic symptoms (tachycardia, tremor); avoid in asthma, bradycardia.
   - **Clonidine** 0.1 mg BID for autonomic; orthostasis caution.
   - **Trazodone** 50–100 mg qHS for insomnia; non-addictive sleep aid.
   - **Mirtazapine** 15 mg qHS for insomnia and anxiety.
   - **CBT for insomnia** (CBT-I) — most evidence-based long-term insomnia therapy.
   - **Mindfulness / relaxation training** during withdrawal.
   - **SSRI/SNRI optimization** for underlying anxiety disorder (long lead-time; start at least 4–6 weeks before active taper).

6. **Pre-taper preparation.**
   - Establish therapeutic alliance; clear shared goal (full discontinuation vs dose minimization).
   - Address underlying indication: optimize SSRI/SNRI for anxiety; CBT-I for insomnia.
   - Engage support: family, therapist, peer-support if applicable.
   - Educate: timeline, expected symptoms, tools.
   - Avoid concurrent substance use: abstain from alcohol; minimize opioid; counsel on caffeine.
   - Set up frequent contact: phone/visit weekly during active reduction.

7. **Seizure prevention.**
   - Never abruptly stop benzodiazepine in long-term users — risk of withdrawal seizure.
   - For high-dose long-term users (e.g., diazepam ≥40 mg/day or equivalent for >6 months), consider inpatient supervised taper or particularly slow outpatient taper.
   - If seizure history (epilepsy on benzo, alcohol withdrawal seizure history): coordinate with neurology; consider AED bridge during taper.
   - Educate patient on missed-dose risk; emergency contact if seizure-like symptoms.

8. **Special populations.**
   - **Elderly:** higher fall and cognitive risk on benzos; especially important to taper; but go slow.
   - **Pregnancy:** consider risk-benefit; rapid weaning risks withdrawal in neonate (NAS) if continued through delivery; managed-care taper preferred when possible.
   - **Polypharmacy with opioids:** dangerous; consider simultaneous opioid reduction or naloxone availability; CDC and FDA boxed warning.
   - **PTSD with sleep / hyperarousal:** prazosin for nightmares; CBT-I; address core trauma.

9. **Document the schedule and reassessment plan.**

## Worked Example

**Input:** 56-year-old man on lorazepam 1 mg PO TID (3 mg/day) for chronic anxiety ×7 years. Stable on SSRI (sertraline 100 mg) ×2 years. Wants to discontinue benzo due to memory and balance concerns. No alcohol/opioids. No seizure history.

**Output:**

```
PATIENT SNAPSHOT:
- 56 y M, lorazepam 1 mg TID (3 mg/day) ×7 years.
- Indication: anxiety; on stable SSRI; cognitive and gait concerns prompting taper.
- Outpatient. Long-term user → very slow taper indicated.

CONVERSION TO LONG-HALF-LIFE AGENT:
- Lorazepam 3 mg/day = diazepam ~15 mg/day equivalent.
- Convert over 1 week:
  - Day 1–3: lorazepam 0.5 mg TID + diazepam 5 mg qHS.
  - Day 4–7: lorazepam 0.5 mg BID + diazepam 5 mg AM + 5 mg qHS.
  - Day 8 onward: diazepam 5 mg BID + 5 mg qHS (15 mg/day total).
- Allow 1–2 weeks at stable diazepam dose before starting taper to confirm equivalence.

TAPER SCHEDULE (slow, given 7-year use):
- **Weeks 1–2 (stabilization):** diazepam 15 mg/day (5 AM, 5 noon, 5 qHS).
- **Weeks 3–4:** **14 mg/day** (5 AM, 4 noon, 5 qHS — about 7% reduction).
- **Weeks 5–6:** 13 mg/day (4 AM, 4 noon, 5 qHS).
- **Weeks 7–8:** 12 mg/day (4 AM, 4 noon, 4 qHS).
- **Weeks 9–10:** 11 mg/day (4 AM, 3 noon, 4 qHS).
- **Weeks 11–12:** 10 mg/day (4 AM, 2 noon, 4 qHS).
- Continue ~10% reductions every 2 weeks until 5 mg/day reached (~week 24).
- **From 5 mg/day to off (hyperbolic micro-taper):**
  - Use diazepam oral solution 1 mg/mL.
  - Reduce by 0.5 mg every 2 weeks; at very low doses (≤2 mg/day), 0.25 mg every 2 weeks.
  - Expected completion: ~12 months from start.

ADJUNCTS:
- **Gabapentin 100 mg TID** starting 1 week before taper begins; titrate to 300 mg TID if needed for breakthrough anxiety / sleep — adjuvant anxiolytic during taper.
- **Trazodone 50 mg qHS prn for insomnia.**
- **SSRI optimization:** sertraline 100 mg is reasonable; consider increase to 150 mg if anxiety re-emerges.
- **CBT-I and CBT for anxiety**: referral now, start in parallel with taper.
- **Propranolol 10–20 mg PO BID prn** for autonomic symptoms (tachycardia, tremor) — episodic use.
- **Caffeine reduction**, mindfulness training, exercise routine reinforced.

MONITORING:
- Weekly phone check during active taper steps (first 6 weeks especially).
- Office visit every 4 weeks; bring symptom log.
- Anxiety scale (GAD-7) at each visit.
- Sleep diary.
- Vital signs (BP, HR) at office visits — autonomic withdrawal signs.

PAUSE / SLOW-DOWN TRIGGERS:
- GAD-7 rise ≥5 from baseline.
- Sleep disturbance unmanaged by trazodone / non-pharm.
- Major autonomic symptoms (BP rise, HR >100, tremor, sweating).
- Acute stressor (job change, bereavement, illness) — hold at current dose.

REASSESSMENT MILESTONES:
- Conversion complete & stable: week 2.
- 50% reduction (7.5 mg/day diazepam): ~week 16.
- 5 mg/day: ~week 24.
- Off: ~week 50–52 (12 months total course is realistic).

WHEN TO ESCALATE:
- Withdrawal seizure: ED, inpatient stabilization.
- Severe psychiatric decompensation: psychiatry consultation, dose hold, possible upward titration to last tolerated dose.
- Patient inability to tolerate any reduction → addiction medicine consultation, consider compounded micro-doses, peer-support program (e.g., Ashton Manual-aligned tapers).

FOLLOW-UP:
- Weekly first 2 months, then biweekly, then monthly.
- Post-discontinuation: 1, 3, 6, 12 months — relapse prevention; anxiety management; CBT continuation; medication review.
- Reinforce: protracted withdrawal can last weeks to months; symptoms gradually fade.
```
