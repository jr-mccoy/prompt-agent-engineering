## Objective

Design a taper off an SSRI or SNRI to minimize discontinuation syndrome and false-positive relapse, with attention to drug half-life (paroxetine and venlafaxine highest risk; fluoxetine effectively self-tapers), prior duration of treatment, history of discontinuation symptoms, and patient sensitivity. Specify hyperbolic / micro-taper logic when needed, identify FINISH symptoms early, and distinguish discontinuation from depression / anxiety relapse.

## Inputs

- Current SSRI / SNRI (drug, dose, duration of treatment)
- Indication and remission status (MDD, GAD, panic, OCD, PTSD, social anxiety; current PHQ-9 / GAD-7)
- Reason for stopping (planned discontinuation after maintenance phase, pregnancy planning, intolerable side effect, ineffective)
- Prior discontinuation attempts and symptom history
- Concomitant medications (especially serotonergic — risk of serotonin syndrome if cross-taper)
- Patient sensitivity (history of intolerance, recurrent depression, anxious temperament)
- Setting and follow-up availability

## Role

Senior psychiatrist / primary care prescriber writing the taper plan with explicit rationale.

## Reasoning Steps

1. **Confirm appropriateness of discontinuation.**
   - **First episode of MDD:** continue antidepressant 6–9 months after remission, then consider taper.
   - **Recurrent MDD (≥3 episodes):** indefinite maintenance often recommended; taper may not be appropriate.
   - **Persistent residual symptoms:** taper risks relapse; address symptoms first.
   - **Pregnancy planning:** discuss continuing vs tapering; relapse risk during pregnancy is high; many patients stay on antidepressant; sertraline most-studied.
   - **Intolerable side effect:** consider switch rather than full discontinuation.

2. **Map taper rate to drug half-life and prior duration.**
   - **Half-life governs how quickly drug levels fall after dose reductions:**
     - **Fluoxetine** (t½ 1–4 days, active metabolite norfluoxetine 7–15 days) — effectively self-tapers; can often stop abruptly from 20 mg without significant FINISH symptoms. From 60–80 mg, brief taper helpful.
     - **Sertraline** (t½ 26 h, weak active metabolite) — moderate; 25–50% reductions every 1–2 weeks usually adequate.
     - **Escitalopram / citalopram** (t½ 27–32 h) — moderate.
     - **Paroxetine** (t½ 21 h, no active metabolite, CYP2D6 substrate with possible autoinhibition causing nonlinear PK) — high FINISH risk; slow taper essential.
     - **Venlafaxine** (t½ 5 h, active metabolite ODV t½ 11 h, no autoinhibition; short effective half-life) — very high FINISH risk; slow taper essential; "venlafaxine flu."
     - **Desvenlafaxine** (t½ 11 h) — moderate-high risk.
     - **Duloxetine** (t½ 12 h) — moderate.
     - **Vortioxetine** (t½ 66 h) — long; lower FINISH risk.
   - **Duration of prior treatment matters:**
     - <6 weeks: usually no taper needed.
     - 6 weeks – 6 months: moderate taper (1–4 weeks).
     - 6 months – 1 year: 4–8 weeks taper.
     - >1 year, especially years: prolonged taper, often 3–6 months; hyperbolic micro-taper may be needed.

3. **Standard taper rate.**
   - General rule of thumb: reduce dose by 25–50% every 1–2 weeks, watch for symptoms.
   - **Example fluoxetine:** 20 mg → 10 mg × 1 week → stop; or stop from 20 mg.
   - **Example sertraline:** 100 mg → 75 mg × 1 week → 50 mg × 1 week → 25 mg × 1 week → stop.
   - **Example paroxetine 40 mg:** 30 mg × 2 weeks → 20 mg × 2 weeks → 15 mg × 2 weeks → 10 mg × 2 weeks → 5 mg × 2 weeks → 2.5 mg × 2 weeks → stop; longer if FINISH symptoms.
   - **Example venlafaxine XR 225 mg:** 150 mg × 2 weeks → 112.5 mg × 2 weeks → 75 mg × 2 weeks → 37.5 mg × 2 weeks → stop; very slow with breaks if symptoms.

4. **Hyperbolic micro-taper for sensitive patients.**
   - Pharmacology basis: SERT occupancy follows a saturable hyperbolic curve. At higher doses, large dose reductions cause relatively small drops in receptor occupancy; at lower doses, small dose reductions cause large drops in occupancy. Linear reductions therefore produce nonlinear withdrawal experiences — worst at the end.
   - Practical solution: switch to liquid formulation (paroxetine 10 mg/5 mL, sertraline 20 mg/mL, fluoxetine 20 mg/5 mL, escitalopram 5 mg/5 mL) or compounded smaller doses; reduce by smaller percentages (e.g., 10%) at the lower end.
   - **Example paroxetine hyperbolic taper after reaching 10 mg:** 9 mg → 8.1 mg → 7.3 mg → 6.6 mg → 5.9 mg → ... reducing by 10% of current dose every 2–4 weeks until trivial dose, then stop.

5. **Recognize and manage FINISH symptoms.**
   - **F**lu-like: malaise, myalgia, fatigue, sweats.
   - **I**nsomnia: vivid dreams, nightmares.
   - **N**ausea: GI upset.
   - **I**mbalance: dizziness, vertigo.
   - **S**ensory disturbances: electric-shock or "brain zap" sensations, paresthesias.
   - **H**yperarousal: irritability, agitation, anxiety.
   - Typical onset: within 1–3 days of dose reduction (shorter half-life agents).
   - Duration: typically 1–2 weeks; can be longer with severe sensitivity.

6. **Distinguish discontinuation syndrome from relapse.**
   - **Discontinuation:** appears within days of dose reduction; resolves on resuming prior dose within 24–48 h; includes flu-like, dizziness, sensory disturbances that are not classic depression.
   - **Relapse:** typically appears over weeks; sustained low mood, anhedonia, hopelessness, sleep and appetite changes that resemble the original depression.
   - If unclear, resume the prior dose; symptoms attributable to discontinuation will improve within 1–2 days, supporting that interpretation.

7. **Manage paroxetine and venlafaxine specifically.**
   - **Paroxetine:** consider switching to fluoxetine 20 mg ×2 weeks first, then taper fluoxetine (or stop). This leverages fluoxetine's long half-life to bypass paroxetine's withdrawal. Caveat: monitor for slight serotonergic syndrome risk during overlap.
   - **Venlafaxine:** can switch to fluoxetine 20 mg (after stabilization) and then taper fluoxetine; same logic.
   - These bridge strategies are well-described in psychiatric literature for difficult tapers.

8. **Adjunct measures.**
   - Education and validation: discontinuation symptoms are real, not weakness or failure.
   - Slow taper rate at the lower end (where most symptoms occur).
   - CBT / therapy support during transition.
   - For akathisia / agitation: clonazepam short-course (controversial — adds another dependence).
   - For sleep: trazodone, mirtazapine, sleep hygiene, CBT-I.

9. **Document and follow up.**

## Worked Example

**Input:** 42-year-old woman, on paroxetine 40 mg PO daily ×3 years for GAD (now in remission). Wants to stop because she is planning pregnancy in 6 months. Prior single attempt to discontinue 2 years ago failed at week 1 with severe "brain zaps," nausea, dizziness — restarted same day.

**Output:**

```
PATIENT SNAPSHOT:
- 42 y F, paroxetine 40 mg ×3 years for GAD, in remission (GAD-7 = 3).
- Planning pregnancy in 6 months → motivated to discontinue (or transition to lower-risk agent).
- Prior failed taper with marked FINISH symptoms → known high sensitivity.

PRELIMINARY DECISION:
- Paroxetine in pregnancy: associated with small cardiac defect signal (FDA category D); avoid if possible. Two options:
  1. **Switch to sertraline first**, stabilize, then taper sertraline later if she wants to be off. Sertraline is preferred SSRI in pregnancy.
  2. **Taper paroxetine to off** before conception. Higher risk of relapse, but if successful she can be SSRI-free during pregnancy.
- Given her remission, prior single failed taper, and time pressure: **Option 1 is preferred** — bridge to sertraline now; consider eventual sertraline taper post-pregnancy if desired.

PLAN: cross-taper paroxetine → sertraline, then maintain sertraline through preconception and pregnancy.

CROSS-TAPER SCHEDULE:
- Day 1–7: paroxetine 40 mg + sertraline 25 mg.
- Day 8–14: paroxetine 30 mg + sertraline 50 mg.
- Day 15–21: paroxetine 20 mg + sertraline 75 mg.
- Day 22–28: paroxetine 10 mg + sertraline 100 mg.
- Day 29–35: paroxetine 5 mg + sertraline 100 mg.
- Day 36 onward: sertraline 100 mg daily; paroxetine off.

DRUG-SPECIFIC CONSIDERATIONS:
- Paroxetine t½ ~21 h; high FINISH risk → slow taper essential.
- Sertraline t½ ~26 h; later if desired, would taper over similar interval.
- Bridging via parallel cross-taper covers serotonergic activity throughout; minor risk of serotonin syndrome during overlap — monitor for tremor, hyperreflexia, clonus, autonomic instability.
- Liquid paroxetine (10 mg/5 mL) available for micro-doses if needed at the bottom of taper.

ANTICIPATED SYMPTOMS:
- FINISH risk highest at 40 → 30 mg and 10 → 5 mg transitions.
- Counsel on: brain zaps, dizziness, nausea, anxiety, vivid dreams, flu-like sensation.
- Resolution: typically within 1–2 weeks of stable dose.
- If symptoms emerge: slow next reduction by extending interval to 2 weeks; consider 5 mg → 3 mg → 2 mg → 0 with liquid formulation.

DISTINGUISHING DISCONTINUATION FROM RELAPSE:
- Discontinuation: physical sensations (zaps, dizziness, nausea), within days of reduction.
- Relapse: GAD-7 elevation, return of worry/restlessness/insomnia over weeks; less prominent physical symptoms.

ADJUNCTS:
- Sleep: trazodone 50 mg qHS prn if insomnia during transition.
- Anxiety acute: short-course hydroxyzine 25 mg prn if needed; avoid benzodiazepines.
- CBT for GAD continuation — booster sessions during taper.
- Lifestyle: exercise, sleep hygiene, caffeine moderation.

MONITORING:
- Phone or visit weekly during the 5-week cross-taper.
- GAD-7 weekly.
- FINISH symptom check at each contact.
- Office visit at week 4 to assess transition stability.
- Pregnancy plan: folic acid 400–800 µg/day pre-conception; perinatal psychiatry consultation early.

PAUSE / SLOW-DOWN TRIGGERS:
- Severe FINISH symptoms → extend current step or revert to prior step; resume next step at slower rate.
- GAD-7 ≥10 → hold transition; reassess.
- Suicidality, severe mood deterioration → resume effective dose; full reassessment.

WHEN TO ESCALATE:
- Frank GAD relapse on sertraline 100 mg → titrate up (150 → 200 mg).
- Persistent intolerance → consider venlafaxine SNRI (acceptable in pregnancy with caution) or vortioxetine (less data in pregnancy, may need to defer).
- Perinatal psychiatry consultation if any complexity in pregnancy planning.

FOLLOW-UP:
- Weekly during 5-week cross-taper.
- Then monthly for 3 months on sertraline alone.
- Pre-conception visit: confirm dose, folate, perinatal plan.
- Pregnancy management: continue sertraline; monitor symptoms; obstetric coordination.
- Postpartum: monitor for postpartum depression (high risk in history of GAD/MDD).
```
