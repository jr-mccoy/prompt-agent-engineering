## Objective

Design an opioid taper for a chronic-pain patient on long-term opioid therapy, distinguishing whether the appropriate path is dose reduction, transition to buprenorphine, transition to methadone (OTP), or continued chronic opioid therapy with optimization. Manage withdrawal, control pain with multimodal alternatives, screen for opioid use disorder, prevent abandonment, and reduce overdose risk. Output a written plan with rationale, taper schedule, adjuncts, and safety measures.

## Inputs

- Current regimen (drug, dose, frequency, formulation; total daily MME), date of initiation, indication
- Functional outcomes on current regimen: pain (0–10), function (sleep, ambulation, work), mood, side effects
- OUD screen (DSM-5 criteria — loss of control, craving, tolerance, withdrawal, social/occupational impairment, continued use despite harm; PDMP review; urine drug screen results; aberrant behaviors)
- Concomitant CNS depressants (benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, alcohol, antipsychotics)
- Mental health (depression, PTSD, anxiety, suicidality)
- Prior overdose, near-overdose, ED visits for opioid-related events
- Reason for taper: patient request, lack of benefit, intolerable side effects, OUD identified, MME reduction per institutional / CDC guideline, transition to buprenorphine
- Goals (full discontinuation vs dose minimization; functional gains; OUD treatment)
- Setting and support (outpatient PCP, pain specialist, OUD specialist, family)

## Role

Senior pain-medicine, addiction-medicine, or primary-care physician writing the opioid taper plan with attention to autonomy, safety, and trauma-informed approach.

## Reasoning Steps

1. **Determine the appropriate path.**
   - **No OUD, on chronic opioid therapy without benefit or with side effects:** **gradual dose reduction** with adjuncts. CDC 2022 emphasizes individualized tapers; no specific MME mandate.
   - **OUD identified (meeting DSM-5 criteria):** **transition to buprenorphine or methadone** (OUD MAT), NOT taper to off without MAT support — high mortality risk if abrupt stop or rapid taper.
   - **Continued benefit, no OUD, manageable risk:** consider continuing with risk-mitigation rather than tapering (patient autonomy + harm-reduction approach).
   - **Mixed picture (some aberrant behaviors, unclear OUD):** addiction-medicine consultation before deciding path.

2. **Taper rate for chronic-pain patient without OUD (no MAT).**
   - **Standard:** 5–10% reduction every 4 weeks (CDC 2022; AAFP).
   - **Slower:** 5% every 4 weeks for patients on opioid therapy >1 year, those with anxiety / OUD-adjacent features, or sensitive patients.
   - **Faster (rare in chronic outpatient):** 10–20% every 1–2 weeks acceptable in motivated patients with shorter duration; never abrupt for chronic users.
   - Never abruptly stop opioid in chronic users — high withdrawal severity and risk of return to use with intervening lower tolerance → overdose.

3. **Choose which dose to reduce first.**
   - Round to nearest available product strength.
   - If on long-acting + short-acting: typically start by tapering short-acting / breakthrough doses (often more discretionary, less anchoring); then taper long-acting.
   - If on combination products (e.g., hydrocodone-acetaminophen 10/325 mg q6h): can taper number of doses per day, or shift to pure opioid component (hydrocodone tablet) to enable finer titration.
   - If on transdermal fentanyl: change patch dose at intervals or transition to oral first.

4. **Manage withdrawal symptoms during taper.**
   - Anticipate: anxiety, restlessness, sweating, lacrimation, rhinorrhea, yawning, piloerection, nausea, abdominal cramping, diarrhea, myalgia, mydriasis, dysphoria.
   - Adjuncts:
     - **Clonidine 0.1 mg PO TID** (or transdermal patch 0.1 mg/24 h) — autonomic symptoms; orthostasis caution.
     - **Lofexidine** 0.54 mg every 4–6 h, up to 2.88 mg/day — FDA-approved for opioid withdrawal; less hypotensive than clonidine; expensive.
     - **NSAIDs / acetaminophen** for myalgia.
     - **Ondansetron, prochlorperazine** for nausea.
     - **Loperamide** for diarrhea (counsel on cardiac risk if very high doses).
     - **Hydroxyzine, trazodone, melatonin** for anxiety / insomnia.
     - **Gabapentin** for anxiety, sleep, neuropathic pain (start 100–300 mg TID, titrate).

5. **Address pain with multimodal alternatives.**
   - **Pharmacologic non-opioid:** acetaminophen, NSAIDs (when safe), topicals (lidocaine 5% patch, diclofenac gel, capsaicin), gabapentinoids, SNRIs (duloxetine for chronic musculoskeletal and neuropathic), TCAs (low-dose amitriptyline), muscle relaxants short-term.
   - **Non-pharmacologic:** PT, occupational therapy, cognitive behavioral therapy for chronic pain (CBT-CP), acceptance and commitment therapy (ACT), mindfulness-based stress reduction, yoga, tai chi.
   - **Interventional:** trigger-point injections, joint injections, nerve blocks, radiofrequency ablation, spinal cord stimulation, epidurals for radicular pain.
   - **Cannabis:** evidence for some pain conditions; legal context varies.

6. **OUD screen and transition to MAT when indicated.**
   - **Buprenorphine transition:**
     - Standard induction: stop full agonist opioid; wait until withdrawal (COWS ≥8–10); give buprenorphine 2–4 mg SL, observe 1–2 h; titrate up to 16 mg/day day 1; maintenance 8–24 mg/day.
     - Micro-induction (preferred for fentanyl-dependent patients to avoid precipitated withdrawal): start buprenorphine 0.5 mg SL daily, titrate up over 7–10 days while gradually decreasing full agonist; conventional methods at LOWER MME also possible.
     - X-waiver no longer required (Mainstreaming Addiction Treatment Act 2023); any provider can prescribe buprenorphine for OUD.
   - **Methadone (only via opioid treatment program — OTP — for OUD):** referral to OTP; methadone 20–30 mg daily induction, titrate slowly.
   - **Naltrexone (oral or long-acting injectable — Vivitrol 380 mg IM q4 weeks):** opioid antagonist; requires complete detox before initiation (7–14 days off short-acting; longer for methadone, buprenorphine).

7. **Safety measures.**
   - **Naloxone prescription** for everyone with opioid use disorder, on chronic opioid therapy ≥50 MME, or with risk factors (concurrent benzo, prior overdose) — nasal 4 mg, 2 doses, train household.
   - **Reduce concurrent CNS depressants:** taper benzodiazepines in parallel (slow benzo taper if also on long-term — see benzo taper prompt).
   - **PDMP review** at every visit.
   - **Urine drug screen** periodically; interpret thoughtfully (synthetic opioids may not detect on standard panels; fentanyl test strips for those at risk).
   - **No abandonment**: continue clinical relationship through taper; do not discharge for slow progress.
   - **Suicide screening** at each visit; CDC notes increased suicidality risk during tapers.

8. **Functional reassessment, not pain-score chasing.**
   - At each visit, assess function — sleep, work, social, mood, activity tolerance.
   - Often function improves with taper despite stable or transient worse pain ratings.
   - Validate experience; acknowledge withdrawal is real but distinct from underlying pain.

9. **Avoid forced tapers.**
   - CDC 2022 explicitly cautions against forced rapid tapers in chronic-pain patients (associated with overdose and suicide); engage patient in shared decision-making; pause if needed.

## Output Format

```
PATIENT SNAPSHOT:
- Current opioid regimen + MME
- Indication, duration on chronic opioid therapy
- OUD screen result
- Mental health, concurrent CNS depressants, prior overdose
- Functional status, goals

PATH SELECTION:
- Dose reduction (no OUD)
- Transition to buprenorphine (OUD or high overdose risk + co-morbidity)
- Methadone via OTP (high-dose OUD, complex social need)
- Naltrexone (after detox; specific motivated patients)
- Continue with optimization (chronic pain with benefit, no OUD)

TAPER SCHEDULE (if dose reduction):
Week/Month 1: [dose, MME]
Month 2: [dose, MME]
... [5–10% reductions every 2–4 weeks]
Final: [off or maintenance dose]

WITHDRAWAL ADJUNCTS:
- Clonidine 0.1 mg PO TID (or lofexidine)
- NSAIDs / acetaminophen
- Ondansetron / loperamide / hydroxyzine
- Sleep aid (trazodone, melatonin)
- Gabapentin if neuropathic or sleep

PAIN MANAGEMENT ALTERNATIVES:
- Pharm: SNRI, gabapentinoid, topical, TCA
- Non-pharm: PT, CBT-CP, mindfulness, acupuncture
- Interventional: injections, RFA, SCS if applicable

NALOXONE:
- Nasal 4 mg, 2 sprays; household training; 911 call after use

PDMP, UDS, MENTAL HEALTH MONITORING:
- PDMP check at every visit
- UDS quarterly or as clinically indicated
- Suicide screen at each visit
- Mood / sleep / function review

WHEN TO PAUSE OR CONSULT:
- Worsening function or mental health on taper → pause, address
- OUD signs emerging → addiction medicine; transition to MAT
- Suicidality, severe withdrawal: stop taper; reassess
- Pregnancy: methadone or buprenorphine for OUD; do not abruptly stop opioids

FOLLOW-UP:
- Visits aligned with taper steps (every 2–4 weeks initially)
- Reassessment of function, withdrawal, mood, suicidality
- No abandonment; long-term continuity
```

## Worked Example

**Input:** 54-year-old man on oxycodone CR 40 mg PO BID + oxycodone IR 10 mg q6h prn (often using all 4 doses; total ~120 mg/day oxycodone = 180 MME/day) for 8 years for chronic low back pain after failed fusion. Function declining (no longer working, sedated mid-day, constipation severe). UDS consistent. PDMP unremarkable. Patient agrees to attempt taper. No OUD by DSM-5. Mild depression on sertraline.

**Output:**

```
PATIENT SNAPSHOT:
- 54 y M, oxycodone CR 40 BID + IR 10 q6h prn (4×/d) = 60 mg CR + 40 mg IR = 100 mg/day oxycodone = 150 MME/day (using MME factor 1.5 for oxycodone). Note: if reported 120 mg/day with all 4 IR + 80 mg CR = wait, let's recompute: oxycodone CR 40 mg BID = 80 mg + IR 10 q6h × 4 = 40 mg → total 120 mg/day = 180 MME/day. (Confirming 180 MME/day.)
- 8 years on chronic opioid therapy. Function declining. UDS clean. No OUD.
- Sertraline-treated mild depression.

PATH SELECTION:
- **Dose reduction** path (no OUD, declining function on current dose, patient consents).
- Multimodal alternatives optimized in parallel.

TAPER SCHEDULE (5–10% every 4 weeks, individualized):
- **Month 0–1:** current regimen (180 MME/day); engage in adjuncts and alternatives.
- **Month 1–2:** **reduce IR by 10 mg/day** — IR 10 mg q8h prn (3×/d max) = 30 mg IR; total 110 mg/day oxycodone = 165 MME/day (8% reduction).
- **Month 2–3:** **reduce IR further** — IR 10 mg q12h prn (2×/d) = 20 mg IR; total 100 mg = 150 MME/day.
- **Month 3–4:** **reduce CR** — oxycodone CR 30 mg AM + 40 mg PM = 70 mg CR + 20 mg IR = 90 mg/day = 135 MME/day.
- **Month 4–5:** CR 30 mg BID = 60 mg CR + 20 mg IR = 80 mg/day = 120 MME/day.
- **Month 5–6:** CR 20 mg AM + 30 mg PM + IR 10 mg BID prn = 50 + 20 = 70 mg/day = 105 MME/day.
- **Month 6–7:** CR 20 mg BID + IR 10 mg daily prn = 40 + 10 = 50 mg/day = 75 MME/day.
- **Month 7–8:** CR 10 mg AM + 20 mg PM + IR 10 mg prn = 30 + 10 = 40 mg/day = 60 MME/day. Reassess goal.
- **Months 8–12 and beyond:** continue 5–10% monthly reductions if patient consents and function holds.
- Reassessment at each step: function, withdrawal severity, pain, mood, suicidality. Pause if needed.

WITHDRAWAL ADJUNCTS:
- **Clonidine 0.1 mg PO TID** during reductions for autonomic symptoms (orthostasis monitoring).
- **NSAIDs / acetaminophen** scheduled for baseline analgesia (assess renal, GI safety).
- **Ondansetron 4 mg PO q8h prn** nausea.
- **Loperamide 2 mg prn** diarrhea (use safely).
- **Hydroxyzine 25–50 mg qHS prn** anxiety / sleep.
- **Trazodone 50 mg qHS prn** insomnia.
- **Gabapentin 300 mg TID** (titrate) — for sleep / anxiety / neuropathic pain (also adjunctive analgesia in chronic low back pain).

PAIN MANAGEMENT ALTERNATIVES:
- **SNRI**: switch sertraline to duloxetine 30 mg → 60 mg daily — covers depression + chronic musculoskeletal / back pain.
- **Topical**: lidocaine 5% patch up to 12 h/day; diclofenac gel.
- **Physical therapy** referral for graded exercise / core strengthening / posture.
- **CBT for chronic pain** (CBT-CP) referral.
- **Pain psychology / mindfulness**.
- **Interventional**: evaluate for radiofrequency ablation, epidural steroid injection, spinal cord stimulation if hardware fusion has trigger sites.
- **Acupuncture**, yoga as adjuncts.
- **Functional goal-setting**: return to walking 30 min/day, sleep 7 h, social activity.

NALOXONE:
- Nasal naloxone 4 mg, 2 sprays — train patient and spouse; 911 after use; replace expired.

MONITORING:
- PDMP at every visit (every 4 weeks during taper).
- UDS every 3 months and as clinically indicated.
- PHQ-9 monthly (depression / suicidality screen).
- COWS or COSS at visits during reductions to gauge withdrawal severity.
- Functional measures (sleep diary, walking minutes, mood, work attempts).

WHEN TO PAUSE OR CONSULT:
- PHQ-9 rises, suicidality emerges → pause taper, escalate mental health support, consider holding or reversing.
- COWS ≥10 unmanaged → slow next step or pause.
- OUD signs (loss of control, escalation, aberrant behaviors) → addiction medicine consult, consider transition to buprenorphine.
- Pregnancy: not applicable here.

FOLLOW-UP:
- Visits every 2–4 weeks during taper (in person or telehealth).
- Continuity of care assured; no discharge for slow progress.
- Reassess functional gains and patient experience; renegotiate goals as needed.
- Document shared decision-making at each step.
```
