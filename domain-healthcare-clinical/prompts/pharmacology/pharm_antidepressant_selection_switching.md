## Objective

Select an antidepressant for a specific patient and clinical context, OR execute a switch from one antidepressant to another safely: choose by side-effect profile and comorbidity match, plan washout / cross-taper based on pharmacology, anticipate discontinuation syndrome, screen for serotonin syndrome risk in transitions, and counsel on time course and reassessment. Output a written plan with drug names, doses, taper rates, and follow-up.

## Inputs

- Primary diagnosis (MDD, persistent depressive disorder, GAD, panic, OCD, PTSD, social anxiety, bipolar depression — important caveat, see step 3)
- Severity (mild, moderate, severe, with/without psychosis or suicidality)
- Comorbidities (anxiety, pain, insomnia, sexual dysfunction, weight, eating disorder, ADHD, substance use, pregnancy/lactation, cardiovascular, seizure, migraine)
- Current and prior antidepressant trials (drug, dose, duration, response, side effects, reason for stopping)
- Concurrent medications including MAOIs, linezolid, methylene blue, triptans, fentanyl, tramadol, SSRIs in combination, lithium, antipsychotics, OTC supplements (St. John's wort, 5-HTP, tryptophan)
- Pregnancy / breastfeeding status, plans for pregnancy
- Family history of response to specific agents
- Patient preference and access (cost, insurance, prior failures)

## Role

Senior psychiatrist (or primary care physician comfortable with psychopharmacology) writing the prescription with explicit rationale for selection, switch plan, and follow-up cadence.

## Reasoning Steps

1. **Confirm diagnosis and rule out bipolar before starting an antidepressant alone.**
   - Screen for prior manic / hypomanic episodes (MDQ, CIDI-3, clinical history). Antidepressant monotherapy in bipolar depression can precipitate mania, mixed states, or rapid cycling.
   - If bipolar, prefer mood stabilizer-based approach (lithium, lamotrigine, quetiapine, lurasidone, cariprazine, olanzapine-fluoxetine combination) — antidepressant only with mood stabilizer cover.
   - Severe MDD with psychosis: antidepressant + antipsychotic, or ECT.
   - Active suicidality / severe agitation: same-day specialist input, safety plan, consider hospitalization.

2. **Choose first-line class.**
   - **SSRIs (first-line for most MDD, GAD, panic, OCD, PTSD, social anxiety):** sertraline 25–50 mg daily → 100–200 mg; escitalopram 10 mg → 20 mg; fluoxetine 20 mg → 40–60 mg; paroxetine 20 mg → 40–60 mg; citalopram 20 mg → 40 mg (max 40 mg due to QTc; max 20 mg if >60 y or hepatic impairment); fluvoxamine for OCD.
   - **SNRIs (option in MDD with neuropathic pain, fibromyalgia, GAD):** venlafaxine XR 37.5–75 mg → 225 mg; duloxetine 30 → 60–120 mg; desvenlafaxine 50 → 100 mg; levomilnacipran 20–120 mg.
   - **Atypicals:**
     - **Bupropion** 150 mg → 300 mg XL: no sexual side effects, weight neutral / loss, helpful for fatigue/anhedonia; avoid in seizure disorder, eating disorder (bulimia/anorexia), abrupt alcohol/benzo withdrawal. Useful add-on or alternative when sexual dysfunction is barrier.
     - **Mirtazapine** 15 → 45 mg qHS: sedating (esp. at low dose), appetite stimulation, useful in insomnia and weight loss; orthostasis, dry mouth, increased TG/cholesterol.
     - **Trazodone** 50–100 mg qHS: low-dose for insomnia; full antidepressant doses (300–600 mg) rarely used due to sedation.
     - **Vortioxetine** 10–20 mg PO daily: multimodal (5-HT3 antagonist, 5-HT7 antagonist, 5-HT1A partial agonist, 5-HT1B partial agonist, 5-HT reuptake inhibitor); cognitive benefits reported; lower sexual dysfunction than SSRI.
     - **Vilazodone** 20–40 mg PO daily (5-HT1A partial agonist + SRI); take with food for absorption.
   - **TCAs (less first-line; useful in pain, migraine prophylaxis, refractory MDD):** amitriptyline, nortriptyline 25–150 mg; sedating, anticholinergic, cardiac (QTc), overdose risk.
   - **MAOIs (refractory MDD, atypical depression):** phenelzine 15 mg TID → 60–90 mg/d; tranylcypromine 10 mg BID → 30–60 mg/d; selegiline TD (Emsam) 6–12 mg/24h. Tyramine-restricted diet at non-TD doses. Requires 14-day washout from most other serotonergic agents (5 weeks from fluoxetine due to its long half-life).
   - **Esketamine intranasal** (Spravato 56–84 mg twice weekly initial → weekly → every 2 weeks): treatment-resistant MDD (≥2 failed trials); REMS program; monitor BP and dissociation post-dose.

3. **Match drug to patient profile.**
   - **MDD + insomnia, weight loss, anorexia:** mirtazapine (sedating, weight gain, appetite).
   - **MDD + sexual dysfunction concerns:** bupropion or vortioxetine; consider mirtazapine.
   - **MDD + neuropathic pain / fibromyalgia / chronic low back:** duloxetine, venlafaxine.
   - **MDD + obesity, type 2 diabetes:** bupropion (weight loss); avoid mirtazapine.
   - **MDD + smoking cessation:** bupropion (also FDA-approved as Zyban for smoking cessation).
   - **GAD:** SSRIs / SNRIs equally effective; buspirone augmentation; consider hydroxyzine; avoid long-term benzodiazepines.
   - **Panic:** SSRI (start low — sertraline 12.5–25 mg — initial activation can worsen panic; titrate slowly).
   - **OCD:** SSRI at higher doses than for MDD (e.g., sertraline 200 mg, fluoxetine 80 mg); clomipramine effective but more side effects.
   - **PTSD:** sertraline, paroxetine (FDA-approved); venlafaxine reasonable; prazosin for nightmares.
   - **Geriatric depression:** start low, go slow; sertraline 12.5–25 mg, escitalopram 5 mg; avoid paroxetine (anticholinergic); citalopram cap 20 mg if >60; watch hyponatremia (SIADH risk with SSRIs).
   - **Pregnancy:** sertraline is most-studied SSRI with favorable risk-benefit; fluoxetine, citalopram, escitalopram acceptable; avoid paroxetine (cardiac defect signal); breastfeeding compatible with most SSRIs (sertraline lowest infant serum levels).
   - **QTc concerns / cardiac:** citalopram >40 mg avoid; escitalopram safer; bupropion neutral.
   - **Seizure disorder:** avoid bupropion at high doses, especially IR formulation; avoid clomipramine.
   - **Bipolar depression:** see step 1; if antidepressant is used (typically with mood stabilizer), bupropion has lowest manic-switch rate among AD options; SSRIs / SNRIs higher.

4. **Set realistic expectations and dosing trajectory.**
   - Most agents need 2–4 weeks to begin showing benefit; full response at 6–12 weeks.
   - Start at low dose for 1 week, then up-titrate to therapeutic range to minimize initial activation, GI side effects, sexual dysfunction.
   - Adequate trial = therapeutic dose × 4–6 weeks at minimum before declaring "failed."
   - Educate on time course, initial worsening that can occur (especially anxiety, sleep), suicidality monitoring in adolescents and young adults (FDA boxed warning).

5. **Plan a switch (when initial agent is inadequate response or intolerance).**
   - **Direct switch (same class, similar pharmacology):** between SSRIs (e.g., sertraline → escitalopram), often direct stop and start next day. Fluoxetine is the exception — its long half-life (and active metabolite norfluoxetine) requires consideration.
   - **Taper-and-switch (across classes when receptor profile differs significantly):** taper old agent by 25–50% per week over 2–4 weeks, then start new agent. Useful when going from SNRI to SSRI or TCA to bupropion.
   - **Cross-taper (overlap):** simultaneously taper old while titrating up new; commonly over 2 weeks. Practical when patient cannot tolerate a gap.
   - **Switching to or from MAOI requires a washout period:**
     - SSRI/SNRI/TCA → MAOI: 14 days off (5 weeks for fluoxetine).
     - MAOI → SSRI/SNRI/TCA: 14 days off MAOI before starting new agent.
     - Other MAOIs (linezolid, methylene blue) — same precaution.
   - **Watch serotonin syndrome:** if any overlap of strongly serotonergic agents during a switch, monitor for tremor, myoclonus, hyperreflexia, autonomic instability (tachycardia, hyperthermia, diaphoresis, mydriasis), and mental status change. Triad of mental status + autonomic + neuromuscular hyperactivity (especially clonus).

6. **Manage discontinuation syndrome.**
   - Most pronounced with short-half-life agents (paroxetine, venlafaxine), least with fluoxetine (self-tapering).
   - Symptoms (FINISH): Flu-like, Insomnia, Nausea, Imbalance, Sensory disturbance (electric-shock sensations), Hyperarousal — onset within days, lasting 1–2 weeks.
   - Taper over 4 weeks minimum for paroxetine/venlafaxine; longer (8–12 weeks) if symptoms emerge; some patients require very slow tapers with compounded smaller doses (hyperbolic taper).
   - Differentiate discontinuation from relapse: discontinuation appears within days; relapse typically over weeks.

7. **Consider augmentation when partial response.**
   - **Lithium 600–900 mg PO daily:** strong evidence for MDD augmentation; check level 0.6–0.8 mEq/L.
   - **Atypical antipsychotic augmentation:** aripiprazole 2–10 mg, brexpiprazole 1–3 mg, quetiapine XR 150–300 mg, olanzapine-fluoxetine combination (OFC), cariprazine.
   - **Triiodothyronine (T3) 25–50 µg/d.**
   - **Bupropion add-on to SSRI** — common in MDD with residual fatigue/anhedonia or sexual dysfunction.
   - **Buspirone augmentation 15–30 mg/day** — mild effect.
   - **Esketamine intranasal** for treatment-resistant MDD.
   - **ECT, TMS** for non-responders or specific clinical contexts (severe, psychotic, suicidal, treatment-resistant).

8. **Follow-up cadence.**
   - 1–2 weeks after initiation or change (phone or visit): tolerability, adherence, initial response, side effects, suicidality.
   - 4 weeks: response check.
   - 6–8 weeks: adequate trial assessment; if inadequate, optimize dose or switch.
   - Once stable: every 3 months; longer for maintenance phase.
   - For first episode: continue 6–9 months after remission; for recurrent: indefinite if ≥3 episodes or persistent residual symptoms.

```
- Drug: [name]

[Tie to symptom profile, comorbidity, side-effect tolerability, prior response, evidence]

SWITCH PLAN (if applicable):
- Old drug: [name] [dose] — taper by [X%] over [N weeks] to off
- New drug: [name] — start at [dose] on day [X] (overlap, direct, or washout strategy)
- Washout period: [days] (if MAOI involved)
- Serotonin syndrome surveillance: [signs to monitor]

DISCONTINUATION SYNDROME ANTICIPATION:
- Likelihood [high / moderate / low based on half-life]
- Counseled: [specific symptoms]

EXPECTED TIMELINE:
- Week 1–2: tolerability; symptoms may worsen briefly
- Week 2–4: early response signs
- Week 6–8: adequate trial reached; decision point
- 6–9 months: continuation after remission for first episode

MONITORING:
- Suicidality (esp. adolescents, young adults) — boxed warning
- Labs (if indicated): Na (SSRI hyponatremia in elderly); LFT (duloxetine); HbA1c / weight (mirtazapine); QTc (citalopram, escitalopram high-dose); lithium level if augmenting
- Drug interactions: [identified]
- Sexual function, sleep, appetite, energy, anxiety, hopelessness — track at each visit

AUGMENTATION PLAN IF PARTIAL RESPONSE AT 6–8 WEEKS:
- Option 1: dose optimization to max tolerated
- Option 2: augmentation with [agent + dose]
- Option 3: switch to different class

WHEN TO REFER / ESCALATE:
- Inadequate response after 2 adequate trials → psychiatry referral; consider TRD strategies (esketamine, ECT, TMS, MAOI under specialist care)
- Emergent suicidality, psychosis, mania: same-day psychiatric assessment

FOLLOW-UP:
- 1–2 weeks initial check (phone or visit)
- 4 weeks office visit
- 6–8 weeks decision point
- Reassess every 3 months once stable
```

## Worked Example

**Input:** 38-year-old woman with moderate MDD ×4 months. On sertraline 100 mg daily for 8 weeks with partial response (PHQ-9 down from 18 to 11). Side effects: sexual dysfunction (decreased libido and anorgasmia) and 5-lb weight gain. Plans pregnancy in next 12 months. Wants to switch agents.

**Output:**

```
PATIENT SNAPSHOT:
- 38 y woman, moderate MDD, 8-week adequate sertraline trial at 100 mg → partial response (PHQ-9 18 → 11) with significant sexual dysfunction and weight gain.
- Planning pregnancy within 12 months → SSRI safety in pregnancy matters.

DIFFERENTIAL CONSIDERATIONS:
- Bipolar screen negative (no prior mania / hypomania / mixed states; family history negative).
- No active suicidality at current visit; safety plan in place.
- No substance / medical contributor.
- Anxiety symptoms minimal.

OPTIONS WEIGHED:
1. **Optimize sertraline first** — try 150 mg, then 200 mg before switching. Counterargument: sexual dysfunction and weight gain already established; unlikely to remit at higher dose. PHQ-9 response is partial after 8 weeks — adequate trial criteria. Patient has expressed preference to switch.
2. **Switch to bupropion XL** — addresses both side-effect concerns (no sexual dysfunction, weight neutral / loss); reasonable pregnancy safety (Category C; large registry data without major teratogenic signal); monotherapy in MDD has decent evidence. Caveat: less effective for anxiety; not indicated if she also has GAD features.
3. **Switch to vortioxetine** — lower sexual dysfunction rate than SSRIs (still some); weight neutral; cognitive benefits reported; pregnancy data more limited; cost can be high.
4. **Augment with bupropion** rather than switch (sertraline + bupropion 150 mg XL) — addresses residual symptoms and may counteract sexual side effects of sertraline. Reasonable next step before full switch; patient could try this for 4–6 weeks.
5. **Add aripiprazole 2–5 mg** — strong augmentation evidence but weight, metabolic, EPS risks.

CHOSEN PLAN:
- **Switch to bupropion XL.** Rationale: side-effect mismatch with sertraline is patient-priority concern; bupropion addresses both sexual dysfunction and weight; reasonable pregnancy safety profile; patient has not had inadequate response to prior bupropion. Will discuss option 4 (augmentation) as alternative if she prefers to keep sertraline; for this plan, proceed with switch.

SWITCH PLAN:
- **Sertraline 100 mg daily → 50 mg daily × 7 days → 25 mg daily × 7 days → stop.** (Sertraline t½ ~26 h; discontinuation syndrome less marked than paroxetine/venlafaxine; modest taper still recommended.)
- **Day 1 of bupropion**: start **bupropion XL 150 mg PO daily AM** (avoid PM dosing due to insomnia risk) overlapping with last week of sertraline taper at 25 mg.
- Day 14: stop sertraline. Continue bupropion XL 150 mg.
- Day 14–28: assess tolerability; if adequate, up-titrate to **bupropion XL 300 mg PO daily AM**.
- No specific washout (no MAOI, no high-risk serotonergic agent involved). Monitor for serotonin syndrome briefly during overlap (low risk with sertraline + bupropion).

SEROTONIN SYNDROME SURVEILLANCE:
- Low risk in this overlap (bupropion is not strongly serotonergic).
- Monitor anyway: tremor, hyperreflexia, clonus, autonomic instability, agitation, mental status — counsel to call/go to ED.

DISCONTINUATION SYNDROME:
- Sertraline t½ ~26 h with active metabolite — modest risk. Taper as above. If FINISH symptoms emerge, slow further.
- Bupropion does not cause meaningful discontinuation syndrome.

PREGNANCY COUNSELING:
- Bupropion is reasonable in pregnancy (FDA category C; reassuring registry data; not a strong teratogen); benefits of treating MDD in pregnancy typically outweigh risks of untreated depression (relapse rates high in untreated, fetal effects of untreated maternal MDD).
- Sertraline is the most-studied SSRI in pregnancy with favorable profile — would also be reasonable if patient elects to stay on SSRI or if bupropion doesn't work.
- Avoid paroxetine (cardiac defect signal).
- Folic acid 400–800 µg daily pre-conception and through pregnancy.
- Engage perinatal psychiatry early in pregnancy planning.

EXPECTED TIMELINE:
- Week 1–2: bupropion overlap; sertraline taper; monitor for anxiety/insomnia from bupropion activation.
- Week 4: full bupropion dose; tolerability + initial response.
- Week 6–8: adequate trial; PHQ-9 reassessment; aim for remission (PHQ-9 ≤4).
- If inadequate at week 8: consider adding sertraline back (bupropion + SSRI combination) or switching to vortioxetine or SNRI.

MONITORING:
- Bupropion-specific: blood pressure (modest BP rise possible), seizure threshold (no history; no bulimia/anorexia, no abrupt benzo/alcohol cessation — low risk; counsel on missing doses and not taking >300 mg/day XL).
- Sexual function reassessment.
- Weight, sleep, mood, anxiety, suicidality.
- 1-week phone check, 4-week visit, 8-week decision point.

WHEN TO REFER / ESCALATE:
- If PHQ-9 ≥10 at 8 weeks → psychiatry referral; consider third trial or augmentation; vortioxetine or SNRI alternative.
- Active suicidality, severe symptoms — same-day specialist.
- Pregnancy occurrence — early prenatal + perinatal psychiatry visit.

FOLLOW-UP:
- 1-week phone check (tolerability, mood, anxiety, side effects).
- 4-week office visit (full assessment, dose increase to 300 mg if appropriate).
- 8-week visit (adequate trial reassessment, PHQ-9).
- Every 3 months once stable; pregnancy-planning visit early.
```
