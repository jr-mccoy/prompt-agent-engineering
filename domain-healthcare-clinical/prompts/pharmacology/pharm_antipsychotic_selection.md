## Objective

Select an antipsychotic for a specific clinical context: primary indication (schizophrenia, schizoaffective, bipolar I mania/depression, MDD augmentation, dementia-related agitation, delirium, acute agitation, OCD augmentation, Tourette's, tic disorder), patient profile (metabolic, cardiac, neurologic, age, pregnancy, prior response, prior EPS/TD), and adherence pattern (oral vs long-acting injectable). Output prescription with dose, titration, monitoring, and switching plan.

## Inputs

- Primary indication and acuity (chronic schizophrenia vs first-episode psychosis vs acute mania vs agitation in ED)
- Symptom profile (positive symptoms, negative symptoms, mood, cognition, suicidality, aggression)
- Prior antipsychotic trials (drug, dose, duration, response, side effects, reason for stopping)
- Medical comorbidities: metabolic (diabetes, obesity, dyslipidemia), cardiac (QTc, MI history), Parkinson's / Lewy body / extrapyramidal sensitivity, hyperprolactinemia, hepatic, renal, seizure, dementia (boxed warning), pregnancy / lactation, smoking status (CYP1A2 substrates)
- Concurrent medications (QT-prolonging, P450 inducers/inhibitors)
- Adherence likelihood (oral compliance, family support, prior LAI experience)
- Substance use comorbidity
- Patient preference, cost, insurance

## Role

Senior psychiatrist writing the antipsychotic prescription with explicit rationale and monitoring plan.

## Reasoning Steps

1. **Confirm diagnosis and target symptoms.**
   - **Schizophrenia / schizoaffective:** chronic treatment; both positive and negative symptoms; cognitive symptoms.
   - **Bipolar I mania:** monotherapy or with mood stabilizer.
   - **Bipolar depression:** quetiapine, lurasidone, cariprazine, olanzapine-fluoxetine combination, lumateperone — FDA-approved for bipolar depression.
   - **MDD augmentation:** aripiprazole, brexpiprazole, quetiapine XR, olanzapine-fluoxetine, cariprazine.
   - **Dementia-related agitation:** brexpiprazole (FDA-approved for Alzheimer's agitation 2023); all antipsychotics carry boxed warning for increased mortality in dementia — use only when non-pharmacologic measures fail and behaviors threaten safety; lowest effective dose, time-limited.
   - **Delirium:** haloperidol, quetiapine, olanzapine; address underlying cause; minimize duration.
   - **Acute agitation:** IM haloperidol + lorazepam ± diphenhydramine; or IM olanzapine, ziprasidone, aripiprazole.
   - **Tourette's / tics:** haloperidol, pimozide (FDA-approved); aripiprazole, risperidone often used.

2. **Match by receptor profile and side-effect priorities.**
   - **First-generation (typical) antipsychotics:** primarily D2 antagonists.
     - **Haloperidol** (high-potency): high EPS / TD risk; minimal weight gain; QTc prolongation; useful for acute psychosis (IM) and delirium.
     - **Chlorpromazine, thioridazine** (low-potency): less EPS, more sedation, anticholinergic, orthostasis, QTc (thioridazine worst).
     - **Fluphenazine, perphenazine:** intermediate.
   - **Second-generation (atypical) antipsychotics:** D2 + 5-HT2A antagonism; varying metabolic and EPS profiles.
     - **High metabolic risk:** clozapine, olanzapine.
     - **Moderate:** quetiapine, risperidone, paliperidone.
     - **Lower metabolic risk:** aripiprazole, ziprasidone, lurasidone, brexpiprazole, cariprazine, lumateperone.
     - **EPS risk:** risperidone (dose-related, esp. >6 mg), paliperidone, lurasidone (less); aripiprazole / cariprazine partial agonists, may cause akathisia.
     - **Prolactin elevation:** risperidone, paliperidone (most); haloperidol; less with aripiprazole (can lower prolactin); negligible with quetiapine, clozapine.
     - **QTc:** ziprasidone, IV haloperidol, thioridazine highest; lurasidone, aripiprazole least.
     - **Sedation:** quetiapine, olanzapine, clozapine; less with aripiprazole, lurasidone, brexpiprazole.

3. **Profile-based selection.**
   - **Obesity / DM / metabolic:** avoid olanzapine and clozapine first-line; choose aripiprazole, ziprasidone, lurasidone, brexpiprazole, cariprazine.
   - **QTc prolongation / cardiac concerns:** avoid ziprasidone, IV haloperidol, thioridazine; lurasidone, aripiprazole favored.
   - **Parkinson's disease psychosis:** **pimavanserin** (5-HT2A inverse agonist, no D2 effect) FDA-approved; clozapine and quetiapine acceptable; avoid D2 antagonists that worsen motor symptoms.
   - **Lewy body dementia:** very sensitive to neuroleptics; pimavanserin or low-dose quetiapine; avoid haloperidol.
   - **Pregnancy:** lowest-risk choices — olanzapine, quetiapine, haloperidol; avoid paliperidone if possible. Discuss risks/benefits; relapse risk often outweighs.
   - **Smoking (CYP1A2 inducer):** olanzapine and clozapine levels lower in smokers; dose adjusts on cessation.
   - **Akathisia or prior TD:** avoid high-potency D2 blockers; prefer clozapine, low-dose quetiapine, pimavanserin if applicable.
   - **Treatment-resistant schizophrenia (failure of ≥2 adequate trials):** **clozapine** — only FDA-approved for TRS; requires REMS for ANC monitoring (weekly ×6 months, biweekly ×6 months, monthly thereafter).

4. **First-episode psychosis nuances.**
   - Lower doses than chronic; greater response and side-effect sensitivity.
   - Avoid olanzapine and other high-metabolic-risk options first.
   - Aripiprazole, risperidone (low-dose), paliperidone, lurasidone reasonable.
   - Consider LAI early to improve adherence.

5. **Long-acting injectable (LAI) considerations.**
   - When adherence is concern, history of relapse on oral, patient preference, court-ordered treatment.
   - Available LAIs:
     - Risperidone (Risperdal Consta — q2 weeks; Perseris — monthly SC).
     - Paliperidone (Invega Sustenna — monthly; Invega Trinza — q3 months; Invega Hafyera — q6 months).
     - Aripiprazole (Abilify Maintena — monthly; Aristada — monthly/q6 weeks/q2 months).
     - Olanzapine (Zyprexa Relprevv — q2-4 weeks; post-injection delirium/sedation syndrome — 3h observation; rarely used now).
     - Haloperidol decanoate (q4 weeks).
     - Fluphenazine decanoate (q2-4 weeks).
   - Initial oral test dose for tolerability before LAI (esp. for newer agents).
   - Most LAIs require oral overlap during first cycle for therapeutic levels.

6. **Dose ranges (oral; chronic schizophrenia maintenance unless otherwise noted).**
   - Aripiprazole: 10–30 mg/day.
   - Brexpiprazole: 2–4 mg/day.
   - Cariprazine: 1.5–6 mg/day.
   - Olanzapine: 10–20 mg/day.
   - Quetiapine IR: 200–800 mg/day; XR similar.
   - Risperidone: 2–6 mg/day (max 8).
   - Paliperidone: 6–12 mg/day.
   - Ziprasidone: 80–160 mg/day with food (absorption requires fat).
   - Lurasidone: 40–160 mg/day with food (≥350 kcal).
   - Lumateperone: 42 mg/day.
   - Clozapine: titrate slowly 12.5 mg → 25 mg → 50 mg/day per week to 300–450 mg/day; max 900 mg.
   - Haloperidol: 5–20 mg/day for schizophrenia; 0.25–2 mg for delirium / agitation in elderly.

7. **Monitoring (APA / ADA consensus for SGAs).**
   - **Baseline:** weight/BMI, waist circumference, fasting glucose, lipids, BP, EKG (QTc for ziprasidone, haloperidol IV), prolactin if symptomatic, ANC for clozapine, AIMS (abnormal involuntary movement scale) for TD.
   - **Follow-up:** weight monthly ×3, then quarterly; glucose / lipids at 12 weeks, then yearly (more often if metabolic syndrome or weight gain); AIMS every 6 months.
   - **Clozapine REMS:** weekly ANC ×6 months, biweekly ×6 months, monthly thereafter; cardiac monitoring (myocarditis), seizure risk, constipation/ileus, sialorrhea.

8. **Switching strategies.**
   - **Direct switch** (same class, similar receptor profile): start new agent, stop old agent.
   - **Cross-taper**: gradually reduce old while titrating new over 1–4 weeks; preferred for receptor-profile differences (e.g., olanzapine → aripiprazole, because anticholinergic withdrawal can cause cholinergic rebound).
   - **Plateau then cross-taper**: titrate new agent to therapeutic dose, then taper old agent — minimizes relapse risk.

## Output Format

```
PATIENT SNAPSHOT:
- Diagnosis, comorbidities, prior trials, current symptoms, key context (metabolic risk, cardiac, pregnancy, smoking, adherence)

SELECTED REGIMEN:
- Drug: [name]
- Form: oral / LAI
- Starting dose: [mg] [route] [frequency]
- Target dose: [mg/day]
- Titration: [step-by-step over N weeks]

RATIONALE:
- Indication match
- Side-effect profile fit (metabolic, cardiac, prolactin, EPS, sedation)
- Receptor profile rationale
- Adherence considerations (LAI vs oral)
- Prior trial guidance

CONTRAINDICATIONS / WARNINGS:
- Dementia-related agitation boxed warning if applicable
- QTc / metabolic / TD considerations
- Pregnancy / lactation if applicable

MONITORING:
- Baseline: weight, BMI, waist, fasting glucose, lipid panel, BP, EKG (if QTc-prolonging), prolactin if symptomatic, AIMS, CBC for clozapine
- Follow-up: weight monthly ×3, then quarterly; metabolic labs 12 weeks, then yearly; AIMS every 6 months
- Clozapine: REMS-mandated ANC schedule

LAI CONSIDERATIONS (if applicable):
- Oral test dose [days] before LAI
- LAI dose conversion: [...]
- Oral overlap [during first cycle for newer LAIs]

SWITCHING PLAN (if applicable):
- Old agent: taper over [N weeks]
- New agent: titrate as above
- Cross-taper schedule

PATIENT COUNSELING:
- Side effects to monitor and report
- Pregnancy / contraception counseling
- Adherence importance
- Driving / sedation precaution at initiation

WHEN TO ESCALATE:
- Two adequate trials failed → clozapine consideration
- Severe EPS / akathisia: anticholinergic (benztropine), propranolol for akathisia, dose reduction, switch
- TD: switch to clozapine or pimavanserin or VMAT2 inhibitor (valbenazine, deutetrabenazine)
- NMS: discontinue, supportive care, dantrolene, bromocriptine

FOLLOW-UP:
- 1–2 weeks initial check
- 4–6 weeks
- Quarterly thereafter
```

## Worked Example

**Input:** 28-year-old man, first-episode psychosis, BMI 23, no metabolic / cardiac issues, smokes 1 pack/day, no prior medication trials, family supportive, lives at home. Initiate antipsychotic.

**Output:**

```
PATIENT SNAPSHOT:
- 28 y M, first-episode psychosis, BMI 23, smoker, no prior AP, family-supported.

SELECTED REGIMEN:
- **Aripiprazole 5 mg PO daily AM.** Titrate to 10 mg after 1 week, then 15 mg if tolerated. Target 10–15 mg/day for first-episode.
- Alternative if AP-LAI strongly preferred from start: paliperidone palmitate (Invega Sustenna) after brief oral risperidone or paliperidone titration to confirm tolerability.

RATIONALE:
- **First-episode psychosis** — lower doses, greater side-effect sensitivity, favor lower-metabolic-risk SGA.
- **Aripiprazole** partial D2 agonist + 5-HT1A partial agonist + 5-HT2A antagonist: low metabolic risk, no significant QTc, no prolactin elevation (lowers prolactin), good evidence for first-episode (CAFE trial subset), reasonable acceptability.
- **Smoker:** CYP1A2 induced — aripiprazole metabolism not strongly CYP1A2 (mostly CYP3A4 + 2D6), unaffected.
- **Family support, lives at home:** adherence likely feasible with oral; consider LAI conversion if relapse occurs.
- **Avoided:** olanzapine and clozapine (metabolic), ziprasidone (food requirement, QTc concern), risperidone (prolactin and EPS).

CONTRAINDICATIONS / WARNINGS:
- No dementia.
- No QTc concern.
- Akathisia possible — counsel and monitor.
- Activating effects possible — morning dosing.

MONITORING:
- Baseline: weight 73 kg, BMI 23, waist circumference, fasting glucose 92, A1c 5.4, lipids (LDL 110, HDL 48, TG 95), BP 122/78, EKG with QTc 412, AIMS done.
- Follow-up: weight at 4, 8, 12 weeks then quarterly; metabolic labs at 12 weeks, then yearly.
- AIMS every 6 months.
- Akathisia screen each visit.

LAI CONSIDERATIONS:
- Not at initiation; reassess at 3–6 months. If patient prefers or relapses despite adherence, options:
  - Abilify Maintena 400 mg IM monthly (oral aripiprazole 10–14 days during first cycle).
  - Aristada 441 mg IM monthly or 882 mg q6 weeks (with 21-day oral overlap or Aristada Initio + single dose oral on day 1).

PATIENT COUNSELING:
- Take in morning; can cause activation, insomnia early — usually settles.
- Report restlessness, jitteriness (akathisia) — treatable with propranolol or dose adjustment.
- Smoking cessation discussion; refer to cessation resources (nicotine replacement, varenicline counseling).
- Avoid alcohol initially.
- Driving caution first few weeks.
- Follow-up in 1 week (phone) and 2 weeks (visit).

WHEN TO ESCALATE:
- Inadequate response at 4–6 weeks at 15 mg → up-titrate to 20–30 mg, or switch to second agent (e.g., risperidone, lurasidone, paliperidone).
- Two adequate trials failed → clozapine consultation.
- Severe akathisia: propranolol 20 mg BID, mirtazapine 15 mg qHS, or benztropine 1–2 mg.
- NMS: emergency stop, hospitalization.

FOLLOW-UP:
- 1 week phone, 2 weeks visit, 4 weeks visit, 12 weeks, then quarterly.
- Psychotherapy referral (CBT for psychosis).
- Family psychoeducation.
- Substance use screen and intervention.
- Coordinated specialty care (early intervention program) referral if available.
```
