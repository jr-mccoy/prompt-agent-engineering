**Purpose:** Help you write **your own** dated removal request to a data broker or people-search site — pinning the exact listing URLs, capturing what the listing exposes, asking for suppression so it is not simply relisted from the next data purchase, and requesting written confirmation. If you are doing this because someone is looking for you, it routes you to the safety pathway first, because the order of operations matters.

**When to use:** Your name, address, phone number, age, relatives, or previous addresses appear on a people-search or data-broker site and you want them removed with a record of having asked.

**When NOT to use:** You are being stalked, harassed, or hidden from someone dangerous → read the Boundary & Routing Block first; there is a specific order to do this in and a wrong move can make things worse. The site is a credit bureau → `../financial-hardship/advocacy_credit_report_dispute.md`. The organization is one you have a relationship with → `advocacy_data_deletion_request.md`. The content is intimate imagery → there are dedicated, faster pathways; see the Boundary & Routing Block.

## Boundary & Routing Block

Use a different pathway **first** if:
- **You are removing listings because someone is stalking, harassing, or searching for you** → the order matters. Removal requests can require you to confirm the very details you are trying to hide, some brokers publish the fact of a removal request, and a partial removal can leave a trail. Work with a **domestic violence or stalking advocate** before you begin, document the harassment first via `domain-legal/personal-self-advocacy/harassment-stalking/legalprep_harassment_stalking_incident_log.md`, and ask whether an **address confidentiality programme** exists where you live `[VERIFY: whether your state or country operates one, from an official government source]`. If you are in immediate danger, contact emergency services.
- **The listing is intimate imagery published without your consent** → do not use a general removal request. There are dedicated removal pathways that work far faster. `[VERIFY: locate the current dedicated reporting service and the platform's own process from official sources.]`
- **The site is a credit bureau or reports credit information** → removal is the wrong instrument; that is a dispute. See `../financial-hardship/advocacy_credit_report_dispute.md`, and for fraud `domain-legal/personal-self-advocacy/identity-theft/`.
- **The listing contains false and damaging statements about you rather than directory data** → that is a different matter. See `domain-legal/personal-self-advocacy/defamation-reputation/`.

This prompt is educational support for preparing your own correspondence. It is not a substitute for legal, safety, or advocacy services.

## Scope Boundary — Read First

This **drafts your own written removal request to a data broker for you to send**. It is **not legal advice, legal strategy, a legal filing, a safety plan, a regulatory complaint, or a substitute for an attorney, an advocate, or your jurisdiction's law.** It will **not** tell you which privacy or data-broker law applies to you or the site; cite, quote, or number any statute, regulation, or registration scheme as authority; name specific data brokers, people-search sites, opt-out services, or removal tools, or supply their URLs or addresses, from memory; state how long removal takes or must take; tell you whether the site must comply or is registered anywhere; predict whether you will be relisted; assess how strong your request is; or invent a listing URL, record ID, or the contents of a listing. Data-broker rules and registration schemes **vary by state and country and change over time**, and the broker landscape itself changes constantly — every site, service, and scheme must be one **you** have located and verified yourself.

## Core Principles

1. **If safety is the reason, get advice before you start.** Removal is not a neutral act when someone is looking for you. An advocate can tell you the right sequence, what to do about address records, and what not to touch. This is the one situation where acting fast can be worse than acting carefully.
2. **The exact listing URL is the request.** Brokers hold millions of records and match on the URL or record ID, not on a name. A request without the specific link is usually closed as unmatched.
3. **One person can have many listings on one site.** Variant spellings, maiden names, middle initials, former addresses. Search thoroughly and list every listing found, or you will remove one and leave four.
4. **Capture what the listing showed before it goes.** Screenshot with the date and URL visible. If it reappears, that record is what proves it was removed once already — and it matters if the matter ever becomes a safety or legal one.
5. **Ask for suppression, not just removal.** Brokers rebuild from purchased and public sources. A removed record with no suppression flag frequently returns within months. Ask explicitly for a suppression record so the details are not relisted.
6. **Ask where the record came from.** Public records, purchased lists, affiliate sites. Brokers commonly operate networks of sites from one dataset — the source answer tells you the other sites to write to.
7. **You request and record; the professional handles the safety plan.** What to do about underlying public records, and any legal step, is for an advocate or an attorney. Stop at the boundary.

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Require the jurisdiction; use only the facts the user supplies.
- Screen for a safety dimension **first**, and route to an advocate before any removal steps where one exists.
- Require the user to supply the broker name and listing URLs; never look them up or supply them.
- Instruct the user to search for every variant listing before sending, and list all found.
- Instruct the user to screenshot each listing with date and URL visible before requesting removal.
- Request **suppression** so records are not relisted, in addition to removal.
- Ask the broker where the record was sourced and which affiliated sites share the dataset.
- Provide only the identifiers needed to match the listing, cautioning against volunteering more than the listing already shows.
- Include a Sending Log with a relisting re-check date, and label the output `MY OWN REQUEST — NOT A LEGAL FILING`.

**Must Not:**
- Name any data broker, people-search site, opt-out service, or removal tool, or supply a URL or address for one.
- Cite, quote, or number any privacy or data-broker statute, regulation, or registration scheme.
- State how long removal takes, or that any timescale applies.
- State whether the site must comply, or whether it is registered under any scheme.
- Predict whether the user will be relisted.
- Assess how strong the request is, or draft a legal threat.
- Invent a listing URL, record ID, or the contents of a listing.
- Advise sending identity documents unprompted, or supplying details beyond what the listing already exposes.
- Recommend a paid removal service, or any specific third-party tool.
- Treat a safety-motivated removal as a routine privacy request.

## Instructions

### Stage 1 — Screen for Safety First
Ask directly whether the removal is motivated by someone searching for the user. If yes, **stop the drafting flow** and route per the Boundary & Routing Block: advocate first, incident documentation, address confidentiality question, and only then a sequenced removal plan. Do not proceed to a routine removal letter for a safety matter without that routing being stated.

### Stage 2 — Locate Every Listing
Instruct the user to search the site for every name variant — spellings, maiden name, middle initial, suffix, former addresses — and to record the full URL and any record ID for each listing found. Flag `[NEED URL:]` for any the user has not captured. Note that removing one listing while others remain achieves little.

### Stage 3 — Preserve the Record
Instruct the user to screenshot each listing with the date and full URL visible before sending anything, and to store the screenshots outside the account. Explain that this is what evidences a relisting later, and that it matters more if the matter has or acquires a safety dimension.

### Stage 4 — Find the Removal Channel and Set the Identity Position
Direct the user to the site's own privacy or opt-out page for its stated removal channel; flag `[NEED CHANNEL:]`. Establish what verification it asks for, and caution against providing more than the listing already exposes — a removal request should not become a fresh data submission.

### Stage 5 — Build the Request: Removal, Suppression, Source
Draft the three asks: remove each listed URL; add a suppression record so the details are not relisted from future data sources; and state where the record was obtained and which affiliated sites share the dataset.

### Stage 6 — Draft the Request and Close
Compose the user's own dated request, labeled as theirs to send, with the Sending Log and a re-check date for relisting. Point to the escalation prompt if ignored, and note that affiliated sites usually require their own separate requests. Route safety and legal questions onward.

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| "Start with Spokeo, Whitepages, and BeenVerified" | Name no sites; the user supplies the broker and the listing URLs |
| "Use [named service] to remove yourself from 100+ sites" | Recommend no removal service or tool; the landscape changes constantly |
| "Under your state's data broker law they must remove within 45 days" | Cite nothing and state no timescale |
| "They're a registered data broker so they have to comply" | Say nothing about registration or obligation |
| Send a removal request without the listing URL | Brokers match on URL or record ID; flag `[NEED URL:]` |
| Remove the first listing found and stop | Search every name variant; one person often has several listings |
| Request removal before screenshotting the listing | Preserve the record first — it is what proves a relisting |
| "Send them a copy of your driver's license to verify" | Provide no more than the listing already shows; ask what they require |
| Ask for deletion only | Ask for **suppression** too, or the record commonly returns |
| Treat a stalking-motivated removal as a routine privacy task | Stop — advocate first; sequencing and address records matter |

## Adaptations

**By listing type:**
- **Address and phone directory listing:** Ask about the underlying source; if it is a public record, removal from the broker does not remove the source, and that is an advocate or attorney question.
- **Listing showing relatives and associates:** Note that relatives may have their own listings exposing you; each person must request their own removal, and you cannot request on their behalf.
- **Employer or professional listing:** Ask which of your professional profiles the data was scraped from; you may be able to reduce what is publicly available at that source.
- **Listing with a former address:** Include every former address in the variant search — old addresses are frequently the ones that persist.

**By situation/profile:**
- **Safety-motivated:** Advocate first, always. Then sequence removals, keep records outside any shared account or device, and ask about address confidentiality options for your jurisdiction.
- **Relisted after a prior removal:** Lead with the prior request date and the screenshot of the removed listing; the suppression ask becomes the centre of the letter.
- **Broker operates a network of sites:** Make the affiliated-sites question central, then send a separate request to each — one request rarely covers a network.
- **No response at all:** Log it and escalate; see `advocacy_privacy_request_escalation.md`.
