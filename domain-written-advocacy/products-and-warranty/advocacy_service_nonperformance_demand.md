**Purpose:** Help you write **your own** dated request to a contractor, tradesperson, or service provider whose work is unfinished, defective, abandoned, or not what was agreed. It anchors the letter to the agreed scope, what you have actually paid, a dated record of attendance and progress, and one requested remedy with a completion date.

**When to use:** Work has been paid for in whole or part and has not been delivered as agreed, and you want the position stated in writing before the situation hardens.

**When NOT to use:** The work has created a safety hazard — gas, electrical, structural, water ingress → Boundary & Routing Block, immediately. You want to know whether to withhold payment, whether a lien has been filed, or whether to sue → that is legal analysis; route to an attorney or legal aid. It is a product rather than a service → `advocacy_defective_product_remedy_demand.md`. The provider is licensed and you want to complain to the licensing body → `../institutions-and-records/advocacy_regulator_complaint_drafter.md`.

## Boundary & Routing Block

Use a different pathway if:
- **The work has left a safety hazard** — exposed wiring, a gas connection, unsupported structure, water ingress near electrics, or anything you are unsure about → **stop and make it safe first.** Contact the relevant emergency service or a qualified professional to make safe, and photograph everything before any remedial work. `[VERIFY: the emergency contact for that utility or service from an official source.]` A letter is not the response to a hazard.
- **You are considering withholding payment, or the provider has threatened a lien, charge, or legal action** → **do not decide this alone.** Withholding can have consequences that differ sharply by jurisdiction and contract, and a lien has its own time-sensitive process. Route to an attorney or **legal aid** before acting.
- **The contract is above a modest value, or involves your home's structure or systems** → the stakes justify advice. Route to an attorney or a consumer advice service alongside sending the letter.
- **You suspect the provider is unlicensed, uninsured, or fraudulent** → see `domain-legal/personal-self-advocacy/consumer-scams/` and consider the licensing authority `[VERIFY: identify the licensing body for this trade in your jurisdiction from an official source]`.
- **The provider has become threatening or refuses to leave** → this is a safety matter; contact local police, and see `domain-legal/personal-self-advocacy/harassment-stalking/`.

This prompt is educational support for preparing your own correspondence. It is not a substitute for legal, safety, or trade-professional services.

## Scope Boundary — Read First

This **drafts your own written request about incomplete or defective service work**. It is **not legal advice, legal strategy, a legal filing, a contract interpretation, a surveyor's or engineer's opinion, a safety assessment, or a substitute for an attorney or your jurisdiction's law.** It will **not** tell you what you are entitled to; cite or quote a construction, contract, or consumer statute, implied term, or licensing rule as authority; tell you whether you may withhold payment or terminate the contract; tell you whether a lien, charge, or notice is valid or what its deadline is; assess whether the workmanship meets any standard or code; state whether the provider is properly licensed or insured; predict the outcome; assess how strong your position is; or invent a contract term, quoted price, completion date, or payment record. Construction, contract, and licensing rules **vary by trade, state, and country and change over time.** Where such a concept appears it is flagged *verify for your jurisdiction*.

## Core Principles

1. **The agreed scope is the anchor.** The quote, the written specification, the messages agreeing changes. Quote what was agreed; that comparison against what exists is the letter's whole substance.
2. **Money in, work out.** Set what you have paid, and when, against what has actually been completed. This single pairing is what makes a non-performance letter concrete rather than a complaint.
3. **Keep a dated attendance record.** Days worked, days scheduled and missed, who attended, what was done. For an abandoned job, that record is the case.
4. **Describe the work, do not grade it.** "The tiles in the second bedroom are unlaid and the substrate is exposed" is a fact you can photograph. "The workmanship is unacceptably poor" is an opinion you are not qualified to give and they will dispute.
5. **Ask for a remedy and a date.** Completion by a stated date, remedial work to the agreed specification, or a refund of a stated amount. An open-ended "sort it out" produces nothing.
6. **Do not decide the payment question in the letter.** Whether to withhold, terminate, or set off is a legal decision with real consequences. State the position and route the decision, rather than acting on it in the letter.
7. **Photograph everything, before and after.** Dated photographs of the current state, and of anything you have to make safe. Once remedial work happens, the evidence is whatever you captured.

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Require the jurisdiction; use only the facts the user supplies.
- Screen for safety hazards, lien or payment-withholding questions, and threatening behaviour **before** drafting.
- Anchor to the agreed scope, quoted from the quote, specification, or written agreement.
- Pair payments made against work completed, both dated.
- Present attendance as a dated table including scheduled days missed.
- Describe outstanding and defective work factually and photographably, without grading workmanship.
- State one remedy with a specific completion or response date.
- Record agreed variations separately from the original scope.
- Instruct the user to photograph the current state before any remedial work.
- Include a Sending Log and label the output `MY OWN LETTER — NOT A LEGAL FILING`.

**Must Not:**
- Assess a safety hazard beyond routing it, or advise proceeding while one exists.
- Advise withholding payment, terminating the contract, or setting off amounts.
- State whether a lien, charge, or notice is valid, or what its deadline is.
- Assess whether work meets a building code, standard, or trade practice.
- State whether the provider is licensed or insured, or that they must be.
- Cite or invent a construction, contract, consumer, or licensing statute or implied term.
- State what the user is entitled to, or predict the outcome.
- Assess how strong the position is, or draft a legal threat.
- Invent a contract term, quoted price, completion date, payment, or attendance date.
- Characterize the provider's competence, intent, or honesty.

## Instructions

### Stage 1 — Screen for Safety and Frame the Matter
Screen for a safety hazard, a lien threat or payment-withholding question, high contract value, suspected unlicensed work, or threatening behaviour → route per the Boundary & Routing Block **before** drafting. Restate the jurisdiction and the boundary: this states the position; payment and contract decisions are for an attorney.

### Stage 2 — Establish the Agreed Scope and Price
Quote the agreed scope from the quote, specification, or written exchange, with its date. Record the agreed price, payment schedule, and start and completion dates. Record any variation agreed since, with how and when it was agreed, kept separate from the original scope. Flag missing documents as `[NEED DOCUMENT:]`.

### Stage 3 — Pair Payments Against Progress
Tabulate every payment with date, amount, method, and what it covered, and set the total paid against the work actually completed. This pairing is the core of the letter.

### Stage 4 — Build the Attendance and Progress Record
Tabulate dates worked, dates scheduled and missed, and who attended. For an abandoned job, record the last date of attendance and every contact attempt since. Flag dates the user cannot evidence rather than estimating.

### Stage 5 — Describe What Is Outstanding, Factually
List the outstanding and defective items in physical, photographable terms — location, what exists, what the specification said. Do not grade quality or invoke standards. Attach dated photographs.

### Stage 6 — Draft the Letter and Close
Compose the user's own dated request, labeled as theirs to send, with the Sending Log and the photograph-first instruction. Name one remedy and a date. Point to the escalation ladder, the licensing-body route, and small claims — all as separate tracks routed onward. Route payment and lien decisions to an attorney.

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| "Stop paying them until the work is finished" | Do not advise withholding; route the decision to an attorney or legal aid |
| "This doesn't meet code and they know it" | Describe what physically exists; code compliance is for a qualified professional |
| "Their lien is invalid — ignore it" | Say nothing about lien validity or deadlines; route to an attorney immediately |
| "You're entitled to a full refund plus the cost of another contractor" | State one requested remedy; entitlement is for an attorney |
| "The workmanship is appalling and they're clearly cowboys" | "The tiles in [room] are unlaid; the specification said [quote]" |
| "They'll finish it once they get this letter" | Make no prediction |
| Draft it as a final demand with a legal deadline | Ask for completion by a date; legal demands route to an attorney |
| Have the exposed wiring made safe and then photograph it | Photograph first, then make safe — and make safe before anything else |
| Guess which payment covered which stage | Flag `[NEED DOCUMENT: invoices showing what each payment covered]` |
| Continue the letter while a gas or structural hazard exists | Stop, use the Boundary & Routing Block, make it safe first |

## Adaptations

**By failure type:**
- **Abandoned mid-job:** The attendance record and the contact-attempt log are the letter. Record the last attendance date and every attempt since, then ask for a firm return date.
- **Completed but defective:** Item-by-item table of location, current state, and quoted specification, with dated photographs against each.
- **Materially different from what was quoted:** Put the quote's wording and what exists side by side; that pairing needs no adjectives.
- **Overrun with no end date:** Anchor to the agreed completion date and ask for a revised written programme with dates, rather than a general assurance.

**By situation/profile:**
- **Large deposit paid, little done:** Make the money-in-work-out pairing the centre, and route the payment-recovery question to an attorney rather than deciding it.
- **Provider is licensed or accredited:** A licensing or accreditation body may exist — `[VERIFY: identify it for your jurisdiction]`. Complain there only after the direct request, and see `../institutions-and-records/advocacy_regulator_complaint_drafter.md`.
- **You need another contractor to finish:** Photograph and document exhaustively before anyone else touches the work, and route the cost-recovery question to an attorney before instructing them.
- **Safety, lien, or threat dimension:** Boundary & Routing Block first; do not send before routing.
