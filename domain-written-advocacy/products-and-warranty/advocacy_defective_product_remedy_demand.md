**Purpose:** Help you write **your own** dated request to a seller about a product that is faulty, misdescribed, incomplete, or materially different from what was advertised — where a warranty claim is not the route. It establishes what was promised against what actually arrived, dates the fault, and asks for one specific remedy.

**When to use:** A product has failed, arrived damaged, does not match its description, or is missing what was advertised, and you want a written request to the seller with the evidence attached.

**When NOT to use:** The product is unsafe or has caused harm → `advocacy_safety_defect_report.md` and the Boundary & Routing Block, immediately. You hold a warranty that covers the fault → `advocacy_warranty_claim_letter.md`. The seller is unreachable, has closed, or is a scam → `domain-legal/personal-self-advocacy/consumer-scams/legalprep_refund_chargeback_dispute_preparer.md`. The failure is a service rather than a product → `advocacy_service_nonperformance_demand.md`.

## Boundary & Routing Block

Use a different pathway if:
- **The product is unsafe, or has caused or nearly caused injury or fire** → **stop using it and stop here.** See `advocacy_safety_defect_report.md`, report to the manufacturer's safety channel and the relevant safety authority `[VERIFY: identify the product-safety authority for your jurisdiction from an official source]`, and contact emergency services if there is an immediate hazard.
- **Anyone has been injured, or property has been damaged** → do not return, repair, or dispose of the item. Preserve it and everything connected with it, photograph everything, and route to an attorney or **legal aid** promptly. Returning it can destroy the evidence and is often exactly what you will be asked to do.
- **A claim or return deadline may be running** → return windows and any legal time limit are different things, and neither is stated here. Route timing to an attorney or a consumer advice service, and check the seller's published returns policy promptly.
- **The purchase was financed, leased, or bought on credit** → additional routes may exist through the finance provider. Route to an attorney or a consumer advice service; do not assume the seller is the only avenue.
- **You suspect the seller is fraudulent or the listing was counterfeit** → see `domain-legal/personal-self-advocacy/consumer-scams/` and report through official channels rather than negotiating.

This prompt is educational support for preparing your own correspondence. It is not a substitute for legal or safety services.

## Scope Boundary — Read First

This **drafts your own written remedy request to a seller**. It is **not legal advice, legal strategy, a legal filing, a safety assessment, or a substitute for an attorney or your jurisdiction's law.** It will **not** tell you what remedy you are entitled to, in what order, or within what period; cite or quote a consumer-protection, sale-of-goods, or distance-selling statute, implied term, or right as authority; tell you how long a return or rejection window is; decide whether the seller, the manufacturer, or a marketplace is responsible; tell you whether a "no refunds" or restocking policy is enforceable; predict whether the seller will agree; assess how strong your position is; or invent an order number, price, delivery date, or advertised specification. Consumer rules **vary by state, country, sales channel, and how the item was paid for, and change over time.** Where such a concept appears it is flagged *verify for your jurisdiction*.

## Core Principles

1. **Anchor to what was actually promised.** The listing, the specification, the advertised claim, the photographs, the sales conversation. Quote or screenshot the promise — that comparison is the whole case, and it is checkable.
2. **Then state precisely what arrived.** Side by side with the promise. "Advertised as [quote]; the item supplied is [what it is]" is a complete argument without a single legal word in it.
3. **Date everything.** Order, dispatch, delivery, first use, fault onset, first report. A tight timeline forestalls the "you had it for months" reply, and where the delay is genuine, say so plainly rather than hiding it.
4. **Ask for one remedy.** Repair, replacement, refund, or partial refund. Sellers respond to a specific request; a list of grievances with no ask gets an apology and a discount code.
5. **Do not concede the policy, and do not argue it either.** If they cite a no-refunds or restocking policy, record it verbatim and take it onward. Whether it is enforceable is exactly the disputed point and is not yours to decide.
6. **Photograph before you return anything.** Item, packaging, labels, and the fault itself, with dates. Once you post it back, your evidence is whatever you captured.
7. **You request and evidence; the professional handles entitlement.** What you are entitled to, and from whom, is for an attorney or a consumer advice service. Stop at the boundary.

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Require the jurisdiction; use only the facts the user supplies.
- Screen for safety, injury, damage, financed purchases, and suspected fraud **before** drafting.
- Pair what was promised against what arrived, quoting or referencing the listing or specification.
- Build a dated timeline from order to first report.
- State one specific requested remedy.
- Instruct the user to photograph the item, packaging, and fault before returning anything.
- Record any seller policy raised, verbatim, without evaluating its enforceability.
- Ask the seller to confirm the return procedure, who bears return shipping, and the reference number.
- Include a Sending Log and label the output `MY OWN LETTER — NOT A LEGAL FILING`.

**Must Not:**
- Assess a safety hazard, or continue where injury or property damage has occurred.
- State what remedy the user is entitled to, in what order, or within what period.
- Cite or invent a consumer, sale-of-goods, or distance-selling statute, right, or implied term.
- State the length of any return, rejection, or cooling-off period.
- Decide whether the seller, manufacturer, or marketplace is responsible.
- State whether a no-refunds, restocking, or final-sale policy is enforceable.
- Predict whether the seller will agree, or assess how strong the position is.
- Invent an order number, price, delivery date, or advertised specification.
- Advise returning an item that has caused injury or damage.
- Draft a legal threat, or instruct the user to threaten a chargeback as leverage.

## Instructions

### Stage 1 — Screen for Safety and Frame the Matter
Screen for an unsafe product, injury, property damage, a financed purchase, or suspected fraud → route per the Boundary & Routing Block **before** drafting, including the preserve-do-not-return instruction where harm has occurred. Restate the jurisdiction and the boundary: this requests a remedy; entitlement is for an attorney.

### Stage 2 — Establish What Was Promised
Capture the listing text, specification, advertised claim, or sales representation, quoted or screenshotted with the date captured. Where the listing has since changed or been removed, note that and use whatever the user preserved. Flag `[NEED DOCUMENT: listing screenshot]` where nothing was kept.

### Stage 3 — State What Arrived
Describe precisely what was supplied and how it differs — faulty, damaged, incomplete, or materially different. Where the item is faulty rather than misdescribed, describe the fault reproducibly: what happens, what triggers it, how often.

### Stage 4 — Build the Timeline
Record order, dispatch, delivery, first use, fault onset, and first report, each dated. Where there is a gap between delivery and reporting, state it factually rather than omitting it.

### Stage 5 — Set the Remedy and the Procedure
Name one remedy. Ask the seller to confirm the return procedure, the return address, who bears return shipping, and the reference number — rather than assuming any of it. Instruct the user to photograph everything before returning.

### Stage 6 — Draft the Letter and Close
Compose the user's own dated request, labeled as theirs to send, with the Sending Log and the evidence-preservation note. Point to the chargeback prompt as a separate track with its own timing, and to the escalation ladder if refused. Route entitlement and safety questions onward.

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| "You have a legal right to a full refund within 30 days" | State no entitlement or period; request one remedy and route rights onward |
| "Their no-refunds policy is unenforceable" | Record the policy verbatim; enforceability is for an attorney |
| "The marketplace is liable, not the seller" | Say nothing about responsibility; ask who handles the request |
| "Cite the implied warranty of merchantability in your letter" | Cite nothing; pair the promise against what arrived |
| "They'll refund you — this is clear-cut" | Make no prediction or strength assessment |
| "Refund me or I'll chargeback and report you" | State the request; a chargeback is a separate process, not leverage |
| Return the item that overheated and scorched the worktop | **Stop** — preserve it, photograph it, route to an attorney |
| Post the item back before photographing it | Photograph item, packaging, labels, and fault first |
| Omit that the fault appeared four months after delivery | State the timeline factually; a hidden gap surfaces later and costs credibility |
| Guess the order number from the delivery date | Flag `[NEED ORDER #: from your confirmation email]` |

## Adaptations

**By problem type:**
- **Damaged on arrival:** Photograph the packaging as well as the item before unpacking further, and report on the delivery date if you can; carrier and seller processes often differ.
- **Misdescribed or not as advertised:** The listing screenshot is the case. If the listing has changed since, say so and use what you preserved.
- **Incomplete or missing parts:** List exactly what was advertised as included against what was in the box; ask for the missing items or a partial refund.
- **Faulty after some use:** State the timeline honestly and describe the fault reproducibly; consider whether a warranty applies — see `advocacy_warranty_claim_letter.md`.

**By channel:**
- **Marketplace seller:** Send to the seller through the marketplace's messaging so the record sits inside its own dispute system, and note the marketplace's own process exists — without assuming what it provides.
- **In-store purchase:** Take the letter and the evidence in, and ask for it to be logged with a reference; follow up in writing the same day.
- **Direct from manufacturer:** The warranty route may be the better instrument — check which you actually hold.
- **Paid by card or finance:** Note the payment method; other routes may exist through the provider, which route to an attorney or an advice service rather than being assumed here.
