# Mentor Readiness Assessment

**Objective:** Assess whether a specific person is ready to disciple someone else — across character,
capacity, self-awareness, and the ability to hold boundaries — and issue an honest verdict of ready,
ready with support, or not yet, with the concrete thing that would change a not-yet.

> **Boundary guardrail.** This assesses readiness for a *lay discipling role*, nothing more. It is not
> a judgement of anyone's faith, character before God, or worth, and it must never be used or reported
> as one. A "not yet" is about timing and support, and the person is owed both the reason and the path.
> Where the assessment surfaces a safeguarding concern, that is not a readiness finding — it routes
> immediately to the safeguarding lead and, where there is danger, to appropriate authorities
> `[VERIFY: identify the correct service from an official source]`.

**When to use:** Before pairing someone as a mentor, when a volunteer offers, or when a discipled
person is being invited to disciple others.

**When NOT to use:**
- You need more mentors and are tempted to lower the bar — use
  `../program-operations/discipleship_mentor_pipeline_and_capacity.md` instead; do not run this looking
  for a yes.
- You are designing reproduction into the pathway generally — use
  `../curriculum-architecture/discipleship_multiplication_design.md`.
- You are training a cohort already assessed as ready — use `discipleship_mentor_training_curriculum.md`.
- You are developing a Bible-study group leader — use
  `domain-biblical-studies/group-leader-facilitation/biblical_groupleader_apprentice_development.md`.

**Audience:** Program leads and pastors assessing a candidate; a prospective mentor assessing themselves.

---

## Inputs / Context

**Required:**

1. **Who this is.** How long they have been a Christian, their own experience of being discipled, and
   what they are volunteering for.
2. **Their current life.** Capacity, stability, and what else they are carrying — work, family, health,
   recent losses or upheavals.
3. **How they handle being wrong.** Evidence, from anyone who knows them, of how they respond to
   correction, disagreement, and not knowing an answer. This predicts mentoring quality better than
   biblical knowledge does.
4. **What the role requires here.** The specific commitment: duration, frequency, who they would be
   paired with, and what support exists around them.

**Optional:**

5. **Declared tradition (optional).** May set formal requirements — membership, elder approval,
   ordination, doctrinal affirmation, gender-specific pairing conventions — which are applied as that
   tradition's gate and named as such.
6. **Their own view.** Whether they think they are ready, and why.
7. **Any concern already raised.** By them or anyone else.

**If any required input is missing:** Ask clarifying questions before proceeding. If nobody can speak
to how the person handles being wrong, say the assessment is incomplete rather than substituting
enthusiasm or knowledge for it.

---

## Constraints

### Must

- Assess across **four dimensions** — character, capacity, self-awareness, boundary-holding — and
  report each separately. A strong score in one does not offset a serious gap in another.
- Weight **self-awareness and boundary-holding above biblical knowledge.** A mentor who knows less but
  refers well does far less harm than the reverse.
- Issue a **decision-forcing verdict** from the fixed list, including an insufficiency branch.
- For any not-yet, name **exactly what would change it** and a realistic timeframe.
- Treat **recent crisis, loss, or upheaval** as a timing question, not a character finding.
- Distinguish **tradition-specific formal requirements** from readiness itself, and report them
  separately.
- Route any **safeguarding concern out immediately**, outside the verdict, without exception.
- State what the assessment **could not see**.

### Must Not

- Assign a numeric readiness score, or compute a total across dimensions.
- Gate readiness on credentials, education, tenure, eloquence, or biblical knowledge alone.
- Pass someone because the program is short of mentors. Capacity pressure is not evidence of readiness.
- Report the finding in language about the person's standing before God, faith, or worth.
- Invent research on mentoring effectiveness, or statistics about mentor failure.
- Quote Scripture text from memory, or use passages about leadership qualification as an assessment
  rubric without noting that traditions read them differently.
- Name a specific service, counselor, or program.
- Treat a safeguarding concern as a factor to be weighed against strengths. It is not weighed; it routes.
- Emit "it depends" as a verdict.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)

- **Must:** where a tradition applies formal qualifications — eldership, ordination, membership
  standing, doctrinal subscription, gender-specific pairing — apply them as that tradition's gate,
  report them in their own section, and keep them distinct from the readiness assessment itself.
- **Must Not:** import one tradition's qualification standards as universal readiness criteria, or
  treat a tradition's formal requirement as evidence about the person's character.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Screen for safeguarding

Before assessing anything, check for any concern involving harm, misconduct, boundary violation, or
risk to a vulnerable person. If present, stop the assessment, route it to the safeguarding lead
`[VERIFY]`, and state that readiness is not the question in front of you.

### Step 2 — Assess character

Look for evidence of: honesty about their own struggles, consistency between what they say and do,
how they speak about people who are not present, and whether they can be told they are wrong. Cite
evidence; do not infer character from role or reputation.

### Step 3 — Assess capacity

Consider time, energy, stability, and what else they are carrying. A recent bereavement, a new baby, a
health crisis, or a job loss makes this the wrong season — and that is a timing finding, not a
character one. Say so in those words.

### Step 4 — Assess self-awareness

Look for: whether they know what they do not know, whether they can say "I don't know," whether they
recognize their own tendency to fix or advise, and whether they understand why someone might find it
hard to disagree with them. This is the strongest predictor available.

### Step 5 — Assess boundary-holding

Can they keep a confidence and also break one when required? Do they understand that routing out is
care? Have they worked `discipleship_mentor_boundaries_and_referral.md`? Would they consult when
unsure, or press on?

### Step 6 — Apply tradition-specific requirements separately

Report any formal qualification the tradition requires in its own section, distinct from the four
dimensions.

### Step 7 — Issue the verdict

Apply the verdict rule mechanically. Name what the assessment could not see. For any not-yet, give the
concrete change and timeframe, and say who will tell the person and how.

## Output Format

Produce exactly this structure.

```
# Mentor Readiness — [candidate]

## Safeguarding Screen
[Clear | Concern raised — assessment stopped and routed to [role]. VERIFY the correct process.]

## The Four Dimensions
| Dimension | Finding | Evidence | Strong / adequate / gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Character | [..] | [..] | [..] |
| Capacity | [..] | [..] | [..] |
| Self-awareness | [..] | [..] | [..] |
| Boundary-holding | [..] | [..] | [..] |

## Timing
[Anything making this the wrong season — stated as timing, not character.]

## Tradition-Specific Requirements
| Requirement | Source | Met? |
|---|---|---|
[Reported separately from readiness. Absence here is not a character finding.]

## Verdict
**[READY | READY WITH SUPPORT | NOT YET | INSUFFICIENT — pending [item]]**

Rationale: [..]

**If READY WITH SUPPORT:** the support required is [..], provided by [role], reviewed at [point].
**If NOT YET:** what would change it is [..]; a realistic timeframe is [..]; this will be said to them
by [role], framed as timing and support.

## What This Assessment Could Not See
[..]
```

**Verdict rule (mechanical, no other verdict may be emitted):**
- **READY** iff character, self-awareness, and boundary-holding are all adequate or strong, capacity is
  adequate, and no safeguarding concern exists.
- **READY WITH SUPPORT** iff one dimension shows a gap that a named, specific support arrangement
  addresses, and that dimension is not boundary-holding.
- **NOT YET** iff boundary-holding shows a gap, or two or more dimensions show gaps, or capacity makes
  this the wrong season.
- **INSUFFICIENT — pending [item]** iff no one could speak to how the person handles being wrong, or
  another required input is missing. Name the single cheapest thing that unblocks it.
- A safeguarding concern is never weighed into this verdict; it stops the assessment.
- "It depends" is banned.

## Verification

- [ ] The safeguarding screen runs first and, if triggered, stops the assessment rather than weighing it.
- [ ] All four dimensions are reported separately with evidence; no total is computed.
- [ ] Capacity findings from recent crisis or loss are stated as timing, not character.
- [ ] Tradition-specific requirements appear in their own section, separate from readiness.
- [ ] The verdict is one of the four permitted values and follows the rule mechanically.
- [ ] Any not-yet names a concrete change, a timeframe, and who will deliver it.

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Read biblical knowledge or fluency as readiness. The most articulate candidate is not reliably the
  safest one.
- Pass someone whose boundary-holding is weak because their character is strong. Good people who cannot
  refer cause most of the harm in lay ministry.
- Treat eagerness as evidence. Eagerness is common and tells you little; how someone responds to being
  told no tells you a great deal.
- Let a mentor shortage move the bar. That pressure is real and it belongs in the pipeline prompt, not
  in this verdict.
- Deliver "not yet" without the path. An unexplained no reads as a judgement on the person.
- Assess character from reputation or position rather than from evidence someone can point to.

✅ **DO:**
- Ask specifically how the person responds to correction, and treat an absent answer as INSUFFICIENT
  rather than filling it with impressions.
- Say "this is the wrong season" in those words when capacity is the issue, so it is not heard as a
  character verdict.
- Keep boundary-holding as the non-negotiable dimension — it is the one that cannot be covered by
  support.
- Separate the tradition's formal gate from readiness, so a person who does not meet a membership or
  ordination requirement is not implicitly told they lack character.
- Use INSUFFICIENT freely. A missing input is a real finding and is far better than a confident guess.
- Name who will deliver a not-yet and how, so the message arrives as care rather than as rejection.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** the safeguarding screen precedes assessment, and
  dimensions are assessed before the verdict rule is applied, so no strength can be weighed against a
  safety concern.
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework):** four independently-reported dimensions with no
  aggregate prevent the single-impression judgement, and make boundary-holding structurally
  non-compensable.
- **CM-02 (Constraint Specification):** the Must Not block bans the field's characteristic failures —
  passing for capacity reasons, gating on credentials, scoring readiness, weighing a safeguarding
  concern.
- **QA-01 (Self-Verification):** the mechanical verdict rule with an explicit insufficiency branch
  forces a committed, reproducible outcome instead of a hedged recommendation.
- **QA-04 (Uncertainty Acknowledgment):** INSUFFICIENT is a first-class verdict and "what this
  assessment could not see" is mandatory, keeping the limits of a character judgement visible.
