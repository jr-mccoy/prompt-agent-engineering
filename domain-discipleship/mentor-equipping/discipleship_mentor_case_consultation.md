# Mentor Case Consultation

**Objective:** Run the conversation where a discipler takes one live, hard relationship to a designated
third party — settling first what may be said and in what form, then working the mentor's actual question
rather than the mentee's situation, checking throughout that consulting is not being used in place of a
referral, and ending with what the mentor does, what the mentee is told, and what is not repeated
anywhere.

> **Boundary guardrail.** This is not clinical supervision, case management, or a review of the mentee.
> A lay mentor consulting another lay person does not become qualified by the conversation, and neither
> does the person they consult. If what is being brought is abuse, self-harm, suicidality, a
> mental-health condition, addiction, a safeguarding matter, or anything involving a minor or vulnerable
> adult, **consultation is the wrong tool** — that routes now, to the safeguarding lead and to licensed
> professionals or authorities `[VERIFY: identify the correct service from an official source]`. The
> most common misuse of this conversation is a mentor talking through, at length and in good faith, a
> situation they should have handed on an hour earlier.

**When to use:** When a mentor is genuinely stuck in a relationship that sits inside their role, and
needs help with what *they* should do — not on a schedule, and not as a way of reporting on how the
mentee is getting on.

**When NOT to use:**
- You are designing where mentors take things and how often — use
  `discipleship_mentor_support_and_sustainability.md`, whose "Supervision Beyond Peers" step designs the
  route; this prompt is the conversation that route delivers.
- You are running the mentors' peer cohort — use
  `../peer-and-accountability/discipleship_peer_cohort_facilitation.md`, which deliberately keeps mentees
  out of the room; consultation is the bounded exception the cohort's rule creates the need for.
- The matter is above the mentor's role — use `discipleship_mentor_boundaries_and_referral.md` and route
  it, rather than talking about it first.
- The season is over and you want a retrospective — use `discipleship_mentor_season_debrief.md`.
- The mentee is in front of you and something has just been disclosed — use
  `../session-and-lesson/discipleship_hard_conversation_navigation.md`.

**Audience:** The mentor bringing the situation and the person they bring it to — a program lead, an
experienced discipler, or whoever the program has designated. Both need this before they start.

## Inputs / Context

**Required:**

1. **The mentor's question, stated as their own.** What *they* are trying to decide or do. If the question
   cannot be phrased that way — if it comes out as "what's going on with him" — the consultation has
   already become a discussion of a person and should be re-framed before it starts.
2. **What the mentee was told about confidentiality, and when.** The covenant's limits, and whether they
   were stated before disclosure or after. This governs what may be said here, and where the limits were
   never set, that fact constrains the whole conversation.
3. **Who the third party is and what standing they have.** A program lead who makes pairing decisions
   hears things differently from a peer with no authority. What is said here may reach a decision, and
   that has to be known in advance by everyone including, in most cases, the mentee.
4. **Whether the mentee knows this conversation happens.** As a standing practice, ideally named at the
   first meeting. Where they do not know, that is the first thing to fix, and the consultation is
   narrowed until it is.

**Optional:**

5. **Declared tradition (optional).** May shape whether confession or disclosure is understood to be
   held under a seal, who may hear what a discipler has been told, and whether an elder or priest has a
   defined role — all of which change what may be repeated and to whom. Where a tradition holds material
   under a seal, that constraint is stricter than this prompt's and governs. Where none is declared, the
   default is de-identified and minimal.
6. **What has already been tried.** So the conversation does not spend its time on ground the mentor has
   covered.
7. **The mentor's own state.** Whether they are stuck, frightened, resentful, or out of capacity — which
   is often the real subject and is a legitimate one.

**If any required input is missing:** Ask before anything is disclosed, not after. If the confidentiality
limits were never set with the mentee, say so plainly and restrict the consultation to the mentor's own
conduct and options until they are — a consultation cannot retroactively license a disclosure the mentee
never agreed to.

## Constraints

### Must

- Settle **what may be said, in what form, and who else may hear it — before any disclosure**, and put
  that at the top of the conversation rather than assuming it.
- **De-identify by default**: role and situation, not name, and no detail that identifies a person in a
  community of that size. State that the mentor decides what is stripped, and that stripping too much is
  a smaller cost than stripping too little.
- Keep the conversation on **the mentor's question and the mentor's options**, and name the drift into
  discussing the mentee as the specific failure to watch for.
- Run the **referral check as a standing item**, near the start and again before any action is agreed:
  is this above the role? If yes, the consultation ends and the routing begins.
- Produce **what the mentor does next**, with a date, and **what the mentee is told about the
  consultation** — including the words.
- State **what does not leave the room**, and what does — because something usually must, and pretending
  otherwise is how a promise gets broken later.
- Name the **third party's own limits**: they are not a clinician, a supervisor in the professional sense,
  or a decision-maker about the mentee's life either.
- Include the case where **the third party is worried about the mentor**, not the mentee, and what happens
  then.

### Must Not

- Discuss, assess, diagnose, characterize, or form a view about the mentee's mental health, character,
  spiritual condition, or motives.
- Produce notes, records, or a file about the mentee, or any document that follows them.
- Let the consultation substitute for, precede, or delay a referral or a safeguarding report.
- Allow material shared here to reach a pairing, program, or membership decision unless the mentee was
  told in advance that it could.
- Repeat anything the mentee said in confidence that the confidentiality frame did not already cover.
- Turn the peer cohort into the venue for this — the cohort exists for the mentors' own formation, and
  the case conference is the failure it is explicitly built against.
- Name any counselor, agency, service, or hotline; state any legal, reporting, or record-keeping duty —
  `[VERIFY]` and route to the safeguarding lead and `domain-legal/`.
- Quote Scripture text from memory, or use any passage to justify a mentor staying in a situation they
  should hand on.
- Score the mentor's handling, rate the relationship, or rank the difficulty of the case.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)

- **Must:** note where traditions differ on the seal of confession, on what a discipler may repeat to a
  leader, on whether an elder, priest, or overseer has a defined right to know, and on how much authority
  the consulted person's counsel carries — and let a declared tradition's stricter constraint govern
  wherever it is stricter than this prompt's default.
- **Must Not:** let a tradition's structure of authority or oversight be used to widen what may be
  repeated beyond what the mentee was told, or to require a mentor to disclose to a leader something the
  mentee shared under limits they were given in advance.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Frame before disclosing

Before the situation is described at all, settle four things out loud: what the mentee was told about
confidentiality and when; what form this will take (de-identified, unless the mentee agreed otherwise);
who the third party is and whether what they hear can reach a decision; and what will not leave the room.
A consultation that starts with the story and sorts the frame afterwards has already disclosed more than
it was entitled to.

### Step 2 — Get the mentor's question into the first person

Have the mentor state what *they* need to decide. "Should I keep meeting her at all" is a consultation
question. "Why does she keep doing this" is not, and re-asking it in the first person usually changes what
the conversation turns out to be about.

### Step 3 — Run the referral check early

Test the situation against the mentor's standing scope. If it is outside — abuse, self-harm, suicidality,
a clinical condition, addiction, a safeguarding matter — stop. The output is the routing and the words,
not advice. Ask the check plainly rather than letting it emerge: a consultation that discovers at minute
forty that this was always a referral has done harm by taking the time.

### Step 4 — Work the mentor, not the mentee

Spend the conversation on what the mentor is doing, avoiding, feeling, and able to sustain. Where the
third party notices the mentor is the one in difficulty — out of capacity, frightened, over-involved,
resentful — that becomes the subject, and it is a legitimate outcome for the consultation to be about the
mentor entirely.

### Step 5 — Decide the smallest next thing

Agree one action, with a date, that is inside the mentor's role. Consultation that ends in general
encouragement changes nothing and will be repeated next month with the same material.

### Step 6 — Settle what the mentee is told

Write the words. A mentee who learns later that they have been discussed, however carefully, experiences
it as a breach even where the frame permitted it — so the default is that they knew this happens, and the
specific instance is named to them where anything about them was said that the standing frame did not
already cover.

### Step 7 — Close the room, then check

State what stays here, what leaves, and where the third party takes anything that troubled them. Then
check: did the frame come before the disclosure? Was the referral question asked out loud? Did the
conversation stay on the mentor? Does any record of the mentee exist as a result of this?

## Output Format

Produce exactly this structure. Held by the mentor and the third party; never given to the mentee.

```
# Consultation — [date] — [situation in four words]

## Frame, Settled First
| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What was the mentee told about confidentiality, and when? | [..] |
| Form of this conversation | [de-identified / named, with consent given on ..] |
| Who I'm speaking to, and what they decide | [role, and whether this can reach a decision] |
| Does the mentee know consultations happen? | [yes, told on .. / no — narrow the conversation] |
| What will not leave this room | [..] |

## Referral Check — Asked Out Loud
| Above the role? | [no — or this ends here and routes] |
|---|---|
**If yes:** destination [VERIFY] · words the mentor says: "[..]"

## The Mentor's Question
> "[In the first person. What I need to decide or do.]"

## Worked
- What I've tried: [..]
- What I'm avoiding: [..]
- What I can actually sustain: [..]
- What the third party noticed about me: [..]

## Next Thing
| Action | Inside my role because | By |
|---|---|---|

## What the Mentee Is Told
> "[Words. Or: covered by the standing frame they were given on ..]"

## Closing the Room
- Stays here: [..]
- Leaves, and to whom: [..]
- No record of the mentee exists as a result of this conversation: [confirmed]
```

## Verification

- [ ] The confidentiality frame is settled before any part of the situation is disclosed.
- [ ] The material is de-identified unless the mentee gave consent in advance to something else.
- [ ] The referral check is asked explicitly and early, and ends the consultation where it returns yes.
- [ ] The conversation and the output are about the mentor's actions and options, not the mentee's
      condition.
- [ ] What the mentee is told is decided and written, including the words.
- [ ] No record, note, or file about the mentee exists as a result.

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Start with the story. Once the situation has been described, the frame can only ratify a disclosure
  that already happened, and the mentor will feel the difference even if nobody says so.
- Consult instead of referring. Talking a safeguarding or clinical matter through with a lay third party
  feels responsible and costs time the situation does not have.
- Let it become a discussion of the mentee. The tell is grammatical: when the sentences stop starting with
  "I" and start starting with their name, the consultation has turned into a case conference.
- Use the peer cohort for this. That room is for the mentors' own formation, and mentees being discussed
  by people they never met is the specific failure it is designed against.
- Take notes about the mentee. A file created in good faith is still a file, and it will outlive the
  reason it was made.
- Let something said here reach a pairing or program decision when the mentee was never told that could
  happen.
- End on encouragement. "You're doing better than you think" is kind and changes nothing; the mentor will
  be back with the same situation.

✅ **DO:**
- Settle the frame in the first two minutes, out loud, as a habit rather than a formality.
- Strip identifying detail by default, and accept losing some texture — over-stripping costs a little
  precision, under-stripping costs someone's privacy.
- Ask the referral question early and plainly, and let it end the conversation.
- Keep the mentor in the first person, and let the consultation be about them when that is what it is.
- Agree one small thing with a date on it.
- Tell the mentee, or make sure the standing frame already told them. Either is fine; neither being true
  is not.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** the frame precedes disclosure and the referral check
  precedes advice — the two orderings that make this conversation safe rather than merely well-meant.
- **CM-02 (Constraint Specification):** the Must Not block encodes what separates consultation from case
  conference: no assessment of the mentee, no record, no route into a decision they were not told about.
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework):** what may be said, who is hearing it, what the mentor
  needs, and what the mentee is told are worked as four axes, so a trusted listener cannot substitute for
  a settled frame.
- **OC-03 (Markdown Table Specification):** the frame table forces each permission question to be answered
  before the conversation proceeds, which prose reliably lets slide.
- **QA-01 (Self-Verification):** the closing check tests for the residue this conversation leaves —
  a record about the mentee, or a disclosure the frame never covered.
