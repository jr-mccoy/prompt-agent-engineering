# Mentor Posture in Doubt and Deconstruction

**Objective:** Prepare a discipler to stay in relationship with someone whose faith is coming apart —
starting with the mentor's own fear rather than the mentee's arguments, fixing the relationship as
unconditional on where the person lands, ruling out the responses that end the conversation (the counter-
argument, the deadline, the verdict, the quiet withdrawal), and separating the doubt itself from
distress, isolation, or harm that belongs somewhere else.

> **Boundary guardrail.** This is about the mentor's posture, not about answering the questions. A lay
> discipler is not an apologist, a theologian, a therapist, or an assessor of anyone's standing before
> God. Where deconstruction is bound up with depression, panic, an eating disorder, self-harm,
> suicidality, or the aftermath of church-related harm or abuse, the faith conversation is not the live
> issue and does not get worked first — that routes to licensed professionals and, where there is danger,
> to appropriate authorities `[VERIFY: identify the correct service from an official source]`. The
> question of whether someone is genuinely converted is never asked, answered, or implied here, by anyone,
> at any point.

**When to use:** When someone a mentor is discipling has begun seriously questioning, is pulling away from
church, or has said outright that they no longer believe — and the mentor has to decide how to be in the
relationship, often while frightened.

**When NOT to use:**
- The mentee wants to work through a specific intellectual objection — that is
  `domain-biblical-studies/learner-self-study/biblical_learner_honest_questions_doubt_explorer.md`, which
  is theirs to run, and handing it to them is often the best thing the mentor does.
- The mentor is preparing for an apologetic conversation with someone outside the faith — use
  `domain-biblical-studies/apologetics-engagement/biblical_apologetics_conversation_prep.md`. This prompt
  is not that, and treating a deconstructing friend as that audience is the error it exists to prevent.
- Growth has simply stalled and the cause is unclear — use
  `../learner-pathways/discipleship_stalled_growth_diagnostic.md`, which differentiates doubt from
  exhaustion, isolation, circumstance, harm, and clinical distress before anything is worked.
- Something was disclosed in the moment and a response is needed now — use
  `../session-and-lesson/discipleship_hard_conversation_navigation.md`.
- Someone in a group has asserted something that contradicts the ecumenical creeds and a leader must
  respond — that is
  `domain-biblical-studies/group-leader-facilitation/biblical_groupleader_heretical_claim_response.md`,
  and its correction frame is explicitly the wrong frame here.

**Audience:** The mentor. Also the program lead training mentors for a situation almost all of them will
meet and almost none have been prepared for.

## Inputs / Context

**Required:**

1. **What the mentee has actually said, in their words.** Not the mentor's summary of their position, and
   not the category the mentor has put it in. "I don't think I believe any of it any more" and "I can't
   get past what the church did" and "I don't know if God is good" are three different situations with
   three different responses.
2. **What the mentor is afraid of.** Losing them, being blamed, being inadequate, being contaminated,
   having to tell someone, discovering they share the doubt. This is the actual working material, and a
   mentor who has not named it will act on it anyway.
3. **What has changed around the mentee.** Church attendance, friendships, family pressure, a recent loss,
   a recent harm, a move, a book, a relationship. Deconstruction rarely starts in an argument.
4. **Who else knows, and what they have done.** Family, church leaders, friends. Pressure from elsewhere
   shapes what the mentor's relationship needs to be, which is often the only place with none.

**Optional:**

5. **Declared tradition (optional).** May shape whether doubt is understood as a phase of faith, a
   spiritual attack, an intellectual problem, or a sin; whether there is a defined pastoral process; and
   what membership or participation depends on. Where a tradition is declared, its own understanding is
   described plainly *as its own* — and the mentee is told what, if anything, follows practically, since
   discovering that later is its own injury. Where none is declared, the posture stays tradition-general.
6. **The mentor's own history with doubt.** An asset if it is not deployed as a template.
7. **Whether the mentee has asked for anything.** Often they have, and it is smaller than the mentor
   assumes — usually to be able to say it out loud without the relationship changing.

**If any required input is missing:** Ask, and start with what the mentee actually said rather than what
the mentor concluded. If the mentor cannot say what they are afraid of, work that before anything else —
it is the input the rest depends on.

## Constraints

### Must

- Work **the mentor's fear first**, explicitly, before any response is planned. Every failure mode below
  is that fear acting unsupervised.
- Screen for **distress, isolation, and prior harm** and route those, stating plainly that where they are
  present the doubt is not the thing to work on first.
- State the **unconditional term out loud and in words the mentee will hear**: this relationship does not
  depend on what you conclude, and there is no point at which it quietly stops.
- Rule out **counter-arguing** as a posture, and say why — a mentee who is met with a case learns that
  honesty costs them the relationship's warmth, and stops being honest.
- Rule out **any deadline, milestone, or expected trajectory**, including the implicit ones: praying about
  it, coming back by Christmas, giving it a season.
- Give the mentor **something to actually say** — verbatim — for the first hearing, for "do you think I'm
  still a Christian", for "what would you do", and for the silence they will want to fill.
- Distinguish **questions the mentor should not carry**: what is theirs (presence, honesty, staying),
  what is the mentee's own to work (the content), and what routes out (distress, harm, crisis).
- Name what the mentor does with **their own doubt** if the conversation raises it, and where they take
  it — which is not to the mentee.
- Name what the mentor does about **other people's pressure**: family or church asking the mentor to
  produce a result, and the words for declining that.

### Must Not

- Assess, state, hint at, or help anyone else determine whether the mentee is genuinely converted, saved,
  backslidden, or still a Christian. This is out of scope for the entire domain and is at its most
  tempting here.
- Supply apologetic content, counter-arguments, or a case to make — including "resources to share" offered
  in a pastoral tone.
- Set, imply, or agree any timeframe for resolution, or describe the relationship as continuing "while
  they work it out".
- Frame doubt as sin, rebellion, pride, a discipline problem, a consequence of unconfessed sin, or an
  attack to be resisted — or use any tradition's category to that effect.
- Recommend that the mentor report the mentee's state to church leaders, family, or a program, or treat
  the doubt as information about them that travels.
- Suggest increased Bible reading, prayer, service, or attendance as a remedy for doubt, or as something
  to keep up in the meantime.
- Treat leaving faith as an outcome to be prevented by the relationship, or make the relationship's
  warmth contingent on its being prevented.
- Name any counselor, agency, or service; state any legal or membership consequence — `[VERIFY]` and route
  to `domain-legal/` and to the community's own governance.
- Quote Scripture text from memory, or deploy any passage about falling away, hardening, or apostasy in
  connection with a real person.
- Score, stage, or track anyone's doubt.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)

- **Must:** name where traditions genuinely differ — on whether doubt is a normal phase of faith, an
  intellectual matter, a spiritual attack, or a sin; on assurance and perseverance; on what participation,
  communion, membership, or service depends on; and on whether a defined pastoral process exists — and
  where a tradition attaches practical consequences to someone's stated unbelief, say so plainly to the
  mentee rather than letting them find out.
- **Must Not:** rule on which tradition's account of doubt is correct; allow any tradition's language about
  falling away, apostasy, hardening, or church discipline to be applied to the person by the mentor; or
  let a tradition's process replace the mentor's own commitment to stay in relationship regardless of
  outcome.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Name the mentor's fear

Before anything about the mentee, get the fear out: what the mentor is afraid will happen, to the mentee,
to themselves, to their sense of having done this well. Then name what that fear will make them do if it
is not held — argue, withdraw, fix, hurry, tell someone. The whole posture is built on this, because a
frightened mentor produces every failure below without choosing any of them.

### Step 2 — Screen before working the doubt

Check for depression, panic, self-harm, suicidality, an eating disorder, addiction, and for church-related
harm or abuse in the background. Where any is present, that is first `[VERIFY]`, and the faith conversation
waits. Deconstruction after harm is not primarily an intellectual event, and treating it as one asks the
person to debate their way out of an injury.

### Step 3 — Fix the term of the relationship, out loud

Write the sentence that makes the relationship unconditional on the outcome, and say it early — before the
mentee has to wonder. Then write what makes it credible: what does not change, what the mentor will still
do, what happens if they stop coming to church, and what happens if they say plainly that they no longer
believe. An unconditional relationship that has never been stated is assumed to be conditional, and
reasonably so.

### Step 4 — Take the arguing off the table

Decide in advance that the mentor is not making a case, and write what they do instead when a challenge is
put to them: ask what is underneath it, say honestly when they do not know, and let the question stand.
Then write what they say if the mentee asks them directly what they think — because refusing to answer is
its own kind of withdrawal, and the honest short answer is usually better than either the case or the
dodge.

### Step 5 — Write the four sentences

Verbatim: the response when they first say it; the response to "do you think I'm still a Christian";
the response to "what would you do"; and what the mentor says instead of filling a silence. These are the
moments where an unrehearsed mentor reaches for the argument.

### Step 6 — Separate what belongs to whom

Three columns: what is the mentor's (staying, honesty, presence, not flinching), what is the mentee's own
to work and at their own pace — with the doubt explorer handed over if they want it — and what routes out.
A mentor who believes the resolution is theirs to produce will not be able to stop pushing.

### Step 7 — Handle the pressure and the mentor's own doubt, then check

Write what the mentor says to a parent, spouse, or church leader who wants a result, and where the mentor
takes their own questions if this raises them — a supervisor, a cohort, a friend outside, never the mentee.
Then check the plan: is there a deadline anywhere, including an implied one? Is there a verdict, even a
kind one? Is the warmth conditional on an outcome? Is there a single sentence here that would tell the
mentee their honesty costs them something?

## Output Format

Produce exactly this structure. Written for the mentor, and short enough to re-read before a meeting.

```
# Staying — [mentee, or "them"]

## What I'm Afraid Of
[Named plainly. Then: what this fear will make me do if I don't hold it.]

## Screen
| Present? | Routes to | Then |
|---|---|---|
| Distress, self-harm, suicidality, panic | [kind of service — VERIFY] | Faith conversation waits |
| Church-related harm or abuse in the background | [VERIFY] + safeguarding lead | Not an argument to be had |
| Isolation — nobody else left | — | Widen first |

## The Term of This Relationship
> "[The sentence. Said early, not held in reserve.]"

**What does not change:** [..]
**If they stop coming to church:** [..]
**If they say they no longer believe:** [..]

## What I Am Not Doing
- Not making a case. Instead: [..]
- No timeframe, including the quiet ones: [..]
- No verdict on where they stand with God — not asked, not answered, not implied.

## The Sentences
| Moment | What I say |
|---|---|
| The first time they say it | "[words]" |
| "Do you think I'm still a Christian?" | "[words]" |
| "What would you do?" | "[words]" |
| The silence I want to fill | "[what I do instead]" |

## Whose Job Is What
| Mine | Theirs | Routes out |
|---|---|---|
| Staying, honesty, presence | The questions themselves, at their pace | [..] |

**Offered, not assigned:** the doubt explorer, if and when they want it.

## Pressure From Elsewhere
| Who | What they want | What I say |
|---|---|---|

## My Own Questions
[Where they go. Not to the mentee.]

## Check Before the Next Meeting
- [ ] No deadline anywhere, including implied
- [ ] Nothing here judges where they stand with God
- [ ] Nothing here makes my warmth conditional on the outcome
```

## Verification

- [ ] The mentor's own fear is named before any response is planned.
- [ ] Distress, harm, and isolation are screened and routed before the doubt is treated as the subject.
- [ ] The relationship's unconditional term is written as a spoken sentence, not an intention.
- [ ] No apologetic content, counter-argument, or resource-to-share appears anywhere in the output.
- [ ] No timeframe, milestone, or expected trajectory appears, including implied ones.
- [ ] Nothing assesses, states, or implies whether the person is genuinely converted, and nothing routes
      their state to leaders, family, or a program as information about them.

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Answer the arguments. A mentee who brings honest doubt and is met with a case has learned what honesty
  costs, and the next thing they stop saying is the thing that mattered.
- Give it a season. "Let's see where you are in a few months" is a deadline in a kind voice, and the mentee
  hears the date.
- Answer "am I still a Christian". Whichever way it is answered, the mentor has taken up a position the
  domain says is not theirs — and a reassuring yes is as much an assessment as a worried no.
- Treat it as an information problem. Most deconstruction starts with an experience — a harm, a loss, a
  hypocrisy, an exhaustion — and the arguments arrive afterwards to explain a shift that has already
  happened.
- Prescribe more input. More Bible reading, more prayer, more church offered as a remedy tells the person
  their doubt is a compliance failure and that the mentor was not listening.
- Report it. Passing on that someone is losing their faith, however pastorally framed, turns the one
  relationship where they could be honest into a channel to everywhere else.
- Withdraw slowly. Meeting less, praying shorter, going quieter — never announced and always noticed. It
  is the most common ending and it confirms the fear that made speaking up hard.

✅ **DO:**
- Name the fear first. Almost every error above is that fear acting without supervision.
- Screen for harm and distress before treating any of it as a conversation about ideas.
- Say the unconditional term out loud and early, and say what makes it credible in practice.
- Answer honestly when asked what you think, briefly, and hand the question back rather than building on
  it. Refusing to answer at all reads as withdrawal.
- Hand over the doubt explorer as an offer rather than an assignment, and let them not take it.
- Say plainly what practical consequences the community actually attaches, if any. Finding out later is a
  second injury on top of the first.
- Take your own questions somewhere that is not the mentee.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-01 (Clear Objective Statement):** the objective fixes the deliverable as *posture* and names the
  four banned responses, so the prompt cannot quietly become a guide to answering objections.
- **CM-02 (Constraint Specification):** the Must Not block prohibits the specific moves this situation
  attracts — the verdict, the deadline, the apologetic resource, the report to leadership.
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework):** the mentor's fear, the mentee's actual words, the
  surrounding pressure, and the routing questions are worked as separate axes, which stops the whole
  situation being read as an intellectual one.
- **QA-04 (Uncertainty Acknowledgment):** the posture requires the mentor to say when they do not know and
  to let a question stand unresolved, which is the behaviour the relationship actually depends on.
- **QA-01 (Self-Verification):** the closing check looks for the implied deadline and the kindly verdict —
  the two failures that survive good intentions and get written down anyway.
