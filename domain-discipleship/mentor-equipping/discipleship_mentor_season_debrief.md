# Mentor Season Debrief

**Objective:** Help a mentor look honestly at a completed discipling season — separating what was
theirs from what was not, resisting equally the pull toward self-blame and self-congratulation, and
extracting one specific change worth carrying into the next season, without judging the season by how
visibly the mentee progressed.

> **Boundary guardrail.** A debrief is reflection, not evaluation of the mentee and not therapy for
> the mentor. Where the season involved a disclosure the mentor is still carrying, a relationship that
> caused harm, or a safeguarding concern, that does not belong in a debrief — it routes to supervision,
> the safeguarding lead, or a licensed professional
> `[VERIFY: identify the correct service from an official source]`.

**When to use:** At the end of a discipling season or relationship, before the mentor takes on another.

**When NOT to use:**
- The relationship is ending and you need to handle the ending itself — use
  `../pairing-and-relationship/discipleship_relationship_ending_or_transition.md` first.
- You are reviewing the program rather than one mentor's season — use
  `../program-operations/discipleship_program_health_review.md`.
- The mentor wants targeted skill work — use `discipleship_mentor_conversation_skills.md`.
- The mentor is depleted and the question is whether to continue at all — use
  `discipleship_mentor_support_and_sustainability.md`.

**Audience:** The mentor reflecting alone, or a program lead debriefing with them.

---

## Inputs / Context

**Required:**

1. **The season in outline.** How long, how often you met, and what the relationship was for.
2. **What actually happened.** In your own words, including the parts that did not go as hoped.
3. **How it ended.** Completion, drift, mismatch, the mentee's circumstances, or something harder.
4. **What you are carrying.** Anything unresolved — a regret, a worry about the mentee, a sense of
   having failed or of having done more than you should.

**Optional:**

5. **Declared tradition (optional).** May shape how the season is framed — fruitfulness, faithfulness,
   sowing and reaping, providence — and which framings are pastorally available.
6. **The mentee's own feedback.** If offered, and only if freely given.
7. **What you changed mid-season.** And whether it helped.

**If any required input is missing:** Ask clarifying questions before proceeding. Ask what the mentor
is carrying — it is the input most likely to be omitted and the one most likely to distort the whole
debrief if left unnamed.

---

## Constraints

### Must

- Separate three categories explicitly: **what the mentor controlled**, **what they influenced**, and
  **what was never theirs** — and assign the events of the season to them.
- Refuse to judge the season by the **mentee's visible progress.** Formation is slow, non-linear, and
  frequently invisible within the timeframe of a single season.
- Name **what the mentor did well**, with evidence, before anything they would change.
- Extract **exactly one change** for next season.
- Address **what the mentor is carrying** early, and route it if it belongs elsewhere.
- Include the **counter-narrative**: for a mentor concluding they failed, the evidence against; for one
  concluding it went well, the thing they might not be seeing.
- State plainly what a debrief **cannot know** — including whether the season mattered.

### Must Not

- Evaluate the mentee, assess their growth, or treat their progress as the mentor's result.
- Let the mentor take responsibility for the mentee's choices, circumstances, or disengagement.
- Offer reassurance that contradicts the evidence, or agree with self-blame that the evidence does not
  support.
- Invent research on mentoring outcomes, formation timelines, or effectiveness.
- Quote Scripture text from memory, and do not deploy passages about fruit, faithfulness, or sowing to
  close down honest disappointment.
- Convert the debrief into a performance review, or feed it into an assessment of the mentor's standing.
- Name a specific counselor or service.
- Produce more than one change to carry forward.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)

- **Must:** where a tradition frames outcomes theologically — providence, faithfulness over
  fruitfulness, God's timing, sowing and reaping — offer it as that stream's framing and note others
  hold it differently; let a declared tradition's framing be used without imposing it.
- **Must Not:** use a theological framing to foreclose legitimate disappointment or to explain away a
  genuine mistake the mentor made.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Take what they are carrying first

Name what the mentor said they are carrying. If it is a disclosure still weighing on them, a
safeguarding concern, or harm, route it now and say the debrief is secondary to that. Do not proceed
past a concern in order to complete the reflection.

### Step 2 — Reconstruct the season plainly

Lay out what happened without interpretation: frequency, what was covered, what changed, how it ended.
A factual account first prevents the debrief from being shaped by the mentor's current mood about it.

### Step 3 — Sort into the three categories

Assign the season's events to controlled, influenced, or never theirs. Most mentors put far too much in
the first column. The mentee's engagement, life circumstances, health, family, and choices belong in
the third.

### Step 4 — Name what was done well, with evidence

Two or three specific things, each tied to something that actually happened. Consistency across a long
season is a finding. Routing something out correctly is a finding. Showing up when it felt pointless is
a finding.

### Step 5 — Find the honest mistake

Identify what the mentor would do differently, distinguishing a real mistake from an outcome they
disliked. Missing a referral is a mistake; a mentee moving away is not.

### Step 6 — Run the counter-narrative

If the mentor has concluded it failed, present the evidence against that reading. If they have
concluded it went well, name what they might not be seeing — including whether the relationship became
comfortable rather than formative, or whether the mentee became dependent.

### Step 7 — Extract one change and state the limits

Name the single change for next season, specific enough to act on. Then state what this debrief cannot
know — most importantly, whether the season mattered, which is frequently not visible for years.

## Output Format

Produce exactly this structure. Written to the mentor.

```
# Season Debrief — [relationship], [duration]

## First, What You're Carrying
[Named. If it routes out, say so plainly here and say the rest is secondary.]

## What Happened
[Factual account. No interpretation yet.]

## What Was Yours
| What happened | You controlled it | You influenced it | It was never yours |
|---|---|---|---|

## What You Did Well
- [..] — because [evidence]
- [..] — because [evidence]

## The Honest Mistake
[What you would actually do differently — distinguished from an outcome you disliked.]

**Not a mistake, though it may feel like one:** [..]

## The Other Reading
[If you've concluded it failed: the evidence against. If you've concluded it went well: what you might
not be seeing.]

## One Change for Next Season
**[the change]** — specific enough to act on.

## What This Debrief Can't Know
[Whether the season mattered. Formation is slow and mostly invisible; a season that felt like nothing
may have been the one that held. This is not consolation — it is a limit on what anyone can see from
here.]
```

## Verification

- [ ] What the mentor is carrying is addressed first, and routed if it belongs elsewhere.
- [ ] Events are sorted into controlled / influenced / never yours, with the mentee's choices and
      circumstances in the third column.
- [ ] What was done well is named with evidence, before anything they would change.
- [ ] A real mistake is distinguished from a disliked outcome.
- [ ] The counter-narrative runs in whichever direction the mentor has concluded.
- [ ] Exactly one change is carried forward; the season is not judged by the mentee's visible progress.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Let the mentee's progress stand as the measure. A mentor can do everything well and see nothing move,
  and the reverse happens too.
- Accept self-blame that the evidence does not support. Agreeing with a mentor's harsh reading because
  it sounds humble is not honesty.
- Offer reassurance that contradicts the facts. If a referral was missed, say so — a debrief that
  cannot name a real mistake is worthless for the next season.
- Use faithfulness-not-fruitfulness language to close the conversation early. It is true and it is
  often deployed to avoid an uncomfortable finding.
- Let the debrief become a performance review, or let it feed one. Mentors will stop being honest.
- Produce a list of improvements. One change is what will actually be carried.

✅ **DO:**
- Start with what the mentor is carrying, because an unnamed weight distorts every judgement that
  follows.
- Put the mentee's engagement, circumstances, and choices firmly in the "never yours" column and leave
  them there.
- Name consistency as an achievement. Showing up weekly for eight months when it felt pointless is the
  substance of most good mentoring, and it never feels like a finding to the person who did it.
- Run the counter-narrative in whichever direction the mentor has gone — a mentor who thinks it went
  well needs the harder question.
- Say plainly that whether it mattered is unknowable from here, and offer it as a limit rather than as
  comfort.
- Keep the debrief out of any assessment process, and say so, so the mentor can be honest in it.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** what the mentor is carrying is taken first and a
  factual reconstruction precedes interpretation, so the debrief is not shaped by their current mood
  about the season.
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework):** the controlled / influenced / never-yours sort is
  the analytic core, separating a mentor's actual practice from the mentee's choices and circumstances.
- **QA-04 (Uncertainty Acknowledgment):** the mandatory "what this debrief can't know" states that
  whether the season mattered is not visible from here — offered as an epistemic limit rather than as
  consolation.
- **QA-01 (Self-Verification):** the counter-narrative step runs against whichever conclusion the mentor
  arrived with, catching both unwarranted self-blame and unexamined satisfaction.
- **RP-02 (Audience-Specific Framing):** written to the mentor and explicitly excluded from any
  assessment process, which is the condition that makes an honest debrief possible.
