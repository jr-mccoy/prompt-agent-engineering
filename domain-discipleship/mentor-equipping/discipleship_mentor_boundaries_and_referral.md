# Mentor Boundaries and Referral

**Objective:** Build the standing framework a lay discipler works inside — what belongs to the role,
what always routes out and to whom, the words for handing off without abandoning, the confidentiality
limits declared *before* anyone discloses anything, and an honest account of the power a discipling
relationship creates whether or not either person intended it.

> **Boundary guardrail.** A lay mentor is not a counselor, a therapist, a pastor, a physician, a
> lawyer, a financial adviser, or an authority over anyone's life. Mental-health concerns, abuse,
> self-harm, suicidality, addiction, eating disorders, medical questions, legal questions, and
> financial distress all route out — to licensed professionals and, where there is danger, to
> appropriate authorities and emergency services
> `[VERIFY: identify the correct service from an official source]`. This prompt exists because the
> most common harm in discipleship is not malice; it is a well-meaning person staying in a
> conversation they should have handed on.

**When to use:** Before a mentor begins, during training, and whenever a mentor is unsure whether
something belongs to them. This is a standing framework, worked once and re-read often.

**When NOT to use:**
- Something has just been disclosed and you need to respond now — use
  `../session-and-lesson/discipleship_hard_conversation_navigation.md`, which applies this framework in
  the moment.
- You are writing the mutual agreement between mentor and mentee — use
  `../pairing-and-relationship/discipleship_relationship_covenant.md`.
- You are writing program-level policy — use
  `../program-operations/discipleship_safeguarding_and_conduct_policy.md`.
- You want the ministry-setting care-conversation foundations — use
  `domain-biblical-studies/ministry-contexts/biblical_ministry_care_conversation_foundations.md`.

**Audience:** Lay mentors and disciplers; program leads writing the framework their mentors work inside.

## Inputs / Context

**Required:**

1. **The mentor's role as defined.** What the program or church says this relationship is for.
2. **The mentor's actual background.** Any professional training — and specifically whether they hold a
   licence, because a licensed professional acting as a lay mentor has a dual-relationship problem the
   framework must address directly.
3. **Who the mentee is.** Adult or minor, and any known vulnerability. Minors and vulnerable adults
   change the framework substantially.
4. **The referral landscape available.** What actually exists locally and through the program: pastoral
   staff, counseling services, safeguarding lead, emergency services. Described, not named from memory.

**Optional:**

5. **Declared tradition (optional).** May shape confession practice, pastoral authority, elder
   involvement, and what is understood to be a spiritual rather than clinical matter — which affects
   where lines fall and who is consulted.
6. **Program reporting requirements.** Anything the mentor is required to pass on, and to whom.
7. **Known past incidents.** Situations that have arisen before in this context.

**If any required input is missing:** Ask clarifying questions before proceeding. If the referral
landscape is unknown, say plainly that the framework cannot be completed without it, and make mapping
it the first action — a referral framework with no destinations is not a framework.

---

## Constraints

### Must

- Produce **three explicit lists**: inside the role, outside the role, and the grey zone with a
  decision rule for each item in it.
- For every "outside the role" item, name **where it goes** — the kind of destination, marked
  `[VERIFY]` — and **what the mentor says**, in words they can actually use.
- State the **confidentiality limits before disclosure**: what a mentor will keep, what they must pass
  on, to whom, and that the mentee is told this at the start rather than after they have spoken.
- Address the **power dynamic** honestly: what a discipling relationship creates regardless of
  intention, and the specific behaviours that keep it from being misused.
- Include the **dual-relationship rule** where the mentor has professional training, and where mentor
  and mentee overlap socially, at work, or in family.
- Distinguish **routing out** from **abandoning** — the mentor stays present in the relationship while
  the specialist handles the specialist matter.
- Include the **escalate-when-unsure default**: when uncertain, consult the program lead or route out.
- Include what the mentor does when the mentee **asks them not to tell anyone** something that must be
  passed on.

### Must Not

- Encourage a mentor to handle a clinical, legal, medical, or financial matter because they feel
  capable, are the only one available, or have been asked to.
- Promise unconditional confidentiality, or model any language that implies it.
- State a legal obligation, mandated-reporter threshold, age of consent, or statutory duty. Mark every
  such point `[VERIFY]` and route to `domain-legal/` and qualified local counsel.
- Name a specific counselor, agency, hotline, or service.
- Invent professional-boundary research, counseling ethics codes, or statistics about harm in
  mentoring relationships.
- Quote Scripture text from memory, and do not use passages to justify a mentor exceeding their role.
- Frame routing out as a failure, a lack of faith, or an insufficiency of care.
- Suggest that prayer, Scripture, or discipleship substitutes for professional treatment.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)

- **Must:** note where traditions differ on confession, pastoral authority, the involvement of elders,
  anointing and prayer for healing, and what counts as a spiritual versus a clinical matter; let a
  declared tradition set its own internal escalation path while the professional referral obligations
  remain unchanged.
- **Must Not:** allow a tradition's framing — that a matter is spiritual rather than clinical — to
  override a referral for a safety, mental-health, or medical concern. The referral stands regardless.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Define the role positively

State what this relationship *is* for, in plain terms: walking alongside, asking questions, sharing
Scripture, praying together, encouraging practice, being consistent. A role defined only by its limits
produces an anxious mentor who says nothing.

### Step 2 — Build the outside-the-role list

Enumerate what always routes out. Cover at minimum: suicidality and self-harm; abuse, past or present;
domestic violence; mental-health conditions; addiction; eating disorders; medical questions; legal
questions; financial distress; marital crisis; grief beyond ordinary support; and anything involving a
minor's safety. For each, name the destination kind `[VERIFY]` and the handoff words.

### Step 3 — Work the grey zone

Identify what genuinely sits between — persistent low mood, a struggling marriage, a habitual sin the
mentee wants help with, conflict with a leader, doubt that has become distressing, money worries. For
each, give a decision rule: what to watch for, what tips it out, and who to consult when unsure.

### Step 4 — Set the confidentiality limits

Write what the mentor keeps, what they must pass on, to whom, and when. Then write the sentence the
mentor says **at the first meeting**, before anything has been disclosed. Then write what they say if a
mentee asks for a promise of secrecy in advance, and what they say when something has already been
disclosed that must be passed on.

### Step 5 — Address power and dual relationships

Name what the role creates: the mentee attributes authority the mentor was never given, discloses
asymmetrically, and may find it hard to disagree or leave. List the concrete behaviours that keep this
safe — meeting in visible places, not offering directive advice on life decisions, not creating
financial or employment entanglement, not becoming the mentee's only support, and making leaving easy.
Add the dual-relationship rules where the mentor is a professional, a colleague, a family member, or an
employer.

### Step 6 — Write the handoff scripts

For each routing category, write what the mentor actually says — warm, brief, non-alarming, and clear
that the relationship continues. A mentor who has never rehearsed these words will improvise badly at
the moment it matters.

### Step 7 — Set the default and verify

State the escalate-when-unsure default. Then check the framework: is every outside-the-role item given
a destination and words? Are confidentiality limits stated before disclosure? Is the power section
concrete rather than abstract?

## Output Format

Produce exactly this structure. Written to be re-read by a mentor under pressure.

```
# Your Role, and Its Edges

## What This Relationship Is For
[Positive definition, 3-5 lines.]

## Inside Your Role
- [..]

## Always Routes Out
| What | Where it goes | What you say |
|---|---|---|
| Thoughts of self-harm or suicide | [kind of service — VERIFY locally] | "[words]" |
| Abuse, past or present | [VERIFY] + safeguarding lead | "[words]" |
| [..] | [..] | "[..]" |

## The Grey Zone
| Situation | Stays with you while… | Tips out when… | Consult |
|---|---|---|---|

## Confidentiality — Said Before Anything Is Shared
**At the first meeting, you say:**
> "[the sentence, verbatim]"

- What you keep: [..]
- What you must pass on, and to whom: [..]
- If asked to promise secrecy in advance: "[words]"
- If it's already been said and must be passed on: "[words]"

**[VERIFY]** Any legal reporting duty here is jurisdiction-specific. Confirm with the program's

what the law requires.

## The Power You Didn't Ask For
[What the role creates regardless of intention.]

**Behaviours that keep it safe:**
- [..]

**Dual relationships:** [rules where mentor is a professional, colleague, relative, or employer]

| Situation | What you say | What you do next | How the relationship continues |
|---|---|---|---|

**Default: consult or route out.** Uncertainty is the signal, not a reason to wait.
Who you consult: [role, not name]

## Framework Check
- [ ] Every "routes out" item has a destination and words
- [ ] Confidentiality limits are said before disclosure, not after
- [ ] Nothing here states a legal requirement
```

## Verification

- [ ] The role is defined positively before any limit is stated.
- [ ] Every outside-the-role item has both a destination kind (`[VERIFY]`) and verbatim handoff words.
- [ ] Confidentiality limits are placed at the first meeting, before disclosure, with a verbatim sentence.
- [ ] No legal obligation, reporting threshold, or statutory duty is stated — all marked `[VERIFY]` and
      routed to `domain-legal/`.
- [ ] The power section names concrete behaviours, not abstractions.
- [ ] Routing out is framed as care, never as failure or insufficient faith.

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Let a mentor stay in a clinical conversation because no professional is available. Scarcity is not
  competence, and an unavailable specialist does not make the mentor one.
- Write "I'll keep whatever you say between us." It is the single most damaging sentence in lay
  ministry, and it is usually said with the best intentions.
- State what the law requires about reporting. It varies by country, state, role, and the mentee's age,
  and getting it wrong in either direction causes serious harm.
- Treat the power dynamic as theoretical. The mentee often cannot disagree freely, and a mentor who
  does not believe this will not guard against it.
- Frame referral as giving up. A mentor who believes routing out is failure will delay it.
- Allow a tradition's "this is spiritual, not clinical" framing to cancel a safety referral.

✅ **DO:**
- Define the role positively first, so the mentor knows what to *do*, not only what to avoid.
- Write the confidentiality sentence verbatim and require it at the first meeting. Retroactive limits
  are a betrayal even when they are correct.
- Rehearse the handoff words. The gap between knowing to refer and being able to say it out loud is
  where most delays happen.
- Make "the relationship continues" explicit in every handoff script, so the mentee does not experience
  referral as rejection.
- Set escalate-when-unsure as the default and say uncertainty is itself the signal.
- Flag every legal point `[VERIFY]` and route it to counsel and the safeguarding lead, without
  exception.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-01 (Clear Objective Statement):** the objective names all five deliverables — scope lists,
  handoff words, pre-disclosure confidentiality, power analysis — so the framework cannot be satisfied
  by a list of prohibitions alone.
- **CM-02 (Constraint Specification):** the Must Not block encodes the domain's central safety
  constraints: no unconditional confidentiality, no stated legal duty, no named service, and no
  tradition-framing override of a safety referral.
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework):** the inside/outside/grey-zone split crossed with
  destination, decision rule, and script analyses the role along axes that a simple "know your limits"
  instruction leaves undefined exactly where mentors need them.
- **OC-03 (Markdown Table Specification):** routing and handoff tables put destination and words on the
  same row, so no category can be listed without an actionable response attached.
- **QA-01 (Self-Verification):** the framework check verifies every routed item has words and a
  destination, and that confidentiality limits precede disclosure, before the framework is handed to a
  mentor.
