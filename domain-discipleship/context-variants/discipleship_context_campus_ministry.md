# Campus Ministry Program Variant

**Objective:** Design a campus discipleship program that runs inside the institution's actual rules
rather than alongside them — recognition, access, publicity, and non-discrimination policy — while
protecting students whose immigration status or academic standing could be affected by participation,
handling the power gap where staff or postgraduates disciple students, and rebuilding leadership every
year against turnover that removes most of it.

> **Boundary guardrail.** This designs a program, not institutional compliance advice or legal
> guidance. Recognition requirements, non-discrimination policy, publicity and access rules, data
> protection, and anything with legal weight are confirmed with the institution and, where necessary,
> qualified counsel `[VERIFY]` — this prompt states none of them. Immigration and visa questions route
> to the institution's international office and qualified immigration advice; a wrong answer can cost a
> student their place in the country. Mental-health concerns, sexual assault, and crisis route to
> professional services and the institution's own channels `[VERIFY: identify the correct service from
> an official source]`.

**When to use:** You are standing up or rebuilding a discipleship program on a campus, where the
institution governs access and the population turns over annually.

**When NOT to use:**
- You need the formation content for this age group — use
  `../life-stage-tracks/discipleship_track_college_and_young_adult.md`, which this variant carries.
- You need the generic program design — use
  `../program-operations/discipleship_program_design_blueprint.md`, which this extends.
- Participants are under 18 — the safeguarding policy and the youth track's structural requirements
  apply in full, and this variant does not replace them.
- The question is institutional compliance. That belongs to the institution and to counsel.

**Audience:** Campus workers, chaplaincy staff, and student leaders designing or rebuilding the program.

---

## Inputs / Context

**Required:**

1. **The institution's rules as the institution states them.** Recognition or registration status,
   room booking and access, publicity, external speakers, funding, non-discrimination requirements
   including any that apply to leadership selection, and data handling. Supplied by the institution —
   never assumed.
2. **The population.** Undergraduate, postgraduate, international proportion, commuter versus resident,
   mature students, apprentices, and whether any participants are under 18.
3. **The annual clock.** Term dates, the recruitment window, exam periods, placement years, and the
   month when leadership turns over.
4. **Who disciples whom.** Whether staff, postgraduates, or chaplaincy disciple students — and any
   academic supervisory, assessment, or employment relationship that could exist between them.
5. **The referral landscape.** Institutional services and external professional help, described rather
   than named from memory.

**Optional:**

6. **Declared tradition (optional).** May foreground a denominational chaplaincy, a fellowship's
   doctrinal basis, sacramental provision, or specific membership practice. Where a doctrinal basis
   interacts with institutional non-discrimination rules, that interaction is a `[VERIFY]` matter for
   the institution and counsel, not a question this prompt answers.
7. **Relationship to local congregations.** Whether the program feeds, complements, or competes with
   them.
8. **Prior institutional history.** Any past dispute, restriction, or condition attached to the group.

**If any required input is missing:** Ask before proceeding. If item 1 cannot be supplied, say so
plainly — a campus program designed against assumed rules risks losing recognition, which costs the
students their community rather than costing the designer anything.

---

## Constraints

### Must

- Restate the **institution's rules as the institution states them**, with source and date, and design
  inside them.
- Protect the **international student** specifically: no activity that could affect visa conditions, no
  assumption about what is safe for a student to be publicly associated with, and routing of every
  immigration question to qualified advice.
- Handle the **power gap**: where a discipler has or may acquire academic supervisory, assessment,
  employment, or reference-writing authority over a disciple, that pairing is either prohibited or
  requires a declared and supervised arrangement.
- Make participation **visibly voluntary**, with no attendance recorded in any way that could reach an
  academic file, and no pressure applied through friendship groups or leadership roles.
- Design **annual rebuild** into the program: leadership succession, institutional memory, and the
  handover that must happen every single year.
- Design for **the turnover cliff** at graduation: what a leaving student takes, and how they find
  community where they go, routed through the college track's handoff.
- Design **entry that works in week one and in week nine**, since a large share of students arrive at
  the second point and find a closed group.
- State **what the program does with student data** — sign-ups, contact lists, prayer requests —
  under the institution's rules and applicable data requirements `[VERIFY]`.
- Route mental health, assault, financial hardship, and academic jeopardy **out** `[VERIFY]`.

### Must Not

- Quote Scripture text from memory or assert verse wording.
- State institutional policy, non-discrimination law, data-protection requirements, or visa conditions
  as fact. Mark `[VERIFY]` and route to the institution and counsel.
- Design activity that could jeopardize a student's visa, academic standing, funding, or accommodation.
- Pair a discipler with a student over whom they hold or may hold academic or employment authority
  without a declared, supervised arrangement.
- Record or share attendance, participation, or spiritual status in any form that could reach an
  academic, employment, or immigration record.
- Use social belonging as leverage for attendance, agreement, or a decision.
- Target, pressure, or single out students by nationality, religion of origin, or perceived
  receptiveness, or make generalizations about any such group.
- Invent campus-ministry research, student faith statistics, or retention data.
- Give clinical, financial, immigration, or legal guidance. All route out.
- Name a specific institution, organization, chaplaincy body, or service.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)

- **Must:** note where traditions differ on the standing of a campus fellowship relative to a local
  congregation, on sacramental provision away from home, on membership at this stage, and on
  ecumenical cooperation between groups on the same campus. Attribute positions to identifiable
  streams; let a declared tradition foreground its own.
- **Must Not:** present one stream's model of campus ministry as the biblical one, or design the program
  so that students are implicitly drawn away from their home tradition without that being visible.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Write the institution's rules down first

Restate them with source and date, including recognition status, access, publicity, external speakers,
funding, non-discrimination, and data. Add the standing principle: where this design and the institution
differ, the institution governs, and questions go to the institution rather than being resolved locally.

### Step 2 — Map the risk to students, not to the program

Work through who could be harmed by participation and how: the international student, the student whose
family does not know, the student on academic probation, the under-18 student, the student whose
funding carries conditions. For each, what the program changes so participation costs them nothing.

### Step 3 — Fix the power rules

Enumerate every relationship in which a discipler could hold authority over a disciple — supervision,
marking, employment, references, accommodation — and state the rule for each: prohibited, or declared
and supervised. Include what happens when the relationship arises mid-year, which it often does.

### Step 4 — Design the annual clock

Lay the program against term dates: the recruitment window, the term rhythm, exam blackouts, placement
years, and the leadership handover month. State what is designed to survive the handover and what is
rebuilt each year.

### Step 5 — Build the leadership pipeline against turnover

Design succession as a standing process, not an annual scramble: how student leaders are identified,
equipped, and released; what institutional memory is written down rather than carried in a person; and
who holds continuity when a whole leadership cohort graduates. Route capacity design to
`../program-operations/discipleship_mentor_pipeline_and_capacity.md`.

### Step 6 — Design entry that stays open

Specify how a student joins in week nine, or in the second year, or after a term away — and how the peer
structure absorbs them. A campus program that is only enterable in freshers' week has closed itself for
forty-eight weeks of the year.

### Step 7 — Fix data and privacy

State what is collected, why, who sees it, how long it is kept, and what is never recorded — with the
requirement that everything is checked against the institution's rules and applicable data law
`[VERIFY]`. Prayer requests are personal data; treat them as such.

### Step 8 — Verify and stress-test

Confirm the design against the institution's stated rules. Then check the standing failure modes: the
program assumed rules it never read; an international student was exposed; a supervisory pairing went
undeclared; the group was only enterable in week one; leadership handover lost everything.

## Verification

- [ ] The institution's rules are restated with source and date and govern the design.
- [ ] Every category of student who could be harmed by participation has a stated mitigation.
- [ ] Every authority relationship has a rule, including what happens when it arises mid-year.
- [ ] No attendance or spiritual-status record can reach an academic, employment, or immigration file.
- [ ] Entry works at four points in the year, not one.
- [ ] No institutional policy, data requirement, or visa condition is stated as fact.

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Design against assumed institutional rules. Losing recognition costs the students their community, and
  they had no part in the decision that lost it.
- Treat visa risk as a hypothetical. For some students, association with a religious group carries
  consequences at home or on re-entry, and the program cannot assess that for them.
- Let a supervisor disciple their own research student without declaring it. It compromises both
  relationships and the student cannot decline freely.
- Record attendance in anything that could be read as a file. Even an informal spreadsheet becomes a
  record the moment anyone asks for it.
- Target students by nationality or religion of origin. It is a serious wrong in itself and it also
  reliably ends institutional recognition.
- Build the whole program around freshers' week. The student who arrives in November finds a closed
  social group and does not come back.

✅ **DO:**
- Restate the rules in the program document, sourced and dated, with the governing principle above them.
- Design the mitigations per student category, so protection is structural rather than pastoral
  improvisation.
- Publish the power rules to both disciplers and students, so a student knows what to expect and can
  raise it.
- Write institutional memory down. A leadership cohort that graduates takes everything undocumented
  with it.
- Design four entry points across the year and staff them.
- Treat prayer requests as personal data, because that is what they are.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-01 (Clear Objective Statement):** the objective names the four forces this variant exists to
  handle — institutional rules, student risk, the power gap, and annual turnover.
- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** the institution's rules and the risk-to-students map
  precede all program design, so protection is never retrofitted onto an existing structure.
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework):** rules, risk, power, clock, and entry are analysed
  separately, which is what the generic blueprint cannot do for a governed setting with annual churn.
- **CM-02 (Constraint Specification):** the Must Not block encodes the load-bearing prohibitions — no
  visa jeopardy, no undeclared authority pairings, no records that could reach a file, no targeting.
- **QA-01 (Self-Verification):** the design is verified against the institution's own stated rules before
  it runs, with the power rules and data practice separately signed off.
