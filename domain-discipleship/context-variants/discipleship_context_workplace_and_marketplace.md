# Workplace and Marketplace Program Variant

**Objective:** Design a discipleship group that meets in or around a workplace without creating the
harms that setting makes easy — participation that is voluntary in name and expected in practice,
managers discipling their own reports, colleagues who decline becoming visibly outside something, and a
group whose existence quietly becomes a channel for advancement.

> **Boundary guardrail.** This designs a voluntary group, not an employer program, an HR initiative, or
> anything that speaks for an organization. Employment policy, non-solicitation rules, religious
> accommodation and discrimination law, use of employer facilities and systems, and confidentiality
> obligations are all `[VERIFY]` matters for the employer and qualified counsel — this prompt states
> none of them. Workplace grievances, harassment, and discrimination route to the employer's own
> processes and to qualified advice `[VERIFY: identify the correct service from an official source]`,
> never to the group. A discipling relationship is never a channel for anything that belongs in a
> performance conversation.

**When to use:** You are starting or restructuring a discipleship or formation group among colleagues,
in a workplace, a professional network, or a marketplace setting.

**When NOT to use:**
- You want the formation content about work itself — use
  `../topical-modules/discipleship_module_work_and_vocation.md`.
- The group is a church small group that happens to include colleagues — use
  `domain-biblical-studies/church-staff-ministry-ops/biblical_churchstaff_small_group_launch_system.md`.
- You are being asked to run something on the employer's behalf. That is an employer program, with
  obligations this design does not cover.
- Someone has a workplace grievance. That is the employer's process and qualified advice, immediately.

**Audience:** Employees and professionals convening a group among colleagues, and program leads
supporting workplace groups from a church or ministry.

---

## Inputs / Context

**Required:**

1. **The employer's applicable policy, as the employer states it.** Use of facilities, meeting rooms,
   internal communications, distribution lists, solicitation, and any policy on religious or affinity
   groups. Supplied by the employer's own documents — never assumed.
2. **Who is in the room, and the reporting lines between them.** Every manager–report, mentor–mentee,
   partner–associate, client–supplier, and interview-panel relationship present or possible.
3. **The visibility of the group.** Whether colleagues who do not attend will know it exists, where it
   meets, and whether attendance is observable.
4. **The employment context.** Whether anyone present is on probation, on a visa tied to employment,
   contracted rather than employed, or otherwise in a position where declining feels costly.

**Optional:**

5. **Declared tradition (optional).** May foreground particular practices — prayer forms, Scripture
   reading patterns, sacramental participation. Contested specifics stay attributed, and no practice
   becomes a participation requirement.
6. **Sector constraints.** Regulated professions, client confidentiality, security clearance, or
   competitor sensitivities.
7. **Existing affinity-group framework.** Where the employer already recognizes such groups and has
   rules for them.

**If any required input is missing:** Ask before proceeding. Item 2 in particular — a group that
discovers halfway through the year that one member manages another has already done the damage.

---

## Constraints

### Must

- Restate the **employer's applicable policy as the employer states it**, with source and date, and
  design inside it.
- **Prohibit a manager from discipling their own report**, and state the same rule for any relationship
  where one person influences the other's pay, progression, assignments, references, or continued
  employment.
- Make participation **demonstrably voluntary**: no invitation from a manager to a report, no attendance
  visible on any work system, no group membership referenced in any work context, and a way to stop
  attending that nobody remarks on.
- Protect **colleagues who do not participate** as carefully as those who do: nothing decided in the
  group affects work, no work information circulates through it, and no in-group shorthand carries into
  meetings.
- Keep the group **off employer systems** unless the employer's policy explicitly permits it, and keep
  no membership list on a work account.
- State plainly that the group **does not speak for the employer** and does not represent the employer's
  position.
- Design **entry and exit that cost nothing socially or professionally**, since a workplace group's
  worst feature is that leaving is observable.
- Keep **work confidentiality intact**: client, patient, commercial, and personnel information does not
  enter prayer requests or discussion, and the group states this rule out loud.
- Route grievance, harassment, discrimination, and any employment matter **out**, immediately and
  entirely `[VERIFY]`.

### Must Not

- Quote Scripture text from memory or assert verse wording.
- State employment policy, non-solicitation rules, accommodation or discrimination law, or any legal
  obligation. Mark `[VERIFY]` and route to counsel and the employer.
- Allow any pairing across a reporting or influence line, however willing both parties are.
- Invite, recruit, or apply pressure in a way that a subordinate could not freely decline — including
  invitations that arrive through a manager, a team channel, or a work meeting.
- Use employer time, systems, rooms, or distribution lists outside the employer's stated policy.
- Let group participation influence, or appear to influence, hiring, assignment, progression, or
  reference-writing.
- Handle a workplace grievance, mediate a workplace conflict, or advise on an employment matter.
- Bring confidential work information into discussion or prayer, including anonymized-but-identifiable
  details.
- Invent workplace-ministry research, statistics on faith at work, or claims about outcomes.
- Name a specific employer, service, or organization.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)

- **Must:** note where traditions differ on witness at work, on proselytism and its limits, on
  cooperation across traditions in a mixed group, and on the standing of a workplace group relative to a
  local congregation. Attribute positions to identifiable streams; let a declared tradition foreground
  its own practice while the group's mixed composition is respected.
- **Must Not:** design the group around one tradition's practice in a way that makes participants of
  other traditions guests in it, or present workplace witness obligations as settled where they are not.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Read the employer's policy and write it down

Restate the applicable policy with source and date. Add the governing principle: where this design and
the employer's policy differ, the policy governs, and the group asks rather than assumes. State that the
group does not speak for the employer.

### Step 2 — Map every influence line in the room

List each person and every relationship in which one influences another's work outcomes — direct
management, matrixed reporting, assignment control, references, client relationships, and interview
panels. Then apply the rule: no discipling pairing across any of them, and a plan for what happens when
a line appears mid-year, which it will.

### Step 3 — Make voluntariness structural

Design invitation, attendance, and departure so that none of them can be observed or inferred by anyone
with authority. Specify who may invite whom, where the group meets, what is never on a work calendar or
channel, and how someone stops attending without a conversation.

### Step 4 — Protect the non-participants

Write the rules that keep the group from becoming an in-group: no work decisions made or previewed in
it, no work information circulating through it, no shared shorthand in meetings, and no social exclusion
of colleagues who are not in it. Then state how the group would notice if this were happening.

### Step 5 — Set the confidentiality rules in both directions

Two rules, stated together: what colleagues share in the group stays there, subject to the standing
limits; and what belongs to the employer, clients, or patients never enters the group at all — including
in prayer. Give the words for redirecting a request that would breach the second.

### Step 6 — Design the content for the setting

Lay out the sessions inside the actual constraint: short, interruptible, and often at lunch. For each:
the question, Scripture by address, the practice — plus *what this session must not ask a colleague to
disclose*, since a colleague's disclosure carries professional consequence a stranger's does not.

### Step 7 — Fix the routing

Table what leaves: grievance, harassment, discrimination, bullying, a conflict between two members, a
concern about someone's conduct, and any matter that belongs in a performance conversation. Destination
`[VERIFY]`, and the words. The group is never the venue for any of these.

### Step 8 — Verify and stress-test

Check the design against the employer's stated policy. Then the standing failure modes: an influence
line went unnoticed; an invitation arrived downward; the group became a networking channel;
confidentiality leaked in a prayer request; declining became visible.

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Let a manager disciple their own report, however good the relationship. The report cannot decline, the
  manager cannot un-know what they hear, and both of those persist into every performance conversation.
- Invite downward. An invitation from someone with authority is not an invitation; it is an
  instruction with a friendly tone.
- Put the group on a work calendar, channel, or distribution list without explicit policy permission.
  Once it is on a system it is a record, and the people who did not attend are on the same record by
  omission.
- Let the group become the place where things are known first. That is how a voluntary group turns into
  a career advantage and how colleagues outside it learn they are outside it.
- Bring a client, patient, or personnel matter into a prayer request. Anonymizing does not work in a
  workplace where everyone can identify the case.
- Mediate a conflict between two members. It puts the group inside an employment matter, which harms
  everyone including the group.

✅ **DO:**
- Read the actual policy and restate it in the design, sourced and dated.
- Map every influence line before the group forms, and plan for the ones that appear later.
- Design departure to be unremarkable, so that leaving carries no professional cost.
- Write the non-participant protections explicitly — they are the group's obligation to people who never
  agreed to anything.
- State both confidentiality rules out loud at the start and rehearse the redirect words.
- Route everything employment-shaped out immediately, and say plainly that the group is not the venue.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-01 (Clear Objective Statement):** the objective names the four harms this setting makes easy —
  coerced participation, authority pairings, in-group formation, and career leakage — as the design's
  subject.
- **CM-02 (Constraint Specification):** the Must Not block encodes the absolutes: no pairing across
  influence lines, no downward invitation, no employer systems outside policy, no confidential
  information in the room.
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework):** policy, influence lines, voluntariness, and
  non-participant impact are worked separately, so a group that is fine on three axes cannot pass while
  failing the fourth.
- **DS-01 (Framework Application):** the domain's pairing-criteria and program machinery are applied and
  then constrained by an employer's policy that overrides them wherever they conflict.
- **QA-01 (Self-Verification):** influence-line mapping, policy check, and the confidentiality statement
  are each verified before the group runs, with signals defined for noticing later drift.
