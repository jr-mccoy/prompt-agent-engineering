# Relationship Covenant

**Objective:** Produce the written agreement two people make at the start of a discipling relationship —
what it is for, how often and for how long, what each brings, how disagreement and drift are handled,
how it ends — with the limits of confidentiality stated plainly and early, before anything has been
disclosed under an assumption that turns out to be false.

> **Boundary guardrail.** A covenant is a mutual working agreement between two people, not a contract,
> a vow, a membership commitment, or anything with legal or spiritual binding force. It must never be
> used to hold someone in a relationship they want to leave. This prompt does not state legal
> obligations about confidentiality, reporting, or minors — those are `[VERIFY]` with the safeguarding
> lead and qualified local counsel, via `domain-legal/`.

**When to use:** At or just after the first meeting of a newly paired mentor and mentee.

**When NOT to use:**
- You need to plan the first conversation itself — use `discipleship_first_meeting_guide.md`.
- You are setting the mentor's standing scope framework — use
  `../mentor-equipping/discipleship_mentor_boundaries_and_referral.md`, which this covenant expresses to
  the mentee.
- You are writing program policy — use
  `../program-operations/discipleship_safeguarding_and_conduct_policy.md`.
- You only need to settle meeting rhythm — use `discipleship_cadence_and_rhythm_design.md`.

**Audience:** The mentor and mentee together; a program lead producing the template both will use.

---

## Inputs / Context

**Required:**

1. **What this relationship is for.** In both people's words, not only the program's.
2. **The program's confidentiality and reporting rules.** What the mentor must pass on, to whom, and
   in what circumstances. Supplied, not assumed.
3. **Practicalities.** Proposed frequency, duration of the season, meeting place and channel, and who
   initiates.
4. **Who the mentee is.** Adult, or a minor or vulnerable adult — which changes the covenant
   substantially and adds requirements this prompt will not state from memory.

**Optional:**

5. **Declared tradition (optional).** May add elements — accountability expectations, confession
   practice, submission language, elder oversight — which are named as that stream's practice and are
   never assumed.
6. **The curriculum or pathway.** If the pair is following one.
7. **Anything either party wants written in.** Including anything they want explicitly excluded.

**If any required input is missing:** Ask clarifying questions before proceeding. If the program's
confidentiality and reporting rules are unavailable, stop — a covenant that guesses at them will mislead
the mentee at precisely the point where being misled does the most damage.

---

## Constraints

### Must

- State the **limits of confidentiality plainly, early, and in the mentee's language** — before any
  section that invites disclosure.
- Be **mutual**: both parties commit, both may raise problems, both may end it. A one-sided document is
  a set of rules, not a covenant.
- Include **how to end it**, with no justification required, written into the document itself.
- Include **how disagreement is raised**, including disagreement with the mentor, and what happens if
  the mentee is uncomfortable saying it directly.
- State the **scope**: what this relationship covers and what it explicitly does not, naming where
  other things route.
- Be **short enough to be read** — one page where possible. A covenant nobody reads protects nobody.
- Set a **review point** at which either party may change or end it without it being a rupture.
- Be written in **plain language**, avoiding legal, clinical, or institutional register.

### Must Not

- Promise unconditional confidentiality, or use any wording that implies it.
- Include vows, oaths, submission clauses, or spiritual-authority language that makes leaving feel like
  a breach of faith.
- State a legal duty, reporting threshold, or age-related requirement — `[VERIFY]` and route.
- Include penalties, consequences, or any requirement to justify ending.
- Require the mentee to disclose anything, attend anything, or reach any standard.
- Invent covenant templates attributed to a named ministry, or cite research on mentoring agreements.
- Quote Scripture text from memory, and do not use covenant language from Scripture to add binding
  weight.
- Run to multiple pages of clauses. Length here is a failure mode, not thoroughness.
- Create financial, employment, or dependency entanglement of any kind.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)

- **Must:** where a tradition includes specific elements — formal accountability, confession,
  spiritual direction conventions, oversight by an elder — name them as that stream's practice, include
  them only if both parties hold them, and note others do not.
- **Must Not:** import submission, accountability, or authority language from a tradition either party
  does not hold, or present any tradition's covenant form as the standard.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Write the purpose in both voices

State what the relationship is for, drawing on how each person described it. If they described it
differently, surface that now — a divergence found in week one is a conversation; found in month four
it is a mismatch.

### Step 2 — Write the confidentiality section first

Before anything else in the document. State: what the mentor keeps, what they must pass on, to whom,
and in what circumstances. Write it in the mentee's language, not policy language. Add the sentence the
mentor says aloud when handing it over. Mark every legal point `[VERIFY]`.

### Step 3 — Set scope and its edges

State what this relationship covers, and what it does not — counseling, crisis support, financial help,
career or life decisions, marriage counseling, medical or legal questions. For each excluded item, say
where it goes instead, so the exclusion reads as routing rather than refusal.

### Step 4 — Fix the practicalities

Frequency, duration, place, channel, who initiates, what happens when someone cannot make it, and how
much notice is expected. Keep it realistic — a covenant that specifies weekly meetings between people
who can manage fortnightly starts the relationship in deficit.

### Step 5 — Write the disagreement and discomfort clauses

How either raises a problem, including with each other. Add a route for the mentee who cannot say it
directly: a named third person in the program they can go to instead, with no consequence for using it.

### Step 6 — Write the ending

How either ends it, that no justification is required, what notice is courteous, and what happens next
for the mentee. Put this in the covenant rather than leaving it to be improvised.

### Step 7 — Set the review and cut it down

Fix a review point. Then cut the document to one page, keeping confidentiality, scope, ending, and
disagreement intact.

## Output Format

Produce the covenant itself, ready to be read and signed by both people.

```
# Our Agreement — [mentor] and [mentee], from [date]

## What this is for
[Both voices. One short paragraph.]

## What I'll keep private — and what I can't
[Mentor's voice, plain language, before anything else.]

- What stays between us: [..]
- What I would have to pass on, and to whom: [..]
- When that would happen: [..]

I'm telling you this now, before you've told me anything, because you should know it in advance.

**[VERIFY]** Reporting duties vary by place and by your age and circumstances. The program's
safeguarding lead confirms these — this document does not state what the law requires.

## What we'll do together
[..]

## What this isn't — and where those things go instead
| Not this | Where it goes |
|---|---|
| Counseling or therapy | [kind of service — VERIFY locally] |
| A crisis line | [VERIFY] |
| Financial or legal help | [..] |

## How we'll meet
- How often: [..] | For how long: [..] | Where / how: [..]
- Who gets in touch: [..]
- If one of us can't make it: [..]

## If something isn't working
- Either of us says so, directly: [..]
- If you'd rather not say it to me, you can go to [role] instead. Using that costs you nothing.

## How this ends
Either of us can end this at any time. **No reason is needed.** [Notice that's courteous.]
If it ends, [what happens next for the mentee].

## We'll check in on this
On [date] we'll look at whether this is working and change it, continue it, or finish it.

Signed: ______________  ______________
```

## Verification

- [ ] Confidentiality limits appear before any section inviting disclosure, in plain language.
- [ ] Nothing promises unconditional confidentiality.
- [ ] Ending requires no justification, and this is written in the document.
- [ ] A route exists for the mentee to raise a problem with someone other than the mentor.
- [ ] No vows, submission language, penalties, or spiritual-authority framing appear.
- [ ] No legal duty is stated; all such points are `[VERIFY]`. Document fits on roughly one page.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Bury confidentiality limits at the end, or in an annex. Placement is the whole point — a limit
  disclosed after a disclosure is a betrayal even when it was always the policy.
- Write it in policy or legal register. A mentee who does not understand the document has not been told
  anything.
- Use covenant language from Scripture to add gravity. It quietly converts a working agreement into
  something that feels binding before God, which makes leaving feel like sin.
- Include a commitment to be honest, teachable, or open. A required disposition is not a commitment,
  and it gives the mentor leverage.
- Let it grow past a page. Every added clause reduces the chance the sections that matter are read.
- Specify a frequency neither person can sustain, because it sounds more serious.

✅ **DO:**
- Put the confidentiality section immediately after the purpose, and have the mentor say it aloud when
  handing the document over.
- Write the exclusions as routing, with destinations, so the mentee hears "that goes here" rather than
  "I won't help with that."
- Name a specific third person the mentee can go to, since the power difference makes raising a problem
  directly hard for many people.
- Put "no reason is needed" in the ending section in those words. It is the sentence that makes the
  exit real.
- Set a review date early enough that changing the agreement is normal.
- Cut the document to a page at the end, and cut clauses rather than the four load-bearing sections.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** the confidentiality section is written second, before
  scope or practicalities, so the document's structure enforces disclosure-before-disclosure rather
  than relying on the author's diligence.
- **CM-02 (Constraint Specification):** the Must Not block bans unconditional confidentiality, vows and
  submission language, penalties, and stated legal duties — the four ways discipleship agreements
  become instruments of pressure.
- **OC-03 (Markdown Table Specification):** the "what this isn't" table pairs every exclusion with a
  destination, so scope limits read as routing rather than refusal.
- **RP-02 (Audience-Specific Framing):** the covenant is written in the mentee's plain language rather
  than institutional register, since a document that is not understood conveys nothing regardless of
  its content.
- **QA-01 (Self-Verification):** the verification block checks placement of the confidentiality section,
  the no-justification exit, and the third-person route before the covenant is used.
