# Ending or Transitioning a Discipling Relationship

**Objective:** Bring a discipling relationship to a close in a way that fits why it is ending —
completion, mismatch, handoff, withdrawal, or harm — with words for each, a handover that does not
leave the mentee stranded, and no requirement that either person justify their decision.

> **Boundary guardrail.** One ending category is not a relational matter to be handled well: where the
> relationship involved harm, boundary violation, coercion, or misconduct, it does not get a warm
> closing conversation. It routes immediately to the safeguarding lead and, where there is danger, to
> appropriate authorities `[VERIFY: identify the correct service from an official source]`. Nobody is
> asked to meet, explain, forgive, or reconcile. That path is separate and it overrides everything else
> in this prompt.

**When to use:** A season is finishing, a pairing is not working, a mentor is stepping back, or a
mentee has gone quiet and someone needs to close it properly.

**When NOT to use:**
- The rhythm is wrong but the relationship should continue — use
  `discipleship_cadence_and_rhythm_design.md`.
- The ending is done and the mentor wants to reflect — use
  `../mentor-equipping/discipleship_mentor_season_debrief.md`.
- The mentee is transitioning into discipling someone else — use
  `../curriculum-architecture/discipleship_multiplication_design.md` alongside this.
- You need general difficult-conversation preparation — use
  `domain-negotiation/difficult-conversations/difficultconvo_pre_brief.md`.

**Audience:** The mentor, the mentee, or the program lead handling the close.

---

## Inputs / Context

**Required:**

1. **Why it is ending.** As honestly as can be stated, including if the reason is unclear or if one
   party simply stopped responding.
2. **Who is ending it.** Mentor, mentee, program, or circumstance.
3. **Where the mentee is.** What they were working on, and whether they want to continue with someone
   else.
4. **What was agreed.** The covenant's ending terms, if one exists.

**Optional:**

5. **Declared tradition (optional).** May include a formal close — a blessing, a commissioning, prayer
   together, a marking of completion — offered where both hold it.
6. **What is unresolved.** Any unfinished conversation, tension, or disappointment either carries.
7. **Whether they want ongoing contact.** And whether both want the same thing, which they often do not.

**If any required input is missing:** Ask clarifying questions before proceeding — but do not require
the ending party to produce a reason. "I'd rather not say" is a complete answer and the close proceeds
without it.

---

## Constraints

### Must

- **Classify the ending** into one of five types, because each needs a materially different close:
  completion, mismatch, handoff, withdrawal, or harm.
- Route **harm out immediately** and stop. No closing conversation, no explanation asked, no
  reconciliation proposed.
- Require **no justification** from whoever is ending it, and say so in the words used.
- Provide a **handover for the mentee** that does not strand them: who they can go to, what continues,
  and what they were working on, in their hands.
- Give **actual words** for the closing conversation, matched to the ending type.
- Name what each person can honestly **thank the other for**, without manufacturing warmth that is not
  there.
- Handle the **silent ending** — a mentee who has stopped responding — including how many attempts,
  what the final message says, and how it leaves the door open without obligation.
- Be clear about **future contact**: what each wants, and that a difference is normal and not a
  rejection.

### Must Not

- Ask either party to explain, justify, or account for the decision to end.
- Propose reconciliation, mediation, or a restorative conversation where harm occurred.
- Frame ending as failure, as giving up, or as a spiritual problem in either person.
- Pressure a mentee into continuing with a different mentor when they want to stop entirely.
- Chase a silent mentee past the stated attempts, or send a final message that induces guilt.
- Quote Scripture text from memory, and do not use passages about perseverance, covenant, or
  faithfulness to make leaving feel wrong.
- Invent research on mentoring relationship duration or attrition.
- Name a specific service where routing is needed.
- Create an obligation to stay in touch.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)

- **Must:** where a tradition marks a completion formally — blessing, prayer, commissioning, a note to
  the congregation — offer it as that stream's practice, use it only if both parties hold it, and note
  others close informally.
- **Must Not:** import a formal ritual into an ending where either party does not want it, or imply
  that an unmarked ending is incomplete.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Screen for harm

Check whether the ending involves harm, boundary violation, coercion, misconduct, or fear. If so, stop
here: route to the safeguarding lead `[VERIFY]`, state that no closing meeting will be arranged, and
that nobody is being asked to explain or forgive. Do not continue to Step 2 for this category.

### Step 2 — Classify the ending

| Type | What it is | What the close needs |
|---|---|---|
| **Completion** | The season finished as intended | Marking it, naming what happened, releasing well |
| **Mismatch** | Fit was wrong; nobody did anything wrong | Saying so plainly, re-matching without blame |
| **Handoff** | The mentee moves to someone better placed | Continuity, warm introduction, information transfer with consent |
| **Withdrawal** | One party is stopping — capacity, circumstance, or choice | No justification, clean handover, door left open |
| **Harm** | — | **Routed at Step 1. Does not proceed here.** |

### Step 3 — Write the conversation

For the classified type, write what is actually said: an opening that names the ending without
preamble, the reason at whatever level of detail the ending party chooses, and a close. Keep it short —
a long ending conversation usually means someone is being talked out of it.

### Step 4 — Handle the silent ending if that is what this is

Specify how many attempts, spaced how far apart, and the wording of the final message: warm, brief,
free of guilt, stating the door is open and that no reply is needed. Then stop, and record it as closed.

### Step 5 — Build the handover

Whatever the mentee was working on goes to them in usable form. Name who they can go to next, whether
they want to continue or not, and confirm that stopping entirely is a legitimate choice.

### Step 6 — Name the honest thanks

Identify what each can genuinely thank the other for. If the relationship was difficult, keep it small
and true rather than generous and false — a manufactured tribute is worse than a brief one.

### Step 7 — Settle future contact

Ask each what they want, separately if needed. Say plainly that wanting different things is normal, and
that the lesser expectation governs. Nobody owes anybody ongoing contact.

## Output Format

Produce exactly this structure.

```
# Closing — [relationship]

## Harm Screen
[Clear — proceed. | **Harm indicated: routed to [role]. No closing meeting will be arranged. Neither
party is asked to explain, meet, or forgive. VERIFY the correct process and services.** — stop here.]

## Type of Ending
**[Completion | Mismatch | Handoff | Withdrawal]** — [one line on why]

## The Conversation
**Opening:**
> "[..]"

**The reason, at the level chosen:**
> "[..]"
[If they'd rather not say: "I'd rather not go into it" is complete. Nothing further is asked.]

**Closing:**
> "[..]"

## If They've Gone Quiet
- Attempts: [n], [spacing]
- The final message:
> "[Warm, brief, no guilt, door open, no reply needed.]"
- Then: recorded as closed. No further contact.

## Handover
- What [mentee] takes with them: [..]
- Who they can go to next: [role]
- **Stopping altogether is a legitimate choice.** [..]

## Honest Thanks
- [mentor] for: [..]
- [mentee] for: [..]
[Small and true beats generous and false.]

## Future Contact
| | What they want | Agreed |
|---|---|---|
| [mentor] | [..] | [the lesser expectation governs] |
| [mentee] | [..] | |

**Nobody owes anybody ongoing contact.**
```

## Verification

- [ ] The harm screen runs first, and a harm ending stops the process rather than being handled warmly.
- [ ] The ending is classified, and the conversation matches the type.
- [ ] Nothing asks either party to justify ending; "I'd rather not say" is explicitly accepted.
- [ ] The silent ending has a bounded number of attempts and a guilt-free final message.
- [ ] The handover names what the mentee takes and who they can go to, and permits stopping entirely.
- [ ] No perseverance or covenant passages are used to make leaving feel wrong.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Treat a mismatch as somebody's failure. Most mismatches are two reasonable people who do not fit, and
  saying so plainly relieves both.
- Arrange a closing conversation where harm occurred. It re-exposes the person harmed, and it is a
  common, well-meant, serious mistake.
- Keep messaging a mentee who has gone quiet. After the stated attempts it becomes pressure, and the
  silence was probably the message.
- Push a re-match on someone who wants to stop. Wanting to stop is allowed, and honouring it is what
  makes returning later possible.
- Manufacture warmth in the thanks. People can tell, and a false tribute sours an otherwise clean
  ending.
- Use covenant or perseverance language. It converts a working arrangement into something leaving
  violates.

✅ **DO:**
- Classify before writing anything. A completion and a withdrawal need almost nothing in common.
- Keep the conversation short. Length here is usually someone resisting the decision.
- Write the final message to a silent mentee as if they will read it in six months, because they might,
  and it should be something that makes returning easy.
- Put what the mentee was working on into their own hands, so the work survives the relationship.
- Say "stopping altogether is a legitimate choice" explicitly, since the alternative is a re-match
  accepted out of politeness and abandoned in a fortnight.
- Let the lesser expectation govern future contact, so neither person carries an obligation they did
  not choose.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** the harm screen is Step 1 and terminates the process,
  so a harm ending can never be routed into the warm-closing path the rest of the prompt describes.
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework):** five ending types with materially different closes
  replace the single "how to end well" script that serves completion adequately and every other case
  badly.
- **CM-02 (Constraint Specification):** no-justification, no-reconciliation-after-harm, bounded chasing,
  and the ban on perseverance passages are hard constraints against the pressure endings otherwise
  accumulate.
- **RP-02 (Audience-Specific Framing):** verbatim words are supplied per ending type, including the
  message to a mentee who may read it months later, because these are the conversations people most
  avoid for want of language.
- **QA-01 (Self-Verification):** the verification block confirms classification, the harm stop, the
  bounded silent-ending protocol, and that stopping entirely was offered.
