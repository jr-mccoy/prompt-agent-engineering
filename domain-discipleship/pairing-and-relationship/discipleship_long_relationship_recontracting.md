# Re-contracting a Long Relationship

**Objective:** Take a discipling relationship that has continued well past whatever it was originally for —
often for years, usually without anyone deciding it should — and reach a named outcome: renew it around a
new focus, change its shape into friendship or peer accountability, or release it; by getting the drift
said out loud, testing what the relationship is still actually doing, and making release available without
anyone having to justify choosing it.

> **Boundary guardrail.** This is an ordinary review of a relationship that has simply carried on, not a
> response to harm. If the relationship has become one the mentee cannot easily leave, if the mentor has
> become the mentee's only source of counsel or reassurance, or if either party's wellbeing is bound up in
> it, that is a different question — see `../after-harm/discipleship_dependency_and_over_attachment.md`.
> If anything has gone wrong that a safeguarding policy would cover, this prompt stops and that governs.
> A lay mentor is not a counselor, and a long relationship does not make them one.

**When to use:** At a year boundary, at a natural break, or at the point one of them notices they have been
meeting out of habit — and specifically when nobody can any longer say what the relationship is for.

**When NOT to use:**
- The relationship has clearly reached its end — use
  `discipleship_relationship_ending_or_transition.md`, which handles all five ending types properly, and
  do not use a re-contracting frame to soften a decision already made.
- The problem is the meeting pattern rather than the purpose — use
  `discipleship_cadence_and_rhythm_design.md`.
- The relationship has closed in on itself — use
  `../after-harm/discipleship_dependency_and_over_attachment.md`.
- The mentor is reviewing a completed season privately — use
  `../mentor-equipping/discipleship_mentor_season_debrief.md`.
- You are deciding what a peer accountability partnership should look like once this becomes one — use
  `../peer-and-accountability/discipleship_accountability_partnership_design.md`, which is the destination
  for one of the three outcomes here.

**Audience:** The pair, working it together. Either can start it, and the one who starts it is not
proposing to end it.

---

## Inputs / Context

**Required:**

1. **What it was originally for, and when.** As best either can remember, including whether an end date was
   ever set. Most of these relationships were never given one, and saying so is the beginning of the
   conversation rather than a criticism of it.
2. **What actually happens now.** Frequency, content, who initiates, what the last three meetings were
   about. Habit and purpose look identical from inside; only the content shows the difference.
3. **What each person would say the relationship is for today**, answered separately before they are
   compared. Two different answers is the common result and is useful.
4. **What has changed in either life since it started.** Roles, seasons, distance, family, health, church.
   A relationship designed for one season and run into another usually needs a shape change rather than an
   ending.

**Optional:**

5. **Declared tradition (optional).** May shape whether ongoing spiritual direction is a normal lifelong
   practice or a time-bounded discipling relationship is the expected form, and whether a leader or
   director has a defined continuing role — which changes whether "it has gone on for years" is drift at
   all. Where a tradition treats a long relationship as ordinary, the question becomes what it is *for*
   rather than how long it has run.
6. **Whether either has quietly wanted to stop.** Asked directly, and answered without consequence.
7. **What else each person has now.** Often the mentee has grown a community the relationship predates, and
   that is the strongest argument for a shape change.

**If any required input is missing:** Ask both people the same questions separately before comparing. If
only one person is willing to have this conversation, that is itself the finding — and the outcome is
whatever the more reluctant person wants, without their having to explain it.

---

## Constraints

### Must

- Have both people answer **what it is for today, separately**, before any comparison — the divergence is
  the most useful input and it disappears if they answer in front of each other.
- **Name the drift as ordinary**, not as a failure of either person. A relationship with no end date
  continuing indefinitely is what such a relationship does.
- Test what the relationship is **still actually doing** against what it once did, using the content of
  recent meetings rather than either person's account of its value.
- Present **three outcomes as equally legitimate** — renew with a new focus, change shape, release — and
  say what distinguishes them.
- Require **no justification for release**, and state that explicitly, in the words used.
- Where the outcome is **change shape**, say what the new thing is and hand off to where it is designed —
  friendship needs nothing designed, peer accountability does.
- Where the outcome is **renew**, require a **new focus and a new end date**. Renewal without either is the
  drift continuing with a meeting attached.
- Give the **words for starting the conversation**, since the person who raises it is usually read as the
  one who wants out, and that misreading is what stops it being raised at all.
- Check that neither person's answer is **shaped by what they think the other wants** — the specific failure
  of two people who like each other.

### Must Not

- Treat length as a problem in itself, or imply that a long relationship has failed to graduate anyone.
- Treat ending as the mature, honest, or default outcome, or frame renewal as avoidance.
- Require the mentee to demonstrate growth, independence, or readiness in order to be released — or produce
  any assessment of where they have got to.
- Produce any score, stage, maturity level, or graduation criterion.
- Let either person's answer be negotiated, argued with, or improved by the other.
- Convert the relationship into peer accountability by declaring it so; the design work belongs elsewhere
  and skipping it produces neither relationship.
- Name any counselor, agency, or service; state any legal duty — `[VERIFY]` and route.
- Quote Scripture text from memory, or use any passage about faithfulness, perseverance, or covenant to
  make either person feel that release is disloyalty.
- Use the re-contracting frame to deliver an ending one person has already decided on.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)

- **Must:** note where traditions differ on whether spiritual direction or accompaniment is a normal
  lifelong relationship or discipling is expected to be time-bounded and reproducing, and on whether a
  director, elder, or leader holds a continuing role — and where a tradition treats a long relationship as
  ordinary, ask what it is for rather than treating its duration as the problem.
- **Must Not:** rule that any tradition's expected duration is correct, or allow a tradition's language of
  covenant, faithfulness, or spiritual fatherhood to make release feel like a breach of loyalty rather than
  an ordinary outcome.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Ask separately, then compare

Each person answers, alone: what is this for now, what do you get from it, when did you last learn
something here, and have you wanted to stop. Then compare. Two different answers is the usual result and it
is not a problem — it is the material.

### Step 2 — Name the drift without blaming it

Say plainly that nobody decided to keep going and nobody decided to stop, and that this is what happens to a
relationship with no end date. Removing the implication of failure is what makes an honest answer possible
at Step 3.

### Step 3 — Test what it is still doing

Look at the last three meetings. What was talked about, who brought it, what changed afterwards. Compare it
with the first three. A relationship that has become a good conversation between friends is not failing at
anything — but it is a different thing, and naming it correctly is the whole exercise.

### Step 4 — Put the three outcomes on the table

Renew with a new focus and a new end date. Change shape — into friendship, which needs no design, or into
peer accountability, which does. Or release, with nothing required to justify it. State them together and
in that order so that release is not the last word, and say plainly that any of the three is a fine place
to land.

### Step 5 — Check for accommodation

Ask each person whether their answer would change if the other had said something different. This is where
two people who value each other quietly produce a joint decision neither of them wanted, and asking it
directly is the only way it surfaces.

### Step 6 — Write the outcome with its requirements

Renew: the new focus, the new end date, what changes about the meetings. Change shape: what the new thing
is, what stops, and the handoff to where it gets designed. Release: the last meeting, what is said, what
continues informally if anything, and no reason recorded.

### Step 7 — Write the opening words, then check

Write what the person raising this says, so that raising it is not read as wanting out. Then check: are all
three outcomes genuinely open? Does renewal have a focus and a date? Has either person been asked to justify
release? Is there any assessment of the mentee anywhere in this?

## Verification

- [ ] Both people answered separately before comparing.
- [ ] The drift is named as ordinary rather than as either person's failure.
- [ ] All three outcomes are presented as equally legitimate, and release requires no justification.
- [ ] A renewal outcome carries both a new focus and a new end date.
- [ ] The accommodation check is asked and both answers recorded.
- [ ] No assessment, score, stage, or graduation criterion for the mentee appears anywhere.

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Treat length as the problem. Some traditions expect lifelong accompaniment and some relationships remain
  useful for a decade; the question is what it is for, not how long it has run.
- Make ending the honest answer. Framing release as maturity and renewal as avoidance decides the outcome
  before the conversation starts.
- Ask the mentee to demonstrate readiness. Release is not graduation, and requiring evidence of growth to
  earn it turns the last act of the relationship into an assessment.
- Let them answer in front of each other. Two people who like each other will converge within a sentence,
  and the divergence that would have been useful never appears.
- Renew without a focus and a date. It is the same drift with a meeting in the calendar and a feeling of
  having addressed it.
- Declare it a peer relationship and stop. Peer accountability that is announced rather than designed
  becomes neither discipling nor accountability, just a diary entry.
- Use this as a soft way to end something already decided. If the decision is made, say so — a
  consultation whose outcome is fixed is worse than a plain ending.

✅ **DO:**
- Ask separately, and expect the answers to differ.
- Say out loud that nobody decided to keep going. It removes the blame that otherwise keeps the honest
  answer unsaid.
- Compare the last three meetings with the first three. Content shows what recollection does not.
- Put all three outcomes on the table together, and say that any of them is a fine place to land.
- Ask the accommodation question directly. It is the only thing that surfaces a joint decision neither
  person wanted.
- Give the opener to whoever raises it, so that raising it is not heard as leaving.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** separate answers precede comparison, and the drift is
  normalized before the relationship is tested — both orderings exist to make an honest answer available.
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework):** original purpose, current content, each person's own
  answer, and the accommodation check are worked as separate axes, so the relationship's warmth cannot
  stand in for its usefulness.
- **CM-02 (Constraint Specification):** the Must Not block bans the two framings that decide the outcome in
  advance — ending as maturity, and release as something to be earned.
- **OC-03 (Markdown Table Specification):** the first-three / last-three comparison is a table because the
  contrast is the finding, and prose lets both columns be described in the same generous terms.
- **QA-01 (Self-Verification):** the final check tests whether renewal was given a focus and a date, which
  is the difference between a decision and a continuation.
