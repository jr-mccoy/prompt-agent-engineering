# First Meeting Guide

**Objective:** Plan the first conversation between a newly paired mentor and mentee so that it does
three things well — establishes what this is for, states the limits of confidentiality before anything
is disclosed, and lets the mentee set the depth — while avoiding the two failure modes: an interview
that extracts information, and a friendly hour that settles nothing.

> **Boundary guardrail.** A first meeting is not an assessment, an intake interview, or a spiritual
> inventory. The mentee owes no disclosure to be accepted. If something serious surfaces in this
> meeting — distress, abuse, self-harm, crisis — the mentor does not handle it here: they route it, per
> `../mentor-equipping/discipleship_mentor_boundaries_and_referral.md`, to a licensed professional or,
> where there is danger, to emergency services
> `[VERIFY: identify the correct service from an official source]`.

**When to use:** Before the first meeting of a newly matched pair. Usable by either party, though
written primarily for the mentor.

**When NOT to use:**
- You need the written agreement itself — use `discipleship_relationship_covenant.md`, which this
  meeting introduces.
- You are planning ongoing sessions — use `../session-and-lesson/discipleship_session_plan_builder.md`.
- You want a question bank to draw on — use
  `../session-and-lesson/discipleship_conversation_question_bank.md`.
- The mentee is a brand-new believer and you need the content path — use
  `domain-biblical-studies/ministry-contexts/biblical_ministry_new_believer_discipleship_path.md`.

**Audience:** The mentor preparing for a first meeting; program leads producing a standard guide.

---

## Inputs / Context

**Required:**

1. **What each person knows about the other.** Whatever came through onboarding and matching, and
   nothing more — the mentor should not arrive holding information the mentee does not know they have.
2. **What the mentee asked for.** In their own words if available.
3. **The confidentiality and reporting rules.** What the mentor must pass on and to whom.
4. **Practical setting.** Where, how long, in person or by video, and whether it is a visible public
   place per the program's conduct expectations.

**Optional:**

5. **Declared tradition (optional).** May shape whether the meeting opens or closes with prayer,
   whether Scripture is read, and what formality is expected.
6. **Known nervousness.** Anything either party has said about being anxious about this.
7. **The pathway.** If the pair will follow a curriculum.

**If any required input is missing:** Ask clarifying questions before proceeding. If the mentor holds
information about the mentee that the mentee has not been told was shared, flag that as a problem to
resolve before the meeting rather than something to manage during it.

---

## Constraints

### Must

- Give the mentor a **plan with actual words**, not principles — opening lines, the confidentiality
  sentence, and a closing.
- Place the **confidentiality limits early**, before any question that invites disclosure, and give the
  verbatim sentence.
- Have the mentor **go first** on any self-disclosure they invite, at the depth they are inviting.
- Let the **mentee set the depth.** No question requires an answer, and the mentor says so.
- Keep the meeting **short and unfinished** — first meetings that run long usually did so because
  someone was extracting or performing.
- End with **one small, concrete next step** and a settled next meeting.
- Include a **plan for silence and for a mentee who does not want to talk**, since that is common and
  most guides omit it.
- Include what to do if **something serious surfaces**: route, do not handle.

### Must Not

- Structure the meeting as a series of questions about the mentee's spiritual history, sin, or
  testimony.
- Ask for a testimony, a conversion account, or a summary of struggles as an opening move.
- Promise unconditional confidentiality, in any wording.
- Require prayer, Scripture reading, or any spiritual activity the mentee has not indicated they want.
- Set expectations, goals, or commitments for the mentee in this meeting.
- Quote Scripture text from memory; if a passage is read, give the address and let it be read aloud
  from a Bible.
- Invent rapport-building research or first-meeting statistics.
- Name a specific service if routing is needed — describe the kind and mark `[VERIFY]`.
- Let the meeting exceed the agreed length in order to get somewhere.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)

- **Must:** where a tradition has conventions for a first meeting — opening prayer, a set liturgy,
  Scripture reading, formality of address — apply them if both parties hold them, and offer rather than
  assume where only one does.
- **Must Not:** default to one stream's devotional opening as though it were neutral, or treat a
  mentee's discomfort with a tradition-specific practice as reluctance about the relationship.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Check what the mentor knows and how they know it

List what the mentor has been told. Flag anything the mentee may not know was shared. Resolve it before
the meeting; arriving with undisclosed knowledge damages trust at the exact moment it is being built.

### Step 2 — Write the opening

Two or three sentences: warmth, why the mentor is here, and what will happen in the next hour. Give the
mentee the shape of the meeting up front — it substantially reduces the anxiety of not knowing what is
coming.

### Step 3 — Write the confidentiality sentence

Verbatim, early, in plain language, before any invitation to disclose. Include what the mentor keeps,
what they would have to pass on, and to whom. Then the line that makes it safe: nothing has to be said
today.

### Step 4 — Plan the mentor's own disclosure

Decide what the mentor shares about themselves and at what depth, and place it before any equivalent
invitation. Depth is set by going first, not by asking.

### Step 5 — Plan the conversation, lightly

Three or four open areas, not a question list: what brought them here, what they are hoping for, what
they would like this to be, and what they would not want. Each is optional and the mentor says so.

### Step 6 — Plan for silence and reticence

Write what the mentor does when the mentee gives short answers or nothing at all: the pause held, the
lower-stakes question, and the explicit permission to sit with it. A first meeting where the mentee
said little is not a failed meeting.

### Step 7 — Close with one step, and prepare the routing

Settle the next meeting, agree one small step, and introduce the covenant if it belongs here. Then
write what the mentor does if something serious surfaces — brief, warm, routed, and the relationship
continues.

## Output Format

Produce exactly this structure, as a plan the mentor takes into the room.

```
# First Meeting — [mentee], [date], [length]

## Before You Go
- What you know about them: [..]
- **Do they know you know it?** [..] — resolve before meeting if not.
- Where you're meeting: [visible / public / video — per conduct expectations]

## Opening — What You Say
> "[Two or three sentences: warmth, why you're here, what the next hour looks like.]"

## The Confidentiality Sentence — Say This Early
> "[Verbatim. What stays between you, what you'd have to pass on, to whom. Then: nothing has to be
> said today.]"

**[VERIFY]** Reporting duties are jurisdiction- and age-specific; the program's safeguarding lead
confirms them.

## What You Share First
[What the mentor discloses, at the depth they're inviting. Before any equivalent question.]

## Areas to Open — Not a Question List
| Area | An opening question | Say it's optional |
|---|---|---|
| What brought you here | "[..]" | yes |
| What you're hoping for | "[..]" | yes |
| What you'd like this to be | "[..]" | yes |
| What you wouldn't want | "[..]" | yes |

## If They Don't Say Much
- Hold the pause: [..]
- A lower-stakes question: "[..]"
- What you say to make the quiet fine: "[..]"
- **A quiet first meeting is not a failed first meeting.**

## If Something Serious Comes Up
- What you say: "[..]"
- Where it goes: [kind of service — VERIFY]
- What you make clear: this doesn't change our relationship.

## Closing
- Next meeting: [settled now]
- One small step: [..]
- The covenant: [introduced now / next time]
> "[Closing words.]"
```

## Verification

- [ ] The confidentiality sentence is verbatim and placed before any question inviting disclosure.
- [ ] The mentor's own disclosure precedes any equivalent invitation.
- [ ] Every conversational area is marked optional, and the mentor says so aloud.
- [ ] A plan for silence and reticence is present, framed as normal.
- [ ] Routing instructions exist for a serious disclosure, with `[VERIFY]` rather than a named service.
- [ ] The meeting closes with a settled next meeting and exactly one small step.

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Open by asking for a testimony. It is the most common first move and it establishes immediately that
  the mentee's job is to supply material.
- Ask questions the mentor would not answer themselves at the same depth. Asymmetric disclosure in
  meeting one sets the pattern for everything after.
- Fill silence. A mentee who is thinking is not a mentee who is stuck, and the mentor's discomfort is
  not a reason to speak.
- Set goals for the mentee. Nothing is known yet, and goals set in meeting one are the mentor's
  projections.
- Run long to reach a breakthrough. Ending slightly early with things unfinished is a better start than
  a two-hour first meeting.
- Arrive holding information the mentee does not know was passed on. It surfaces eventually and reads
  as surveillance.

✅ **DO:**
- Tell the mentee the shape of the hour at the start. Not knowing what is coming is most of the anxiety.
- Say the confidentiality sentence out loud even though it is also written. Spoken and written are
  different acts, and the spoken one is what is remembered.
- Go first, and at real depth. It is the only reliable way to make an invitation to depth non-coercive.
- Say "you don't have to answer that" and mean it — then move on without a pause that implies they
  should reconsider.
- Plan explicitly for the reticent mentee, since a mentor without a plan will fill the silence and
  train the mentee to expect it.
- Close with one small step, not a programme. The first step's job is to prove the relationship
  continues.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** the confidentiality sentence and the mentor's own
  disclosure are both sequenced before any invitation to disclose, so the meeting's structure enforces
  what a principle alone would not.
- **RP-02 (Audience-Specific Framing):** the output is a plan with verbatim words rather than
  guidance, because a nervous mentor in the room needs sentences, not principles.
- **CM-02 (Constraint Specification):** the Must Not block bans the genre's default openings —
  testimony requests, spiritual-history questions, goal-setting — that convert a first meeting into an
  intake interview.
- **ED-01 (Iterative Scaffolding):** depth is scaffolded by the mentor going first, and the meeting
  closes with a single small step whose purpose is continuity rather than progress.
- **QA-01 (Self-Verification):** the verification block checks sentence placement, disclosure order,
  and the presence of a silence plan before the mentor walks in.
