# Informal Pairing Without a Program

**Objective:** Set up a discipling relationship in the ordinary case this domain otherwise assumes away —
two people, no program, no lead, no policy, nobody to report to — by establishing who the third party will
be, what the agreement is when nothing enforces it, what the safeguarding floor is, and where a concern
would actually go; and by returning **"not like this"** where the arrangement cannot be made safe, rather
than writing a plan around a gap.

> **Boundary guardrail.** This prompt cannot manufacture protection that does not exist. Where a **minor or
> a vulnerable adult** is involved and there is no organization behind the relationship, the honest answer
> is that it should not be set up this way at all — it needs to sit under a body with a safeguarding policy
> and screening `[VERIFY: identify the appropriate body and confirm requirements from an official source]`
> — and that answer is the deliverable, not a caution attached to a plan. The same applies where one person
> holds authority over the other's job, housing, immigration status, money, or standing in a community.
> A lay mentor here is not a counselor or a pastor, and with no institution behind them the routing-out
> obligations are stricter, not looser.

**When to use:** When two adults in the same church, workplace, or friendship are about to start meeting
for discipleship and there is no program to join, no lead to tell, and no template anyone will hand them.

**When NOT to use:**
- A program exists, however small — use `discipleship_pairing_criteria_design.md` and
  `../program-operations/discipleship_participant_onboarding_design.md`.
- You lead something small and are working out what structure you genuinely need — use
  `../program-operations/discipleship_minimum_viable_program.md`, which asks that question directly.
- You are the person being discipled and want to know what normal looks like before agreeing to anything —
  use `discipleship_what_to_expect_as_a_mentee.md`, which is shorter, is yours, and comes first.
- A minor, a young person, or a vulnerable adult is involved — this needs an organization; see the
  guardrail above.
- You want the content of a first-steps path rather than its structure — use
  `domain-biblical-studies/ministry-contexts/biblical_ministry_new_believer_discipleship_path.md`.

**Audience:** The two people involved, together. Also the pastor of a church with no program who is asked
"could you set us up with someone" and has nothing to hand over.

## Inputs / Context

**Required:**

1. **Who both people are, in relation to each other.** How they know each other, whether either holds any
   authority over the other — employer, landlord, supervisor, teacher, elder, group leader, creditor,
   immigration sponsor — and whether they are family. This determines whether the relationship should
   exist in this form at all, and is the first question rather than a detail.
2. **That both are adults, and whether either is in a vulnerable situation.** Not a judgement about
   capability: recent bereavement, a live crisis, isolation, a recent exit from a high-control setting, or
   dependence on the other person for something material. Any of these changes the answer.
3. **Who could be the third party.** A named person who will know this relationship exists — not to
   supervise it, but so that it is not invisible. If nobody can be named, that is the central finding and
   the output has to say so rather than proceed.
4. **What either person is expecting.** Duration, frequency, subject matter, and what happens if it does
   not work. Two people who have not said this out loud have two different arrangements.

**Optional:**

5. **Declared tradition (optional).** May shape whether a lay person disciples at all or only an ordained
   or appointed one, whether an elder or priest should know, what confession practice applies, and what
   authority the relationship is understood to carry — which affects both who the third party should be
   and what the agreement should say. Where none is declared, the setup stays tradition-general.
6. **What church or community sits around this**, even loosely. A church with no program still has people
   in it, and the third party usually comes from there.
7. **Whether either has done this before**, and how it ended.

**If any required input is missing:** Ask before anything is arranged. If no third party can be named, say
plainly that the relationship would be invisible, what that means, and what would have to change — and do
not produce an agreement that quietly proceeds without one. If the relationship crosses an authority line
or involves a minor or vulnerable adult, stop at the "not like this" output.

## Constraints

### Must

- **Run the not-like-this gate first**, and return it as a complete output where it triggers: a minor or
  vulnerable adult with no organization behind the arrangement; an authority relationship; a family
  relationship where the discipling role would confuse an existing one; or nobody who can be the third
  party.
- Establish a **named third party before the first meeting** — someone who knows this exists, whom either
  person could go to — and state plainly what it means if there is nobody.
- State the **safeguarding floor that survives having no policy**: where they meet, what visibility looks
  like, what contact happens outside meetings, and what is never done one-to-one and unobserved.
- Write the **agreement between two people with nothing enforcing it** — scope, frequency, duration, end
  date, and how either leaves — and note that its force comes from having been said aloud and written
  down, which is more than most such relationships have.
- Settle **confidentiality and its limits before anything is disclosed**, including the fact that with no
  program there is no internal route, so some things go straight to a professional or an authority.
- Name **where a concern would actually go** for each person, `[VERIFY]`, given there is no lead — and
  establish this before it is needed rather than at the point it is.
- Fix an **end date**, since a relationship with no institution has nothing else that will ever end it.
- Give **both people the same document**, and say so — an arrangement one person holds is not mutual.
- State what routes out and the **words for it**, with the note that the absence of a program makes
  routing more urgent rather than less.

### Must Not

- Describe, imply, or design around a protection that does not exist — a lead, a policy, a screening
  process, a reporting route — or let good character stand in for any of them.
- Recommend that a minor or vulnerable adult be discipled one-to-one outside an organization with a
  safeguarding policy, under any conditions.
- Set up a relationship across an authority line by adding safeguards to it.
- State any legal, screening, background-check, or reporting requirement. Mark `[VERIFY]` and route to
  `domain-legal/` and to whatever body the arrangement could attach to.
- Name any counselor, agency, hotline, church, network, or service.
- Suggest that the informal arrangement is equivalent to a run program, or that structure is legalism,
  bureaucracy, or a lack of trust.
- Give either person authority over the other's decisions, and do not use covering, submission, or
  spiritual-authority language to describe the relationship.
- Quote Scripture text from memory, or use any passage to argue that a relationship needs no accountability
  because it is between believers.
- Produce a maturity assessment of either person, or any score, level, or readiness rating.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)

- **Must:** note where traditions differ on whether lay people disciple one another at all or whether this
  belongs to an ordained or appointed office, on whether an elder, priest, or overseer should know, on
  confession practice, and on the authority such a relationship carries — and where a tradition expects a
  leader to be informed, treat that as satisfying the third-party requirement rather than as an addition
  to it.
- **Must Not:** rule that any tradition's structure is correct or that informal lay discipling is
  illegitimate; and do not allow any tradition's language of covering, submission, or spiritual authority
  to be used to give one of two friends standing over the other's decisions.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Run the gate before designing anything

Test four things: is anyone involved a minor or vulnerable adult; does either hold authority over the other;
would this confuse an existing family role; is there anyone at all who could be the third party. Any of the
first three, or a no to the last, produces the "not like this" output — what it is, why, and what would have
to change. Say it plainly and completely. A gentle version of this finding will be read as an obstacle to
work around.

### Step 2 — Name the third party out loud

Pick a person and tell them. Not a supervisor and not a referee — someone who knows this relationship
exists, and whom either party could go to if it went wrong. Invisibility, not malice, is what makes informal
relationships risky, and one named person removes most of it at almost no cost.

### Step 3 — Set the floor that survives having no policy

Decide where they meet and how that is visible, what contact happens between meetings and on what channel,
what is not done alone and unobserved, and who else knows the pattern. Keep it short enough to be kept.

### Step 4 — Write the agreement, and give both people a copy

Scope, frequency, length, duration with an end date, what each brings, how disagreement is handled, and how
either ends it with no reason required. Two adults writing this down have more structure than most informal
discipling ever gets, and the writing down is the part that does the work.

### Step 5 — Settle confidentiality before anything is said

What is kept, what is not, and the specific point that with no program there is no internal escalation — so
certain things go directly to a professional or an authority. Write the sentence the mentor says at the
first meeting, and the sentence for when they are asked to promise secrecy in advance.

### Step 6 — Establish where a concern goes, before it is needed

For each person: if they became concerned about this relationship, who would they tell? Name it now,
`[VERIFY]`, while nothing is wrong. Working this out during a crisis is how informal relationships end in
silence.

### Step 7 — Fix the end date, then check

Set a date to stop or renew. Then check the output: does it claim any protection that does not exist? Is
the third party named and told? Would either person know how to leave? Is there anything here that gives one
person standing over the other's life?

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Design around a missing third party. "They'll be accountable to each other" is not visibility; it is the
  condition that makes informal relationships hard to see into, described as a feature.
- Add safeguards to an authority relationship. A manager and their report, or a landlord and their tenant,
  do not become a safe pairing by meeting in a coffee shop — the problem is the standing, not the venue.
- Set up a minor or vulnerable adult this way under any conditions, including a parent's enthusiastic
  agreement and an unimpeachable adult. That case needs an organization, and saying so is the help.
- Imply the informal version is equivalent to a run program. It is not, and both people should know exactly
  what they are and are not covered by.
- Treat structure as distrust. Writing it down protects the relationship rather than doubting it, and the
  people most reluctant to write anything down are not always the people to worry about — but the practice
  costs nothing either way.
- Leave it open-ended. With no program to review it, an informal relationship has literally nothing that
  will ever end it, and it will drift instead.
- Use covering or submission language between two friends. It converts a mutual arrangement into a chain of
  command that nobody agreed to and nothing oversees.

✅ **DO:**
- Run the gate first, and let "not like this" be a complete answer with no consolation plan attached.
- Name the third party and actually tell them. It is the single highest-value item here and takes one
  conversation.
- Say what you do not have, in plain words. A person who knows there is no complaints process behaves
  differently from one who assumes there is.
- Write the confidentiality sentence and say it at the first meeting, including the point that some things
  bypass everyone internal because there is no internal.
- Work out where a concern would go while nothing is wrong.
- Put an end date on it, and let renewing be a choice rather than a default.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** the gate runs before any design, which is what makes
  "not like this" a real output rather than a warning attached to a plan that proceeds anyway.
- **CM-02 (Constraint Specification):** the Must Not block bans the two failures that look like generosity
  — describing protection that does not exist, and adding safeguards to a relationship that should not be
  set up at all.
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework):** standing between the two people, visibility, agreement,
  and escalation are worked as separate axes, so a good agreement cannot compensate for an authority
  problem and a named third party cannot compensate for an absent end date.
- **OC-03 (Markdown Table Specification):** the gate is a table with an explicit answer per row, because a
  prose judgement about whether a pairing is appropriate is exactly where enthusiasm gets in.
- **QA-01 (Self-Verification):** the final check tests for the claim of a protection the arrangement does
  not have, which is the error most likely to survive a well-intentioned draft.
