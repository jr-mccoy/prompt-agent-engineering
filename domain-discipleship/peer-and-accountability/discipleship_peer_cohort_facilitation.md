# Peer-Cohort Facilitation

**Objective:** Design how a mentors' peer cohort meeting is actually run — the shape of the time, the
standing questions, the words that interrupt a mentee entering the room, the response when a group has
gone quiet and nobody is admitting anything, and the handling of a group whose members do not hold equal
power.

> **Boundary guardrail.** The facilitator is a facilitator, not a supervisor, a counselor, or an
> assessor. They do not diagnose members, resolve a mentee's situation, or carry what is said into
> program decisions. A member's mental health, burnout, or personal crisis routes to licensed help
> `[VERIFY: identify the correct service from an official source]`; a safeguarding concern goes to the
> safeguarding lead immediately and leaves the room with it.

**When to use:** You facilitate a mentors' cohort and want the meeting to do what it is for, or you are
preparing someone to take it on.

**When NOT to use:**
- You are designing the season's content — use `discipleship_peer_cohort_curriculum.md`.
- You are facilitating a Bible-study group — use
  `domain-biblical-studies/group-leader-facilitation/biblical_groupleader_facilitation_dynamics.md`.
- You are running a discipleship triad or small group of disciples — use
  `../session-and-lesson/discipleship_small_group_discipleship_format.md`.
- The group has an unresolved conflict rather than a facilitation problem — that is a different task.

**Audience:** Cohort facilitators, program leads, and mentors taking a turn at facilitating.

---

## Inputs / Context

**Required:**

1. **The cohort's stated purpose and proportions**, from the curriculum: how much of the meeting is
   formation, support, skill, and accountability.
2. **The confidentiality rule as written**, so the facilitator is enforcing an agreed standard rather
   than improvising one.
3. **Who is in the room, and the power between them.** Any member with authority over another — an
   overseer, a manager, a pastor — named explicitly, because it changes what can be said and the
   facilitator is the only person positioned to compensate.
4. **Time available**, honestly. The meeting that has ninety minutes and plans for two hours ends
   mid-sentence on the hardest item, every time.

**Optional:**

5. **Declared tradition (optional).** May shape opening and closing practice, prayer form, confession,
   and how silence is used.
6. **The group's history.** How long it has met, what it has been through, and whether trust exists yet.
7. **Anything live in the program** that will be in the room whether or not it is on the agenda.

**If any required input is missing:** Ask before proceeding — especially item 3. A facilitator who does
not know who holds power over whom will read a member's silence as reticence when it is prudence.

---

## Constraints

### Must

- Give the meeting a **fixed shape** with a stated proportion of time to each part, and hold it — a
  cohort whose shape drifts becomes whatever its most talkative member needs.
- Open with a **standing question every member answers**, short enough that nobody can hide and low
  enough that nobody has to perform.
- Give the facilitator **the exact words** for the interventions that matter: a mentee entering the
  conversation, advice-giving that has become a pile-on, a member who is fixing rather than listening,
  and a disclosure that must leave the room.
- Handle **the quiet meeting** deliberately: what to do when nobody is struggling, which almost always
  means the group does not yet believe it is safe — and the answer is the facilitator going first.
- Name and compensate for **power in the room**: what the facilitator does when a member's overseer is
  present, including asking whether that person should be.
- Protect the **confidentiality rule actively**, not by hoping — with the interruption rehearsed and
  used early, before a mentee's situation is half-described.
- End with something **carried out of the room**: one thing each member is taking, and one thing they
  are putting down.
- Record **nothing** about members, and state that out loud.
- Route mental health, burnout, and safeguarding **out** `[VERIFY]`, with words and with the
  relationship continuing.

### Must Not

- Quote Scripture text from memory or assert verse wording.
- Allow a mentee to be described in identifiable terms, however useful the example would be.
- Let the meeting become advice-giving, case-solving, or a round of suggestions for someone who asked to
  be heard.
- Assess, diagnose, or comment on a member's spiritual state, capability, or fitness for the role.
- Carry anything from the room into a program decision, a review, or a conversation with an overseer.
- Require a member to speak, disclose, pray aloud, or share a struggle.
- Invent group-dynamics research, facilitation studies, or statistics about mentor attrition.
- Handle a safeguarding matter in the room, or delay routing it to the end of the meeting.
- Let a single member's crisis consume a meeting without naming what has happened and what the group
  will do about the rest.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)

- **Must:** note where traditions differ on peer confession, on silence and contemplative practice, on
  extemporaneous versus fixed prayer, and on the authority of a facilitator — letting a declared
  tradition set its own opening and closing practice.
- **Must Not:** require a prayer form, confession practice, or silence discipline that a member's own
  tradition does not hold, or treat unfamiliar practice as reticence.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Set the shape and defend it

Design the meeting's parts with time against each: opening question, formation content, what members
bring, rehearsal, and close. State how the facilitator returns to the shape when it drifts, in words.

### Step 2 — Write the standing question

Design the question every member answers at the start. It must be answerable in two sentences, must not
require a struggle to be produced, and must be the same every meeting — so that a change in someone's
answer is visible without anyone being interrogated.

### Step 3 — Rehearse the four interventions

Write verbatim words for each:
- **A mentee is entering the room.** Used early, warmly, and every time.
- **Advice has become a pile-on.** Returning the room to the person who spoke.
- **A member is fixing rather than listening.** Naming it without shaming them.
- **Something must leave the room.** Safeguarding or a matter needing authority, routed now.

### Step 4 — Plan for the quiet meeting

Design the response to a group where nobody is struggling: the facilitator goes first with something
real and current, the standing question is given more time, and the cause is named — usually that trust
is not there yet, sometimes that someone with power is present.

### Step 5 — Handle power explicitly

State what the facilitator does when a member holds authority over another: naming it at the start,
asking whether that person should be in this cohort, and if they stay, what they undertake — usually to
speak last, to disclose first, and to keep nothing from the room in their other role.

### Step 6 — Build the rehearsal segment

Specify how the group practises: pairs, short scenarios, the words rather than the concepts, and a
debrief that is specific rather than encouraging. Route the underlying skill content to
`../mentor-equipping/discipleship_mentor_conversation_skills.md`.

### Step 7 — Design the close

End with each member naming one thing they carry out and one thing they put down. State that nothing is
recorded, and say it every meeting rather than once at the beginning of the season.

### Step 8 — Verify after the meeting

Give the facilitator a short self-check: did the shape hold; did a mentee enter the room; did anyone
speak only in generalities all evening; did power operate unnamed; did anything need to leave the room
and did it leave promptly.

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Wait to interrupt a mentee's story until it is clear where it is going. By then the group knows enough
  to identify them, and the rule has already been broken.
- Let the group solve it. A mentor who brings something and receives five suggestions has been managed,
  not heard, and will bring less next time.
- Read silence as contentment. A quiet cohort is usually an unsafe one, and the facilitator's own honest
  disclosure is the fastest available repair.
- Leave power unnamed. Everyone in the room knows who reports to whom; only the facilitator can say it
  out loud, and not saying it does not make it inoperative.
- Take notes. Even helpful notes make the room a record, and members calibrate accordingly within one
  meeting.
- Push a crisis to the end of the agenda. If something must leave the room, it leaves when it appears.

✅ **DO:**
- Write the four interventions as verbatim words and rehearse them before facilitating.
- Ask the standing question every meeting, unchanged, so that a shift in an answer is visible without
  anyone being asked about it.
- Go first when the room is quiet, with something real and current rather than resolved.
- Ask openly whether a member with authority over others should be in this cohort at all.
- Practise words rather than discussing concepts, and debrief specifically.
- Say "nothing is recorded" every meeting. Members do not believe it after one announcement, and they
  should not have to.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-01 (Clear Objective Statement):** the objective fixes the deliverable as the mechanics of the
  meeting — shape, questions, interventions, power — rather than as a description of good facilitation.
- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** the shape is set, then the standing question, then the
  interventions, so the facilitator has the words before encountering the moments that need them.
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework):** shape, disclosure safety, power, and routing are
  handled as separate axes, because a meeting can be well-shaped and still unsafe.
- **OC-03 (Markdown Table Specification):** interventions and routing appear as tables pairing each
  situation with verbatim words, so no situation is listed without a usable response.
- **QA-01 (Self-Verification):** the facilitator's post-meeting self-check verifies the five things that
  most commonly went wrong, while the meeting is still recoverable.
