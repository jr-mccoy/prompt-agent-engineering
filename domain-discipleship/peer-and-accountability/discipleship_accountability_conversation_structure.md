# Accountability Conversation Structure

**Objective:** Structure the conversation two accountability partners actually have — a fixed shape that
survives a bad week, questions answerable without confession, the verbatim response when the answer is
no, and the periodic checks that catch the conversation drifting from support into inspection.

> **Boundary guardrail.** This is a peer conversation, not confession, counseling, or an intervention.
> A partner does not probe, diagnose, prescribe, or press. Where what surfaces is compulsion, distress,
> self-harm, abuse, or danger, the conversation stops being an accountability conversation and becomes a
> referral `[VERIFY: identify the correct service from an official source]` — and the partner says so
> plainly rather than continuing. Where either partner is a minor, the safeguarding policy governs.

**When to use:** A partnership exists and you want the meeting itself to work — or an existing
partnership has become an interrogation and needs restructuring.

**When NOT to use:**
- The partnership itself needs designing — use `discipleship_accountability_partnership_design.md`
  first; this prompt assumes its commitments, scope, and bans are already in place.
- The relationship is mentor–mentee — use `../session-and-lesson/discipleship_session_plan_builder.md`.
- Something heavy has just been disclosed — use
  `../session-and-lesson/discipleship_hard_conversation_navigation.md`.
- The person needs to work something through alone — a journal companion is a better fit.

**Audience:** Accountability partners, and program leads resourcing them.

---

## Inputs / Context

**Required:**

1. **The partnership's agreed commitments and out-of-scope list.** From the design. The conversation
   works only inside them.
2. **Time available.** Honestly — twenty minutes on a phone call is a real and workable format, and a
   structure that assumes an unhurried hour will be abandoned.
3. **Channel.** In person, phone, video, or written — each changes what is possible and what silence
   means.
4. **What has gone wrong before.** In this partnership or a previous one: probing, shame, drift into
   advice, or one person always going first.

**Optional:**

5. **Declared tradition (optional).** May shape opening and closing prayer, examen, or a confession
   practice that both hold.
6. **Anything currently live** for either person that will be in the conversation whether or not it is
   raised.

**If any required input is missing:** Ask before proceeding. If item 1 is missing, the conversation has
no boundaries and will find them the hard way — design the partnership first.

---

## Constraints

### Must

- Give the conversation a **fixed shape** that works in the real time available, with both people
  answering the same questions.
- **Alternate who goes first**, and state it — the person who always goes second answers a
  conversation that has already been shaped by the first.
- Use questions that are **answerable without confession**: about the week, the obstacle, the intention,
  and what would help — never about the detail of a failure.
- Write the **verbatim response to "I didn't"**, including what is not said, and stop there unless the
  person asks for more.
- Include a **question about what is going well** that is not a set-up for the next question.
- Include **"is there anything you want me to ask you about?"** — which hands control of depth to the
  person, and is the single most protective question in the structure.
- Design the **close** so the conversation ends where it started: no lingering probe, no summarizing of
  the other person's situation back to them, no assignment.
- Build in **periodic drift checks**: is this becoming inspection, is one person always the subject, has
  a metric appeared, has out-of-scope been tested.
- Include the **stop point**: what a partner says when the conversation has stopped being an
  accountability conversation, and where it goes `[VERIFY]`.

### Must Not

- Quote Scripture text from memory or assert verse wording.
- Ask for detail about a failure, a temptation, a relapse, or anything on the out-of-scope list.
- Follow up an "I didn't" with a probe, a plan, a comparison, an expression of disappointment, or a
  reference to a previous week.
- Introduce any counting, scoring, streak, or comparison into the conversation.
- Give advice unless asked, and then briefly.
- Pray about someone's disclosure in a way that repeats it back, or in front of anyone else.
- Continue an accountability conversation once compulsion, distress, or danger has surfaced.
- Carry anything from the conversation to anyone, including in a prayer request.
- Invent research about accountability, confession, or behaviour change.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)

- **Must:** note where traditions differ on mutual confession, absolution, examen, and prayer form, and
  let a declared tradition shape the opening and closing while the conversation's structure holds.
- **Must Not:** import a confession practice into the conversation as an expectation on a partner who
  holds differently, or treat a tradition's absence of such practice as reticence.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Set the shape to the real time

Design the conversation for the time that actually exists. A workable twenty-minute structure that
happens weekly beats an excellent hour that happens twice. State the parts and their proportions.

### Step 2 — Write the standing questions

Design the four or five questions asked every time, in order. They must be answerable in a sentence,
must not require a struggle to be produced, and must not ask for detail. Include the question about what
is going well, and place it where it is not a set-up.

### Step 3 — Write the hand-over-control question

"Is there anything you want me to ask you about?" — or the pair's own version. This question is what
allows depth to be chosen rather than extracted, and it belongs in every conversation.

### Step 4 — Write the "I didn't" response verbatim

The words, and what is not said. Then the one follow-up — usually about what got in the way — and the
full stop after it. Write the rule explicitly: nothing further unless the person asks.

### Step 5 — Design the close

End without a summary of the other person's situation, without an assignment, and without a lingering
question. Where the tradition includes prayer, state how it is done without repeating the disclosure
back.

### Step 6 — Build the drift checks

Design the periodic check — monthly, or every sixth conversation. Four questions: has this become
inspection; is one person always the subject; has a metric appeared; has anyone's out-of-scope been
tested. Give the words for raising it.

### Step 7 — Write the stop point

Write what a partner says when the conversation has stopped being an accountability conversation:
compulsion, distress, self-harm, abuse, or danger. The words, the destination `[VERIFY]`, and the
explicit statement that the friendship continues.

### Step 8 — Verify

Check: does the structure fit the real time; do both answer the same questions; does the order
alternate; is every question answerable without confession; is "I didn't" answered and then stopped; do
the drift checks exist.

## Verification

- [ ] The structure fits the honest time available and both partners answer the same questions.
- [ ] Who goes first alternates, and this is tracked rather than assumed.
- [ ] Every standing question is answerable in a sentence without disclosing detail.
- [ ] The hand-over-control question is present, and "no" is treated as a complete answer.
- [ ] The "I didn't" response is verbatim, has one follow-up, and then stops.
- [ ] Drift checks exist with words for raising them, and the stop point routes out with the friendship
      continuing.

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Ask for the detail. "What happened?" sounds like care and functions as an interrogation, and the
  person learns that a truthful answer costs more than a vague one.
- Follow "I didn't" with a plan. The person almost always has one; what they lack is a friend who can
  hear a no without reorganizing their week.
- Let the same person always go first. The second speaker calibrates to the first, and after a few weeks
  the conversation belongs to one of them.
- Reference last week. It turns the conversation into a record, and a record is a metric with a memory.
- Pray the disclosure back. Repeating what someone just admitted, out loud and in God's hearing, is
  experienced as exposure however kindly it is meant.
- Keep going once compulsion or distress has surfaced. The conversation's structure is not built for it,
  and continuing delays the help that is.

✅ **DO:**
- Design for twenty minutes if twenty minutes is what exists. Consistency is the whole mechanism.
- Include the hand-over-control question every single time, and accept "no" as complete.
- Write the "I didn't" response verbatim and stop after one follow-up.
- Alternate who goes first and track it, because nobody notices the drift on their own.
- Run the drift checks on a schedule, with words for raising them — the checks that only happen when
  someone feels brave never happen.
- Say the stop-point words plainly, and say the friendship continues, so routing out is not experienced
  as being handed on.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-01 (Clear Objective Statement):** the objective names the conversation's two failure modes —
  interrogation and drift — and makes the verbatim "I didn't" response part of the deliverable.
- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** the shape is set to real time before questions are
  written, so the structure that gets designed is the one that will actually be used weekly.
- **OC-03 (Markdown Table Specification):** questions, responses, drift checks, and stop points are
  tabulated with verbatim words, so no situation is listed without something sayable beside it.
- **CM-02 (Constraint Specification):** the Must Not block encodes the prohibitions that keep the
  conversation from curdling — no detail-seeking, no probe after "I didn't", no metrics, no carrying
  anything out.
- **QA-01 (Self-Verification):** the drift checks are scheduled self-verification, catching the four
  ways this conversation reliably degrades while it is still repairable.
