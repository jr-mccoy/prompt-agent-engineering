# Personal Growth Plan

**Objective:** Build one believer's plan for their next season of growth — a single focus, two or three
practices sized to their actual capacity, a floor that survives a bad week, the people involved, and a
date to look at it again — with what was deliberately left out stated openly.

> **Boundary guardrail.** This is a personal formation plan — NOT counseling, therapy, spiritual
> direction, or crisis support. A growth plan is not the right response to depression, trauma,
> addiction, abuse, or acute distress; those route to a licensed professional, a trusted pastor, or
> emergency services `[VERIFY: identify the correct service from an official source]`, and to
> `domain-psychology/`. Building a plan on top of untreated distress reliably produces failure and then
> self-blame.

**When to use:** You know roughly where you are and what you want to grow in, and you need a plan you
will actually keep rather than an aspirational list.

**When NOT to use:**
- You do not yet know where you are — use `discipleship_growth_self_assessment.md` first.
- You know the focus but not which practices fit you — use `discipleship_spiritual_practices_designer.md`.
- Your constraints are the whole problem — use `discipleship_life_constraints_adaptation.md`.
- You want a Scripture study plan specifically — use
  `domain-biblical-studies/learner-self-study/biblical_learner_self_directed_study_plan.md`.
- You want general habit mechanics in a non-religious frame — use
  `domain-personal-development/prompts/habits/habits_habit_design_blueprint.md`.

**Audience:** The individual believer, alone or with a mentor.

---

## Inputs / Context

**Required:**

1. **Your focus.** What you want this season to be about — one thing, in your own words. If several are
   offered, help choose one and say why the others are being deferred rather than dropped.
2. **Season length.** How long this plan runs. Six to twelve weeks is usually right; longer plans
   quietly become permanent aspirations.
3. **Real capacity.** How much time genuinely exists in a normal week, and which weeks are already
   known to be bad ones.
4. **What has failed before.** Previous attempts at this and what actually went wrong. This is the most
   useful input and the one most often skipped.

**Optional:**

5. **Declared tradition (optional).** May shape which practices are natural and available — sacramental
   participation, fixed-hour prayer, small group, confession, fasting, service.
6. **People available.** Anyone already walking with you: mentor, group, friend, spouse, pastor.
7. **Self-assessment output.** If `discipleship_growth_self_assessment.md` was run, supply its edges.

**If any required input is missing:** Ask clarifying questions before proceeding. Always ask what has
failed before if it is not supplied — a plan that repeats a known failure is worse than no plan,
because it costs the person another round of self-blame.

---

## Constraints

### Must

- Build around **one focus.** Name the deferred items explicitly as deferred, not abandoned.
- Size practices to the **stated real capacity**, using the person's *worst* normal week as the design
  constraint, not their best.
- Define a **floor** — the minimum version that still counts, doable on the hardest week. The floor is
  the plan; everything above it is bonus.
- Include a **recovery rule** for missed days that is specific and contains no penance, no catching up,
  and no restarting from zero.
- Name **at least one real person** who will know about this plan, or state plainly that there is
  nobody and what to do about that.
- Set a **review date** and say what will be looked at then — including permission to change or stop
  the plan.
- State what was **deliberately left out** and why.
- Address the **known failure** from the inputs directly: what is different this time, mechanically.

### Must Not

- Build a plan with more than three practices, or one that requires a good week to function.
- Attach numbers that measure spiritual progress — streaks as achievement, completion percentages,
  daily scores, reading-plan progress bars framed as growth.
- Use guilt, shame, urgency, or fear as motivation, or frame missed days as failure requiring make-up.
- Quote Scripture text from memory; give addresses only, for the person to read.
- Invent research, habit statistics, or claims about how long formation takes.
- Prescribe a practice the person's tradition does not hold, or imply their tradition's practices are
  insufficient.
- Name a specific counselor, service, app, book, or program — mark `[VERIFY]` and describe the kind.
- Build a plan on top of a described distress that should route out first.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)

- **Must:** draw practices from the shared core and from the person's own tradition; where a practice
  is stream-specific, name it as such and offer an alternative from the shared core.
- **Must Not:** import a tradition's distinctive practice as though it were universal Christian
  discipline, or rank one stream's devotional pattern above another's.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Narrow to one focus

If several things were offered, help choose. Name the criterion used — usually what is most live, not
what is most impressive — and list the deferred items explicitly, with a note that they are waiting,
not cancelled.

### Step 2 — Examine the previous failure

Take what has failed before and identify the mechanism: too ambitious, wrong time of day, no
accountability, depended on a quiet house that does not exist, or built on a season that ended. Say
what is structurally different in this plan. If nothing is different, say that and redesign.

### Step 3 — Choose two or three practices

Pick practices that serve the focus and fit the capacity. For each, say what it is, when it happens,
how long it takes, and what makes it likely to actually occur. Prefer attaching a practice to something
already reliably happening over creating a new slot in the day.

### Step 4 — Set the floor

For each practice, define the minimum version that still counts on the worst week. Make the floor
genuinely small — small enough that the person would be slightly embarrassed to call it a plan. That is
the correct size, and say why: a floor that is reachable on a bad week is what keeps the plan alive
through the bad weeks.

### Step 5 — Write the recovery rule

State exactly what happens after a missed day or a missed week. Return to the floor at the next
opportunity; nothing is owed; no restart. Write it in the words the person will actually re-read.

### Step 6 — Name the people and the review date

Identify who will know. If nobody, say so plainly and make finding one person a step in the plan. Set
the review date, and state what will be examined — including explicit permission to change or stop.

### Step 7 — State the omissions and check

List what was left out and why. Then check the plan against the four failure patterns: **built for the
good week**, **too many practices**, **no floor**, and **nobody knows**.

## Output Format

Produce exactly this structure. Write it to be re-read by the person on a bad week.

```
# Growth Plan — [focus], [n] weeks

## The Focus
[One sentence, in the person's own words.]

**Deferred for now (not cancelled):** [..]

## What Went Wrong Last Time
- What happened: [..]
- The actual mechanism: [..]
- What is structurally different now: [..]

## The Practices
| Practice | When | How long | What makes it likely | The floor (worst week) |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## If You Miss
[The recovery rule, in plain words. No catching up. No starting over. Return to the floor.]

## Who Knows
- [..]
[If nobody: say so, and make finding one person step one.]

## Review
- Date: [..]
- What you'll look at: [..]
- Explicit permission: you may change this, shrink it, or stop it at the review. That is the plan
  working, not failing.

## Deliberately Left Out
| What | Why | When it might return |
|---|---|---|

## Scripture for This Season
[Addresses only, with a note to read them in your own translation. No verse text supplied here.]
```

## Verification

- [ ] Exactly one focus; deferred items named as deferred.
- [ ] No more than three practices, all sized to the worst normal week.
- [ ] Every practice has a floor small enough to survive a genuinely bad week.
- [ ] The recovery rule contains no catching up, no restart, and no penance.
- [ ] The previous failure is named with its mechanism, and something structural is different.
- [ ] No number measures spiritual progress; no Scripture text is quoted.

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Design for the person's stated intentions rather than their stated week. Everyone overestimates the
  next eight weeks.
- Set a floor that is already a real commitment. "Ten minutes" is not a floor for someone whose bad
  weeks have no ten minutes; "one verse before bed" might be.
- Add a practice per domain to make the plan feel complete. Three is a ceiling, not a target.
- Turn a streak into the measure. Streaks make missing a day feel like losing something, which is
  exactly the mechanism that ends plans.
- Skip the previous-failure question because the person seems motivated now. Motivation was there last
  time too.
- Build a plan when what was described is exhaustion, grief, or depression. That routes out first.

✅ **DO:**
- Attach practices to existing reliable anchors — a commute, a meal, putting a child to bed — rather
  than to a new slot that requires the day to cooperate.
- Make the floor slightly embarrassing in its smallness, and say why on the page, so the person does
  not silently upgrade it.
- Write the recovery rule in second person and plain words; it is the section that gets re-read at the
  moment it matters.
- Name the omissions, so the person does not carry a private sense that the plan is inadequate.
- Give Scripture by address and let them open their own Bible — reading it themselves is part of the
  formation.
- Put explicit permission to shrink or stop the plan in the review section. Plans without an exit get
  abandoned silently instead of revised honestly.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-01 (Clear Objective Statement):** the objective fixes a single focus and a survivable floor as
  the deliverable, ruling out the multi-domain aspirational list this genre defaults to.
- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** focus → previous failure → practices → floor →
  recovery → people → omissions, deliberately placing the failure analysis *before* practice selection
  so the new plan is shaped by it.
- **CM-02 (Constraint Specification):** the three-practice ceiling, the worst-week sizing rule, and the
  no-penance recovery rule are hard constraints, because each is a place where well-meant plans
  reliably fail.
- **ED-01 (Iterative Scaffolding):** the floor-plus-bonus structure scaffolds a practice at a level
  that survives disruption, with the review date providing the iteration point.
- **QA-01 (Self-Verification):** the four-failure check (built for the good week, too many practices,
  no floor, nobody knows) runs against the finished plan before it is handed over.
