# Spiritual Practices Designer

**Objective:** Design a small set of spiritual practices a believer can actually sustain — matched to
their temperament, season, capacity, and tradition, drawn from across the historic streams of Christian
practice without ranking them — with a floor that survives a bad week and an honest statement of what
each practice does and does not do.

> **Boundary guardrail.** Spiritual practices are formation, not treatment. They are not a substitute
> for medical or mental-health care, and they will not resolve depression, trauma, addiction, or an
> unsafe situation — those route to a licensed professional, a trusted pastor, or emergency services
> `[VERIFY: identify the correct service from an official source]`, and to `domain-psychology/`.
> Practices prescribed as a fix for distress reliably deepen the distress by adding failure to it.

**When to use:** You know what you want to grow in but not which practices fit you, or the practices
you have tried keep collapsing.

**When NOT to use:**
- You need the whole plan, not just practices — use `discipleship_personal_growth_plan.md`.
- Your constraints are the main obstacle — use `discipleship_life_constraints_adaptation.md`.
- Practices are collapsing and you do not know why — use `discipleship_stalled_growth_diagnostic.md`.
- You specifically want a Bible reading habit — use
  `domain-biblical-studies/learner-self-study/biblical_learner_bible_reading_habit_builder.md`.
- You want to be guided through meditating on a passage — use
  `domain-biblical-studies/sermon-devotional/biblical_meditation_reflection_guide.md`.

**Audience:** The individual believer, alone or with a mentor.

---

## Inputs / Context

**Required:**

1. **What you are hoping practices will serve.** In your own words — steadiness, attentiveness,
   knowing Scripture, generosity, connection, rest.
2. **What you have tried.** Practices attempted, and specifically how they collapsed: too long, wrong
   time, boring, guilt-inducing, no privacy, no energy left.
3. **Your week as it actually is.** Shape of the days, when you are alert, where the reliable gaps are,
   and what is genuinely non-negotiable.
4. **Your temperament, roughly.** Whether you think best in silence or in words, alone or with people,
   still or moving, in structure or in freedom.

**Optional:**

5. **Declared tradition (optional).** If supplied, practices foreground that stream's forms — daily
   office, quiet time, sacramental participation, examen, fasting, spontaneous prayer, lectio — while
   the shared core remains available.
6. **Physical and access constraints.** Health, disability, private space, literacy, language,
   childcare, transport, safety at home.

**If any required input is missing:** Ask clarifying questions before proceeding. Always ask how
previous practices collapsed — the collapse mechanism determines the design more than the goal does.

## Constraints

### Must

- Offer practices from **across the streams** — word-centred, contemplative, liturgical, charismatic,
  active/service, communal — described neutrally, so the person sees a real range rather than one
  tradition's list.
- Match to **temperament and the actual week**, not to what is most commended.
- Recommend **no more than three** practices, and say what the third one costs.
- Give every practice a **floor** doable on the worst week, and state the floor is the practice.
- State for each practice **what it does and what it does not do** — plainly, without overselling.
- Attach practices to **existing reliable anchors** in the day wherever possible.
- Name the **collapse mechanism** from the person's history and say what is different structurally.
- Include at least one practice that requires **no privacy, no quiet, and under five minutes**, for the
  weeks when nothing else is possible.

### Must Not

- Rank practices by spiritual value, or imply that contemplative practice is deeper than active
  service, that longer is better, or that morning is more faithful than evening.
- Prescribe a practice from a tradition the person does not hold without saying so and offering an
  alternative.
- Attach streaks, counts, completion percentages, or progress tracking framed as spiritual measurement.
- Quote Scripture text from memory; addresses only.
- Invent research on spiritual practices, claims about how long habits take to form, or historical
  claims about a practice's origin.
- Recommend a named app, book, devotional, or program — describe the kind and mark `[VERIFY]`.
- Assume privacy, silence, a spare hour, literacy, health, or a safe home.
- Present practices as a remedy for described distress.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)

- **Must:** name which stream a practice comes from when it is stream-specific; where the person's
  declared tradition has its own form of a practice, use that form; keep the shared core available
  throughout.
- **Must Not:** treat one stream's devotional pattern as normative Christian discipline, or imply that
  a person's tradition has left something essential out.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Understand the collapse

Take the practices that failed and name the mechanism precisely: duration, timing, energy, privacy,
boredom, guilt, isolation, or a season that ended. Most practice failure is a fit problem, not a
devotion problem, and saying so plainly matters.

### Step 2 — Map the real week

Identify when the person is actually alert, where reliable gaps sit, and what anchors already exist.
Mark the hostile times honestly — a practice placed in one will fail regardless of how good it is.

### Step 3 — Survey the streams

Present a short, neutral survey of practice families with examples of each, so the person sees the
range. Note which stream each belongs to and whether their tradition holds it.

| Stream | What it centres on | Example forms |
|---|---|---|
| Word-centred | Scripture read, studied, memorized | reading, study, memorization, meditation on a text |
| Contemplative | Attentive stillness before God | silence, examen, breath prayer, solitude |
| Liturgical | Shared, structured, time-shaped prayer | daily office, fixed-hour prayer, liturgical calendar, sacraments |
| Charismatic | Responsive, expectant, expressive prayer | spontaneous prayer, praying in the Spirit, worship, prayer ministry |
| Active | Formation through obedience and service | service, hospitality, generosity, justice work |
| Communal | Formation with and by others | shared meals, small group, confession, accountability, corporate worship |
| Abstinence | Formation by voluntary going-without | fasting, sabbath, simplicity, silence from media |

### Step 4 — Select and fit

Choose up to three practices, matched to temperament, week, and tradition. For each: the form, when it
happens, the anchor it attaches to, and why it suits this person specifically.

### Step 5 — Set floors and the five-minute fallback

Define each practice's floor. Then ensure at least one option needs no privacy, no quiet, and under
five minutes — for the weeks when the rest is impossible.

### Step 6 — Say what each does and does not do

For each practice, state honestly what it tends to form and what it will not do. Silence will not fix
loneliness. Fasting will not resolve a conflict. Service will not substitute for being known.

### Step 7 — Check the design

Run the four checks: **stream monoculture** (all three from one family), **privacy assumption**,
**good-week sizing**, and **repeat of the known collapse mechanism**.

## Verification

- [ ] Practices come from more than one stream, and each stream-specific form is labelled.
- [ ] At most three practices, each with a floor doable on the worst week.
- [ ] A five-minute, no-privacy fallback is present.
- [ ] Every practice has an honest "will not do" statement.
- [ ] The previous collapse mechanism is named and structurally addressed.
- [ ] No streaks, counts, or progress measures framed as spiritual attainment; no Scripture text quoted.

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Recommend silence and solitude to everyone. For an isolated person it can deepen the isolation, and
  for an exhausted parent it is a fantasy.
- Treat the morning quiet time as the default Christian practice. It is one tradition's form, and it
  fails badly for shift workers, parents of infants, and people who are not alert until midday.
- Prescribe fasting without asking about eating history, health, pregnancy, or medication. Route any
  concern to a physician `[VERIFY]`.
- Stack three practices from the same stream, which produces a narrow formation and a single point of
  failure.
- Frame a practice as the thing that will fix loneliness, anxiety, or a broken relationship.
- Assume the person can be alone, be quiet, read fluently, or be safe at home.

✅ **DO:**
- Name the collapse mechanism first and design against it specifically.
- Show the full range of streams even when the person has a declared tradition — knowing what exists is
  itself formative, and it is offered without recommendation.
- Anchor practices to something already reliable: a commute, a kettle boiling, a child's bedtime, a
  shift change.
- Include the five-minute fallback every time. It is what keeps a season from becoming a gap.
- State what a practice will not do, so the person does not conclude they practiced it wrong when it
  fails to solve something it was never going to solve.
- Offer a communal or active practice to someone whose life has no quiet in it, rather than a smaller
  version of a solitary one.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** collapse analysis → week mapping → stream survey →
  selection → floors → honest limits → check, so practices are selected against the real week rather
  than against an ideal of devotion.
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework):** the seven-stream survey crossed with temperament,
  timing, and access prevents the single-stream monoculture that makes practice sets fragile.
- **CM-02 (Constraint Specification):** the three-practice ceiling, the mandatory five-minute fallback,
  and the no-assumed-privacy rule are hard constraints targeting the ways practice design excludes
  people whose lives do not look like the designer's.
- **OC-03 (Markdown Table Specification):** the stream table and the does/doesn't-do table put the
  range and the honest limits in front of the person rather than burying them in prose.
- **QA-04 (Uncertainty Acknowledgment):** every practice carries an explicit "will not do," which
  protects the person from reading an unmet expectation as their own failure.
