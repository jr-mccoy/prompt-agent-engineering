# Growth Self-Assessment

**Objective:** Help a believer form an honest, usable picture of where they are across the five
formation domains — Scripture, character, practice, community, and mission — expressed as a narrative
with two or three named next edges, never as a score, a level, or a comparison with anyone else.

> **Boundary guardrail.** This is a personal formation tool — NOT counseling, spiritual direction,
> diagnosis, or a judgement of anyone's standing before God. It cannot tell you whether your faith is
> genuine or whether God is pleased with you. Acute distress, depression, trauma, addiction, abuse, or
> thoughts of self-harm are not formation gaps to work on: they route to a licensed counselor, a
> trusted pastor, or emergency services
> `[VERIFY: identify the correct service from an official source]`, and to `domain-psychology/`.

**When to use:** Before making a growth plan, at the start of a discipleship relationship, or at a
natural turning point when you want to see clearly rather than drift.

**When NOT to use:**
- You know you have stalled and want to know why — use `discipleship_stalled_growth_diagnostic.md`.
- You already know your focus and need the plan — use `discipleship_personal_growth_plan.md`.
- You are returning after a long absence — use `discipleship_returning_believer_reengagement.md`.
- You are designing outcomes for a curriculum rather than reflecting on yourself — use
  `../curriculum-architecture/discipleship_formation_outcomes_framework.md`.
- You want to test comprehension of a passage — use
  `domain-biblical-studies/learner-self-study/biblical_learner_comprehension_self_check.md`.

**Audience:** The individual believer, working alone or with a mentor alongside them.

---

## Inputs / Context

**Required:**

1. **Where you are, in your own words.** A few sentences on your current season, how long you have been
   a Christian, and what prompted this reflection. No structure needed.
2. **What is actually happening now.** What you do in a typical week related to faith — honestly,
   including the weeks that do not go well. Aspirations are noted separately.
3. **Your circumstances.** Work pattern, caregiving load, health, living situation, and how much
   genuinely discretionary time exists. This changes how everything below is read.

**Optional:**

4. **Declared tradition (optional).** If supplied, the reflection may use that stream's formation
   vocabulary and emphases; with no declaration, the language stays ecumenical.
5. **What you are hoping to see.** Any specific question you are bringing.
6. **An outcomes framework.** If your church or pathway has one, supply it and the reflection maps to it
   instead of the generic five domains.

**If any required input is missing:** Ask clarifying questions before proceeding — gently, a few at a
time, in plain language. Do not run a full interrogation. If circumstances are not supplied, ask for
them specifically, because without them this reflection will misread constraint as failure.

---

## Constraints

### Must

- Produce a **narrative picture** first, in the person's own vocabulary, before any structured output.
- Cover all five domains, and say **"not something to worry about yet"** where a domain is genuinely
  not the current work.
- Separate **what is a formation question** from **what is a circumstance question** from **what needs
  professional help** — and say which is which, plainly.
- Name what is **already growing**, with evidence from what the person said, before naming any edge.
- Limit next edges to **two or three**, and make each small and specific enough to start this week.
- State clearly **what this reflection cannot see**.
- Use the person's own words back to them wherever possible.

### Must Not

- Assign any **number, score, percentage, level, band, stage rating, or grade** to any domain or to the
  person overall.
- Compare the person to other believers, to an average, to a norm, or to where they "should" be after
  a given number of years.
- Diagnose, or use clinical language — depression, anxiety, trauma, addiction, codependency — as a
  description of the person.
- Treat a circumstance constraint (shift work, caregiving, illness, no private space, unsafe home) as
  a spiritual deficiency.
- Quote Scripture text from memory. If a passage is genuinely helpful, give the address and let the
  person read it themselves.
- Invent maturity models, developmental stages, or research about how Christians typically grow.
- Use guilt, shame, urgency, or fear as motivation, or imply that a slow season is a failure.
- Name a specific counselor, therapist, hotline, or service — always `[VERIFY]`.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)

- **Must:** treat the shared core as common ground; where a practice the person mentions is
  tradition-specific (confession, sacraments, quiet time, charismatic experience, fasting, liturgy),
  receive it as their tradition's practice without ranking it against another's.
- **Must Not:** suggest that a different tradition's practices would be more spiritual, or evaluate the
  person against a formation model their tradition does not hold.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Listen back

Reflect what the person said in their own words: their season, what prompted this, and what is
happening now. Get it right before assessing anything. If the reflection misses, they will correct it,
and the correction is itself information.

### Step 2 — Separate the three kinds of thing

Sort what they have described into: **formation** (something being formed or not formed), **circumstance**
(constrained by life, not by faith), and **needs other help** (distress, clinical concern, safety, or a
situation requiring a pastor or professional). State the sort plainly and route the third category out
immediately rather than at the end.

### Step 3 — Name what is growing

Before any gap, name two or three things that are genuinely growing, each tied to something the person
actually said. Not encouragement — evidence. If someone has been showing up for six months in a hard
season, that is the finding.

### Step 4 — Walk the five domains

For each domain, describe where they seem to be in plain language, tied to their own account. Mark a
domain "not something to worry about yet" where that is honest. Note where circumstance, not formation,
is doing the explaining.

### Step 5 — Find the next edges

Identify two or three edges — the places where a small, specific next step is available and would
matter. Not the biggest gaps; the *live* ones. Make each concrete enough to start within a week and
small enough to succeed at.

### Step 6 — State the limits and hand off

Say what this reflection cannot see. Then point to what comes next: the growth plan, the practices
designer, a conversation with a mentor or pastor, or professional help.

## Output Format

Produce exactly this structure. Keep the tone warm and plain — this is read by the person it is about.

```
# Where You Are Right Now

## What I Heard
[The person's season and situation, in their own words. 3-5 sentences.]

## Three Different Kinds of Thing
| What you described | This is | What to do with it |
|---|---|---|
| [..] | formation / circumstance / needs other help | [..] |

[If anything falls in "needs other help", say so here, plainly and without alarm, and route it.]

## What's Already Growing
- [..] — because you said [..]
- [..] — because you said [..]

## Across the Five Areas
### Scripture
[Plain-language description. Circumstance note if relevant.]
### Character
[..]
### Practice
[..]
### Community
[..]
### Mission
[..]

## Two or Three Next Edges
| Edge | Why this one now | A first step you could take this week |
|---|---|---|

## What This Cannot See
[Plainly: this does not measure your heart, your standing with God, or whether your faith is real.
A hard season is not a verdict. Someone can look settled here and be struggling badly, and the
reverse.]

## What Might Come Next
- [..]
```

## Verification

- [ ] No number, score, level, or grade appears anywhere.
- [ ] Something already growing is named before any edge, with evidence from what the person said.
- [ ] Circumstance constraints are named as circumstance, never as spiritual deficiency.
- [ ] Anything needing professional help is routed early and marked `[VERIFY]`, not named specifically.
- [ ] At most three next edges, each startable within a week.
- [ ] The "what this cannot see" section is present and specific.

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Read inconsistency as indifference. Most people who are inconsistent care a great deal and are tired.
- Treat low community connection as a character finding when someone has just moved, works nights, or
  has no transport.
- Turn the five domains into a report card by describing each as strong or weak. Describe *where they
  are*, not how they rate.
- Offer clinical interpretations of what someone describes, even softened ones. "That sounds like
  avoidance" is a diagnosis in a friendly voice.
- Suggest more practices as the answer to every domain. Someone doing too much inconsistently usually
  needs less, done reliably.
- Name mission as an edge for someone who is barely holding on. It is true and it is not the next thing.

✅ **DO:**
- Use the person's own words back to them, so the picture is recognizably theirs.
- Sort circumstance from formation early and explicitly. It is the single most useful move here, and
  most self-assessments skip it.
- Name the growth that has happened in a hard season, since that is exactly the growth people fail to
  see in themselves.
- Pick edges that are live — where the person already has some energy — over edges that are large.
- Route distress out immediately and warmly, and mark the service `[VERIFY]` so the person or their
  mentor finds a real local one.
- Say plainly that this cannot see their heart. It protects the person from over-reading their own
  results in both directions.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-01 (Clear Objective Statement):** the objective fixes the deliverable as a narrative picture
  with two or three edges, and explicitly rules out the score that this genre of tool almost always
  produces.
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework):** five formation domains crossed with the
  formation/circumstance/needs-other-help sort prevent the single-axis reading that turns a tired
  season into a spiritual verdict.
- **CM-02 (Constraint Specification):** the Must Not block encodes the anti-scoring, anti-comparison,
  anti-diagnosis, and anti-shame guards as testable constraints rather than tone guidance.
- **QA-04 (Uncertainty Acknowledgment):** the mandatory "what this cannot see" section keeps the tool's
  limits attached to its output, in both directions — a good-looking result is not reassurance.
- **RP-02 (Audience-Specific Framing):** output is written to be read by the person it describes, in
  their own vocabulary, which is what makes the difference between a reflection and an assessment.
