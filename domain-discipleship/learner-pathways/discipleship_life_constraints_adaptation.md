# Life Constraints Adaptation

**Objective:** Rebuild a discipleship pathway around the real conditions of a person's life — surfacing
the assumptions the standard pathway makes about time, privacy, literacy, health, money, safety, and
access, and replacing each with something that forms the same thing under the conditions that actually
exist.

> **Boundary guardrail.** Some constraints are not logistics. An unsafe home, coercive control, an
> exploitative work arrangement, or a health crisis is not a scheduling problem to design around, and
> naming it as one causes harm. Those route to appropriate professionals and, where there is danger, to
> authorities and emergency services `[VERIFY: identify the correct service from an official source]`,
> plus `domain-psychology/` and `domain-psy-ops/personal-defense/`. Adapt the pathway; do not
> accommodate the danger.

**When to use:** A pathway, plan, or set of practices does not fit someone's life — and the answer is
to change the pathway, not to ask the person to change their life.

**When NOT to use:**
- Capacity is fine and the issue is choosing practices — use `discipleship_spiritual_practices_designer.md`.
- You need the plan itself — use `discipleship_personal_growth_plan.md`.
- The issue is specifically meeting frequency and channel with a mentor — use
  `../pairing-and-relationship/discipleship_cadence_and_rhythm_design.md`.
- You are designing the pathway from scratch — use
  `../curriculum-architecture/discipleship_curriculum_architecture.md` and build the constraints in.

**Audience:** The individual, a mentor, or a program lead adapting for a specific person.

---

## Inputs / Context

**Required:**

1. **The pathway as written.** What the standard plan, curriculum, or practice set expects — supplied
   in a `<pathway>` block, or described.
2. **The actual conditions.** What is true about this person's life: work pattern, caregiving,
   health, housing, money, transport, language, literacy, technology access, safety.
3. **What has already failed.** Which parts of the pathway have been attempted and how they broke.

**Optional:**

4. **Declared tradition (optional).** Some adaptations touch tradition-specific expectations —
   attendance at a service, sacramental participation, fasting, set prayer times — and those are
   negotiated within the tradition rather than overridden.
5. **What matters most to keep.** If the person has a view on what they would least like to lose.
6. **Available support.** Anyone who could carry part of the load.

**If any required input is missing:** Ask clarifying questions before proceeding. Ask about conditions
specifically and concretely — "is there anywhere in your home you can be alone?" surfaces what a general
question about time never will.

---

## Constraints

### Must

- **Surface the pathway's hidden assumptions explicitly** before adapting anything: assumed time,
  privacy, literacy, health, transport, technology, money, childcare, safety, and language.
- For each assumption the person cannot meet, **preserve the formation and change the form** — say what
  the element was forming, then find another way to form it.
- Treat the constraint as **a fact about the world, never a deficiency in the person**, in every line
  of output.
- Include options requiring **no privacy, no silence, no reading, and no fixed time**, since these are
  the four assumptions that exclude the most people.
- Distinguish **temporary** constraints from **enduring** ones, and design differently for each.
- Name what genuinely **cannot be preserved** under these conditions, honestly, rather than pretending
  every element has an equivalent.
- Where a constraint indicates danger or exploitation, **route it out** rather than designing around it.

### Must Not

- Suggest the person create time, get up earlier, or find privacy, when the stated conditions rule it
  out. Waking earlier is not available to someone finishing a night shift or feeding an infant.
- Frame the adaptation as a lesser, reduced, or concessionary version. It is the pathway for this
  person.
- Imply that the constraints are a season to be endured until real discipleship can begin.
- Assume literacy, fluency in the pathway's language, smartphone or data access, or the money for
  materials, transport, or childcare.
- Quote Scripture text from memory; addresses only, with an audio option noted where reading is a
  constraint.
- Invent research about discipleship among any population, or statistics about access.
- Name a specific app, service, church, or resource — describe the kind and mark `[VERIFY]`.
- Treat an unsafe home or a coercive relationship as a logistical constraint.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)

- **Must:** where a tradition expects something the person cannot do — attendance, sacramental
  participation, fasting, fixed-hour prayer — name it as that tradition's expectation and suggest the
  person raise the adaptation with their own leaders rather than deciding it unilaterally here.
- **Must Not:** override a tradition's practice on the person's behalf, or imply that a tradition with
  fixed expectations is failing them.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Screen for danger

Check the stated conditions for an unsafe home, coercive control, exploitative work, or an untreated
health crisis. If any is present, name it plainly, route it, and say clearly that it is not something
to design around. Adapt only what remains.

### Step 2 — Surface the hidden assumptions

Walk the pathway and list every assumption it makes. Be concrete and exhaustive — this step is the
substance of the work.

| Assumption | Where the pathway relies on it |
|---|---|
| Time (a block of n minutes) | [..] |
| Privacy (able to be alone) | [..] |
| Silence | [..] |
| Literacy and reading fluency | [..] |
| Language | [..] |
| Health, energy, mobility | [..] |
| Transport | [..] |
| Technology and data | [..] |
| Money (materials, travel, childcare) | [..] |
| Childcare | [..] |
| Predictable schedule | [..] |
| Safety | [..] |

### Step 3 — Test each against the conditions

Mark each assumption met, partly met, or not met — and say what specifically breaks when it is not met.

### Step 4 — Preserve the formation, change the form

For each broken assumption, state what the pathway element was forming, then design an alternative that
forms the same thing. A written reflection becomes a spoken one. Solitary prayer becomes prayer on a
commute. A reading plan becomes an audio plan. A weekly meeting becomes asynchronous messages.

### Step 5 — Build the no-privacy, no-reading, no-fixed-time set

Assemble a small set of options that require none of the four most exclusionary assumptions. Every
adapted pathway needs this layer, whether or not the person's constraints demand it, because conditions
change without warning.

### Step 6 — Separate temporary from enduring

Mark each constraint temporary or enduring. Temporary constraints get a lighter adaptation and a review
date. Enduring ones get a genuine redesign — not a holding pattern, because a holding pattern implies
real discipleship is waiting elsewhere.

### Step 7 — State the honest losses

Name what cannot be preserved under these conditions. Say what would restore it, if anything. Do not
manufacture an equivalent for something that has none.

## Output Format

Produce exactly this structure. Written so the person reads it as their pathway, not a downgrade.

```
# Your Pathway, Built for Your Life

## First
[If danger, coercion, or exploitation is in view: name it, route it, and say plainly it is not
something to design around. Otherwise: one line confirming these constraints are facts to design
with, not failures to apologize for.]

## What the Standard Pathway Assumes
| Assumption | Where it's relied on | Met for you? | What breaks |
|---|---|---|---|

## Your Pathway
| Original element | What it was forming | Your version | Why this forms the same thing |
|---|---|---|---|

## For the Hardest Weeks
[Options needing no privacy, no silence, no reading, and no fixed time. At least three.]

## Temporary or Enduring
| Constraint | Temporary / enduring | Design response | Review |
|---|---|---|---|

## What Can't Be Preserved Right Now
| What | Why | What would restore it |
|---|---|---|

## To Raise With Your Church or Tradition
[Any adaptation touching a tradition-specific expectation, phrased as a question for their own
leaders rather than a decision made here.]

## Scripture
[Addresses only. Note where an audio version would serve better than reading.]
```

## Verification

- [ ] Danger, coercion, and exploitation are screened first and routed, not designed around.
- [ ] The hidden-assumption list is concrete and covers time, privacy, literacy, language, health,
      transport, technology, money, childcare, schedule, and safety.
- [ ] Every adaptation states what the original was forming and why the replacement forms the same thing.
- [ ] A no-privacy, no-reading, no-fixed-time set is present with at least three options.
- [ ] Nothing frames the adapted pathway as lesser, reduced, or provisional.
- [ ] Genuine losses are named honestly rather than given manufactured equivalents.

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Suggest waking earlier, using a lunch break, or finding a quiet corner without checking those exist.
  They frequently do not, and suggesting them tells the person their life is the problem.
- Assume reading. Limited literacy, dyslexia, vision impairment, and second-language reading are all
  common, and a reading-shaped pathway silently excludes all of them.
- Assume a smartphone, data, or a private device. Shared and monitored devices are ordinary.
- Treat the adapted version as a stopgap until life calms down. For many people it will not, and the
  implication is corrosive.
- Design around an unsafe home. That is not a constraint to accommodate.
- Invent an equivalent for something that has none — sustained silence has no five-minute substitute,
  and saying so is more honest than pretending.

✅ **DO:**
- Ask concrete questions: is there anywhere you can be alone, do you have a device only you use, is
  there money for the book. General questions about time miss all of it.
- Name what each element was forming before replacing it. That is what keeps adaptation from becoming
  dilution.
- Build the no-privacy, no-reading, no-fixed-time layer for everyone, since conditions change suddenly.
- Distinguish temporary from enduring, and design enduring constraints as the real pathway rather than
  a waiting room.
- Route tradition-specific adaptations back to the person's own leaders as a question, rather than
  settling them here.
- Note audio alternatives for Scripture wherever reading is a constraint, without making it a
  concession.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** danger screen → assumption surfacing → testing →
  re-forming → fallback layer → duration → honest losses, so adaptation is driven by an explicit
  assumption audit rather than by guesswork about what will help.
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework):** twelve assumption axes (time, privacy, silence,
  literacy, language, health, transport, technology, money, childcare, schedule, safety) surface the
  exclusions that a time-only analysis of capacity always misses.
- **CM-02 (Constraint Specification):** the Must Not block bans the genre's characteristic failures —
  telling people to make time, framing adaptation as reduction, and treating danger as logistics.
- **OC-03 (Markdown Table Specification):** the assumption table and the element-to-adaptation table
  force each replacement to declare what it preserves, which is what prevents adaptation from becoming
  dilution.
- **RP-02 (Audience-Specific Framing):** the output is written to be read by the person as *their*
  pathway, not as an accommodation granted to them — a framing difference that carries real pastoral
  weight.
