# Returning Believer Re-engagement

**Objective:** Help someone returning to faith, church, or both after a long absence build a re-entry
plan that takes seriously *why they left* — distinguishing hurt from drift from doubt from
circumstance — and paces the return so that belonging never depends on explaining themselves,
performing recovery, or resolving their questions first.

> **Boundary guardrail.** Some people left because they were harmed — by a leader, a community, or a
> theology used against them. Spiritual abuse, coercive control, and the aftermath of assault or
> exploitation are not drift to be reversed, and they are not this prompt's to resolve. They route to
> a licensed counselor with relevant expertise, and where a crime may have occurred, to appropriate
> authorities `[VERIFY: identify the correct service from an official source]`, plus
> `domain-psychology/` and `domain-psy-ops/personal-defense/`. Return is never the goal in those cases;
> safety is.

**When to use:** You are coming back after months or years away, or you are walking with someone who
is, and you want a re-entry that is honest rather than a reset that pretends nothing happened.

**When NOT to use:**
- You never left but have stalled — use `discipleship_stalled_growth_diagnostic.md`.
- You are exploring Christian faith rather than returning to it — use
  `domain-biblical-studies/ministry-contexts/biblical_ministry_seeker_intro_to_bible.md`.
- The core issue is a specific unresolved intellectual question — use
  `domain-biblical-studies/learner-self-study/biblical_learner_honest_questions_doubt_explorer.md`.
- You already have a plan and want practices — use `discipleship_personal_growth_plan.md`.

**Audience:** The returning person themselves, or a mentor or friend walking with them.

---

## Inputs / Context

**Required:**

1. **How long, and what "away" meant.** Away from church but praying, away from everything, or somewhere
   in between. These are different returns.
2. **Why you left — as much as you want to say.** Whatever is offered is enough. It is explicitly fine
   to say "I don't know" or "I'd rather not go into it."
3. **What is drawing you back now.** The pull, in your own words.
4. **What you are afraid of about coming back.** Judgement, questions, being asked to explain, being
   expected to pick up where you left off, meeting particular people.

**Optional:**

5. **Declared tradition (optional).** Including whether you are returning to the tradition you left or
   deliberately looking at a different one — both are ordinary and neither needs defending.
6. **What is unresolved.** Questions or objections you have not settled and may not settle.
7. **Who knows you are considering this.**

**If any required input is missing:** Ask gently and only for what is needed. Do not press on why they
left. If they decline to say, proceed — the plan can be built without it, and pressing reproduces the
interrogation they are afraid of.

---

## Constraints

### Must

- Take the **reasons for leaving seriously** and address them before any return step is proposed.
- Sort the reasons into **hurt / drift / doubt / circumstance / harm**, since each needs a materially
  different return.
- Make clear that **belonging does not depend** on resolving doubts, explaining absence, confessing
  anything publicly, or resuming a previous level of involvement.
- Pace the return with **low-exposure first steps** and an explicit right to stop, slow down, or leave
  again at any point.
- Provide **scripts** for the moments the person is dreading — "where have you been?", running into
  someone, being asked to serve immediately.
- Name what is **legitimately unresolved** and confirm it may stay unresolved through the return.
- Where the reason was harm, **route it out** and do not treat return as the objective.

### Must Not

- Frame the absence as backsliding, falling away, or a period requiring repentance before return.
- Ask the person to justify, explain, or account for the time away.
- Rush to reconciliation with people or institutions that caused harm, or suggest forgiveness as a
  precondition or a first step.
- Assume the person should return to the same church, tradition, or role — or to church at all as the
  first move.
- Treat unresolved doubt as an obstacle to be cleared before belonging.
- Quote Scripture text from memory, and do not deploy passages about returning or the prodigal as
  emotional leverage.
- Invent statistics or research about dechurching, deconstruction, or return rates.
- Name a specific church, counselor, service, or program — mark `[VERIFY]`.
- Use urgency, guilt, fear, or love-bombing to accelerate return.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)

- **Must:** treat returning to a different tradition, or remaining undecided about tradition, as
  ordinary; where a tradition has formal re-entry practices (confession, reaffirmation, membership
  restoration, catechesis), name them as that stream's practice and note others do not require them.
- **Must Not:** imply that returning to the tradition of origin is the faithful option, or that any
  formal re-entry step is universally required.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Screen for harm

Check what was described for spiritual abuse, coercive control, exploitation, assault, or a leader's
misconduct. If any is in view, name it, route it, and state plainly that return is not the goal here
and that they owe no one a return. Continue only with what they want to continue with.

### Step 2 — Sort the reasons

Sort what was offered into hurt, drift, doubt, circumstance, or harm — noting that most returns involve
more than one, and that the person may not know. Say what each implies for the shape of the return.

### Step 3 — Name what is drawing them back

Reflect the pull in their own words. This is the actual engine of the return, and it should shape the
first steps more than any diagnosis of the leaving does.

### Step 4 — Address the fears with scripts

Take each stated fear and give a concrete handling, including words they can use. Cover at minimum:
being asked where they have been, being asked to serve or commit immediately, running into someone from
before, and being asked what they now believe.

### Step 5 — Build a paced re-entry

Propose a sequence beginning with the **lowest-exposure step available** — which is often not a Sunday
service. Private Scripture reading, one conversation with one safe person, a midweek gathering, a
different congregation, or online attendance may all precede it. State the right to stop or repeat any
step indefinitely.

### Step 6 — Hold the unresolved

List what remains unresolved and confirm explicitly that these may stay open. Offer a route for the
questions they want to work on and permission to leave the rest alone.

### Step 7 — Name what this cannot fix

State plainly what a re-entry plan does not do: it does not repair the institution that failed them, it
does not resolve their questions, and it does not guarantee the reception they get.

## Output Format

Produce exactly this structure. Warm, unhurried, and free of any implication that they owe anyone this.

```
# Coming Back — At Your Pace

## First
[If harm is in view: name it, route it, and say plainly that they owe no one a return and that safety
comes first. If not: one sentence saying there is nothing here they need to justify.]

## What the Time Away Was
| What you described | This looks like | What it means for coming back |
|---|---|---|
| [..] | hurt / drift / doubt / circumstance / harm | [..] |

## What's Drawing You Back
[Their own words, reflected.]

## The Things You're Dreading
| What you're afraid of | How it usually goes | Words you could use |
|---|---|---|
| "Where have you been?" | [..] | "[a short, complete, non-explaining answer]" |
| Being asked to serve straight away | [..] | "[..]" |
| Running into [person] | [..] | "[..]" |
| Being asked what you believe now | [..] | "[..]" |

## A Pace That's Yours
| Step | What it involves | Exposure | When you're ready — no timeline |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [lowest-exposure option, often not a Sunday service] | low | [..] |

**You can stop at any step, stay on one indefinitely, or go back. None of that is failure.**

## What Stays Unresolved
- [..] — and it can stay open. You do not have to settle this to belong.

## Scripture, If You Want It
[Addresses only. Nothing about prodigals or returning unless they raised it themselves.]

## What This Plan Can't Do
[It cannot fix the church that hurt you, answer your questions, or promise how people will react.]
```

## Verification

- [ ] Harm is screened first, and where present, return is explicitly not framed as the goal.
- [ ] The reasons for leaving are sorted before any return step is proposed.
- [ ] Nothing in the output asks the person to explain, justify, or account for their absence.
- [ ] The first step is genuinely low-exposure and is not assumed to be a Sunday service.
- [ ] Unresolved questions are confirmed as allowed to stay unresolved.
- [ ] No prodigal or returning passages are deployed unless the person raised them; no service is named.

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Treat the absence as the problem to be explained. For many people the absence was the healthiest
  available response to their situation.
- Reach for the prodigal son. It casts the person as the one who squandered and needs to come home
  contrite, and for someone who left because they were harmed it is close to cruel.
- Make Sunday morning the first step. It is the highest-exposure, highest-scrutiny hour in church life.
- Propose reconciliation with the people involved early, or at all unless the person asks. Their
  timeline is theirs.
- Treat returning to a different tradition as second-best or as unfinished business.
- Require doubts to be resolved, or offer apologetics unprompted. Belonging is not a reward for
  intellectual settlement.

✅ **DO:**
- Give an actual sentence for "where have you been?" — short, complete, and explaining nothing. That
  one script removes more friction than any amount of encouragement.
- Sort hurt from drift early, because a drift return can be brisk and a hurt return cannot.
- Let the pull they described set the direction. Someone returning because they miss singing needs a
  different first step from someone returning because they want their children to know the faith.
- Say explicitly and more than once that stopping again is allowed and is not failure.
- Name the unresolved questions as permitted to stay unresolved, in writing, since that is usually the
  thing they most fear will be inspected.
- Say what the plan cannot do, especially about how people will react — the person cannot control the
  reception and should not be set up to expect a good one.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** the harm screen precedes the reason sort, which
  precedes any re-entry step, so the plan cannot propose return while an unaddressed harm is in view.
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework):** the five-way sort (hurt, drift, doubt, circumstance,
  harm) yields materially different returns, replacing the single "came back / fell away" axis that
  makes most re-engagement advice unusable.
- **CM-02 (Constraint Specification):** the Must Not block bans the genre's characteristic harms —
  backsliding framing, demanded explanation, rushed reconciliation, prodigal leverage — as hard
  constraints.
- **RP-02 (Audience-Specific Framing):** output is written for the returning person to read directly,
  including verbatim scripts for the encounters they are dreading, which is the practical deliverable.
- **QA-04 (Uncertainty Acknowledgment):** unresolved questions are held open by design, and the closing
  section states what the plan cannot do — including that it cannot promise how people will receive them.
