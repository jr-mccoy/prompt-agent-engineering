# Work and Vocation Module

**Objective:** Design a discipleship module that forms how a person understands and inhabits their
work — paid, unpaid, chosen, or endured — without ranking ministry above ordinary labour, without
telling anyone their job is their platform, and without letting the language of calling become a reason
to stay somewhere that is harming them.

> **Boundary guardrail.** This designs formation, not career advice, employment law, or workplace
> advocacy. Harassment, discrimination, unsafe conditions, wage theft, and unlawful treatment route to
> qualified help and, where relevant, to legal or regulatory channels
> `[VERIFY: identify the correct service from an official source]` — never to a mentor's judgment.
> Career decisions belong to the participant; a mentor holds the question open, they do not answer it.

**When to use:** You are building the work-and-vocation module inside a discipleship curriculum, or a
mentee's work has become the live question and one conversation will not hold it.

**When NOT to use:**
- The participant is unemployed and in the acute aftermath of job loss — start with
  `domain-personal-development/prompts/life-transitions/lifetransition_job_loss_recovery_plan.md`.
- The participant's capacity is the real constraint (caregiving, disability, shift patterns) — use
  `../learner-pathways/discipleship_life_constraints_adaptation.md`.
- You need career-transition or repositioning machinery — that is
  `domain-personal-development/career-transformation/`, in a non-religious frame.
- You need a theology of work synthesized across the canon — use
  `domain-biblical-studies/theology-research/biblical_topical_theology_synthesis.md`.

**Audience:** Curriculum designers and experienced mentors building the work module a pathway needs,
including in workplace and marketplace groups.

---

## Inputs / Context

**Required:**

1. **Where this module sits.** Stage, prerequisites, and session count.
2. **The actual work in the room.** The honest range: shift work, care work, professional work, manual
   trades, self-employment, gig and platform work, unpaid domestic and caring labour, study,
   unemployment, retirement, and work that is degrading or unsafe. Name what is really there.
3. **The degree of choice participants have.** Whether people in this group can realistically change
   jobs. A module built on vocational choice is cruel where there is none.
4. **What this community already teaches about work.** Existing assumptions — that ministry outranks
   ordinary work, that success signals blessing, that ambition is suspect, that rest is laziness.

**Optional:**

5. **Declared tradition (optional).** May foreground a vocation-and-station framing, a
   monastic-and-simplicity emphasis, a marketplace-ministry emphasis, a Sabbath practice, or a
   common-good framing. Contested specifics stay attributed.
6. **Sector concentration.** If most participants share an industry, its specific pressures matter more
   than generic ones.
7. **Local labour realities.** Informal economies, migration status affecting work, or high
   unemployment that makes "find better work" meaningless.

**If any required input is missing:** Ask before proceeding. In particular, do not assume choice. The
most common failure in this module is designing for a professional with options and delivering it to
people without any.

---

## Constraints

### Must

- Treat **all honest work as legitimate**, including unpaid care, domestic labour, study, and work done
  only for money.
- Include the participant who is **unemployed, underemployed, retired, or unable to work**, with
  content that is genuinely for them and not a footnote.
- Reference Scripture **by address only**, translation named for verification.
- Distinguish **calling, vocation, job, career, and role** as different things, and say which the module
  is using where.
- Address **work that is genuinely bad** — boring, degrading, exploitative, or harmful — without
  spiritualizing endurance and without telling anyone to leave.
- Cover **rest** as a formation practice, not a productivity technique, and note that traditions differ
  on Sabbath.
- Cover **ambition, comparison, and identity-through-achievement** as formation questions rather than
  character faults.
- Attach a practice to each session that is **doable inside the participant's actual working week**,
  including a week with no discretionary time.
- Route harassment, discrimination, unsafe conditions, wage theft, and immigration-linked employment
  vulnerability **out** `[VERIFY]`.

### Must Not

- Quote Scripture text from memory or assert verse wording.
- Invent workplace research, vocation statistics, employment data, or studies about faith and work.
- Rank vocations spiritually, or imply that ministry, missions, or "full-time Christian work" is a
  higher calling.
- Tell a participant what job to take, leave, or pursue, or frame a career decision as a matter of
  obedience with one right answer.
- Use calling or submission language to justify remaining in an exploitative, unsafe, or abusive
  workplace.
- State employment law, worker rights, visa conditions, or benefits rules. Mark `[VERIFY]` and route to
  qualified advice and `domain-legal/`.
- Present workplace evangelism obligations, or design practices that would put a participant's job or
  immigration status at risk.
- Treat unemployment as a spiritual condition, or unpaid domestic labour as non-work.
- Name a specific employer, recruiter, career service, or organization.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)

- **Must:** note where traditions diverge — vocation as station versus vocation as personal calling,
  Sabbath observance and its day and shape, the standing of monastic and simplicity commitments,
  marketplace-ministry framings, attitudes to ambition and wealth creation, and how the dignity of
  ordinary labour is grounded. Attribute positions to identifiable streams and let a declared tradition
  foreground its own.
- **Must Not:** present one stream's account of calling as the biblical one, or let a Sabbath practice
  be imposed as universal when its shape and day are genuinely disputed.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Fix the room's real working life

Write the honest spread of work the participants do, including the unpaid and the unavailable. Then
state the choice constraint: can these people change jobs? Everything downstream depends on the answer,
and getting it wrong turns formation into accusation.

### Step 2 — Define the vocabulary before using it

Set out what this module means by calling, vocation, job, career, and role — and say explicitly which
questions the module will not answer, chiefly "what should I do with my life?" That question is the
participant's; the module gives them better tools to hold it, not an answer.

### Step 3 — Work the five territories

| Territory | The formation question under it |
|---|---|
| The work itself | What am I actually doing all day, and what does it make of me? |
| The people | Who do I have power over, and who has power over me? |
| The reward | What am I working *for*, and what happens to me when it does not come? |
| The rest | What do I do when I stop, and what does stopping expose? |
| The limits | What will I not do for money, advancement, or approval? |

For each: the Scripture addresses, the distortion common in this group, and the practice.

### Step 4 — Design for the participant without choice

Write the strand of the module for people whose work cannot change: the shift worker, the carer, the
person in a job market with nothing else, the person whose visa is tied to an employer. This strand must
offer something real — presence, craft, dignity, limits, endurance named honestly as endurance — without
telling them their situation is God's plan for them. Neither resignation nor false hope.

### Step 5 — Design for the participant with choice

Write the strand for people who have options: discernment without divine-signal-hunting, ambition
examined rather than condemned, the cost of a decision to the people around them, and the difference
between a closed door and a hard one. Say plainly that the module will not tell them the answer.

### Step 6 — Build the sessions

Lay out sessions against the count. For each: the question, Scripture by address, territory, practice,
and which strand it primarily serves — with a note on how it lands on the other strand. Route exegesis
to `domain-biblical-studies/exegesis-interpretation/`.

### Step 7 — Write the referral and safety edges

Enumerate what leaves this module: harassment, discrimination, unsafe conditions, wage theft, bullying,
immigration-linked vulnerability, burnout that has become a health matter, and workplace conflict that
has become a legal matter. Destination kind `[VERIFY]` plus the mentor's words for each.

### Step 8 — Stress-test

Check against four standing failure modes: the sacred-secular ranking crept back in; the module assumed
choice; endurance was spiritualized where the situation was actually unjust; the module became career
coaching. Name the early signal and the fix for each.

## Verification

- [ ] Every Scripture reference is an address only — no verse text is quoted or asserted.
- [ ] No vocation is ranked spiritually above another anywhere in the module.
- [ ] The unemployed, unpaid, retired, and unable-to-work participant has real content, not a footnote.
- [ ] The no-choice strand offers something concrete without framing an unjust situation as providence.
- [ ] No career decision is made for the participant, and no decision is framed as obedience.
- [ ] No employment law, right, or visa condition is stated; all such points are `[VERIFY]`.

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Let "your workplace is your mission field" become an obligation. For many participants it is also a
  risk — to their job, their standing, or their visa.
- Use calling language to make leaving feel like disobedience. That is how people stay in harmful jobs
  for years.
- Build discernment content around looking for signs. It reliably produces paralysis and, when a
  decision goes badly, a crisis of faith about a job.
- Treat unpaid caring work as background to the real topic. For a large share of participants it *is*
  the work.
- Design a rest practice that assumes a weekend. Shift workers, carers, and the self-employed do not
  have one, and Sabbath's day is contested anyway.
- Turn the module into career coaching with Scripture attached. There are better tools for the career
  question and they are not this domain's job.

✅ **DO:**
- State the choice constraint at the top, so the whole module is built for the people actually in it.
- Write both strands and check each with someone from the other. The no-choice strand written by
  someone with choices usually reads as consolation.
- Name endurance as endurance when that is what a situation requires, and say plainly that enduring
  something unjust does not make it just.
- Put the limits territory in — "what I will not do" is where this module does its most durable work.
- Route the legal and safety edges out immediately and completely.
- Leave "what should I do with my life?" open, and say so out loud.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-01 (Clear Objective Statement):** the objective names the three failures the module is designed
  against — sacred-secular ranking, mandated workplace platform, and calling-as-reason-to-stay.
- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** vocabulary is defined and the choice constraint is
  fixed before any session is written, so the module cannot be built for a participant who is not there.
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework):** five territories crossed with two strands prevents
  the single-axis "find your calling" module that serves only the participant with options.
- **CM-02 (Constraint Specification):** the Must Not block encodes the load-bearing prohibitions — no
  spiritual ranking of work, no career direction, no calling-language justification for staying in harm.
- **QA-05 (Citation Requirements):** Scripture by address with a named translation, and every legal,
  statistical, and service claim marked `[VERIFY]`.
