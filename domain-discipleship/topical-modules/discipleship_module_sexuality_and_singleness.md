# Sexuality and Singleness Module

**Objective:** Design a module that lets a discipleship group engage sexuality, singleness, marriage,
desire, and the body honestly — presenting where Christian traditions genuinely disagree and attributing
each position, while refusing sexual-history disclosure, refusing to treat any participant as a problem
the module exists to fix, and routing abuse, compulsion, and distress out the moment they appear.

> **STRONG-GUARD — boundary guardrail.** This is the highest-risk module in the domain, and the risk is
> not doctrinal, it is human. Sexual abuse, assault, coercion, exploitation, compulsive behaviour,
> distress about identity, and self-harm all route immediately to licensed professionals and, where
> there is danger, to appropriate authorities and emergency services
> `[VERIFY: identify the correct service from an official source]`. A lay mentor does not counsel,
> assess, diagnose, or attempt to change anyone's sexuality — under any framing, including a spiritual
> one. Any practice resembling orientation-change effort is out of scope for this domain and is not to
> be designed, referenced, or implied. Where the participant is a minor, the safeguarding policy
> governs everything here.

**When to use:** You are designing this module for an existing curriculum and you have decided to
handle it deliberately rather than let it arrive by accident in a one-to-one conversation.

**When NOT to use:**
- Something has just been disclosed and you need to respond now — use
  `../session-and-lesson/discipleship_hard_conversation_navigation.md`.
- You want a position on a contested question decided — this module does not do that, and
  `domain-biblical-studies/theology-research/` is where multi-view work belongs.
- You want the traditions compared on a practice for a learner's own study — use
  `domain-biblical-studies/learner-self-study/biblical_learner_compare_traditions_on_practice.md`.
- The participants are minors and no safeguarding policy is in place — build
  `../program-operations/discipleship_safeguarding_and_conduct_policy.md` first.
- Anyone involved is in distress or in the aftermath of harm — that is `domain-psychology/` and a
  licensed professional, not a module.

**Audience:** Experienced curriculum designers and discipleship leads. This module is not appropriate
for a first-time mentor to design or deliver alone.

---

## Inputs / Context

**Required:**

1. **Who is in the room, and their ages.** Adults only, mixed, or minors. Minors change everything
   about what may be discussed, how, and with what consent and oversight.
2. **The delivery setting.** One-to-one, same-sex group, mixed group, or written material. Some of this
   content is unsafe in some settings and the design must say which.
3. **The community's stated position, and whether it is stated.** Whether the sponsoring body holds a
   defined position on contested questions, whether participants know it before joining, and whether
   dissent is safe. If participants do not know the position before they arrive, that is the first
   thing to fix.
4. **The referral landscape.** What professional help actually exists locally and through the program,
   described rather than named from memory.

**Optional:**

5. **Declared tradition (optional).** May foreground its own teaching on marriage, celibacy, singleness,
   contraception, divorce and remarriage, and same-sex relationships. Declaring it lets the module state
   that teaching *as that tradition's* — it never converts the teaching into an unmarked universal, and
   alternatives stay visible.
6. **Known pastoral history in this community.** Prior harm, a scandal, or a public conflict changes what
   can be safely opened and how.
7. **Participant-stated boundaries.** Anything participants have said in advance about what they do not
   want raised.

**If any required input is missing:** Ask before proceeding. If item 3 cannot be answered — if nobody
can say what this community holds or whether disagreeing is safe — stop and say so. A module on this
topic delivered into an unstated position is a trap for the participant.

## Constraints

### Must

- State the community's position, its status (settled teaching, working consensus, open question), and
  **whether disagreement is safe**, at the top of the module and before any session.
- Present contested questions as **contested**, naming the positions and attributing each to
  identifiable streams, with the strongest form of each rather than a caricature.
- Make **participation voluntary at the granularity of a session**, with a stated way to opt out of any
  session without explanation and without consequence.
- Give **singleness content that is not about waiting**. Single participants of every kind — never
  married, widowed, divorced, celibate by conviction, celibate by circumstance — get formation content
  in their own right.
- Include **the body, desire, shame, loneliness, and friendship** as formation territory, not only
  behaviour rules.
- Place the **referral and safety edges before the content**, not after it: disclosure of abuse,
  compulsion, distress, and self-harm all route out.
- State the **confidentiality limits before any session opens**, referencing the covenant and the
  mentor's standing boundaries framework.
- Say explicitly that **nobody is required to disclose anything** about their history, desires,
  orientation, or practice, and design every question so that it can be answered without doing so.
- Where minors are present, subordinate the entire module to the **safeguarding policy** and require
  parental and organizational consent processes `[VERIFY]`.

### Must Not

- Quote Scripture text from memory or assert verse wording.
- Adjudicate any contested question, or present one stream's position as the biblical one while
  describing others as compromise, capitulation, or error.
- Design, reference, imply, or leave room for **any orientation-change or identity-change effort**,
  including prayer, accountability, or discipleship framed as producing such change.
- Ask, invite, or create conditions that pressure a participant to disclose sexual history, orientation,
  temptation, or practice — including through group testimony, confession rounds, or paired sharing.
- Treat any participant as a case, a struggle, an issue, or a project.
- Invent research, statistics, or clinical claims about sexuality, celibacy, marriage outcomes,
  pornography, or identity.
- Give clinical guidance on compulsive behaviour, trauma, dysfunction, gender-related healthcare, or
  fertility. All route out.
- State any law: consent, marriage, safeguarding, reporting, or discrimination. Mark `[VERIFY]` and
  route to `domain-legal/` and qualified counsel.
- Use shame, purity-status language, or metaphors that describe a person as damaged, used, or devalued.
- Name a specific counselor, ministry, program, service, or organization.
- Allow a mentor to become the person who monitors another person's sexual behaviour.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)

- **Must:** name the genuinely contested questions — same-sex relationships and marriage, the standing of
  celibacy and vowed singleness, divorce and remarriage, contraception, cohabitation, gender and
  identity questions, and what constitutes chastity within and outside marriage — and attribute
  positions to identifiable streams, stating each in the form its own adherents would recognize. Where a
  tradition is declared, present its teaching as that tradition's teaching, plainly and without
  apology, while the existence of other positions stays visible.
- **Must Not:** flatten these disagreements into a single smoothed statement, imply that all positions
  are held with equal seriousness by no one in particular, or present the module's handling as a
  neutral resolution. Neutrality here means *not adjudicating*, not *pretending there is no
  disagreement*.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Decide whether to run this module at all

Before design, answer three questions in writing: is the community's position stated and known to
participants in advance; is disagreement safe; is there a referral route for what will surface. If any
answer is no, the deliverable is a note saying so and naming what must be fixed first. This is a
legitimate output of this prompt.

### Step 2 — Write the participation frame

Set out, in the participant's own hands: what the module covers, what it will not ask of them, that any
session can be skipped without explanation, that nothing about their history is required, what the
mentor or leader must pass on and to whom, and where to go instead if a session is the wrong place for
them. This frame is delivered before session one, in writing.

### Step 3 — Place the safety edges first

Build the referral table before the content table. Cover at minimum: past or present sexual abuse or
assault, coercion within a relationship, exploitation, compulsive behaviour, distress or self-harm
connected to identity or sexuality, and any concern involving a minor. Destination kind `[VERIFY]`,
mentor's words, and how the relationship continues afterward.

### Step 4 — Map the contested ground honestly

Build the contested-questions table. For each: the question, the positions, the streams that hold them,
the strongest case each makes, and how this module handles it. Where the community holds a position,
say so in the same row — participants should be able to see both the disagreement and where they are.

### Step 5 — Build the singleness strand as a full strand

Design content for single participants that is not about marriage, preparation, or waiting: friendship,
belonging, the household, loneliness, desire, freedom, and vocation. Include the widowed and the
divorced explicitly. If the module cannot survive the removal of its marriage content, the singleness
strand has not been built.

### Step 6 — Build the sessions

Lay out sessions against the count. For each: the question, Scripture by address, territory, the
practice — and two mandatory columns: *what this session must not ask*, and *who in the room this
session is most likely to wound, and the wording that prevents it*. Exegesis routes to
`domain-biblical-studies/exegesis-interpretation/`.

### Step 7 — Design questions that require no disclosure

Rewrite every discussion question so it can be answered fully without revealing anything about the
speaker's history, orientation, or practice. Questions about the topic, the text, the culture, and what
the participant has been taught all pass this test. "Share your struggle" does not.

### Step 8 — Stress-test

Check against the standing failure modes: someone was made into a case study; a contested question was
quietly settled; the singleness content was really about marriage; a session created pressure to
disclose; a disclosure had nowhere to go. Name the early signal and the fix for each, and identify who
outside the design team will review the module before it runs.

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Open this module in a community whose position is unstated. Participants discover it mid-session, at
  their own cost, and the trust does not come back.
- Build a session around personal testimony of struggle. It reads as courage and functions as pressure,
  and the person who does not share is now visible.
- Treat a participant's orientation or identity as the module's subject. They came for formation, like
  everyone else.
- Write singleness content that is really about preparation for marriage. Single participants recognize
  it immediately, and it tells them the module is not for them.
- Let a mentor take on monitoring someone's behaviour. It converts a formation relationship into
  surveillance, which is the failure mode this whole domain guards against.
- Use purity metaphors that describe a person as damaged or devalued. The harm outlasts the module by
  decades.
- Quietly resolve a contested question by giving one position more space, warmer language, or the last
  word.

✅ **DO:**
- Put the run/don't-run decision first and be willing to return "not yet."
- Give participants the frame in writing before session one, including the right to skip any session
  with no explanation.
- Place the safety edges before the content in the document itself, so anyone delivering it reads them
  first.
- State each contested position in the form its own adherents would recognize, then stop.
- Design every discussion question to pass the no-disclosure test, and show the test in the output.
- Have someone outside the design team read the module before it runs — ideally someone the content is
  most likely to land hardest on.
- Route every disclosure out immediately, and make continuation of the relationship explicit so
  referral is not experienced as rejection.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-01 (Clear Objective Statement):** the objective fixes the module as formation design that
  *presents* disagreement, and rules out adjudication, disclosure-seeking, and change-effort in the
  same sentence.
- **CM-02 (Constraint Specification):** this prompt's load-bearing element — the Must Not block encodes
  the absolute prohibitions (no change effort, no disclosure pressure, no stated law, no case studies)
  as testable constraints rather than tone.
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework):** contested questions are analysed across position,
  stream, strongest case, module handling, and community stance, so no dimension can be silently
  collapsed into the designer's own view.
- **QA-04 (Uncertainty Acknowledgment):** the module is required to hold genuine disagreement open and
  to state where this community stands without converting that into a settled answer.
- **QA-05 (Citation Requirements):** Scripture by address only, and every clinical, statistical, legal,
  and service claim marked `[VERIFY]` — on the topic where confident fabrication does the most damage.
