# Money and Generosity Module

**Objective:** Design a discipleship module that forms how a person actually handles money — earning,
spending, giving, owing, saving, and wanting — while leaving the contested questions contested,
refusing to shame anyone whose finances are constrained rather than disordered, and naming the
conflict of interest created when the community teaching generosity is the community receiving it.

> **Boundary guardrail.** This designs formation, not financial advice. Debt restructuring, insolvency,
> tax questions, benefits, investment decisions, and predatory-lending situations route to qualified
> professionals and, where relevant, to legal help `[VERIFY: identify the correct service from an
> official source]`. Financial distress severe enough to affect housing, food, health, or safety is a
> care matter before it is a curriculum matter, and a mentor's first move is referral, not a module.

**When to use:** You are building or replacing the money module inside a discipleship curriculum, or a
mentor and mentee have arrived at money and you need more than one conversation's worth of shape.

**When NOT to use:**
- You need the whole curriculum this module sits inside — use
  `../curriculum-architecture/discipleship_curriculum_architecture.md`.
- You need one teaching session built out — use `../session-and-lesson/discipleship_lesson_builder.md`.
- You need what Scripture says on wealth synthesized across the canon — use
  `domain-biblical-studies/theology-research/biblical_topical_theology_synthesis.md`.
- The person is in acute financial crisis — route out first; formation waits.
- You are designing a church's giving campaign or stewardship drive. That is fundraising, and this
  module is not a tool for it.

**Audience:** Curriculum designers, discipleship pastors, and experienced mentors building the money
module a pathway needs.

---

## Inputs / Context

**Required:**

1. **Where this module sits.** Which stage of the pathway, what has already been covered, and how many
   sessions it gets.
2. **The participants' real financial range.** Not their numbers — the honest spread. A module written
   for people with surplus lands as accusation on people without it, and a module written only for
   scarcity has nothing to say to the person with real assets and real decisions.
3. **The relationship between teacher and treasury.** Whether the person or body delivering this module
   receives, benefits from, or is measured on the participants' giving. Answer plainly; it changes what
   this module may and may not do.
4. **What the surrounding culture assumes about money and faith.** Prosperity teaching, debt-normalcy,
   remittance obligations, extended-family claims on income, shame around poverty — whatever is
   actually in the room.

**Optional:**

5. **Declared tradition (optional).** May foreground a tithing norm, a proportional-giving norm, a
   community-of-goods emphasis, a vow of simplicity, or a sacrificial-giving framing. Contested
   specifics stay attributed, and alternatives stay visible.
6. **Existing material.** Any money curriculum or book already in use, supplied as text or description.
7. **Local pressures.** Cost of living, currency instability, informal economies, or benefit rules that
   make generic budgeting advice useless.

**If any required input is missing:** Ask before proceeding — especially item 3. A module on giving
designed by the recipient of the giving, without that fact named, is the single most common way this
topic goes wrong.

---

## Constraints

### Must

- Treat money as a **formation** subject — desire, trust, fear, status, and neighbour — not primarily a
  budgeting subject.
- Cover, at minimum: **earning, spending, giving, owing, saving, and wanting**, with at least one
  practice attached to each that a participant can attempt within a week.
- Reference Scripture **by address only**, with the translation named for the user to verify against.
- Present the **tithe question as contested**, attributing positions to identifiable streams, and never
  resolve it as the module's answer.
- **Declare the conflict of interest** in the module itself where the teaching body receives the giving,
  and state the guard: no giving totals shared, no individual giving known to the mentor, no pressure
  applied in a discipleship relationship.
- Distinguish **constrained finances from disordered finances**, and say plainly that having little is
  not a spiritual failure.
- Include the **person with money** — decisions about surplus, inheritance, business ownership, and
  power are formation questions too, and modules routinely skip them.
- Keep every practice **scalable to zero income**: any practice that requires disposable money must have
  a non-monetary form that is not presented as a lesser version.
- Route debt, insolvency, tax, benefits, and predatory lending **out** to qualified help `[VERIFY]`.

### Must Not

- Quote Scripture text from memory, or assert a verse's wording.
- Invent giving statistics, generosity research, denominational giving averages, or financial data.
- Assert that giving produces financial return, protection, healing, or blessing as a transaction.
- Ask a participant to disclose income, debts, giving amounts, or net worth to a mentor or a group.
- Set a giving figure, percentage, or target for anyone, or frame a level of giving as the evidence of
  a converted heart.
- Use guilt, comparison, urgency, testimony-of-the-sacrificial-giver, or public commitment as a
  motivator.
- State tax, benefit, charity-registration, or gift-aid rules. Mark them `[VERIFY]` and route to
  qualified local advice.
- Treat poverty as a discipline problem, or wealth as automatic evidence of either blessing or guilt.
- Name a specific financial service, charity, counselor, debt agency, or app.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)

- **Must:** name where traditions genuinely diverge — the tithe as binding versus superseded,
  proportional giving, first-fruits framing, giving to the local congregation versus the poor directly,
  vows of poverty and simplicity, community of goods, and how the prosperity teaching that circulates in
  many contexts is regarded across streams. Attribute each to identifiable positions. Let a declared
  tradition foreground its own practice while alternatives stay in view.
- **Must Not:** present one stream's giving norm as the biblical standard, or use a contested passage as
  though its application to modern giving were settled.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Fix the module's job and its honest range

State what this module is for in one sentence, and describe the actual financial range of the people who
will walk it. Write the two extremes as real people: the participant choosing between electricity and
a bus fare, and the participant deciding what to do with an inheritance. The module must have something
true to say to both.

### Step 2 — Declare the conflict of interest

If the delivering body receives the giving, write that into the module in plain words, along with the
guards: no individual giving is known to the mentor, no totals are shared, no ask is made inside a
discipleship conversation, and a participant may complete the module having given nothing to that body.
If there is no such relationship, say so — the participant should know either way.

### Step 3 — Map the six territories

Work each of earning, spending, giving, owing, saving, and wanting. For each, name the formation
question underneath it (not the technique), the Scripture addresses that speak to it, the common
distortion in this participant group, and the practice attached.

| Territory | The formation question under it |
|---|---|
| Earning | What am I willing to do for money, and what does my work cost others? |
| Spending | What do my ordinary purchases reveal about what I trust and who I compare myself to? |
| Giving | Who has a claim on what I have, and how do I decide? |
| Owing | What does what I owe do to my freedom, my relationships, and my anxiety? |
| Saving | What am I storing against, and what is the difference between prudence and hoarding? |
| Wanting | What have I been taught to want, and by whom? |

### Step 4 — Flag the contested ground explicitly

Build the module's contested-practice table before writing sessions: the tithe, giving to the
congregation versus directly to the poor, debt as sin or as circumstance, wealth accumulation, vows of
simplicity, and prosperity teaching. For each, state the disagreement, the streams and their positions,
and how this module handles it — which is always "presents, attributes, does not resolve."

### Step 5 — Build the sessions

Lay out the sessions against the allocated count. For each: the question it answers, Scripture by
address, the territory it covers, the practice attempted before the next meeting, and the specific way
this session could shame someone — with the wording that prevents it. Route exegesis of any passage to
`domain-biblical-studies/exegesis-interpretation/`.

### Step 6 — Make every practice scalable to zero

Take each practice and write its zero-income form. Giving becomes giving of time, skill, food,
hospitality, or presence — described as genuine generosity, not a substitute for the real thing.
Budgeting becomes tracking, which costs nothing. If a practice has no honest zero-income form, cut it.

### Step 7 — Write the referral edges

State what leaves this module: unmanageable debt, benefit and tax questions, insolvency, predatory
lending, financial coercion or economic abuse inside a relationship, gambling, and compulsive spending.
For each, the destination kind `[VERIFY]` and the words the mentor uses. Economic abuse in particular
is a safety matter, not a stewardship matter.

### Step 8 — Stress-test

Check the module against its four standing failure modes: it became budgeting advice; it became a
fundraising ask; it shamed the poor; it had nothing to say to the rich. Name the early signal of each
and the fix.

## Verification

- [ ] Every Scripture reference is an address only — no verse text is quoted or asserted.
- [ ] The tithe and every other contested question is presented and attributed, never resolved.
- [ ] The conflict of interest is declared in the module, or its absence is stated.
- [ ] No participant is asked to disclose income, debt, giving, or net worth; no figure or target is set.
- [ ] Every practice has an honest zero-income form that is not framed as lesser.
- [ ] No tax, benefit, or lending rule is stated; all such points are `[VERIFY]` and routed out.

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Let the module become a budgeting course. Budgeting is useful and available everywhere; formation
  around money is what a discipleship pathway is uniquely placed to do.
- Attach a promise to giving — return, protection, provision, blessing. It is the oldest distortion in
  this topic and it wounds people whose giving is followed by loss.
- Ask for numbers. A mentor who knows what a mentee gives cannot un-know it, and the relationship
  changes the moment they do.
- Treat the constrained participant as a project. Someone doing well on very little may be the most
  formed person in the room.
- Skip the wealthy participant because their situation feels awkward. Surplus carries formation
  questions — power, insulation, inheritance, leverage over others — that the module owes them.
- Present a tithe percentage as the settled Christian norm, or as obviously superseded. Both are
  positions, and this module holds neither.
- Use a sacrificial-giving testimony as a motivator. It is comparison with a halo on it.

✅ **DO:**
- Name the conflict of interest out loud, in the module, where participants can see it.
- Write the wording that prevents shame directly into the session row, so the person delivering it sees
  the flag when they need it.
- Give every practice a zero-income form and describe it as generosity, not as consolation.
- Separate constrained from disordered explicitly, and say that having little is not a spiritual verdict.
- Put economic abuse in the referral table, not in the stewardship discussion. It is a safety matter.
- Keep the exegesis of the hard passages routed to `domain-biblical-studies/`, so this module stays a
  module and does not quietly become a position paper.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-01 (Clear Objective Statement):** the objective fixes the deliverable as a module design and
  explicitly excludes financial advice and fundraising, the two things this topic most often becomes.
- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** the steps force the conflict-of-interest declaration
  and the contested-ground table *before* sessions are written, so neither can be retrofitted around
  content already committed to.
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework):** the six territories (earning, spending, giving,
  owing, saving, wanting) prevent the collapse into giving-only that this module almost always suffers.
- **CM-02 (Constraint Specification):** the Must Not block encodes the non-negotiables — no disclosure
  of amounts, no targets, no transactional promises, no stated tax or benefit law.
- **QA-05 (Citation Requirements):** Scripture by address with a named translation, and every service,
  statistic, and legal point marked `[VERIFY]`, applied to a topic dense with confident false numbers.
