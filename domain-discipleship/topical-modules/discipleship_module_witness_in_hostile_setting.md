# Witness in a Hostile Setting Module

**Objective:** Design a module that forms a disciple's witness where being known as a Christian costs
something real — grading the hostility from social awkwardness through family rupture, employment loss,
legal jeopardy, and physical danger — building honest speech and durable presence rather than technique,
and leaving every decision about risk with the person who will actually carry it.

> **Boundary guardrail.** This designs formation, not security. This module does not produce
> operational security guidance, evasion methods, concealment techniques, communications-security
> instructions, or any assessment of what is legal or safe in a specific place. Those depend on facts
> the model does not have and cannot verify, and a wrong answer gets people hurt. Every such question
> routes to trusted local leaders who know the setting, to organizations with direct presence there
> `[VERIFY: identify the correct organization from a source you already trust]`, and where relevant to
> qualified legal counsel. Persecution trauma, fear, and the aftermath of violence route to
> `domain-psychology/` and licensed professionals.

**When to use:** You are building the witness module for a context where hostility is real rather than
rhetorical — a workplace with genuine professional cost, a family that would break, a country or
community where identification carries legal or physical danger.

**When NOT to use:**
- The setting is merely indifferent or mildly awkward. A general witness module is a better fit, and
  overstating hostility does formation harm.
- The live need is preparation for a specific conversation with a specific person — use
  `domain-biblical-studies/apologetics-engagement/biblical_apologetics_conversation_prep.md`.
- The live need is a safety or legal assessment. This module cannot provide it; route out.
- The participant is in the aftermath of violence or threat. Care and referral first.

**Audience:** Experienced curriculum designers and leaders working in a specific hostile context, with
local knowledge available to them. This module should not be designed at a distance from the setting.

---

## Inputs / Context

**Required:**

1. **The setting, described by someone who is in it.** Which pressures, from whom, with what
   consequences, and how they actually fall — often unevenly, by gender, ethnicity, class, migration
   status, or family position.
2. **The hostility grade in view.** Social cost, family rupture, employment or educational cost, legal
   jeopardy, or physical danger. More than one may apply to different participants in the same group.
3. **Who bears the risk beyond the participant.** Family, employer, congregation, and — critically —
   anyone the participant might speak to, who may face worse consequences than they do.
4. **What local leadership advises.** Whatever the people with direct knowledge of the setting already
   say. Their judgment governs; this module organizes formation inside it.

**Optional:**

5. **Declared tradition (optional).** May foreground martyrdom and confessor traditions, a
   quiet-presence emphasis, a public-proclamation emphasis, sacramental participation under
   restriction, or teaching on prudence and concealment. Contested specifics stay attributed.
6. **Prior incidents.** What has already happened to people in this community.
7. **Existing guidance in force.** Any protocol the community already operates under.

**If any required input is missing:** Ask before proceeding — and if item 1 or item 4 cannot be supplied,
say plainly that the module cannot be designed responsibly without them. Designing witness formation
for a hostile setting from outside it, without local judgment, is how people get hurt by well-meant
material.

---

## Constraints

### Must

- **Grade the hostility explicitly** and design differently for each grade. Social awkwardness and
  physical danger are not the same subject and must not share a session.
- Treat **prudence as faithful**, not as failure of nerve — and treat courage as faithful too, without
  ranking them or implying that the participant who stays quiet has failed.
- Put **the decision with the person who bears the cost**, every time, and say so in the module.
- Account for **third-party risk**: the person spoken to may face far worse consequences, and the module
  must make this an explicit consideration before any conversation is encouraged.
- Build **honesty under pressure** — what to say when asked directly, how to decline to answer, how to
  avoid lying about others — rather than persuasion technique.
- Include **witness that is not speech**: presence, work, hospitality, and conduct, which are the primary
  forms available in most hostile settings.
- Include the **cost that has already been paid**: participants who have lost family, work, or standing,
  with content for them rather than about them.
- Reference Scripture **by address only**, translation named, noting where a persecution passage's
  application to the present setting is an inference.
- Route every operational, legal, and safety question **out** to local leadership and qualified counsel
  `[VERIFY]`.

### Must Not

- Quote Scripture text from memory or assert verse wording.
- Produce security, evasion, concealment, surveillance-avoidance, encryption, or communications guidance
  of any kind.
- Assess or assert what is legal, safe, tolerated, or dangerous in any specific country, region,
  community, employer, or family.
- Name specific countries, regions, groups, or organizations as hostile, safe, or persecuting, or make
  generalizations about how members of a religion, nationality, or ethnicity respond to Christians.
- Teach manipulative or covert persuasion technique, deceptive framing, or relationship-building
  conducted as a means to an end.
- Encourage, praise, or design toward provocation, deliberate confrontation, or risk-seeking as evidence
  of faith.
- Invent persecution statistics, martyrdom figures, conversion data, or country reports.
- Frame a participant's caution as weak faith, or their silence as denial.
- Give clinical guidance on fear, trauma, or hypervigilance. All routes out.
- Name a specific organization, network, or service as a destination without `[VERIFY]`.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)

- **Must:** note where traditions differ on public confession versus prudent concealment, on the
  standing of martyrdom, on whether sacramental participation may be suspended under restriction, on
  proselytism and its limits, and on the legitimacy of quiet presence as witness. Attribute positions to
  identifiable streams; let a declared tradition foreground its own while alternatives stay visible.
- **Must Not:** present one stream's account of faithful witness under pressure as the biblical one, or
  let a martyrdom emphasis be taught in a way that shames the participant who has chosen prudence.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Grade the hostility, honestly

Write the grade or grades present, with the actual consequences at each, sourced from the people in the
setting. Then state that overstating hostility is itself a formation harm — it produces a persecution
identity where there is only unpopularity — and understating it gets people hurt.

### Step 2 — Establish who decides

State the module's governing rule: the person who bears the cost makes the decision, every time, and
nobody in the group — mentor, leader, or peer — may apply pressure to it. Write the sentence that says
so, and repeat it in every session that touches a decision.

### Step 3 — Map third-party risk

Before any content encouraging conversation, work the question of who else is exposed: the person spoken
to, their family, the congregation, the participant's own household. In several settings the listener
faces greater danger than the speaker, and formation material that ignores this is dangerous.

### Step 4 — Build the four capabilities

| Capability | What the participant can do afterwards |
|---|---|
| Presence | Live and work in a way that is legible without a word being said |
| Honest answer | Say something true when asked directly, at a level of disclosure they have chosen |
| Declining | Not answer, without lying and without implicating others |
| Enduring | Carry the cost already paid, and stay in relationship with those who imposed it |

For each: Scripture by address with the inference named, the practice, and the rehearsal.

### Step 5 — Design for the cost already paid

Write the strand for participants who have already lost something — family, work, standing, home. What
this module offers them is not preparation but company: naming the loss, lament (routing to the
suffering module), and the relationships that remain. Do not use their story as anyone else's
motivation.

### Step 6 — Build the sessions

Lay out sessions against the count. For each: the question, Scripture by address, capability, practice,
rehearsal — plus two mandatory columns: *the grade this session is for*, and *the harm if this session
is delivered at a higher grade than it was designed for*. Exegesis routes to
`domain-biblical-studies/exegesis-interpretation/`.

### Step 7 — Write the routing table

Everything operational, legal, or security-related leaves. Build the table: the question, why this
module will not answer it, and where it goes — local leadership, organizations with direct presence
`[VERIFY]`, or qualified counsel. Include fear and trauma routing to licensed help.

### Step 8 — Stress-test

Check against the standing failure modes: hostility was overstated; a decision was pressured; third-party
risk was ignored; prudence was framed as failure; the module drifted into security or technique. Early
signal and fix. Then name who in the setting reviews the module before it runs.

## Verification

- [ ] Hostility is graded, and no session mixes grades or is delivered above the grade it was built for.
- [ ] The governing rule — the person bearing the cost decides — appears in the module and in every
      decision-touching session.
- [ ] Third-party risk is worked before any content encouraging conversation.
- [ ] No security, evasion, concealment, or communications guidance appears anywhere.
- [ ] No country, region, group, or organization is characterized as hostile, safe, or persecuting, and
      no generalization is made about how any group responds to Christians.
- [ ] Prudence and courage are both treated as faithful, with neither ranked.

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Answer a safety or legality question. The model does not know the setting, the answer changes by
  street and by month, and a confident wrong answer is the worst output this module could produce.
- Let martyrdom language do motivational work. In a genuinely dangerous setting it functions as pressure
  on people whose caution is protecting their children.
- Encourage a conversation without working who else is exposed. In many settings the listener carries
  the heavier risk, and inviting them to hear is inviting them to danger.
- Import a persecution frame into a merely uncomfortable setting. It forms a grievance identity and
  makes the participant unable to recognize real hostility when it appears.
- Teach persuasion technique. Relationship pursued as a method is dishonest anywhere, and in a hostile
  setting it also confirms exactly what opponents claim.
- Generalize about a nationality, religion, or ethnicity. The module has no basis for it and the
  generalization does harm wherever it travels.

✅ **DO:**
- Require the setting to be described by someone in it, and treat local leadership's judgment as
  governing.
- Write the governing rule as a quotable sentence and repeat it wherever a decision appears.
- Build the non-speech capabilities first; in most hostile settings presence and conduct are the whole
  available witness for long periods.
- Include the participants who have already lost something, as people to accompany rather than
  illustrations to deploy.
- Put the grade in the session table, with the harm of over-delivery, so nobody hands social-cost
  material to someone facing physical danger.
- Route every operational question out visibly, in a table, so the boundary is legible to everyone using
  the module.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-01 (Clear Objective Statement):** the objective fixes graded formation as the deliverable and
  rules out security guidance and technique in the same sentence, which is the boundary this module
  lives or dies on.
- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** hostility is graded and third-party risk is mapped
  before any capability content, so no session can encourage a conversation whose cost has not been
  examined.
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework):** four capabilities crossed with five hostility grades
  and the already-paid strand covers a space that a single "how to share your faith" module flattens.
- **CM-02 (Constraint Specification):** the Must Not block encodes the absolute prohibitions — no
  security guidance, no country or group characterization, no legality assessment, no persuasion
  technique, no provocation.
- **QA-04 (Uncertainty Acknowledgment):** the module is required to state what it cannot know about the
  setting and to route those questions to people who do, rather than producing a plausible answer.
