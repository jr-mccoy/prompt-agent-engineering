# Anger and Conflict Module

**Objective:** Design a module that forms how a disciple handles anger and conflict in real
relationships — treating anger as information about what someone believes has been violated, teaching
the biblical conflict material with its limits explicitly flagged, building actual capability for
confrontation and repair, and separating conflict from intimidation so that abuse is never processed as
a disagreement.

> **Boundary guardrail.** This designs formation, not intervention. Domestic abuse, intimidation,
> coercive control, threats, violence, and fear of another person are not conflict and are never treated
> as such: they route to appropriate professionals and, where there is danger, to authorities and
> emergency services `[VERIFY: identify the correct service from an official source]`. A mentor never
> mediates a conflict between other people, never coaches a participant into confronting someone who
> frightens them, and never treats persistent rage or violent behaviour as a discipline problem — that
> routes to `domain-psychology/` and licensed help.

**When to use:** You are building the anger-and-conflict module in a curriculum, or a mentee's live
conflicts have become the recurring content and you want structure rather than repeated triage.

**When NOT to use:**
- The injury is already done and the live question is what is owed — use
  `discipleship_module_forgiveness_and_reconciliation.md`.
- Something has just been disclosed — use `../session-and-lesson/discipleship_hard_conversation_navigation.md`.
- The conflict is inside a Bible-study group you are facilitating — use
  `domain-biblical-studies/group-leader-facilitation/biblical_groupleader_conflict_resolution.md`.
- There is fear, intimidation, or violence. Route out; this is not a conflict module's business.

**Audience:** Curriculum designers and mentors building the module, and small-group leaders whose groups
keep hitting the same unaddressed friction.

---

## Inputs / Context

**Required:**

1. **Where this module sits.** Stage, prerequisites, session count.
2. **The conflicts actually in view.** Family, marriage, workplace, church, friendship, or online — and
   whether participants hold power in them, are subject to it, or both.
3. **What this community currently teaches about anger.** Many teach that anger is itself sin, or that
   confrontation is unloving; the module has to work with what participants already believe, not
   against a blank slate.
4. **The referral landscape.** Local and program-level help, described rather than named from memory.

**Optional:**

5. **Declared tradition (optional).** May foreground a specific reading of the conflict passages, a
   communal discipline process, a peace-church nonviolence commitment, or a particular counsel on
   anger. Contested specifics stay attributed.
6. **Cultural conflict norms in the room.** Direct and indirect conflict styles differ sharply, and a
   module that teaches one as maturity mislabels the other as avoidance or aggression.
7. **Recent community conflict.** Live disputes change what can be safely used as material.

**If any required input is missing:** Ask before proceeding — particularly the power question in item 2.
The same confrontation advice given to a manager and to their employee produces opposite outcomes.

---

## Constraints

### Must

- Treat **anger as information** — a signal that something the person believes matters has been violated
  — and work the question of what it is reporting before any question of managing it.
- Separate **anger, aggression, and harm** as three different things, and be explicit about which the
  module addresses.
- Teach the biblical conflict material **by address**, with the power-imbalance limit flagged in the
  same breath: these patterns assume parties who can speak freely to each other.
- Build real **capability**: what to actually say, when to raise something, when to let it go, and how
  to tell those apart.
- Include **being confronted** — receiving criticism, sitting with an accusation, and distinguishing a
  fair charge from an unfair one.
- Include **conflict avoidance** as a formation issue with its own costs, without framing every
  avoidance as cowardice.
- Address **anger at God** and **anger at the church** honestly rather than routing them straight to
  repentance.
- Flag **cultural difference in conflict style** and refuse to code one style as spiritual maturity.
- Route intimidation, fear, threats, violence, and persistent uncontrolled rage **out** `[VERIFY]`.

### Must Not

- Quote Scripture text from memory or assert verse wording.
- Teach that anger is inherently sinful, or that a participant's anger is evidence of a spiritual
  problem.
- Apply a conflict-between-equals pattern to a situation with a serious power imbalance, or to abuse,
  without flagging it as inapplicable and harmful.
- Coach a participant into confronting someone they are afraid of.
- Have a mentor mediate, arbitrate, or carry messages between parties.
- Invent anger research, conflict-style instruments, personality typologies, or de-escalation studies
  presented as evidence.
- Give clinical guidance on anger disorders, trauma responses, or intermittent rage. All route out.
- Present a communal discipline process as universal church practice.
- Treat one culture's conflict norm — direct or indirect — as the mature or biblical one.
- Name a specific counselor, mediation service, or organization.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)

- **Must:** note where traditions differ on the reading of the conflict and church-discipline material,
  on nonviolence and peace-church commitments, on the standing of righteous anger, on whether
  confrontation is a duty or a last resort, and on who has standing to raise a matter. Attribute
  positions to identifiable streams; let a declared tradition foreground its own practice.
- **Must Not:** present one stream's discipline process or reading of the conflict passages as the
  church's settled practice, or let a nonviolence commitment be presented as either obviously required
  or obviously naive.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Reset the frame on anger

Establish the module's working frame: anger reports a perceived violation; the report may be accurate or
inaccurate; what a person does with it is where the formation happens. Then name what the community
currently believes about anger and say where this module agrees and differs. Skipping this leaves
participants defending themselves rather than learning.

### Step 2 — Separate anger, aggression, and harm

Define the three, with examples. State which this module addresses (the first two) and where the third
goes (out, immediately). Every subsequent section depends on this line holding.

### Step 3 — Teach the conflict material with its limits attached

Work the biblical conflict passages by address and, in the same table, state the conditions the pattern
assumes: comparable standing, physical and psychological safety, and the freedom to speak and to
withdraw. Then state what to do when those conditions do not hold — which is not the pattern.

### Step 4 — Build the four capabilities

| Capability | What the participant can do afterwards |
|---|---|
| Reading the anger | Name what the anger is reporting, and test whether the report is accurate |
| Raising it | Choose the moment, open the conversation, and say the thing plainly |
| Receiving it | Sit with being confronted without collapsing or counter-attacking |
| Letting it go | Distinguish genuine release from suppression, and know the cost of each |

For each: Scripture by address, the practice, and a rehearsal in the session.

### Step 5 — Work power and culture

Design the strand that adjusts for power: what changes when the participant is the one with authority,
and what changes when they are subject to it — including when raising something risks their job,
housing, standing, or safety. Then the cultural strand: direct and indirect conflict norms both handled
as legitimate, with the translation problem named.

### Step 6 — Build the sessions

Lay out sessions against the count. For each: the question, Scripture by address, capability, practice,
and a rehearsal — plus *the situation in which this session's advice would be dangerous*, stated
explicitly. Exegesis routes to `domain-biblical-studies/exegesis-interpretation/`.

### Step 7 — Write the referral edges

Enumerate what leaves: fear of another person, intimidation, threats, violence, coercive control,
uncontrolled rage, and conflict that has become a legal or employment matter. Destination kind
`[VERIFY]`, the mentor's words, and how the relationship continues.

### Step 8 — Stress-test

Check against the standing failure modes: anger was moralized; the conflict pattern was applied where it
does not hold; a participant was coached toward a dangerous confrontation; one culture's style was
taught as maturity; the mentor became a mediator. Early signal and fix.

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Teach that anger is sin. Participants who believe it will hide their anger rather than examine it, and
  hidden anger is the one that does the damage.
- Hand the conflict passages over without their conditions. They assume two people who can speak freely
  to each other; used elsewhere they become an instrument for pressuring the person with less power.
- Let a mentor mediate. It looks like service, it makes them a party to the conflict, and it usually
  ends the discipling relationship along with the dispute.
- Give the same confrontation advice to everyone in the room. For some participants raising it costs a
  difficult evening; for others it costs their job.
- Call indirect conflict handling avoidance. In many cultures it is the skilled and respectful form, and
  labelling it immaturity is a formation error with cultural contempt inside it.
- Process intimidation as conflict. If someone is afraid, this module has already ended.

✅ **DO:**
- Name what the community currently teaches about anger, and say where this module differs, before
  teaching anything.
- Put the "where this session's advice would be dangerous" column in the session table, so the person
  delivering it sees the flag at the point of use.
- Rehearse. Conflict capability is behavioural, and a participant who has only discussed confrontation
  cannot do it.
- Include being confronted. Most curricula teach only the raising, and receiving is the harder half.
- Handle anger at God and at the church as real, and resist routing them straight to repentance.
- Route fear out immediately, with words, and make continuation of the relationship explicit.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-01 (Clear Objective Statement):** the objective fixes anger-as-information and the conflict/abuse
  separation as the module's defining commitments, ruling out the anger-management framing by design.
- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** the frame reset and the three-way separation precede
  the conflict material, so the passages are never taught before the conditions that limit them.
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework):** four capabilities crossed with the power and culture
  strands analyses conflict along axes a single "how to confront" module cannot reach.
- **CM-02 (Constraint Specification):** the Must Not block encodes the load-bearing prohibitions — no
  moralized anger, no unflagged pattern application, no coaching toward feared confrontation, no
  mentor mediation.
- **QA-05 (Citation Requirements):** Scripture by address with a named translation, no invented conflict
  instruments or research, and every service marked `[VERIFY]`.
