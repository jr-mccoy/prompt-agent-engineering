# Hard Conversation Navigation

**Objective:** Give a lay mentor a usable plan for the moment something heavy is disclosed — how to
classify what was said, what to do in the first sixty seconds, what to say, what routes out and how to
say that without it landing as rejection, and what happens after the meeting, including what the mentor
is now carrying.

> **Boundary guardrail.** This helps a mentor *respond and route* — it does not equip anyone to handle
> a disclosure of abuse, self-harm, suicidality, addiction, or crisis. Those go to licensed
> professionals, the safeguarding lead, and where there is immediate danger, emergency services
> `[VERIFY: identify the correct service from an official source]`. Where there is immediate risk to
> life, contacting emergency services comes before anything else in this prompt. Nothing here states
> what the law requires about reporting — that is `[VERIFY]` with the safeguarding lead and qualified
> counsel via `domain-legal/`.

**When to use:** Immediately — something has just been said, or is about to be, and the mentor needs a
plan. Also usable in training, rehearsed before it is needed.

**When NOT to use:**
- You are building the standing scope framework — use
  `../mentor-equipping/discipleship_mentor_boundaries_and_referral.md`, which this applies in the moment.
- You are designing the ordinary meeting — use `discipleship_session_plan_builder.md`.
- There is immediate danger to life — contact emergency services now, not this prompt.

**Audience:** The lay mentor, during or immediately after a meeting; trainers rehearsing this in advance.

---

## Inputs / Context

**Required:**

1. **What was said.** As close to the person's own words as possible.
2. **What the mentor said back.** Including if they froze, changed the subject, or said something they
   regret. This is normal and the plan must work from wherever they are.
3. **Where things stand now.** Still in the meeting, just ended, or days ago.
4. **What the covenant said** about confidentiality and what must be passed on.

**Optional:**

5. **Declared tradition (optional).** May shape whether an elder or pastor is involved, how confession
   is handled, and the internal escalation path — which sits alongside, never instead of, professional
   routing.
6. **Whether the mentee asked for secrecy.** Before or after saying it.
7. **What the mentor is feeling.** Which affects their judgement and belongs in the plan.

**If any required input is missing:** Ask only what is needed to classify and route. Do not run a full
intake while a mentor is mid-crisis — get the classification, give the immediate response, and gather
the rest afterwards.

---

## Constraints

### Must

- **Classify first**, into: immediate danger · abuse or harm · mental-health concern · addiction ·
  ordinary hard-but-within-scope · confession of wrongdoing · something about a third party.
- Give the **first sixty seconds** explicitly — what the mentor does before they have worked anything
  out.
- Supply **actual words** for each classification, not principles.
- Handle the case where the mentor has **already responded badly** — froze, minimized, changed the
  subject, or over-promised confidentiality — and give the repair.
- Say what the mentor does when the mentee **asked for secrecy** about something that must be passed on,
  including if secrecy was promised before it was said.
- Make routing land as **care rather than rejection**, with the relationship's continuation stated
  explicitly in the words used.
- Cover **after the meeting**: what is written down, who is told, when, and what the mentor does with
  what they are now carrying.
- State that in **immediate danger, emergency services come first** — before consultation, before the
  covenant, before anything.

### Must Not

- Encourage the mentor to keep handling something outside their scope because a professional is not
  immediately available.
- Suggest wording that minimizes, reframes as spiritual, or moves to silver linings.
- Advise the mentor to promise confidentiality, or to honour a promise of secrecy that conflicts with a
  safeguarding obligation.
- State a legal reporting duty, threshold, or timeframe. `[VERIFY]` and route.
- Name a specific hotline, agency, counselor, or service.
- Suggest the mentor investigate, gather evidence, question the mentee for detail, or contact a third
  party the disclosure concerned.
- Quote Scripture text from memory, or offer a passage as the first response to a disclosure.
- Invent trauma-response protocols, crisis-intervention techniques, or clinical guidance.
- Leave the mentor carrying it alone.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)

- **Must:** where a tradition provides an internal path — confession to a priest, elder involvement,
  pastoral oversight — name it as that stream's practice and place it *alongside* professional routing,
  never as a substitute.
- **Must Not:** allow a tradition's framing of a matter as spiritual to displace a safeguarding or
  clinical referral, or treat the internal path as sufficient where there is risk.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Check for immediate danger

If there is immediate risk to life or safety — to the mentee, a child, or anyone else — that comes
before everything. Contact emergency services `[VERIFY]`. Stay with the person if safe to do so.
Everything else in this prompt waits.

### Step 2 — Classify what was said

| Classification | Marker | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|
| **Immediate danger** | Risk to life now | Emergency services `[VERIFY]` — first, before anything |
| **Abuse or harm** | Past or present, to them or a child | Safeguarding lead `[VERIFY]` — always, regardless of what was asked |
| **Mental-health concern** | Distress beyond ordinary difficulty | Licensed professional `[VERIFY]` |
| **Addiction** | Substance or behavioural dependence | Specialist support `[VERIFY]` |
| **Hard but in scope** | Doubt, grief, conflict, ordinary struggle | Stays — this is the relationship's actual work |
| **Confession of wrongdoing** | Something they have done | Depends on harm to others — check safeguarding |
| **About a third party** | Concerns someone not present | Care with hearsay; safeguarding if a child or risk |

### Step 3 — The first sixty seconds

Whatever the classification: stop the session plan. Do not fill the silence. Do not reach for a
solution, a passage, or reassurance. The response is short, and it says: I heard you, thank you for
telling me, and I am not going anywhere.

### Step 4 — Say the words

Take the classification and give the mentor the sentences. For anything routing out, the words must do
three things: take it seriously, name that someone better placed is needed, and make plain the
relationship continues.

### Step 5 — Handle secrecy and repair

If secrecy was requested, give the words for saying honestly that this is one of the things that cannot
be kept — who will be told and why. If it was already promised, give the repair: acknowledge the promise,
say plainly it cannot be kept and why, apologize for having made it, and tell them what happens next
before it happens.

If the mentor already responded badly, give the repair: name it, do not over-explain, and return to the
disclosure.

### Step 6 — After the meeting

Say what is recorded, factually and in the mentee's own words where possible; who is told and when;
whether the mentee is told they are being told; and what follow-up the mentor does with them.

### Step 7 — What the mentor is carrying

Name it. Route it to supervision rather than to peers, per
`../mentor-equipping/discipleship_mentor_support_and_sustainability.md`. A mentor who holds a
disclosure alone often stops mentoring within months.

## Output Format

Produce exactly this structure. Written to be read fast, by someone unsettled.

```

## Is Anyone in Immediate Danger?
**If yes — contact emergency services [VERIFY] now. Stay with them if it's safe. Everything below
waits.**

## What Was Said
[Their words.]

## Classification
**[category]** — because [..]
**Where it goes:** [kind of service — VERIFY locally] + [safeguarding lead, if applicable]

## The First Sixty Seconds
- Stop the session plan.
- Don't fill the silence.
- Don't reach for a solution, a verse, or reassurance.
> "[Short. I heard you. Thank you for telling me. I'm not going anywhere.]"

## What You Say Next
> "[Words matched to the classification — serious, names that someone better placed is needed, makes
> clear the relationship continues.]"

**If asked before they said it:** "[..]"
**If you already promised:** "[Acknowledge the promise. Say plainly it can't be kept, and why.
Apologize for making it. Tell them what happens next, before it happens.]"

## If You Already Responded Badly
[Name it briefly. Don't over-explain. Return to what they said.]
> "[..]"

## After the Meeting
| What | Detail |
|---|---|
| What you write down | [factual, their words, dated] |
| Who you tell | [role] |
| When | [..] |
| Does the mentee know they're being told? | [..] |
| Your follow-up with them | [..] |

**[VERIFY]** Reporting duties are jurisdiction-specific. The safeguarding lead confirms them. Nothing

## What You're Now Carrying
[Named.] This goes to [supervision route], not to your peer group. Don't hold it alone.
```

## Verification

- [ ] Immediate danger is checked first and overrides everything, including the covenant.
- [ ] The disclosure is classified before any response is given.
- [ ] Verbatim words are supplied for the first sixty seconds and for the routing.
- [ ] The secrecy case is handled, including where confidentiality was already promised.
- [ ] Every routing sentence states that the relationship continues.
- [ ] No legal duty, named service, or clinical technique appears; the mentor's own load is routed.

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Reach for a Bible passage as the first response. It is a common reflex and it lands as deflection at
  the moment someone has taken a risk.
- Keep handling it because no professional is available today. Unavailability does not transfer
  competence, and the interim plan is to route and stay present, not to substitute.
- Honour a promise of secrecy that conflicts with a safeguarding obligation. The promise was a mistake;
  keeping it compounds it.
- Question the mentee for detail. It is not the mentor's role, it can contaminate a later process, and
  it can be experienced as interrogation.
- Contact the third party a disclosure concerned. That decision belongs to the safeguarding lead or
  the authorities.
- Let the mentor carry it alone because it feels like a confidence. That is how mentors leave.

✅ **DO:**
- Give the sixty-second response first, because that is the part that happens before anyone has worked
  anything out.
- Say the relationship continues in the actual words of the routing sentence — otherwise referral is
  heard as being handed off and dropped.
- Give the repair script for a promise already made. Mentors make that promise often, and they need a
  way back that does not compound it.
- Tell the mentee they are being told about before it happens, wherever that is safe to do.
- Record factually in the mentee's own words, dated, and nothing more — no interpretation, no
  speculation.
- Route the mentor's own load to supervision rather than peers, since the peer group is exactly where
  the disclosure should not circulate.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** the danger check precedes classification, which
  precedes any response, so a mentor working the prompt under pressure cannot reach a warm reply before
  a safety question has been asked.
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework):** seven disclosure classifications with distinct
  destinations replace the single "refer if serious" instruction that leaves mentors guessing at
  exactly the wrong moment.
- **CM-02 (Constraint Specification):** the bans on scripture-as-first-response, on honouring conflicting
  secrecy promises, on questioning for detail, and on contacting third parties are hard constraints
  against the well-meant errors that do the most damage.
- **RP-02 (Audience-Specific Framing):** the output is formatted to be read fast by someone unsettled,
  with verbatim sentences rather than guidance, because this is a prompt used mid-crisis.
- **QA-01 (Self-Verification):** the verification block confirms the danger-first ordering, the presence
  of verbatim words, and that the mentor's own load has been routed before the response is considered
  complete.
