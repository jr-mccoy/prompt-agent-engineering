# Session Plan Builder

**Objective:** Build the reusable shape of a discipling pair's regular meeting — how the time is
divided between catching up, Scripture, practice, and prayer, ending in one concrete next step — sized
honestly to the meeting length, with a shorter version for hard weeks and explicit permission for the
plan to yield when the conversation matters more than the agenda.

> **Boundary guardrail.** A session plan is a shape, not a script to be completed. If something surfaces
> that the plan did not anticipate — distress, a disclosure, a crisis — the plan is abandoned, not
> worked around; see `discipleship_hard_conversation_navigation.md`. Anything clinical, legal, medical,
> or safety-related routes out per `../mentor-equipping/discipleship_mentor_boundaries_and_referral.md`.

**When to use:** Setting up how a pair's meetings will run, or fixing meetings that have become either
aimless chat or content delivery.

**When NOT to use:**
- You need the teaching content for a module — use `discipleship_lesson_builder.md`.
- You need questions to draw on — use `discipleship_conversation_question_bank.md`.
- Something hard has just been said — use `discipleship_hard_conversation_navigation.md`.
- You are settling frequency and channel rather than the meeting's internal shape — use
  `../pairing-and-relationship/discipleship_cadence_and_rhythm_design.md`.

**Audience:** The mentor; a program lead producing a default session shape for all pairs.

---

## Inputs / Context

**Required:**

1. **Meeting length and channel.** The real length, including the arriving and leaving, and whether it
   is in person, video, phone, or a walk.
2. **What the pair is working through.** A curriculum, a book, a passage, or nothing formal.
3. **What has been happening in the meetings.** Whether they drift into chat, become content delivery,
   or run out before the important part.
4. **Both people's preparation capacity.** What either will realistically do between meetings — often
   nothing, and the plan must work if so.

**Optional:**

5. **Declared tradition (optional).** May shape whether prayer opens or closes, whether a set form is
   used, whether Scripture is read aloud, and whether silence has a place.
6. **The mentee's stage.** New believer, long-time believer, returning.
7. **What the mentee has said they want more or less of.**

**If any required input is missing:** Ask clarifying questions before proceeding. Ask specifically about
preparation capacity — plans that assume between-meeting work quietly collapse when it is not done, and
the mentee experiences that as their own failure.

---

## Constraints

### Must

- Allocate time in **actual minutes** that sum to the real meeting length, with arriving and leaving
  counted.
- Include an **opening check-in that is real**, not a formality — and protect enough time that a
  significant answer can be followed rather than deferred.
- End with **exactly one next step**, small, concrete, and named by the mentee where possible.
- Provide a **short version** for the meeting that starts late or where someone arrives depleted.
- Make the plan **work with no preparation** by either party, treating preparation as a bonus.
- State explicitly that the plan **yields** — if the check-in opens something that matters, the rest is
  dropped without apology.
- Balance the four elements so no single one — usually content — consumes the meeting by default.
- Route Scripture work to `domain-biblical-studies/` rather than building exegesis into the session
  plan.

### Must Not

- Design a plan that requires between-meeting preparation to function.
- Fill the whole meeting with content, or treat covering material as the measure of a good session.
- Make prayer, sharing, or disclosure compulsory at any point.
- Build in a review of whether the mentee completed their last step in a way that functions as
  inspection or produces shame for not having done it.
- Quote Scripture text from memory; addresses only, read from a Bible in the meeting.
- Invent research on meeting structure or attention spans.
- Allocate more minutes than the meeting has.
- Assume both parties arrive with energy, or that the meeting starts on time.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)

- **Must:** where a tradition has a customary session shape — opening prayer, a liturgical form, a set
  reading, examen, extempore prayer, silence — use it if both hold it and offer rather than assume where
  only one does.
- **Must Not:** treat any one stream's meeting shape as the natural default, or imply a session without
  spoken prayer is deficient.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Count the real minutes

Take the stated meeting length and subtract arriving, settling, and leaving. What remains is the actual
working time, and it is usually fifteen minutes less than people plan for.

### Step 2 — Diagnose the current drift

If meetings have become aimless chat, the check-in has no boundary and nothing follows it. If they have
become content delivery, the content block has no ceiling. Name which is happening, because the
allocation fixes it.

### Step 3 — Allocate across the four elements

Divide the working minutes:

| Element | What it is | Typical share |
|---|---|---|
| **Check-in** | How they actually are, followed rather than noted | The largest single block early on |
| **Scripture** | A passage read and discussed together | Substantial, but bounded |
| **Practice** | What they are trying to live, and how it is going | Small and consistent |
| **Prayer / close** | However this pair closes, plus the next step | Short and protected |

State the minutes. Protect the close from being consumed by the elements before it.

### Step 4 — Write the check-in so it is real

Give one or two opening questions that invite an honest answer, and state the rule: if the answer is
significant, it becomes the meeting. Content can wait a fortnight; the thing someone just said usually
cannot.

### Step 5 — Bound the Scripture block

Say what happens: the passage by address, read aloud, and two or three questions. Keep it bounded so it
cannot expand into a lesson. Route the exegetical work to
`domain-biblical-studies/exegesis-interpretation/` and the study method to
`study-methods-teaching/`.

### Step 6 — Design the practice check without inspection

Ask how the last step went in a way that makes "I didn't do it" an easy and consequence-free answer,
and treat that answer as information about the step's size, not the person's commitment.

### Step 7 — Build the short version and the next step

Write a ten-to-fifteen-minute version keeping only check-in and close. Then specify how the next step is
chosen — by the mentee, small, and concrete.

## Output Format

Produce exactly this structure.

```
# Session Shape — [pair], [n] minutes

## Real Working Time
- Meeting length: [..] | Arriving, settling, leaving: [..] | **Working minutes: [..]**

## What's Been Going Wrong
[Drift into chat / content delivery / running out before the important part] — and what fixes it.

## The Shape
| Element | Minutes | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Check-in | [..] | [..] |
| Scripture | [..] | [..] |
| Practice | [..] | [..] |
| Prayer / close + next step | [..] | [..] |
| **Total** | **[= working minutes]** | |

## The Check-in
> "[One or two questions that invite an honest answer.]"

**The rule: if the answer matters, it becomes the meeting.** Content waits. Nothing here needs
covering today.

## Scripture
- Passage: [address only — read it aloud from a Bible]
- Two or three questions: [..]
- Deeper study routes to `domain-biblical-studies/`.

## Practice Check — Not an Inspection
> "[Question that makes 'I didn't' an easy answer.]"

If it didn't happen, the step was probably too big. That's information about the step.

## Close and the Next Step
- How this pair closes: [..]
- The next step: chosen by [mentee], small enough to do this week.

## Short Version — [10-15] minutes
[Check-in and close only. Nothing else. This is a complete meeting, not a failed one.]

## When the Plan Yields
If something significant opens, drop the rest. No apology, no catching up next time.
```

## Verification

- [ ] Minutes sum to the real working time, with arriving and leaving subtracted.
- [ ] The check-in has protected time and an explicit rule that a significant answer becomes the meeting.
- [ ] The plan functions with no preparation by either party.
- [ ] The practice check is worded so "I didn't" is easy and consequence-free.
- [ ] A short version exists and is framed as a complete meeting.
- [ ] The session ends with exactly one next step, named by the mentee.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Plan to the full stated length. Fifteen minutes vanish into arriving, settling, and leaving, and a
  plan that ignores them ends by cutting the close.
- Let content have unbounded time. It is the element that expands, and it expands into exactly the
  parts of the meeting that matter more.
- Build the plan on between-meeting preparation. Most weeks it will not have happened, and a plan that
  depends on it converts an ordinary week into a failure.
- Turn the practice check into accountability. A mentee who dreads the question stops attempting the
  step rather than stops attending.
- Treat covering the material as the measure. A meeting where nothing was covered and something real
  was said is the better meeting.
- Make prayer compulsory. For some people, including new believers, being required to pray aloud is the
  most stressful part of the hour.

✅ **DO:**
- Write the yield rule into the plan explicitly, so the mentor does not feel they are abandoning their
  responsibility by following a conversation that matters.
- Give the check-in the largest early block. It is where the relationship's actual work usually happens.
- Bound the Scripture block by design and route deeper study out, so the session does not silently
  become a Bible class.
- Word the practice check so the honest answer is easy, and treat "I didn't" as evidence the step was
  too big.
- Build the short version before it is needed, and call it a complete meeting, so a depleted week does
  not become a skipped one.
- Have the mentee name the next step. Steps assigned by the mentor get performed; steps chosen get done.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** real working minutes are counted before allocation,
  so the plan is built against the meeting that exists rather than the one on the calendar.
- **ED-01 (Iterative Scaffolding):** the repeating check-in → Scripture → practice → next-step loop
  scaffolds formation across meetings, with the single next step carrying continuity between them.
- **OC-03 (Markdown Table Specification):** the allocation table totals to the working minutes, making
  an over-allocated plan structurally visible instead of discovered in the room.
- **CM-02 (Constraint Specification):** the no-preparation-required rule, the bounded content block,
  and the no-inspection practice check are hard constraints against the three ways one-to-one meetings
  reliably degrade.
- **QA-01 (Self-Verification):** the verification block confirms the arithmetic, the yield rule, and the
  presence of a short version before the shape is adopted.
