# Small Group Discipleship Format

**Objective:** Design a discipleship format for three to five people — with rotating responsibility so
it does not collapse into one leader and an audience, confidentiality rules that hold when several
people share them, deliberate protection for the quietest member, and a plan for the dynamics that only
appear once a relationship has more than two people in it.

> **Boundary guardrail.** A group multiplies both the chance of a hard disclosure and the number of
> people who hear it. Confidentiality is harder to hold and easier to breach, and a disclosure in a
> group cannot be un-heard. Anything clinical, abusive, or safety-related routes out immediately per
> `../mentor-equipping/discipleship_mentor_boundaries_and_referral.md` — and members must know before
> they speak that a group is not the place for what needs a professional.

**When to use:** Discipling three to five people together, either by design or because mentor capacity
requires it.

**When NOT to use:**
- It is a one-to-one pairing — use `discipleship_session_plan_builder.md`.
- It is a Bible-study group focused on the text — use
  `domain-biblical-studies/group-leader-facilitation/` and
  `study-methods-teaching/biblical_smallgroup_discussion_guide.md`.
- The group is larger than five — the format here does not scale, and the dynamics change again.
- You are handling a specific facilitation problem — dominance, silence, tangents, conflict — use
  `domain-biblical-studies/group-leader-facilitation/biblical_groupleader_facilitation_dynamics.md`.

**Audience:** A mentor leading a triad or small group; program leads choosing between pairs and groups.

---

## Inputs / Context

**Required:**

1. **Who is in it.** How many, their stages, and how well they already know each other — including any
   pre-existing relationship (spouses, colleagues, close friends, a supervisory relationship) that
   changes what can safely be said.
2. **Why a group rather than pairs.** Design choice or capacity constraint — this determines what the
   format must compensate for.
3. **Meeting length and frequency.**
4. **What they are working through.** A curriculum, a book, or nothing formal.

**Optional:**

5. **Declared tradition (optional).** May shape confession practice in a group, whether prayer is
   shared aloud, and who may lead.
6. **Known dynamics.** Anyone dominant, anyone silent, any tension already present.
7. **Whether responsibility rotates.** Or whether one person is the designated leader.

**If any required input is missing:** Ask clarifying questions before proceeding. Ask specifically about
pre-existing relationships inside the group — a person will not speak freely in front of their manager,
their spouse, or their close friend's partner, and no format compensates for that.

---

## Constraints

### Must

- Design **rotating responsibility** so the group does not become one teacher and an audience — and say
  what rotates and what does not.
- Write **group confidentiality** explicitly: what stays in the room, that it binds every member not
  just the leader, what the leader must still pass on, and what happens if it is breached.
- Protect the **quietest member** structurally — through the format itself, not through the leader
  remembering to include them.
- Guarantee **airtime** so that a dominant member cannot consume the meeting without the leader having
  to confront them in public.
- Surface **pre-existing relationships** and state what they make unsayable in this group.
- State plainly that **some things should not be shared here**, and where they go instead — before
  anyone shares them.
- Include the **absence rule**: what happens when someone misses, and how they are caught up without
  the group re-narrating another member's disclosure.
- Say what a group **does better than a pair**, and what it does worse, so the choice is honest.

### Must Not

- Design a group where one person always teaches and the others always receive.
- Rely on the leader noticing to include a quiet member — quiet members are missed precisely when the
  meeting is going well.
- Promise group confidentiality without stating its limits, or imply members can keep something the
  leader must pass on.
- Include public confession, mutual accountability disclosure, or sharing rounds that make declining
  conspicuous.
- Quote Scripture text from memory; addresses only.
- Invent group-dynamics research or optimal-size claims.
- Assume everyone can meet at the same time in the same place — group scheduling excludes more people
  than pair scheduling.
- Treat the group as a cheaper substitute for pairs without naming what is lost.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)

- **Must:** where a tradition has group conventions — shared confession, testimony, spoken prayer in
  turn, a designated leader by office — apply them if the group holds them and offer rather than assume
  where members differ.
- **Must Not:** import one stream's accountability-group conventions as the standard small-group form,
  or treat a group without shared confession as less serious.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Test whether a group is right

State why a group rather than pairs. If it is capacity, say so plainly and name what is being traded:
less individual attention, slower depth, and less safety for the most vulnerable member. That trade may
be worth making; it should be made knowingly.

### Step 2 — Map the pre-existing relationships

List every relationship already present among members. For each, state what it makes unsayable. A group
containing a manager and their report has a permanent ceiling, and the format cannot lift it — it can
only be honest about it.

### Step 3 — Write the confidentiality agreement

What stays in the room; that it binds every member; what the leader must still pass on and to whom;
what happens on a breach. Everyone agrees to it aloud at the first meeting, and it is repeated
periodically. Then say what should not be shared here at all, and where it goes.

### Step 4 — Design rotating responsibility

Decide what rotates — opening, leading the discussion, choosing the practice, closing — and what stays
with the leader, which is always the safeguarding and routing responsibility. Rotation is what stops a
group becoming a class.

### Step 5 — Build in airtime and protect the quiet member

Use a structural device rather than leader vigilance: a round where everyone speaks briefly and anyone
may pass, a timed check-in, or paired conversations inside the meeting. Make declining unremarkable by
building it into the device.

### Step 6 — Write the absence rule

What happens when someone misses: what they are told, what is not re-narrated, and how they rejoin
without the group repeating another member's disclosure to catch them up.

### Step 7 — State the honest comparison and check

Say what this group does better than pairs — shared experience, multiple perspectives, less
mentor-dependence, natural multiplication — and worse. Then check: does anyone always teach, is the
quiet member protected structurally, does confidentiality state its limits, are pre-existing
relationships named?

## Verification

- [ ] Responsibility rotates, with safeguarding and routing always retained by the leader.
- [ ] Group confidentiality binds every member and states its limits explicitly.
- [ ] The quiet member is protected by a structural device, not by leader vigilance.
- [ ] Pre-existing relationships are mapped with what each makes unsayable.
- [ ] An absence rule exists that does not re-narrate another member's disclosure.
- [ ] What a group does worse than a pair is stated honestly.

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Choose a group purely for capacity without naming the trade. It is often the right call, and it is
  always a trade.
- Rely on the leader to draw out the quiet member. In a good meeting the leader is absorbed in the
  conversation, which is exactly when the quiet member disappears.
- Run sharing rounds where everyone answers in turn without a pass option. Declining becomes visible,
  and a reticent member will over-share rather than be the one who passed.
- Promise the group's confidentiality without limits. Members will assume the leader is bound in the
  same way they are, and they are not.
- Put a manager and their report, or a couple, in the same group without naming the ceiling it creates.
- Let the group re-tell a member's disclosure to catch up whoever missed.

✅ **DO:**
- Have everyone say the confidentiality agreement aloud at the first meeting and repeat it periodically.
  Written-only agreements are forgotten by meeting four.
- Rotate the visible responsibilities and keep safeguarding with the leader, so the group shares
  ownership without diffusing accountability.
- Build passing into the structural device, so declining is a normal move rather than a statement.
- Name what pre-existing relationships make unsayable, since the format cannot fix it and pretending
  otherwise is worse.
- State what should not be shared in the group *before* anyone shares it, and route it.
- Give the honest group-versus-pair comparison, so the format is chosen rather than defaulted into.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** the group-versus-pair test and relationship map
  precede format design, so the format is built against the group that exists rather than an idealized
  one.
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework):** confidentiality, airtime, rotation, absence, and
  pre-existing relationships are separate design axes, each with a failure mode that only appears above
  two people.
- **CM-02 (Constraint Specification):** the structural-protection rule, the limits-stated
  confidentiality rule, and the ban on conspicuous sharing rounds are hard constraints against the
  group-specific harms.
- **OC-03 (Markdown Table Specification):** the rotation table shows what stays with the leader in the
  same view as what rotates, making diffused safeguarding accountability structurally visible.
- **QA-01 (Self-Verification):** the four-check design review confirms rotation, structural protection,
  stated confidentiality limits, and named relationships before the group starts.
