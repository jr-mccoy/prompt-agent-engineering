# Lesson Builder

**Objective:** Build the content for one discipleship session inside a module — the single question it
answers, the Scripture it works from by address, discussion questions that are genuinely open, one
practice to attempt before next time, and honest treatment of anything traditions disagree about —
while handing every piece of exegetical work to `domain-biblical-studies/` rather than attempting it
here.

> **Boundary guardrail.** A lesson forms understanding and practice; it is not counseling, and a module
> touching weighty material — suffering, forgiveness after harm, sexuality, family conflict, money
> pressure — must say so in advance and note that a participant in distress routes out rather than
> being taught through it. See `discipleship_hard_conversation_navigation.md`.

**When to use:** You have a module from the curriculum and need the actual session content for a pair
or a small group.

**When NOT to use:**
- You need the meeting's time structure rather than its content — use
  `discipleship_session_plan_builder.md`.
- You have not sequenced the modules — use
  `../curriculum-architecture/discipleship_module_scope_and_sequence.md`.
- You are building a Bible-study lesson for a class or group — use
  `domain-biblical-studies/study-methods-teaching/biblical_lesson_plan_builder.md`.
- You need the passage worked out first — use
  `domain-biblical-studies/exegesis-interpretation/biblical_passage_observation_beginner.md` and the
  rest of that subdirectory.

**Audience:** Mentors preparing a session; curriculum designers producing sessions for others to use.

---

## Inputs / Context

**Required:**

1. **The module and its place.** Which module, which stage, and what has already been covered.
2. **The question this session answers.** One question. If several are offered, narrow to one.
3. **The Scripture.** Passage addresses the module works from, and which translation the pair uses.
4. **Who is in the room.** Their starting point, reading fluency, and whether this is a pair or a small
   group.
5. **Available time.** The minutes the session's Scripture and practice blocks actually have — from
   `discipleship_session_plan_builder.md`.

**Optional:**

6. **Declared tradition (optional).** May determine which reading is foregrounded on a contested point,
   which is then labelled as that stream's reading with alternatives kept visible.
7. **The previous session's next step.** So this session connects rather than restarts.
8. **Known sensitivities.** Anything in the room that this content might touch.

**If any required input is missing:** Ask clarifying questions before proceeding. Do not proceed without
the passage addresses — a lesson built around remembered content will misattribute Scripture, which is
this prompt's highest-consequence failure.

---

## Constraints

### Must

- Answer **one question** per session. A session with three questions has none.
- Reference Scripture **by address only**, naming the translation to read from. The passage is read
  aloud in the meeting from an actual Bible.
- Write discussion questions that are **genuinely open** — ones the mentor cannot predict the answer to
  and that do not contain their own conclusion.
- Include **one practice** to attempt before next time, small and concrete.
- Flag anything **traditions disagree about**, naming the streams and their positions, rather than
  resolving it.
- Fit the **stated minutes**, and say what was cut.
- Route all exegetical work — word meanings, historical background, genre, difficult passages — to the
  relevant `domain-biblical-studies/` prompt by name.
- Note where the content might **touch something painful**, and what the mentor does if it does.

### Must Not

- Quote, paraphrase, or assert Scripture wording from memory. Addresses only.
- Assert historical, cultural, linguistic, or archaeological claims. Route them or mark `[VERIFY]`.
- Resolve a contested interpretive question, or present one tradition's reading as the plain meaning.
- Write questions whose answer is contained in the question, or that have one acceptable answer the
  mentor is waiting for.
- Invent illustrations presented as true events, statistics, or research.
- Include more content than the stated minutes allow.
- Assume reading fluency, prior biblical knowledge, or familiarity with church vocabulary without
  saying so.
- Design a practice that requires privacy, money, transport, or a quiet hour without offering an
  alternative.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)

- **Must:** where the session's question is answered differently across streams, present the positions
  with attribution and let a declared tradition foreground its own while alternatives remain visible;
  say plainly when a question is genuinely disputed rather than settled.
- **Must Not:** smooth a real disagreement into a single consensus answer, or teach a stream's
  distinctive as though it were the shared Christian position.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Fix the one question

State the single question this session answers, in plain words a participant would use. If the module
suggests several, choose one and note the others as future sessions.

### Step 2 — Set the Scripture and route the exegesis

Give the passage addresses and the translation. Name which `domain-biblical-studies/` prompt handles the
interpretive work — genre, background, difficult verses, word study — and do not attempt it here. If the
passage is genuinely hard, say so and route it.

### Step 3 — Write the opening

Two or three minutes that connect to the previous session's step and open the question — without
telegraphing the answer.

### Step 4 — Write genuinely open discussion questions

Three or four. Test each: could a thoughtful person answer this in a way the mentor did not expect? If
not, it is a comprehension check dressed as discussion — rewrite it. Include at least one that invites
disagreement with the text or the reading.

### Step 5 — Flag the contested points

Identify anything in this session traditions disagree about. Name the disagreement, the streams, and
what the mentor says: that this is genuinely disputed, what the positions are, and where to read more.

### Step 6 — Design the practice

One practice, small, concrete, attemptable in an ordinary week. Give an alternative for anyone without
privacy, money, transport, or a quiet hour.

### Step 7 — Note the sensitivities and fit the time

Say where this content could touch something painful and what the mentor does — including that
distress routes out rather than being taught through. Then total the minutes and state what was cut.

## Output Format

Produce exactly this structure.

```
# Session — [module], [n] of [total]

## The Question
[One question, in plain words.]

**Deferred to later sessions:** [..]

## Scripture
- Read: [addresses only] — from [translation], aloud, from an actual Bible.
- **Interpretive work routes to:** [named domain-biblical-studies/ prompt]
- Anything hard here: [..] → [named prompt]

## Opening — [n] min
[Connects to last session's step, opens the question, doesn't telegraph the answer.]

## Discussion — [n] min
| Question | Could it surprise you? |
|---|---|
| "[..]" | yes — [why] |
| "[..]" | yes — [why] |
| "[..]" (invites disagreement) | yes — [why] |

## Where Traditions Differ
| The point | Positions and streams | What you say |
|---|---|---|
| [..] | [..] | "This one is genuinely disputed. Here's the range…" |

[If nothing in this session is contested: say so.]

## The Practice — [n] min to set up
- What: [small, concrete, this week]
- If you have no privacy / money / transport / quiet hour: [alternative]

## If This Touches Something Painful
[Where it might, what the mentor does, and that distress routes out — not through.]

## Time
| Block | Minutes |
|---|---|
| **Total** | **[= available]** |

**Cut to fit:** [..]

## [VERIFY]
Any historical, cultural, or linguistic claim here is marked [VERIFY] and confirmed against a named
resource — not asserted from memory.
```

---

## Verification

- [ ] Exactly one question is answered; others are deferred explicitly.
- [ ] All Scripture is addresses only, with a named translation; no wording is quoted or asserted.
- [ ] Every discussion question could produce an answer the mentor did not expect.
- [ ] Contested points are flagged with streams and positions, not resolved.
- [ ] One practice, with an alternative for constrained circumstances.
- [ ] Minutes total the available time, with cuts named; exegetical work is routed by prompt name.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Write questions with a right answer the mentor is waiting for. Participants detect it instantly and
  begin guessing what the mentor wants rather than thinking.
- Paraphrase a verse to make the point. Paraphrase from memory is how misquotation enters a curriculum
  and stays for years.
- Explain what a Greek or Hebrew word "really means." That is the single highest-fabrication-risk move
  available, and it routes to `domain-biblical-studies/original-languages/`.
- Resolve a contested point because the disagreement is inconvenient in a short session. Say it is
  disputed and give the range; that takes thirty seconds.
- Invent an illustration and present it as something that happened.
- Design a practice needing a quiet half hour without an alternative — for many participants that does
  not exist.

✅ **DO:**
- Narrow to one question and name what was deferred, so the mentor is not privately trying to cover
  three things.
- Give addresses and have the passage read aloud from a real Bible in the meeting. The reading is part
  of the formation.
- Test each discussion question against the surprise standard and rewrite the ones that fail.
- Include a question that invites disagreement with the text or the reading — it is what makes
  discussion real rather than performed.
- Route the interpretive work by prompt name, so the mentor knows exactly where to go when a hard
  question arrives.
- State where the content might land painfully, so the mentor is not caught unprepared by a reaction
  the material predictably provokes.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** the one question is fixed and the exegesis routed
  before any content is written, so the lesson cannot drift into a Bible study the mentor is not
  equipped to lead.
- **ED-01 (Iterative Scaffolding):** each session opens from the previous session's practice and closes
  with the next one, making the module a connected sequence rather than a series of standalone talks.
- **CM-02 (Constraint Specification):** the no-paraphrase, no-historical-claim, and no-resolution-of-
  contested-points constraints target the three ways lesson content most reliably introduces error.
- **QA-05 (Citation Requirements):** Scripture by address with a named translation, exegesis routed by
  prompt name, and `[VERIFY]` on every factual claim form the domain's anti-fabrication discipline at
  the point where it matters most.
- **OC-03 (Markdown Table Specification):** the discussion-question table carries a "could it surprise
  you" column, which makes a closed question visible in the design rather than in the room.
