# Safeguarding and Conduct Policy

**Objective:** Draft the safeguarding and conduct policy a discipleship program operates under —
screening, conduct expectations, visibility norms, reporting paths, and how a concern is handled — as a
usable document whose every legal requirement is flagged for verification with qualified local counsel
rather than asserted.

> **STRONG-GUARD prompt.** Safeguarding is where a confident, plausible, wrong answer does the most
> damage. Language models routinely assert mandated-reporter thresholds, background-check requirements,
> ages of consent, retention periods, notification timeframes, and the names of statutory agencies —
> all of which vary by country, state, role, and organization type, and none of which this prompt may
> state. **Every legal or statutory point is `[VERIFY]` and routed to qualified local counsel and the
> relevant statutory body via `domain-legal/`.** This prompt produces the policy's *structure and
> conduct content*; a qualified professional supplies its legal content. A policy adopted without that
> step is worse than no policy, because it looks like diligence.

> **Boundary guardrail.** This document does not make anyone safe by existing. Screening, training,
> visibility, and a working reporting path do that. Where a live concern exists now, it does not wait
> for a policy — it routes immediately to the appropriate authority
> `[VERIFY: identify the correct body from an official source]`.

**When to use:** Standing up a discipleship or mentoring program — before matching anyone. Or reviewing
a policy that was written once and never tested.

**When NOT to use:**
- A concern has arisen right now — route it; do not write policy.
- You are designing matching criteria — use
  `../pairing-and-relationship/discipleship_pairing_criteria_design.md`, which this policy constrains.
- You are setting one mentor's scope — use
  `../mentor-equipping/discipleship_mentor_boundaries_and_referral.md`.
- You need the whole program design — use `discipleship_program_design_blueprint.md`.

**Audience:** Program leads, church leadership, and platform operators — working with qualified counsel
and a designated safeguarding lead.

---

## Inputs / Context

**Required:**

1. **Who participates.** Adults only, or minors and vulnerable adults — which changes nearly every
   section and adds requirements this prompt will not state.
2. **The organization.** What kind of body runs this — church, charity, company, informal network — and
   under what jurisdiction. Both determine what applies.
3. **Who the safeguarding lead is.** A named role, with the authority to act. If there is none, that is
   the first finding.
4. **How people meet.** In person, video, asynchronous, in homes, in public places — since visibility
   norms differ for each.
5. **What already exists.** Any existing policy, insurer requirement, denominational framework, or
   platform terms.

**Optional:**

6. **Declared tradition (optional).** Denominational safeguarding frameworks frequently exist and take
   precedence; where one does, this policy conforms to it rather than competing with it.
7. **Prior incidents.** How they were handled, and what failed.
8. **Insurance and regulatory context.** Which often imposes concrete requirements.

**If any required input is missing:** Ask clarifying questions before proceeding. If no safeguarding
lead exists, say plainly that the policy cannot be completed without one — a policy with no owner and no
route is a document, not a safeguard.

---

## Constraints

### Must

- Mark **every legal, statutory, or regulatory point `[VERIFY]`**, name who confirms it, and route to
  `domain-legal/` and qualified local counsel.
- Name a **safeguarding lead by role**, with a deputy for when they are unavailable or are the subject
  of the concern.
- Define **conduct expectations concretely** — not "act appropriately" but the specific behaviours
  expected and prohibited.
- Set **visibility norms** for each meeting channel, including video and asynchronous messaging, which
  most policies address only for in-person meetings.
- Define the **reporting path**: who a concern goes to, in what timeframe, what happens if the concern
  is about the lead, and what happens if the person reporting is dissatisfied.
- State that **a concern is never handled internally instead of** being referred to statutory
  authorities where referral is required `[VERIFY]`.
- Include the **response to a concern**: immediate steps, who is suspended from contact pending review,
  and support for the person who raised it.
- Include a **review cycle** and a test of whether the reporting path actually works.
- Cover **records**: what is kept, who can see it, and where retention is `[VERIFY]`.

### Must Not

- State any age threshold, mandated-reporter duty, background-check requirement, notification
  timeframe, retention period, or statutory offence.
- Name a statutory agency, regulator, helpline, police contact route, or check provider.
- Suggest that a concern be assessed internally first to determine whether it is serious enough to
  refer.
- Suggest that forgiveness, reconciliation, restoration, or pastoral process substitutes for, precedes,
  or replaces a referral.
- Rely on the character or reputation of a volunteer in place of screening.
- Invent safeguarding standards, statistics, or frameworks, or attribute a framework to a named body.
- Quote Scripture text from memory, and do not use passages about forgiveness, restoration, or not
  judging to soften a safeguarding provision.
- Produce a policy so long nobody reads it, or so vague it cannot be breached.
- Treat the policy's existence as evidence of safety.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)

- **Must:** where a denomination or network has its own safeguarding framework, conform to it and say
  so; where a tradition has confession practices with claimed confidentiality, flag the interaction with
  reporting obligations as `[VERIFY]` with counsel rather than resolving it here — this is a genuinely
  contested and jurisdiction-specific area.
- **Must Not:** allow any tradition's internal process, confessional practice, or authority structure to
  displace a statutory referral obligation, or present a denominational framework as satisfying legal
  requirements without verification.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Establish scope and who confirms the law

State who participates, what organization runs this, and the jurisdiction. Then name explicitly who
supplies the legal content: qualified local counsel and the relevant statutory body. Everything legal in
this document is a placeholder until they fill it.

### Step 2 — Name the lead and the deputy

A role, not a person's disposition. Include the deputy path for when the lead is unavailable, and the
escalation path for when the concern is about the lead — which most policies omit and which is exactly
the case where a policy is most needed.

### Step 3 — Write screening as structure, not judgement

State what screening consists of for each role, marking every check requirement `[VERIFY]`. Include
references, application, interview, and a stated period of being known to the community where that is
the program's rule. Say explicitly that reputation is not a substitute.

### Step 4 — Write conduct expectations concretely

Specific behaviours: where meetings happen, what contact outside meetings is permitted, what is never
done alone, gifts, money, transport, physical contact, social media contact, and messaging out of hours.
Concrete enough that a breach is identifiable.

### Step 5 — Set visibility norms per channel

For in person, video, phone, and messaging: what makes each visible and accountable. Cover the channels
most policies miss — a video call has no observer, and asynchronous messaging leaves a record nobody
reads.

### Step 6 — Write the reporting path and the response

Who a concern goes to, in what form, in what timeframe `[VERIFY]`, and the alternative route if the
concern is about the lead. Then the response: who stops having contact pending review, who is told,
what support the person raising it receives, and the explicit statement that internal process never
substitutes for a required referral.

### Step 7 — Add records, review, and the working test

State what is recorded and who sees it, with retention `[VERIFY]`. Set a review cycle. Then design the
test: can a participant actually name who they would tell and reach them? A reporting path nobody knows
is not a reporting path.

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- State what the law requires. It varies by jurisdiction, organization type, role, and the participant's
  age, and a confident wrong statement here causes serious harm in both directions — under-reporting
  and over-reporting.
- Name the agency to contact. Names, remits, and routes change, and a wrong one delays a referral at
  the worst possible moment.
- Build in an internal assessment step before referral. It is the mechanism by which institutions have
  repeatedly failed, and it is usually introduced with good intentions.
- Exempt long-standing, well-known, or senior people from screening. That exemption is the single most
  common failure pattern in faith-community safeguarding.
- Write "act appropriately." A policy that cannot be breached cannot be enforced.
- Let forgiveness or restoration language enter the response section. It belongs to a different process,
  later, and never as a precondition.

✅ **DO:**
- Put the `[VERIFY]` register at the top and again at the bottom, so the policy cannot be adopted while
  looking complete.
- Write the route for a concern about the safeguarding lead. It is the case most often omitted and the
  one where omission is most costly.
- Set visibility norms for video and messaging explicitly — the channels where a discipling relationship
  now mostly lives and where most policies say nothing.
- Make conduct expectations concrete enough to identify a breach: named locations, named contact rules,
  named limits on money and transport.
- Run the working test annually — ask participants without warning who they would tell. The answer is
  the real state of the policy.
- Say plainly, in the document, that the policy's existence is not safety. Screening, training,
  visibility, and a path people know are.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** scope and the legal-confirmation owner are fixed
  before any content is drafted, so no section can be written as though the legal position were known.
- **CM-02 (Constraint Specification):** the Must Not block is the STRONG-GUARD itself — no threshold, no
  agency name, no internal-assessment step, no exemption by reputation, no restoration before referral.
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework):** screening, conduct, visibility, reporting, response,
  and records are treated as separate axes, so a policy strong on screening cannot mask an absent
  reporting path.
- **OC-03 (Markdown Table Specification):** the conduct and visibility tables force per-area and
  per-channel specificity, which is what converts "act appropriately" into an enforceable expectation.
- **QA-05 (Citation Requirements):** the `[VERIFY]` register names a confirmer for every legal point and
  appears twice in the document, so the policy cannot be adopted while its legal content is still
  placeholder.
