# Minimum Viable Program

**Objective:** Establish what a discipleship program actually requires at the scale most of them run at —
three pairs, one leader, no budget, and that leader holding a job as well — by separating the floor that
does not move at any size from the structure that exists only because larger programs have people to run
it, sizing what remains to real hours, and saying plainly which parts a solo leader should not attempt at
all.

> **Boundary guardrail.** Small does not mean lighter on safeguarding. The floor below — screening
> appropriate to the setting, conduct expectations, visibility, a named second person, and a route for a
> concern that does not run through the leader alone — is the part that does not scale down, and a program
> that cannot meet it should run fewer pairs rather than a thinner floor. Where minors or vulnerable adults
> are involved, requirements are set by law and by whatever body the church sits under, never by capacity
> `[VERIFY: confirm requirements with qualified local counsel and the relevant statutory body]`. This
> prompt states no legal requirement of any kind.

**When to use:** When a small church, a plant, or a bivocational or volunteer leader wants to start or run
discipleship pairing and the full blueprint reads like something written for an organization they do not
have.

**When NOT to use:**
- You have staff, a budget, and a cohort — use `discipleship_program_design_blueprint.md`, which this
  prompt reduces rather than replaces.
- There is no program and no intention of one, just two people meeting — use
  `../pairing-and-relationship/discipleship_informal_pairing_without_a_program.md`.
- You are writing the safeguarding policy itself — use
  `discipleship_safeguarding_and_conduct_policy.md`. Its output is not optional at any size and is not
  reduced here.
- You are designing a congregation-wide pathway rather than a pairing program — use
  `domain-biblical-studies/church-staff-ministry-ops/biblical_churchstaff_discipleship_pathway_design.md`.
- The constraint is an external body's rules rather than your own capacity — use `../context-variants/`,
  where someone else's rules govern.

**Audience:** A solo pastor, a bivocational leader, a church planter, or the volunteer who has been asked
to "get discipleship going" and has four hours a month.

---

## Inputs / Context

**Required:**

1. **Real hours available, per month, from the person who will actually do it.** Not the hours they wish
   they had and not the hours in the role description. Everything below is sized to this number, and a
   program designed for hours that do not exist fails in month three with the pairs still running.
2. **How many pairs, and who is available to mentor.** Actual named people, not a hope. Three pairs and two
   possible mentors is a different design from a waiting list of twelve.
3. **Who the second person could be.** Anyone other than the leader whom a participant could go to. Another
   leader, a trustee, someone from a neighbouring church, a denominational contact. If nobody can be named,
   that is the finding that governs everything else.
4. **Whether any minors or vulnerable adults are involved.** If yes, the requirements are external and
   fixed, and capacity is not an input to them.

**Optional:**

5. **Declared tradition (optional).** May supply an existing denominational or network safeguarding
   framework, a required reporting line, or an overseeing body — all of which reduce what has to be built
   locally, and are usually the single largest saving available to a small church. Where a framework
   exists, conform to it rather than writing a local one. Where none is declared, the design stays
   structure-general.
6. **What the church already has** for other ministries — a policy, a DBS or background-check process, an
   existing safeguarding lead. Reusing these is almost always right.
7. **What has been tried before**, and what stopped.

**If any required input is missing:** Ask, starting with the hours. If no second person can be named
anywhere — including outside the church — say plainly that the program should not run until one is found,
and that finding one is the first task. A pairing program in which every route runs through the one person
who also does the pairing has no route at all.

## Constraints

### Must

- Separate the output into **floor** (does not move at any size), **deferred** (real, but not at three
  pairs), and **not attempted** (needs people this program does not have) — and put every element of the
  full blueprint into exactly one.
- Hold the **safeguarding floor fixed**, and state that the correct response to insufficient capacity is
  fewer pairs, never a thinner floor.
- Require a **named second person** who is not the leader, and treat the absence of one as a stop
  condition rather than a risk to note.
- Require a **route for a concern that does not pass through the leader**, including where the concern is
  about the leader — which in a one-leader church is the hardest and most necessary item here.
- **Size everything to the stated hours** and show the arithmetic, including the leader's own time in each
  recurring commitment.
- Say what the program does when it **cannot meet demand**: a waiting list, a smaller cohort, or not
  starting — never lowering the mentor bar, which is the standing rule the pipeline prompt already sets.
- Name a **growth trigger** for each deferred item: the number of pairs, or the event, at which it stops
  being deferrable.
- Include the **succession question** — what happens to the pairs if the leader leaves, is ill, or steps
  down — because a three-pair program is usually one person away from ending without anyone noticing.
- Reuse the **denomination's or network's existing framework** wherever one exists rather than writing a
  local substitute.

### Must Not

- Reduce, simplify, or defer any element of safeguarding, screening, conduct expectations, or visibility on
  grounds of size, informality, mutual knowledge, or trust.
- Treat "we all know each other" as a substitute for structure — it is the condition under which most small
  organizations discover they had none.
- Recommend that the leader be the only route for a concern, or design a process in which a complaint about
  the leader goes to the leader.
- State any legal, statutory, screening, background-check, reporting, or retention requirement. Mark every
  such point `[VERIFY]` and route to counsel and the relevant body.
- Name any agency, service, checking body, or denomination-specific process from memory.
- Design a program that requires more hours than were stated, or leave the arithmetic implicit.
- Suggest that lower capacity justifies pairing people who should not be paired, or shortening mentor
  readiness.
- Quote Scripture text from memory, or use any passage to argue that structure reflects a lack of faith.
- Produce any score or rating of the program's maturity, health, or readiness.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)

- **Must:** note where traditions differ on who may authorize a ministry, whether a denominational
  safeguarding framework already applies, whether an external overseer or bishop must be informed, and
  whether lay leadership of such a program is ordinary — and where an existing framework or oversight
  exists, treat conforming to it as the design rather than as an extra step.
- **Must Not:** rule on which polity is correct, or allow a tradition's congregational independence to be
  used as a reason to have no external route for a concern. Independence of governance is not independence
  from having somewhere to go.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Get the real number of hours

Ask for the honest monthly figure from the person who will do the work. Write it at the top of the output
and treat it as the binding constraint for everything below. A design that quietly exceeds it produces a
program that runs for one term.

### Step 2 — Fix the floor before anything is cut

State what does not move: screening appropriate to the setting and to who is involved `[VERIFY]`, written
conduct expectations, visibility norms, a named second person, and a route for a concern that bypasses the
leader. Establish these first, so that the reductions that follow are made from what remains rather than
from here.

### Step 3 — Solve the route-past-the-leader problem explicitly

In a one-leader church this is the hard part and it is usually skipped. Name a real person outside the
leader's authority — another church's leader, a denominational or network contact, a trustee — and write
what participants are told about them, at the start, before anything is wrong.

### Step 4 — Sort the full blueprint into three columns

Take each element of a full program — intake forms, matching criteria, mentor training cohorts, onboarding
sessions, health reviews, pipeline planning, curriculum architecture — and place it in floor, deferred, or
not attempted. Give each deferred item the trigger at which it returns.

### Step 5 — Do the arithmetic out loud

For what remains, add up the leader's hours: pairing conversations, check-ins, the review, the unexpected.
Compare with the stated figure. If it exceeds, cut pairs rather than cutting the floor, and say so in the
output.

### Step 6 — Answer the demand and succession questions

Write what happens when more people want a mentor than there are mentors — waiting list, smaller cohort,
or not yet — with the bar held fixed. Then write what happens to the existing pairs if the leader stops:
who knows they exist, who would pick it up, and what the pairs are told now rather than then.

### Step 7 — Check what was cut

Review the deferred and not-attempted columns against the floor. Did anything safeguarding-shaped drift out
of the floor because of hours? Does every concern route bypass the leader? Is the arithmetic shown? Would
this program survive the leader being unavailable for two months?

## Verification

- [ ] The available hours are stated at the top and the arithmetic is shown against them.
- [ ] Every element of a full program is sorted into exactly one of floor, deferred, or not attempted.
- [ ] Nothing safeguarding-shaped appears outside the floor.
- [ ] A named second person exists, and a concern about the leader has a route that does not reach them.
- [ ] Deferred items each carry a trigger for their return.
- [ ] No legal, screening, or reporting requirement is stated — all `[VERIFY]` and routed.

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Scale down safeguarding with everything else. It is the one column that does not respond to size, and a
  small program is where the reasoning "we all know each other" is most persuasive and least true.
- Leave the leader as the only route. A concern about the person who runs the program, reported to the
  person who runs the program, is not a route — and this is the most commonly skipped item in a small
  church because solving it means asking an outsider for help.
- Design to the hours in the role description. The gap between contracted and actual hours is where the
  program quietly dies, usually around month four.
- Lower the mentor bar to meet demand. Fewer pairs is the answer; the pipeline prompt already fixes this
  and size does not create an exception.
- Write a local safeguarding framework when the denomination has one. It is more work and less protection.
- Skip succession because there are only three pairs. Three pairs with one person holding them is precisely
  the case where a leader's departure ends the relationships silently.
- Defer something and never name the trigger. Deferred without a trigger means abandoned with a note.

✅ **DO:**
- Start with the honest hours and show the arithmetic where everyone can see it.
- Fix the floor before making any cut, so reductions are made from what is left over.
- Solve the route-past-the-leader problem with a real named person, even if it means asking a neighbouring
  church or a network contact.
- Sort every blueprint element into exactly one column, so nothing is silently half-implemented.
- Reuse whatever framework the denomination, network, or the church's other ministries already have.
- Answer the succession question now, and tell the pairs the answer.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** the floor is fixed before anything is cut, which is the
  ordering that stops capacity pressure from reaching safeguarding by way of a hundred small reasonable
  decisions.
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework):** hours, safeguarding obligation, demand, and succession
  are worked as separate axes, so a program that fits the calendar cannot pass while having no route for a
  concern.
- **CM-02 (Constraint Specification):** the Must Not block prohibits the specific small-church rationales —
  mutual knowledge, informality, trust — that convert an absent structure into a stated virtue.
- **OC-03 (Markdown Table Specification):** the three-column sort forces every blueprint element to land
  somewhere explicit, which is what prevents the common outcome of a program half-implementing everything.
- **QA-01 (Self-Verification):** the final check looks specifically for safeguarding items that drifted out
  of the floor under time pressure — the failure this prompt exists to prevent.
