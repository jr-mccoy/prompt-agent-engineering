# Program Health Review

**Objective:** Review how a discipleship pairing program is actually doing — which pairs have stalled,
where people are leaving, who is unmatched, how loaded the mentors are — while keeping a hard line
between what activity data can show and what only conversation reveals, and refusing to let meeting
counts stand in for whether anyone is being formed.

> **Boundary guardrail.** A health review looks at the program, never at individuals' spiritual state.
> It must not produce rankings of mentors, scores for pairs, or any assessment of participants' growth.
> Where a review surfaces a safeguarding concern, that leaves the review immediately and routes to the
> safeguarding lead `[VERIFY: identify the correct process from an official source]` rather than
> appearing as a finding.

**When to use:** On a set review cadence, or when pairs have gone quiet, mentors are leaving, or
leadership is asking whether the program is working.

**When NOT to use:**
- The question is about curriculum content rather than program operation — use
  `../curriculum-architecture/discipleship_curriculum_balance_audit.md`.
- One mentor's season is being reviewed — use
  `../mentor-equipping/discipleship_mentor_season_debrief.md`.
- The issue is mentor supply — use `discipleship_mentor_pipeline_and_capacity.md`.
- The program has not launched — use `discipleship_program_design_blueprint.md`.

**Audience:** Program leads and leadership. Findings are for improving the program, never for
evaluating individuals.

---

## Inputs / Context

**Required:**

1. **What you can actually see.** Meeting frequency, pair status, completions, withdrawals, unmatched
   numbers — and honestly, which of these you do not track.
2. **What people have said.** Debriefs, feedback, exit comments, or informal reports. Usually more
   informative than the counts.
3. **The period under review.** And what changed during it: staffing, curriculum, recruitment,
   circumstances.
4. **The program's stated purpose.** So health is measured against what it set out to do rather than
   against activity.

**Optional:**

5. **Declared tradition (optional).** May shape what leadership expects to see reported and to whom.
6. **Prior reviews.** For trend rather than snapshot.
7. **Known incidents.** Handled separately from health findings.

**If any required input is missing:** Ask clarifying questions before proceeding. Ask what is *not*
tracked — the gaps determine which questions this review can answer, and they must bound every finding.

---

## Constraints

### Must

- State **what is measured and what is not** before any finding, and bound the findings accordingly.
- Distinguish **what activity data can show** (meetings happening, pairs continuing, people entering and
  leaving) from **what it cannot** (whether anyone is being formed, whether relationships are good,
  whether the quiet pairs are thriving or failing).
- Separate **stalled** from **completed** from **ended-well** from **quietly-abandoned**, since they
  look identical in a spreadsheet and mean different things.
- Investigate attrition by **asking people**, and report where nobody has asked.
- Report **mentor load** and unmatched participants as first-class findings, not footnotes.
- Grade findings by confidence: **evidenced**, **probable**, **unknown**.
- Produce at most **three changes** for the next period, each with an owner.
- Route any safeguarding matter **out of the review** rather than reporting it as a finding.

### Must Not

- Use meeting counts, completion rates, or attendance as a measure of formation.
- Rank, score, or compare mentors, or produce anything usable as a mentor performance assessment.
- Assess participants' spiritual growth, or aggregate anything about their formation.
- Infer why someone left without asking them. Report it as unknown.
- Invent benchmarks, sector comparisons, or "healthy" attrition rates.
- Quote Scripture text from memory.
- Treat all attrition as a problem — some endings are completions, and some are people correctly
  deciding this is not for them.
- Produce more than three changes, or changes without owners.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)

- **Must:** where leadership expects reporting in a particular form — to elders, a board, a
  denominational body — produce it in that form while keeping the no-individual-assessment rule intact.
- **Must Not:** allow a reporting expectation to convert this into an evaluation of individuals'
  faithfulness or a ranking of mentors.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Bound the review

State what is tracked, what is not, and what that means for the findings. A program that does not know
why people left cannot report on attrition causes, and saying so is the first honest finding.

### Step 2 — Classify every pair

Sort each pair into: active and meeting · active but slowed · stalled · completed · ended well ·
quietly abandoned · never started. The last two are usually collapsed into "attrition" and they are very
different problems.

### Step 3 — Investigate the stalls and the abandonments

For pairs that stalled or faded, report what is known and — crucially — whether anyone asked. Where
nobody asked, that is the finding and the action, not a gap to be filled with inference.

### Step 4 — Report mentor load and the unmatched

Mentors carrying more than the stated capacity limit, and participants unmatched beyond the no-match
threshold. Both are structural findings and both are usually visible in the data the program already has.

### Step 5 — Separate what the data cannot see

Write explicitly what this review cannot determine: whether the quiet pairs are deep or dying, whether
formation is happening, whether the curriculum is landing, whether anyone was harmed. Name what would
be needed to know — usually conversations, not more tracking.

### Step 6 — Measure against stated purpose

Compare findings to what the program set out to do. A program with perfect meeting attendance and no
formation is failing, and only this comparison surfaces it.

### Step 7 — Choose three changes

At most three, each with an owner and a date. Say what was considered and not chosen, so the next review
does not rediscover it.

## Verification

- [ ] What is and is not tracked is stated before any finding, and findings respect the bound.
- [ ] Pairs are classified into all seven states, not collapsed into active/inactive.
- [ ] Attrition causes are reported as unknown where nobody asked, rather than inferred.
- [ ] No mentor ranking, mentor score, or assessment of participants' growth appears.
- [ ] The "what the data cannot tell us" section explicitly states that meeting counts do not measure
      formation.
- [ ] At most three changes, each with an owner and a date.

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Report meeting frequency as program health. It measures whether meetings happen, which is a
  precondition for formation and not evidence of it.
- Collapse completions and abandonments into an attrition number. One is the program working and the
  other is it failing, and the number hides which.
- Infer why someone left. The most common reasons — capacity, life change, mismatch — are not
  distinguishable from the outside, and guessing produces confident wrong fixes.
- Produce anything that ranks mentors. It will be used for evaluation regardless of what the header
  says, and mentors will stop being honest in debriefs.
- Treat all attrition as failure. Someone deciding this is not for them, and leaving cleanly, is the
  program working.
- Respond to unknowns by adding tracking. Most of what matters here is found by asking people, not by
  measuring more.

✅ **DO:**
- Put the bound first and let it constrain every finding. A review that overstates what it knows
  produces changes aimed at imagined problems.
- Use all seven pair states. The distinction between "ended well" and "quietly abandoned" is the single
  most informative split available.
- Report "nobody asked" as a finding with an action, since it is both true and fixable.
- Name mentor overload and long unmatched waits as structural findings — they are visible, actionable,
  and routinely buried.
- State plainly that formation is not measurable here, so leadership does not read good attendance as
  success.
- Cap changes at three with named owners. Reviews that produce a dozen actions produce none.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** the review is bounded before pairs are classified and
  findings drawn, so no conclusion can exceed what the program actually tracks.
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework):** seven pair states plus load, unmatched, and
  purpose-alignment axes replace the active/inactive binary that hides the difference between a program
  working and one failing.
- **OC-03 (Markdown Table Specification):** the "did anyone ask" column turns an absent conversation
  into a visible finding rather than a gap filled by inference.
- **QA-04 (Uncertainty Acknowledgment):** "unknown" is a first-class confidence level and the
  what-the-data-cannot-tell-us section states outright that meeting counts do not measure formation.
- **QA-01 (Self-Verification):** the verification block confirms the bound is respected, that no ranking
  or individual assessment appears, and that changes are capped at three with owners.
