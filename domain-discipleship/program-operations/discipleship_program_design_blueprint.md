# Program Design Blueprint

**Objective:** Design a discipleship pairing program end to end — the roles that must exist, how people
enter, how they are screened and matched, what rhythm they keep, what support surrounds them, and how
the whole thing is reviewed — sequenced so that safeguarding precedes matching, sized to the volunteer
capacity that actually exists, and with the program's own likely failure modes named before it launches.

> **Boundary guardrail.** A program that pairs people for spiritual mentoring creates relationships of
> real influence and asymmetry. It must not launch before its safeguarding policy exists, its mentors
> are screened and trained, and its referral routes are real — see
> `discipleship_safeguarding_and_conduct_policy.md`. Nothing in this blueprint states a legal
> requirement; those are `[VERIFY]` with qualified counsel via `domain-legal/`.

**When to use:** Starting a pairing program from nothing, or restructuring one that has grown
informally and now needs actual design.

**When NOT to use:**
- You need the safeguarding policy — write that first; this blueprint depends on it.
- You need matching criteria specifically — use
  `../pairing-and-relationship/discipleship_pairing_criteria_design.md`.
- You need the curriculum participants will follow — use
  `../curriculum-architecture/discipleship_curriculum_architecture.md`.
- You are launching small groups rather than pairs — use
  `domain-biblical-studies/church-staff-ministry-ops/biblical_churchstaff_small_group_launch_system.md`.
- You want to design the software platform — that routes to `domain-idea-to-product/`.

**Audience:** Program leads, church staff, and platform operators. Output serves three audiences at
once — leadership, mentors, and participants — and must be usable by each.

---

## Inputs / Context

**Required:**

1. **Who this serves and at what scale.** Expected participants on each side, in the first cohort and at
   twelve months. Be realistic: most programs launch smaller than planned.
2. **Who runs it.** Named roles and their actual available hours — including whether the coordinator
   role is somebody's fifth responsibility, which most often it is.
3. **What already exists.** Safeguarding policy, curriculum, existing mentors, an existing informal
   version of this.
4. **The constraints.** Budget, staff time, geography, platform or tooling, and whether participants can
   meet in person at all.

**Optional:**

5. **Declared tradition (optional).** May determine oversight, who authorizes mentors, whether
   membership is required, and pairing conventions.
6. **Why now.** The need that prompted this, which should be visible in the design.
7. **Prior attempts.** What was tried before and how it ended.

**If any required input is missing:** Ask clarifying questions before proceeding. Ask specifically for
the coordinator's real available hours — programs are most often designed around a coordinator role that
nobody has time to do, and they fail there rather than at matching or content.

## Constraints

### Must

- Sequence so that **safeguarding, screening, and training precede any matching.** State this as a hard
  gate.
- Name every **role that must exist**, with its actual hours — including the coordinator, the
  safeguarding lead, and whoever handles a mismatch.
- Size the first cohort to **screening and support capacity**, not to demand.
- Design the **whole participant journey on both sides**: how someone hears, applies, is screened,
  matched, meets, is supported, and finishes.
- Define what happens when things go wrong: **mismatch, disclosure, mentor withdrawal, participant
  disengagement** — before launch, not after the first one.
- Include a **review cadence** and what would cause the program to pause or stop.
- Name the program's **three most likely failure modes** with an early signal for each.
- State what the program **does not do**, and where those needs route.
- Serve three audiences — **leadership, mentors, participants** — with the parts each needs.

### Must Not

- Launch matching before safeguarding, screening, and training exist. State this as a refusal, not a
  recommendation.
- Size the cohort to demand. Unmet demand is uncomfortable; unscreened mentors are dangerous.
- Assume volunteer coordinator capacity that has not been confirmed in hours.
- State a legal requirement about screening, records, minors, or data — `[VERIFY]` and route.
- Invent program-design benchmarks, mentoring-outcome research, or participation statistics.
- Quote Scripture text from memory; addresses only where a passage grounds a design commitment.
- Design a program whose success depends on one person continuing indefinitely.
- Promise participants an outcome, a timeline, or a match by a given date.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)

- **Must:** where a tradition governs who may mentor, whether oversight is by elders or staff, whether
  membership is required, and how pairing conventions apply, build it in as that stream's requirement
  and label it as such.
- **Must Not:** present one tradition's oversight or authorization model as necessary program design, or
  assume a congregational structure exists where the program may be running outside one.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — State the gate

Before anything else: matching does not begin until the safeguarding policy exists, mentors are
screened, and training is complete. Write it as a gate with a named owner, so it cannot erode under
launch pressure.

### Step 2 — Name the roles and their real hours

List every role: coordinator, safeguarding lead, mentor trainer, whoever handles mismatches, whoever
reviews. Give each its actual weekly or monthly hours. Where a role has no owner, that is a finding and
the program is not ready.

### Step 3 — Size the first cohort honestly

Work backwards from screening and support capacity. If ten mentors can be screened and supported, that
is the cohort — regardless of how many people are waiting. State the unmet demand plainly and say what
happens to those people.

### Step 4 — Map both journeys

Walk the participant journey for each side from first hearing to finishing, naming what happens at each
step, who does it, and how long it takes. The two journeys differ substantially and designing only the
mentee's is a common omission.

### Step 5 — Design the failure paths before launch

For mismatch, disclosure, mentor withdrawal, and participant disengagement: what happens, who acts, and
how quickly. These are the situations that determine whether a program is trusted, and improvising them
under pressure produces the harm.

### Step 6 — Set the review cadence and the stop conditions

When the program is reviewed, by whom, against what. Then the conditions under which it pauses or stops
— a safeguarding failure, loss of the coordinator, mentors unsupported. A program with no stop condition
continues past the point it should.

### Step 7 — Name the failure modes and what it does not do

Three most likely failure modes with early signals. Then what the program does not do — counseling,
crisis support, financial help, dispute resolution — and where each routes.

## Verification

- [ ] The gate is stated first, with a named owner, and matching cannot precede it.
- [ ] Every role has an owner and confirmed hours; unowned roles are flagged as blocking.
- [ ] Cohort size is derived from screening and support capacity, not demand, with unmet demand named.
- [ ] Both participant journeys are mapped, not only the mentee's.
- [ ] Mismatch, disclosure, withdrawal, and disengagement all have designed responses before launch.
- [ ] Stop conditions exist; no legal requirement is stated.

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Size the first cohort to the waiting list. The pressure to match everyone who asked is exactly the
  pressure that puts unscreened mentors in place.
- Assume the coordinator role will fit into someone's existing week. Confirm the hours, because this is
  where programs actually fail.
- Design only the mentee's journey. Mentors who are recruited, trained, and then left alone drop out
  within two cohorts.
- Leave the mismatch path to be worked out when it happens. The first mismatch sets whether participants
  trust the program.
- Launch with a safeguarding policy still full of open `[VERIFY]` items. It looks like diligence and
  is not.
- Design a program that depends on one person indefinitely. Name the succession, or name the dependency
  as a risk.

✅ **DO:**
- Write the gate as a refusal with an owner, so it survives the enthusiasm of launch.
- Put the hours in a table. An unowned or under-resourced coordinator role becomes visible immediately
  and is the single most predictive design fact.
- State unmet demand explicitly and say what those people are offered instead, so they are not left in
  silence.
- Design the failure paths before launch, since these are what determine trust and they are the worst
  things to improvise.
- Set stop conditions. A program that cannot stop will continue past a safeguarding failure.
- Label sections by audience, so leadership, mentors, and participants can each find what applies to
  them without reading the whole document.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** the safeguarding gate is Step 1 and cohort sizing
  follows capacity rather than demand, so the sequence itself prevents the launch-pressure failure.
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework):** roles, journeys, failure paths, review, and stop
  conditions are separate design axes, preventing a program strong on content from launching with no
  mismatch path.
- **NE-14 (Multi-Audience Documentation Targeting):** output is labelled for leadership, mentors, and
  participants, since a single blueprint must be usable by three audiences with different needs.
- **CM-02 (Constraint Specification):** the no-matching-before-safeguarding gate, capacity-not-demand
  sizing, and confirmed-hours rule are hard constraints against the ways well-intentioned programs
  launch unsafely.
- **QA-01 (Self-Verification):** the verification block checks role ownership, gate placement, both
  journeys, and designed failure paths before launch is approved.
