# Youth and Teen Discipleship Track

**Objective:** Design a discipleship track for teenagers in which safeguarding governs the structure
before content is chosen — visibility, consent, contact rules, and reporting settled first — and in
which the content itself takes adolescent development seriously, treats doubt as ordinary, keeps
guardians informed without turning the mentor into an informant, and applies no pressure of any kind to
a young person's spiritual decisions.

> **STRONG-GUARD — boundary guardrail.** Everything in this track is subordinate to the program's
> safeguarding and conduct policy and to the requirements of the jurisdiction, which this prompt does
> not and will not state `[VERIFY: confirm every legal requirement with qualified local counsel and the
> safeguarding lead]`. No one-to-one unobserved meetings, no private one-to-one digital contact, no
> transport alone, no contact outside agreed channels. Mental-health concerns, self-harm, suicidality,
> eating disorders, abuse, exploitation, and grooming route immediately to licensed professionals,
> guardians where safe, and appropriate authorities. A youth mentor is not a counselor, a parent, or a
> confidant who keeps secrets — and a young person is told that before they say anything.

**When to use:** You are designing or rebuilding a discipleship track for minors and the safeguarding
policy is already in place — or you are about to discover that it is not.

**When NOT to use:**
- There is no safeguarding and conduct policy. Build
  `../program-operations/discipleship_safeguarding_and_conduct_policy.md` first; this prompt will
  otherwise produce a track that cannot lawfully or safely run.
- You need a youth Bible study designed — use
  `domain-biblical-studies/ministry-contexts/biblical_ministry_youth_bible_study_designer.md`.
- The participants are 18+ and in transition — use `discipleship_track_college_and_young_adult.md`.
- A specific young person is showing signs of distress — that is a care and referral matter now, and
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/ages-13-18/` holds the caregiver-facing signal prompts.

**Audience:** Youth pastors, program leads, and safeguarding-trained volunteers designing the track.
Not a track for an untrained volunteer to design or run alone.

---

## Inputs / Context

**Required:**

1. **Ages and the spread.** Thirteen and seventeen are different people; a single track that treats them
   as one group serves neither. State the actual range and whether it will be split.
2. **The safeguarding policy in force.** What it requires: screening, ratios, visibility, contact rules,
   record-keeping, reporting lines. If it does not exist, say so and stop.
3. **Guardian relationship and consent.** What guardians have consented to, what they are told, what
   they may see, and what a young person can hold privately. This must be settled before content.
4. **Setting and supervision.** Church, school, camp, home, online, or mixed — with who else is present,
   in each.
5. **The referral landscape.** Local and program-level professional help, described rather than named
   from memory.

**Optional:**

6. **Declared tradition (optional).** May foreground confirmation, catechesis, a rite of passage, a
   baptism or profession pathway, or a specific youth-ministry model. Contested specifics stay
   attributed, and no rite becomes a pressure point.
7. **Guardian faith position.** Whether guardians share the community's faith, differ, or object —
   which changes what may be offered and how.
8. **Existing material.** Any youth curriculum already in use.

**If any required input is missing:** Ask before proceeding. If item 2 is missing, the only correct
output is a statement that the track cannot be designed until the policy exists, and a pointer to the
policy prompt. This is a legitimate deliverable.

## Constraints

### Must

- Settle **structure before content**: ratios, visibility, contact channels, consent, and reporting are
  fixed and written before a single session is designed.
- State the **confidentiality limits to the young person in their own words**, at the start, before
  anything is disclosed — including that some things must be passed on, and to whom.
- Design **guardian communication** that keeps guardians informed of the track without making the mentor
  a reporter of a young person's private conversation — and state exactly where that line sits.
- Take **adolescent development seriously**: identity formation, peer belonging, autonomy-seeking,
  abstract reasoning arriving unevenly, and the fact that a teenager's questions are usually real
  questions.
- Treat **doubt as development**, not rebellion, and design at least one session where disagreeing with
  the material is the exercise.
- Make participation **genuinely voluntary**, including for the young person whose guardian enrolled
  them, with a way to disengage that costs them no belonging.
- Design **response and worship moments** — where a tradition includes them — with explicit anti-pressure
  guards: no isolation, no repeated appeals, no public counting, no manufactured urgency, no decision
  under emotional peak.
- Include the young person whose **home is not safe or not supportive**, without exposing them.
- Provide **age-appropriate routing** for mental health, self-harm, abuse, exploitation, and online
  harm, with the mentor's words and the safeguarding lead's involvement.

### Must Not

- Quote Scripture text from memory or assert verse wording.
- State any legal requirement — age of consent, reporting duty, screening requirement, record retention,
  or data rules. Mark `[VERIFY]` and route to `domain-legal/` and the safeguarding lead.
- Design any unobserved one-to-one contact, private digital messaging, lone transport, or overnight
  arrangement outside the policy.
- Ask a young person to keep something from their guardians, or promise to.
- Use guilt, fear, shame, hell-urgency, peer pressure, public commitment, isolation from peers, sleep
  deprivation, or emotional escalation to produce a spiritual response.
- Count, publish, or set targets for decisions, baptisms, or responses.
- Invent adolescent development research, faith-retention statistics, or youth-ministry outcome data.
- Give clinical guidance on self-harm, eating disorders, gender-related distress, or mental illness. All
  route out.
- Treat a young person's sexuality, identity, or questions as a problem the track exists to correct.
- Name a specific counselor, service, agency, or app.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)

- **Must:** note where traditions differ on confirmation, catechesis, the age and meaning of baptism or
  profession, rites of passage, and what a young person is considered ready to decide. Attribute
  positions to identifiable streams, and let a declared tradition run its own pathway while stating that
  its timing and meaning are that tradition's.
- **Must Not:** present one tradition's initiation moment as the universal marker of a young person's
  faith, or design a rite in a way that makes a young person's participation socially unavoidable.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Fix the structure and write it down

Before content: ratios, who else is in the room, meeting locations, agreed contact channels and who else
is on them, consent already obtained, what guardians receive, and the reporting line. Produce this as a
one-page structural sheet that anyone working on the track can check against. If any item is unresolved,
the track does not run.

### Step 2 — Write the confidentiality statement for a fifteen-year-old

Draft the verbatim sentence a mentor says at the start — plain, short, and honest: what stays here, what
cannot, who it goes to, and why. Then the sentence for when a young person asks for a promise first, and
the sentence for when something already said has to be passed on.

### Step 3 — Set the guardian line

Define precisely what guardians are told: the track's content, schedule, and aims — always; and what a
young person said in a conversation — not, except where safety requires it. Write both sides in words a
guardian will accept and a teenager will recognize as honest.

### Step 4 — Design for the developmental range

Split the age range if it needs splitting. For each band, state what is developmentally real:
abstraction, identity work, peer weight, autonomy, and capacity for commitment. Then design content that
meets it rather than content that treats teenagers as short adults or as older children.

### Step 5 — Build the track

Lay out stages and sessions against the duration. For each session: the question, Scripture by address,
the practice, the discussion questions — and two mandatory columns: *what this session must not ask a
minor to disclose*, and *how a young person disagrees with this session safely*.

### Step 6 — Design the response moments with guards attached

Where the tradition includes an invitation, a rite, or a response, write the guard in the same place: no
isolation, no repeated appeals, no counting, no decision at an emotional peak, and a clearly available
"not now" that costs nothing. If the tradition includes none, say so.

### Step 7 — Build the routing and the hard-moment scripts

Table the routing: self-harm, suicidality, eating disorder, abuse or exploitation, online harm,
substance use, and a home that is not safe. Destination kind `[VERIFY]`, safeguarding lead's role, the
mentor's words, and what the young person is told about what happens next — because being routed without
explanation is frightening.

### Step 8 — Verify the whole track against the policy

Check the finished track line by line against the safeguarding policy and the structural sheet. Name who
signs it off, and who reviews it again after the first term.

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Design content before structure. A brilliant track that violates the contact policy cannot run, and
  discovering that late usually means it runs anyway with the policy quietly bent.
- Tell a young person their conversation is private. It is the same sentence that damages adults, and
  with a minor it can obstruct a safeguarding response.
- Treat doubt as a phase to be managed. Teenagers detect that instantly, and the ones who leave usually
  leave because their questions were handled rather than answered.
- Run a response moment at an emotional peak — late night, after intense music, after a story designed
  to move. Decisions made there are extracted, not made, and the young person often knows it later.
- Count decisions. The moment a number exists, the track begins optimizing for it and the pressure
  arrives without anyone choosing it.
- Design as though every home is safe and supportive. Some of the young people in the room are managing
  something at home, and the track must not expose them to find that out.

✅ **DO:**
- Produce the structural sheet as a standalone page anyone can check against.
- Write the confidentiality words for the actual age of the actual young people, and rehearse them.
- Build one session where disagreeing with the material is the exercise, and mean it.
- Put the "how to disagree safely" column in the session table, so it is visible at delivery.
- Attach the guard to the response moment in the same row, not in a policy document nobody re-reads.
- Tell a young person what will happen next when something is routed out. Being handled without
  explanation is frightening and teaches them not to speak next time.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-01 (Clear Objective Statement):** the objective puts safeguarding structure ahead of content in
  the deliverable itself, so a track designed content-first has failed its brief rather than merely
  taken a different route.
- **CM-02 (Constraint Specification):** the load-bearing element here — the Must Not block encodes the
  absolutes for minors: no unobserved contact, no promised secrecy, no pressure mechanics, no stated
  law.
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework):** structure, guardian relationship, developmental
  band, and content are worked as separate axes, so a decision on one cannot silently be made by a
  decision on another.
- **ED-01 (Iterative Scaffolding):** the track stages build across developmental bands rather than
  repeating one register, with what a young person can do by the end of each stage stated.
- **QA-01 (Self-Verification):** the track is checked line by line against the safeguarding policy and
  signed off before it runs, with a review date fixed at design time.
