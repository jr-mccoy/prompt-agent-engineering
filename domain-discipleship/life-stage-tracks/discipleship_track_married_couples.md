# Married Couples Track

**Objective:** Design a discipleship track for married couples that forms each spouse as a disciple in
their own right as well as forming the marriage — screening for abuse and coercive control before any
joint work begins, holding the contested teaching on marital roles as contested, protecting each
spouse's private formation from becoming the other's business, and staying clearly outside marriage
counseling.

> **Boundary guardrail.** This is not marriage counseling, mediation, or therapy, and a lay mentor
> never becomes any of those. Domestic abuse, coercive control, sexual coercion, financial control,
> intimidation, and fear of a spouse route immediately to specialist services and, where there is
> danger, to authorities `[VERIFY: identify the correct service from an official source]` — never to
> joint sessions, never to a mediation attempt, and never to a couples track. Affairs, addiction,
> untreated mental illness, and marital crisis route to licensed professionals. Separation and divorce
> decisions belong to the couple and, where legal questions arise, to `domain-legal/`.

**When to use:** You are building a couples track inside a discipleship program, or discipling a married
couple and want a structure that does not quietly turn into counseling.

**When NOT to use:**
- Anyone is afraid of their spouse, or abuse is suspected. Route out. Do not run a couples track, do not
  meet jointly, and do not raise it in a joint session.
- The marriage is in crisis — affair, separation, addiction. That is professional territory.
- You want a couples' Bible study — use
  `domain-biblical-studies/ministry-contexts/biblical_ministry_marriage_enrichment_study.md`.
- You want the positions on marital roles compared — use
  `domain-biblical-studies/theology-research/biblical_interpretive_views_comparison.md`.
- One spouse does not share the faith and has not consented to participate. Disciple the willing spouse
  individually; do not construct a track around a person who did not agree to it.

**Audience:** Program leads and experienced mentor couples designing the track. Delivery should be by
people trained in the abuse screen, not by an enthusiastic volunteer couple alone.

---

## Inputs / Context

**Required:**

1. **Who the couples are.** Newly married, long-married, blended families, remarried, older couples,
   couples in caregiving years, couples with one spouse who does not share the faith or does not want
   to participate.
2. **The screening in place.** What the program does before a couple joins, who conducts it, and what
   happens when a concern appears. If nothing exists, that is the first deliverable.
3. **Session structure available.** Joint only, individual only, or both — and whether the program can
   support individual conversations with each spouse separately, which the safety design generally
   requires.
4. **The referral landscape.** Specialist domestic-abuse services, licensed counselors, and safeguarding
   leads — described rather than named from memory.

**Optional:**

5. **Declared tradition (optional).** May foreground complementarian or egalitarian teaching, sacramental
   marriage, covenant framing, or particular practices around headship, submission, mutual submission,
   contraception, or divorce and remarriage. Declaring it lets the track state that teaching *as that
   tradition's*, with alternatives visible.
6. **Cultural expectations in the room.** Extended-family authority, arranged-marriage backgrounds, and
   differing norms about privacy between spouses all change the design.
7. **Existing material.** Any marriage curriculum already in use.

**If any required input is missing:** Ask before proceeding. If item 2 cannot be answered — if there is
no screen and no plan for what happens when a concern appears — the correct output is that the track
cannot safely run yet, and what must be built first.

---

## Constraints

### Must

- Run an **abuse and coercive-control screen before any couple joins**, conducted with each spouse
  separately, by someone trained, with a defined response that never involves a joint conversation.
- Design **individual formation alongside joint formation**. Each spouse is a disciple in their own
  right, and a track that only forms "the marriage" leaves two people undiscipled.
- Protect each spouse's **private formation**: what someone works on individually is not automatically
  the other's business, and the track must say so and structure for it.
- Hold the **roles question as contested**, stating positions in the form their own adherents would
  recognize, attributing each, and — where a tradition is declared — presenting its teaching as that
  tradition's rather than as the settled reading.
- Design for the **couple at different places**: different maturity, different interest, different pace,
  and one spouse participating reluctantly.
- Keep the track **out of counseling**: no diagnosis, no conflict mediation, no sexual-relationship
  instruction, no advice on whether to stay or leave.
- Give the **mentor couple explicit limits** and a referral route, including the rule that they never
  meet with one spouse about the other.
- Include the **spouse who does not share the faith** honestly — including the decision not to run a
  couples track at all where consent is absent.
- Reference Scripture **by address only**, translation named, noting where the marriage passages are
  disputed and why.

### Must Not

- Quote Scripture text from memory or assert verse wording.
- Present one tradition's teaching on headship, submission, or mutual submission as the biblical
  position while describing the other as compromise or error.
- Run any joint session, exercise, or disclosure where abuse or fear has been indicated or suspected.
- Ask a couple to disclose sexual, financial, or conflict details to a mentor or a group.
- Instruct on sexual practice, fertility, or contraception, or make any of these a track requirement.
- Advise a participant to stay in, leave, or return to a marriage.
- Invent marriage research, divorce statistics, outcome studies, or claims about what makes marriages
  succeed.
- Treat a difficult marriage as a discipleship deficiency, or a good marriage as evidence of maturity.
- State law on marriage, divorce, custody, or protective orders. Mark `[VERIFY]` and route to
  `domain-legal/`.
- Name a specific counselor, service, program, or organization.
- Allow a mentor to hold information from one spouse about the other, or to become a channel between
  them.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)

- **Must:** name the genuine divergence on marital roles and authority, on marriage as sacrament or
  covenant, on divorce and remarriage, on contraception, on the standing of a mixed-faith marriage, and
  on who decides what within a household. Attribute each to identifiable streams. A declared tradition
  may run its own teaching, stated plainly as its own.
- **Must Not:** let a roles teaching be delivered in a way that pressures a spouse to accept treatment
  they are unwilling to accept, or that could be used to justify control. Where a tradition's teaching
  touches authority in a marriage, the abuse guard is stated in the same place.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Build the screen before the track

Design the pre-join screen: separate conversations with each spouse, who conducts them, what is asked
(and what is not), what signals a concern, and the response — which is routing to specialist help and
*not* admitting the couple to a joint track. Write the words for the person conducting it. This is the
first deliverable and the track does not proceed without it.

### Step 2 — Separate the three formation surfaces

Define what happens on each: **individually** (each spouse's own formation, private by default),
**jointly** (what they build together), and **communally** (what happens with other couples, if
anything). State what moves between surfaces and what does not — and specifically, that individual
material does not automatically become joint material.

### Step 3 — Handle the roles question deliberately

Build the contested table before designing sessions: roles and authority, decision-making, sacrament or
covenant, divorce and remarriage, contraception, mixed-faith marriage. Positions, streams, strongest
form of each, how this track handles it, and where the community stands. Attach the abuse guard to the
authority row in the same place it appears.

### Step 4 — Design for asymmetry

Write the design for the couple who are not in the same place: one enthusiastic and one reluctant, one
formed and one beginning, one who wants to talk and one who does not. State what the track asks of the
reluctant spouse — which should be very little — and what it never asks.

### Step 5 — Build the sessions

Lay out sessions. For each: the question, Scripture by address, surface (individual / joint /
communal), the practice — and two mandatory columns: *what this session must not ask a couple to
disclose*, and *how this session could be used by a controlling spouse*, with the wording that prevents
it.

### Step 6 — Fix the mentor couple's limits

Write the limits explicitly: no meeting one spouse about the other, no carrying messages, no assessment
of the marriage, no advice on staying or leaving, no involvement in a conflict as a party. Then the
referral route, with words, and the rule for what happens if one spouse discloses something in an
individual conversation.

### Step 7 — Design the exit and the harder outcomes

State what a couple leaves with, and what the track does when a marriage separates or ends during it —
which is care for both people as individuals, no side-taking, no membership consequence applied by the
track, and referral. Route polity questions to
`domain-biblical-studies/theology-research/biblical_theology_church_government_polity.md`.

### Step 8 — Stress-test

Check against the standing failure modes: the screen was skipped or run jointly; a roles teaching became
leverage; the track became counseling; individual formation disappeared into the marriage; the reluctant
spouse was pressured. Early signal and fix.

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Screen a couple together. Someone experiencing coercive control cannot answer honestly with their
  spouse in the room, and a joint screen produces a clean result and a trapped person.
- Teach authority in marriage without attaching the guard in the same breath. Where the teaching is held,
  it is held alongside a prohibition on control — and separating them is how the teaching gets misused.
- Let the track become counseling because the mentor couple is good at listening. Skill is exactly what
  makes over-reach feel responsible.
- Collapse two disciples into one marriage. The most common quiet failure of couples tracks is that
  neither spouse is formed individually and both assume the other is doing it.
- Run a couples track around a spouse who did not agree. Disciple the willing spouse individually and
  say plainly why.
- Present a strong marriage as evidence of spiritual maturity. It is not, and the couple struggling in
  the room hears the implication immediately.

✅ **DO:**
- Build the screen first and treat it as the gate it is.
- Keep individual formation private by default, and say so out loud so nobody assumes disclosure.
- Put the "how a controlling spouse could use this" column in the session table, where it is visible at
  delivery.
- Design explicitly for the reluctant spouse, and keep what is asked of them small.
- Fix the mentor couple's limits in writing, including the rule for a solo disclosure.
- Decide in advance what the track does if a marriage ends during it — and make it care for two people,
  not a verdict.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-01 (Clear Objective Statement):** the objective names both deliverables — two disciples and a
  marriage — and rules out counseling, which is the drift this track is most prone to.
- **CM-02 (Constraint Specification):** the Must Not block encodes the absolutes: no joint work where
  abuse is indicated, no roles teaching as leverage, no stay-or-leave advice, no mentor as message
  channel.
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework):** three formation surfaces crossed with the asymmetry
  design reaches the individual-spouse dimension that marriage material almost always collapses.
- **DS-01 (Framework Application):** the contested-ground table applies the domain's multi-view
  machinery to the roles question, with the abuse guard bound into the same row.
- **QA-05 (Citation Requirements):** Scripture by address with a named translation, no invented marriage
  or divorce statistics, and every legal point and service marked `[VERIFY]`.
