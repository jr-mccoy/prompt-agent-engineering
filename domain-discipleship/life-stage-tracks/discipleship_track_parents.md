# Parents Track

**Objective:** Design a discipleship track for parents that forms *the parent* — in the exhaustion,
fragmentation, fear, and love that parenting actually produces — while refusing at every point to treat
a child's faith, behaviour, or outcomes as the measure of the parent's discipleship, and while handing
parenting technique and child-behaviour questions to the domains built for them.

> **Boundary guardrail.** This is not a parenting course, a child-behaviour resource, or family therapy.
> Child mental health, developmental concerns, family violence, parental burnout that has become a
> health matter, and any safeguarding concern about a child route to licensed professionals and, where
> there is danger, to appropriate authorities `[VERIFY: identify the correct service from an official
> source]`. Where a concern about a child's safety arises, the safeguarding policy governs immediately
> and is not a track matter. Parenting practice itself belongs to `domain-parenting/`.

**When to use:** You are building a parents track inside a discipleship program, or discipling parents
whose formation keeps getting described in terms of their children.

**When NOT to use:**
- The need is parenting practice — behaviour, routines, ages and stages, neurodivergence — use
  `domain-parenting/`.
- The need is family devotions or Scripture with children — use
  `domain-biblical-studies/ministry-contexts/biblical_ministry_family_devotions_designer.md`.
- The parent's capacity is the binding constraint on any pathway — start with
  `../learner-pathways/discipleship_life_constraints_adaptation.md`.
- A child's safety is in question. That is immediate, and it is not a track.

**Audience:** Program leads, curriculum designers, and mentors working with parents at any stage from
newborns to adult children.

---

## Inputs / Context

**Required:**

1. **Which parents.** Stage of children, and the household shape: two parents, single parent, separated
   or co-parenting, step and blended, foster or adoptive, kinship carers, grandparents raising
   grandchildren, parents of disabled or seriously ill children, parents of adult children.
2. **Actual available capacity.** Honest minutes per day and per week, and whether that time is ever
   uninterrupted. This governs the entire design.
3. **What this community currently communicates to parents.** Whether children's faith is treated as the
   parent's report card, whether family devotions are an expectation, and what a parent hears when
   their teenager stops attending.
4. **The referral landscape.** Local and program-level help for child, parent, and family concerns —
   described rather than named from memory.

**Optional:**

5. **Declared tradition (optional).** May foreground infant baptism and its promises, dedication,
   catechesis at home, family worship practice, or particular teaching on parental authority. Contested
   specifics stay attributed.
6. **A spouse or co-parent who does not share the faith.** Which changes what can be practised at home
   and what may not be asked.
7. **Existing material.** Any parenting or family-ministry curriculum already in use.

**If any required input is missing:** Ask before proceeding. Do not guess item 2 — a track built on
thirty uninterrupted minutes a day, delivered to parents of a newborn and a three-year-old, is a track
that teaches parents they are failing.

## Constraints

### Must

- State at the top, and repeat in the track, that **a child's faith, behaviour, or choices are not the
  measure of the parent's discipleship**, and design nothing that implies otherwise.
- Form **the parent's own faith** as the primary content: their prayer, their Scripture, their
  community, their character — in fragments and interruptions rather than in an imagined quiet hour.
- Build every practice to a **floor that survives the worst week**, and make the floor the real practice
  rather than the fallback.
- Include **guilt and comparison** as explicit formation territory, since they are the dominant
  religious experience of parenting in most communities.
- Include the parent of a child who has **left the faith, is in crisis, or is estranged**, with content
  that is company rather than correction, and that never implies a technique would have prevented it.
- Cover the **household shapes actually present** — single, separated, blended, foster, adoptive,
  kinship, disability, adult children — not a default two-parent household with young children.
- Hand parenting practice, child behaviour, and family Scripture teaching **out** to `domain-parenting/`
  and `domain-biblical-studies/`, and say so plainly.
- Reference Scripture **by address only**, translation named, and flag that the parenting proverbs and
  household passages are widely proof-texted and disputed in application.
- Route child safeguarding, child mental health, family violence, and parental health concerns **out**
  `[VERIFY]`.

### Must Not

- Quote Scripture text from memory or assert verse wording.
- Treat a child's faith or behaviour as evidence about the parent, in content, framing, testimony, or
  implication.
- Present a passage about children and instruction as a guarantee of outcome, or use it to attribute a
  child's departure to a parental failure.
- Give parenting advice, discipline guidance, behaviour techniques, or developmental assessment.
- Set family devotions, household worship, or a parenting practice as a requirement, or measure
  participation by it.
- Invent research or statistics about faith transmission, family worship, church retention of children,
  or parenting outcomes.
- Give clinical guidance on child or parental mental health, neurodivergence, or postnatal illness. All
  route out.
- Design practices that require a co-parent's participation where that co-parent has not agreed.
- Use another parent's child as an illustration, positive or negative.
- Name a specific service, counselor, school, or organization.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)

- **Must:** note where traditions differ on infant baptism and the promises attached, on dedication, on
  catechesis at home, on household worship practice, on parental authority and its limits, and on when a
  child's faith is understood to be their own. Attribute positions to identifiable streams; let a
  declared tradition foreground its own practice and promises.
- **Must Not:** present one tradition's household practice as the biblical family model, or let a
  covenantal or dedicatory framing be used to tell a parent what their child's later choices mean about
  them.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Say the thing that governs the track

Write, at the top and in the participant's hands: your child's faith is not your report card. State what
follows from it — that the track will not measure them by their children, will not offer techniques for
producing belief, and will not treat a child's departure as a diagnosis of the parent.

### Step 2 — Establish the real capacity

Write the honest time budget, including interruption. Then design the floor: the smallest practice that
survives the worst week of this parenting stage. State clearly that the floor is the practice, not a
concession — because parents who believe the floor is failure will abandon it and then abandon the whole
thing.

### Step 3 — Work the four territories

| Territory | The formation question under it |
|---|---|
| My own faith in fragments | What does prayer, Scripture, and attention look like in ninety-second pieces? |
| What parenting exposes | What has this shown me about my anger, control, fear, and need to be needed? |
| Guilt and comparison | Whose standard am I failing, and where did I get it? |
| Letting go | What is mine to carry, and what belongs to my child? |

For each: Scripture by address with the disputed applications flagged, the distortion common here, and
the practice at the floor.

### Step 4 — Design for the household shapes present

Take each shape actually in the group and state what changes: what the single parent cannot do, what the
separated parent must negotiate, what the foster or kinship carer holds that others do not, what the
parent of a disabled child has been told too often, what the parent of adult children is now living.
Content that mentions these in passing has not designed for them.

### Step 5 — Build the strand for the hardest cases

Design content for the parent whose child has left the faith, is in crisis, is estranged, or has died.
This strand is company: lament (routing to
`../topical-modules/discipleship_module_suffering_and_lament.md`), the refusal of causal accounts, and
the relationships that remain. It must contain no implied technique and no retrospective diagnosis.

### Step 6 — Build the sessions

Lay out sessions. For each: the question, Scripture by address, territory, the floor practice — and two
mandatory columns: *the parent this session could shame*, and the wording that prevents it; plus *what
this session hands off, and to where*.

### Step 7 — Fix the handoffs

Write the handoff table: parenting technique, child behaviour, developmental concerns, family devotions,
teen-specific signals, and co-parenting conflict. Each goes to `domain-parenting/`,
`domain-biblical-studies/`, or a professional `[VERIFY]`. A parents track that answers these questions
is doing three jobs badly.

### Step 8 — Stress-test

Check against the standing failure modes: children's outcomes became the measure; the track became a
parenting course; the floor was framed as failure; only one household shape was designed for; the
grieving or estranged parent had nothing. Early signal and fix.

## Verification

- [ ] The governing statement — a child's faith is not the parent's report card — appears in the track
      itself and nothing contradicts it.
- [ ] Every practice has a floor version that survives the worst week, framed as the practice.
- [ ] Every household shape present in the group has its own row with what changes and what is never
      asked.
- [ ] The hardest strand exists and contains no implied technique and no retrospective diagnosis.
- [ ] Parenting technique, child behaviour, and family Scripture teaching are handed off, not answered.
- [ ] No faith-transmission or retention statistic appears anywhere.

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Let a child's church attendance become the visible metric of a parent's discipleship. It is the
  standard failure of parents' ministry, it is rarely stated out loud, and every parent in the room can
  feel it.
- Offer the household passages as a guarantee. Parents whose children have left have usually already
  been handed that verse by someone, and it did real damage.
- Turn the track into a parenting course. It is a different skill, other domains hold it properly, and
  attempting it produces thin advice with Scripture attached.
- Design the floor as an emergency fallback. Parents who believe the two-minute version is failure will
  stop doing it and then stop entirely.
- Build for two parents with young children and mention the others. Single, kinship, foster, and
  disability households are structurally different, not variations.
- Use a parent's child as an illustration. The child did not consent and the parent cannot object
  without appearing defensive.

✅ **DO:**
- Put the governing statement in the participants' hands in writing, and repeat it whenever outcomes
  come up.
- Fix the honest capacity first, including interruption, and design everything downstream from it.
- Include guilt and comparison as content. It is what parents actually carry, and naming it is
  frequently the most useful thing the track does.
- Design the hardest strand as company, and route the lament properly.
- Fill the handoff table completely, so the track can decline three adjacent jobs without leaving the
  parent stranded.
- Route any child-safety concern immediately and outside the track entirely.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-01 (Clear Objective Statement):** the objective makes the refusal to measure parents by their
  children part of the deliverable, which is what distinguishes this track from most parents' ministry.
- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** the governing statement and the honest capacity are
  fixed before any content, so the track is built for the time parents actually have.
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework):** four territories crossed with the household shapes
  and the hardest strand reach parents that a single default-household design silently excludes.
- **CM-02 (Constraint Specification):** the Must Not block encodes the load-bearing prohibitions — no
  outcome-as-measure, no guaranteed-outcome proof-texting, no parenting advice, no required devotions.
- **RP-02 (Audience Framing):** the track is written to parents in their real conditions — fragmented,
  interrupted, and frequently guilty — rather than to an idealized parent with a quiet hour.
