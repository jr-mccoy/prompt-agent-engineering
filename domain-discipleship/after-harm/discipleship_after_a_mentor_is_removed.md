# After a Mentor Is Removed

**Objective:** Plan what happens to everyone else once a discipler has been removed or a concern about
them has been substantiated — the mentees who were in relationship with them, the mentor cohort they sat
in, and the wider community who will hear something — deciding who is told what, in what order, by whom,
and what each group is actually offered, while the process that determines facts and consequences runs
entirely elsewhere.

> **Boundary guardrail.** This prompt does not investigate, determine what happened, weigh evidence,
> decide whether an allegation is founded, discipline anyone, restore anyone, or advise on any of it. It
> begins only *after* the responsible body — safeguarding lead, church leadership, employer, or an
> authority — has acted, and it never precedes, substitutes for, or delays a referral. Where a criminal
> matter, a minor, a vulnerable adult, or an ongoing risk is involved, the relevant authority governs
> everything here, including what may be said and when
> `[VERIFY: identify the correct authority and confirm with qualified local counsel]`. If a referral
> has not yet been made, stop: that comes first, and nothing in this document is more urgent than it.

**When to use:** After a mentor has been removed, suspended, or stood down, or a concern about them has
been substantiated by whoever is responsible for determining that — and the program now has to look
after the people around it.

**When NOT to use:**
- A concern has just been raised and nothing has been determined — that is the safeguarding policy's
  reporting path, `../program-operations/discipleship_safeguarding_and_conduct_policy.md`, and it comes
  first.
- You are writing the policy that governs how concerns are handled — same file.
- You need to end one relationship well for ordinary reasons — use
  `../pairing-and-relationship/discipleship_relationship_ending_or_transition.md`, which covers
  completion, mismatch, handoff, withdrawal, and ending because of harm.
- You are supporting a specific person harmed in the relationship and helping them decide about a future
  one — use `discipleship_harmed_by_previous_discipling.md`, which is theirs and not the program's.
- You want to know what the law requires you to disclose, retain, or report — that is
  `domain-legal/` and qualified local counsel, and nothing here answers it.

**Audience:** The program lead, safeguarding lead, or church leadership responsible for the people
around a removed mentor.

---

## Inputs / Context

**Required:**

1. **What the responsible body has actually decided, and who they are.** Not what is believed, suspected,
   or rumoured — what has been determined, by whom, and what they have said may be communicated. Where a
   process is still running, that constrains everything, and the output must be built inside the
   constraint rather than around it.
2. **Who is affected, by circle.** The mentees in relationship with this person; anyone previously in
   relationship with them; the mentor cohort; volunteers and staff; the wider congregation or community.
   Named as groups, not as a list of individuals to be managed.
3. **Whether anyone was harmed, and whether they are being supported already.** Their needs govern the
   order of everything else, and if they are not already supported, that is the first action and it is
   not this document's to design.
4. **What constraints apply to communication.** Anything an authority, an insurer, an employer, or
   counsel has said about what may be disclosed. Where these are unknown, they are established before
   anything is said, not after.

**Optional:**

5. **Declared tradition (optional).** May shape who speaks to a congregation, whether leadership acts
   collectively or through an office, what is said in a gathered setting versus privately, and what
   restoration language exists in the tradition — the last of which must not be allowed to set the pace
   of care for the people affected. Where none is declared, the plan stays governance-general.
6. **What people already know or believe.** Not to be corrected point by point, but because a plan built
   on the assumption of silence will be overtaken.
7. **Whether the removed person has their own support.** They are a person too, and someone other than
   the people caring for those affected should be the one holding it.

**If any required input is missing:** Ask. If the responsible body has not yet acted, or if it is unclear
whether a referral has been made, stop and say so: this document cannot be written before that, and
writing it anyway converts a safeguarding matter into a communications exercise.

## Constraints

### Must

- **Order the care by proximity to harm.** Anyone harmed first, their mentees second, the cohort third,
  the community last — and say explicitly that this order is not negotiable for reasons of convenience or
  optics.
- Offer every affected mentee a **named alternative and a real choice**, including the choice to stop, to
  pause, to move, or to want nothing at all — with no requirement that they explain which.
- State **who says what to whom**, in what order and in what setting, with the actual words for the
  hardest of them.
- Distinguish clearly between **what is known, what cannot be said, and what nobody yet knows**, and give
  the sentence that says the second and third honestly rather than filling them.
- Provide the response to **"what did they do?"** — a sentence that neither invents detail nor implies
  the questioner is wrong to ask.
- Address the **mentor cohort as people with their own reaction**: colleagues, often friends, who may
  feel implicated, defensive, guilty, or afraid, and who will need somewhere other than the affected
  mentees to take it.
- Include a **review of what the program's own design allowed**, scheduled separately and later, so it
  neither delays care nor gets skipped once the immediate need passes.
- Mark **every legal, disclosure, retention, and reporting question `[VERIFY]`** and route it to counsel
  and the responsible body.

### Must Not

- Investigate, weigh, characterize, or restate what happened, or offer any view on whether the decision
  was right.
- Assess, defend, condemn, or attribute motive to the removed person; or advise on their discipline,
  restoration, employment, or return.
- Design messaging to protect the program's reputation, minimize fallout, control a narrative, or manage
  a story. Care and candour are the objectives; reputation is not one of them.
- Instruct anyone to keep the matter confidential in a way that could discourage a person from reporting,
  seeking help, or speaking to an authority.
- Ask, encourage, or arrange for anyone to forgive, reconcile with, meet, hear from, or accept an apology
  from the removed person — or present any of that as a next stage.
- Speak on behalf of anyone harmed, disclose their identity or account, or use their situation as content
  in any communication.
- Name any counselor, agency, hotline, service, or authority.
- State a legal duty, a disclosure obligation, a retention period, or a reporting threshold.
- Quote Scripture text from memory, or use any passage to encourage silence, unity, submission to
  leadership, forgiveness, or moving on.
- Produce a timeline that treats the community's discomfort as the thing to be resolved.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)

- **Must:** note where traditions differ on who may address a congregation, whether authority sits with
  a board, elders, a bishop, or a single office, what is said in a gathered service versus privately, how
  confession and restoration are understood, and whether a removed person's return is a possibility the
  tradition contemplates — and let a declared tradition follow its own governance for the parts that are
  genuinely its own.
- **Must Not:** allow any tradition's restoration, confession, forgiveness, or unity language to set the
  pace of care for those affected, to shorten what they are offered, to require their participation in
  anything, or to override an authority's instruction. Care for the harmed is not a variable.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Confirm this document is not the first thing happening

Check that the responsible body has acted, that any referral has been made, and that anyone harmed is
already being supported by someone qualified `[VERIFY]`. If any of these is unresolved, the output is a
single instruction to do it, and the plan waits.

### Step 2 — Establish what may be said

Write down what has been determined, what an authority or counsel has restricted, and what is genuinely
unknown. Everything downstream depends on this line being drawn honestly. A plan that treats "we can't
say" and "we don't know" as the same sentence will be caught out on both.

### Step 3 — Map the circles and order them

List the affected groups by proximity to the harm. Fix the order of communication and care by that
proximity, and state it in the document so that a later pressure to reassure the widest group first has
something to run into.

### Step 4 — Plan for the mentees first

For each mentee in relationship with the removed person: who contacts them, when, in what setting, what
is said, what is offered — a different mentor, a pause, a stop, nothing at all — and who follows up
later, because the reaction at week six is often not the reaction on the day. Include what is said if
they ask directly what happened, and what is said if they defend the removed person, which is common and
must not be treated as a problem.

### Step 5 — Plan for the cohort and the community separately

The cohort needs a place to bring their own reaction that is not the affected mentees, and a clear
statement of what they may and may not say. The community needs something true, brief, and free of
detail that is not theirs — plus an honest answer to the question they will ask, and a named person to
take further questions to. Neither group is offered speculation to fill a silence.

### Step 6 — Write the hardest sentences out

Write verbatim: the sentence to a mentee who asks what happened; the sentence when the answer cannot be
given; the sentence to someone who is angry that the person was removed; the sentence to someone who
asks whether they are safe; and the sentence to a person who asks if it was their fault for not seeing
it. These are the moments a plan fails, and they are not improvised well.

### Step 7 — Schedule the design review, then check the plan

Set a date, after the immediate care, to ask what the program's structure allowed: visibility, screening,
supervision, the reporting route, whether anyone raised something earlier and what happened to it. Then
verify the plan itself: is the order by proximity to harm? Does any part of it manage reputation? Does
anything ask anyone to forgive, meet, or accept an apology? Is every legal question marked `[VERIFY]`?

Produce exactly this structure.

## Verification

- [ ] The plan begins only after the responsible body has acted and any referral has been made.
- [ ] Care is ordered by proximity to harm, with that order stated as non-negotiable.
- [ ] Nothing in the output investigates, characterizes, or judges what happened or who did it.
- [ ] Every affected mentee is offered a real choice including stopping entirely, with no explanation
      required.
- [ ] No forgiveness, meeting, apology, restoration, or reconciliation is asked of anyone.
- [ ] No service or authority is named, and no legal duty, retention period, or disclosure obligation is
      stated — all `[VERIFY]` and routed to counsel.

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Write this before a referral has been made. A care plan produced in place of a referral is how a
  safeguarding failure becomes an institutional one.
- Let reputation enter the objectives. The moment a sentence is chosen because of how it will land
  publicly rather than because it is true and kind, the document has become a communications strategy and
  should be stopped.
- Collapse "we can't say" into "we don't know". People can tell the difference later, and being caught
  having blurred them costs more trust than the original silence would have.
- Ask anyone to keep it quiet. Even a reasonable-sounding confidentiality request can read as pressure
  not to report, and it will be remembered that way.
- Offer the affected mentees a replacement mentor as the default. Being reassigned is not care; being
  asked what they want, including nothing, is.
- Treat a mentee's defence of the removed person as a problem to correct. It is a normal response to loss
  and confusion, and arguing with it teaches them not to say what they think.
- Put restoration on the agenda. Whatever the tradition holds about it, it is not part of caring for the
  people who are still here, and raising it now tells them where they rank.

✅ **DO:**
- Establish what may be said before anything is said, with the responsible body and counsel, and write
  the honest sentence for what cannot be.
- Order everything by proximity to harm, and write the order down so a later pressure has something to
  argue against.
- Write the five hardest sentences verbatim. Every one of them will be asked, usually by someone standing
  up, usually sooner than expected.
- Give the cohort somewhere of their own to react, so their distress does not land on the mentees.
- Schedule the design review separately and far enough out that it does not compete with care — and near
  enough that it actually happens.
- Route every question about facts, consequences, disclosure, and law to the body that owns it, and keep
  this document to the people.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** the steps enforce the ordering the situation is most
  likely to break — referral and determination before planning, communication constraints before
  communication, and care before the design review.
- **CM-02 (Constraint Specification):** the Must Not block converts the predictable institutional
  temptations — narrative management, confidentiality requests, early restoration talk — into
  prohibitions rather than warnings.
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework):** affected parties, what may be said, what is offered,
  and timing are worked as separate axes, which prevents the plan from treating one message to everyone
  as a plan.
- **OC-03 (Markdown Table Specification):** the sayable/restricted/unknown table forces the three
  categories apart on the page, which is the distinction the whole plan depends on and the one most
  likely to be blurred.
- **QA-01 (Self-Verification):** the final check tests for the two failures that look like competence —
  a care order rearranged for optics, and a sentence chosen for how it will read.
