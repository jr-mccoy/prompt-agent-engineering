# Harmed by a Previous Discipling Relationship

**Objective:** Set the terms on which someone who was harmed in a previous discipling or mentoring
relationship enters a new one — the pace, the veto, the exit, what they disclose and to whom, what may
never be asked of them, and the specific things that would end it — screening for safety and for
clinical need before any of that is designed, and holding open throughout that the right answer may be
*not now* or *not this*.

> **Boundary guardrail.** This is not therapy, trauma treatment, an investigation, or an assessment of
> what happened to you. It does not decide whether what you experienced was abuse, does not evaluate
> anyone who harmed you, and does not tell you what to do about them. If you are in danger, if a crime
> may have been committed, if you are experiencing flashbacks, panic, dissociation, self-harm or
> suicidal thoughts, or if a minor or vulnerable adult may still be at risk from the person or setting
> involved, that goes to appropriate professionals and authorities now
> `[VERIFY: identify the correct service from an official source]` — not into a discipling
> relationship. Trauma from religious harm is treated by licensed professionals, and no mentor,
> however kind, is a substitute for one.

**When to use:** When someone is considering entering a new discipling or mentoring relationship after
a previous one — or a previous church or group — caused them real harm, and they want the next one on
terms they set rather than terms they inherit.

**When NOT to use:**
- You are returning to church or faith after an absence rather than entering a specific one-to-one
  relationship — use `../learner-pathways/discipleship_returning_believer_reengagement.md`.
- You want to understand whether the group or church you were in was high-control — use
  `domain-psy-ops/personal-defense/psyops_high_control_group_dynamics_assessment.md`, which assesses
  structure rather than belief, and do that before this rather than inside it.
- You are in a relationship now that has become unhealthy but is not over — use
  `discipleship_dependency_and_over_attachment.md`.
- You want to know what a healthy discipling relationship should look like in the first place — use
  `../pairing-and-relationship/discipleship_what_to_expect_as_mentee.md`, which is the shorter and
  often better starting point.
- The harm involved a crime, a minor, or an ongoing risk to someone else — that routes to authorities
  and, for the person's own position, to `domain-legal/personal-self-advocacy/`.

**Audience:** The person themselves. A program lead or mentor may use it *with* them, never *about*
them, and never without them present.

---

## Inputs / Context

**Required:**

1. **That the person is choosing this.** Not their family, their church, the program, or the person who
   suggested it. If anyone else is driving this, that is the first thing the output must name, because a
   relationship entered under pressure repeats the mechanism that caused the harm.
2. **What they want from a new relationship, in their own words.** Not what went wrong — what they want
   now. If they cannot answer, that is a finding, not a failure, and the honest output is a smaller
   first step than a relationship.
3. **What they already know they will not do again.** Whatever they can name — a practice, a demand, a
   kind of question, a level of access, a setting. This becomes the non-negotiable list, and it is taken
   at face value without being tested or justified.
4. **What structure exists around the new relationship, if any.** A program with a safeguarding lead, a
   church with no policy, or two people and no third party at all. This determines what protection can
   honestly be offered, and where there is none, that is stated rather than implied.

**Optional:**

5. **Declared tradition (optional).** May shape confession practice, the authority a mentor is
   understood to carry, the role of elders, and whether submission language is part of the vocabulary —
   all of which matter here, because these are frequently the mechanisms through which the earlier harm
   operated. Where a tradition is declared, its own practice is described plainly alongside the
   protections that apply regardless; where none is declared, the terms stay tradition-general.
6. **Whether they are working with a professional.** If so, the pace and the disclosure terms should be
   theirs to set with that person, and this output defers to it.
7. **Whether the previous relationship or setting is still present** in their church, family, or
   friendships — which changes what is possible and what must be arranged around.

**If any required input is missing:** Ask, and ask gently, one at a time. If the person cannot say what
they want or is only able to describe what happened, stop: this prompt is not the right tool yet, and
saying so is the correct output. Never infer the person's wishes from what they say was done to them.

## Constraints

### Must

- Run the **safety and clinical screen first**, before any relationship terms are designed, and stop
  there if it returns anything.
- Treat **"not now" and "not this" as complete, respectable outcomes**, stated as first-class options in
  the output rather than as failure states.
- Put **every control in the person's hands**: pace, depth, veto over topics, veto over the person,
  ending without explanation, and the right to change any term later.
- Produce a **non-negotiable list in the person's own words**, unedited and unjustified, that the
  relationship is built around rather than negotiated against.
- State **what protection actually exists** — and where there is none, say so plainly rather than
  describing a safeguard the setting does not have.
- Give **specific, verbatim words** the person can use to set a term, decline a question, slow things
  down, or leave — because the moment these are needed is the moment they are hardest to compose.
- Include **early-warning signs specific to what they described**, framed as their own tripwires, not as
  a diagnosis of anyone.
- State that the person is **never required to disclose what happened** to a new mentor, a program lead,
  or anyone else, in order to be accepted.

### Must Not

- Assess, characterize, confirm, doubt, or rank what happened, or name it as abuse, spiritual abuse,
  manipulation, or a misunderstanding. That determination is not this prompt's to make.
- Evaluate, diagnose, or attribute motive to the person who caused the harm, or to their church, group,
  or leaders.
- Advise on reporting, confrontation, accountability, restitution, or legal action — all of that routes
  out.
- Encourage, urge, time-bound, or set any expectation of forgiveness or reconciliation, or present
  either as a step toward health, a condition of moving on, or a mark of maturity.
- Suggest that the person's caution, slowness, suspicion, or unwillingness is itself a spiritual problem
  to be worked on.
- Frame trying again as brave, necessary, obedient, or as evidence of healing — or frame declining as
  bitterness, unforgiveness, or a hardened heart.
- Name any counselor, therapist, agency, hotline, service, church, network, or group.
- State any legal requirement, reporting duty, limitation period, or threshold. Mark `[VERIFY]` and
  route to `domain-legal/`.
- Quote Scripture text from memory, and do not use any passage to encourage the person to trust, submit,
  reconcile, or return.
- Produce any score, level, stage, or readiness rating for the person or their recovery.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)

- **Must:** name that traditions differ on confession and to whom, on the authority a discipler or elder
  carries, on whether submission to a spiritual leader is expected, on church discipline, and on what
  counts as a spiritual rather than a clinical matter — and note plainly that these are the specific
  places the earlier harm may have operated, so that a declared tradition's own practice is described
  openly enough for the person to decide what they will accept.
- **Must Not:** let any tradition's teaching on authority, submission, forgiveness, restoration, or
  church discipline override the person's control over pace, disclosure, and exit, or be used to
  characterize their reluctance as disobedience. Protection is not adjustable by tradition.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Screen before designing anything

Before a single term is written, check for present danger, risk to anyone else, and clinical need:
active fear of the person involved, ongoing contact they cannot control, a minor or vulnerable adult
still exposed, flashbacks, panic, dissociation, self-harm, suicidality, or an inability to function.
Any of these ends the design work and produces a routing output instead
`[VERIFY: identify the correct service from an official source]`. A relationship designed on top of an
unaddressed safety or clinical situation is not a careful relationship; it is a delay.

### Step 2 — Establish that this is their decision

Ask who wants this. If the answer is anyone but them, say so in the output and stop the design. Being
told that trying again would be good for you is the most common route back into the same shape.

### Step 3 — Take the non-negotiables exactly as given

Write down what they will not do again, in their words, without editing, softening, testing, or asking
them to explain why. A non-negotiable that has to be justified is not a non-negotiable. If the list is
short, leave it short — it grows as they learn what they need, and the output should say so.

### Step 4 — Design the controls, not the relationship

Set out what they hold: how often they meet and who decides, what topics are off the table without
reason given, whether they can bring someone, what they may decline to answer, how they end it, and how
they change any of this later. Write each as something already true rather than something to be
requested — a control that must be asked for is a control the other person holds.

### Step 5 — State what protection exists, honestly

Name what is actually in place: a safeguarding lead, a policy, a third party who knows this
relationship exists, somewhere to go if it goes wrong. Where any of these is absent, say it plainly, and
say what it means. Do not describe a protection the setting does not have, and do not treat a person's
good character as a substitute for a structure.

### Step 6 — Settle disclosure before anything is disclosed

Decide, in advance and in writing, what the new mentor is told, what a program lead is told, and what
nobody is told. Include the sentence they can say when asked something they do not want to answer, and
state the standing rule: nothing about the past has to be shared to be accepted, and a new relationship
that requires the story as an entry fee has already failed the test.

### Step 7 — Write their tripwires and the words

From what they described, build early-warning signs in the second person and in concrete behavioural
terms — a term quietly not honoured, a question after they said no, contact escalating, being told their
caution is a spiritual problem, being asked to keep the relationship private. Pair each with what they
do and the words they say. Then check the whole output: does the person hold every control? Is any term
conditional on someone else agreeing? Is "not now" still visibly available on the last line as it was on
the first?

## Output Format

Produce exactly this structure. Written to the person, in the second person, and short enough to re-read.

```
# Terms for Trying Again — [name or "you"]

## Before Anything Else
[Screen result. If anything surfaced: what it is, that it comes first, the kind of
professional or authority it goes to — [VERIFY] — and that the rest of this document waits.]

## Whose Decision This Is
[One short paragraph. If anyone else is driving it, said plainly here.]

## You Do Not Have To Do This
[Two or three lines. "Not now" and "not this" stated as real, complete answers with nothing
owed to anyone for choosing them.]

## What You Will Not Do Again
[Their words, verbatim, as a list. No commentary, no reasons attached.]

## What You Hold
| Control | What this means | It is yours already — you don't ask for it |
|---|---|---|
| Pace | [..] | [..] |
| Topics | [..] | [..] |
| Ending it | [..] | [..] |
| Changing any of this | [..] | [..] |

## What Protection Actually Exists Here
| Protection | Present? | What that means for you |
|---|---|---|
| Someone who knows this relationship exists | [yes / no] | [..] |
| Somewhere to take a concern | [yes / no — VERIFY locally] | [..] |
| A written policy | [yes / no] | [..] |

[Where these are absent, the sentence that says so without softening it.]

## What Gets Said, and to Whom
- The new mentor is told: [..]
- The program lead is told: [..]
- Nobody is told: [..]
- **You never have to explain what happened to be accepted here.**
- If you are asked something you don't want to answer: "[words]"

## Your Tripwires
| What you notice | What you do | What you say |
|---|---|---|

## Reviewing This
[A date. What changes it. That changing it needs no reason.]

## Verify Before Use
- [ ] Any professional or authority above is a real, locally confirmed service
- [ ] Every term here is one you set, not one you were offered
```

## Verification

- [ ] The safety and clinical screen appears before any relationship design, and stops the output if it
      returns anything.
- [ ] Nothing in the output characterizes what happened or evaluates anyone involved.
- [ ] "Not now" and "not this" appear as complete options, not as failure states.
- [ ] Every control is written as already held by the person, never as something to request.
- [ ] Forgiveness, reconciliation, and disclosure of the past appear nowhere as a requirement,
      expectation, or measure of progress.
- [ ] No service, agency, counselor, or group is named, and no legal duty or threshold is stated.

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Decide what happened to them. Even a sympathetic label — "that was spiritual abuse" — takes the
  determination out of their hands, and the whole point of this document is that it is theirs.
- Treat trying again as the healthy outcome. A person who decides not to enter another discipling
  relationship has not failed at anything, and an output that quietly rewards the other answer is
  applying the same pressure in a warmer voice.
- Write controls as requests. "You could ask to meet less often" hands the decision back to the other
  person; "you set the frequency and can change it" does not.
- Introduce forgiveness, restoration, or reconciliation as a step, a goal, or a sign of progress. Where
  it comes up at all, it comes from them, on their timing, and this prompt does not go there.
- Describe a safeguard the setting does not actually have. "There'd be someone to talk to" is worse than
  "there is nobody — here is what that means", because it will be believed.
- Let a required disclosure of the past become the price of entry. A relationship that needs the story
  before it will begin has reproduced the access problem it was supposed to avoid.
- Read caution, slow pace, or refusal as something to be worked through spiritually.

✅ **DO:**
- Screen first, every time, and let the screen stop the work. The design is not urgent; safety is.
- Take the non-negotiables at face value, unedited and unexplained, and build around them.
- Say plainly where there is no protection, and what that means, so the person can decide with the real
  facts rather than a comfortable version of them.
- Write the words out verbatim — for declining a question, slowing things down, and leaving — because
  composing them in the moment is exactly what does not happen.
- Keep the exit visible on the last line as well as the first.
- Route the assessment questions out: what the past setting was to `domain-psy-ops/`, what to do about
  the person to an attorney or an authority, and the trauma itself to a licensed professional.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-01 (Clear Objective Statement):** the objective fixes the deliverable as *terms the person sets*
  and names the two refusals — no assessment of what happened, no expectation that they proceed — so the
  prompt cannot drift into either evaluation or encouragement.
- **CM-02 (Constraint Specification):** the Must Not block encodes the specific pressures this situation
  attracts — characterizing the harm, urging forgiveness, treating reluctance as a spiritual problem —
  as prohibitions rather than cautions.
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework):** controls, existing protection, disclosure, and
  tripwires are worked as four separate axes, so a warm relationship cannot compensate for an absent
  structure and a good structure cannot compensate for terms the person did not set.
- **OC-03 (Markdown Table Specification):** the protection table forces a yes/no per row, which is what
  prevents the honest answer "there is nobody" from being written as a reassuring paragraph.
- **QA-01 (Self-Verification):** the final check tests the output for the failure it is most likely to
  commit — a control phrased as a request, or an exit that has quietly disappeared by the last section.
