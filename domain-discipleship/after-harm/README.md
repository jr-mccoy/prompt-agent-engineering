# After Harm

**4 prompts** for what the rest of this domain does not cover: the point at which a discipling
relationship has already gone wrong.

Everything else here is built to prevent that — screening, boundaries, covenants, safeguarding policy,
readiness. These four begin after prevention has failed, and they share one constraint, which is what
makes them a set rather than four files that happen to be about the same subject:

> **None of these prompts determines what happened, adjudicates it, disciplines anyone, brokers
> reconciliation, or treats anyone.** They act after harm is already established or already suspected by
> someone else, and every one of them can return "this is not ours — route it."

Four vantage points on one event class: the person who was harmed, the pair it is happening inside, the
mentor who caused it, and the program left holding everyone else.

## The failure modes these are built against

**Repairing what should be reported.** The most serious failure available in this subdirectory, and it
arrives with genuine remorse attached. A sincere apology offered instead of a referral obstructs a
process and increases harm, so two of the four open with a gate that stops the prompt rather than a
caution that qualifies it.

**Care becoming communications.** Once a removal is public, the pull toward managing a story is
immediate and does not feel like a compromise. The aftermath plan therefore fixes the order of care by
proximity to harm and writes that order down, so a later pressure to reassure the widest group first has
something to argue against.

**Treating the person's caution as the problem.** After religious harm, slowness, suspicion and refusal
are read as bitterness or unforgiveness remarkably fast. No prompt here asks anyone to forgive,
reconcile, meet, or explain themselves, and none treats trying again as the healthy outcome.

**Diagnosing instead of describing.** Dependency, trauma, attachment, and abuse are all clinical or
determinative categories, and none of them is this domain's to assign. These prompts work on observable
behaviour and hand every assessment question to whoever owns it.

Consistent with the domain's third load-bearing convention — **formation is not a metric** — nothing here
scores a person, a recovery, a relationship, or an apology.
