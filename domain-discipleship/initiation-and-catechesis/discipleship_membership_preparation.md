# Membership Preparation

**Objective:** Design the preparation someone goes through before becoming a member of a church or
community — with full disclosure of what they are agreeing to, including authority, discipline, giving
expectations, doctrinal assent, and how membership ends — while presenting honestly that traditions
differ on what membership is, and while keeping belonging, care, and welcome entirely unconditional on
it.

> **Boundary guardrail.** This designs preparation and disclosure, not polity. What authority a church
> holds, how discipline works, and who governs are contested questions that route to
> `domain-biblical-studies/theology-research/biblical_theology_church_government_polity.md`; this prompt
> takes the community's polity as given and makes it visible to the person considering it. Anything with
> legal weight — constitutions, trusteeship, charitable obligations, data handling, employment, or the
> handling of a safeguarding matter — is `[VERIFY]` and routes to qualified counsel and
> `domain-legal/`. Where a community's discipline process could affect someone's housing, employment,
> family, or immigration, the disclosure of that is not optional.

**When to use:** You are designing a membership process, or preparing a specific person, and want them
to know what they are agreeing to before they agree to it.

**When NOT to use:**
- You want the polity question worked — use
  `domain-biblical-studies/theology-research/biblical_theology_church_government_polity.md`.
- You want the community's confession analysed — use
  `domain-biblical-studies/theology-research/biblical_theology_creed_confession_analysis.md`.
- You are designing baptism preparation — use `discipleship_baptism_preparation.md`; where a tradition
  binds them, design both and say plainly that they are bound.
- You want the mentor–mentee agreement — use
  `../pairing-and-relationship/discipleship_relationship_covenant.md`.

**Audience:** Pastors, elders, membership coordinators, and discipleship leads designing the process.

## Inputs / Context

**Required:**

1. **What membership means here.** Covenant, contract, sacramental status, roll entry, voting right,
   accountability relationship, or some combination — stated as this community understands it.
2. **The full set of obligations and rights.** What a member commits to, what they receive, what they
   may do that non-members may not, and what is expected of them financially, practically, and
   doctrinally.
3. **Authority and discipline as actually practised.** What the community may do, to whom, on what
   grounds, by what process, with what appeal, and with what consequences — including social ones.
4. **How membership ends.** Resignation, transfer, lapse, removal — the process for each, and what
   happens to the person afterwards.
5. **Whether anything material depends on membership.** Housing, employment, a role, a school place,
   childcare, immigration sponsorship, or a family relationship. If yes, the disclosure and the
   voluntariness analysis both change substantially.

**Optional:**

6. **Declared tradition (optional).** Where declared, the process runs its own vocabulary, vows, and
   confession, stated as that tradition's own.
7. **The confession or statement of faith** required, and the level of assent expected — full agreement,
   non-contradiction, or willingness to be taught.
8. **Existing membership material.**

**If any required input is missing:** Ask before proceeding. If item 3 or item 4 cannot be answered
concretely, that is the finding: a community that cannot describe its own discipline process or its exit
process is not ready to ask anyone to join, and the first deliverable is that statement.

## Constraints

### Must

- **Disclose before commitment**, not after: obligations, authority, discipline, giving expectations,
  doctrinal assent, and exit — all in the candidate's hands, in writing, before they are asked to
  decide.
- Describe **discipline concretely**: who may initiate, on what grounds, what process, what appeal,
  what the outcome can be, and what the social consequences are in practice as distinct from on paper.
- Describe **exit fully**, including the fact that a member may leave at any time, how, and what the
  community will and will not do afterwards — including whether their departure is announced.
- State **giving expectations honestly** — required, expected, hoped-for, or private — with no figure
  set for anyone and no giving record visible to anyone making membership decisions.
- State the **level of doctrinal assent** required, and what happens to a member who later comes to
  disagree.
- Keep **belonging, care, welcome, and pastoral support unconditional** on membership, and say so
  explicitly.
- Present the **divergence on membership itself** — traditions differ on whether formal membership
  exists, what it confers, whether it is required for communion or service, and what discipline means.
- Where **anything material depends on membership**, disclose it prominently and analyse whether consent
  can be freely given at all.
- Include a **stated, cost-free "not now"** and a real path for someone who participates fully without
  ever joining.
- Reference Scripture **by address only**, translation named, noting where the membership and discipline
  passages are read differently.

### Must Not

- Quote Scripture text from memory or assert verse wording.
- Present formal membership as a biblical requirement where traditions differ, or describe communities
  without it as deficient.
- Withhold, soften, or postpone disclosure of discipline, authority, or exit until after someone has
  joined.
- Make friendship, care, service, participation, or a mentor relationship conditional on membership.
- Set a giving figure or percentage, disclose anyone's giving to decision-makers, or link membership
  standing to giving.
- Use urgency, an event date, a cohort deadline, or public commitment to press a decision.
- Design a discipline process that operates without a stated appeal, or that mixes a safeguarding matter
  into an internal process — safeguarding routes out and to authorities `[VERIFY]`.
- State law on constitutions, charity governance, employment, data, or defamation. Mark `[VERIFY]`.
- Invent statistics or research on membership, commitment, or retention.
- Publish a member's departure, discipline, or disagreement beyond what the person has agreed to and
  what the community's own stated process requires.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)

- **Must:** name the genuine divergence — traditions that hold formal membership, traditions that hold
  sacramental incorporation instead, traditions with no membership category at all; differences on
  whether membership gates communion, service, or voting; and differences on the meaning and reach of
  discipline. Attribute each to identifiable streams; let the declared tradition run its own.
- **Must Not:** present this community's polity as the settled biblical model, or design the process so
  that a candidate from another tradition must treat their previous belonging as invalid.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Write down what membership actually is here

State it plainly: covenant, contract, sacramental status, roll, or accountability relationship. Then
state what other traditions do, briefly and without evaluation, so the candidate can locate what they
are joining.

### Step 2 — Build the disclosure pack before the process

Assemble everything the candidate receives before deciding: obligations, rights, authority, discipline
with appeal, giving expectations, doctrinal assent, and exit. If any of it is uncomfortable to write
down, that discomfort is the reason it must be written down.

### Step 3 — Describe discipline as it is actually practised

Write the process concretely: initiation, grounds, steps, who decides, appeal, outcomes, and the social
reality — what actually happens to a person's relationships in this community. Then state what is
excluded from the process entirely: any safeguarding matter, which goes to authorities and specialist
handling `[VERIFY]` and is never an internal disciplinary question.

### Step 4 — Describe exit as generously as entry

Write resignation, transfer, lapse, and removal. State that a member may leave at any time and how,
whether departure is announced and in what terms, what records are kept `[VERIFY]`, and what the
community does and does not do afterwards. A community whose exit is harder to find than its entry has
a problem the membership process should not conceal.

### Step 5 — Test whether consent can be free

Where anything material depends on membership — housing, work, a role, childcare, a family relationship
— write it prominently and assess honestly whether a "no" is available. Where it is not, say so, and
state what the community will do to make declining genuinely possible.

### Step 6 — Design the preparation sessions

Lay out sessions. For each: the question, Scripture by address with the differently-read passages
flagged, what the candidate does, and *what this session must not require them to disclose* — including
their giving, their doubts, and their past.

### Step 7 — Build the "not now" and the non-member path

Write the words for declining and the leader's response. Then design the real path for someone who
participates fully and never joins: what they can do, what they cannot, and how nothing about their
welcome changes.

### Step 8 — Verify

Check: is every disclosure in the candidate's hands before the decision; is discipline concrete with an
appeal; is exit as clear as entry; is belonging unconditional; is any material dependency disclosed and
analysed; is safeguarding entirely outside the discipline process.

- [ ] Every disclosure — obligations, authority, discipline, giving, assent, exit — is in the candidate's
      hands before the decision.
- [ ] Discipline is described concretely, with an appeal, and safeguarding is entirely outside it.
- [ ] Exit is documented as fully as entry, including whether departure is announced.
- [ ] No giving figure is set, and no giving record is visible to anyone making membership decisions.
- [ ] Belonging, care, and welcome are stated as unconditional on membership, with a real non-member
      path.

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Disclose the discipline process after someone joins. If it is only acceptable once someone is
  committed, that is information about the process, not about the timing.
- Let membership gate friendship or care. It converts a covenant into a condition of belonging, which is
  the opposite of what every tradition claims membership is for.
- Set a giving figure or let giving records reach the people who decide about membership. It creates a
  financial test with a spiritual name.
- Bury the exit. A process where joining takes six sessions and leaving takes an unanswered email tells
  the candidate something true that nobody said out loud.
- Handle a safeguarding matter through church discipline. It delays proper handling, protects the wrong
  person, and can obstruct an investigation.
- Run a membership cohort with a deadline. It turns a personal decision into a scheduling problem and
  produces members who joined because the class was running.

✅ **DO:**
- Write the disclosure pack first, and treat any reluctance to write something down as the signal it is.
- Describe the social reality of discipline, not just the paper process — that is what actually happens
  to people.
- Document exit as generously as entry, including what is said publicly.
- Test whether "no" is genuinely available where anything material depends on membership.
- Build the non-member path and staff it, so full participation without joining is real rather than
  theoretical.
- Route polity questions out. This process makes the community's polity visible; it does not defend it.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-01 (Clear Objective Statement):** the objective makes full pre-commitment disclosure the
  deliverable, which reframes membership preparation from persuasion to informed consent.
- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** the disclosure pack is assembled before the sessions
  are designed, so nothing uncomfortable can be deferred into a later conversation that never happens.
- **CM-02 (Constraint Specification):** the Must Not block encodes the absolutes — no post-commitment
  disclosure, no conditional belonging, no giving-linked standing, no safeguarding inside discipline.
- **OC-03 (Markdown Table Specification):** obligations, discipline, and exit are tabulated so that no
  element can be listed without its process, appeal, and real-world consequence beside it.
- **QA-04 (Uncertainty Acknowledgment):** the divergence on what membership even is — including
  traditions with no such category — is held open rather than settled in the community's favour.
