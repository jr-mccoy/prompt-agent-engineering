# Catechesis Design

**Objective:** Design a catechesis — a structured, sequenced instruction in the faith, in a tradition's
own vocabulary and often in question-and-answer form — that teaches with confidence while leaving the
learner's questions genuinely open, marks what is contested among Christians as contested, and never
lets memorization, examination, or an attached rite become an instrument of pressure.

> **Boundary guardrail.** This designs instruction, not doctrine. The content of a tradition's
> catechism, creed, or confession is that tradition's own; this prompt does not write doctrine, settle
> disputed questions, or evaluate a confession's claims — that routes to
> `domain-biblical-studies/theology-research/`. Where catechumens are minors, the safeguarding policy
> governs, guardians are involved, and no examination or rite may be used to pressure a young person's
> decision. Where a catechumen's questions have become distress rather than inquiry, that routes to
> pastoral care and, where clinical, to licensed professionals `[VERIFY]`.

**When to use:** You are building a catechesis — for adult inquirers, for young people, for new
believers, or for those preparing for a rite — and want structure that forms rather than merely
transmits.

**When NOT to use:**
- You want the doctrine itself worked or compared — use
  `domain-biblical-studies/theology-research/`.
- You want a single teaching session built — use
  `domain-biblical-studies/study-methods-teaching/biblical_lesson_plan_builder.md`.
- You want a formation curriculum rather than doctrinal instruction — use
  `../curriculum-architecture/discipleship_curriculum_architecture.md`; catechesis is one strand inside
  it, not a replacement for it.
- You want the initiation rite's preparation — use `discipleship_baptism_preparation.md` or
  `discipleship_membership_preparation.md`.

**Audience:** Catechists, clergy, curriculum designers, and discipleship leads building or rebuilding a
catechetical program.

---

## Inputs / Context

**Required:**

1. **The tradition and its catechetical source.** Which catechism, creed, confession, or framework the
   instruction follows — supplied by the user as text or description, never reconstructed from memory.
2. **Who the catechumens are.** Adults, young people, children with guardians, converts from another
   faith or tradition, or the previously-churched — and their literacy, language, and prior knowledge.
3. **What the catechesis leads to.** A rite, membership, communion, confirmation, or nothing formal —
   and whether completion is a condition of anything.
4. **Duration and cadence.** How long, how often, and whether the process is fixed-length or paced to
   the learner.

**Optional:**

5. **Declared tradition (optional).** In this subdirectory a tradition is usually declared by default,
   since a catechesis is a tradition's own instrument. Its vocabulary, ordering, and confession run
   plainly as its own.
6. **Sponsors, godparents, or catechetical companions**, where the tradition includes them.
7. **Existing material** already in use, supplied as text.

**If any required input is missing:** Ask before proceeding — especially item 1. A catechesis
reconstructed from a half-remembered confession will contain plausible sentences that the tradition does
not hold, and catechumens will learn them as authoritative.

## Constraints

### Must

- Work from the **user-supplied catechetical source**, quoting or paraphrasing only what they provide,
  and marking anything not supplied as `[VERIFY: supply from the tradition's own text]`.
- **Sequence for understanding**, not for the source document's order: state where the instruction
  departs from the catechism's sequence and why.
- Distinguish, visibly and throughout, between what is **held by nearly all Christians**, what is **this
  tradition's own**, and what is **genuinely contested** — the three-tier marking is the design's
  central mechanism.
- Keep **questions open**: build a standing place in every session for the learner's own questions, and
  design at least one session where the hardest objection to the material is stated in its strongest
  form.
- Pair each unit with **practice and prayer**, not only content — a catechesis that produces recall
  without practice has taught the wrong thing about the faith it is transmitting.
- Where **memorization** is part of the tradition, design it as formation with a purpose stated, never
  as a test with a pass mark or a public performance.
- Where **examination or a rite** follows, remove every pressure mechanism: no deadline for a learner's
  readiness, no cohort that must finish together, no public appeal, and a stated cost-free "not yet."
- Design for the **learner who does not assent**: what happens if someone completes the instruction and
  does not believe it, which must be welcome and honest rather than failure.
- Design for **literacy, language, and oral learners**, with an oral route that is equal rather than
  reduced.
- Reference Scripture **by address only**, translation named.

### Must Not

- Quote Scripture text from memory, or quote a catechism, creed, or confession from memory. Every such
  text comes from the user.
- Invent catechism questions and answers, article numbers, conciliar decisions, historical dates, or
  attribution of doctrines to figures or councils.
- Present this tradition's distinctives as the shared Christian position, or contested questions as
  settled.
- Score, rank, grade, or publish a catechumen's answers, progress, or readiness.
- Use examination, memorization, or the approaching rite to press a decision, or run a cohort deadline
  that a slower learner must meet.
- Treat a learner's questions, objections, or non-assent as resistance, rebellion, or a discipline
  matter.
- Design for minors outside the safeguarding policy and guardian consent.
- Require public recitation, testimony, or confession of belief as a condition of proceeding.
- State law relating to religious instruction, guardianship, or education. Mark `[VERIFY]`.
- Name a specific published curriculum, author, or edition as though its content were known.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)

- **Must:** run this tradition's catechesis plainly and without apology as its own, while the three-tier
  marking keeps visible what is shared, what is distinctive, and what is contested — with the contested
  items attributed to identifiable streams so the learner knows where the disagreement lies.
- **Must Not:** flatten the tradition into a generic Christianity, or present its distinctives as
  uncontested. A catechesis that hides its own distinctiveness has misled the learner about what they
  are joining.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Fix the source and the destination

State the catechetical source as supplied, and what the instruction leads to — rite, membership, or
nothing formal. State whether completion conditions anything, and if it does, say so plainly at the
start rather than at the end.

### Step 2 — Build the three-tier marking scheme

Before sequencing, set the scheme the whole catechesis uses: **shared** (held by nearly all Christians),
**ours** (this tradition's own), **contested** (genuinely disputed, with the streams named). Every unit
carries its markings. This scheme is what allows the instruction to be confident without being
misleading.

### Step 3 — Sequence for understanding

Order the units by what a learner needs first, not by the source document's arrangement. State each
departure from the source's order and why. Note the dependencies: what must be understood before what.

### Step 4 — Design the standing place for questions

Build the question mechanism into every session: how questions are collected, what happens to a question
the catechist cannot answer (which is honesty and a route to `domain-biblical-studies/`, not
improvisation), and how a question that recurs across cohorts changes the catechesis.

### Step 5 — Pair content with practice

For each unit, attach the practice, prayer, or act that belongs with it — so that instruction on prayer
produces praying and instruction on the sacraments meets the tradition's actual practice. Content
without practice trains catechumens to treat the faith as information.

### Step 6 — Design memorization and examination without a pass mark

Where the tradition memorizes, state what is memorized and why, and design it as formation. Where the
tradition examines, design the examination as a conversation that establishes readiness and understanding
— never as a graded test, never publicly, and never with a fixed date the learner must meet.

### Step 7 — Design for the learner who does not assent

Write what happens when a catechumen completes the instruction and does not believe it, or believes part
of it, or has moved to a different position. This is the design's honesty test: the answer must be
welcome, an open door, and no change in their standing as a person in this community.

### Step 8 — Verify

Check: is every catechetical text user-supplied; is the three-tier marking applied to every unit; is
there a standing place for questions; is memorization free of a pass mark; is there a real "not yet";
is the non-assenting learner welcome; does safeguarding govern where catechumens are minors.

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Reconstruct a catechism from memory. The output will be fluent, close, and wrong in places, and
  catechumens will memorize it as their tradition's teaching.
- Teach the tradition's distinctives as though all Christians held them. The learner discovers otherwise
  eventually, and the discovery undermines everything taught alongside it.
- Grade catechumens. A score turns instruction into performance, and the learner who is struggling stops
  asking the questions that would have helped them.
- Run a cohort to a fixed rite date. It converts personal readiness into a scheduling constraint, and
  the slower learner proceeds because the calendar did.
- Treat objections as resistance. The hardest question in the room is usually the one several people are
  holding silently, and answering it well is the most formative moment available.
- Require public recitation or testimony to proceed. It selects for confidence rather than
  understanding, and it frightens the people who most need the instruction.

✅ **DO:**
- Work only from the user's own supplied text, and mark every gap `[VERIFY]`.
- Apply the three-tier marking to every unit — it is what lets a catechesis be confident and honest at
  the same time.
- Sequence for understanding and state where you departed from the source's order and why.
- Pair every unit with a practice, so instruction on the faith produces the faith's practices.
- Design the strongest-objection session deliberately and state the objection in its strongest form.
- Say in advance what happens if someone completes the catechesis and does not believe it — and make the
  answer a genuinely open door.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-01 (Clear Objective Statement):** the objective holds two things together — teach a tradition
  confidently, and keep the learner's questions open — which is the tension catechesis usually resolves
  in the wrong direction.
- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** the marking scheme is built before sequencing, so
  every unit inherits the shared/ours/contested distinction rather than having it applied selectively.
- **ED-01 (Iterative Scaffolding):** units are sequenced by dependency and understanding rather than by
  the source document's arrangement, with departures stated and justified.
- **CM-02 (Constraint Specification):** the Must Not block encodes the absolutes — nothing quoted from
  memory, no invented questions or articles, no grading, no deadline pressure, no required public
  recitation.
- **QA-05 (Citation Requirements):** Scripture by address, and every catechetical text traced to a
  user-supplied source with gaps marked `[VERIFY]` — the discipline that keeps a catechesis from
  transmitting fabrications as doctrine.
