# Baptism Preparation

**Objective:** Design the preparation a person walks through before baptism — inside the community's own
practice, which this prompt takes as given rather than evaluates — while showing the candidate honestly
that Christian traditions differ on who is baptized, how, and what it means, and while removing every
form of pressure, deadline, counting, and belonging-conditionality from the process.

> **Boundary guardrail.** This prompt does not decide between infant and believer baptism, between
> modes, or between accounts of what baptism effects. Those are contested among Christians and are
> presented, attributed, and left unresolved — the community's own practice governs what actually
> happens, and its authority for that decision sits with the community, not here. Where a candidate is
> a minor, the safeguarding policy governs and consent involves guardians. Where a candidate's baptism
> would create danger — family, legal, or physical — that risk assessment belongs to people who know
> the setting `[VERIFY]`, and no preparation may urge a decision whose cost the candidate alone will
> bear.

**When to use:** You are designing the preparation process a candidate goes through, or preparing one
person, and want it to form them rather than process them.

**When NOT to use:**
- You want the traditions compared for a learner's own study — use
  `domain-biblical-studies/learner-self-study/biblical_learner_compare_traditions_on_practice.md`.
- You want the doctrine worked across traditions — use
  `domain-biblical-studies/theology-research/biblical_doctrine_study_neutral.md`.
- You want the whole formation pathway — use
  `../curriculum-architecture/discipleship_curriculum_architecture.md`.
- You are designing membership rather than baptism — use `discipleship_membership_preparation.md`.
  Where a tradition binds them, design both and say plainly that they are bound.

**Audience:** Pastors, catechists, discipleship leads, and mentors preparing candidates.

---

## Inputs / Context

**Required:**

1. **The community's practice, stated plainly.** Who is baptized, at what age or stage, by what mode,
   by whom, and what the community understands it to do. This is an input, not a question.
2. **Who the candidate is.** Adult, young person, or infant with guardians; new to faith, returning,
   raised in the community, or coming from another tradition.
3. **Whether a prior baptism exists.** This is the single most sensitive question in the process, and
   how the community handles it must be stated before preparation begins rather than discovered
   mid-way.
4. **What preparation the community already requires**, and what it means by "ready."

**Optional:**

5. **Declared tradition (optional).** Where declared, the preparation runs its rite, vocabulary, and
   promises as that tradition's own — stated plainly as its own, with alternatives still visible to the
   candidate.
6. **Cost or risk to the candidate.** Family rupture, employment, immigration, or physical danger.
7. **Sponsors, godparents, or witnesses**, where the tradition includes them.

**If any required input is missing:** Ask before proceeding. If item 3 is unresolved — if the community
has not decided how it treats a prior baptism from another tradition — resolve that first. Discovering
it mid-preparation puts a candidate in the middle of a question that is not theirs.

---

## Constraints

### Must

- Run the preparation inside the **community's stated practice**, and say plainly that it is the
  community's practice rather than the only Christian one.
- Show the candidate the **genuine divergence** — subjects (infant or believer), mode (immersion,
  affusion, aspersion), meaning (sign, seal, sacrament, ordinance, means of grace), the standing of a
  prior baptism, and rebaptism — with each position stated in the form its own adherents would
  recognize.
- Impose **no deadline, target, event date, or seasonal pressure** on a candidate's decision, and offer
  a stated, cost-free way to say "not yet" at any point.
- Make clear that **belonging is not conditional** on baptism: the candidate is in the community's life
  before, during, and if they never proceed.
- Handle the **prior-baptism question with the community's answer stated up front**, and treat a
  candidate's own conviction about their earlier baptism as theirs to hold.
- Prepare the candidate for **what actually happens** — the words, the water, the questions asked, who
  is present, what they will say — so nothing on the day is a surprise.
- Include **what baptism does not do**: it does not complete formation, guarantee feeling, resolve
  doubt, or fix a life. Candidates who expect otherwise are set up for a hard week afterwards.
- Design the **afterwards** — what continues, who walks with them, and what the next stage of formation
  is — before designing the day.
- Where the candidate faces real cost, ensure the **risk conversation happens with people who know the
  setting**, and that no one urges the decision.
- Reference Scripture **by address only**, translation named, noting which passages are read differently
  by each position.

### Must Not

- Quote Scripture text from memory or assert verse wording.
- Adjudicate the subjects, mode, meaning, or rebaptism questions, or describe another tradition's
  practice as invalid, deficient, or unbiblical.
- Apply pressure of any kind: urgency, emotional escalation, public appeal, repeated asking, a
  scheduled event that must be filled, or the implication that delay is disobedience or unbelief.
- Count, publish, target, or report baptism numbers.
- Make community belonging, friendship, service, or standing conditional on proceeding.
- Baptize or prepare a minor outside the safeguarding policy and guardian consent processes.
- Urge a candidate whose baptism would carry family, legal, or physical danger toward a decision, or
  assess that danger on their behalf.
- Invent baptism statistics, historical claims, patristic citations, or liturgical history.
- State law on consent, guardianship, or religious registration. Mark `[VERIFY]`.
- Promise what baptism will feel like, produce, or change.
- Use a candidate's story, testimony, or images publicly without freely given, revocable consent — and
  never where risk exists.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)

- **Must:** present infant and believer baptism, each mode, each account of what baptism effects, and
  each position on rebaptism as held positions with identifiable streams behind them, stated in their
  strongest form. The community's own practice is run plainly and without apology *as its own*.
- **Must Not:** resolve the disagreement, present the community's practice as the settled Christian
  position, or handle a candidate arriving from another tradition in a way that requires them to
  disown their own history.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — State the community's practice, and its status

Write what this community does and what it understands baptism to be — plainly, without hedging. Then
state, in the same place, that other Christians hold other positions and that the candidate will see
them. A candidate who later discovers the disagreement independently loses trust in everything else
they were taught.

### Step 2 — Settle the prior-baptism question before anyone asks

State the community's position on a baptism received elsewhere, and on rebaptism, before preparation
begins. Include how a candidate's own conviction is respected where it differs, and what the community
will and will not do.

### Step 3 — Show the divergence honestly

Build the comparison: subjects, mode, meaning, rebaptism, and the passages each position reads
differently. Route the exegesis to `domain-biblical-studies/exegesis-interpretation/` and the doctrinal
comparison to `theology-research/`. State that this is offered so the candidate understands what they
are doing and what others do — not so they can choose a different community.

### Step 4 — Remove every pressure mechanism

Audit the process for pressure: a date already booked, an event to fill, a public appeal, a repeated
ask, a testimony expectation, a seasonal rhythm that makes delay conspicuous. Remove each, or state the
guard. Then write the "not yet" — the words a candidate can use, and the words the leader says back.

### Step 5 — Prepare the candidate for the day itself

Write what will happen: the words, the questions, who will be there, what they will say, what they will
wear, what happens if they cry or freeze. Include the practical detail nobody thinks to mention, which
is what candidates are actually anxious about.

### Step 6 — Say what baptism does not do

Write it plainly and give it a session: it does not complete formation, produce a feeling, settle doubt,
or repair a life. State what the days afterwards often feel like, so an ordinary anticlimax is not
experienced as a verdict on the candidate's faith.

### Step 7 — Design the afterwards first

Before finalizing the day, design what follows: the next formation stage, who walks with them, what
happens in the first month, and how the community marks that this is a beginning. A baptism with no
designed afterwards teaches that the event was the point.

### Step 8 — Verify

Check the preparation: is the community's practice stated as its own; is the divergence honestly shown;
is every pressure mechanism removed; is the "not yet" real and cost-free; does the afterwards exist;
does safeguarding govern where the candidate is a minor.

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Book the date and then prepare the candidate. The moment a date exists, everything afterwards is
  pressure, however gently it is applied.
- Hide the disagreement. Candidates find it, usually within a year, and discovering that it was withheld
  damages their trust in the community's honesty far more than the disagreement itself would have.
- Let baptism function as the entry to belonging. It reliably produces people who proceed for the
  community rather than for God, and they know it later.
- Count baptisms. The number changes the behaviour of everyone who can see it, and no one chooses that
  drift deliberately.
- Promise a feeling. Many people feel very little, and a candidate expecting transformation experiences
  an ordinary Tuesday as evidence against their own faith.
- Urge a candidate whose baptism would break their family or endanger them. The cost is entirely theirs
  and the decision must be too.

✅ **DO:**
- State the community's practice without apology and as its own.
- Settle the prior-baptism question in writing before anyone is in the middle of it.
- Give "not yet" real words, for the candidate and for the leader, and make sure nothing changes when it
  is used.
- Cover the practical details of the day — people are anxious about what they will wear and whether
  they will cry, not about sacramental theology.
- Say plainly what baptism does not do, and describe the ordinary week that often follows.
- Design the afterwards first, so the event is a doorway rather than a finish line.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-01 (Clear Objective Statement):** the objective separates *preparing well inside a practice* from
  *adjudicating the practice*, which is the distinction this whole subdirectory depends on.
- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** the prior-baptism question and the pressure audit come
  before session design, and the afterwards is designed before the day — orderings that each prevent a
  specific, common harm.
- **CM-02 (Constraint Specification):** the Must Not block encodes the absolutes: no adjudication, no
  pressure mechanism, no counting, no conditional belonging, no urging where risk exists.
- **QA-04 (Uncertainty Acknowledgment):** the divergence table is required to hold each position in its
  strongest form and leave the question open while the community's own practice proceeds plainly.
- **QA-05 (Citation Requirements):** Scripture by address with the differently-read passages identified,
  and no invented patristic, historical, or liturgical claims.
