# Multiplication Design

**Objective:** Design a discipleship pathway that reproduces — specifying what a disciple must be able
to hand on, how handing-on is deliberately taught rather than assumed to emerge, how depth is preserved
across generations, and what safeguards prevent multiplication from degrading into recruitment pressure
or thinned-out formation.

> **Boundary guardrail.** Multiplication is an invitation, never an obligation. No participant is
> required to disciple someone else, and declining carries no penalty, no loss of standing, and no
> withdrawal of care. A pathway that treats reproduction as a debt owed by the discipled has become
> coercive and must be redesigned.

**When to use:** Your discipleship pathway currently ends with a formed disciple, and you want it to
end with a disciple who can and will walk someone else through the same thing.

**When NOT to use:**
- You are designing the base pathway itself — use `discipleship_curriculum_architecture.md` first.
- You are assessing whether one specific person is ready to mentor — use
  `../mentor-equipping/discipleship_mentor_readiness_assessment.md`.
- You need more mentors for an existing program right now — use
  `../program-operations/discipleship_mentor_pipeline_and_capacity.md`.
- You are developing an apprentice *Bible-study group leader* — use
  `domain-biblical-studies/group-leader-facilitation/biblical_groupleader_apprentice_development.md`.

**Audience:** Discipleship pastors, program designers, and ministry leaders whose pathway currently
terminates rather than reproduces.

---

## Inputs / Context

**Required:**

1. **The existing pathway.** Its stages, duration, and end horizon — what a completer currently is and
   can do.
2. **Current reproduction rate.** Honestly: of those who have completed, how many have gone on to
   disciple someone? If unknown, say unknown; do not estimate.
3. **Capacity reality.** How many mentors exist, how many people are waiting, and what the program can
   actually support.
4. **What "handing on" means here.** Whether a reproducing disciple runs the same curriculum, an
   adapted version, or an informal relationship — and who decides.

**Optional:**

5. **Declared tradition (optional).** May shape who is understood to be authorized to disciple others,
   what commissioning or recognition looks like, and whether ordination, membership, or eldership gates
   apply. These are named as that stream's commitments, not universals.
6. **Known failures.** Previous attempts at multiplication in this context and how they went.

**If any required input is missing:** Ask clarifying questions before proceeding. In particular, do not
proceed on an assumed reproduction rate — a multiplication design built on an imagined baseline
optimizes for a problem the program may not have.

---

## Constraints

### Must

- Specify precisely **what a disciple must be able to hand on** — described as capacities, not as
  content coverage.
- Make handing-on **taught and practiced**, with graduated responsibility, not assumed to follow from
  being discipled well.
- Include a **generational depth test**: what would have to be true for a third and fourth generation
  to be as well formed as the first, and how the design would detect dilution.
- Include an **explicit off-ramp**: how someone declines or steps back from reproducing without
  losing standing, care, or belonging.
- Name the **dilution mechanism** specific to this design — the concrete way quality would degrade at
  generation three — and the countermeasure.
- Address what the **original mentor releases**, and how the relationship changes rather than ends.
- State the **capacity arithmetic** honestly: what multiplication does and does not solve about the
  waiting list, and over what time.

### Must Not

- Frame reproduction as an obligation, a debt, a proof of genuine faith, or a condition of continued
  investment.
- Invent multiplication statistics, generational-growth models, church-growth research, or claims about
  reproduction rates in other programs.
- Emit a numeric readiness score for whether someone may disciple another.
- Present a specific multiplication model — a named method, a fixed generational structure, a
  particular number of people each disciple must reach — as the biblical pattern.
- Quote Scripture text from memory; addresses only where a passage grounds a design choice.
- Design a structure in which a person's standing, access, or care depends on how many people they have
  discipled.
- Solve a mentor shortage by lowering the readiness bar. Route capacity problems to
  `../program-operations/discipleship_mentor_pipeline_and_capacity.md`.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)

- **Must:** note where authorization to disciple others is understood differently across streams —
  informal and lay-led, elder-approved, formally commissioned, ordained, or gender-restricted in some
  traditions — and let a declared tradition set its own gate while alternatives stay visible.
- **Must Not:** treat any one tradition's authorization structure or multiplication model as the
  biblical norm, or imply that a tradition with formal gates is less committed to reproduction.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Establish the honest baseline

Restate the pathway, the end horizon, and the actual reproduction rate. If it is unknown, say so and
name what would need to be tracked. Say plainly whether the current pathway even attempts reproduction
or merely hopes for it.

### Step 2 — Define the reproducible core

Specify what must survive transmission: the capacities, practices, and relational habits a reproducing
disciple must carry. Distinguish the **core** (must be handed on intact) from the **local** (can and
should be adapted by each generation) from the **incidental** (currently in the pathway but need not
travel at all). Getting this three-way split right is what prevents both dilution and rigidity.

### Step 3 — Design the handing-on apprenticeship

Build graduated responsibility into the pathway itself, so a participant learns to disciple while still
being discipled. Stage it — observing, contributing, co-leading, leading with support, leading
independently — and say what support and feedback exist at each stage. Do not defer all of this to
after completion.

### Step 4 — Define the invitation and the off-ramp

Specify how the invitation to reproduce is extended: when, by whom, in what words, and with what
explicit permission to decline. Write the off-ramp concretely — what someone says, what happens next,
and what does not change about their standing.

### Step 5 — Build the generational depth test

Describe what generation three should look like if the design works. Then name the specific dilution
mechanism — usually one of: core content lost in retelling, practice replaced by information,
relationship replaced by curriculum delivery, or the reproducing mentor never having been formed in the
first place. State how each generation is supported and how dilution would be detected early.

### Step 6 — Design the release

Say what changes between the original mentor and the disciple who is now discipling someone else. Name
what the mentor hands over, what they keep offering, and how the relationship avoids both abrupt
severance and permanent dependence.

### Step 7 — Do the capacity arithmetic and stress-test

Work out what multiplication actually does to capacity over a stated horizon, with the assumptions
visible. Then check the design against the four multiplication failures:

- **Pressure drift** — invitation has become expectation, and declining now costs something.
- **Dilution** — generation three receives a thinner version and nobody noticed.
- **Premature sending** — capacity pressure pushed people into discipling before they were ready.
- **Franchise drift** — reproduction is measured in units started rather than people formed.

## Verification

- [ ] Reproduction is framed as invitation throughout; the off-ramp is concrete and costs nothing.
- [ ] The reproducible core is split three ways (core / local / incidental), not treated as one block.
- [ ] Handing-on is taught inside the pathway, not deferred entirely to after completion.
- [ ] A specific dilution mechanism is named, with an early-detection signal — not a generic warning.
- [ ] No invented statistics, growth models, or reproduction-rate claims appear.
- [ ] No numeric readiness score gates who may disciple others.

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Assume that being well discipled produces the ability to disciple. It is a distinct skill and it has
  to be taught.
- Let "everyone disciples someone" become the design goal. Some people should not, some cannot right
  now, and a design that cannot absorb that will apply pressure.
- Measure multiplication in groups launched or chains started. Those numbers rise fastest exactly when
  formation is thinning.
- Hand on everything. A pathway that must travel intact in every detail will not survive contact with
  a different person in a different context.
- Send someone because the waiting list is long. Premature sending damages two people and the design
  that permitted it.
- Cite a generational multiplication model or church-growth finding as evidence.

✅ **DO:**
- Name the incidental elements explicitly. Telling a reproducing disciple what they may drop is as
  important as telling them what they must keep.
- Build the apprenticeship into the last third of the pathway, so reproduction is practiced under
  support rather than attempted alone afterward.
- Write the decline script yourself. If the words for saying no are not provided, the invitation is not
  genuinely free.
- Pick the single most likely dilution mechanism for *this* design and instrument for it, rather than
  listing all four generically.
- Show the capacity arithmetic with assumptions visible, including what multiplication will not fix.
- Keep the original mentor available in a changed role, so release is a transition rather than a
  disappearance.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-01 (Clear Objective Statement):** the objective binds reproduction to preserved depth, so the
  prompt cannot be satisfied by a design that multiplies units while thinning formation.
- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** baseline → core → apprenticeship → invitation →
  depth test → release → arithmetic, forcing the honest reproduction rate to be established before any
  design decision rests on it.
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework):** the core/local/incidental split and the four-failure
  check analyze the design along axes — transmission fidelity, consent, capacity, quality — that a
  single-axis growth framing collapses.
- **ED-01 (Iterative Scaffolding):** the five-stage apprenticeship (observe → contribute → co-lead →
  lead with support → lead independently) builds discipling capability under decreasing support inside
  the pathway itself.
- **CM-02 (Constraint Specification):** the Must Not block encodes the domain's anti-coercion guard —
  no obligation, no standing tied to reproduction, no readiness score, no shortage solved by lowering
  the bar.
