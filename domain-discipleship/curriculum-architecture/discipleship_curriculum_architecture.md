# Discipleship Curriculum Architecture

**Objective:** Design a complete multi-stage discipleship curriculum that moves a person from where
they actually are toward maturity in Christ — with named stages, sequenced modules, observable
formation outcomes, honest evidence of growth, a realistic pace, and defined entry and exit points —
without endorsing one tradition's distinctives as the biblical model or reducing spiritual formation
to a score.

> **Boundary guardrail.** This designs a *curriculum*. It is not pastoral counseling, spiritual
> direction, clinical care, or a substitute for a local congregation. Where a participant's needs
> include mental-health concerns, abuse, addiction, self-harm, or crisis, the curriculum must route
> them to licensed professionals and — where there is danger — to appropriate authorities and
> emergency services, not absorb them into a discipleship track.

**When to use:** You are building a discipleship pathway from scratch, or rebuilding one that has
become a pile of unrelated studies. You need the whole arc — not a single lesson, not a single
person's plan.

**When NOT to use:**
- You need a *congregation-wide* pathway with membership and leadership gates — use
  `domain-biblical-studies/church-staff-ministry-ops/biblical_churchstaff_discipleship_pathway_design.md`.
- You need a *single new believer's* first-steps path designed by a ministry teacher — use
  `domain-biblical-studies/ministry-contexts/biblical_ministry_new_believer_discipleship_path.md`.
- You need one person's next-season plan — use `../learner-pathways/discipleship_personal_growth_plan.md`.
- You need to sequence modules you have already defined — use
  `discipleship_module_scope_and_sequence.md`.
- You need to build the actual teaching session — use `../session-and-lesson/discipleship_lesson_builder.md`.

**Audience:** Discipleship pastors, ministry leaders, program designers, and experienced disciplers
building a reusable pathway for others to walk.

## Inputs / Context

**Required:**

1. **Who this is for.** The starting point of the intended participant: brand-new believer, returning
   after years away, long-time attender who has never been discipled, or a believer moving toward
   discipling others. Name the *typical* participant, not an idealized one.
2. **Scope and duration.** How long the full pathway runs (e.g. 12 months), and the realistic
   participant time budget per week — including whether that time is solitary, with a mentor, or in a
   group.
3. **Delivery context.** One-to-one with a mentor, triad or small group, self-paced with check-ins, or
   a mix. Include the channel: in person, video, asynchronous, or hybrid.
4. **Non-negotiables.** Anything the sponsoring church, ministry, or platform requires the curriculum
   to include or exclude — stated plainly so it can be labelled as a commitment rather than presented
   as a universal.

**Optional:**

5. **Declared tradition (optional).** If supplied, the curriculum may foreground that stream's
   practices, vocabulary, and catechetical resources — but contested specifics stay neutral and
   alternatives remain visible. With no declaration, the default is an ecumenical, multi-view shape.
6. **Existing material.** Any curriculum, book, or study already in use that the design should
   incorporate or replace. Supply it as text or a description; do not expect it to be known.

**If any required input is missing:** Ask clarifying questions before proceeding. In particular, do not
guess the participant's starting point — a curriculum aimed at the wrong starting point is worse than
no curriculum, because it teaches people that discipleship is not for them.

## Constraints

### Must

- Anchor every stage in **observable practice and understanding**, described in plain language a
  participant would recognize in themselves.
- Reference Scripture **by address only** (book, chapter, verse). State which translation the user
  should verify against. Where a passage is central to a module, say why it was chosen.
- Sequence modules so that each depends only on what has actually been covered before it, and state
  the dependency explicitly.
- Build in **deliberate revisits** of foundational material at greater depth, rather than treating
  foundations as completed and abandoned.
- Give the pathway **multiple honest entry points** — a person who joins at month seven with prior
  background must have a defined way in.
- Give the pathway a **defined exit** that is a beginning, not a graduation: what the participant is
  equipped to do next, including whether they are being prepared to disciple someone else.
- Size the plan to the **stated time budget**, then state what was cut to make it fit.
- Name the **failure modes** of this specific design and what would signal each one is occurring.

### Must Not

- Quote Scripture text from memory or assert verse wording. Addresses only; the user verifies.
- Invent citations, cross-references, authors, councils, dates, discipleship research, spiritual-maturity
  assessment instruments, or growth statistics.
- Emit any **numeric score, percentage, index, tier, or level that purports to measure a person's
  spiritual maturity or walk with God.** Stage names describe where someone is working, never how good
  a Christian they are.
- Use a stage gate to rank participants against each other, to withhold belonging or community, or to
  create a visible hierarchy among them.
- Build in guilt, fear, shame, urgency, or manufactured deadlines as motivators.
- Present a contested practice — baptism as a stage gate, confirmation, catechesis, a specific
  accountability structure, a particular view of spiritual gifts — as the biblical model.
- Name a specific counselor, hotline, agency, or service. Write `[VERIFY: identify the correct service
  from an official source]` instead.
- Recommend named published resources as though their content, quality, or availability were known.
  Describe the *kind* of resource needed and mark it `[VERIFY]`.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)

- **Must:** build the pathway on the shared core (who Jesus is, the gospel, Scripture, prayer, the
  church, love of neighbour) as common ground; where a module touches a contested practice, note that
  traditions differ, attribute positions to identifiable streams, and let a declared tradition
  foreground its own approach while alternatives stay visible.
- **Must Not:** endorse one tradition's distinctives as correct, bury genuine disagreement in a single
  smoothed-over module, or imply there is one valid shape of Christian growth.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Fix the starting point and the horizon

Restate who the participant is and what they can realistically do in a week. Name the honest end
horizon: what someone who completes this pathway is able to understand, practice, and do. Resist an
aspirational horizon — a pathway that ends in "mature disciple" without a description is not designed,
it is decorated.

### Step 2 — Define the stages

Propose three to five stages spanning the duration. For each, give a plain-language name, the shift it
represents, and roughly how long it takes. Stages describe **a person's current work**, not their rank.
If a declared tradition supplies stage language (catechumen, confirmand, member, and so on), use it and
say it is that tradition's framing.

### Step 3 — Define formation outcomes per stage

For each stage, write outcomes across five domains:

| Domain | What it covers |
|---|---|
| **Scripture** | What they can read, find, understand, and handle on their own |
| **Character** | What is being formed in them, described behaviourally |
| **Practice** | What they actually do — prayer, Scripture intake, giving, service, rest |
| **Community** | How they are connected, known, and accountable to real people |
| **Mission** | How they love and serve those outside the community, and speak of their faith |

Every outcome is observable and described in the participant's own terms. A domain that has no honest
outcome at a given stage gets "not yet in view" — do not manufacture one for symmetry.

### Step 4 — Build the modules

Populate each stage with modules. For each module give: the title, the question it answers, the
Scripture addresses it works from, the outcomes it serves, the practice it introduces or deepens, and
its length in sessions. Route the exegesis for each passage to
`domain-biblical-studies/exegesis-interpretation/` rather than attempting it here.

Flag any module that touches a contested practice with the specific disagreement and the streams that
hold each position.

### Step 5 — Specify evidence of growth

For each stage, describe what growth **looks like** — the observable practices, the changed patterns,
the things a participant or a mentor would notice. Write these as descriptions a person could
recognize, never as a checklist to be completed or a rubric to be scored. State explicitly what this
evidence **cannot** tell you: it does not measure the state of someone's heart, and a person can meet
every description and still be struggling badly.

### Step 6 — Set pace, entry, and exit

Lay out the calendar against the stated time budget. State what was cut to fit and what would be added
first if more time became available. Define every entry point (who joins where, and what they need to
have covered) and the exit (what the participant is equipped for next, including whether they are being
prepared to disciple someone else).

### Step 7 — Design the plateau and the off-ramp

Say what happens when someone stalls, disengages, or moves slower than the pathway assumes — and make
clear that a slower pace is a variation, not a failure. Define a dignified way to pause or leave
without losing belonging. Route the specific causes of stalling to
`../learner-pathways/discipleship_stalled_growth_diagnostic.md`.

### Step 8 — Stress-test and state failure modes

Name the three ways this specific design is most likely to fail, the early signal of each, and the
adjustment. Then check the whole design against the four standing risks:

- **Knowledge-only drift** — modules teach content but form no practice.
- **Performance drift** — the pathway rewards visible compliance over honesty.
- **Dependence drift** — the participant is formed around the mentor rather than Christ and the church.
- **Tradition smuggling** — a contested distinctive has entered as an unmarked assumption.

- [ ] No numeric score, level, percentage, or index measures anyone's spiritual maturity.
- [ ] Every module's prerequisites are covered earlier in the sequence, and the dependency is stated.
- [ ] Every contested practice is flagged with the disagreement and attributed to streams, not resolved.
- [ ] The plan fits the stated time budget, and what was cut is named.
- [ ] No named counselor, hotline, agency, service, or published resource appears unmarked by `[VERIFY]`.

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Treat covering a topic as forming a person. A module on prayer that produces no praying has failed
  regardless of how well it was taught.
- Design the pathway for the participant you wish you had — the motivated, unhurried, biblically
  literate one. Most people arrive tired and inconsistent.
- Let stage names become identity labels. "He's still a Stage 1" is the exact harm this design guards
  against.
- Present your own tradition's ordering of topics as the natural or obvious one. The order you learned
  it in is a tradition, not a given.
- Fill all five outcome domains at every stage because the table has rows. Mission outcomes at week
  three are usually manufactured.
- Recommend a specific book, curriculum, or app as though you knew its current content or quality.
- Absorb a participant's crisis into the curriculum as a "module on suffering." Crisis routes out.

✅ **DO:**
- Pair every unit of content with a practice the participant will actually attempt before the next
  meeting, and make that practice small enough to succeed at.
- Design for the honest median participant, then note how the pathway flexes for someone with more or
  less capacity.
- Write stage descriptions as *what someone is working on now*, so the language stays usable when a
  person moves backward.
- Mark contested practices in the module table itself, so the person delivering the curriculum sees
  the flag at the moment they need it.
- Leave a domain's outcome as "not yet in view" when that is the truthful answer.
- Describe the *kind* of resource needed — "a plain-language overview of the whole biblical narrative"
  — and mark it `[VERIFY]` for the user to source.
- State plainly what the evidence of growth cannot see, every time you present it.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-01 (Clear Objective Statement):** the objective fixes the deliverable as a complete pathway
  blueprint, distinguishing it from the single-person plan and the church-wide pathway prompts that
  sit either side of it.
- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** the eight steps run start-point → stages → outcomes
  → modules → evidence → pace → plateau → stress-test, so that outcomes are fixed before modules are
  chosen and the curriculum cannot become a reading list with outcomes retrofitted.
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework):** the five formation domains (Scripture, character,
  practice, community, mission) force the design across axes that a single-axis curriculum — almost
  always knowledge — silently drops.
- **ED-01 (Iterative Scaffolding):** stages build on prior stages with stated dependencies, and
  foundational material is deliberately revisited at depth rather than marked complete.
- **QA-05 (Citation Requirements):** Scripture appears by address with a named translation to verify
  against, and every resource, service, and referral route is marked `[VERIFY]` — the domain's
  anti-fabrication discipline applied at its highest-risk surface.
