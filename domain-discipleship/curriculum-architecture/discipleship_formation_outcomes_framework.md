# Formation Outcomes Framework

**Objective:** Define what growth actually looks like at each stage of a discipleship pathway — across
Scripture, character, practice, community, and mission — as observable, recognizable descriptions that
guide design and conversation, while refusing to convert spiritual formation into a score, a level, or
a rank.

> **Boundary guardrail.** These outcomes describe patterns, not people's standing before God. They are
> not a diagnostic instrument, a screening tool, or a basis for judging anyone's salvation, sincerity,
> or worth. Where a participant's difficulty is clinical — depression, trauma, addiction, compulsion —
> that is not a formation outcome to be worked on, and it routes to licensed professionals and
> `domain-psychology/`.

**When to use:** Before designing or auditing a discipleship curriculum, when you need to say what you
are forming people toward with enough precision that modules can be built against it.

**When NOT to use:**
- You want the whole curriculum, not just its outcomes — use `discipleship_curriculum_architecture.md`
  (its Step 3 covers outcomes at lower resolution).
- You want one person to reflect on where they are — use
  `../learner-pathways/discipleship_growth_self_assessment.md`.
- You want academic or program-level learning outcomes in a secular frame — use
  `domain-education-teaching/program-outcomes-assessment/teaching_program_outcomes_framework.md`.

**Audience:** Curriculum designers, discipleship pastors, and program leads who need a shared
definition of growth before content decisions get made.

---

## Inputs / Context

**Required:**

1. **The pathway this serves.** Its stages (or the intent to define them here), duration, and intended
   participant starting point.
2. **Delivery context.** One-to-one, group, or self-paced — because an outcome like "known by others"
   means something different in each.
3. **What the outcomes are for.** Curriculum design, mentor conversation, participant self-reflection,
   or program review. This governs how the outcomes are worded and how much they must not resemble a
   test.

**Optional:**

4. **Declared tradition (optional).** If supplied, outcomes may foreground that stream's formation
   vocabulary and emphases (sacramental participation, personal devotions, charismatic experience,
   catechetical knowledge) while noting that other traditions weight the domains differently.
5. **Existing outcome language.** Any framework, membership covenant, or maturity description already
   in use that this should extend or replace. Supply the text.

**If any required input is missing:** Ask clarifying questions before proceeding. Outcomes written
without a known starting point default to describing a mature adult believer at every stage, which
makes the framework useless and discouraging.

---

## Constraints

### Must

- Write every outcome as something **observable or self-recognizable**, in plain language the
  participant would use about themselves.
- Cover all five domains — Scripture, character, practice, community, mission — and mark a domain
  **"not yet in view"** at a stage where no honest outcome exists, rather than manufacturing one.
- State, for every stage, **what the evidence cannot see**.
- Distinguish **capacity** ("can find a passage on their own") from **pattern** ("reads most weeks")
  from **disposition** ("turns to Scripture when troubled"), and label which each outcome is.
- Note where an outcome is **strongly affected by circumstance** — capacity, health, life stage,
  access, caregiving load — so it is never read as a character verdict.
- Where traditions weight a domain differently, say so and attribute the weighting to streams.

### Must Not

- Attach any **number, score, percentage, level, tier, band, or rating** to any outcome, or arrange
  outcomes so that a total could be computed.
- Produce anything that functions as an assessment instrument, screening tool, or eligibility test for
  belonging, membership, service, or leadership.
- Invent discipleship research, validated maturity instruments, developmental models, or statistics,
  or attribute a framework to a named author or institution.
- Quote Scripture text from memory; reference by address only where a passage informs an outcome.
- Write outcomes that describe internal states only God can see ("loves God more deeply") as though
  they were observable.
- Frame any outcome so that failing to meet it implies deficient faith, insufficient effort, or a
  spiritual problem.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)

- **Must:** build outcomes on the shared core across traditions; where a stream emphasizes a domain
  distinctively — sacramental practice, personal quiet time, charismatic gifting, catechetical
  knowledge, social justice engagement — name it as that stream's emphasis and keep the others visible.
- **Must Not:** encode one tradition's formation theology as the neutral baseline, or treat a
  tradition-specific practice as a universal marker of maturity.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Fix the frame

Restate the pathway, its stages, the participant starting point, and what the outcomes are for. If
stages are not yet defined, propose three to five and say they are provisional pending
`discipleship_curriculum_architecture.md`.

### Step 2 — Define each domain for this context

Write a one-paragraph working definition of each of the five domains as it applies to *this* pathway
and *this* participant. A definition of "community" for a fully-remote program is not the same as for a
congregation-based one; say what it means here.

### Step 3 — Draft outcomes stage by stage

For each stage and domain, write one to three outcomes. Label each as **capacity**, **pattern**, or
**disposition**. Write each in the participant's own plausible words. Mark any domain with no honest
outcome as "not yet in view" and say why.

### Step 4 — Add the recognition descriptions

For each outcome, add what it looks like when present and what its absence looks like — without moral
framing. "Has not yet found a rhythm that survives a busy week" is a description; "lacks discipline" is
a verdict.

### Step 5 — Add the circumstance notes

Flag every outcome materially affected by circumstance rather than formation: shift work, caregiving,
disability, chronic illness, isolation, poverty, unsafe home, limited literacy, no transport, no
private space. State how the outcome should be read differently in those conditions.

### Step 6 — Write the "what this cannot see" statement

For each stage, state plainly what these outcomes do not and cannot tell anyone. This is not a
disclaimer; it is the part that keeps the framework honest in use.

### Step 7 — Run the misuse test

Ask directly: how would this framework be misused? Name at least three specific misuses — ranking
participants, gating belonging, mentor self-justification, participant self-condemnation, leadership
selection — and add the wording or structural change that makes each harder.

## Verification

- [ ] No outcome carries a number, score, level, band, or rating, and no total can be computed.
- [ ] Every outcome is labelled capacity, pattern, or disposition.
- [ ] At least one domain at an early stage is honestly marked "not yet in view."
- [ ] Every stage has a "what these outcomes cannot see" statement.
- [ ] Absence descriptions are behavioural, not moral verdicts.
- [ ] The misuse register names at least three specific misuses with a concrete mitigation each.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Write outcomes that are really doctrinal assent tests ("affirms the authority of Scripture") and call
  them formation.
- Describe internal states as observable. Nobody can see whether someone loves God more than they did
  last year.
- Let "not yet" language become "behind." A pathway is not a race and stages are not a ranking.
- Assume the participant has private space, a quiet hour, transport, literacy, or a safe home. Many do
  not, and outcomes that assume it convert circumstance into spiritual failure.
- Produce a tidy grid where every domain is populated at every stage. Tidiness here is a symptom of
  invention.
- Cite a maturity model, developmental stage theory, or discipleship study by name.

✅ **DO:**
- Write outcomes a participant could read about themselves without flinching, and recognize as fair.
- Separate what someone *can* do from what they *habitually* do from what they *instinctively* do —
  the three grow at different rates and conflating them produces bad design.
- Say "not yet in view" and give the reason. An early-stage mission outcome usually is not real.
- Attach the circumstance note directly to the outcome, so whoever uses it sees the caveat at the
  moment of judgement.
- Treat the misuse register as design input: if a misuse is easy, change the framework, don't just warn
  about it.
- State the limits of the evidence every single time it is presented, not once in a preamble.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-01 (Clear Objective Statement):** the objective names both what the framework produces and the
  thing it explicitly refuses to produce, so the anti-scoring constraint is load-bearing rather than
  advisory.
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework):** five formation domains crossed with three outcome
  types (capacity, pattern, disposition) prevent the single-axis knowledge framework that most
  discipleship outcome sets collapse into.
- **CM-02 (Constraint Specification):** the Must Not block encodes the domain's central guard — no
  score, no instrument, no eligibility test — as a testable constraint rather than a tone preference.
- **OC-03 (Markdown Table Specification):** the stage tables put outcome, type, presence, and absence
  side by side, so an outcome that is secretly a moral verdict becomes visible when its "not yet"
  column is written.
- **QA-04 (Uncertainty Acknowledgment):** the mandatory "what these outcomes cannot see" statement and
  the circumstance notes keep the framework's own limits attached to it in use.
