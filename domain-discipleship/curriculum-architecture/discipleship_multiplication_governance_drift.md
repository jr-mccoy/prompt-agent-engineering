# Multiplication Governance and Material Drift

**Objective:** Work out what a multiplying discipleship program is actually teaching once it is three or
four generations out — auditing a material library that has accumulated without attribution, deciding what
is genuinely core and what is legitimately local, establishing who is responsible for the answer, and
designing how a correction travels — without building a licensing regime, a franchise, or a mechanism that
makes local adaptation a matter of permission.

> **Boundary guardrail.** This governs *material and responsibility*, not people. It does not assess any
> discipler, rank any generation, or determine whether anyone is teaching faithfully — and it does not
> adjudicate doctrine. Where material has drifted into a genuine theological question, that is described
> and routed to `domain-biblical-studies/theology-research/`, which handles multi-view work properly, and
> a creed-level contradiction is handled at
> `domain-biblical-studies/group-leader-facilitation/biblical_groupleader_heretical_claim_response.md`
> rather than here. Where material has drifted into something harmful — coercive practice, pressure,
> unsafe teaching about abuse, suffering, or medical care — that is a safeguarding matter and stops being
> a curriculum question `[VERIFY: identify the correct authority from an official source]`.

**When to use:** When a program has reproduced past the point where the people running it know everyone
running it — typically the third generation — and nobody can say with confidence what is being taught, who
wrote it, or who would notice if it changed.

**When NOT to use:**
- You are designing for reproduction one generation out — use `discipleship_multiplication_design.md`,
  which sets the core/local/incidental split and the no-cost off-ramp this prompt governs downstream.
- You are auditing one curriculum for balance — use `discipleship_curriculum_balance_audit.md`.
- You are evaluating a specific published resource — use `discipleship_material_evaluation.md`.
- The concern is that the program has become controlling — use
  `../program-operations/discipleship_program_control_drift_audit.md`; drift in material and drift toward
  coercion are different audits with different objects.
- You want a church's teaching scope and sequence — use
  `domain-biblical-studies/church-staff-ministry-ops/biblical_churchstaff_curriculum_scope_sequence.md`.

**Audience:** Discipleship pastors, network leads, and whoever inherits a program that has multiplied
further than its documentation.

## Inputs / Context

**Required:**

1. **What is actually in use, gathered rather than assumed.** The material each generation is running,
   however collected — sent, photographed, recalled. What the centre thinks is in use is not an input;
   what is in use is.
2. **Generational structure.** How many generations, roughly how many groups or pairs at each, and who at
   each generation is in contact with whom. Most programs discover the map is thinner than believed.
3. **What the original core was.** The non-negotiables as first set, if they were ever written. Where they
   were not, that is the first finding, because nothing can be said to have drifted from an unstated
   standard.
4. **Who currently believes they are responsible.** Named. In multiplied programs this is frequently
   nobody, or several people who each assume it is someone else.

**Optional:**

5. **Declared tradition (optional).** May supply an existing confessional standard, catechism, or
   doctrinal statement that already defines the core, and may locate authority in an overseer, presbytery,
   or bishop rather than in the program — which changes both what the core is and who decides. Where such a
   standard exists, use it rather than composing a local one. Where none is declared, the core stays
   whatever the program itself stated, and the audit says so.
6. **What has already been noticed.** A phrase, an emphasis, a practice someone was surprised by.
7. **Whether anything travelled through translation.** Drift and translation compound, and the two are
   frequently mistaken for each other.

**If any required input is missing:** Ask for the material as it is actually being used. If the original
core was never written down, stop and write it first — an audit against an unstated standard produces a
list of differences from the centre's current preferences, which is a different and much less useful thing.

## Constraints

### Must

- Audit **what is in use**, gathered from the generations, rather than what the library is believed to
  contain.
- Sort every element into **core, legitimately local, or drift**, and require the core to have been stated
  originally rather than reconstructed now to fit what the centre currently prefers.
- Treat **local adaptation as expected and good** by default, and require a specific reason for anything
  classified as drift rather than as difference.
- Give each piece of material an **attribution status** — authored, adapted, inherited, unknown — and treat
  unknown as an ordinary finding rather than a failure.
- Name **who is responsible** for each part of the answer going forward, as a role with a named holder, and
  record where the answer is currently nobody.
- Design **how a correction travels**: through whom, in what form, and how it is distinguished from a
  preference — including what happens when a generation declines it.
- Preserve the **no-cost off-ramp** the multiplication design established: a group may decline to multiply,
  and now also may decline an adaptation, without losing standing.
- Separate **material drift from theological question from safety concern**, and route the second and third
  out rather than resolving them here.
- State the **audit's blind spot**: what is taught in a room is not what is written on the page, and no
  material audit sees the difference.

### Must Not

- Build a licensing, certification, approval, or franchise mechanism, or make continued participation
  conditional on using centrally approved material.
- Require permission for local adaptation, or treat contextualization, translation, or cultural adaptation
  as drift by default.
- Adjudicate any contested theological question, or rule on which of two defensible readings a generation
  should hold.
- Assess, rank, or score any discipler, group, or generation, or produce a fidelity, alignment, or
  faithfulness rating of any kind.
- Recommend removing, replacing, or disciplining anyone, or design a consequence for a generation that
  declines a correction.
- Treat distance from the centre as itself a problem, or the centre's current practice as the standard by
  which earlier material is judged.
- Assess the quality of a translation or claim what a word means in a language the model cannot read —
  that constraint belongs to `../cross-cultural/discipleship_translated_material_pitfalls.md` and holds
  here too.
- Name any publisher, author, organization, or resource, or state its reception, sales, or reputation.
- State any copyright, licensing, or legal requirement about the material — `[VERIFY]` and route to
  `domain-legal/ip/`.
- Quote Scripture text from memory, or use any passage about unity, sound doctrine, or guarding a deposit
  to justify centralized control.

### Tradition-neutral stance (Must / Must Not)

- **Must:** note where traditions differ on who defines the core — a confession, a catechism, a bishop, a
  presbytery, an association, or the local congregation — and on how much local variation is expected; and
  where a tradition already has a confessional standard, treat it as the definition of core rather than
  composing a parallel one.
- **Must Not:** rule on which tradition's locus of authority is correct, treat congregational independence
  as drift, or let any tradition's language about guarding sound doctrine be used to convert this
  governance design into a control mechanism over people.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Gather what is actually running

Collect the material in use at each generation, by asking rather than by checking a repository. Note what
could not be gathered and from where — the gaps are usually where the interesting drift is, and they are
information about the map rather than about the people.

### Step 2 — Recover the original core, or write it now

Set out what was stated as non-negotiable at the start. If nothing was, stop and write it, and say plainly
in the output that the audit runs against a core defined now — because an audit that quietly uses the
centre's current preferences as the baseline will find drift everywhere and will be right about none of it.

### Step 3 — Sort, with local adaptation as the default reading

Place each element in core, legitimately local, or drift. Anything that differs is local unless a specific
reason places it in drift: it contradicts a stated core commitment, it has lost something the core
required, or it has acquired a practice the program would not have chosen. Difference is not drift, and
treating it as such is how a multiplying program stops multiplying.

### Step 4 — Attribute what can be attributed

Mark each piece authored, adapted, inherited, or unknown, with whatever provenance exists. Unknown is
common by generation three and is not a fault; recording it is what lets a later question be answered at
all.

### Step 5 — Assign responsibility, including where there is none

For each part — the core, the shared material, each generation's local material — name a role and a person.
Where the honest answer is that nobody currently holds it, write that, because an unowned library is the
condition every other finding here follows from.

### Step 6 — Design how a correction travels

Say who raises something, to whom, in what form, and how a correction is distinguished from a preference —
the test being whether it touches a stated core commitment. Then say what happens when a generation
declines: which corrections are genuinely non-negotiable because they concern safety or a stated core, and
which are advice that may be refused without consequence. A governance design with no permitted refusal is
a control mechanism.

### Step 7 — Route what is not a material question, then check

Separate out the theological questions and send them to multi-view work, and the safety concerns and send
them to safeguarding `[VERIFY]`. Then check: was the core stated originally or reconstructed? Is anything
classified as drift merely different? Does any part of this require permission to adapt? Is there a
consequence anywhere for a generation that declines?

## Verification

- [ ] The audit runs against a core that was stated originally, or says explicitly that the core is being
      defined now.
- [ ] Every element is sorted, with local adaptation as the default reading and drift requiring a specific
      stated reason.
- [ ] Each material item carries an attribution status, with unknown recorded rather than resolved.
- [ ] Responsibility is assigned by role and person, including where the honest answer is nobody.
- [ ] Declining a non-safety correction carries no consequence to standing or participation.
- [ ] No approval mechanism, licence, fidelity score, or assessment of any person or generation appears.

## False-Positive Prevention

❌ **DON'T:**
- Audit against what the centre does now. Three generations of the centre's own evolution will read as
  everyone else's drift, and the finding will be confidently wrong in every direction.
- Treat difference as drift. Contextualization is what multiplication is for, and a program that corrects
  every local variation will stop reproducing within a generation.
- Build an approval process. It is the intuitive response to an unattributable library, it converts a
  movement into a franchise, and it never gets removed once added.
- Score fidelity. A number attached to a generation's faithfulness is both an assessment of people and a
  metric of formation, which this domain does not produce under any name.
- Resolve the theology here. When drift turns out to be a genuine contested question, this is the wrong
  instrument, and answering it anyway is how a governance audit becomes a doctrinal ruling.
- Treat unknown attribution as a failure. By generation three it is the normal condition, and recording it
  honestly is more useful than reconstructing a provenance nobody can verify.
- Make a correction unrefusable when it is a preference. The distinction between "this contradicts what we
  said we would keep" and "we would have done it differently" is the whole design.

✅ **DO:**
- Gather what is actually in use, and record what you could not reach.
- Write the core down now if it was never written, and say in the output that this is what happened.
- Let local be the default and make drift argue for itself with a specific reason.
- Name a person for each part, and write "nobody" where that is true.
- Distinguish non-negotiable corrections — safety and stated core — from advice that can be declined at no
  cost, and put both in writing.
- State the blind spot: the page is not the room, and no audit of material sees what is actually said.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** the core is recovered or written before anything is
  sorted, which is the ordering that stops the centre's current practice from silently becoming the
  standard.
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework):** what is in use, attribution, responsibility, and
  correction routes are worked as separate axes, so a well-documented library with no owner cannot pass as
  governed.
- **CM-02 (Constraint Specification):** the Must Not block prohibits the intuitive response to this
  situation — approval, licensing, fidelity scoring — which is where a multiplication design usually dies.
- **OC-03 (Markdown Table Specification):** the library table forces a classification and, where the answer
  is drift, a specific reason on the same row, which is what prevents difference from being logged as
  drift by default.
- **QA-01 (Self-Verification):** the closing check tests whether the baseline was genuinely original and
  whether any refusal carries a consequence — the two ways this design turns into control.
