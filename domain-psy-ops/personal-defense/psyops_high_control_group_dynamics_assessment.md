# High-Control Group Dynamics Assessment

**Objective:** Assess a group — religious, political, commercial, therapeutic, wellness, multi-level marketing, workplace, or online — against documented **structural** criteria for high-control dynamics, with the group's actual beliefs held deliberately out of scope. The questions are: **what does it cost to leave**, **who controls what members can know**, **is dissent survivable**, **is the leadership accountable to anything**, and **is there language that ends thought rather than expressing it**.

Keeping beliefs out of scope is not neutrality theater — it is what makes the assessment work. Unusual, intense, demanding, and minority beliefs are held by countless groups that are not controlling, and assessing beliefs instead of structure produces an instrument that flags every unfamiliar religion and misses the abusive company, the exploitative therapy practice, and the controlling wellness community. A group can hold entirely mainstream beliefs and be highly controlling. A group can hold beliefs you find bizarre and leave members completely free.

The output is a structural description the user can act on themselves. It does not tell anyone what to do about their community, their faith, or their job.

> **Safety.** If leaving this group could put you at risk of losing housing, income, immigration status, custody, or your entire support network, that is exactly the situation where outside help matters most. Speak to someone outside the group — a doctor, a lawyer, a support organization for former members. **Look up any organization or number from an official source rather than relying on details stated from memory by an AI.** If you are in immediate danger, contact emergency services.

**When to use:**
- You are wondering whether a group you are part of is healthy, and cannot get perspective from inside.
- Someone close to you has joined something and you want to assess it fairly rather than react to the beliefs.
- You are considering leaving and want to understand what you are facing.
- You are evaluating an organization — an employer, a training program, a therapeutic community — before committing.

**When NOT to use:**
- The controlling dynamic is one person in a relationship — use `psyops_coercive_control_pattern_recognition.md`.
- You are worried specifically about radicalization toward harm — use `psyops_concern_for_someone_radicalizing.md`.
- You want to evaluate whether the group's beliefs are true. That is a separate question and this prompt does not touch it.

**Audience:** Current or former members, concerned family and friends, and people evaluating an organization before joining.

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **The group.** What it is and what it does — described functionally, not doctrinally.
2. **Your position.** Member, prospective member, former member, or concerned outsider. This changes what you can see.
3. **Time and money.** What is expected, and what happens if you give less.
4. **Leaving.** What you know or have been told about what happens to people who leave, and what you would lose.
5. **Outside relationships.** Whether members keep meaningful ties outside the group, and whether that is encouraged, tolerated, or discouraged.
6. **Handling of criticism.** What happens when someone raises a concern internally, and how external criticism is characterized.
7. **Leadership.** Who decides, how they are chosen, whether anything constrains them, and whether they are subject to the same rules.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Assess **structure only**. Beliefs, doctrines, practices, and rituals stay out of scope regardless of how unusual they are.
- Centre the **exit cost**, which is the most diagnostic single dimension: what a member loses by leaving, across relationships, money, housing, status, identity, and afterlife or ideological stakes.
- Assess **information control**: whether members can access outside information about the group, and how external criticism is framed.
- Assess whether **dissent is survivable** — what happens to members who disagree openly and remain.
- Assess **leadership accountability**: whether anyone can correct or remove leadership, and whether leaders live under the rules they set.
- Note **thought-terminating language**: phrases that close a line of inquiry rather than answer it.
- Report **healthy structural features** where they exist, so the assessment is a description rather than an indictment.
- Note **degree and direction** — most groups sit on a spectrum and are moving along it.

### Must Not
- Assess or characterize the group's beliefs, or use "cult" as a finding. The word is a conclusion that ends thinking and it is applied almost entirely to unfamiliar minority religions.
- Tell the user to leave or stay.
- Treat commitment, intensity, sacrifice, distinctive practice, or communal living as findings. Monastic orders, activist movements, elite sports programs, and startups all demand these.
- Fabricate anything about the group's history, finances, leadership, or practices.
- Name individual members or leaders as abusers.
- Suggest confrontation, intervention, or "deprogramming" — approaches which mainstream guidance regards as harmful and which reliably drive people further in.
- State any support organization or number as verified from memory.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Restate the group functionally
Describe what it does and asks of members without any doctrinal content. If the description cannot be written without the beliefs, that itself is worth noticing.

### Step 2 — Map the exit cost first
What would a member lose by leaving: relationships, family contact, housing, employment, income, professional standing, community, identity, and any ideological or spiritual consequence they have been taught to expect. High exit cost is the core structural finding, because it makes all other consent conditional.

### Step 3 — Assess information control
Can members read criticism of the group? How is outside information characterized? Is there information available only at higher levels, and are members told things they will "understand later"?

### Step 4 — Test whether dissent is survivable
What happens to a member who disagrees openly and stays? Not one who leaves — one who stays and disagrees. If no such person exists, that absence is the finding.

### Step 5 — Assess leadership accountability
Who selects leadership, what could remove them, who can tell them no, and do the rules apply to them? Financial transparency belongs here.

### Step 6 — Listen for thought-terminating language
Phrases used to close inquiry: appeals that reframe doubt as a personal failing, characterizations of questioners as spiritually or morally deficient, or stock responses that answer any objection identically.

### Step 7 — Record healthy features
Genuine consent, real external ties, tolerated disagreement, transparent finances, low exit cost, accountable leadership. Most groups have some of these; recording them is what makes the assessment honest.

### Step 8 — Place on the spectrum and pick a next step
Describe degree and direction of travel rather than a category. Then a next step the user chooses — often simply talking to someone outside the group.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **Beliefs assessed instead of structure.** The central failure. It flags unfamiliar religions and misses controlling employers, MLMs, and therapy practices with entirely ordinary beliefs.
2. **Intensity read as control.** Demanding commitment, sacrifice, and distinctive practice are features of monasteries, activist movements, startups, and elite sport. Cost of exit is the discriminator, not cost of membership.
3. **"Cult" as a finding.** The word ends analysis, carries no agreed definition, and is applied overwhelmingly to minority faiths. Describe structure instead.
4. **Outsider view mistaken for full view.** A concerned family member sees a fraction of the group and none of the member's experience. State the vantage point's limits.
5. **Ignoring healthy features.** Producing a one-sided indictment. If nothing healthy is recorded, the assessment is probably measuring unfamiliarity.
6. **Intervention suggested.** Confrontation and deprogramming are widely regarded as harmful and reliably increase commitment.
7. **Binary categorization.** Groups sit on a spectrum and move along it. Direction of travel is more useful than a label.
8. **Individual naming.** Attaching structural findings to named leaders or members as accusations.

## Verification

- [ ] The group's beliefs are not assessed anywhere, and the output says so explicitly.
- [ ] Exit cost is assessed first and treated as the core dimension.
- [ ] Commitment, intensity, and sacrifice are not treated as findings on their own.
- [ ] The word "cult" is not used as a conclusion.
- [ ] The dissent test asks about members who disagree *and stay*.
- [ ] Healthy structural features are recorded where they exist.
- [ ] The assessment places the group on a spectrum with a direction, not in a category.
- [ ] No instruction to leave or stay, and no intervention, confrontation, or deprogramming is suggested.
- [ ] No individual is named as an abuser, and nothing about the group was fabricated.
- [ ] The vantage point's limits are stated, and any support resource is routed to official verification.
