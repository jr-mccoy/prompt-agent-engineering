# Manipulation Recognition (Personal)

**Objective:** Work out whether something happening in your life involves manipulation, by separating three things that get fused when you are inside a situation: **what was actually said and done**, **the interpretation you were offered**, and **what you concluded about yourself as a result**. That third layer is where manipulation does its real damage, because a persistent interpretation eventually stops feeling like someone's claim and starts feeling like a fact about you.

This prompt organizes your own observations. It does not diagnose anyone, it does not tell you whether to stay or leave, and it does not decide for you. It builds a record you can look at outside the moment — which matters because the defining feature of these situations is that your own judgment feels unreliable, and the standard advice to "trust your gut" is useless when your gut is exactly what is in dispute. Written observations are more stable than recollection, and they are what a therapist, lawyer, or trusted friend can actually work with.

It also takes seriously that **most difficult relationships are not manipulative**. Ordinary conflict, poor communication, mismatched needs, stress, and two people who both feel unheard produce most of what hurts. Concluding manipulation where there is none damages relationships that could be repaired and is unfair to someone who is struggling rather than scheming.

> **Safety.** If you are afraid of someone, if someone is controlling your movement, money, communication, or access to other people, or if you are being threatened, this goes beyond what any prompt can help with. Contact a domestic violence or victim support service in your country and speak to someone trained. **Look up the current number for your country rather than relying on a number stated from memory by any AI, including this one — verify it from an official source.** If you are in immediate danger, contact emergency services. If you are having thoughts of harming yourself, contact a crisis line in your country now. This prompt organizes observations; it is not therapy, not legal advice, and not a safety plan.

**When to use:**
- You keep replaying interactions and cannot work out whether your reaction is proportionate.
- You notice you apologize a lot, or explain yourself a lot, and are not sure why.
- Someone tells you that events you remember did not happen that way, often enough that you have stopped trusting your memory.
- You want to organize what has actually happened before talking to a therapist, friend, or lawyer.

**When NOT to use:**
- There is fear, control, or a safety concern — go to `psyops_coercive_control_pattern_recognition.md`, and to a professional.
- You want to evaluate a relationship's overall health rather than a manipulation question — use `domain-personal-development/prompts/relationships/relationships_relationship_audit.md`.
- You are analyzing a piece of media rather than a relationship — use `../technique-analysis/psyops_emotional_manipulation_decoder.md`.
- You are in crisis. This is not the tool; please reach out to a person.

**Audience:** Individuals trying to make sense of a specific relationship or interaction. No clinical or analytic background assumed.

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **Specific incidents.** Two to five concrete occasions, with what was said and done as close to verbatim as you can manage. Specificity is what makes this work; general impressions cannot be assessed.
2. **The interpretation offered.** What you were told the incident meant — about your intentions, your memory, your reasonableness, or your character.
3. **What you concluded about yourself.** What you now believe about yourself that you did not believe before.
4. **The relationship.** Who this is, how long, and what you each want from it.
5. **Corroboration available.** Messages, emails, or other people who were present — noting that its absence proves nothing.
6. **What else is going on.** Illness, grief, job loss, addiction, or acute stress in either of you. This is context, not an excuse, and it changes what is likely.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Keep **observation, interpretation, and self-conclusion in three separate columns** for every incident. This separation is the entire method.
- Work from **specific incidents**, and decline to assess general impressions without them.
- Offer the **ordinary-conflict reading** for every incident — the version where this is two people handling something badly.
- Distinguish a **pattern** from a bad week. Frequency, consistency, and direction over time are what distinguish them.
- Note **where the user's own behavior belongs in the record**, without treating that as cancelling anything.
- Route to **qualified support** for anything involving fear, control, safety, or acute distress, and repeat the resource-verification instruction.
- State plainly that this produces a **record, not a verdict**, and that the user decides what it means.

### Must Not
- Diagnose the other person, or apply clinical labels — narcissist, sociopath, borderline — to anyone. These are diagnoses, they require a clinician and an evaluation, and applied at a distance they are both unreliable and harmful.
- Tell the user to leave, stay, confront, or forgive. That is their decision and it depends on things this prompt cannot see.
- Confirm manipulation to be supportive. Validating a conclusion the evidence does not support is not kindness — it can wreck a repairable relationship or entrench an unfair belief.
- Dismiss the user's perception either. Both errors are available and neither is safe.
- State any hotline number, service name, or resource from memory as though verified. Direct the user to look it up from an official source.
- Fabricate detail, fill gaps in an incident, or smooth an account into a cleaner story than the user gave.
- Treat absence of corroboration as evidence that nothing happened.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Take the incidents one at a time
For each, write only what was said and done — words, actions, sequence. No interpretation, no adjectives about intent. If you cannot recall specifics, say so; a vague incident stays vague.

### Step 2 — Add the interpretation you were given
Separately, what were you told it meant? "You're too sensitive." "That's not what happened." "You always do this." "I only did it because you..." Quote it if you can.

### Step 3 — Add what you concluded about yourself
What do you now believe about yourself as a result? This column is often the shortest and the most important.

### Step 4 — Write the ordinary-conflict reading for each
For every incident, the version where both people are tired, scared, or bad at this. Some incidents will fit this well — mark those honestly.

### Step 5 — Look for pattern rather than instance
Across the incidents: does the interpretation always run the same direction? Does your account of reality get revised toward theirs? Is your self-conclusion column growing? A pattern is directional and persistent; a bad patch is neither.

### Step 6 — Locate your own part
Where did you contribute — escalation, withdrawal, unspoken expectations, your own hard interpretations? Record it. This does not cancel anything; it makes the record accurate and therefore useful.

### Step 7 — Note corroboration and context
What could be checked against messages or witnesses. Then any illness, grief, or crisis in play — which explains without excusing, and which changes what is likely.

### Step 8 — Decide the next step, not the verdict
Choose one concrete next step: talk to a specific person, keep the log for a few more weeks, book a therapist, contact a support service, or raise one specific incident directly. Not a conclusion about the relationship — a next step.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **Ordinary conflict labeled manipulation.** Most relationship pain is not manipulation. Two people who both feel unheard produce nearly identical symptoms.
2. **Diagnosis at a distance.** Calling someone a narcissist because their behavior matches an article. Diagnosis needs a clinician; the label changes nothing you can act on and forecloses accurate thinking.
3. **Validation mistaken for help.** Confirming the user's fear to be supportive. If the evidence does not support it, saying so is the supportive act.
4. **Dismissal in the other direction.** Explaining away a real pattern as sensitivity. Both errors are live; the three-column record is what protects against each.
5. **Impression without incident.** Assessing a feeling with no specific occasion attached. Ask for incidents or decline the assessment.
6. **Single-instance conclusions.** Everyone manipulates occasionally under stress. The finding is about direction and persistence over time.
7. **Omitting the user's own part.** A record that makes one person entirely blameless is usually incomplete, and it will not survive contact with a therapist or a court.
8. **Fabricated resources.** Producing a hotline number or service name from memory. Always instruct verification from an official source.

## Output Format

```
# Observation record

## Incidents
### Incident 1 — [date, roughly]
| What was said/done (as close to verbatim as I can) | Interpretation I was given | What I concluded about myself |
|---|---|---|
| [...] | [...] | [...] |

**Ordinary-conflict reading:** [the version where this is two people handling it badly]
**Fits that reading?** [well / partly / poorly]

### Incident 2 — ...

## Pattern check
- Does the interpretation always run the same direction? [yes/no + evidence]
- Does my account of events get revised toward theirs? [yes/no + evidence]
- Is my self-conclusion column growing over time? [yes/no]
- Is this a pattern, or a hard period? [assessment + what would tell them apart]

## My own part
[Where I contributed — recorded to make this accurate, not to cancel anything]

## Context
[Illness, grief, stress, addiction on either side — explains without excusing]

## What could be checked
[Messages, emails, people present — and noting that absence of corroboration proves nothing]

## Safety check
[Any fear, control over movement/money/contact, or threats? If yes → stop here and contact a
support service; verify the current number from an official source for your country.]

## My next step (not a verdict)
[One concrete thing — a person to talk to, more logging, an appointment, one incident to raise]

## What this is
A record I made, not a conclusion about another person. What it means is mine to decide,
and I can take it to someone qualified to help me think about it.
```

## Verification

- [ ] Every incident is recorded in three separate columns: observation, interpretation given, self-conclusion.
- [ ] Incidents are specific; general impressions were not assessed as though they were evidence.
- [ ] An ordinary-conflict reading is offered for every incident, and incidents that fit it are marked honestly.
- [ ] No clinical label or diagnosis is applied to anyone.
- [ ] No instruction to stay, leave, confront, or forgive appears anywhere.
- [ ] The user's own contribution is included in the record.
- [ ] Pattern is assessed by direction and persistence, not by a single instance.
- [ ] The safety check is present, and the user is told to verify any resource from an official source rather than trusting a number stated from memory.
- [ ] Nothing was fabricated, filled in, or smoothed beyond what the user reported.
- [ ] The output ends in a next step, not a verdict about another person.
