# Documentary Treatment

**Objective:** Produce a treatment that lets a reader see the film before it
exists — who we follow, what question drives it, what we have access to, how it
will look and sound, and how it might end — while being honest that a
documentary's story is discovered, not written.

**When to Use:**
- Applying to a documentary fund, pitching a commissioner, or approaching a
  broadcaster or streamer.
- Aligning a director, producer, and editor on what the film *is* before
  shooting or before the edit.
- You have research and access and the idea still reads as a topic, not a film.

**Not this prompt if:**
- You are writing scripted fiction (feature, pilot, stage) →
  `domain-creative-writing/script-stage/writing_screenplay_framework.md`.
- The piece is prose journalism or a book → `domain-creative-writing/creative-nonfiction/writing_narrative_nonfiction_and_literary_journalism.md`.
- You only need the logline and comparable titles →
  `domain-creative-writing/publishing-career/writing_pitch_logline_and_comp_titles.md`.
- You need a shot list for a scheduled shoot →
  `domain-professional-writing/content-production/content_video_shot_list_preproduction.md`.

> **Factual contract:** a documentary treatment describes real people and
> events. This prompt shapes only what the filmmaker supplies. It never invents
> a subject, a quote, an event, a date, access, or a funding partner. Anything
> not supplied is marked `[VERIFY]` or `[ACCESS NOT YET SECURED]`.

## Inputs

1. **Subject and why now.**
2. **Characters**: who, what they want, and what access is confirmed (signed
   release, verbal yes, approached, not yet approached).
3. **Research**: documents, interviews, archive identified, with sources.
4. **Events on the calendar** that will happen whether or not you film them.
5. **Intended form**: length, series or single, observational / participatory /
   expository / essay / archival mode.
6. **Audience and destination**: fund guidelines, commissioner brief, festival.
7. **Known sensitivities**: minors, vulnerable people, legal exposure, safety.

## Method

1. **Frame the brief (CM-01).** Restate destination, length, and the reader
   of the treatment (fund panel, commissioning editor, co-producer); follow the
   destination's word limit and required headings if given.
2. **Find the central question.** One question the film is driven by and cannot
   answer in advance: "Will the last fishing family on the island sell the boat?"
   A topic ("overfishing") is not a question.
3. **Set out characters and access honestly (RT-05).** For each: role, want,
   obstacle, and access status. A treatment that implies access you do not
   have is the fastest way to lose a funder's trust.
4. **Sketch the arc with its uncertainty.** Known scheduled events, likely
   turning points, and at least two plausible endings — with what the film is
   about under each. Say which scenes are observational (will happen) and which
   are hoped for.
5. **Define structure and point of view (ST-03).** Mode, narrator or none,
   interviews on camera or voice only, time structure (chronological, braided,
   countdown), use of archive or reconstruction (labelled as such on screen).
6. **Describe visual and sound approach.** Camera relationship to subjects,
   look, recurring images, music and sound design intentions — in terms a
   reader can picture.
7. **Address ethics and duty of care (CM-02).** Consent and releases, how
   subjects will see the film before release (if promised), risk to
   participants, handling of minors or vulnerable people, and where
   reconstruction or composite would *not* be used.
8. **Self-check (QA-01)**: every claim sourced or marked; access statuses honest.

## Output Format

```
# [Working title] — Documentary treatment
Destination: [...]  Length/form: [...]  Mode: [...]

## Logline
## The question at the heart of the film
## Why now
## Characters and access
| Person | Role in film | Want / obstacle | Access status |
## Story and arc
Scheduled events · likely turns · possible endings (A / B)
## Structure and point of view
## Visual and sound approach
## Ethics and duty of care
## Research and sources
## Open items ([VERIFY] / [ACCESS NOT YET SECURED])
```

## Verification

- [ ] The central question cannot be answered before filming.
- [ ] Every character has an explicit access status.
- [ ] At least two possible endings are described.
- [ ] Every factual claim cites a supplied source or is marked `[VERIFY]`.
- [ ] Any reconstruction or archive use is described as such.
- [ ] The destination's word limit and required headings are respected.

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **A topic is not a film.** "A film about loneliness" has no character and
   no question; push for who and what's at stake.
2. **Scripted certainty is a red flag in documentary.** Writing the ending as
   if known misrepresents the form and invites steering subjects to fit it.
3. **Implied access is misrepresentation.** "We follow the CEO" when the CEO
   has not agreed must be restated as intent.
4. **Style words are not a visual approach.** "Cinematic and intimate" says
   nothing; describe camera distance, light, and what we see.
5. **Do not dramatise real people with invented interiority.** Describe
   what is observable or what they have said.
6. **Fund-speak is not impact.** Claims about social change need evidence or
   should be stated as hopes.

## Example Output

```
# The Last Boat Out — Documentary treatment
Destination: regional documentary development fund (1,200 words max)
Length/form: 80-min feature · Mode: observational, no narrator

## Logline
On an island where the fleet has shrunk to one boat, a father and daughter
spend a final season deciding whether to sell it — and with it, the family's
place on the water.

## The question
Will the Ross family sell the boat, and who will they be if they do?

## Why now
The harbour authority's buy-back scheme closes in March [VERIFY closing date
against the authority's notice supplied 2026-07].

## Characters and access
| Person | Role | Want / obstacle | Access |
|---|---|---|---|
| Donald Ross, 61 | Skipper | Keep fishing / failing knees, quotas | Signed release |
| Isla Ross, 29 | Deckhand, daughter | Take over / a job offer on the mainland | Signed release |
| Harbour master | Institutional voice | — | Approached, verbal interest [ACCESS NOT YET SECURED] |

## Story and arc
Scheduled: season opens (Sept), buy-back decision deadline (March), Isla's
job start date (April). Likely turns: the first bad catch; the buyer's visit.
Ending A — they sell: a film about letting go with dignity.
Ending B — Isla takes the boat: a film about inheritance and its cost.
Observational scenes will happen (fishing days, the kitchen table); the
buyer's visit is hoped for, not arranged.

## Structure and point of view
Chronological across one season, chaptered by month. No narrator; no sit-down
interviews — conversation captured in the wheelhouse and kitchen.
Archive: family home video 1988–2004, on screen with dates.

## Visual and sound approach
Camera at arm's length in the wheelhouse; wide, static frames of the empty
harbour as a recurring image that fills or empties across the film. Engine
noise and radio chatter carry the sound; score used only at chapter breaks.

## Ethics and duty of care
Both family members see a rough cut before picture lock (promised in writing).
No reconstruction. Filming at sea follows a safety plan with the skipper's veto.

## Research and sources
Fleet numbers from the harbour authority's 2025 register (supplied). Family
history from recorded conversations with Donald and Isla, July 2026.

## Open items
[VERIFY] buy-back closing date · [ACCESS NOT YET SECURED] harbour master.
```

## Techniques Used

- **CM-01 Explicit Context Framing** — destination, length, and reader of the treatment.
- **ST-03 Output Format Specification** — the standard treatment sections in order.
- **CM-02 Constraint Specification** — ethics, consent, and no-reconstruction rules.
- **RT-05 Evidence-Based Reasoning** — access statuses and sourced claims.
- **QA-01 Self-Verification** — checks for invented facts and implied access.
