# TV Pilot & Series Bible

**Objective:** Turn a show idea into a pilot outline and a series bible that
answer the question every reader of a TV pitch asks: *what does this show do
every week, and why will it still be doing it in season three?*

**When to Use:**
- You have a show idea, a pilot draft, or a feature idea that might be a series.
- A reader said "great pilot, but what's episode 2?"
- You are preparing a bible or pitch document for a fellowship, competition,
  producer, or writers' room.

**Not this prompt if:**
- You are writing a single feature, short, or stage play, or need scene-level
  screenplay craft (formatting, visual storytelling, dialogue) →
  `domain-creative-writing/script-stage/writing_screenplay_framework.md`.
  This prompt uses that one for page craft and adds the *series* layer.
- You only need a logline and comparable titles →
  `domain-creative-writing/publishing-career/writing_pitch_logline_and_comp_titles.md`.
- The project is a documentary series → `writing_documentary_treatment.md`.
- You need a bible for a YouTube channel or content series →
  `domain-professional-writing/content-production/content_series_channel_bible.md`.

> **No-fabrication rule:** do not invent network interest, attached talent,
> ratings, or comparable-show performance. Comparable shows are used only for
> tone and format, and only those the writer names or confirms; anything else
> is marked `[VERIFY]`.

## Inputs

1. **Premise** and the world it happens in.
2. **Central characters**: what each wants and why they cannot stop wanting it.
3. **Intended format**: length (half-hour/hour), procedural vs serialized, limited or returning.
4. **Tone** (and any comparable shows the writer names).
5. **Pilot draft or outline**, if one exists.
6. **Destination**: fellowship, competition, producer meeting, room.

## Method

1. **Frame the destination (CM-01).** Restate format, destination, and any page
   or word limits for the bible; follow the destination's required sections.
2. **Name the engine.** In one or two sentences: the repeatable situation that
   generates story without new setup. Examples of engine *types*: a case of the
   week, a workplace with a new crisis each shift, a family business under
   siege, a con per episode, a mystery season. If the engine only works once,
   the idea is a feature or a limited series — say so.
3. **Break down series → season → episode (DT-01).**
   - **Series level:** the central question the whole show circles, and each
     main character's long arc (where they start, how they could end).
   - **Season level:** the season-one arc, the midseason turn, the finale's
     cliffhanger or closure.
   - **Episode level:** the A/B/C story pattern the format uses.
4. **Choose the pilot type.** *Premise pilot* shows the inciting change (the
   first day, the arrival); *non-premise pilot* drops us into an ordinary
   episode of the running show. Choose the one that best proves the engine.
5. **Break the pilot.** Use the act structure for the format (teaser + 4–5
   acts for hour drama; cold open + 2–3 acts + tag for half-hour comedy;
   streaming may drop act breaks but still needs turns). For each act: the
   turn, and which piece of the engine it demonstrates.
6. **Write episode springboards.** Eight to ten one- or two-line ideas for
   future episodes that come from the engine, not from new premises.
7. **Stress-test the engine (QA-02).** Ask adversarially: Can you pitch episode
   2, episode 10, and a season-two premise without adding a new core character or
   location? Does the protagonist's want reset believably each week, or does
   solving it end the show? What stops the central tension resolving in
   season one?
8. **Self-check (QA-01)** against Verification.

## Output Format (ST-03)

```
# [Show title] — Pilot & series bible
Format · Length · Procedural/serialized/hybrid · Limited/returning · Destination
## Logline
## The engine
## Tone & format references (writer-supplied only)
## World
## Characters
| Character | Want | Flaw | S1 arc | Possible endgame |
## The central question of the series
## Pilot
Type (premise/non-premise) · Act-by-act breakdown (turn + engine demonstrated)
## Season one arc
Opening · midseason turn · finale
## Episode springboards (8–10)
## Future seasons
## Engine stress test
| Test | Result / fix |
## Open items ([VERIFY])
```

## Verification

- [ ] The engine is stated in two sentences or fewer and repeats without new setup.
- [ ] Each main character has a long arc with a possible endgame.
- [ ] The pilot type is named and justified.
- [ ] Every pilot act names the engine element it demonstrates.
- [ ] At least eight springboards come from the engine.
- [ ] Episode 2, episode 10, and season two can be pitched without a new core element.
- [ ] No industry facts, attachments, or performance claims are invented.

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **Do not force a returning series onto a limited-series idea.** If the story
   has one ending, recommend the limited format rather than inventing an engine.
2. **Do not require a case-of-the-week.** Serialized shows have engines too
   (an ongoing conflict with fresh fronts each episode).
3. **Do not treat page counts as rules.** Streaming pilots vary; flag only
   length that the destination prohibits.
4. **Do not demand an ensemble.** Single-protagonist shows are valid; check
   that supporting characters can carry B-stories.
5. **Do not fill the bible with lore.** World detail belongs only where it
   produces story.
6. **Do not invent comparable shows.** Use only those the writer names; mark
   any suggestion `[VERIFY]`.

## Example

**Input (summary):** Hour-long hybrid drama, returning. Premise: Ada Mensah, a
disgraced structural engineer, becomes the only inspector for a coastal county
where storms are getting worse. Destination: a TV writing fellowship (bible ≤ 10 pages).

```
# Load Bearing — Pilot & series bible
Hour · hybrid (building of the week + serialized cover-up) · returning · fellowship

## Logline
A structural engineer blamed for a collapse she warned about takes the job
nobody wants — inspecting a storm-battered county — and finds the collapse
was no accident.

## The engine
Every week a structure is failing and someone wants it signed off anyway.
Each inspection pulls Ada closer to the people who buried her report.

## Tone & format references
Writer names none; tone described as "procedural warmth, conspiratorial chill."

## World
Marlow County: a pier town, a new-build estate, a caravan park, an ageing sea wall.

## Characters
| Character | Want | Flaw | S1 arc | Possible endgame |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ada | Clear her name | Trusts drawings over people | Learns the town before the truth | Testifies against her old firm |
| Jonah (county clerk) | Keep the county solvent | Signs what he's told | Starts hiding her reports | Becomes her witness |
| Mrs. Reyes (developer) | Build the estate | Charm as leverage | Revealed as the old firm's client | Loses the estate or buys Ada |

## The central question of the series
Can one honest inspector hold a place up when everyone profits from letting it fall?

## Pilot
Premise pilot.
Teaser: the pier sways under a wedding party; Ada orders everyone off. (Engine: failing structure)
Act 1: Hired by Jonah; nobody else applied. (World)
Act 2: The caravan park owner begs her to sign; she won't. (Pressure to sign)
Act 3: Midpoint — the pier's steel matches her old collapse. (Serial thread)
Act 4: Storm; she evacuates the pier; it partly collapses — nobody hurt.
Act 5: Mrs. Reyes offers her a consultancy. Tag: Jonah shreds her report.

## Season one arc
Opening: the pier. Midseason: the sea-wall report is altered. Finale: storm
surge; Ada must choose between the estate and the evidence.

## Episode springboards
1. A school roof after snow load. 2. A listed church a charity wants demolished.
3. A fairground ride on its last licence. 4. Her own rented flat fails inspection.
5. A bridge the only ambulance route uses. 6. A home built by the clerk's brother.
7. A flood barrier after a council budget cut. 8. The old firm bids for the sea wall.

## Future seasons
S2: the county upgrades; the firm returns as contractor. S3: Ada runs for office.

## Engine stress test
| Test | Result / fix |
|---|---|
| Episode 2 without new core element? | Yes — springboard 1 |
| Episode 10? | Yes — springboard 8 |
| Does solving the cover-up end the show? | Risk: the cover-up must widen in S2, not close |
| Season two? | Yes — firm as contractor |

## Open items
[VERIFY] fellowship's required section headings and page limit.
```

## Techniques Used

- **CM-01 Explicit Context Framing** — format and destination.
- **DT-01 Hierarchical Task Breakdown** — series, season, episode levels.
- **QA-02 Adversarial Stress-Test** — episode 2, 10, season two, and the reset test.
- **ST-03 Output Format Specification** — the bible sections.
- **QA-01 Self-Verification** — engine and no-fabrication checks.
