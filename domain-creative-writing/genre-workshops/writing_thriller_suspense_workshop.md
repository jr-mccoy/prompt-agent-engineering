# Thriller & Suspense Workshop

**Objective:** Produce a thriller plot in which a proactive antagonist's plan
runs on its own clock, the protagonist starts outmatched, every move draws a
counter-move, the reader's knowledge is managed for maximum suspense, and the
climax is won by the protagonist's own choice — with no hole a reader can
drive the villain through.

**When to Use:**
- Plotting a psychological, political, legal, medical, techno, spy, or
  domestic thriller, or a suspense novel.
- Readers said "the villain was dumb," "the hero just reacted," "the stakes
  plateaued," or "too many twists."
- The book has action but not *suspense*.

**Not this prompt if:**
- The engine is a past crime and the pleasure is solving it (whodunit, cozy,
  procedural) → `domain-creative-writing/genre-workshops/writing_mystery_and_crime_craft.md`.
  **Thriller vs mystery:** a mystery asks *what happened?* and hides the answer
  in clues; a thriller asks *what will happen?* and often shows the reader the
  threat. Hybrids (a detective racing a killer before the next victim) should
  run the mystery workshop for the clue plan and this one for the clock.
- You need the multi-genre overview → `writing_genre_specific_guidance.md`.
- The threat is supernatural and the aim is dread → `writing_horror_genre_workshop.md`.

## Inputs

1. **Protagonist**: skills, limits, what they stand to lose personally.
2. **Antagonist**: goal, resources, and what they believe justifies them.
3. **The threat**: what happens if nobody stops it, and by when.
4. **Subgenre** and setting.
5. **Draft or outline**, and the sticking point.

## Method

1. **Set the contract (CM-01).** Subgenre, the scale of stakes (one life, a
   family, a city), the clock (a hard deadline or an accelerating threat), and
   whether the reader will know the antagonist's identity (open) or not (closed).
2. **Write the antagonist's plan first.** A timeline of what the antagonist does
   *if the protagonist never interferes*: preparation, key steps, the moment of
   no return. This is the book's hidden spine; without it, the villain only
   exists when on the page.
3. **Define the disadvantage.** State what the antagonist has that the
   protagonist lacks (information, power, allies, legitimacy) and why the
   protagonist cannot simply hand the problem to someone else.
4. **Chain move and counter-move (RT-07).** For each protagonist action, trace
   the cascade: what the antagonist learns, how the plan adapts, and what new
   cost falls on the protagonist. Every link should raise stakes or shrink
   time. Aim for at least three escalations that are worse in kind (personal,
   then relational, then moral).
5. **Manage information for suspense.** For each major sequence, record who
   knows what: reader ahead of hero (suspense — we fear for them), reader level
   with hero (mystery-like tension), reader behind (surprise). Suspense needs
   the reader to know a danger exists before it arrives.
6. **Budget the twists.** List each reversal and the earlier scene that makes it
   fair on reread. More than one major twist per act strains trust; a twist with
   no plant is a cheat.
7. **Stress-test the plot (QA-02).** Play the antagonist adversarially: "Why
   doesn't the villain just…" kill the witness, wait a week, change the
   password, leave the country? Answer each with a reason on the page or fix it.
   Then do the same for the protagonist: "Why don't they just call the police?"
8. **Design the climax (ST-02).** The protagonist confronts the antagonist
   directly, at the moment of no return, and wins (or loses) through a choice
   that draws on what the story has cost them — not through rescue or luck.

## Output Format (ST-03)

```
# [Title] — Thriller plan
## Contract
Subgenre · Stakes scale · Clock · Open/closed antagonist
## Antagonist's plan (uninterrupted timeline)
| Day/time | Step | Point of no return? |
## The disadvantage
## Move / counter-move chain
| # | Protagonist move | Antagonist learns | Plan adapts | New cost / less time |
## Information map
| Sequence | Reader knows | Hero knows | Effect (suspense/surprise) |
## Twist budget
| Twist | Act | Planted in |
## "Why doesn't X just…" stress test
| Question | Answer on the page / fix |
## Climax
## Diagnosis & top 3 revisions
```

## Verification

- [ ] The antagonist's plan would succeed if nobody interfered.
- [ ] The protagonist's disadvantage is specific and cannot be delegated away.
- [ ] Each counter-move raises stakes or shortens time.
- [ ] At least one major sequence puts the reader ahead of the hero.
- [ ] Every twist has a named plant.
- [ ] Every "why doesn't X just" question has an answer.
- [ ] The climax turns on the protagonist's choice.

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **Do not demand fair-play clueing.** Thrillers owe the reader plausibility
   and plants for twists, not a solvable puzzle; that standard belongs to mystery.
2. **Do not treat an open antagonist as a spoiled mystery.** Showing the
   villain early is a suspense technique, not a mistake.
3. **Do not equate pace with short chapters or constant action.** Flag missing
   escalation or missing release, not a quiet chapter.
4. **Do not require global stakes.** Domestic and psychological thrillers work
   at the scale of one household; stakes must be *personal*, not large.
5. **Do not flag every coincidence.** Coincidence that puts the hero *in*
   trouble is acceptable; coincidence that gets them *out* is the flaw.
6. **Do not add twists to "fix" a sagging middle.** The usual cure is a
   stronger counter-move, not another reversal.
7. **Do not require authentic tradecraft detail beyond the reader's need.**
   Flag errors that break plausibility, not missing procedure.

## Example

**Input (summary):** Domestic thriller, closed-to-open antagonist. Hana, a
night-shift pharmacist, notices that a regular customer's prescriptions are
being collected by a different man each week. Sticking point: "The middle is
Hana investigating and nothing pushes back."

```
# The Night Counter — Thriller plan
## Contract
Domestic/medical · one family's lives · hard clock (the insurance policy
matures in 10 days) · antagonist revealed at 40%

## Antagonist's plan
| Day | Step | No return? |
|---|---|---|
| 1–7 | Gavin (the patient's son-in-law) swaps her heart medication weekly | No |
| 8 | Arranges to be abroad with an alibi | No |
| 10 | Her death reads as natural; policy pays | Yes |

## The disadvantage
Hana has suspicion but no proof; confidentiality rules bar her from calling the
family; her manager thinks she is overtired.

## Move / counter-move chain
| # | Hana moves | Gavin learns | Plan adapts | Cost / time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Flags the collections in the log | Log is visible to the delivery driver he pays | Switches to home delivery | Hana loses her evidence trail |
| 2 | Visits the patient on her day off | Neighbour mentions "the pharmacist" | Complains she harassed the family | Hana suspended |
| 3 | Takes a sample from the returned blister pack | Nothing — she is unseen | — | She now holds evidence illegally |
| 4 | Goes to the police with it | Police call the family | Moves the plan to day 7 | Three days lost |

## Information map
| Sequence | Reader | Hana | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ch12 Gavin's POV prologue-style chapter | Knows the new date | Doesn't | Suspense |
| Ch18 the suspension hearing | Level | Level | Tension |

## Twist budget
| Twist | Act | Planted in |
|---|---|---|
| The patient's daughter knows | 3 | Ch6 she refuses to meet Hana's eyes at the counter |

## Stress test
| Question | Answer / fix |
|---|---|
| Why doesn't Gavin just overdose her once? | A sudden overdose triggers a post-mortem; gradual swaps do not (stated Ch12) |
| Why doesn't Hana call the police at once? | She does at move 4; before that she has no evidence — add her reasoning in Ch5 |

## Climax
Day 7: Hana, suspended, enters the house as the only person who can identify the
swapped pills — risking her licence to save the patient.

## Diagnosis & top 3 revisions
The middle lacked counter-moves. 1. Add moves 1–2 so Gavin pushes back.
2. Add the Ch12 antagonist chapter to put the reader ahead. 3. Plant the daughter.
```

## Techniques Used

- **CM-01 Explicit Context Framing** — stakes, clock, open/closed antagonist.
- **RT-07 Cascade Effect Analysis** — the move/counter-move chain.
- **QA-02 Adversarial Stress-Test** — "why doesn't X just" for both sides.
- **ST-02 Structured Sequential Instructions** — plan first, climax last.
- **ST-03 Output Format Specification** — the fixed plan layout.
