# Romance Genre Workshop

**Objective:** Build (or diagnose) a romance in which the developing relationship
*is* the spine of the book — two people with real reasons to resist each other,
a barrier that erodes through choices rather than coincidence, and an ending that
delivers the genre's promise of a happily-ever-after (HEA) or happy-for-now (HFN).

**When to Use:**
- Plotting a contemporary, historical, romantic-suspense, paranormal/romantasy,
  or romantic-comedy novel where the love story is the main plot.
- Beta readers said "I didn't believe they'd fall for each other," "the middle
  sagged," or "the breakup felt contrived."
- You have a trope (enemies-to-lovers, fake dating, second chance) and need the
  specific beats that make it pay off.

**Not this prompt if:**
- You want a quick survey of conventions across many genres →
  `domain-creative-writing/genre-workshops/writing_genre_specific_guidance.md`
  (this prompt goes deeper on romance only).
- The romance is a subplot in a mystery or thriller → build the main plot with
  `writing_mystery_and_crime_craft.md` or `writing_thriller_suspense_workshop.md`
  and treat the relationship as a B-story.
- The book is for a teen or child reader → `domain-childrens-writing/`.
- A bittersweet or tragic love story is intended: that is love-story literary or
  women's fiction, not genre romance — the HEA/HFN rule below does not apply.

## Inputs

1. **The two (or more) leads**: who they are, what each wants, what each fears.
2. **Subgenre and setting** (contemporary, historical, romantic suspense, etc.).
3. **Trope(s)** the book promises on its cover copy.
4. **Heat level** the author has chosen (closed door, fade-to-black, open door)
   and any content the author has ruled out.
5. **POV plan** (dual first, dual close third, single POV).
6. **Current draft or outline**, and the specific sticking point.

## Method

1. **State the reader contract (CM-01).** Subgenre, trope promise, heat level,
   and ending type (HEA or HFN) in one paragraph. Every later step is checked
   against this paragraph; a romance that breaks its contract feels like a cheat
   even when the prose is good.
2. **Build each lead's internal arc.** For each: the wound (past event), the lie
   they believe because of it, the want they pursue, and the need the other
   person exposes. The two arcs should *interlock* — what one lead needs is
   something the other is uniquely placed to challenge.
3. **Design the barrier (RT-02).** Analyse the barrier on three axes:
   - **Internal** (the lie that makes loving this person frightening),
   - **External** (job, family, rivalry, danger, distance),
   - **Situational** (the trope's engine: the fake relationship, the shared
     inheritance, the forced proximity).
   The internal axis must carry the most weight. If one honest conversation would
   dissolve the whole barrier, it is a plot convenience, not a barrier.
4. **Lay out the relationship beat sheet (ST-02).** Place these beats, with
   approximate percentage marks, and name the scene that delivers each:
   - Meet (the setup of the dynamic, not just the first sight)
   - No way (why neither will pursue this)
   - Adhesion (what keeps them in contact against their will)
   - Deepening (first vulnerability; the reader sees them fit)
   - Midpoint (a public or physical threshold: first kiss, the lie becomes real)
   - Retreat (the internal lie reasserts itself as intimacy grows)
   - Dark moment (they separate *because of* the lie, not a coincidence)
   - Grand gesture / choice (a lead acts against their lie at real cost)
   - HEA / HFN (the relationship shown working, not only declared)
5. **Pay off the trope.** For the chosen trope, list the two or three moments
   readers come for (fake dating: the rules, the slip where it stops being fake,
   the reveal) and confirm each is on the page.
6. **Handle intimacy at the chosen heat level.** Whatever the level, intimate
   scenes must change the relationship — reveal, complicate, or deepen — and
   depict clear consent. Scenes that could be removed without changing the
   relationship are the ones to cut or rewrite.
7. **Choose and audit POV.** Dual POV lets the reader see both lies at once;
   single POV creates suspense about the other lead's feelings. Check that each
   POV chapter moves *that* lead's arc.
8. **Self-check (QA-01)** against the Verification list before reporting.

## Output Format (ST-03)

```
# [Working title] — Romance plan
## Reader contract
Subgenre · Trope · Heat level · Ending (HEA/HFN)
## Leads
| Lead | Wound | Lie | Want | Need | What the other exposes |
## Barrier analysis
Internal · External · Situational — and the one-conversation test result
## Relationship beat sheet
| Beat | ~% | Scene | What changes between them |
## Trope payoffs
## Intimacy & heat notes
## POV plan
## Diagnosis of the sticking point
## Revision priorities (top 3)
```

## Verification

- [ ] The reader contract names subgenre, trope, heat level, and HEA/HFN.
- [ ] Each lead's lie is specific and the two arcs interlock.
- [ ] The barrier survives the one-honest-conversation test.
- [ ] The dark moment is caused by a lead's lie, not an accident or a third party alone.
- [ ] The grand gesture costs the character something tied to their lie.
- [ ] Every beat names a scene, and the ending shows the relationship working.

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **Do not flag tropes as clichés.** Romance readers seek tropes on purpose; the
   question is execution, not novelty.
2. **Do not recommend a sad ending "for depth."** HEA/HFN is the genre's defining
   promise; advising against it misclassifies the book.
3. **Do not treat heat level as a quality dial.** Closed-door and open-door
   romance are equally legitimate; critique only whether scenes do relational work.
4. **A misunderstanding is not automatically a flaw.** It fails only when one
   conversation would fix it *and* the characters have no reason not to have it.
5. **Do not demand a villain.** Many romances have no antagonist; the leads'
   own fears are the opposition.
6. **Do not apply beat percentages rigidly.** Slow burns move the midpoint late;
   second-chance romances front-load backstory. Report deviations, not errors.
7. **Do not rewrite the author's leads into different people.** Diagnose within
   the characters supplied.

## Example

**Input (summary):** Contemporary romance, closed door, HEA. Trope: forced
proximity. Priya Nair, a restorer of church organs, is hired to rebuild the
instrument in a village chapel; Tom Hale, the chapel's caretaker, opposes the
project because the organ was his late father's. Sticking point: "The middle is
just them bickering in the loft."

```
# Pipes and Promises — Romance plan
## Reader contract
Contemporary · forced proximity · closed door · HEA

## Leads
| Lead | Wound | Lie | Want | Need | What the other exposes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Priya | Engagement ended when she took a job abroad | "Staying anywhere costs me my work" | Finish and leave in 8 weeks | A place that wants her craft | Tom's village wants her to stay |
| Tom | Father died mid-repair of the organ | "If I let it change, I lose him" | Keep the organ untouched | To grieve by finishing, not freezing | Priya can finish what his father began |

## Barrier analysis
Internal (dominant): each lie makes the other person a threat.
External: the parish council's deadline. Situational: they share one key to the loft.
One-conversation test: passes — even if they talk openly, Priya's contract ends
and Tom's grief is unresolved.

## Relationship beat sheet
| Beat | ~% | Scene | What changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Meet | 3 | Tom refuses to hand over the loft key | Opposition set |
| No way | 10 | Priya books her next job for week 9 | Exit fixed |
| Adhesion | 15 | Council rules one key, shared hours | Forced proximity |
| Deepening | 30 | Tom finds his father's pencilled notes; Priya reads them with him | First vulnerability |
| Midpoint | 50 | First pipe sounds; they kiss in the dark loft | Attraction made real |
| Retreat | 60 | Priya declines a festival invite "to keep it professional" | Her lie reasserts |
| Dark moment | 78 | Tom hides the last notebook so she cannot finish and leave | His lie causes the break |
| Grand gesture | 88 | Tom returns the notebook and plays the organ publicly, unfinished | Acts against his lie |
| HEA | 97 | Priya turns down week 9; opens a workshop in the vestry | Shown, not declared |

## Trope payoffs
The shared key (rules), the night the key is left in the door (slip), the
council's discovery (reveal).

## Intimacy & heat notes
Closed door kept: the midpoint kiss ends the chapter; tension carried by touch
over the keyboard, never explicit.

## POV plan
Dual close third, alternating; Tom's chapters carry the notebooks.

## Diagnosis of the sticking point
Bickering scenes change nothing between them. Replace two of them with
Deepening beats built on the father's notes.

## Revision priorities
1. Add the notebook discovery at ~30%. 2. Make Tom's hiding of the notebook the
cause of the dark moment. 3. Cut one loft argument per act.
```

## Techniques Used

- **CM-01 Explicit Context Framing** — the reader contract governs every check.
- **ST-02 Structured Sequential Instructions** — the relationship beat sheet.
- **RT-02 Multi-Dimensional Analysis** — the three-axis barrier.
- **ST-03 Output Format Specification** — the fixed plan layout.
- **QA-01 Self-Verification** — the one-conversation and cause-of-dark-moment checks.
