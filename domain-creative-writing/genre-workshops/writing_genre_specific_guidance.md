**Purpose:** Provide genre-specific craft guidance so writers can meet reader expectations while finding fresh ways to deliver. Each genre has conventions that readers expect, techniques that work best, and traps that catch newcomers.

**When to use:** When writing in a specific genre and want to understand its conventions; when feedback says "this doesn't feel like a [genre]"; when blending genres and need to understand what each brings; when transitioning from one genre to another.

**Input needed:**
- Your genre (or genre blend)
- Your story concept
- Your experience with the genre (as reader and writer)
- What you want to do that might bend or break conventions

## Instructions

### Step 1: Genre Identification

Identify the genre and subgenre to apply the right expectations:

| Genre | Core Promise to Reader |
|-------|----------------------|
| **Mystery** | A puzzle that will be solved; justice or truth will emerge |
| **Thriller** | The protagonist will face escalating danger and survive through skill/will |
| **Romance** | Two people will fall in love and get a satisfying ending together |
| **Science Fiction** | "What if?" — exploring ideas through speculative scenarios |
| **Fantasy** | Immersion in a world where the impossible is possible |
| **Horror** | You will feel fear, dread, or unease |
| **Literary Fiction** | Beautiful language, complex characters, thematic depth |
| **Historical Fiction** | Transport to another time; blend of research and imagination |

**The genre promise is your contract with the reader.** You can surprise them within it, but breaking it feels like betrayal.

### Step 2: Genre-Specific Guidance

---

#### Mystery / Crime Fiction

**Subgenres:** Cozy, hardboiled, police procedural, amateur sleuth, locked room, noir, domestic suspense

**Core Structure:**
1. A crime (usually murder) occurs or is discovered
2. Investigation begins — clues and suspects emerge
3. Red herrings mislead; complications deepen
4. Detective (professional or amateur) pieces truth together
5. Solution is revealed — satisfying and fair

**Essential Conventions:**

| Convention | Why It Matters |
|-----------|---------------|
| **Play fair** | All clues available to the reader must be present before the reveal |
| **The solution must be satisfying** | Readers should think "Of course!" not "That's random" |
| **Red herrings must be plausible** | Misdirection that's too obvious insults the reader |
| **The detective must earn the solution** | Not luck, not coincidence — skill and deduction |
| **Stakes must be personal** | Why does THIS detective care about THIS case? |

**Craft Techniques:**
- Plant clues in plain sight (hidden by context, not omission)
- Give the reader all pieces but arrange them to mislead
- Each suspect needs means, motive, and opportunity
- The best clue is one the reader sees but doesn't recognize as significant
- Pacing: alternate investigation scenes with character/relationship scenes

**Common Mistakes:**
- Withholding information the POV character knows (unfair)
- Making the solution depend on expertise the reader can't possess
- Villain reveals motive in a monologue at the end
- Red herrings that feel random rather than organic
- Neglecting the detective's personal stakes

#### Thriller / Suspense

**Subgenres:** Psychological, political, legal, medical, techno-thriller, domestic suspense, spy

**Core Structure:**
1. Protagonist discovers or is thrust into danger
2. Stakes escalate — personal and/or global
3. Antagonist is powerful and proactive
4. A ticking clock compresses the timeline
5. Climax is a direct confrontation; protagonist wins through growth

**Essential Conventions:**

| Convention | Why It Matters |
|-----------|---------------|
| **Ticking clock** | Urgency drives pacing; deadline creates tension |
| **Escalating stakes** | Each act must raise what's at risk |
| **Worthy antagonist** | The villain must be smarter, stronger, or better-resourced |
| **Protagonist under pressure** | Constant danger, difficult choices, personal cost |
| **Page-turner pacing** | Short chapters, cliffhangers, momentum |

**Craft Techniques:**
- End every chapter on a hook or question
- Use dramatic irony (reader knows danger the protagonist doesn't)
- Create set pieces — memorable, cinematic action sequences
- Alternate tension and release (all tension numbs the reader)
- Give the protagonist skills but make the problem exceed them

**Common Mistakes:**
- Passive protagonist who is rescued rather than acting
- Stakes that plateau rather than escalate
- Unrealistic competence (protagonist is good at everything)
- Too many twists (reader stops trusting the story)
- Neglecting character in favor of plot mechanics

#### Romance

**Subgenres:** Contemporary, historical, paranormal, romantic suspense, rom-com, dark romance, YA romance

**Core Structure:**
1. Meet — the two leads are introduced (often with friction)
2. Attraction — chemistry is established (physical, emotional, intellectual)
3. Obstacles — internal and external forces keep them apart
4. Dark moment — the relationship seems impossible
5. Grand gesture / Reconciliation — they choose each other
6. HEA/HFN — Happily Ever After or Happy For Now

**Essential Conventions:**

| Convention | Why It Matters |
|-----------|---------------|
| **HEA or HFN** | This is non-negotiable in romance; readers expect it |
| **Central love story** | The romance IS the main plot, not a subplot |
| **Both characters must be developed** | Each lead needs their own arc, goals, and growth |
| **Emotional beats matter more than plot** | Readers come for the feelings |
| **Chemistry on the page** | Tension, banter, attraction must be palpable |

**Craft Techniques:**
- Use the "push-pull" dynamic — attraction and resistance alternating
- Internal obstacles are more compelling than external ones
- Each interaction should deepen the connection AND raise the stakes
- The dark moment should feel genuinely threatening to the relationship
- Show vulnerability — the moment each character lets their guard down
- Banter reveals character; it's not just witty dialogue

**Common Mistakes:**
- Telling us they're in love without showing the emotional journey
- One-dimensional love interest (exists only to love the protagonist)
- Conflict that could be resolved by a single honest conversation
- Dark moment that's manufactured rather than organic
- Skipping the HEA/HFN (this is a genre requirement, not optional)

#### Science Fiction

**Subgenres:** Hard SF, soft SF, space opera, cyberpunk, climate fiction, first contact, time travel, near-future

**Core Structure:** Variable — depends on subgenre. The constant: a speculative premise explored through story.

**Essential Conventions:**

| Convention | Why It Matters |
|-----------|---------------|
| **Internal consistency** | The rules of your world must be followed once established |
| **Ideas matter** | SF readers expect intellectual engagement alongside story |
| **Extrapolation** | "If X is true, then Y follows" — logical consequences |
| **Sense of wonder** | At least one moment where the reader goes "Wow" |
| **Human story in speculative context** | Technology serves the human drama, not the reverse |

**Craft Techniques:**
- Introduce the speculative element early; don't bury your premise
- Show technology through use, not exposition (characters use it, don't explain it)
- Explore second-order effects (if X exists, how has society changed?)
- Ground unfamiliar worlds in familiar human emotions
- Hard SF: get the science right or plausibly extrapolate
- Soft SF: the social/psychological speculation must be rigorous

**Common Mistakes:**
- Info-dumping technology explanations
- Technology without social consequences
- Aliens/AI that are just humans in costume
- Ignoring how speculative changes affect daily life
- All concept, no character

---

#### Fantasy

**Subgenres:** Epic/high, urban, dark, YA, cozy, grimdark, sword & sorcery, mythic, portal

**Core Structure:** Variable, but often follows quest or hero's journey patterns.

**Essential Conventions:**

| Convention | Why It Matters |
|-----------|---------------|
| **Magic system with rules** | Readers need to understand what's possible and what isn't |
| **Worldbuilding that serves story** | Rich world, but shown through character experience |
| **Clear stakes** | What's at risk must be tangible, even in fantastical settings |
| **Internal consistency** | The world must follow its own rules |
| **Sense of wonder** | The fantastic should feel wondrous, not mundane |

**Craft Techniques:**
- Reveal worldbuilding through character experience, not lectures
- Magic with costs and limits creates better stories than unlimited power
- Ground the fantastic in sensory detail (what does magic smell, sound, feel like?)
- Give cultural depth — religion, food, language, daily life
- Subgenre determines darkness, scope, and tone — know your lane

**Common Mistakes:**
- Worldbuilding infodumps (the chapter-long history lesson)
- Chosen One without personal agency
- Magic system without meaningful limitations
- Fantasy racism used as lazy worldbuilding
- Monocultures (one culture per species/nation)

---

#### Horror

**Subgenres:** Psychological, cosmic, body horror, gothic, supernatural, slasher, folk horror, quiet horror

**Core Structure:**
1. Normalcy — establish what the character values/loves
2. Intrusion — something wrong enters their world
3. Escalation — the threat grows, options narrow
4. Dread — the full scope of horror becomes apparent
5. Confrontation — face the horror (survive, succumb, or transform)

**Essential Conventions:**

| Convention | Why It Matters |
|-----------|---------------|
| **Build dread, not just surprise** | Sustained unease > jump scares |
| **Relatable characters** | We must care before we can be scared for them |
| **Escalation** | Each encounter should be worse than the last |
| **The unknown is scarier** | What's implied terrifies more than what's shown |
| **Atmosphere** | Setting and mood do more work than monsters |

**Craft Techniques:**
- Use sensory detail to create atmosphere (cold, silence, wrong smells)
- Delay the reveal — the longer the reader's imagination fills in, the scarier
- Ordinary settings made wrong are more unsettling than inherently creepy ones
- Give the horror thematic weight (what does it represent?)
- Pacing: slow build punctuated by sharp shocks
- The best horror makes the reader complicit in their own fear

**Common Mistakes:**
- Gore without dread (visceral but not scary)
- Explaining the monster too fully (mystery is scarier)
- Characters making obviously stupid decisions to drive plot
- All shock, no atmosphere
- Horror without thematic resonance (what does it MEAN?)

---

#### Literary Fiction

**Subgenres:** Realist, experimental, autofiction, historical literary, speculative literary

**Core Structure:** Variable — often character-driven rather than plot-driven.

**Essential Conventions:**

| Convention | Why It Matters |
|-----------|---------------|
| **Prose quality matters** | Sentence-level craft is expected to be high |
| **Thematic depth** | The story should illuminate something about human experience |
| **Character interiority** | Deep access to characters' inner lives |
| **Ambiguity is welcome** | Not every question needs an answer |
| **Form can be content** | How the story is told can be as meaningful as what's told |

**Craft Techniques:**
- Invest in voice — distinctive, considered prose
- Use structure as meaning (fragmented form for fragmented experience)
- Subtext and implication over direct statement
- Earn your ambiguity (productive uncertainty, not confusion)
- Small moments can carry enormous weight
- Let themes emerge from story rather than imposing them

**Common Mistakes:**
- Beautiful prose with no story (style without substance)
- Confusing obscurity with depth
- Passive, observing protagonists who never act
- Self-conscious "literary" writing that calls attention to technique
- Plot-phobia (literary fiction can and should have narrative drive)

### Step 3: Genre Blending

**When combining genres, identify:**

1. **Primary genre:** This determines the core promise and ending expectations
2. **Secondary genre:** This adds flavor, techniques, and audience appeal
3. **Conflict points:** Where do the genres' conventions contradict?

| Blend | What Works | Watch Out For |
|-------|-----------|---------------|
| **Romance + Mystery** | Two satisfying resolutions (crime solved + couple united) | Both plots need full development |
| **Sci-Fi + Horror** | Cosmic dread meets speculative wonder | Don't let tech undercut the fear |
| **Literary + Thriller** | Propulsive plot with deep characters | Pacing must satisfy thriller readers |
| **Fantasy + Romance** | Rich world + emotional connection | Worldbuilding can't overwhelm the love story |
| **Horror + Literary** | Elevated horror with thematic depth | Don't be afraid to actually scare people |

## Quality Indicators

**Genre-aware writing includes:**
- [ ] Delivers on the core genre promise
- [ ] Meets reader expectations while offering fresh execution
- [ ] Uses genre-appropriate pacing and structure

- [ ] Understands which conventions are flexible and which are load-bearing

## False-Positive Prevention

**DON'T:**
- Dismiss genre fiction as "lesser" than literary fiction
- Apply one genre's rules to another (romance needs HEA; literary fiction doesn't)
- Assume conventions are limitations (they're a shared language with readers)
- Recommend breaking conventions without understanding why they exist
- Treat genre labels as rigid boxes (the best work often blends)

**DO:**
- Respect the genre's reader base and their expectations
- Distinguish between conventions (flexible) and promises (load-bearing)
- Encourage fresh execution within genre frameworks
- Ask what the writer wants readers to feel (that determines genre approach)
- Recommend reading widely within the target genre and subgenre
