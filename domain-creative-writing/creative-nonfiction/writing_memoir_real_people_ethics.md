# Writing About Real People in Memoir and Essay

**Objective:** Give a memoirist or essayist a clear, person-by-person decision for
everyone who appears on the page — what the story needs from them, what the
portrayal could cost them, what the writer actually knows versus infers, and which
handling (keep, reframe, ask, show, disguise, composite, delay, cut) the writer
chooses — with legal exposure flagged for professional review rather than guessed.

**When to Use:**
- Your memoir or essay includes family members, an ex, friends, coworkers, or
  children, and you are unsure what you may or should say.
- Someone in the story has asked you not to write about them, or you expect they will.
- You are considering changing names, merging people into a composite, or adding an
  author's note.
- The draft includes private facts (health, addiction, sexuality, abuse, arrests,
  money) about someone other than you.
- **Not this prompt if** the piece is reported journalism or narrative nonfiction
  about people beyond your own life — use
  `domain-creative-writing/creative-nonfiction/writing_narrative_nonfiction_and_literary_journalism.md`
  (stricter sourcing; no composites). If you need the statement-by-statement legal
  screen (fact vs opinion, truth, privilege, public-figure analysis), use
  `domain-legal/ip/legal_defamation_publicity_risk_screen.md`; this prompt decides
  *how to portray* people and sends the risky statements there. For finding the
  story itself, use `creative-nonfiction/writing_memoir_and_personal_essay.md`.

> **Not legal advice.** Defamation, privacy, and publicity law vary by country and,
> in the US, by state. This prompt organises ethical choices and flags statements
> for review; it does not clear anything for publication. Anything marked
> `[LEGAL REVIEW]` goes to counsel or to the publisher's legal read.

## Inputs / Context

1. **The draft** (or the relevant chapters), with real names as written.
2. **The people in it**: relationship, living or dead, age (any minors), whether
   public figures, and current contact with the writer.
3. **Sources for claims** about others: letters, texts, diaries, records, photos,
   what others told the writer, the writer's memory.
4. **Known objections** and anyone already asked or shown the draft.
5. **Publication plan**: venue type, country of publication, whether a publisher
   with a legal read is involved.
6. **The writer's own lines**: what they will not change regardless.

## Method

1. **Build the cast inventory.** Everyone identifiable — by name, or by role plus
   place plus a unique detail ("my brother, the only vice-principal in a town of
   3,000"). For each: role in the story, living/dead, minor/adult, public/private
   figure, how identifiable after any planned disguise.
2. **Tag every claim about another person (RT-23).**
   `[documented]` (a record or artefact from the time), `[remembered]` (the
   writer's memory of what they saw or heard), `[told]` (someone else's account —
   name the teller), `[inferred]` (the writer's reading of motives or inner states).
   Inferences about another person's mind must be framed as the writer's view on the
   page ("I think now she was…"), never stated as fact.
3. **Weigh need against harm.** For each person: what does the story lose without
   this detail? What could the disclosure cost them (job, relationships, safety,
   custody, a child's privacy)? Private facts that do not serve the story's
   question are the first candidates to cut.
4. **Choose a handling for each person** from this ladder:
   | Handling | Use when |
   |---|---|
   | Keep as written | claim is the writer's own experience, fairly framed, story needs it |
   | Reframe | an inference or label is stated as fact; convert to observed scene or "I" interpretation |
   | Ask | consent is not legally required in many places, but the relationship or ethics call for it |
   | Show the draft | the person is central and the writer wants their correction of facts (not a veto) |
   | Disguise | the person is peripheral; change name and non-essential identifiers — note it does not protect a person their community can still identify |
   | Composite (disclosed) | several minor people do the same narrative job; disclose in an author's note |
   | Delay | harm is time-bound (a child, an ongoing custody matter, a dying parent) |
   | Cut | the detail serves the writer's grievance more than the story |
5. **Flag once, then defer (GT-09).** Raise each ethical concern once, with the
   reason, then follow the writer's informed decision. The writer owns their own
   experience; the prompt's job is to make the choice visible, not to make it.
6. **Route legal exposure (DD-05).** Mark `[LEGAL REVIEW]` on any statement that:
   asserts a crime, misconduct, or disease about an identifiable living person;
   discloses private facts with no story need; rests on `[told]` or `[inferred]`
   rather than `[documented]` or `[remembered]`; or names a minor. Send those
   statements to `domain-legal/ip/legal_defamation_publicity_risk_screen.md`. If a
   publishing contract is involved, note that author warranties and indemnities
   commonly place this risk on the writer — counsel should read them.
7. **Draft the author's note.** State what was changed (names, identifying details,
   composites, compressed time, reconstructed dialogue) plainly and briefly.
8. **Hold the must-nots (DP-04).** The prompt must not invent facts about any
   person, must not supply a legal conclusion, and must not soften the writer's own
   experience into vagueness to avoid discomfort.

## Output Format

```
# Real-people review — [title / chapter]
Publication: [venue type · country] · Legal read by publisher: [yes | no | unknown]

## Cast inventory
| Person (as on page) | Role in story | Living | Minor | Public/private | Identifiable after disguise? |

## Claims and provenance
| Person | Claim (¶) | Tag | Framing issue | [LEGAL REVIEW]? |

## Need vs harm
| Person | Story needs | Possible harm | Verdict |

## Handling decisions (writer's choice recorded)
| Person | Handling | Concern raised (once) | Writer decision |

## [LEGAL REVIEW] list for counsel / legal screen
## Author's note draft
## Conversations to have before publication
```

## Verification

- [ ] Every identifiable person is in the cast inventory, including peripheral ones.
- [ ] Every claim about another person carries a provenance tag.
- [ ] No inference about another person's mind is stated as fact.
- [ ] Each person has a handling and a recorded writer decision.
- [ ] Every crime, misconduct, health, minor, or `[told]`-only claim about a living
      person is marked `[LEGAL REVIEW]`.
- [ ] The author's note matches the changes actually made.
- [ ] No legal conclusion is stated; no fact is supplied by the prompt.

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **Consent is not a veto by default.** Memoir about one's own life does not
   generally require others' permission; asking is an ethical choice the writer makes.
2. **Disguise is not protection.** A changed name does not stop family and
   neighbours from recognising someone, and can implicate an innocent lookalike.
3. **Unflattering is not defamatory.** Truthful accounts of the writer's own
   experience, fairly framed, are the genre; flag false or unsupported factual claims,
   not hard truths.
4. **Composites are not neutral.** Acceptable in memoir only when disclosed; never
   in journalism.
5. **Death changes but does not end the question.** Defamation claims for the dead
   are generally limited in many jurisdictions `[VERIFY by jurisdiction]`, but
   living relatives' privacy and feelings remain.
6. **Do not moralise.** Raise each concern once; the writer decides.

## Techniques Used

- **RT-23 Input Provenance Tagging** — every claim about another person tagged documented, remembered, told, or inferred.
- **GT-09 Flag-Once-Then-Defer** — each concern raised once with its reason; the writer's informed decision governs.
- **DD-05 Human Review Flags** — `[LEGAL REVIEW]` routing for statements that need counsel, not a model's opinion.
- **DP-04 Must-Not Constraints** — no invented facts, no legal conclusions, no softening of the writer's own experience.
