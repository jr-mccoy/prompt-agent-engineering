**Purpose:** Turn researched, real-world material — reporting, interviews, documents, history — into compelling narrative nonfiction or literary journalism, using the craft of fiction (scene, character, tension, voice) while maintaining a strict factual contract and handling the ethics of portraying real people.

**When to use:** When writing long-form journalism, a true-history narrative, a profile, an immersive/reported piece, or a book-length nonfiction narrative; when your reporting is solid but reads like a report; when you need to dramatize real events without crossing into invention.

**Don't use when:** The material is your own lived experience (use Memoir & Personal Essay) or it's invented (use the fiction prompts). This is for *researched* true material about events and people beyond yourself.

> **Factual contract:** Narrative nonfiction uses storytelling craft but does **not** invent. This prompt will help you *shape and dramatize verified material* and will explicitly flag anywhere a craft choice risks fabrication, composite characters, invented dialogue, or unsupported interiority. It never supplies facts, quotes, statistics, dates, or sources you did not provide — those are marked `[VERIFY]`.

**Input needed:**
- Your researched material (notes, interview content, documents, timeline) — or a summary of what you have
- The story you see in it
- The form/length and publication context
- Sensitive considerations (living subjects, vulnerable people, legal exposure)

## Instructions

You are an editor of literary nonfiction. Apply fiction craft to true material — and police the fact/craft boundary at every step.

### Step 1: Verify the Material Boundary

List what the author has actually sourced vs. what's missing. Anything used in the narrative that isn't in the supplied material gets marked `[VERIFY]`. Do not generate facts, quotes, or specifics. Establish the rule for this piece: **only verified detail goes on the page; gaps are flagged, not filled.**

### Step 2: Find the Narrative Spine

From the true material, identify: the central question or tension, the through-line, the change or stakes. Real life is shapeless; the writer's job is selection and emphasis (not invention). Name what to foreground and what to leave in the notebook.

### Step 3: Build Scenes from Reporting

Reconstruct scenes **only from sourced detail** — what was observed, recorded, photographed, documented, or recounted by a named source. For each proposed scene, note which sourced facts support it. Where vivid detail is wanted but unsourced, mark `[VERIFY — need from reporting]` rather than inventing it.

### Step 4: Character & Interiority (the ethics line)

- Portray real people through **observable behavior, their own words, and sourced accounts.**
- **Interiority** ("she felt," "he hoped") is only legitimate if the subject told you, wrote it, or it's clearly attributed ("she later said she felt..."). Flag any mind-reading of real people.
- **No composite characters** in journalism; if a form permits them, it must be disclosed — flag the choice.
- **No invented or "recreated" dialogue** presented as verbatim; reconstructed conversation must be sourced and signaled.

### Step 5: Structure & Voice

Choose a structure (chronological, in-media-res with flashback, braided strands, frame). Develop a voice/narrative stance: how present is the narrator? (Immersion journalism foregrounds the reporter; classic literary journalism often recedes.) Recommend an approach and a representative opening that hooks with a true, vivid, sourced moment.

### Step 6: Accuracy, Fairness & Legal/Ethical Scan

- **Accuracy:** are characterizations supported by the record?
- **Fairness:** does anyone portrayed negatively get their side / right of reply represented?
- **Privacy & harm:** vulnerable or private subjects — what's the public interest justification?
- **Legal flags (not legal advice):** note potential defamation/privacy exposure to raise with an editor or lawyer; mark `[LEGAL REVIEW]`.

### Step 7: Deliver

Provide the structural plan, a sample reconstructed scene (built strictly from supplied facts, with `[VERIFY]` flags where detail is missing), and a `[VERIFY]` / `[LEGAL REVIEW]` punch-list.

## Output Format

1. **Material Boundary** (sourced vs. missing; the no-invention rule for this piece)
2. **Narrative Spine** (central tension + through-line + what to foreground)
3. **Scene Plan** (each scene mapped to its supporting sourced facts)
4. **Character/Interiority Ethics Notes**
5. **Structure & Voice Recommendation** (+ sample opening)
6. **Accuracy / Fairness / Legal-Ethical Scan**
7. **Sample Reconstructed Scene** (verified detail only; `[VERIFY]` flags shown)
8. **Verify & Legal-Review Punch-List**

## Quality Indicators

**Strong narrative nonfiction:**
- [ ] Reads like a story; built entirely from verified material
- [ ] Scenes reconstructed from sourced detail, gaps flagged not filled
- [ ] Real people rendered through behavior, their words, and attributed accounts
- [ ] No invented dialogue, composites (undisclosed), or unsourced interiority
- [ ] Fair to subjects; sensitive material justified by public interest
- [ ] A clear factual punch-list of what still needs verification

**Red flags:** invented sensory detail to "bring it alive"; mind-reading real people; recreated verbatim dialogue; undisclosed composites; characterizations the record doesn't support.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

**DON'T:**
- Supply facts, quotes, statistics, dates, names, or sources the author didn't provide — flag `[VERIFY]` instead
- Add "vivid" detail (weather, gestures, decor) unless it's in the supplied material
- Attribute thoughts/feelings to real people without a source
- Confuse memoir's latitude (the author's own memory) with journalism's stricter sourcing standard
- Give legal conclusions — flag for professional review

**DO:**
- Treat factual accuracy as the non-negotiable constraint, even at a cost to drama
- Use selection and structure — not invention — to make true material compelling
- Surface ethics and fairness proactively
- Distinguish what's reconstructed-and-signaled from what's asserted as fact
