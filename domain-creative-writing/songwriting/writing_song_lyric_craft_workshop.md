# Song Lyric Craft Workshop

**Objective:** Help a songwriter get from an idea to a complete, singable first
draft of an original lyric — one central idea, a title that earns its
repetitions, verses that move the story or feeling forward, and a chorus the
listener can carry out of the room.

**When to Use:**
- You have a title, a line, a feeling, or a situation, and no song yet.
- You write melodies easily and lyrics come out generic ("fire / desire").
- You are a poet or prose writer trying songs and the words sound good on the
  page but fall apart when sung.

**Not this prompt if:**
- You have a full draft and it doesn't sit on the melody, the hook lands in the
  wrong place, or sections repeat each other's job →
  `domain-creative-writing/songwriting/writing_song_structure_lyric_revision.md`.
- You are writing a poem to be read → `domain-creative-writing/poetry/writing_poetry_craft_and_forms.md`.
  Distinct from it: songs rely on repetition, the ear, and a melody that
  controls stress and length; poems carry their own music on the page.
- You want a picture-book or children's rhyme → `domain-childrens-writing/`.

> **Originality guard:** this workshop writes original lyrics only. It may
> quote a *brief* line from a published song to analyse a technique, with the
> song and writer named, but never reproduces a copyrighted lyric in full or
> in substantial part, and never writes "in the exact words of" an existing
> song. Style influence is fine; copying is not.

## Inputs

1. **The seed**: a title, a line, a situation, or a feeling — whatever exists.
2. **Genre and feel** (e.g. country ballad, indie folk, pop, hip-hop hook, worship).
3. **Music status**: melody already written? chord progression? tempo/groove?
   Or lyric first?
4. **Who is singing to whom**, if known.
5. **What the writer has tried** and why it didn't work.
6. **Reference songs** for feel (titles only; the workshop will not reproduce them).

## Method

1. **Frame the song (CM-01).** Restate genre, feel, tempo, and whether melody
   exists. A lyric for a 70 bpm ballad has room for longer lines than one for a
   120 bpm pop chorus; name that constraint before writing.
2. **Find the central idea in one sentence.** "A woman returns the ring by mail
   because she can't say it to his face." If the idea needs two sentences, it
   is two songs.
3. **Find or test the title.** A title works when it (a) is singable on a
   stressed beat, (b) can mean slightly more each time it returns, (c) is
   concrete or surprising. Offer 3–5 candidates and say why each would or
   would not hold a chorus.
4. **Choose point of view and a concrete world.** First/second/third person;
   the time and place; 5–8 specific objects, actions, or details the song can
   use ("the ring box," "a stamp with a flag"). Abstractions ("pain", "love")
   go on a banned list for verses.
5. **Assign section jobs (ST-02).** Verse 1: situation. Chorus: the title and
   the feeling that sums it up. Verse 2: development — new information, not a
   restatement. Bridge (optional): the turn — a new angle, time, or admission.
   Final chorus: same words, changed meaning.
6. **Draft to the ear (CM-02).** Constraints while drafting:
   - Sing or speak every line aloud; mark the stressed syllables.
   - Match line lengths across parallel sections (verse 1 line 1 ≈ verse 2 line 1).
   - Prefer open vowels on long or high notes.
   - Rhyme serves meaning: allow near and family rhyme; never pick a word
     *only* because it rhymes.
7. **Iterate in passes (DT-03).** Draft 1 fast. Then: a concreteness pass
   (replace abstractions), a "does verse 2 move?" pass, and a title-placement
   pass. Show what changed and why.
8. **Self-check (QA-01)** against the Verification list; hand off to the
   revision prompt for prosody work.

## Output Format

```
# [Working title] — first draft
Frame: [genre · feel · tempo · melody status]
Central idea: [one sentence]
Title candidates: [3–5, with why/why not]  → chosen: [...]
POV & world: [person · place · time] — detail bank: [...]  Banned abstractions: [...]

## Section map
| Section | Job | New information it adds |

## Draft 1 lyric (original)
[Verse 1] … [Chorus] … [Verse 2] … [Bridge] … [Chorus]

## Pass notes
Concreteness · movement · title placement — what changed and why

## Open questions for the writer
## Hand-off: what to take to the revision prompt
```

## Verification

- [ ] The central idea fits in one sentence.
- [ ] The title appears in the chorus on a stressed position.
- [ ] Verse 2 contains information verse 1 did not.
- [ ] Every verse line contains a concrete noun or action, or is deliberately abstract.
- [ ] Parallel lines in parallel sections are roughly the same syllable count.
- [ ] No published lyric is reproduced beyond a brief, attributed analytic quote.

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **Perfect rhyme is not quality.** Forcing "heart/apart" at the cost of the
   sense is the commonest amateur tell; near rhyme is standard in professional
   writing.
2. **A clever line is not a chorus.** Choruses need to be singable and
   repeatable; wit belongs in the verses more often than in the hook.
3. **Page-poetry density does not sing.** Lines packed with consonant
   clusters and multi-clause syntax will be unsingable at tempo.
4. **Don't impose a genre's conventions the writer didn't ask for.** A folk
   song does not need a pre-chorus; a hip-hop hook does not need rhyme on the
   line end.
5. **Don't write the song for them.** Offer lines as options and explain the
   craft choice; the writer's voice and details come first.
6. **Style reference is not permission to copy.** Never rewrite a known
   lyric with swapped nouns.

## Example Output

```
# "Return to Sender (Postage Paid)" — first draft
Frame: country ballad · 72 bpm · lyric first, melody to follow
Central idea: she mails the engagement ring back because she can't say it to his face.
Title candidates: "Return to Sender" (strong image, but a famous title — too
familiar); "Postage Paid" (concrete, ironic: she paid for the ending) ✓;
"Small Box" (quiet, might suit a bridge line).  → chosen: "Postage Paid"
POV & world: first person, small-town post office, a Tuesday in March.
Detail bank: ring box, bubble wrap, flag stamp, the clerk who knows her mother,
his mother's cursive on the return address.  Banned: pain, forever, heart.

## Section map
| Section | Job | New information |
|---|---|---|
| V1 | Situation | At the counter with the box |
| Chorus | Title + feeling | She paid so she wouldn't have to say it |
| V2 | Development | Why not in person: she'd say yes again |
| Bridge | Turn | The clerk asks "anything fragile?" |
| Final chorus | Changed meaning | Same words, now about her |

## Draft 1 lyric (original)
[Verse 1]
Bubble wrap and a flag stamp, Tuesday line is long
Mrs. Carter at the counter hums her radio song
I wrote your mama's address 'cause I can't write yours clean
It's the smallest box they sell, and it's the biggest thing

[Chorus]
Postage paid, postage paid
Three dollars and change for the promise we made
No goodbye at your door, no scene, no parade
Just a box in a bin and the postage paid

[Verse 2]
If I drove out to your place I know just what I'd do
I'd stand there on the porch and I'd say yes to you
So I'm letting the mail truck make the trip I can't make
There's a mercy in a stranger's hands for heaven's sake

[Bridge]
She says, "Anything fragile?" and I almost laugh
Honey, only everything, just not in the bag

[Chorus — final]
Postage paid, postage paid …

## Pass notes
Concreteness: V1 "sad goodbye" → "biggest thing" (the box carries the feeling).
Movement: V2 originally repeated V1's post office; rewritten to give the reason.
Title placement: title opens and closes the chorus, both on the downbeat.

## Open questions
Is "for heaven's sake" your voice or filler? Does the bridge answer need a
second line or should it stay a single-beat punch?

## Hand-off
Take to revision: stress on "po-STAGE" vs "POST-age" once the melody exists;
V2 line 4 syllable count runs long against V1 line 4.
```

## Techniques Used

- **CM-01 Explicit Context Framing** — genre, tempo, and melody status set before drafting.
- **CM-02 Constraint Specification** — sing-aloud, line-length, and rhyme-serves-meaning rules.
- **ST-02 Structured Sequential Instructions** — idea → title → world → section jobs → draft.
- **DT-03 Iterative Refinement** — concreteness, movement, and title passes.
- **QA-01 Self-Verification** — checklist including the originality guard.
