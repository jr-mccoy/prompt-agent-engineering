# Co-Writing Session Workflow and Split Sheet

**Objective:** Help two or more songwriters run a productive co-write and leave the
room with a finished work tape, a lyric sheet, and a signed split sheet — so the song
can be pitched, recorded, and registered without a later argument about who wrote
what and who owns how much.

**When to Use:**
- You are about to co-write with someone new and want the session and the
  conversation about splits planned in advance.
- You have finished songs with friends and nothing was written down about ownership.
- A producer, artist, or topliner joined the session and you are unsure how their
  contribution is treated.
- A song is about to be released or pitched and the writers need their registration
  details aligned.
- **Not this prompt if** you need help writing the song's words — use
  `domain-creative-writing/songwriting/writing_song_lyric_craft_workshop.md` (drafting)
  or `songwriting/writing_song_structure_lyric_revision.md` (prosody and structure).
  If the question is a disputed ownership claim or a publishing or label contract,
  this prompt only organises the facts — use an attorney and
  `domain-legal/contracts-transactional/legal_contract_review_full_redline.md`. For a
  business partnership or band-company equity split, use
  `domain-negotiation/contexts/negotiation_partnership_equity_split.md`.

> **Not legal advice; verify organisation rules.** Copyright, publishing, and
> collection rules differ by country and change. Names of collecting organisations
> below are examples; their membership, registration steps, and share rules must be
> confirmed with the organisation `[VERIFY]`. Disputes and contracts → `[LEGAL REVIEW]`.
>
> **Originality guard:** as with the other songwriting prompts, no published lyric
> is reproduced; a sample or interpolation of someone else's song needs clearance
> and is flagged, never treated as covered by the split sheet.

## Inputs / Context

1. **Writers**: names, roles they usually play (lyrics, topline melody, track or
   chords, production), and whether one is the artist.
2. **Target**: an artist cut, a sync brief, a writer's own release, or a pitch.
3. **Each writer's details** for the split sheet: legal name, performing rights
   organisation (PRO) and IPI number if affiliated, publisher if any.
4. **Pre-existing material** anyone plans to bring (a title, a hook, a track).
5. **Split convention** each writer expects, if known.
6. **For songs already written without paperwork**: who was present, the dated
   voice memos or files, and what each person recalls contributing.

## Method

1. **Prep before the session (ST-02).** Each writer brings 2–3 titles or hooks.
   Agree the target and reference feel. Agree, in a message before the day, the
   split convention: an equal split among everyone who writes in the room
   (a convention widely followed in some co-writing communities) or a negotiated
   split by contribution. Agree who records the work tape and who keeps the files.
2. **Disclose pre-existing material.** A title, riff, or chorus written before the
   session is named at the start. The writers either fold it into the equal split
   or agree a different share for that writer — in writing, before work begins.
3. **Run the session.** A short catch-up; choose the idea; settle the title and
   central idea; assign rough roles without making them rigid; write; and stop
   with time for capture. Keep one shared lyric document with timestamps.
4. **Capture the work tape.** A simple voice-and-instrument recording of the
   whole song, plus the lyric sheet and chord chart, named with title, date,
   writers, and version (`PorchLight_2026-10-03_v1_JR-MC-PS.m4a`). Dated files are
   the record of when the song existed.
5. **Complete the split sheet before anyone leaves.** Fields: song title (and
   working or alternate titles); date and place of session; for each writer —
   legal name, role, PRO, IPI number, publisher and its PRO/IPI, ownership share;
   whether any samples or interpolations are used; signatures. Shares must sum to
   exactly 100%.
6. **Separate the two copyrights.** The split sheet covers the *composition*
   (music and lyrics). The *sound recording* (master) is owned under separate
   terms — producer fees or royalty points, label ownership — and does not belong
   on the songwriter split sheet. A producer who also writes music or lyrics in the
   room is a co-writer for that contribution; the share is agreed like any other.
7. **Explain the writer/publisher halves.** Under many collecting systems
   performance income is paid in a writer's share and a publisher's share; a writer
   without a publisher may collect both, often through their own publishing
   designation `[VERIFY with the PRO]`.
8. **Hand off registration (NE-20).** A short package for each writer: the signed
   split sheet, final lyric, work tape, and a task list — register the work with
   each writer's PRO, and with mechanical collection bodies where applicable (for
   example The MLC for US digital mechanicals) `[VERIFY current steps]`; who
   demos; who pitches; who notifies the others if the song is cut or changed.
9. **Hold the must-nots (DP-04).** The prompt must not decide a disputed share,
   must not state an organisation's rules as fact without a `[VERIFY]`, and must not
   let a later rewrite change shares without a new signed sheet. Any dispute,
   uncleared sample, or contract question is marked `[LEGAL REVIEW]` (DD-05).

## Output Format

```

Target: [...] · Writers: [...] · Split convention agreed: [equal | negotiated] (date)

## Pre-session agreements
## Pre-existing material disclosed
| Writer | Material | Agreed treatment |

## Session run-of-show
## Capture checklist (work tape, lyric sheet, chord chart, file names)

## Split sheet
| Writer (legal name) | Role | PRO | IPI | Publisher (PRO/IPI) | Share % | Signature |
Total: 100% · Samples/interpolations: [none | listed → LEGAL REVIEW]

## Not on this sheet: master / producer terms
## Registration hand-off (task · owner · due)
## [VERIFY] and [LEGAL REVIEW] items
```

## Verification

- [ ] The split convention was agreed before writing, with a date.
- [ ] Every pre-existing contribution is disclosed with an agreed treatment.
- [ ] Shares sum to exactly 100% and every writer has signed.
- [ ] Each writer's PRO, IPI, and publisher are filled in or marked "not affiliated".
- [ ] Master and producer terms are kept off the composition split sheet.
- [ ] Every organisation rule carries `[VERIFY]`; every dispute or sample `[LEGAL REVIEW]`.

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **Equal split is a convention, not a law.** It is common and avoids line-counting,
   but writers may agree otherwise; record what was agreed.
2. **Words are not worth more than music.** Do not default to a lyric/music split
   unless the writers chose one.
3. **A producer is not automatically a writer.** Engineering, arranging, and mixing
   are master contributions; writing melody, lyrics, or the musical bed may be
   composition contributions. Ask what was contributed.
4. **A split sheet is not a publishing deal.** It records ownership among writers;
   publishing, administration, and label terms are separate contracts.
5. **Later edits do not change shares on their own.** A rewrite by some writers
   needs a new, signed agreement.
6. **Recollections conflict.** For songs written without paperwork, lay out the
   evidence (dated files, messages) and let the writers agree; do not adjudicate.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-02 Structured Sequential Instructions** — prep → disclosure → session → capture → split sheet → hand-off, in order.
- **NE-20 Third-Party Handoff Package** — signed sheet, lyric, work tape, and task list packaged for publishers and collecting organisations.
- **DD-05 Human Review Flags** — disputes, samples, and contracts routed to `[LEGAL REVIEW]`.
- **DP-04 Must-Not Constraints** — no adjudicating shares, no unverified organisation rules, no share changes without a new signed sheet.
