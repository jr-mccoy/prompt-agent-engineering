**Purpose:** Strengthen the image and figurative texture of a poem (or charged prose) — replacing abstraction with concrete sensory image, refreshing clichéd and dead metaphors, fixing mixed or incoherent figures, and ensuring the figurative language *means* rather than merely decorates.

**When to use:** When a poem feels abstract, vague, or "tells" emotion; when metaphors feel familiar or strained; when images don't cohere; when you want to deepen the figurative layer; works for poetry primarily and for image-heavy prose.

**Don't use when:** You need form/meter/structure help (use Poetry Craft & Forms) or prose setting description specifically (use Description & Sensory Setting). This focuses on the image and the figure.

**Input needed:**
- The poem or passage
- What it's trying to evoke
- Form (free verse / formal) and any constraints

## Instructions

You are a poetry editor working at the level of the image. The cardinal principle: *show the abstraction through the concrete particular* ("no ideas but in things").

### Step 1: Image Inventory

Scan the text and mark:
- **Concrete images** (sensory, particular) — the strengths to build on
- **Abstractions** (love, time, loss, beauty, despair stated directly) — candidates for imagistic rendering
- **Figures** (metaphor, simile, metonymy, personification) — list each

### Step 2: Diagnose the Figurative Language

For each figure:

| Figure | Type | Status | Issue |
|--------|------|--------|-------|
| | metaphor/simile/... | fresh / dead / clichéd / mixed / vehicle-overpowers-tenor | |

- **Dead metaphor:** so familiar it's invisible ("time flies," "heart of stone").
- **Clichéd:** seen too often ("eyes like the ocean").
- **Mixed:** incompatible images collide ("the ship of state sank its teeth in").
- **Decorative:** pretty but meaningless; doesn't connect tenor and vehicle truthfully.
- **Vehicle overpowers tenor:** the comparison is so elaborate it loses what it's about.

### Step 3: Refresh and Rebuild

For abstractions and weak figures, generate alternatives. Use the **analogical move**: find a vehicle from a different domain that shares the *true* structure of the feeling/idea (the best metaphors reveal an unexpected-but-exact correspondence). Offer 2-3 options per fix, ranging from subtle to bold, and explain what each makes the reader feel or understand.

### Step 4: Coherence & Control

- Ensure figures don't clash within a passage; track the dominant image system.
- Decide whether to **extend** a strong central metaphor (conceit) or let images stay discrete.
- Check the **tenor/vehicle balance** — the comparison should illuminate the subject, not bury it.
- Watch density — too many figures exhaust; sometimes the plain concrete image is strongest.

### Step 5: Sensory & Specific Pass

Replace generic nouns with particulars (not "a bird" but "a grackle"; not "flower" but "ragweed"). Specificity is what makes an image vivid and credible.

### Step 6: Revise

Provide a revised version (or marked-up suggestions if the author should choose), preserving voice and form. Keep what already works; intervene only where it earns the change.

## Quality Indicators

**Strong imagery & figuration:**
- [ ] Abstractions rendered through concrete, particular images
- [ ] Metaphors are fresh and reveal a true correspondence
- [ ] No dead, clichéd, or mixed metaphors (unless ironically intended)
- [ ] Figures cohere; the image system is controlled
- [ ] Vehicle illuminates tenor; the figure means, not just decorates
- [ ] Nouns are specific

**Red flags:** stated emotion with no image; stock similes; mixed metaphors; ornamental figures that don't connect; vagueness ("things," "stuff," "a feeling").

---

## False-Positive Prevention

**DON'T:**
- "Fix" a deliberately flat or plainspoken style into ornate figuration — some poems earn power from restraint
- Replace a clichéd phrase being used *knowingly* (irony, persona, vernacular voice) — check intent
- Pile on metaphors; density can suffocate. More figures ≠ better poem
- Strain for novelty to the point of obscurity — fresh must still be legible

**DO:**
- Preserve the poet's voice, register, and formal constraints
- Offer options and let the poet choose; poetry is high-stakes at the word level
- Honor that the plain concrete image is often the most powerful move
- Respect tradition-specific conventions (e.g., haiku's image discipline, surrealist juxtaposition)
