**Purpose:** Create complex, believable characters with depth, motivation, internal conflict, and clear character arcs for fiction writing.

**When to use:** When starting a new story and need to develop protagonists, antagonists, or supporting characters; when existing characters feel flat or one-dimensional.

**Input needed:**
- Genre and tone of your story
- Character's role (protagonist, antagonist, supporting)
- Basic concept or starting idea for the character
- Story themes (optional but helpful)

---

## Your Input

**Genre/Tone:** [e.g., Fantasy/Epic, Thriller/Dark, Romance/Light, Literary Fiction]
**Character Role:** [Protagonist / Antagonist / Supporting / Mentor / Love Interest]
**Basic Concept:** [Brief description - e.g., "A detective who lost their partner" or "A princess who refuses to marry"]
**Story Themes:** [Optional - What larger ideas does your story explore?]
**POV:** [Will this character narrate? First person? Close third?]

---

## Instructions

Develop a comprehensive character profile through these steps:

### Step 1: Core Identity Check

First, confirm understanding of the character concept and ask 2-3 clarifying questions if needed:
- What makes this character interesting to YOU?
- What's one thing this character would never do?
- What's one contradiction in this character?

### Step 2: External Identity

**The Surface Layer - What the World Sees:**

| Attribute | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Name** | Full name, nicknames, what they prefer to be called |
| **Age** | At story start, significant life stages |
| **Appearance** | 3 distinctive physical traits that reveal character |
| **Voice/Speech** | How they sound, vocabulary, verbal tics |
| **Occupation/Role** | What they do, their status |
| **First Impression** | What someone notices in 30 seconds |

### Step 3: Internal Landscape

**The Deeper Layer - What Drives Them:**

**Core Want (External Goal):**
What are they actively pursuing? This is the plot-level goal.

**Core Need (Internal Goal):**
What do they actually require for fulfillment? Often they don't recognize this.

**The Gap:**
How do want and need conflict? This tension drives character arc.

**Greatest Fear:**
What terrifies them? This should connect to their wound and create obstacles.

**The Lie They Believe:**
What false belief about themselves or the world holds them back?

**The Wound:**
What past experience created the lie? This is the backstory that shaped them.

**The Ghost:**
How does the wound manifest in present behavior?

### Step 4: Behavioral Patterns

**How the Internal Becomes External:**

**Under Stress:**
- First reaction (instinctive)
- Second reaction (considered)
- What calms them down

**In Relationships:**
- How they show affection
- How they push people away
- What they hide from others

**Decision-Making:**
- Head vs. heart tendency
- Impulsive vs. deliberate
- What overrides their normal pattern

### Step 5: Character Arc Design

**The Transformation Journey:**

| Arc Stage | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Starting State** | Who they are at chapter 1 (defined by the Lie) |
| **Catalyst** | What forces them to confront the Lie |
| **Resistance** | How they fight against change |
| **Mirror Moment** | When they see themselves clearly |
| **Choice** | The decision that proves change |
| **Ending State** | Who they become (or fail to become) |

### Step 6: Voice Sample

Write a brief internal monologue (100-150 words) showing:
- How this character thinks
- Their distinctive voice
- One key concern or desire

### Step 7: Scene Recommendations

Suggest 3 scenes that would effectively reveal this character:
1. **Establishing scene:** First impression that hooks readers
2. **Depth scene:** Moment that shows their vulnerability or contradiction
3. **Defining scene:** Choice that reveals their true nature

---

## Output Format

Structure the response as:
1. **Character Snapshot** (1 paragraph summary)
2. **External Identity Table**
3. **Internal Landscape** (detailed breakdown)
4. **Behavioral Patterns**
5. **Character Arc Map**
6. **Voice Sample**
7. **Recommended Scenes**
8. **Craft Notes** (what makes this character work, potential pitfalls to avoid)

---

## Quality Indicators

**Strong character development includes:**
- [ ] Clear contradiction (strength that's also a flaw)
- [ ] Want/Need gap creates internal tension
- [ ] Wound explains behavior without excusing it
- [ ] Voice is distinctive and consistent
- [ ] Arc has clear before/after states
- [ ] Character serves story themes

**Avoid:**
- Characters defined only by their trauma
- Flaws that never cause real problems
- Perfect characters with "quirks"
- Backstory that doesn't connect to present story
- Inconsistent behavior without explanation
