**Purpose:** Analyze and improve dialogue to make it more distinctive, revealing, and engaging while maintaining natural speech patterns and appropriate subtext.

**When to use:** When dialogue feels stilted, on-the-nose, or when all characters sound alike; when conversations don't advance plot or reveal character.

**Input needed:**
- The dialogue passage to improve
- Brief context about the characters
- What the scene needs to accomplish
- Genre and tone

## Instructions

Analyze and enhance the dialogue through these steps:

### Step 1: Diagnostic Analysis

Evaluate the current dialogue against these criteria:

| Criterion | Assessment | Issue |
|-----------|------------|-------|
| **Distinctiveness** | Can you tell who's speaking without tags? | [Yes/No - why] |
| **Subtext** | Is there unspoken meaning beneath the words? | [Yes/No - what's missing] |
| **Purpose** | Does every line advance plot or reveal character? | [Yes/No - which lines] |
| **Natural Flow** | Does it sound like real speech (for the genre)? | [Yes/No - issues] |
| **Conflict/Tension** | Do characters want different things? | [Yes/No - what] |

### Step 2: Character Voice Profiles

For each character, establish:

**Character A Voice:**
- Vocabulary level: [Simple / Average / Elaborate]
- Sentence pattern: [Short and punchy / Long and flowing / Mixed]
- Verbal habits: [Repeated words, filler phrases, unique expressions]
- What they avoid saying: [Topics, words, emotions]
- Subtext tendency: [Direct / Indirect / Evasive]

**Character B Voice:**
(Same categories)

### Step 3: Subtext Layer

For each exchange, identify:

| Said | Meant | Unspoken Motivation |
|------|-------|---------------------|
| "[Line]" | "[What they really mean]" | "[Why they can't say it directly]" |

If subtext is missing, suggest what could be hidden beneath:
- Competing agendas
- Hidden knowledge
- Suppressed emotions
- Power dynamics
- Lies or omissions

### Step 4: Dialogue Techniques

Apply relevant techniques:

**For flat dialogue:**
- **Oblique responses:** Characters don't answer directly
- **Interruptions:** Lines cut off naturally
- **Trailing off:** Unfinished thoughts
- **Topic avoidance:** Redirecting uncomfortable subjects

**For "on-the-nose" dialogue:**
- **Show, don't tell feelings:** Actions instead of declarations
- **Contradiction:** Say one thing, mean another
- **Displacement:** Talk about something else entirely
- **Silence:** What's NOT said matters

**For sameness:**
- **Contrasting rhythms:** One character short, one long
- **Vocabulary gaps:** Different education/background shows
- **Verbal tics:** Distinct speech habits
- **Interruption patterns:** Who talks over whom

### Step 5: Enhanced Version

Provide rewritten dialogue with:
- Distinct voices for each character
- Subtext beneath the surface
- Action beats integrated (not just dialogue tags)
- Appropriate pacing and rhythm

**Format:**
```
[Character A action beat] "Dialogue."
"Dialogue," [Character B response, showing internal reaction].
```

### Step 6: Before/After Comparison

Show specific improvements:

| Original Line | Problem | Enhanced Line | Why It's Better |
|---------------|---------|---------------|-----------------|
| "[Line]" | [Issue] | "[New line]" | [Improvement] |

### Step 7: Dialogue Tag Audit

Evaluate dialogue tags and beats:
- Replace said-bookisms with action beats where possible
- Ensure tags are invisible (he said/she said) or eliminated
- Add physical behavior that reveals emotion
- Remove redundant tags

## Quality Indicators

**Strong dialogue includes:**
- [ ] Each character sounds distinctly different
- [ ] Subtext operates beneath surface meaning
- [ ] Every line serves a purpose
- [ ] Conflict or tension drives the exchange
- [ ] Action beats reveal character
- [ ] Pacing matches emotional intensity

**Common Dialogue Problems:**

| Problem | Sign | Solution |
|---------|------|----------|
| **"As you know, Bob"** | Exposition in dialogue | Cut or move to action/narration |
| **Talking heads** | No physical grounding | Add action beats and setting |
| **Overtagging** | "Said angrily," "whispered softly" | Show emotion through action |
| **Perfect articulation** | Characters say exactly what they mean | Add hesitation, indirection |
| **Monologuing** | Long uninterrupted speeches | Add interruptions, reactions |

---

## False-Positive Prevention

**DON'T:**
- Make dialogue "realistic" by adding filler (um, uh) unless purposeful
- Over-apply dialect or accent markers (a little goes far)
- Remove all direction for obliqueness's sake
- Add subtext where directness serves the scene better
- Give characters unique verbal tics that become annoying

**DO:**
- Preserve clear communication when the scene requires it
- Balance naturalism with readability
- Match dialogue style to genre expectations
- Use silence and non-response deliberately
- Read dialogue aloud to test flow
