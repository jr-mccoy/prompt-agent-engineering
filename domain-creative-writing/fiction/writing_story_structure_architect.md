**Purpose:** Design a complete story structure with proper setup, escalating conflict, meaningful midpoint, satisfying climax, and resonant resolution.

**When to use:** When planning a new story, novel, or screenplay; when a draft feels aimless or lacks momentum; when stuck in the "sagging middle."

**Input needed:**
- Genre and target length
- Core concept or premise
- Main character(s) and their goals
- Theme (optional but helpful)

**Genre:** [e.g., Mystery, Fantasy, Literary Fiction, Thriller, Romance]
**Target Length:** [Short story / Novella / Novel / Screenplay]

**Protagonist:** [Brief description with main goal]
**Antagonist/Opposition:** [What opposes the protagonist]
**Theme:** [Optional - What question does this story explore?]

## Instructions

Architect your story structure through these steps:

### Step 1: Premise Validation

Before structuring, verify the premise has:
- **A protagonist** with a clear, urgent goal
- **Opposition** that creates genuine obstacles
- **Stakes** that matter (what's lost if they fail?)
- **A ticking clock** or escalating pressure (optional but powerful)

If any element is weak, flag it and suggest strengthening.

### Step 2: Choose Structure Framework

Select the most appropriate structure for this story:

| Framework | Best For | Key Features |
|-----------|----------|--------------|
| **Three-Act** | Most stories | Setup → Confrontation → Resolution |
| **Save the Cat** | Commercial fiction | 15 beats, clear emotional turns |
| **Hero's Journey** | Adventure, fantasy | 12 stages, mythic resonance |
| **Seven-Point** | Plot-driven stories | Hook → Plot Turns → Resolution |
| **Kishotenketsu** | Twist-focused, literary | Setup → Development → Twist → Reconciliation |

Recommend the best fit with reasoning.

### Step 3: Act-by-Act Breakdown

**ACT ONE: Setup (Approximately 25%)**

| Beat | Purpose | Your Story |
|------|---------|------------|
| **Opening Image** | Establish tone, show "before" state | [Specific scene] |
| **Ordinary World** | Protagonist's normal life, what's missing | [Setting, situation] |
| **Inciting Incident** | The disruption that starts everything | [The event] |
| **Debate/Refusal** | Protagonist hesitates | [Their resistance] |
| **First Plot Point** | Commitment to the journey | [Point of no return] |

**ACT TWO: Confrontation (Approximately 50%)**

| Beat | Purpose | Your Story |
|------|---------|------------|
| **Fun and Games** | Promise of the premise | [The "trailer moments"] |
| **B-Story** | Subplot that reflects theme | [Secondary story] |
| **Midpoint** | False victory OR false defeat | [The shift] |
| **Bad Guys Close In** | Complications intensify | [Rising obstacles] |
| **All Is Lost** | Lowest point | [The death/loss] |
| **Dark Night** | Processing, realization | [What they learn] |

**ACT THREE: Resolution (Approximately 25%)**

| Beat | Purpose | Your Story |
|------|---------|------------|
| **Break Into Three** | New plan, internal change | [The decision] |
| **Finale** | Climactic confrontation | [The showdown] |
| **Final Image** | Show "after" state | [Resolution scene] |

### Step 4: Tension Mapping

Create a tension graph for each act:

```
Tension Level:
High    |           *CLIMAX*
        |          /
        |    *MP* /
Medium  |   /    \_____/
        |  /
Low     |_/
        ├──────────────────────────────────
        Act 1    |    Act 2    |    Act 3
```

Identify:
- **Tension peaks** (at least 3-4 major moments)
- **Breathing room** (where readers can recover)
- **Escalation pattern** (each peak higher than last)

### Step 5: Scene Sequence

List 10-15 key scenes in order:

| # | Scene | Purpose | Tension |
|---|-------|---------|---------|
| 1 | [Scene] | [What it accomplishes] | Low/Med/High |
| 2 | [Scene] | [What it accomplishes] | Low/Med/High |
| ... | ... | ... | ... |

### Step 6: Subplot Integration

For each subplot, identify:
- **Connection to theme:** How it reflects the central question
- **Connection to main plot:** Where it intersects
- **Resolution timing:** When it concludes (before, during, or after main plot)

### Step 7: Pacing Recommendations

Based on genre and length:
- **Chapter/section breaks:** Where to place them
- **Scene length guidance:** When to expand, when to compress
- **Time jumps:** Where they can work

## Output Format

Structure the response as:

2. **Recommended Structure** (with reasoning)

4. **Tension Map** (visual representation)
5. **Key Scene Sequence** (10-15 scenes)

## Quality Indicators

**Strong structure includes:**
- [ ] Clear protagonist goal from Act 1
- [ ] Stakes that escalate throughout
- [ ] Midpoint that changes everything
- [ ] "All Is Lost" that feels genuinely dark
- [ ] Climax that requires protagonist's growth
- [ ] Resolution that reflects theme

**Common Structure Problems to Avoid:**
- **Sagging middle:** Add midpoint reversal and B-story
- **Anticlimactic ending:** Ensure climax is hardest challenge
- **Deus ex machina:** Solution must come from protagonist's growth
- **Rushed resolution:** Allow emotional payoff time
- **Missing stakes:** Ensure readers know what's at risk
