**Purpose:** Develop immersive, internally consistent fictional worlds with believable cultures, systems, and histories that serve your story's needs without overwhelming it.

**When to use:** When starting a fantasy, sci-fi, or speculative fiction project; when existing worldbuilding feels shallow or inconsistent; when the world needs to support specific plot elements.

**Input needed:**
- Genre and story premise
- One or two "big ideas" that make this world unique
- What the story needs from the world
- Scope (single city, nation, planet, multiverse)

**Genre:** [Fantasy / Sci-Fi / Alternate History / Post-Apocalyptic / Other]

## Instructions

Build your world systematically through these interconnected layers:

### Step 1: Core Premise Lock

Before expanding, nail down:

**The One Thing:** What single element makes this world fundamentally different from ours?

**Ripple Effects:** How does this one thing change:
- Daily life for ordinary people?
- Power structures and who holds them?
- What conflicts naturally arise?
- What the story can explore thematically?

**Consistency Rule:** All other worldbuilding should flow from this core premise.

### Step 2: Physical World

**Environment & Geography:**

| Element | Description | Story Impact |
|---------|-------------|--------------|
| **Terrain** | [Mountains, deserts, seas, etc.] | [How it affects plot/culture] |
| **Climate** | [Weather patterns, seasons] | [Daily life implications] |
| **Resources** | [Scarce and abundant] | [Economic/conflict drivers] |
| **Flora/Fauna** | [Unique species, if any] | [Danger, utility, symbolism] |
| **Map Sketch** | [Key locations] | [Travel times, borders] |

**For Sci-Fi/Space:**
- Star system configuration
- Planetary conditions
- FTL/travel mechanics and limitations
- Communication across distances

### Step 3: Social Structures

**Power & Governance:**
- Who rules and by what authority?
- How is power transferred or challenged?
- What institutions enforce order?
- Where are the cracks in the system?

**Social Hierarchy:**
- What classes or groups exist?
- How does one move between them?
- What creates status (wealth, birth, magic, skill)?
- Who is marginalized and why?

**Economic System:**
- What do people trade and why?
- Who controls key resources?
- What work do most people do?
- What does poverty/wealth look like?

### Step 4: Culture & Belief

**Religion/Philosophy:**
- What do people believe about existence/afterlife?
- How does belief influence daily behavior?
- Are there competing belief systems?
- What's sacred, profane, taboo?

**Cultural Markers:**
- Naming conventions
- Food and dining customs
- Clothing and status markers
- Art, music, entertainment
- Rites of passage

**Interpersonal Norms:**
- Family structures
- Courtship and relationships
- Hospitality expectations
- Conflict resolution

### Step 5: Technology & Magic System

**For Fantasy - Magic System:**

| Element | Specification |
|---------|---------------|
| **Source** | Where does magic come from? |
| **Access** | Who can use it and why? |
| **Cost** | What does using it take? |
| **Limits** | What can it NOT do? |
| **Rules** | What governs its behavior? |
| **Social Impact** | How does magic change society? |

**Sanderson's Laws Applied:**
1. **First Law:** Magic solving problems must be understood by readers
2. **Second Law:** Limitations > Powers (constraints create story)
3. **Third Law:** Expand what you have before adding new

**For Sci-Fi - Technology:**
- What tech exists and how does it work?
- What tech does NOT exist (and why)?
- Who has access to advanced tech?
- What problems has tech solved? Created?

### Step 6: History & Lore

**Essential History Only:**

Focus on history that impacts your story:

| Era | What Happened | Current Impact |
|-----|---------------|----------------|
| **Founding** | [Origin of this society] | [Myths, legitimacy claims] |
| **Golden Age** | [Peak period] | [What's been lost] |
| **Great Conflict** | [War/disaster] | [Ongoing tensions] |
| **Recent Past** | [Last generation] | [Living memory, current dynamics] |

**Avoid:** Detailed histories that don't serve the story.

### Step 7: Iceberg Principle

Apply the iceberg approach:

**Above Surface (Shown in Story):**
- Details directly relevant to plot
- Sensory worldbuilding (food, weather, texture)
- Information characters would know and notice

**Below Surface (Known to Writer):**
- Deeper systems that explain what's shown
- History that shapes character motivation
- Consistencies that make the world feel real

**Never Written:**
- Encyclopedic details no one needs
- History that doesn't connect to anything

### Step 8: Consistency Audit

Check for internal logic:

| Question | Answer | Verification |
|----------|--------|--------------|
| If [X exists], why doesn't [logical consequence]? | [Explanation] | [Check] |
| How does [system A] interact with [system B]? | [Interaction] | [Check] |
| What prevents [obvious exploit]? | [Limitation] | [Check] |

---

## Output Format

Structure the response as:
1. **Core Premise** (the one thing + ripple effects)
2. **Physical World Summary** (essential geography/environment)
3. **Social Structure Overview** (power, hierarchy, economy)
4. **Culture Snapshot** (belief, customs, norms)
5. **Magic/Technology System** (rules and limits)
6. **Essential History** (only what matters)
7. **Iceberg Notes** (what's implied vs. shown)
8. **Story Integration Points** (how this serves your specific plot)
9. **Consistency Checklist** (potential logic issues to address)

---

## Quality Indicators

**Effective worldbuilding:**
- [ ] Core premise is clear and unique
- [ ] Systems have limitations, not just powers
- [ ] Culture feels lived-in, not designed
- [ ] History connects to present story
- [ ] Details serve narrative, not encyclopedia
- [ ] World enables the story you want to tell

**Common Worldbuilding Pitfalls:**

| Pitfall | Sign | Solution |
|---------|------|----------|
| **Infodump disease** | Paragraphs of explanation | Show through character experience |
| **Magic solves everything** | No real obstacles | Strengthen limitations |
| **Monoculture planet** | One climate, one culture | Add regional variation |
| **History without impact** | Detailed lore readers skip | Cut or connect to plot |
| **Rule inconsistency** | Magic works differently each time | Document rules, follow them |
| **Copy-paste real world** | Fantasy Europe, Space America | Transform familiar through premise |
