# Preboarding and First-Week Plan

**Objective:** Make day one work — the laptop on, the accounts live, someone waiting,
the person's questions answered before they had to ask — and give week one a shape
that ends with something real finished, so the 30/60/90 starts on solid ground.

**When to Use:**
- An offer has been accepted and the start date is two to six weeks away.
- New hires keep spending day one without a working laptop or system access.
- Your first week is a stack of policy videos and nobody owns the new hire's calendar.
- **Not this prompt if** you need the ramp plan for days 1–90 against the role's
  outcomes — use `domain-hr-management/onboarding/hr_onboarding_thirty_sixty_ninety.md`
  (this plan ends where that one's week-one checkpoint begins). For whether the
  onboarding *programme* is working across many hires, use
  `domain-hr-management/onboarding/hr_onboarding_program_audit.md`. Offer terms and
  contract wording belong to
  `domain-legal/employment-labor/legal_employment_offer_and_separation_package.md`;
  customer onboarding is `domain-sales-customer/customer-success/`.

## Inputs / Context

1. **Role, start date, location** (on-site, hybrid, remote), and jurisdiction.
2. **What they need to work**: hardware, software licences, system access, building
   badge, phone or headset, desk — and who grants each.
3. **Lead times** for each item, from IT, facilities, and payroll (not guesses).
4. **People**: manager, buddy, and the three to five people they must meet in week one.
5. **A small real task** they could finish by Friday.
6. **Payroll and benefits cut-offs** that depend on the start date.

## Method

1. **Backward-schedule from the start date (DT-01).** List every item with its lead
   time and owner; the latest request date is start date − lead time − 2 working days
   of slack. Items whose latest request date has already passed are flagged now.
2. **Contact plan, accept to start.** Manager welcome within two working days of
   acceptance; a logistics message about a week out (time, place or link, who meets
   them, what to bring, how to reach someone on the morning); one optional informal
   touchpoint. **Do not ask for unpaid pre-start work**; any optional pre-reading is
   genuinely optional, and paid pre-start training follows local rules.
3. **Paperwork, flagged by jurisdiction.** Work authorisation, tax and payroll forms,
   policy acknowledgements — with the deadline each carries. Examples: in the US, Form
   I-9 Section 1 is completed by the first day of employment and Section 2 within three
   business days of it; in the UK, the right-to-work check is done before employment
   starts. *Verify against current rules with people ops or counsel.* Collect only what
   is required, through the system built for it.
4. **One neutral accommodations question, to everyone.** "Is there anything we should
   set up so day one works well for you?" Requests route to HR or people ops; the
   manager does not ask about health or medical details.
5. **Day one, hour by hour.** A human greeting; working equipment and access by mid-
   morning; lunch with the buddy or team; a 30–45 minute manager 1:1 that hands over the
   draft 30/60/90 and "what good looks like at 90 days"; an early, unhurried finish.
   Compliance training is spread across the week, not stacked on day one.
6. **Week one.** One small real task finishable by Friday; three to five introductions
   booked in calendars (not "reach out to people"); the buddy's daily 15 minutes; an
   end-of-week check-in with three questions — what was confusing, what is blocking you,
   what did we promise that has not happened.
7. **T−2 readiness check (DS-36).** Each item green or red. A red item gets a fallback
   (loaner laptop, temporary access, shadowing plan), an owner, and a candid note to the
   new hire. Escalate reds that block the first task.
8. **Self-audit table (DD-07).** Before sending, check the plan against the verification
   list below and record gaps rather than hiding them.

## Verification

- [ ] Every item has an owner, a lead time from its owner, and a latest request date.
- [ ] Items already past their latest request date are flagged with a fallback.
- [ ] No unpaid pre-start work is requested.
- [ ] Paperwork deadlines are flagged by jurisdiction with a "verify" note.
- [ ] The accommodations question is asked neutrally and routed to HR.
- [ ] Day one has working equipment by mid-morning, a manager 1:1, and a person waiting.
- [ ] Week one has one finishable real task and booked introductions.
- [ ] The T−2 check is dated, with fallbacks for every red item.

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **A checklist with no dates.** "IT to set up laptop" is not a plan; the lead time and
   latest request date are what make it happen.
2. **Silence between accept and start.** Weeks without contact invite second thoughts
   and counteroffers. A short, useful message beats a glossy welcome pack.
3. **Pre-start homework.** Asking for work before employment begins can be unpaid work
   and sets the wrong tone. Optional means optional.
4. **Day one as compliance day.** Six hours of videos teaches the person that this job
   is about forms. Spread it.
5. **Introductions left to the new hire.** "Reach out to anyone" produces no meetings.
   Book them.
6. **Hiding a red item.** If the laptop will be late, say so on the logistics message
   and give the fallback; discovering it at 9:00 on Monday costs more trust.
7. **Medical detail to the manager.** Accommodations go through HR; the manager learns
   what to set up, not why.

## Techniques Used

- **DT-01 Hierarchical Task Breakdown** — every day-one need backward-scheduled from the start date.
- **OC-03 Markdown Table Specification** — schedule, contact plan, day one, and week one as owned tables.
- **DS-36 Blocker Escalation Framework** — T−2 red items with fallback, owner, and candid notice.
- **DD-07 Self-Audit Table** — the plan checked against the verification list before it is sent.
