# Pantry-First Weeknight Cooking

**Objective:** Answer "what can I make with what I've got?" for tonight and the next few
weeknights — using the most perishable food first, staying inside the time you actually
have, and shopping for at most three things.

**When to Use:**
- It is 6 pm, the fridge is full of odds and ends, and you are about to order takeaway.
- Food keeps going off before you use it.
- Money is tight this week and you want to shop as little as possible.
- You have leftovers from a big cook and want them to become different meals.
- **Not this prompt if** you are planning the whole week and a shopping list from scratch —
  `home_meal_plan_week.md` (it plans forward; this one works backward from what is already
  there). If you cannot yet cook the basics, `home_learn_to_cook_progression.md` first. For
  *what* to eat for health, `domain-health-wellness/nutrition/nutrition_eating_pattern_audit.md`;
  medical diets go to a clinician or registered dietitian.

**Audience:** Anyone cooking for themselves or a household on a weeknight.

## Inputs / Context

1. **Inventory:** fridge, freezer, cupboard — with rough quantities and, for perishables,
   when opened or cooked and any use-by date. Name the actual items; "a stocked pantry" is
   not usable.
2. **Tonight's time:** active minutes available and when people need to eat.
3. **Next nights:** how many more dinners to cover before the next shop (usually 2–3).
4. **People and constraints:** how many eating; allergies and hard restrictions (hard
   constraints); strong dislikes (soft constraints).
5. **Equipment:** hob, oven, microwave, air fryer, rice cooker.
6. **Willingness to shop:** none, or a quick stop for a few items.

## Method

1. **Safety triage first (DS-26).** Discard before planning: cooked leftovers older than 3–4
   days; anything left out more than 2 hours; mould on soft foods (bread, soft cheese, yoghurt,
   berries, cooked dishes) — throw away; small mould on hard cheese or firm vegetables — cut at
   least 1 inch (2.5 cm) around it. When in doubt, throw it out. Raw meat or fish past its
   use-by date is not "used up" — it goes. (US food-safety guidance — check your country's.)
2. **Sort by urgency (DS-06).** **Today:** fish, raw meat or poultry at or near its date,
   cooked leftovers at day 3, soft herbs and salad leaves, opened cream or milk near its date.
   **This week:** most vegetables, eggs, opened jars, cheese. **Stable:** frozen, tinned, dry
   goods, condiments. Tonight's dinner must use at least one "today" item.
3. **Match to templates.** Templates are forgiving frames that absorb odds and ends:
   - *Fried rice / noodles* — day-old rice + egg + any veg + soy or other sauce (15–20 min).
   - *Frittata / omelette* — eggs + wilting greens + cheese ends + leftover potato (20 min).
   - *Grain or salad bowl* — grain + roast or raw veg + protein + dressing (15 min with cooked grain).
   - *Pasta* — pasta + tinned tomato or oil and garlic + veg + cheese (20 min).
   - *Soup* — onion + stock + any veg + tinned beans or lentils (30–40 min, makes lunches).
   - *Quesadilla / wrap* — tortillas + beans or leftover meat + cheese + salsa (10 min).
   - *Sheet-pan* — protein + hardy veg + oil and spice, one tray (35–45 min, mostly hands-off).
4. **Fit the time and constraints (CM-02).** Tonight's dish fits the stated active minutes.
   Allergies are never "probably fine"; check labels of sauces and stocks. Cook raw meat and
   poultry to safe temperatures (poultry 165°F / 74°C; ground meat 160°F / 71°C).
5. **Plan the next nights.** Use what tonight's cooking creates (extra rice, roast veg,
   half a tin of beans) as tomorrow's starting point. Move a "today" item you cannot cook
   tonight into the freezer now if it freezes well.
6. **Write the gap list.** At most three items that unlock more than one meal. If a meal needs
   more than three bought items, pick a different template.
7. **Grow the base pantry.** Note what was missing that would have made tonight easier (stock,
   a tin of tomatoes, eggs, tortillas, a hot sauce) — the restock list for next time.
8. **Check (QA-01).** Every "today" item is used, frozen or discarded; no allergen conflict;
   tonight fits the time.

## Output Format

```
# Pantry-first dinners — [date], [n] people, [minutes] tonight, [n] nights to cover
## Discard now (safety)
## Use-first triage | Today | This week | Stable |
## Tonight: [dish] — template · uses · steps (short) · time
## Next nights | Night | Dish | Uses | Prep tonight that helps |
## Freeze now
## Gap list (≤3 items)
## Base pantry additions for next shop
```

## Verification

- [ ] Discard rules applied before planning; doubts resolved by discarding.
- [ ] Tonight uses at least one "today" item and fits the active time.
- [ ] Every "today" item is used tonight, frozen, or discarded.
- [ ] Allergies and hard restrictions respected, with labels checked.
- [ ] Gap list has three items or fewer.
- [ ] No diet prescriptions.

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **"Still smells fine."** Smell does not detect the bacteria that cause most food poisoning;
   go by time and storage.
2. **Using up the safest food first.** Tins and frozen food can wait; plan around what spoils.
3. **A clever recipe that needs eight purchases.** That is a shopping trip, not pantry cooking.
4. **Leftovers eaten as the same meal three nights.** Transform them (roast veg → frittata →
   soup) so they actually get eaten.
5. **Allergen creep.** Stocks, sauces and spice mixes hide allergens; read the label.
6. **Ambition on a tired night.** A 10-minute quesadilla eaten beats a 45-minute plan abandoned.

## Example Output

```
# Pantry-first dinners — Tue 2026-10-06, 2 people, 25 min tonight, 3 nights to cover

## Discard now: Thai takeaway from Thursday (day 5); half a tub of hummus with mould
## Use-first triage
| Today | chicken thighs ×4 (use-by Wed); rice cooked Sunday (day 2); half bag spinach,
  wilting; coriander |
| This week | 5 eggs; 2 peppers; half onion; carrots; cheddar end; tortillas ×6 |
| Stable | frozen peas; tin chickpeas; tin tomatoes; soy sauce; cumin; chilli flakes; pasta |

## Tonight: egg fried rice with spinach and peas — rice + 3 eggs + spinach + peas + onion +
  soy + coriander. Hot pan, rice in two batches, eggs scrambled in last. 20 min.
## Next nights
| Wed | sheet-pan chicken thighs, chickpeas, peppers, carrots, cumin (to 165°F) | chicken,
  chickpeas, peppers | cut veg tonight while rice heats |
| Thu | chicken-and-cheddar quesadillas with salsa from tinned tomato + chilli | leftover
  chicken, tortillas, cheddar | roast 2 extra thighs Wed |
| Fri | tomato pasta with leftover roast veg | pasta, tin tomatoes, veg | — |
## Freeze now: nothing (chicken cooked Wed is within its date)
## Gap list: 1 lemon (rice + chicken), 1 jar salsa (optional), milk
## Base pantry additions: stock cubes; a second tin of chickpeas
```

## Techniques Used

- **DS-06 Prioritization and Severity Guidance** — today / this week / stable triage drives the order.
- **CM-02 Constraint Specification** — time ceiling, allergies, three-item gap limit.
- **DS-26 Safe Defaults Pattern** — discard-when-in-doubt and safe cooking temperatures.
- **QA-01 Self-Verification** — every urgent item accounted for before finishing.
