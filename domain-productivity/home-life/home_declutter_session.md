# Declutter Session Planner

**Objective:** Plan and execute a single, bounded declutter session on a specific room or area. Produces a session structure with time blocks, decision rules calibrated to the user's style, and concrete action lists for everything removed.

**When to use:** When you have a few hours and a specific area that's become unmanageable — not when you want to overhaul your entire home. Works best on areas small enough to complete in one session: a closet, kitchen counters, a storage shelf, a bathroom cabinet, a kids' toy area.

**Audience:** Adults who want to declutter a specific, bounded area in a single session. Works for all decision-making styles from fast-and-ruthless to careful-and-sentimental — the approach adjusts. Not for professional organizers or people planning a whole-house overhaul across multiple sessions.

---

## Inputs Required

1. **The specific area.** Be precise: not "the bedroom" but "the bedroom closet" or "the dresser top and nightstands." Not "the kitchen" but "the kitchen counters and the junk drawer." The narrower the scope, the more likely the session gets finished.

2. **Time available.** How long for the session: 30 minutes, 1 hour, 2 hours, half a day. This determines how much can be realistically tackled and whether the scope needs to be reduced.

3. **Decision-making style.** Pick the closest match — this calibrates the rules:
   - **Fast and clear:** "I want to cut ruthlessly. If I haven't used it in a year, it goes."
   - **Balanced:** "I want to be thoughtful but not spend 10 minutes deciding on a spatula."
   - **Sentimental:** "I know I hold onto things — I want rules that help me let go without feeling like I'm being forced."

4. **What the area currently contains (rough description).** What category of stuff is in here: clothes, kitchen tools, kids' toys, books, paperwork, random accumulated items ("junk drawer"). This shapes the item-specific rules.

5. **Any constraints or hard keeps.** Anything off-limits: "my partner's half of the closet," "my grandmother's china — not deciding today," "work documents — not touching." These are excluded from the session scope.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Scope and time-check

Map the stated area against the time available. A realistic estimate:
- Kitchen junk drawer: 30–45 minutes
- Single closet (clothes): 1–2 hours
- Kids' toy room: 2–3 hours
- Garage or basement section: half a day

If the area is too large for the time available, do one of two things:
- **Narrow the scope:** Propose a smaller sub-area to tackle in the time available. Better to finish a small area than partially tackle a large one.
- **Time-box it:** If the user wants to work on the full area anyway, set clear stopping rules so the session ends cleanly even if the work is incomplete.

### Step 2 — Set decision rules for this session

Based on the decision-making style and item category, define clear rules. Apply them consistently so the user doesn't re-negotiate every item.

**Standard decision tree:**

For each item encountered:
1. **Is it broken or expired?** → Trash. No debate.
2. **Is it a duplicate?** → Keep the best one. Donate or trash the rest. (Two identical spatulas, six phone chargers, three of the same toy.)
3. **Have I used this in the past 12 months?** → If yes, keep. If no, continue.
4. **Is it aspirational?** → An item purchased for a version of yourself that hasn't shown up yet. Ask: if you were starting fresh, would you buy this today? If no, donate.
5. **Is it sentimental?** → Apply the sentimental rule (see below).
6. **Does it belong here?** → If it belongs somewhere else in the home, don't decide whether to keep it — put it in the "relocate" pile.

**Sentimental item rules (adjusts by style):**
- Fast: One designated box per category (one box of childhood memorabilia total). If the box is full, something must come out to add something new.
- Balanced: Keep if it connects to a specific memory you can articulate. If you'd feel nothing if it disappeared tomorrow, let it go.
- Sentimental style: Give yourself a "keep for now" pile with a 30-day rule — if you haven't thought about the item in 30 days, it goes.

**Category-specific rules:**

*Clothes:* If it doesn't fit now (not "when I lose weight") → donate. If it's not been worn in 12 months (not "I keep it for special occasions" when you haven't worn it once in a year) → donate.

*Kitchen tools:* If you own a better version of it, donate the worse one. If it requires a special dish you make once a year, that's a keep — but be honest about frequency.

*Kids' toys:* Broken → trash. Outgrown and child shows no interest → donate. Multiple of the same type (10 stuffed animals, 6 bouncy balls) → thin to a defined number. Involve the child for things they care about; make the call yourself for things they won't notice.

*Books:* Read and won't re-read → donate to library. Reference books for a field you've left → donate. Aspirational reading pile older than 2 years → if you haven't picked it up, you won't.

*Paperwork:* Current and needed → keep in one organized pile. Tax documents (keep 7 years). Everything else: shred or recycle.

### Step 3 — Build the session plan

Structure the session into time blocks:

1. **Sort phase** (roughly 60–70% of the time): Pull everything out of the area and sort into four piles: Keep, Donate/Sell, Trash, Relocate. Do not organize while sorting — this is a decision phase only.

2. **Relocate phase** (5–10 min): Immediately take "relocate" items to their correct location in the home. Do not let this turn into a full tidying trip to other rooms.

3. **Purge phase** (5–10 min): Bag and remove trash. Stage donate items near the door (or directly in the car if possible).

4. **Return and organize phase** (remaining time): Put "keep" items back neatly. If more items fit neatly than before, the session succeeded. If not, something still needs to go.

Build a session timeline based on available time:
- 30 min session: sort only, skip return/organize (stage for a follow-up 15 minutes)
- 1 hour: full session as described
- 2 hours: can include a organize-in-place step after returning keeps

### Step 4 — Produce the action list

After sorting, produce three lists:

**Donate/Sell list:** What's going and where it should go. If anything has resale value, flag it (e.g., "Kids' Pottery Barn dresser — could sell on Facebook Marketplace, est. $50–$75"). Otherwise, batch items for a donation drop-off.

**Trash list:** What's being discarded. Note any items that require special disposal (electronics, paint, batteries, medications).

**Relocate list:** Items that are being kept but moved to a different room or area of the home — these still need a destination assigned.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Scope the session to what is actually completable in the stated time
- Apply consistent decision rules — no re-litigating on individual items during the sort phase
- Separate the relocate pile from the donate pile (keeping something vs. moving it are different decisions)
- Produce specific action lists, not vague categories
- Flag items requiring special disposal (electronics, chemicals, medications)

### Must Not
- Moralize about accumulating possessions or imply the user has a problem
- Reference Marie Kondo or any other specific framework as a revelation — assume the user has already heard of it
- Suggest organizing the area during the sort phase — sorting and organizing are different steps
- Let the session expand beyond the stated scope ("while you're at it, do the hallway closet too")
- Assign a donation destination without knowing what's actually available locally — leave destination as "donation drop-off TBD" unless the user specifies

---

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **Scope expansion mid-session:** The plan starts in the closet and creeps into the bedroom, then the hallway. The session ends with everything half-done. Scope is fixed at the start. If the user finds themselves wanting to expand, finish the original scope first.

2. **Decision paralysis during sort:** The user spends 20 minutes deciding on one item and loses the whole session. The decision rules from Step 2 are the answer to every item — refer back to them. If still stuck: put the item in a "decide later" bag and keep moving. After the session, apply the 30-day rule.

3. **Relocate pile that never moves:** Items flagged as "relocate" get put in a pile and then sit there for three weeks. The relocate step in the session plan is to immediately move these items — not stage them.

4. **Donate list with no next action:** Items are bagged for donation but the bag sits in the hallway for six months. The action list must include a specific next step: "drop off at [Goodwill / library / neighbor] by [day of this week]."

5. **Organize before purge:** The user starts putting things back neatly before the sort is done, meaning they're organizing around items that haven't been decided yet. The four-phase order from Step 3 is strict: sort fully first.

## Output Format

```
## Declutter Session Plan
### Area: [Specific area] | Time: [X hours] | Style: [Fast/Balanced/Sentimental]

---

### Scope Confirmation
[Confirmed area + any sub-areas excluded (partner's section, specific items off-limits)]

If time is insufficient for full area: [Recommended sub-scope for this session]

---

### Decision Rules for This Session

**Always trash:**
- Broken, expired, or unusable items (no exceptions)
- [Category-specific trash rules]

**Always donate:**
- [Category-specific donate rules based on item type]

**Sentimental items:**
- [Rule calibrated to user's style]

**If still stuck on an item:**
- [Specific tiebreaker rule]

---

### Session Timeline

| Phase | Time | What happens |
|-------|------|-------------|
| Sort | [X min] | Pull everything out, sort into 4 piles. No organizing. |
| Relocate | [X min] | Move "relocate" items to correct room now. |
| Purge | [X min] | Bag trash, stage donate near door or in car. |
| Return & organize | [X min] | Put keeps back. If it doesn't fit neatly, something still needs to go. |

---

### Action Lists

**Donate / Sell**
- [ ] [Item or category] → [Donation drop-off / sell on [platform] / offer to [person]] by [this week]
- [ ] [Items with resale value flagged] — Est. $[X]

**Trash**
- [ ] [Item] — standard trash
- [ ] [Item] — requires special disposal ([how: e-waste drop-off / hazmat / pharmacy return])

**Relocate**
- [ ] [Item] → belongs in [specific room or area]
- [ ] ...

---

### Notes
[Any specific callouts: items that needed a decision deferred to the 30-day rule, anything the user should come back to, follow-up step if session ran out of time]
```

## Verification

- [ ] Session scope fits within the stated time
- [ ] Decision rules are defined before the session starts, not during
- [ ] Four-pile method is the structure (keep, donate, trash, relocate)
- [ ] Relocate pile has a specific next action (move now, in the session)
- [ ] Donate list includes a specific next action and timing
- [ ] Special disposal items are flagged
- [ ] Aspirational items are addressed explicitly in the rules
- [ ] Session does not moralize or reference generic declutter systems as novelties
