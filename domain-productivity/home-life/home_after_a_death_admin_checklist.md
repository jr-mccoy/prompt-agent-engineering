# After a Death — Admin Checklist

**Objective:** Give the person handling things after a death a calm, ordered list — only what
matters now at the top, everything else parked with a date — with each task assigned, each
organisation tracked, and every legal, tax and funeral-specific question handed to the
professional whose job it is.

**When to Use:**
- Someone close has died — a parent, partner, sibling — and you, or a small group of you,
  are handling the practical side.
- You have been named as the person who settles the estate, or have simply ended up doing it.
- Weeks have passed and letters keep arriving; you need to see what is done and what is not.

**Not this prompt if:**
- You are planning your own or a parent's estate in advance — `domain-personal-development/major-decisions/personal_estate_wishes_attorney_prep.md`
  and `domain-finance/personal-finance-planning/finance_estate_beneficiary_review.md`.
- You are looking for help with grief itself — `domain-psychology/client-self-use/grief-loss/`
  (e.g. `clientself_complicated_vs_normal_grief_reflection.md`, `clientself_anniversary_reaction_plan.md`).
  If you are in crisis or thinking of harming yourself, contact local emergency services or a crisis line now.
- The death was by suicide — `domain-psychology/client-self-use/crisis-self-triage/clientself_after_suicide_loss_support.md` alongside this list.

**Audience:** A bereaved adult doing practical admin. Written to be read on a bad day: short
lines, one action each, nothing that needs to be done tonight unless it truly does.

## Inputs Required

1. **Relationship to the person** and whether you are the named or likely estate representative.
2. **Where and how they died** (home, hospital, care home; expected or sudden) — this changes
   the first steps and who is involved; keep it brief.
3. **Where they lived** (country/region) — procedures differ and every deadline is marked for verification.
4. **Known documents:** will, funeral wishes, pre-paid plan, the "where things are" sheet (`home_household_paperwork_system.md`) if one exists.
5. **Dependants, pets and property** needing immediate care or security.
6. **Who can help** and what each is good at.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Acknowledge, then shrink the list (NE-07)
Open with one or two sentences recognising the loss, then say plainly: most of this can wait.
Split every task into **now (48 hours)**, **soon (first weeks)** and **later (months)**.

### Step 2 — The first 48 hours (DS-06)
Only what cannot wait:
- The official steps after a death where they died — obtaining the medical certificate/
  pronouncement and registering the death — follow the hospital, care home, doctor or
  funeral director's guidance; mark local deadlines "[VERIFY locally]".
- Care of dependants and pets.
- Securing the home, vehicle and valuables if empty.
- Choosing a funeral director (check for a pre-paid plan or written wishes first).
- Telling close family and friends — delegate a caller list.

### Step 3 — Build the notification tracker
Organisations to tell, each with: who tells them, how, what they need (often a copy of the death
certificate — order several), date done, reference number. Typical groups: employer/pension
payer, government benefits and tax authority, banks and card issuers, insurers, utilities,
landlord or mortgage lender, subscriptions, doctors and dentist, professional bodies, digital
accounts. Where a government "tell everyone once" service exists, note it as "[check if available]".

### Step 4 — Hand off what is not yours to decide (OC-09)
A routing table: probate or estate administration, whether a will is valid, taxes of the
deceased and the estate, joint accounts, debts and who is liable, property sale → **estate
attorney / accountant**; funeral options and costs → **funeral director**. Never pay the
deceased's debts from your own money, or distribute belongings, before advice.

### Step 5 — Sequence the months (ST-02)
A later list with target months: estate valuation, final tax return, closing accounts, the
home, belongings, memorial or headstone, digital legacy. Date these loosely and mark
jurisdiction-specific deadlines "[VERIFY with attorney/accountant]" (QA-04).

### Step 6 — Share the load
Assign each task to a named helper by strength (a sibling good at phone calls takes the
notification tracker; one who lives nearby handles the home). Set a short weekly check-in.
Note who is doing too much.

## Constraints

### Must
- Put only genuinely time-critical items in the first 48 hours.
- Mark every legal, tax and registration deadline "[VERIFY]".
- Route estate, tax, debt and will questions to the relevant professional.
- Route grief and distress to psychology resources, and crisis to emergency help.

### Must Not
- State deadlines, fees, tax rules or legal duties as fact.
- Advise paying the deceased's debts personally or distributing assets before advice.
- Fill the page with grief counselling; acknowledge, then be practical.

## Output Format

```
## After [name/relationship]'s death — admin checklist · updated [date]
Estate representative: [name / not yet known] · Professionals: funeral director [..], attorney [..], accountant [..]

### Now (first 48 hours)
- [ ] [task] — [who] — [VERIFY locally where relevant]
### Soon (first weeks)
### Later (months) — [target month]

### Notification tracker
| Organisation | Who tells | How | Needs | Done | Ref |

### Hand-offs
| Question | To whom | Status |

### Who is doing what · weekly check-in: [day]
### Support: grief resources → domain-psychology/client-self-use/grief-loss/ · crisis → [local emergency number]
```

## Verification

- [ ] The first-48-hours list contains only time-critical items.
- [ ] Every deadline is marked "[VERIFY]"; none asserted.
- [ ] Every legal/tax/debt/will question sits in the hand-offs table.
- [ ] The notification tracker has an owner per row.
- [ ] Tasks are spread across helpers; nobody is holding everything.
- [ ] Grief and crisis routes are present and brief.

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **Everything urgent.** A list that puts closing a streaming subscription beside registering
   the death overwhelms. Most things can wait weeks.
2. **Deadlines from memory.** Registration and tax deadlines vary by place and change; a wrong
   one causes panic or a missed filing. Mark and verify.
3. **Personal liability by kindness.** Paying the deceased's bills from your own account or
   handing out belongings early can create problems. Ask first.
4. **One person doing all of it.** The nearest or most organised relative quietly absorbs the
   whole list. Assign explicitly.
5. **Therapy in a checklist.** Long passages on grief stages are not what someone needs from an
   admin list; point to the right resource and stay practical.
6. **Assuming the will says what you think.** Its validity and effect are for the attorney.

## Techniques Used

- **NE-07 Emotional Validation First** — a brief acknowledgement, then practical help.
- **ST-02 Structured Sequential Instructions** — now / soon / later sequencing.
- **DS-06 Prioritization and Severity Guidance** — only time-critical items in the first 48 hours.
- **OC-09 Capability Boundary Specification** — the hand-offs table to attorney, accountant and funeral director.
- **QA-04 Uncertainty Acknowledgment** — every deadline marked for local verification.
