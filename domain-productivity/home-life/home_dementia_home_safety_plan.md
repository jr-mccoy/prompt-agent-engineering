# Dementia Home Safety and Daily-Routine Plan

**Objective:** Turn scattered worries ("what if she leaves the gas on?") into a rated list
of hazards, a dated fix for each serious one, a protocol for the day they go missing, and a
daily routine — plus the specific signs that mean home alone is no longer safe enough.

**When to Use:**
- Someone with a dementia diagnosis lives alone or alone for part of the day.
- There has been a near-miss: a pan left burning, a night-time walk, a missed or doubled
  dose, a minor crash, a fall.
- The family disagrees about how risky things are and needs shared criteria.
- A clinician or social worker has suggested a home-safety review and you want to prepare.
- **Not this prompt if** the question is *where* they should live —
  `domain-personal-development/major-decisions/personal_caring_for_aging_parent.md` decides
  the arrangement, and `home_parent_cannot_live_alone_talk.md` plans that conversation. For
  how to *talk* with them day to day, `home_dementia_caregiver_communication.md`. For a
  general (non-dementia) home maintenance schedule, `home_seasonal_maintenance_calendar.md`.

**Audience:** Family carers. Practical safety planning, not a clinical or occupational-therapy
assessment; it prepares for one and points to it.

## Inputs / Context

1. **The person:** diagnosis and stage as given by their clinician, mobility, eyesight and
   hearing, continence, sleep pattern, what they still do independently.
2. **Living situation:** alone or with someone; hours alone per day; home layout (stairs,
   bathroom location, exits, garden, nearby roads or water).
3. **Incidents so far:** each near-miss with date — stove, wandering, medications, falls, driving.
4. **Current supports:** visits, paid carers, neighbours, pendant alarm, pharmacy services.
5. **Specific items present:** gas or electric stove, firearms, power tools, car and keys,
   pools or ponds, medicines including over-the-counter, alcohol.
6. **The person's own wishes** about independence, driving and help in the home.

## Method

1. **Rate each hazard area (DP-28).** Use stated criteria, not gut feel:
   - **Green** — no incidents; the person manages it with existing supports.
   - **Amber** — one near-miss, or warning signs, or it depends on one person remembering.
   - **Red** — repeated incidents, an injury, or a single incident that could have been fatal
     (fire, getting lost at night, a crash, a double dose of a blood thinner).
   Areas: wandering/getting lost · driving · medications · cooking and fire · falls ·
   firearms, tools and hazardous products · scams and money · time alone · night-time.
2. **Prioritise (DS-06).** Reds get a fix dated within 7 days; ambers within 30; greens
   reviewed monthly. Life-safety items (fire, firearms, getting lost, driving) outrank comfort.
3. **Apply the standard fixes, chosen to the person (CM-02).** Prefer changes to the
   environment over relying on memory or nagging:
   - *Wandering:* ID bracelet with a contact number, a recent photo kept ready, door chimes or
     sensors, a key safe for helpers, neighbours told who to call, a GPS device or phone
     location sharing *with the person's agreement where they can give it*.
   - *Cooking and fire:* automatic stove shut-off device or knob covers or isolating the
     cooker; tested smoke alarms (and carbon-monoxide alarm with gas); microwave-ready meals;
     kettle with auto shut-off.
   - *Medications:* one person accountable; pharmacy-filled blister or dose packs; a locked
     supply with only today's doses accessible; an alarmed dispenser; a list from the pharmacy,
     not from memory. Dose changes and "can she skip this?" go to the prescriber.
   - *Falls:* lighting on routes and night lights to the toilet, remove loose rugs and cords,
     grab rails, non-slip footwear, chair-height bed; any fall gets reported to the clinician.
     Ask for an occupational-therapy home assessment `[VERIFY: how to request one locally]`.
   - *Firearms and tools:* remove firearms from the home, or at minimum lock them unloaded
     with ammunition stored separately and keys held by someone else; lock away power tools,
     chemicals and car keys if driving has stopped.
   - *Scams and money:* route to `domain-psy-ops/personal-defense/psyops_elder_targeted_fraud_recognition.md`;
     authority to manage their money is a legal question for an attorney.
   - *Hot water:* set the water heater to about 120°F / 49°C to reduce scald risk.
4. **Write the missing-person protocol (AG-19).** Minute 0–15: check the home, garden, car,
   and the two or three places they usually go; call the people on the list. **At 15 minutes,
   call emergency services** — do not wait longer to "be sure". Have ready: recent photo,
   description of clothes, medical conditions, places from their past (old home, old workplace).
   Afterwards: log what happened and add a fix.
5. **Plan the driving conversation.** Warning signs: getting lost on familiar routes, new dents,
   near-misses, confusing pedals, others refusing to ride along. The decider is the clinician
   or a formal driving assessment, not the family's opinion — ask the doctor to raise it and,
   where available, request an occupational-therapy driving evaluation. Licensing reporting
   rules differ by place `[VERIFY: your licensing authority's rules]`. Plan the replacement
   transport *before* the keys go, so the loss is of a car, not of a life.
6. **Set the daily routine.** Same wake, meal, activity and bed times; daylight and activity
   in the morning; medications tied to meals; an evening wind-down. Write it where they and
   visitors can see it.
7. **Name the escalation triggers.** Same-day clinician: sudden confusion, a fall, not eating
   or drinking. Social services / adult social care `[VERIFY locally]`: needs exceed what
   family can cover. Adult protective services `[VERIFY locally]`: suspected neglect, self-neglect
   or exploitation. Re-open the living decision: two reds that cannot be fixed, or a red
   recurring after its fix.

## Output Format

```
# Home safety plan — [person], [diagnosis as given], lives [alone / with …], from [date]

## Hazard ratings
| Area | Rating | Evidence (dated) | Fix | Owner | Due |

## Missing-person protocol (card by the door and in every helper's phone)
## Driving: current status · warning signs seen · who raises it · replacement transport
## Medications: who is accountable · system · prescriber contact
## Daily routine (posted on the fridge)
## Escalation triggers → who to call
## Review: monthly on [date]; immediately after any incident
```

## Verification

- [ ] Every area is rated against the stated criteria, with dated evidence.
- [ ] Every red has a fix, an owner and a date within 7 days.
- [ ] The missing-person protocol has the 15-minute call rule and a photo ready.
- [ ] Firearms are addressed explicitly (present or not).
- [ ] Driving decision is assigned to a clinician or formal assessment, with transport planned.
- [ ] No medication or dose decision is made in the plan.
- [ ] Escalation triggers name a route for each; local services marked [VERIFY].

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **"She's always been fine."** Past competence does not predict the next stage; rate on
   recent evidence.
2. **Reminders as safety measures.** A note saying "turn off the stove" relies on the memory
   that is failing; change the environment instead.
3. **Waiting before calling about a missing person.** Delay is the main risk; call at 15 minutes.
4. **Taking the keys with no transport plan.** Isolation follows; plan rides first.
5. **Over-locking.** Removing every choice increases distress and resistance; fix the reds
   and leave the person as much control as is safe.
6. **Treating a fall as a one-off.** Falls often signal a medical change; report each one.
7. **Tracking without consent where they can give it.** Ask; record the conversation.

## Techniques Used

- **DP-28 Traffic-Light Verdict System** — green/amber/red per hazard with explicit criteria.
- **DS-06 Prioritization and Severity Guidance** — 7-day reds, 30-day ambers, life-safety first.
- **AG-19 Time-Critical Response Protocol** — the 0–15 minute missing-person sequence.
- **CM-02 Constraint Specification** — no dose decisions, consent for tracking, environment over memory.
- **OC-03 Markdown Table Specification** — hazard table with owner and due date.
