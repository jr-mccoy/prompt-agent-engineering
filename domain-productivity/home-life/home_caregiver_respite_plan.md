# Caregiver Respite Plan

**Objective (ST-01):** Put protected, recurring breaks for the primary carer on the calendar
— with cover arranged, handover written and a fallback for when cover falls through — so
that rest happens by design rather than only after a collapse.

**When to Use:**
- You (or a family member) are the main carer and have not had an unbroken day off in weeks.
- Breaks keep getting cancelled because cover falls through.
- The family's care rota (`home_family_caregiving_coordination.md`) has no time off in it.
- You want to try a day programme or short-stay care and need to plan the first one.

**Not this prompt if:**
- You want to understand whether you are burnt out, and what kind — `domain-personal-development/prompts/agency/agency_burnout_recovery.md`.
- You feel hopeless, trapped, or are thinking of harming yourself or the person you care for —
  that needs help now: `domain-psychology/client-self-use/crisis-self-triage/clientself_am_i_in_crisis_self_triage.md`,
  a crisis line or emergency services. This plan comes later.
- Persistent low mood, anxiety or grief is the main problem — `domain-psychology/client-self-use/`
  and a licensed professional.
- Caregiver burnout or stress is the main problem — `domain-psychology/client-self-use/specialty/clientself_caregiver_burnout_plan.md`.
- The care arrangement itself is no longer workable — revisit `domain-personal-development/major-decisions/personal_caring_for_aging_parent.md`.

**Audience:** The primary carer, or a sibling or partner planning breaks for them. Practical
scheduling only; it does not assess anyone's health.

## Inputs Required

1. **The care routine:** what the person needs across a typical day and night, and which
   parts are hardest to hand over.
2. **Current breaks:** when the carer last had two hours, one day, and several days free.
3. **Possible cover:** family, friends, neighbours, paid sitters or agency, day programmes,
   short-stay/respite placements, faith or community groups — and what each could do.
4. **What the carer would do with a break** (rest, a friend, a hobby, their own appointments) —
   a break with no plan tends to fill with chores.
5. **Budget and funding questions** — marked for verification with local services.
6. **The cared-for person's view** on new people or places.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Set three layers of break (DS-26)
Safe defaults, adjusted to the situation:
- **Weekly:** one protected block of at least 3–4 hours, same day and time.
- **Monthly:** one full day.
- **Annually:** at least one multi-day break.
Start with whatever is achievable now; a weekly two hours that happens beats a monthly day that doesn't.

### Step 2 — Assign cover for each layer (OC-03)
For each break: primary cover, backup cover, and what cover can and cannot do (e.g. a
neighbour can sit and make tea but not help with the bathroom). Paid and formal options —
sitters, day programmes, short-stay care — each carry "[VERIFY availability, eligibility and cost locally]".

### Step 3 — Write the handover sheet
What cover needs for the specific break: routine for those hours, likes and dislikes, what
calms and what upsets, where the medication list and emergency contacts are (point to the care
information sheet — do not rewrite care instructions), and when to call the carer versus
emergency services. Short enough to read in five minutes.

### Step 4 — Protect the break (CM-02)
Rules the family agrees: the carer leaves the house or goes somewhere uninterrupted; cover
calls only for the reasons on the sheet; a cancelled break is rescheduled within the week, not
dropped. The break is on the shared calendar like any appointment.

### Step 5 — Plan the first trial
For a new sitter, day programme or short stay: start short, with the carer nearby or reachable,
and note what went well for both people. Many first trials are bumpy; one bumpy trial is not a verdict.

### Step 6 — Early-warning list and routing (QA-20)
A short list the carer and family agree to watch for: breaks repeatedly cancelled, sleep
badly broken for weeks, snapping at the person cared for, dropping one's own medical
appointments, feeling nothing helps. If these appear, the response is to **increase respite and
seek support** — the burnout prompt, a GP or doctor, a carer support service, or psychology
resources — not to push through. Keep this section practical and non-judgemental; no diagnosis,
and no lecture that makes the carer feel they are failing.

## Constraints

### Must
- Name backup cover for every break.
- Keep the handover sheet pointing to authoritative care instructions.
- Mark every formal service's availability, eligibility and cost "[VERIFY locally]".
- Route distress and crisis to the resources above.

### Must Not
- Diagnose burnout, depression or anything else.
- Moralise about self-care or imply the carer is doing it wrong.
- Plan breaks that depend on the carer doing care tasks during them.

## Output Format

```
## Respite plan — [carer] caring for [relationship], from [date]
Last break: 2 h [date] · 1 day [date] · multi-day [date]

### Break schedule
| Layer | When | Duration | Primary cover | Backup | Cover can / can't | Cost [VERIFY] |

### What I'll do with the break
### Handover sheet (one page)
### Agreed rules
### First trial: [option] on [date] — notes after
### Early-warning list → routes to: [burnout prompt / doctor / carer support / crisis line]
```

## Verification

- [ ] All three layers scheduled, or a stated reason and a first step for any missing layer.
- [ ] Every break has a backup.
- [ ] The handover sheet is one page and points to the care information sheet.
- [ ] All formal service details marked "[VERIFY locally]".
- [ ] The early-warning list routes to named resources and a crisis option.
- [ ] No diagnostic or judgemental language.

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **Breaks with no backup.** The first time the sister cancels, the break disappears for a month.
2. **Breaks spent on errands.** Time off filled with the cared-for person's shopping is not respite.
3. **Waiting until the carer is desperate.** Recurring small breaks prevent more than rare large ones rescue.
4. **Treating distress as a scheduling problem.** Persistent hopelessness or thoughts of harm need
   people, not a calendar — route them.
5. **Lecturing.** "You must look after yourself" without cover arranged is noise; arrange the cover.
6. **Assuming formal help is unavailable or unaffordable.** Many areas have carer support or
   short-stay options; check before ruling them out.
7. **Giving up after one rough trial.** New carers and places take adjustment for both people.

## Example Output

```
## Respite plan — Jo caring for Mum (lives at home, L2), from 2026-10-01
Last break: 2 h — August · 1 day — June · multi-day — none in 2026

### Break schedule
| Weekly | Wed 13:00–17:00 | 4 h | Agency sitter (extend Wed shift) | Rita (neighbour) | sitter: all care; Rita: company + tea only | [VERIFY agency rate] |
| Monthly | 2nd Saturday | full day | Sam (brother, stays over Fri) | Priya flies in quarterly | all care | — |
| Annual | May | 5 days | Short-stay placement at Oakview | Sam + agency | all care | [VERIFY availability, cost] |

### What I'll do: Wed — swim + coffee with Nita; Saturday — sleep in, see a film
### Handover sheet: points to care sheet in blue folder; "call Jo if Mum won't eat or asks for her twice; call emergency services for falls with pain"
### Agreed rules: Jo leaves the house; cancelled Wed rescheduled by Friday; on shared family calendar
### First trial: Oakview day visit 10-18, Jo nearby — notes after
### Early-warning list: two cancelled breaks in a month; Jo's own GP appointment skipped; "nothing helps"
→ Sam raises it; Jo reads agency_burnout_recovery; carer support line [VERIFY local]; crisis: emergency services
```

## Techniques Used

- **ST-01 Clear Objective Statement** — breaks by design, with cover that holds.
- **DS-26 Safe Defaults Pattern** — weekly / monthly / annual starting defaults.
- **CM-02 Constraint Specification** — agreed rules that protect the break.
- **QA-20 Dual-Failure Quality Test** — neither ignoring distress nor lecturing or diagnosing.
- **OC-03 Markdown Table Specification** — the break schedule with cover and limits.
