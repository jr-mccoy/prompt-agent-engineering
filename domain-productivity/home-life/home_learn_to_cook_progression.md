# Learn-to-Cook Skill Progression

**Objective:** Take someone who "can't cook" to cooking a handful of dinners confidently
without a recipe — by learning safety first, one technique a week, and repeating the same
dish until it is reliable, rather than chasing new recipes.

**When to Use:**
- You are an adult who mostly eats takeaway, ready meals or the same pasta, and want to learn.
- You have moved out, been widowed, separated, or your household's cook is no longer around.
- You follow recipes but nothing sticks; every meal feels like starting over.
- **Not this prompt if** you can already cook and want this week's meals and a shopping list —
  `home_meal_plan_week.md` (planning logistics). For tonight's dinner from what is in the fridge,
  `home_pantry_first_weeknight_cooking.md`. For *what* to eat for health, performance or weight,
  `domain-health-wellness/nutrition/nutrition_meal_structure_planner.md`; medical diets
  (diabetes, kidney disease, allergies, eating disorders) go to a clinician or registered dietitian.

**Audience:** Adult beginners cooking at home. This builds skills; it does not prescribe a diet.

## Inputs / Context

1. **Starting point:** what you can already make; what goes wrong (burning, bland, raw middle,
   takes forever).
2. **Kitchen:** hob type (gas, electric, induction), oven yes/no, pans and knives you own,
   thermometer yes/no, counter space.
3. **Time:** realistic weeknight minutes and one longer weekend slot.
4. **Budget:** weekly food spend.
5. **What you like to eat** and any hard restrictions (allergies, religious, vegetarian) —
   restrictions are constraints, not targets.
6. **Goal:** e.g. four home dinners a week; cook for a partner; batch-cook lunches.

## Method

1. **Week zero: safety defaults (DS-26).** Taught before any recipe:
   - *Hygiene:* wash hands before and after raw meat, fish or eggs; a separate board (or wash
     between) for raw meat; do not rinse raw chicken (it spreads bacteria around the sink).
   - *Temperature:* fridge at or below 40°F / 4°C; perishable food out no more than 2 hours
     (1 hour above 90°F / 32°C); leftovers eaten within 3–4 days; cool cooked rice quickly
     and refrigerate within about an hour.
   - *Doneness by thermometer* (US food-safety guidance — check your country's): poultry 165°F /
     74°C; ground meat 160°F / 71°C; whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb and fish 145°F / 63°C (meats
     rested 3 minutes). An instant-read thermometer is the best first purchase.
   - *Knife safety:* a sharp knife slips less than a dull one; damp towel under the board;
     "claw" grip with fingertips tucked; never catch a falling knife.
   - *Fire:* pan handles turned inward; a pan fire gets a lid and heat off — never water on
     burning oil; keep a lid near the hob.
2. **Sequence one technique per week (ST-02).** Default sequence, adjusted to the kitchen:
   1. Knife cuts (onion dice, garlic, herbs) + eggs three ways.
   2. Heat control: preheating, not crowding, sautéing vegetables.
   3. Grains and pasta: rice by package ratio, pasta in salted water, saving pasta water.
   4. Oven roasting: sheet-pan vegetables and chicken thighs.
   5. Searing and a pan sauce (deglaze, reduce, butter or cream).
   6. Soups and a one-pot braise.
   7. Dressings and seasoning: salt, acid, fat, heat — taste and adjust.
   8. Cooking without a recipe: templates (stir-fry, frittata, grain bowl, pasta, soup).
   Weeks 9–10 repeat the two weakest techniques.
3. **Practise by repetition.** Two short weeknight sessions plus one longer weekend session.
   Pick **one anchor dish per technique** and make it two or three times that week, changing
   one thing each time. Repetition builds judgment; new recipes every night do not.
4. **Set observable benchmarks (DS-02).** Each week has a "can do" test, e.g. dice a medium
   onion into even ½-inch pieces in under 3 minutes; cook rice with no burnt base and no hard
   centre; roast chicken thighs to 165°F with browned skin; make a vinaigrette at roughly 3 parts
   oil to 1 part acid and adjust it by taste.
5. **Keep a cooking log (QA-01).** After each session: what you made, time taken, what went
   wrong, what you will change. Review weekly against the benchmark; repeat the week if not met.
6. **Shop for the plan, not the recipe.** Overlapping ingredients across the week's sessions;
   a starter pantry built gradually (oil, salt, pepper, garlic, onions, one acid, stock, a
   tinned tomato, rice, pasta, eggs).

## Output Format

```
# Learn-to-cook plan — [name], [kitchen], [budget/week], from [date]
## Week 0 — safety basics checklist (with first purchases)
## Progression
| Week | Technique | Anchor dish | Sessions (when) | Can-do benchmark |
## Starter pantry and equipment (buy now / later)
## Cooking log template | Date | Dish | Time | What went wrong | Change next time |
## Weekly review rule: benchmark met → next week; not met → repeat
```

## Verification

- [ ] Week zero covers hygiene, fridge temperature, two-hour rule, thermometer temperatures,
      knife and fire safety, with "check your country's guidance" noted.
- [ ] One technique per week, each with a single anchor dish repeated.
- [ ] Every benchmark is observable (time, temperature, texture), not "feel confident".
- [ ] Sessions fit the stated time and budget.
- [ ] No diet prescriptions; medical diets routed to a clinician or dietitian.

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **Recipe collecting as progress.** Ten new recipes teach less than one dish made three times.
2. **"Cooked when it looks done."** Colour misleads; use the thermometer for meat.
3. **Starting with showpieces.** Week-one risotto for guests teaches fear, not skill.
4. **Equipment before skill.** One good knife, one heavy pan, a sheet tray and a thermometer
   cover the first ten weeks.
5. **Bland food blamed on the recipe.** Usually under-salting and no acid; taste and adjust.
6. **Turning a skills plan into a diet.** Health targets belong to the nutrition prompts and,
   for medical needs, a professional.

```
## Week 0: buy instant-read thermometer ($15), second chopping board ($8), oven mitt;
fridge thermometer shows 44°F → turn dial colder, recheck to ≤40°F.

| Wk | Technique | Anchor dish | Sessions | Benchmark |
| 1 | Knife + eggs | onion-pepper omelette | Tue, Thu, Sun | dice onion <3 min; omelette
  set but not browned |
| 2 | Heat control | garlic-ginger veg stir-fry with rice | Tue, Thu, Sun | veg crisp-tender,
  pan not crowded (two batches) |
| 3 | Grains/pasta | pasta with tomato sauce + pasta water | Tue, Thu, Sun | sauce clings,
  pasta al dente |
| 4 | Roasting | sheet-pan chicken thighs + potatoes | Wed, Sun | thighs 165°F, skin browned |
| 5 | Sear + pan sauce | pork chop, mustard pan sauce | Wed, Sun | chop 145°F + 3-min rest;
  sauce coats a spoon |
| 6 | Soup | lentil soup (batch for 2 lunches) | Sun | cooled and in fridge within 2 h |
| 7 | Seasoning | grain bowl + vinaigrette | Tue, Thu | adjusts salt/acid by taste twice |

```

## Techniques Used

- **DS-02 Metric Specification** — observable can-do benchmarks per week.

- **QA-01 Self-Verification** — the cooking log reviewed weekly against the benchmark.
