# Solo Developer Weekly Operating Rhythm

**Objective:** Design a sustainable weekly operating rhythm for a solo developer running a startup — covering time allocation across development, user support, marketing, business operations, and learning — using day theming, batch processing strategies, and "CEO time" for strategic thinking — producing a weekly schedule that keeps the business running without burning out.

**When to Use:** Use this prompt when starting a solo app business, when you feel overwhelmed by context-switching, when important non-development tasks keep falling through the cracks, or when you realize you've spent 3 weeks coding without responding to users, updating the store listing, or checking your finances. This addresses the core solo developer challenge: being CEO, CTO, designer, marketer, support rep, and accountant — all at once.

**Important context:** The fatal mistake solo developers make is spending 100% of their time on development and 0% on everything else. Your app can't succeed on code quality alone — it needs users, support, marketing, and business management. But the equally fatal mistake is trying to do everything every day. The solution is structured time allocation with themes and batching. This prompt teaches you how to design that system for your specific situation.

---

## Context Gathering

Before designing the operating rhythm, understand your specific situation:

1. **Current State:**
   - "How many hours per week can you dedicate to your app business?"
   - "Is this full-time or alongside a day job?"
   - "What does your typical week look like now?"
   - "What tasks consistently fall through the cracks?"

2. **Business Stage:**
   - "Is your app pre-launch, recently launched, or established?"
   - "How many active users do you have?"
   - "How many support requests do you get per week?"
   - "Do you have paying customers?"

3. **Personal Preferences:**
   - "When are you most productive (morning, afternoon, evening)?"
   - "Are there days you can't work on the business?"
   - "What types of tasks do you enjoy most? Least?"
   - "Do you have any recurring commitments (meetings, pickups, etc.)?"

4. **Pain Points:**
   - "What causes you the most stress about running everything alone?"
   - "How often do you context-switch in a day?"
   - "Do you feel like you're always coding but never marketing? Or vice versa?"
   - "Have you missed important deadlines (policy compliance, tax filings, etc.)?"

## Instructions

### CRITICAL: Honest Constraints

**Before designing ANY schedule, you MUST:**

1. **Be realistic about available hours** — A sustainable 30-hour week beats an unsustainable 70-hour week. Design for what you can maintain for a year, not what you can do for a sprint.
2. **Accept that some things won't get done** — You cannot do everything. The operating rhythm is as much about what you deprioritize as what you prioritize.
3. **Protect deep work time** — Development and creative work require 2-4 hour uninterrupted blocks. Don't fragment them with meetings, emails, or support.
4. **Build in slack** — A schedule packed to 100% capacity will fail the first week something unexpected happens. Plan for 80% capacity.
5. **Review and adjust** — This is a starting point. Review after 4 weeks and adjust based on what actually happened.

### False-Positive Prevention

- ❌ Do NOT create a schedule that requires 60+ hours/week — it's not sustainable
- ❌ Do NOT suggest equal time across all areas — development should get the majority
- ❌ Do NOT recommend daily context switching between all roles
- ❌ Do NOT create a rigid schedule that breaks if one meeting overruns
- ❌ Do NOT assume the developer needs to do everything themselves — some tasks should be skipped or outsourced
- ✅ DO design around deep work blocks for development
- ✅ DO batch similar tasks to reduce context-switching overhead
- ✅ DO include buffer time for unexpected issues
- ✅ DO prioritize by business stage (pre-launch vs. post-launch changes the mix)
- ✅ DO make the schedule visually simple — if it needs a PhD to follow, it won't stick

### Phase 1: Time Allocation by Business Stage

#### 1.1 Recommended Time Splits

The allocation changes based on where your business is:

**Pre-Launch (building the product):**

| Activity | % of Time | Hours (40h week) | Why |
|----------|-----------|-------------------|-----|
| Development | 65% | 26h | Building the product is the priority |
| Marketing/Pre-launch | 15% | 6h | Building in public, landing page, beta recruitment |
| Business Setup | 10% | 4h | LLC, accounts, store listing, assets |
| Learning | 10% | 4h | Skills you need but don't have |

**Post-Launch Month 1 (launch and stabilize):**

| Activity | % of Time | Hours (40h week) | Why |
|----------|-----------|-------------------|-----|
| Development (bug fixes) | 45% | 18h | Fix launch issues, critical bugs |
| User Support | 15% | 6h | Respond to every review and support request |
| Marketing | 20% | 8h | Launch momentum, community engagement |
| Analytics/Business | 10% | 4h | Learn from data, plan next iteration |
| Operations | 10% | 4h | Monitoring, cost review, compliance |

**Established (3+ months, stable product):**

| Activity | % of Time | Hours (40h week) | Why |
|----------|-----------|-------------------|-----|
| Development (features) | 55% | 22h | New features, improvements, tech debt |
| Marketing/Growth | 15% | 6h | Content, ASO, community |
| User Support | 10% | 4h | Reviews, emails, help content |
| Business/Strategy | 10% | 4h | Metrics review, planning, finances |
| Operations | 5% | 2h | Monitoring, maintenance, updates |
| Learning | 5% | 2h | Stay current, fill skill gaps |

#### 1.2 Adjust for Part-Time

If you have a day job, scale proportionally but protect development time:

| Available Time | Development | Everything Else |
|---------------|-------------|----------------|
| 20 hours/week | 14h (70%) | 6h (30%) |
| 15 hours/week | 11h (73%) | 4h (27%) |
| 10 hours/week | 8h (80%) | 2h (20%) |

Below 10 hours/week, focus almost exclusively on development. Marketing and operations can be done in micro-batches (30 minutes).

### Phase 2: Day Theming

#### 2.1 The Concept

Day theming assigns a primary focus to each day. You still do other things, but your main energy goes to the theme. This dramatically reduces context-switching.

#### 2.2 Recommended Day Themes

**Full-time schedule (5 days):**

```markdown
Monday:    PLAN + BUSINESS
           Morning: Weekly metrics review (30 min)
           Morning: Priority setting for the week (30 min)
           Mid-day: Business operations (finances, emails, admin) (2h)
           Afternoon: Development — start the week's main task (3h)

Tuesday:   BUILD (Deep Work Day 1)
           All day: Development — uninterrupted coding (6-8h)
           No meetings, no email, no support
           Phone on DND. This is your most productive day.

Wednesday: BUILD (Deep Work Day 2)
           Morning: Development (4h)
           Afternoon: User support batch (1h)
           Afternoon: Development (2-3h)

Thursday:  BUILD + MARKET
           Morning: Development (3h)
           Afternoon: Marketing — content creation, social posts, community (3h)
           Late afternoon: Review response and support (1h)

Friday:    MAINTAIN + LEARN
           Morning: Operations — monitoring, updates, cost review (2h)
           Mid-day: Learning — tutorials, documentation, skills (2h)
           Afternoon: Week review, plan next week, loose ends (2h)
           Early finish if possible — sustainable pace.
```

**Part-time schedule (evenings + weekends):**

```markdown
Weekday Evenings (2h each, 3 evenings):
  Evening 1 (Tue): Development — deep work
  Evening 2 (Thu): Development — deep work
  Evening 3 (Fri): Support + marketing batch

Weekend (4-6h on Saturday OR Sunday, not both):
  Morning: Development (3-4h)
  Afternoon: Business ops + planning (1-2h)
```

#### 2.3 The "No Interruption" Rule

Your deep work days (Tuesday, Wednesday morning) must be protected:

- **No email** — Check email only during business/support blocks
- **No social media** — Schedule posts ahead of time; don't scroll
- **No support** — Users can wait 24-48 hours for a response
- **No meetings** — If you must have a meeting, schedule it on Monday or Friday
- **No "quick fixes"** — Write them down, do them in the next business block

### Phase 3: Batch Processing

#### 3.1 What to Batch

| Task | Frequency | Batch Block | Estimated Time |
|------|-----------|-------------|----------------|
| Read & respond to reviews | 2x/week | Wed + Thu afternoon | 30 min each |
| Support emails | 2x/week | Wed + Thu afternoon | 30 min each |
| Social media posting | 1x/week | Thu afternoon | 1 hour |
| Metrics check | 1x/week | Monday morning | 30 min |
| Financial review | 1x/month | First Monday | 1 hour |
| Cost monitoring | 1x/week | Friday morning | 15 min |
| Dependency updates | 1x/month | Friday morning | 2 hours |
| Content creation | 1x/week | Thu afternoon | 1-2 hours |
| Crash/error review | 2x/week | Mon + Fri | 15 min each |

#### 3.2 What NOT to Batch

Some things need immediate attention:

| Trigger | Response Time | Why |
|---------|-------------|-----|
| App crash rate > 5% | Immediately | Users are affected now |
| Security vulnerability alert | Within hours | Potential data exposure |
| Play Store policy violation notice | Within 24 hours | Clock ticking on remediation |
| Cost anomaly alert (> 2x budget) | Within hours | Could be runaway spending |

Set up alerts for these — don't check manually. Everything else can wait for its batch.

### Phase 4: Weekly Rituals

#### 4.1 Monday Morning Startup (30 minutes)

```markdown
## Weekly Startup Checklist

1. [ ] Check Play Console: new reviews, rating changes, download trend
2. [ ] Check Crashlytics: any new crash clusters since Friday?
3. [ ] Check Firebase Console: cost tracking, usage anomalies
4. [ ] Review last week's goals: what got done, what didn't?
5. [ ] Set this week's goals: max 3 priorities (not 10)
6. [ ] Block time for the #1 priority task

Output: 3 written goals for the week
Time: 30 minutes max — don't let this expand
```

#### 4.2 Friday Afternoon Closeout (30 minutes)

```markdown
## Weekly Closeout Checklist

1. [ ] Commit and push all work in progress
2. [ ] Update any public roadmap or changelog
3. [ ] Log what you shipped this week (even small things)
4. [ ] Note any blockers for next week
5. [ ] Check: Is there anything time-sensitive next week?
6. [ ] Close laptop. You're done for the week.

Output: Brief week summary (for your own records)
Time: 30 minutes max — then stop working
```

#### 4.3 Monthly Business Review (1 hour, first Monday of month)

```markdown
## Monthly Review Template

### Metrics
- Downloads this month: [N]
- Active users (DAU/MAU): [N]
- Revenue: $[N]
- Expenses: $[N]
- Crash-free rate: [N]%
- Store rating: [N] stars
- Support requests: [N]

### What Went Well
- [Achievement 1]
- [Achievement 2]

### What Didn't Go Well
- [Issue 1]
- [Issue 2]

### Key Decisions Made
- [Decision 1 and reasoning]

### Next Month's Focus
- Priority 1: [Focus area]
- Priority 2: [Focus area]
- Skip/defer: [What you're intentionally NOT doing]
```

### Phase 5: Common Traps and Mitigations

| Trap | Symptom | Mitigation |
|------|---------|------------|
| **Endless coding** | Weeks pass with no marketing, no support | Set a hard stop on dev days; batch other tasks |
| **Inbox-driven days** | Responding to emails/reviews all day | Check email only during batch blocks (2x/week) |
| **Perfectionism** | Feature never ships, always "one more thing" | Set ship dates and honor them; good enough > perfect |
| **Shiny object syndrome** | Starting new features before finishing current ones | Max 1 feature in progress at a time |
| **No boundaries** | Working until midnight, weekends | Set end-of-day time and enforce it |
| **Comparison trap** | Demoralized by competitors with teams | Focus on YOUR metrics, not theirs |
| **Skipping reviews** | Never checking metrics or finances | Automate the Monday startup ritual |

## Customization Guide

- **For part-time developers (< 20h/week):** Compress to 3 blocks: 2 development sessions + 1 everything-else session. Skip weekly rituals, do biweekly instead.
- **For pre-launch:** Shift 15% from marketing to development. Replace support time with beta tester management.
- **For high-support apps (> 20 support requests/week):** Increase support allocation; consider creating self-service help content to reduce volume over time.
- **For apps with employees or contractors:** Add a coordination block on Mondays for standup/planning with your team.
- **For developers with families:** Protect evenings and weekends. The rhythm must fit around life, not the other way around. A 6AM-8AM morning block before the family wakes up works better than fragmented evening sessions.
- **For developers feeling overwhelmed right now:** Start with just the Monday startup ritual. Do that for 2 weeks. Then add the Friday closeout. Build the habit before building the full system.
