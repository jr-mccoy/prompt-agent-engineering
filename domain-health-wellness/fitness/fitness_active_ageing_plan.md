# Active Ageing Plan

**Objective:** Give an older adult a plan that keeps them strong enough to get out of a
chair, steady enough to catch a trip, and fit enough to do what they want — built
from public-health guidance for older adults, gated by a falls screen, and bound by
whatever limits their clinician has set.

> **Readiness gate.** Start from a Readiness Profile
> (`foundations/wellness_readiness_and_red_flag_screen.md`). No profile → ask its
> blocks 1–8 first. URGENT-CARE-NOW, CLINICIAN-FIRST, or OUT-OF-SCOPE → stop and
> restate the route. An urgent result means contacting the local emergency number now
> (e.g. 911 in the US). **GO-WITH-LIMITS → this prompt requires the clinician's limits
> recorded verbatim in the profile.** The screen *recommends* that conversation for a
> sedentary or deconditioned older adult; for balance and power work it is required
> here. No recorded limits → output only the appointment question in Method step 1.

**When to Use:**
- You are around 65 or older (or younger with the same goals) and want to stay strong,
  steady on your feet, and independent.
- You already walk or garden but have never done strength or balance work.
- A family member wants a plan they can do *with* you.
- **Not this prompt if** you have fallen in the last year, feel unsteady, or have
  osteoporosis or a fracture from a minor fall — Method step 1 routes you to a
  clinician's falls-risk assessment first. Under ~65 with no ageing-specific goal, use
  `fitness/fitness_beginner_training_plan.md`. For range of motion only, use
  `fitness/fitness_mobility_and_flexibility_routine.md`. A clinician reviewing an older
  patient's falls risk and medications uses
  `domain-psychology/populations/geriatric/psychology_geriatric_intake_with_polypharmacy_review.md`,
  not this.

## Inputs / Context

1. **Readiness Profile** — gate result, limits (verbatim), starting point, capacity.
2. **Falls screen** (Method step 1), asked plainly.
3. **Current activity:** walking, gardening, housework, sport; minutes per week.
4. **Goal in your words** ("carry my grandchildren", "climb the stairs without the rail").
5. **Equipment and space:** a kitchen counter, a sturdy chair without wheels, bands,
   light dumbbells; whether someone is home during sessions.
6. **Capacity:** days per week and minutes.

## Method

1. **Run the gates and the falls screen (QA-08).** Ask, adapted from the key questions
   in the CDC's STEADI falls-prevention toolkit, plus three ageing-specific checks:
   - Have you fallen in the past year?
   - Do you feel unsteady when standing or walking?
   - Do you worry about falling?
   - Do you get dizzy or light-headed when you stand up?
   - Have you been told you have osteoporosis, or broken a bone from a minor fall or
     bump since age 50?

   Any *yes* → **CLINICIAN-FIRST for this prompt**: no plan, no "gentle" balance moves.
   Give the question to take: *"I'd like to start strength and balance training three
   times a week. Could you assess my falls risk and tell me which balance positions,
   how much load, and whether fast movements are safe for me? Is a supervised
   falls-prevention programme (such as Otago) available?"* When limits come back,
   they are copied verbatim into the profile and the prompt runs under them.
2. **Fix who decides what (CM-09).** The plan decides structure, order, and progression
   *inside* limits. The clinician decides falls risk, bone-loading limits, and whether
   unsupported balance or fast movement is safe. A physiotherapist or trained
   instructor teaches balance progressions in person; text gives at most two cues.
3. **Anchor to guidance, labelled as general.** WHO's 2020 guidelines for adults 65+:
   150–300 minutes of moderate aerobic activity a week (or 75–150 vigorous);
   muscle-strengthening at moderate or greater intensity on 2+ days; and varied
   multicomponent activity emphasising **functional balance and strength on 3+ days a
   week** to prevent falls. A 2019 Cochrane review found exercise programmes built on
   balance and functional training reduced the rate of falls by about a quarter.
4. **Take baselines to track, not to diagnose (DS-02).**
   - **30-second chair stand** — arms crossed, chair against a wall; count full stands.
     STEADI lists below-average cut-points by age and sex (e.g. under 12 for men and
     under 10 for women aged 70–74). Below the cut-point with a clean screen → the plan
     starts at the supported level and the result goes to the next clinician visit.
   - **Balance hold** — at the counter, fingertips resting: seconds held (to 30) in
     feet-together, semi-tandem, and tandem stance.
5. **Write the three blocks.**
   - **Balance, 10–15 min, 3+ days.** Ladder: feet together → semi-tandem → tandem →
     single-leg; within each, two hands → fingertips → hands hovering over the counter.
     Then add head turns, then reaching. Move up one rung when 30 s is held steadily
     on two sessions in a row. Always at a counter or in a corner with a chair in front.
     Eyes-closed work only with an instructor or clinician.
   - **Strength, 2–3 days.** Six patterns: sit-to-stand (squat), hip hinge to the
     counter or light dumbbell deadlift (hinge), wall or counter push-up (push), band
     row (pull), step-up on the bottom stair with the rail (single-leg), suitcase carry
     (carry). Weeks 1–4: 2 × 10–15 at RPE 5–6. Weeks 5–12: 2–3 × 8–12 at RPE 6–7,
     double progression as in the beginner plan. GO-WITH-LIMITS: the clinician's cap.
   - **Power, from week 5 (GO only, or with the clinician's explicit agreement).**
     The same sit-to-stand and step-up with *fast intent*: up as quickly as is
     controlled, down over ~3 seconds; 3 × 6–8 at light load (RPE ~5), 60–90 s rest.
     Power declines faster than strength with age and is what rises from a chair or
     catches a stumble.
   - **Walking** toward 150 min/week, by the talk test.
6. **Write the stop rules.** Chest pain or pressure, one-sided weakness, face droop, or
   sudden confusion → emergency services now. Dizziness or near-fainting → stop, sit;
   if it recurs, clinician before the next session. **A fall during practice → stop
   balance work until a clinician has reviewed it**; if hurt or unable to get up, call
   for help. Sharp or joint pain → swap the exercise; persists a week → clinician or
   physiotherapist.
7. **Dual-failure check (QA-20).** Did a screen *yes* become "just some gentle moves"?
   Did a fit 72-year-old get chair-only exercises and a page of warnings? Fix either.

## Verification

- [ ] The falls screen ran before any exercise appeared; any *yes* produced only the appointment question.
- [ ] GO-WITH-LIMITS output exists only with verbatim clinician limits, and every limit is visible.
- [ ] Balance appears on 3+ days with a support set-up and a numeric progression rule.
- [ ] Power starts no earlier than week 5 and only under GO or explicit clinician agreement.
- [ ] Guidance figures are attributed (WHO 2020, STEADI, Cochrane) and labelled general.
- [ ] Stop rules include a fall during practice and the stroke-sign emergency route.

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **Age is not frailty.** A screen-clean 75-year-old gets moderate-intensity strength
   and power, not chair stretches; under-dosing is its own failure.
2. **A chair-stand score is not a diagnosis.** It tracks change; risk is the clinician's call.
3. **"Gentle balance work" for someone who has fallen is still a plan.** Route first.
4. **Don't progress balance by the calendar.** Rungs move on held seconds, not weeks.
5. **Don't take away the support too early.** Hovering hands come before no counter.
6. **Don't rewrite clinician limits.** "Avoid bending forward under load" stays word for
   word; the hinge pattern changes to fit it, the limit does not.
7. **Don't diagnose a dizzy spell.** It routes; it is never explained.

## Example Output

Scenario: Joan, 72, GO (walks the dog 30 min daily; no flags), falls screen all *no*;
goal "keep lifting my grandson and manage the stairs without the rail"; counter, sturdy
chair, bands, 3 and 5 kg dumbbells; husband home; 3 days × 40 min.

```
# Active ageing plan — Joan, 12 weeks
Readiness: GO; clinician limits: none
Falls screen: all no
Baselines: chair stand 11 (STEADI cut-point, women 70–74: under 10 → above) ·
  holds: together 30 s / semi-tandem 30 s / tandem 12 s

## Week shape
| Mon | 15 min | 20 min | from wk 5 | 30 min dog walk |
| Tue | —      | —      | —         | 30 min dog walk |
| Wed | 15 min | 20 min | from wk 5 | 30 min |
| Fri | 15 min | 20 min | from wk 5 | 30 min |
| Sat/Sun | 5-min "tooth-brushing" balance holds | — | — | 30–40 min |
Walking ≈ 230 min/week (already above 150 — keep it).

## Balance ladder — where you start
Tandem stance, fingertips on the counter; move to hands hovering after 30 s held on 2
sessions running; single-leg after that. Chair in front, shoes on.

## Strength session
| Squat  | Sit-to-stand, arms crossed       | 2 × 12 | 5–6 | stand tall at the top |
| Hinge  | Counter hip hinge → 5 kg DB deadlift | 2 × 12 | 5–6 | hips back, back long |
| Push   | Counter push-up                  | 2 × 10 | 5–6 | body in one line |
| Pull   | Band row, door anchor            | 2 × 12 | 5–6 | elbows to ribs |
| Single-leg | Step-up, bottom stair, rail  | 2 × 8/side | 5–6 | whole foot on the step |
| Carry  | Suitcase carry, 5 kg             | 2 × 20 m/side | 5–6 | no lean |

## Power (from week 5)
Fast sit-to-stand 3 × 6 (up quick, down 3 s); fast step-up 3 × 6/side; 90 s rest.

## Weeks 1–12
| 1–4  | 2 sets, RPE 5–6, 10–15 reps | tandem, fingertips → hovering | — | 230 |
| 5–8  | 2–3 sets, RPE 6–7, 8–12     | tandem hovering → single-leg, fingertips | 3 × 6, RPE ~5 | 230 |
| 9–12 | 3 sets, RPE 6–7; DB up 1 step | single-leg + head turns | 3 × 8 | 230 |

## Stop rules
Chest pain, one-sided weakness, face droop, or confusion → 911 now. Dizzy → sit; if it
recurs, see your doctor first. A fall while practising → stop balance work until your
doctor reviews it; if hurt or unable to get up, call your husband or 911.

## Retest: weeks 4, 8, 12 — chair stand, three holds
```

## Techniques Used

- **QA-08 Gate-Based Verification** — readiness gate, then a five-question falls screen before any exercise.
- **CM-09 Authority Boundary Specification** — plan inside limits; falls risk, bone loading, and fast movement belong to the clinician.
- **DS-02 Metric Specification** — chair-stand and hold baselines, RPE bands, rung-progression and power doses.
- **QA-20 Dual-Failure Quality Test** — neither soften a falls flag nor under-dose a fit older adult.
