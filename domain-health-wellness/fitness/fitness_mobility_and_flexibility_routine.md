# Mobility and Flexibility Routine

**Objective:** Give a healthy adult a short, specific routine that improves the range
of motion they actually need — measured by a position they can retest — with a dose,
a placement around their training, and a stop rule that keeps stiffness work from
turning into pain work.

> **Readiness gate.** Start from a Readiness Profile
> (`foundations/wellness_readiness_and_red_flag_screen.md`). No profile → ask its
> blocks 1–8 first. URGENT-CARE-NOW, CLINICIAN-FIRST, or OUT-OF-SCOPE → stop and
> restate the route. An urgent result means contacting the local emergency number now
> (e.g. 911 in the US). GO-WITH-LIMITS → every limit binds this routine (for example
> no end-range loading, or holds only, if the clinician said so).

**When to Use:**
- You feel stiff — from sitting, from lifting, or from age — and want range back
  without pain being involved.
- A training movement is limited by range (heels lift in a squat, arms will not go
  fully overhead) and you want a targeted fix rather than a generic stretch video.
- You want a 10-minute routine for desk breaks, before sessions, or rest days.
- **Not this prompt if** an area *hurts*, was recently injured, or has been operated
  on — that is rehabilitation and goes to a clinician or physiotherapist. For a full
  strength-and-aerobic plan use `fitness/fitness_beginner_training_plan.md`; for
  where mobility sits in the week, `foundations/wellness_sustainable_routine_designer.md`.
  Stretching or yoga for chronic pain or low mood belongs with a clinician or
  `domain-psychology/client-self-use/`, not here.

## Inputs / Context

1. **Readiness Profile** — gate result, limits, starting point, capacity.
2. **Tasks you want range for**, in your words ("squat to a chair without heels
   lifting", "put a bag in the overhead locker", "sit on the floor with my kids").
3. **Where you feel stiff**, and whether any of it is pain (location, when, what it
   stops you doing). Pain, numbness, or tingling routes out — see Method step 1.
4. **Training you already do** and when, so mobility can be placed around it.
5. **Time available:** minutes per day or per session; equipment (wall, band, step,
   light dumbbell, mat).
6. **Joint history:** joints that have ever dislocated, slipped out, or given way.

## Method

1. **Run the gates, then the area screen (QA-08).** After the readiness gate, ask per
   target area: pain at rest or at night; pain that changes how you move; numbness,
   tingling, or shooting pain down a limb; a joint that has dislocated or gives way;
   injury or surgery there in the last 3 months. Any *yes* → that area gets no routine
   and routes to a clinician or physiotherapist; other areas may continue. Plain
   stiffness with no pain → proceed.
2. **Turn each goal into a retest position.** Two to four areas only. Each gets one
   position the person can repeat every two weeks and write down:

   | Area | Retest position | Record |
   |---|---|---|
   | Ankle | Knee-to-wall lunge, heel down | toe-to-wall distance each side |
   | Hip flexors | Half-kneeling, squeeze glute, shift forward | can you stay upright without arching? (Y/N) |
   | Hips / adductors | Deep squat hold holding a doorframe | heels down? seconds held |
   | Hamstrings | Standing hip hinge, knees soft | where the fingertips reach on the leg |
   | Shoulders | Back flat to wall, arms overhead | do thumbs touch the wall without the back arching? |
   | Thoracic spine | Seated rotation, arms crossed | how far the shoulder turns past a fixed mark |

3. **Choose the method per area.** *Flexibility* is passive range (static stretch).
   *Mobility* is range you can control (slow end-range reps, loaded stretch such as a
   goblet-squat hold). Reviews comparing them have found full-range strength training
   improves range about as well as stretching, so a person who lifts gets at least one
   loaded-range option per area, and range used in training is the range that lasts.
4. **Set the dose from general guidance (DS-26).** Widely cited exercise guidance for
   adults (ACSM) is the starting point, not a prescription:

   | Parameter | Default | Change when |
   |---|---|---|
   | Static hold | 10–30 s, repeated to accumulate ~60 s per stretch | 65+: holds of 30–60 s are commonly advised |
   | Intensity | mild tension, ~3–5 / 10 — never pain | always |
   | Frequency | ≥2–3 days a week; daily changes range faster | time is short → 3 days of 8 min beats 1 day of 25 |
   | Dynamic reps | 6–10 controlled reps, range growing each rep | — |
   | Active end-range | 3–5 slow reps, 3–5 s pause at the end | — |
   | Contract-relax (PNF) | 3–6 s gentle push (≤ half effort) then 10–30 s stretch | only after 2 weeks of plain holds |
   | Timeline | noticeable change in ~4–8 weeks of consistent practice | gains fade within weeks if stopped |

5. **Place it around training.** Before training: 5–8 minutes of *dynamic* work for
   the joints that session uses. Long static holds just before strength or power work
   can briefly reduce output, so keep pre-session holds short (under ~60 s per muscle)
   or move them to after the session or a separate 10–15 minute slot. Desk days get a
   3–4 minute micro-version twice a day.
6. **Write the must-nots and the stop rule (DP-04).**
   - Must not stretch into pain; discomfort above ~5/10 means back off.
   - Must not bounce at end range or force a joint (e.g. a knee twisted to reach a
     cross-legged or pigeon position — the hip should do the work).
   - Must not stretch through numbness, tingling, or shooting pain — stop; if it
     recurs, see a clinician.
   - Must not add stretching to joints that already move unusually far and have
     slipped or dislocated — that is a stability question for a physiotherapist.
   - Must not be presented as treatment for back, neck, or joint pain.
7. **State what text cannot do (OC-09).** It cannot tell a soft muscular limit from a
   hard, blocked end-feel, judge hypermobility, or find why one side differs. A hard
   stop, a marked side-to-side difference that does not change in 4 weeks, or a click
   with pain → one session with a physiotherapist or qualified coach.
8. **Write the routine and the retest calendar**, then hand placement to the routine
   designer if the week is crowded.

## Output Format

```
# Mobility routine — [name]
Readiness: [result]; limits applied: [...]
Areas screened: [area → proceed | routed (reason)]

## Targets and retests
| Area | Task it serves | Retest position | Baseline | Retest dates |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## Routine A — before training ([n] min, dynamic)
| Move | Reps | Cue to check |
|---|---|---|

## Routine B — after training or stand-alone ([n] min)
| Move | Method (static / active / loaded / PNF) | Dose | Cue to check |
|---|---|---|---|

## Desk micro-version ([n] min, [n]× a day)
[3–4 moves]

## Weekly placement
[days × routine]

## Must-nots and stop rule
[list; where routed signs go]

## What this can't do
[one line; when to see a physio or coach]
```

## Verification

- [ ] The area screen ran before any area got a routine; routed areas get nothing.
- [ ] Every target links to a task and a retest position with a baseline.
- [ ] Holds, reps, and frequency are numeric and labelled as general guidance.
- [ ] Long static holds are not placed immediately before strength or power work.
- [ ] Each area has at least one active or loaded option if the person lifts.
- [ ] The stop rule names pain, nerve symptoms, and instability, and where each goes.
- [ ] Every GO-WITH-LIMITS limit is visible in the routine.

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **Tight is not always short.** A hamstring that feels tight can be guarding, or a
   nerve that dislikes tension; stretching harder is not the answer to a nerve symptom.
2. **More range is not always better.** Someone who already bends easily needs
   control, not more stretch; don't prescribe splits to the hypermobile.
3. **Stretching does not prevent injury on its own.** Do not sell it as injury
   insurance; sell it as range for tasks.
4. **Don't rehab by the back door.** "Gentle stretches for your sore knee" is rehab;
   route the knee and keep the routine for areas that only feel stiff.
5. **Don't stack twenty moves.** Two to four areas, 8–15 minutes; a routine that
   takes 40 minutes is skipped by week two.
6. **Don't call a retest a diagnosis.** A short knee-to-wall number is a baseline to
   beat, not evidence of a problem.
7. **Don't pad a clean GO.** No paragraph of warnings for someone who is merely stiff.

## Example Output

Scenario: 37, GO, desk job, lifting 2×/week (Mon/Thu evenings), goal "squat to depth
without my heels lifting, and sit cross-legged on the floor with my kids"; stiff hips
and ankles, no pain; 10 min on lifting days, 15 min Saturday; wall, band, 8 kg dumbbell.

```
# Mobility routine — Dan
Readiness: GO; limits applied: none
Areas screened: ankles → proceed · hips → proceed · upper back → proceed

## Targets and retests
| Ankle      | heels-down squat     | knee-to-wall, heel down | L 6 cm, R 8 cm | wk 2, 4, 6, 8 |
| Hips       | squat depth, floor sitting | doorframe deep squat | heels lift; 10 s | wk 2, 4, 6, 8 |
| Upper back | bar position, overhead | wall arms-overhead    | thumbs 5 cm off wall | wk 4, 8 |

## Routine A — before lifting (6 min, dynamic)
| Knee-to-wall rocks       | 10/side | heel stays down |
| Bodyweight squat to box  | 8, box lowered weekly | chest tall, knees track toes |
| Open-book rotations      | 8/side  | hips stay stacked |
| Band pull-aparts         | 12      | ribs down |

## Routine B — after lifting / Saturday (10–15 min)
| Goblet-squat hold, 8 kg  | loaded   | 3 × 20 s          | elbows push knees out |
| Half-kneeling hip flexor | static   | 2 × 30 s/side     | squeeze glute, no back arch |
| Wall calf stretch, knee bent | static | 2 × 30 s/side | heel down |
| Seated wide-leg lean     | static → PNF from wk 3 | 2 × 30 s | hinge at hips, not the low back |
| Thoracic extension over rolled towel | active | 5 × 5 s | ribs stay down |

## Desk micro-version (3 min, 2× a day)
Standing calf stretch 30 s/side · seated rotation 5/side · doorframe squat hold 20 s

## Weekly placement
Mon/Thu: A before, B after lifting · Tue/Fri: desk version · Sat: B (15 min) · Sun: off

## Must-nots and stop rule
Mild pull 3–5/10 only. No bouncing. Floor sitting: if the knee twists or hurts, sit on a
cushion and let the hips open over weeks. Tingling down the leg in the wide-leg lean →
stop that move; if it recurs, see a clinician.

## What this can't do
Judge why the left ankle is stiffer. If L stays ≥2 cm behind R at week 4, one physio visit.
```

## Techniques Used

- **QA-08 Gate-Based Verification** — readiness gate, then a per-area pain screen before any routine.
- **DS-26 Safe Defaults Pattern** — hold, intensity, frequency, and PNF defaults with stated reasons to change.
- **OC-09 Capability Boundary Specification** — what text cannot judge (end-feel, hypermobility, asymmetry) and who can.
- **DP-04 Must-Not Constraints** — explicit must-nots for pain, bouncing, forced joints, nerve signs, and instability.
