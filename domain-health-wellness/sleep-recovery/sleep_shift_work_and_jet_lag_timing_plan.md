# Shift-Work and Jet-Lag Sleep Timing Plan

**Objective:** Place sleep, naps, light, and caffeine on a healthy adult's real roster
or itinerary so the body clock is pushed in the right direction (or deliberately left
alone), the drive home is safe, and the return to a normal week is planned — after
routing anything that looks like a sleep disorder or a safety problem.

> **Readiness gate.** Start from a Readiness Profile
> (`foundations/wellness_readiness_and_red_flag_screen.md`). No profile → ask its
> blocks 1–8 first. URGENT-CARE-NOW, CLINICIAN-FIRST, or OUT-OF-SCOPE → stop and
> restate the route. An urgent result means contacting the local emergency number now
> (e.g. 911 in the US). The profile's *Sleep flags* line is re-checked in step 1 below.

**When to Use:**
- You work nights, early starts, or a rotating roster and feel wrecked on nights, on
  the drive home, or on days off.
- You are flying across three or more time zones and want to arrive working.
- **Not this prompt if** your hours are ordinary and you still sleep badly — use
  `domain-health-wellness/sleep-recovery/sleep_routine_and_environment_audit.md`. Trouble
  sleeping 3+ nights a week for 3+ months with daytime impact is insomnia: see a
  clinician, and use
  `domain-psychology/client-self-use/coping-by-concern/clientself_sleep_cbt_i_sleep_restriction_calculator.md`
  only with or after clinician review. Trip logistics (flights, bookings, packing) are
  `domain-productivity/home-life/home_travel_trip_planner.md`; meal timing around shifts
  goes to `domain-health-wellness/nutrition/nutrition_meal_structure_planner.md` and its
  STRONG-GUARD.

## Inputs / Context

1. **Readiness Profile**, including *Sleep flags*.
2. **Mode A — shifts:** two to four weeks of roster (start and end times, runs of nights,
   days off), commute and how you travel, whether naps are allowed at work.
3. **Mode B — travel:** home and destination time zones, direction, flight times, days
   at destination, fixed commitments there, and the return.
4. **Current pattern:** usual sleep times on each shift type or at home, caffeine timing,
   sleepiness on the drive home or on arrival (rough 1–9 rating is fine).
5. **Bedroom for daytime sleep:** darkness, noise, household.

## Method

1. **Route first (QA-08).** Any of these → clinician before a plan, stated plainly:
   dozing while driving or at work, or nodding off in safety-critical tasks (and today:
   do not drive drowsy — nap first, or use a ride or transit); snoring with witnessed
   pauses or gasping; the insomnia pattern above (CBT-I prompt only with or after
   clinician review); known bipolar disorder or a seizure disorder (sleep loss and
   shifting can trigger episodes); any medicine taken by the clock — insulin, other
   blood-sugar or heart-rate medicines, contraceptives — across a time-zone change
   (pharmacist or clinician plans that). Sleep aids, melatonin, and stimulants are
   clinician or pharmacist questions; this prompt gives no doses or timing for them.
2. **Pick the mode (OC-08)** and a strategy by length.
   - *Shifts:* short runs of 1–3 nights → stay mostly day-oriented, use a pre-shift nap,
     and recover fast. Long runs (4+ consecutive nights) → hold a consistent daytime
     sleep window across the run and shift partway toward nights.
   - *Travel:* under ~3 days at the destination → stay near home time where commitments
     allow. Longer → adapt; the body clock commonly moves about an hour a day, more
     slowly eastward (general estimate, not a promise).
3. **Place sleep windows and naps on the calendar (DS-06).** Main sleep first, then a
   pre-night nap (often 1–2 h in the afternoon before a first night), then short naps
   (≤ ~20–30 min) where allowed. For travel, a pre-departure shift of about an hour a day
   for 1–3 days if the schedule allows (earlier for eastward, later for westward).
4. **Light, by direction.** Night shift: bright light in the first half of the shift;
   dim the trip home (dark glasses only if safe for driving) and a dark bedroom. Travel
   westward: seek late-afternoon and evening light, avoid bright early-morning light for
   the first days. Eastward: seek morning light — but after a big eastward jump, avoid
   light in the first hours after dawn for a day or two and seek late-morning light.
5. **Caffeine timing, not doses.** Early in the shift or the destination morning; none
   in roughly the last 6–8 hours before the main sleep.
6. **The transition.** After the last night: a short morning sleep (about 2–3 h), up
   for daylight, an early normal bedtime. After travel: the return gets its own plan.
7. **Drive-home and arrival safety.** A nap before driving when sleepy; no driving when
   fighting sleep; arrival-day driving avoided after an overnight flight where possible.
8. **Experiment and review.** Two rosters or the trip, the plan as written, one measure
   (sleepiness at a fixed point, or total sleep after nights), a success line stated
   before starting, and one change at review.
9. **Dual-failure check (QA-20).** Did a route sign become "just try earlier light"? Did
   a clean GO get buried in warnings? Fix either before sending.

## Output Format

```
# Sleep timing plan — [name] — Mode [A shifts | B travel]
Readiness: [result] | Route: none | [sign → clinician / pharmacist]

## Strategy: [day-oriented short runs | consistent window for long runs | stay on home time | adapt]
## Calendar
| Day | Work / travel | Main sleep | Nap | Light: seek / avoid | Caffeine cut-off |
## Drive-home / arrival safety
## Transition: [after last night | return trip]
## Experiment
Measure: [..] | Counts as helped if: [..] | Review: [date]
## Not included, on purpose
No melatonin, sleep aids, stimulants, or medication timing (ask a clinician or pharmacist) · no diagnosis
```

## Verification

- [ ] Route signs (drowsy driving, apnea signs, insomnia pattern, bipolar or seizure disorder, clock-timed medicines) were checked first.
- [ ] Strategy matches run length or trip length.
- [ ] Every sleep window and nap sits on the actual roster or itinerary.
- [ ] Light advice matches the direction of the shift.
- [ ] Caffeine has a cut-off before the main sleep and no doses.
- [ ] Drive-home or arrival safety is explicit.
- [ ] No melatonin, sleep aid, stimulant, or medication timing is given.
- [ ] A measure, success line, and review date exist; dual-failure check done.

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **Don't plan around drowsy driving.** Dozing at the wheel is a referral and a
   same-day safety step, not a scheduling problem.
2. **Don't fully flip for two nights.** Rotating your whole clock for a short run costs
   more on days off than it saves on nights.
3. **Don't give one light rule for every trip.** Morning light helps eastward and
   hinders westward; after a big eastward jump, too-early light pushes the wrong way.
4. **Don't dose melatonin or sleep aids.** It is widely used for jet lag and shift work;
   timing and dose belong with a clinician or pharmacist.
5. **Don't promise adaptation.** Many night workers never fully adapt; the goal is safer
   nights and better recovery, not a reset clock.
6. **Don't ignore the household.** Daytime sleep fails on noise and interruptions as
   often as on biology; plan the room and the people.

## Example Output

Scenario: 34, warehouse team lead, GO, sleep flags none; roster D D N N off ×4 (12 h,
06:00/18:00 changeovers); 25-min drive; feels heavy-eyed on the drive home after nights,
never dozed; naps allowed on the 02:00 break; coffee at 17:00, 22:00, 03:00.

Mode B, briefly: London → New York (westward, 5 zones, 6 days) — two days before,
bed and wake 1 h later each day; on arrival seek light until ~19:00 local and stay up
to ~22:00; avoid bright light before ~08:00 for two days. Return (eastward, 5 zones):
avoid bright light before ~10:00 London time for the first two days, then seek
late-morning light; no driving on the morning of arrival.

## Techniques Used

- **QA-08 Gate-Based Verification** — readiness gate and sleep-disorder and safety routes before any plan.
- **OC-08 Multi-Mode Prompt Architecture** — one prompt, shift-work and travel modes with their own strategies.
- **DS-06 Prioritization and Severity Guidance** — main sleep, then pre-night nap, then short naps; safety first.
- **QA-20 Dual-Failure Quality Test** — no softened routes, no buried GO.
