# Hydration and Heat Plan

**Objective:** Give a healthy adult who trains a simple drinking approach and a plan for
hot days — when to move, shorten, or skip a session, how to adapt to heat over two
weeks — with the emergency signs of heat illness and over-drinking stated before
anything else.

> **Readiness gate.** Start from a Readiness Profile
> (`foundations/wellness_readiness_and_red_flag_screen.md`). No profile → ask its
> blocks 1–8 first. URGENT-CARE-NOW, CLINICIAN-FIRST, or OUT-OF-SCOPE → stop and
> restate the route. An urgent result means contacting the local emergency number
> now (e.g. 911 in the US). GO-WITH-LIMITS → every limit binds this prompt, and the
> *Nutrition guard* line must still read CLEAR (the STRONG-GUARD block below).
> If the readiness gate and the STRONG-GUARD both fire, the gate's route wins; the
> audit's redirect template is used only once the gate is GO or GO-WITH-LIMITS.

> **STRONG-GUARD prompt.** Fluids are a quiet route into restriction and compensation:
> "sweating it off", drinking water to quiet hunger, or skipping drinks to look lighter
> on the scale. This prompt **stops and redirects** — no modified plan, no "gentle"
> version — when the Readiness Profile's nutrition guard reads STOP, or the
> conversation shows any of: fluid restriction, sauna suits, diuretics, or deliberate
> dehydration to change weight or "make weight"; drinking large amounts to suppress
> appetite; restriction for weight control or out of fear; compensation (vomiting,
> laxatives, fasting, or exercise to "undo" food); binge or loss-of-control eating;
> thoughts about food, weight, or body shape that crowd out other things; in people
> who train, missed or stopped periods, repeated bone-stress injuries, or energy and
> performance falling while training more (signs of RED-S); a rapid weight-loss goal
> (more than about 1% of body weight a week, a large loss by a date, or an intake below
> the commonly cited unsupervised floors of roughly 1,200 / 1,500 kcal a day — guard
> triggers, not targets); age under 18; pregnancy or breastfeeding; or a diagnosed
> condition, including a clinician-set fluid limit (some heart and kidney conditions).
>
> **This prompt never** gives a sodium, electrolyte, or supplement dose, sets a fixed
> fluid volume, or uses body-weight change to measure sweat loss. When the guard
> fires, the whole response is the **Redirect template** in
> `nutrition/nutrition_eating_pattern_audit.md` (inside its STRONG-GUARD block), used
> verbatim with the same adjustments for minors, pregnancy, and diagnosed conditions —
> it names a clinician and a registered dietitian, links the eating self-monitoring
> prompt, and gives 988 in the US — then stop. **The emergency signs in Method step 2
> are always given, even when the guard fires.**

**When to Use:**
- You train outdoors and a hot spell, a summer event, or a trip somewhere hot is coming.
- You get headaches, cramps, or feel wiped out after hot sessions.
- You are unsure whether you are drinking too little — or too much — on long sessions.
- **Not this prompt if** you want what to *eat* around training
  (`nutrition/nutrition_meal_structure_planner.md`) or a full event build
  (`fitness/fitness_endurance_event_build_plan.md`, whose urgent stops this expands).
  Someone collapsed or confused in the heat right now needs emergency services, not a
  plan. Hot bedrooms and poor sleep go to `sleep-recovery/sleep_routine_and_environment_audit.md`.

## Inputs / Context

1. **Readiness Profile** — *Gate result* GO or GO-WITH-LIMITS, and *Nutrition guard* CLEAR.
2. **Training:** type, usual session length, time of day, indoor or outdoor; any
   session over ~90 minutes; an event date and its expected conditions.
3. **Climate:** usual and forecast temperatures and humidity; whether your weather
   service issues heat warnings; whether a WBGT reading is available (some events and
   sports bodies publish one).
4. **What happens now:** what and when you drink; any headaches, cramps, dizziness, or
   very dark urine after sessions.
5. **Regular medications**, by name if you know them (some change heat tolerance).
6. **Acclimatisation:** how many recent sessions you have done in similar heat.

## Method

1. **Run the gates (QA-08).** *Gate result* GO or GO-WITH-LIMITS, else restate the route.
   Then *Nutrition guard* CLEAR and no STRONG-GUARD signal; otherwise the redirect
   template, plus the emergency signs, and stop.
2. **State the warning signs first, by time window (AG-19).**

   | Window | Signs | Action |
   |---|---|---|
   | **Emergency — now** | Confusion, odd behaviour, slurred speech, collapse, seizure, very hot skin (even if still sweating) — possible heat stroke. Or, after drinking a lot: worsening headache, vomiting, confusion, swollen hands — possible over-drinking (low blood sodium) | Call emergency services (911 in the US, or local). Move to shade, remove extra clothing, cool with cold water, ice, wet towels, and fanning while waiting. Do not give drinks to someone confused; after heavy drinking, give no more water |
   | **Next 30 minutes** | Heavy sweating, dizziness, headache, nausea, cramps, pale clammy skin, weakness — heat exhaustion | Stop. Cool place, lie down, sip water if fully alert. Not clearly better within 30 minutes, or any emergency sign → emergency services |
   | **Same day** | Very dark (cola-coloured) urine with severe muscle pain or swelling | Same-day urgent care |
   | **Same day** | Dark urine, headache, washed out after a session, otherwise well | Drink to thirst, eat a normal meal; no hard training in heat today |

3. **Set thirst-led drinking, and say why there is no number (CM-11).**
   - Default: drink with meals and to thirst through the day; pale-straw urine most of
     the day is a reasonable everyday check.
   - Sessions under ~60–90 minutes in ordinary conditions: water to thirst is enough.
   - Long or hot sessions: plan *where* drinks are (carry them, map water stops) and
     drink to thirst at each; for sessions over ~90 minutes, sports drinks with
     carbohydrate and electrolytes are a common option — practise them in training,
     never first on event day.
   - **Why no fixed volume or electrolyte figure:** sweat and salt losses vary
     several-fold between people and days, so a number is wrong for many; drinking on a
     schedule beyond thirst during long events is how over-drinking (low blood sodium)
     happens, and it can kill; and salt or electrolyte dosing interacts with blood
     pressure, kidney conditions, and medications. Individual sweat testing and
     electrolyte planning → a registered (sports) dietitian; medication questions → a
     pharmacist.
4. **Rate the day.** Use the local heat warning or, where published, WBGT. Commonly
   used event flag bands (ACSM, general guidance): below ~18 °C low risk; ~18–23 °C
   moderate; ~23–28 °C high — shorten, slow, add shade and drinks, unacclimatised people
   ease right off; above ~28 °C extreme — cancel or move hard and long sessions. With
   only air temperature, treat humidity, direct sun, and no wind as moving the day up a band.
5. **Adjust hot-day sessions.** In order: move to the coolest hours or indoors; hold
   *effort*, not pace (talk test or RPE — pace will slow); shorten; swap intervals for
   easy work; light, loose clothing and shade. Risk raisers that lower the bar further:
   not acclimatised, a recent fever or stomach bug (no training in heat until well),
   short sleep, alcohol the night before, older age, heavy gear, and some regular
   medications — ask a pharmacist whether yours affect heat tolerance.
6. **Write the acclimatisation ramp.** About 10–14 days of training in the heat, starting
   with 20–30 minutes easy and adding time before intensity; most adaptation (earlier
   and more sweating, lower heart rate at the same effort) arrives within that window
   and fades over a few weeks without heat.
7. **Dual-failure check (QA-20).** Did a guard signal get a hydration plan instead of
   the redirect? Did a clean, cool-weather 45-minute gym routine get a heat lecture?
   The emergency table is the only warning a clean GO needs.

## Output Format

```
# Hydration and heat plan — [name]
Readiness: GO | GO-WITH-LIMITS  (anything else → restate the route; nothing below)
Nutrition guard: CLEAR  (if STOP → redirect template + emergency signs only)

## Warning signs — read first
| Window | Signs | Action |

## Everyday drinking
[thirst-led default; session rules; why no number]

## Rating the day
[source: heat warning | WBGT]; bands → what changes

## Hot-day session moves
[ordered moves for this person's sessions]

## Acclimatisation ramp
| Day | Session in heat | Notes |

## Not included, on purpose
No fixed fluid volume · no salt/electrolyte/supplement doses · no weighing → [who to ask]
```

## Verification

- [ ] Gate, then guard; a guard signal produced only the redirect plus the emergency table.
- [ ] The emergency table appears before any plan content and includes over-drinking.
- [ ] No sodium, electrolyte, supplement, or fixed fluid-volume number appears.
- [ ] No body-weight measurement is used or suggested.
- [ ] Heat bands are attributed and labelled general guidance.
- [ ] Acclimatisation adds duration before intensity over ~10–14 days.

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **"Drink more" is not always safer.** Over-drinking on long events is a real
   emergency; thirst-led is the default for a reason.
2. **Sweating does not rule out heat stroke.** Very hot skin plus confusion is an
   emergency whether the person is sweating or not.
3. **Cramps are not proof of salt loss.** Don't answer cramps with a salt dose; answer
   them with the heat moves and, if persistent, a clinician or sports dietitian.
4. **Urine colour is a rough guide.** Some vitamins and foods change it; never use it to
   diagnose.
5. **Don't fix a hydration plan onto a weight goal.** "I want to drop water weight for a
   photo" is a guard signal.
6. **Don't over-warn cool-weather training.** A clean GO for a 45-minute indoor session
   gets the emergency table and "drink to thirst" — nothing more.

## Example Output

Scenario: Ana, 36, GO, nutrition guard CLEAR; runs 4×/week at 18:00, 40–70 min; 10K race
in 3 weeks at 09:00; forecast heat wave (33 °C afternoons, humid); no heat sessions yet;
headaches after the last two evening runs; no regular medications.

```
# Hydration and heat plan — Ana
Readiness: GO
Nutrition guard: CLEAR

## Warning signs — read first
| Emergency — now | confusion, odd behaviour, collapse, very hot skin; or after a lot of
  drinking: worsening headache, vomiting, confusion | 911; shade; cool with cold water,
  ice, fanning; no drinks if confused |
| Next 30 min | dizzy, sick, cramping, clammy | stop, cool, lie down, sip if alert; not
  better in 30 min → 911 |
| Same day | cola-coloured urine + severe muscle pain | urgent care |

## Everyday drinking
To thirst with meals and through the workday; the post-run headaches suggest starting the
18:00 run already short — have a drink with your 16:00 snack. Runs under 70 min: carry a
small bottle, drink to thirst. No fixed amount — sweat varies too much between people.

## Rating the day
Weather-service heat warnings. Warning in force → move runs to 06:30, or treadmill.

## Hot-day session moves
Run by talk test, accept ~30–60 s/km slower; Thursday intervals → easy 40 min; shaded
riverside loop with a fountain at 3 km.

## Acclimatisation ramp (race is at 09:00, likely ~26 °C)
| Days 1–3   | 25–30 min easy in the warm part of the evening | stop at any warning sign |
| Days 4–7   | 40 min easy                                     | add time, not speed |
| Days 8–12  | 50–60 min, one with 3 × 5 min steady            | — |
| Days 13–19 | normal training, cooler hours for hard sessions | race week: easy |

## Not included, on purpose
No fixed fluid volume · no salt or electrolyte doses (sports dietitian) · no weighing
```

## Techniques Used

- **QA-08 Gate-Based Verification** — readiness gate, then the STRONG-GUARD, before any plan.
- **AG-19 Time-Critical Response Protocol** — warning signs tiered by window: now, 30 minutes, same day.
- **CM-11 Reasoning-Based Constraint Design** — the no-number rule explained (variability, over-drinking, interactions).
- **QA-20 Dual-Failure Quality Test** — no plan through a guard signal; no heat lecture on a clean GO.
