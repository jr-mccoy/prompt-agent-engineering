# Domain: Negotiation

Negotiation as a working discipline, across the whole arc: what you do before you walk in, what you do once it starts, what happens in the medium the conversation actually travels through, how it changes with three or more parties, and what you owe the agreement after it is signed. The 46 prompts here treat negotiation as a set of specific, drillable decisions rather than a temperament — where to anchor and why, which concession buys what, which of five impasses you are actually in, what a counterpart's behaviour does and does not reveal, and when no deal is the correct outcome.

The domain spans the lifecycle. **Before:** size the preparation, derive the walkaway, map interests, build the packages, design the concession ladder, model the counterpart (`preparation/`). **During:** question, read signals, defend against coercion, break the deadlock, handle the emotional layer, close (`at-the-table/`). **Through a medium:** written, async, remote, and across contexts whose norms you have not established (`channels/`). **With more parties:** coordinate your own side, face a bloc, or run a negotiation you are not a party to (`multi-party/`). **After:** debrief, implement, reopen, or recover from no deal (`after-the-deal/`). Then the named specializations (`contexts/`), the relationship-primary track where the goal is to be understood rather than to win (`difficult-conversations/`), and the layer that makes any of it compound (`craft/`).

Users are executives, founders, salespeople, people leaders, lawyers, project leads, and individuals heading into anything with material stakes — salary, contract, term sheet, vendor renewal, partnership split, property, or dissolution. The domain assumes you will negotiate more than once, and that the second time should go better than the first.

## When to use this domain

- Preparing for a negotiation with material stakes, and unsure how much preparation it warrants.
- You don't know your walkaway — or have never estimated the other side's.
- The conversation has stalled, hardened, or turned hostile, and you need a diagnosis rather than more of what already failed.
- A counterpart is applying pressure and you want to respond without capitulating or escalating.
- Terms are agreed and nothing is happening, or circumstances have changed and terms need to move.
- You negotiate regularly, your outcomes cluster in a way you can't explain, and experience alone has stopped producing improvement.

## When NOT to use this domain (use a different one)

- **Drafting the legal position paper, redlining the contract, or valuing a settlement** → `domain-legal/contracts-transactional/` (`legal_negotiation_position_paper.md`, `legal_playbook_builder_for_contract_type.md`), `domain-legal/litigation/legal_settlement_value_range_analysis.md`.
- **Divorce or custody mediation strategy** → `domain-legal/divorce/` and `domain-legal/custody/` (party-side mediation prep, briefs, impasse strategy). `multi-party/negotiation_facilitator_third_party.md` is the neutral-role complement, not a substitute.
- **Evaluating an offer rather than negotiating it** → `domain-personal-development/major-decisions/personal_career_offer_evaluation.md`, or `personal_major_purchase_research.md`.
- **Setting your price before the conversation** → `domain-personal-development/prompts/solo-dev/solo_dev_pricing_value_confidence.md`.
- **Live in-character role-play rather than written preparation** → `domain-conversation-practice/conversation_practice_simulator.md`.
- **Aligning internal stakeholders rather than bargaining** → `domain-decision-making/decisioning_rapid_stakeholder_alignment.md`; org politics generally → `domain-personal-development/prompts/stakeholder/`.
- **Personal-life boundaries and relationship repair** → `domain-personal-development/prompts/relationships/`, `domain-psychology/client-self-use/relational/`.
- **Low-stakes asks where preparation overhead exceeds the value** — just ask.

## How prompts in this domain compose

The spine runs **triage → prepare → table → after**. `preparation/negotiation_prep_depth_triage.md` is the entry point: it scores the negotiation and emits an ordered prompt sequence, so the rest of the domain is consumed by tier rather than exhaustively. Inside preparation the chain is `batna_analysis` (the floor and the range) → `leverage_audit` (everything the floor doesn't capture) → `interest_mapping` (what the range is actually about) → `package_trade_design` (offers that capture it) → `opening_offer_design` (the ceiling) → `concession_anchoring_plan` (the ground between ceiling and floor) → `counterpart_simulation` and `information_plan` (who you're facing, and what you'll ask and protect) → `pre_meeting_rehearsal` (all of it, as spoken moments).

`at-the-table/` prompts are consulted **during**, not read in sequence — `question_sequencing_live` opens, `reading_signals_and_bluffs` interprets, and the other five are triggered by events: pressure tactics, deadlock, an approval claim, heat, or a close in reach. `channels/` overlays whichever medium the conversation travels through, and `multi-party/` overlays party count and team composition.

Afterward, `after-the-deal/negotiation_post_negotiation_debrief.md` scores process and outcome separately and feeds `craft/negotiation_pattern_library_builder.md`, which aggregates across negotiations and feeds back into `prep_depth_triage` — the loop that makes the domain compound rather than merely accumulate. `implementation_and_relationship` runs on any deal that closed and hands to `renegotiate_existing_agreement` when terms need to move; `no_deal_recovery` runs on any that didn't.

`contexts/` prompts are entry points for their named situations and cross-link **upstream** rather than restating theory — the salary prompt assumes `batna_analysis`, the vendor prompt assumes `leverage_audit`. `difficult-conversations/` is a parallel track, not a stage: use it whenever the goal is to be understood rather than to claim value, and note it carries its own prefix and an empathic rather than adversarial reasoning profile.

## Frontmatter conventions specific to this domain

All prompts carry the repo-standard fields plus the machine-readable `reasoning:` block (styles, stakes, horizon, uncertainty, evidence_quality, domain_complexity, collaboration, output_format, user_role, mode), with `uncertainty: ambiguity` as a domain invariant — you are always reasoning about a counterpart whose true position is unobservable.

The diagnostic field for routing within the domain is **`styles`**. The `negotiation_*` prompts carry `adversarial` or `strategic`: they are structurally adversarial by design, and each flags where that framing would corrode a relationship-primary negotiation. The `difficultconvo_*` prompts carry `empathic` instead and drop `negotiation` from their tags entirely. `craft/` prompts carry `reflective` and `evaluative` and are the only ones with `stakes: low` — the practice layer is deliberately cheap. **`mode`** separates the lifecycle: `plan`/`decide`/`rehearse` in `preparation/`, `diagnose`/`respond` in `at-the-table/`, `audit`/`synthesize`/`document` in `after-the-deal/` and `craft/`.

`category` always matches the subdirectory (`negotiation/preparation`, `negotiation/at-the-table`, …). `related_prompts` is exactly three entries, at least one intra-domain. One prompt — `channels/negotiation_cross_cultural.md` — carries an explicit guard block, the only one in the domain: it refuses to generate or accept generalizations about how people of a nationality, ethnicity, or region negotiate, and requires every profile entry to trace to an observation of the specific counterpart or a sourced institutional fact.
