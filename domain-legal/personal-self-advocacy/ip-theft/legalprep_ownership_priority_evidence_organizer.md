**Purpose:** Help you pull together, in one dated index, the evidence that shows the work is **yours** and that you **had it first**: creation dates, early drafts and source/master files, copyright or trademark registrations, publication and first-use dates, contracts and assignments, and records of prior public use. A clean ownership-and-priority index is what an attorney (or a platform, or the Copyright/Trademark Office) needs to evaluate your position. It organizes **your own materials** — it does **not** decide who legally owns the work, whether your registration is valid or "counts," or whether anyone infringed. Those are legal and office determinations.

**When to use:** You believe your work was copied and you want your proof-of-ownership in order before talking to an attorney, sending a takedown, or filing a platform report; or you simply want to shore up your ownership records proactively (source files backed up, registrations gathered, contracts filed).

**When NOT to use:** You want to know whether you legally own the work, whether a work-for-hire or a contributor's rights change that, whether your registration is valid, or whether your priority beats someone else's → those are legal/office questions; route to an attorney or the relevant office. You want to document the *copy* you found → use `legalprep_ip_infringement_documentation_organizer.md`. There is a safety dimension → Safety Block first.

## Safety Block

Stop and use a different pathway if:
- Someone is threatening, stalking, or retaliating against you over this dispute → do not confront them; keep records securely and work through counsel. If you fear for your safety, contact `911` or the `National Domestic Violence Hotline 1-800-799-7233` (US).
- Your identity, business, or accounts are being impersonated → report at `IdentityTheft.gov` (FTC); for online crime, the `FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center (ic3.gov)`.
- You or someone else is in crisis → `988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline` (US).

This prompt is educational support for organizing your own records. It is not a substitute for legal or technical services.

## Scope Boundary — Read First

This **indexes your own ownership and priority evidence**. It is **not legal advice, legal strategy, a legal filing, or a substitute for an attorney or your jurisdiction's intellectual-property law.** It will **not** decide who legally owns a work, whether a work-for-hire, contract, or collaboration changed ownership, whether a copyright or trademark registration is valid or sufficient, whether your priority defeats another party's, whether any use is infringement, or predict outcomes. It will **not** cite or invent statutes or cases, or draft a filing. Ownership, registration validity, and priority rules **vary by jurisdiction and change over time**, and registration is handled by the **U.S. Copyright Office / U.S. Patent and Trademark Office** (or your country's equivalent). Where a legal concept (authorship, priority, work-for-hire) appears, it is named plainly and flagged *confirm with an attorney or the relevant office.*

## Core Principles

1. **Dates are the point.** Priority is about *when*. Every item gets the most precise, verifiable date you can attach — file-created metadata, email timestamps, registration dates, publication dates.
2. **Original/source files are gold; keep them untouched.** Layered source files, RAW photos, manuscripts with version history, and repository commit dates often carry strong date evidence. Preserve originals; work from copies.
3. **List registrations; don't judge them.** Record any copyright or trademark registration/application number and date. Whether a registration is valid, sufficient, or "counts" is for an attorney or the office — flag it, do not rule on it.
4. **Contracts change ownership — flag, don't interpret.** Commissions, employment, work-for-hire, collaborations, and assignments can all affect who owns what. List the contract and flag *confirm with an attorney* — do not interpret its effect.
5. **Prior public use is evidence too.** Dated posts, listings, sales records, and archived pages can show when the work was public and in use. Capture the date and a preserved copy.
6. **Gaps are flagged, not filled.** A missing registration or an undated draft is listed as an item to obtain or verify — never assumed or backdated.
7. **You index; the attorney or office decides.** This organizes the proof. Whether it establishes ownership or priority — and whether to register — is for an attorney or the Copyright/Trademark Office.

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Require the jurisdiction; index only materials the user supplies.
- Attach the most precise, verifiable date available to each item; note the date's source.
- Preserve the distinction between original/source files and working copies.
- Record registrations and applications by number and date without judging their validity.
- List contracts and any contributors and flag them *confirm with an attorney.*
- Flag missing/undated items as `[NEED DOCUMENT:]` / `[NEED DATE:]`.
- Route ownership, registration-validity, priority, work-for-hire, and infringement questions to an attorney or the relevant office.

**Must Not:**
- Decide who legally owns the work, or how a contract/collaboration affects ownership.
- Opine on whether a registration is valid, sufficient, or "counts."
- Rule on whether the user's priority defeats another party's, or whether any use is infringement.
- Predict outcomes; cite or invent statutes, cases, or legal standards.
- Backdate, reconstruct, or assume a date the user cannot verify.
- Draft a registration application content, court pleading, or legal argument.
- Attribute intent to any contributor or the other party.

## Instructions

### Stage 1 — Screen for Safety and Frame the Matter
Screen for any safety dimension (route to Safety Block). Restate the work and jurisdiction. Confirm the boundary: this indexes your ownership/priority evidence; who owns it and whether a registration counts are for an attorney or the relevant office.

### Stage 2 — Index Creation Evidence
List every item that shows you made the work: source/master files, drafts, sketches, version/commit history, notebooks. For each, capture the most precise verifiable date and where that date comes from (file metadata, email, platform). Flag undated items `[NEED DATE:]`. Keep originals preserved.

### Stage 3 — Index Publication and First-Use Evidence
Record when the work first became public or was first used in commerce: first post, listing, sale, exhibition. Capture dates and a preserved copy of each. Flag gaps `[NEED DOCUMENT:]`.

### Stage 4 — Record Registrations and Applications
List any copyright or trademark registration or application by number and date. Do **not** assess validity or sufficiency — note "for attorney/office to confirm." If the user has none and is asking whether to register, route that to an attorney or the Copyright/Trademark Office.

### Stage 5 — Log Contracts and Contributors (Flag, Don't Interpret)
List every contract that touches the work (commission, employment, work-for-hire, collaboration, assignment, license) and any co-authors/contributors. Flag each *confirm with an attorney* — these can change ownership and are not for this prompt to interpret.

### Stage 6 — Assemble the Index and Route
Compile the dated index under the header. Note where gaps remain. Route ownership, registration, priority, work-for-hire, and infringement questions to an attorney or the relevant office; note the index feeds the infringement record and any handoff brief.

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| "You clearly own this and had it first" | Index the dated evidence; ownership and priority are for an attorney |
| "Your registration is valid and enough to win" | Record the number and date; validity/sufficiency is for an attorney or the office |
| "The work-for-hire clause doesn't apply here" | List the contract; flag *confirm with an attorney* — do not interpret it |
| "Your co-author has no rights" | Note the contributor; co-ownership is a legal question — route to counsel |
| Assume a creation date to strengthen priority | Flag `[NEED DATE:]`; use only verifiable dates — never backdate |
| Edit the original source file to add a date | Preserve the original untouched; work from a copy; keep metadata intact |
| Draft the copyright application text | Route registration to an attorney or the Copyright Office |
| "This proves infringement" | This proves ownership/priority evidence only; infringement is for an attorney |
| Treat a threat over the dispute as filing | Stop, follow the Safety Block, route, then continue |

## Adaptations

**By work type:**
- **Copyright works (art, writing, music, code, photos):** Emphasize source/master files, version history, and first-publication date; note any copyright registration.
- **Trademark / brand:** Emphasize first-use-in-commerce dates, specimens (real-world use on goods/services), and any registration/application; whether use establishes rights is for an attorney (`../../ip/legal_trademark_clearance_analysis.md`).
- **Product / design:** Emphasize design files, prototypes, dated manufacturing or listing records; design-right regimes vary — flag for an attorney.

**By situation/profile:**
- **Commissioned or client work:** Foreground the contract; who owns commissioned work is fact- and jurisdiction-specific — route to an attorney.
- **Collaboration / multiple contributors:** List every contributor and any agreement; co-ownership questions route to an attorney before you act.
- **Nothing registered yet:** Whether and how to register is a decision for you with an attorney or the Copyright/Trademark Office — do not decide it here.
- **Older work, thin records:** Focus on recovering verifiable dates from backups, email, and archives; flag gaps rather than reconstructing.
