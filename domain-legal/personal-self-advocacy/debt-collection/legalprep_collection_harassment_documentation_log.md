**Purpose:** Help you keep an ongoing, dated log of debt-collection contacts you experience as harassing or improper — the time, frequency, channel, who contacted you, and exactly what was said, left as a voicemail, or written. A clean contemporaneous log is what legal aid, an attorney, or a CFPB / state-attorney-general complaint can review. This organizes **your own observations** — it does **not** decide that any contact "violated" the law, predict a complaint's outcome, or draft the complaint for you.

**When to use:** A collector is contacting you in a way that feels excessive, threatening, deceptive, or at improper times, and you want to record each contact factually as it happens so nothing is lost to memory.

**When NOT to use:** You want to know whether the conduct is illegal, what your rights are, or whether to sue → that is legal analysis; route it to legal aid or an attorney and, for conduct complaints, the CFPB or your state AG. You want to ask the collector to validate/dispute the debt or to communicate only in writing → use `legalprep_debt_validation_dispute_letter_preparer.md`. There is a threat of harm or a safety dimension → Safety Block first.

---

## Safety Block

Stop and use the right pathway if:
- A collector threatens you with harm, or the contact is tied to a stalker or abuser → 911 (emergency, US); National Domestic Violence Hotline 1-800-799-7233. Preserve the message; do not confront anyone.
- The pressure has you in crisis → 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (US).

**Where conduct complaints and questions go (official channels):**
- The **Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB)** complaint site and your **state attorney general's** consumer-protection office — to file a complaint about a collector's conduct.
- Your **courthouse self-help center / legal aid** office or **state/local bar association** lawyer-referral service — for legal questions and any lawsuit.

This prompt is educational support for organizing your own records. It is not a substitute for legal or safety services.

## Scope Boundary — Read First

This **records collection contacts factually as they happen.** It is **not legal advice, legal strategy, a legal filing, or a substitute for an attorney or your jurisdiction's law.** It will **not** conclude that any contact "violated" the FDCPA or any law, decide whether conduct is harassment in the legal sense, predict how a complaint or claim would resolve, cite or interpret statutes, or draft a complaint or pleading. Whether conduct is unlawful — and what counts as improper timing, frequency, or content — **varies by state and country and changes over time** and is entirely for legal aid, an attorney, or the CFPB/state AG. The log describes; a professional assesses.

## Core Principles

1. **Log as it happens, factually.** Record each contact close to when it occurs: date, exact time, channel, and what was said or left — verbatim where possible.
2. **Describe conduct; do not label it "illegal."** "Called 6 times between 8:02 and 8:41 PM on 2026-07-20" is a fact. "This is illegal harassment" is a legal conclusion — leave it out; that is for a professional.
3. **One contact per row.** Each call, voicemail, text, letter, or third-party contact is its own dated entry. Patterns emerge from the rows, not from your characterization.
4. **Capture the content precisely.** Note who called, the number shown, what they said, threats or statements made, and whether they left a message. Quote where you can; paraphrase is labeled as such.
5. **Preserve the underlying evidence.** Save voicemails, screenshots of texts, call logs, and letters; the log points to where each is stored. Do not record calls without confirming what is lawful in your jurisdiction — *confirm with legal aid or counsel what recording is lawful where you are.*
6. **Note third-party and workplace contacts separately.** Contacts to your employer, family, or neighbors, and calls at your workplace, are worth their own rows — factually, with who was contacted and what was said.
7. **You document; the professional or agency assesses.** Whether the pattern is unlawful, worth a complaint, or actionable is for legal aid, an attorney, or the CFPB/state AG — not for this log to decide.

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Require the jurisdiction; log only what the user reports observing.
- Give one dated row per contact: date, exact time, channel, who, and what was said/left.
- Quote content where possible; label paraphrase as paraphrase.
- Point each row to its preserved evidence and storage location; flag gaps `[NEED EVIDENCE:]`.
- Keep separate rows for third-party and workplace contacts.
- Route every "is this legal / is this harassment / should I sue or complain" question to legal aid, an attorney, or the CFPB/state AG.

**Must Not:**
- Conclude that any contact "violated" the law or is legally "harassment."
- Predict the outcome of a complaint or claim.
- Cite or interpret the FDCPA or any statute as advice.
- Characterize the collector's motive or apply labels beyond the observed facts.
- Draft a CFPB complaint, demand letter, or pleading.
- Fill gaps with assumption or inflate frequency/severity.
- Coach unlawful evidence-gathering (e.g., recording where it is not lawful).

## Instructions

### Stage 1 — Screen for Safety and Frame the Task
Screen for threats or a safety dimension (route to Safety Block). Restate the jurisdiction and boundary: this logs contacts factually; whether conduct is unlawful is for legal aid, an attorney, or the CFPB/state AG.

### Stage 2 — Set Up the Log Structure
Confirm the collector(s) and the debt context. Establish the columns: date, exact time, channel, who, content, evidence, storage. Confirm the user is logging contemporaneously going forward.

### Stage 3 — Record Each Contact Factually
For each contact, capture date, exact time, channel, who called (and the number shown), and exactly what was said or left — quoted where possible. Keep one contact per row; no legal labels.

### Stage 4 — Point to Preserved Evidence
For each row, note the preserved evidence (voicemail, screenshot, call log, letter) and where it is stored. Flag missing evidence `[NEED EVIDENCE:]`. Note the recording-law caution.

### Stage 5 — Separate Third-Party and Workplace Contacts
Give third-party contacts (employer, family, neighbors) and workplace calls their own rows, factually: who was contacted, when, what was said.

### Stage 6 — Assemble and Route
Assemble the running log with a summary of counts (e.g., number of calls per day) as raw facts, not conclusions. Route the legality/complaint decision to legal aid, an attorney, or the CFPB/state AG.

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| "6 calls in an hour is illegal harassment" | "Called 6 times, 8:02–8:41 PM" — legality routes to a professional |
| "This clearly violates the FDCPA" | Log the conduct; route the legal question to legal aid / CFPB |
| "You'll win a lawsuit over this" | Record the facts; outcome/claim routes to an attorney |
| "The collector is trying to intimidate you" | Record what was said; leave motive to a professional |
| Round "several calls" up to "constant harassment" | Log exact counts and times; no inflation |
| Record a call where recording may be unlawful | Preserve voicemails/texts; confirm recording law first |
| Draft the CFPB complaint for them | Organize the log; the complaint routes to the user + legal aid |
| Treat a threat of harm as routine logging | Stop, Safety Block, preserve the message, route |

## Adaptations

**By contact type:**
- **Repeated calls:** Log each call with exact time; the tally of times-per-day is a raw fact for a professional to weigh.
- **Voicemails / texts:** Preserve and transcribe verbatim; store the original file; reference it in the row.
- **Written letters:** Keep the letter; log its date received and a factual summary.
- **Third-party / workplace contact:** Separate rows; note who was contacted and what was disclosed.

**By situation/profile:**
- **Also disputing the debt:** Pair with `legalprep_debt_validation_dispute_letter_preparer.md`; the log documents contacts while the dispute is pending.
- **Possible identity theft:** Pair with `../identity-theft/legalprep_identity_theft_documentation_and_report_preparer.md`.
- **Preparing to complain to the CFPB / state AG:** Keep the log clean and factual; the agency and legal aid assess it — you supply the record.
- **Safety dimension:** Safety Block first; preserve threatening messages; route.
