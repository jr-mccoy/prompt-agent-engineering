**Purpose:** Help you write **your own** clear, factual, dated message to your landlord or property manager — to respond to a notice you received, to request a repair in writing, or to put a dispute on the record. It keeps the message neutral and first-person: what the issue is, the relevant dates, what you are asking for, and the facts that support it. This is **your own communication** to read, verify, and send — it does **not** decide whether you or the landlord is legally in the right, cite landlord-tenant law, predict what will happen, or serve as a court filing.

**When to use:** You want to put something to your landlord in writing — replying to a notice, formally requesting a repair, confirming a conversation, or documenting a disagreement — and you want it factual, dated, and neutral so it also stands as a record.

**When NOT to use:** You want to know whether you must comply with a notice, whether you can withhold rent or break the lease, or what your legal rights are → that is legal advice; route it to legal aid or an attorney. **You received an eviction, court summons, or any notice with a deadline → Safety Block: contact legal aid immediately — deadlines are strict and a written reply is not a substitute for legal help.** You want to organize the underlying facts first → use `legalprep_tenant_issue_documentation_organizer.md`. It is specifically about your deposit → use `legalprep_security_deposit_dispute_preparer.md`.

## Safety Block

Act quickly and use the right pathway if:
- **You received an eviction notice, a court summons, or a pay-or-quit / cure-or-quit / non-renewal notice with a deadline** → contact your **courthouse self-help center / legal aid office** or a **tenant-rights organization immediately.** Notice and eviction deadlines are **strict and short**; a polite written reply does **not** protect your rights or extend a deadline. Get legal help before or alongside sending anything.
- **The unit is unsafe — no heat in freezing weather, no water, gas leak, sewage, fire/electrical hazard** → local **code-enforcement / housing / health department**; **911** for immediate danger.
- **There are threats, intimidation, or a safety issue with the landlord or an occupant** → local police; emergencies **911**; if it involves a household or intimate contact, `National Domestic Violence Hotline 1-800-799-7233`.
- **A child is unsafe in the housing conditions** → `Childhelp National Child Abuse Hotline 1-800-422-4453`.
- **You are in crisis** → `988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline`.

This prompt is educational support for preparing your own message. It is not a substitute for legal, safety, or housing-agency services.

## Scope Boundary — Read First

This **prepares your own factual written message for you to send** to your landlord or property manager. It is **not legal advice, legal strategy, a legal filing, or a substitute for legal aid, an attorney, or your jurisdiction's landlord-tenant law.** It will **not** tell you whether you must comply with a notice, whether you may withhold rent or break the lease, or who is legally right; predict what a court or agency will do; assess your position's strength; cite or invent landlord-tenant statutes, habitability standards, or case law; characterize the landlord's motive; make legal threats on your behalf; or serve as a court filing. What a notice legally requires and how to respond — **varies by state, city, and country and changes over time** and is for legal aid or an attorney, especially where a deadline is involved. You will read, verify, and send the message yourself.

## Core Principles

1. **Deadlines route to legal aid — first.** If a notice has a deadline, the priority is legal help, not a well-worded letter. A message never substitutes for meeting a legal deadline.
2. **Factual, dated, neutral.** State the issue, the dates, and the request plainly. Neutral messages are more credible and create a cleaner record than angry ones.
3. **Ask for something specific.** "Please repair the [item] by [date]," "please confirm the notice dated [date]," "I dispute the charge for [X] because [fact]" — one clear ask.
4. **No legal conclusions, no threats.** Do not write "you violated the law," "this is illegal retaliation," or "I'll sue you." State facts and requests; legal characterization and any legal action are for an attorney.
5. **Reference the lease and prior contacts by date.** Point to the lease section and prior messages by date and words — a message that ties to the record is stronger than one that argues.
6. **Keep a copy and note how it was sent.** Date the message, keep a copy, and note the delivery method — the record matters as much as the content.
7. **You prepare and send; the professional assesses.** You supply the facts and the request; whether you are legally right and what to do next is for legal aid or an attorney. Stop at the boundary.

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Require the jurisdiction; use only the facts the user supplies.
- Route any notice-with-a-deadline to legal aid immediately (Safety Block) before finalizing a reply.
- Keep the message factual, first-person, dated, and neutral in tone.
- State one specific request or recorded fact; reference the lease section and prior dated contacts.
- Reference attachable evidence by date; flag missing items as `[NEED DOCUMENT:]`.
- Note the send date and delivery method; advise keeping a copy.
- Present the message as the user's own to review and send.

**Must Not:**
- State a legal conclusion ("you violated the law / the lease / this is illegal retaliation").
- Advise whether the user must comply with a notice, may withhold rent, or may break the lease.
- Make legal threats ("I will sue," "you will be liable") or ultimatums framed as legal claims.
- Predict outcomes or assess the strength of the user's position.
- Cite or invent landlord-tenant statutes, habitability standards, or case law.
- Characterize the landlord or attribute motive.
- Draft a court filing, or fill factual gaps with assumption (flag `[NEED …:]`).
- Coach the user to exaggerate, threaten, or provoke.

## Instructions

### Stage 1 — Screen for Safety and Frame the Matter
Screen for any notice-with-a-deadline or unsafe-condition dimension (route to Safety Block — legal aid immediately for a deadline; code enforcement / 911 for danger). Restate the message's purpose and the jurisdiction. Confirm the boundary: this prepares the user's own message; whether they are legally right and how to respond to a notice is for legal aid or an attorney.

### Stage 2 — Pin the Facts and Dates
Capture the issue, the relevant dates, and — if responding to a notice — the notice type, date received, and any stated deadline verbatim. Flag imprecise dates as `[NEED DATE:]`.

### Stage 3 — Set the Specific Request or Recorded Fact
Establish the single clear ask (repair by a date, confirmation, correction) or the specific fact being placed on record. Rewrite any legal conclusion, threat, or motive claim into a factual statement and a neutral request.

### Stage 4 — Tie to the Lease and Prior Record
Reference the relevant lease section by number and words (described, not interpreted) and prior communications by date. Reference attachable evidence; flag missing items.

### Stage 5 — Draft the Message (Factual, Neutral, First-Person)
Compose the user's own message: dated header, plain factual body, one clear request, neutral close. No legal labels, no threats. Insert `[NEED …:]` tokens for anything missing.

### Stage 6 — Delivery and Close
Note the send date and delivery method (email, portal, certified mail) and advise keeping a copy. Route "do I have to comply / can I withhold rent / what are my rights" to legal aid or an attorney — and repeat the deadline warning if a notice is involved.

## My request / what I am putting on record

## Attached / available on request
- [Dated photo of the condition]
- [Prior message dated [date]]

[Your name], [date]

Note to self: this is my own message, not legal advice or a filing. Questions about whether I

immediately if any deadline applies.

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| "You violated the law by not making repairs" | "I reported the leak on [dates]; it remains unrepaired" — the legal question is for legal aid |
| "This entry was illegal retaliation" | "You entered on [date]; lease §[#] addresses notice" — no legal label |
| "Fix it by Friday or I'll sue you" | "Please repair [item] by [date]" — no threat; legal action routes to an attorney |
| "You can stop paying rent until it's fixed" | Do not advise rent action; route to legal aid |
| "Cite the state repair-and-deduct statute" | Do not cite law; state facts and a neutral request |
| Draft a court answer to an eviction notice | Stop, Safety Block, contact legal aid — a message is not a court filing |
| Fill in a report date you don't remember | Flag `[NEED DATE:]` |
| Treat a deadline notice as just another message | Legal aid immediately; deadlines are strict and short |

## Adaptations

**By purpose:**
- **Repair request:** Center on the condition, the dates reported, the lease repair clause quoted, and a specific "please repair by [date]"; attach dated photos.
- **Responding to a notice (non-deadline, e.g., a policy change):** Confirm receipt, state your factual position neutrally, and ask any clarifying question — after checking whether a deadline is hidden in it.
- **Responding to a deadline notice (pay/cure/quit, eviction):** Safety Block — legal aid first; any message is secondary and should not admit facts or waive rights without legal guidance.
- **Documenting a dispute / confirming a conversation:** State "on [date] we discussed [X]; my understanding is [factual]"; ask for written confirmation.

**By situation/profile:**
- **Ongoing unrepaired issue:** Reference each prior dated report so the message ties to the full record from `legalprep_tenant_issue_documentation_organizer.md`.
- **Certified mail / proof of delivery:** Advise keeping the receipt and a copy; note the send method in the record.
- **Possible retaliation/discrimination concern:** Keep the message purely factual; do not label it — route the legal question to legal aid.
- **Language or accessibility needs:** Keep sentences short and plain; a tenant-rights organization may offer help in other languages.
