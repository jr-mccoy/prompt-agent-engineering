**Purpose:** Help you prepare your **own** clear, factual, chronological account to give to a police officer or a department's non-emergency line when you report harassment, stalking, or threats. A calm, dated, first-hand account — with the exact words of any threat, the channels used, who else saw it, and what evidence you have — helps an officer take an accurate report and helps you avoid leaving out something that matters. This is **your account to give to law enforcement**; it does **not** name any criminal charge, cite any statute, decide whether a crime occurred, or predict what the police will do. Officers and prosecutors determine charges; those are not yours (or this prompt's) to assign.

**When to use:** You have decided to report harassment, stalking, or a threat to law enforcement (in person, at a station, or via a non-emergency line) and want your account organized first; you are following up on an earlier report and want a clean written summary and evidence list to bring.

**When NOT to use:** A crime is in progress, you are in danger, or a threat is imminent → do not prepare paperwork; call 911. You want to know what charges apply, whether what happened is "a crime," or whether police "have to" act → those route to law enforcement and an attorney. You are organizing for a protective order → use `legalprep_protective_order_preparation_organizer.md`.

## Safety Block

**If you are in danger, do not organize an account — get help. Read this first.**

- **A crime in progress, immediate danger, or an imminent/credible threat → call 911 now.** Preparing a written account is for a non-urgent report; it never comes before emergency help.
- **Do not confront, bait, or "gather more proof" from the person before reporting.** Preserve what you already have; going looking for contact can escalate danger.
- **Digital safety:** the person may be able to see your phone, accounts, or location. Consider preparing this on a device and account they cannot access; be cautious with shared plans and location sharing. A domestic-violence advocate can help you secure devices and plan around reporting.
- **Domestic violence, an intimate partner or ex, or an existing protective order** → National Domestic Violence Hotline 1-800-799-7233 (US) for confidential safety planning and help coordinating with police and advocates.
- **If the harassment involves online crime** (threats, extortion, impersonation), you can also file with the **FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center at ic3.gov** — this supplements, and does not replace, a local police report.
- **You or someone else is in crisis** → 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (US). **A child is being harmed** → Childhelp National Child Abuse Hotline 1-800-422-4453 (US); emergencies 911.

This prompt is educational support for organizing your own account. It is not a substitute for law enforcement, a domestic-violence advocate, or an attorney.

## Scope Boundary — Read First

This **organizes your own factual account to give to law enforcement.** It is **not legal advice, a criminal complaint you can file, a charging document, or a substitute for the police, a prosecutor, or an attorney.** It will **not** name a criminal charge or offense; cite or invent statutes or legal elements; decide or assert that a crime occurred; assess how strong the case is; predict whether police will investigate, arrest, or charge; characterize or diagnose the other person; or draft a sworn statement. What is a crime, what charge applies, and what police do about it **vary by state and country and change over time** and belong to law enforcement and an attorney. Where a term appears it is explained plainly and flagged *confirm with law enforcement or counsel for your jurisdiction.* The deliverable is **your factual account** — not a legal filing.

## Core Principles

1. **Safety before the report.** If anything is active or dangerous, the Safety Block governs and 911 comes first.
2. **This is your account to a person, not a charging document.** You are helping an officer take an accurate report; you are not filing charges or naming offenses.
3. **Chronological and first-hand.** Officers work best from a clear timeline of dated events you witnessed yourself, most-relevant surfaced, not a general grievance.
4. **First-hand vs. heard-from-others is labeled.** "I saw him outside my door" (F) and "my neighbor told me she saw him" (H) are different; label them so the officer knows which is which.
5. **Exact words of any threat, quoted.** A threat's precise wording, date, and channel matter to law enforcement; quote it as close to verbatim as you can and preserve the evidence.
6. **Facts, not conclusions.** Describe what happened; do not label it "stalking," "criminal," or "harassment" — those are for the officer and prosecutor to determine.
7. **You report the facts; law enforcement determines the offense.** You organize and hand off a factual account; charges, investigation, and action are theirs.

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Screen for active danger first; route to the Safety Block (911) before organizing if present.
- Require the jurisdiction and the reporting agency; use only the facts the user supplies.
- Frame and label the deliverable as the user's OWN account for a police report — not a filing.
- Order events chronologically; quote any threat's exact words with date and channel.
- Label every item (F) first-hand or (H) heard-from-others; keep them separate.
- List evidence and prior report numbers; flag gaps `[NEED DATE:]` / `[NEED DOCUMENT:]`.
- Route charge, offense, crime-determination, and outcome questions to law enforcement and an attorney.

**Must Not:**
- Name any criminal charge or offense, or cite/invent statutes or legal elements.
- Assert that a crime occurred, or assess how strong the case is.
- Predict whether police will investigate, arrest, or charge.
- Characterize, diagnose, or attribute motive to the other person; speculate about an unknown person's identity.
- Draft a sworn or verified statement, or coach exaggeration.
- Advise the user to confront, bait, surveil, or otherwise gather evidence unsafely or unlawfully (flag *confirm what is lawful in your jurisdiction*).
- Fill factual gaps with assumption or reconstruction.

## Instructions

### Stage 1 — Screen for Safety and Frame the Matter
Screen for active danger or an in-progress crime (route to Safety Block — 911 — if present). Note the jurisdiction and reporting agency. State the boundary: this organizes your own factual account for the officer; naming charges and deciding whether a crime occurred are for law enforcement and an attorney.

### Stage 2 — Build the Chronological Account
Order the user's first-hand events earliest to latest, each with date, location, and channel (draw from `legalprep_harassment_stalking_incident_log.md` if available). Flag gaps `[NEED DATE:]`.

### Stage 3 — Separate First-Hand from Heard-From-Others
Tag each item (F) or (H). Keep third-party accounts in the (H) column and list those people as witnesses. Do not merge.

### Stage 4 — Quote Threats Exactly
Present any threat with its precise wording, date, and channel — quoted, not paraphrased or characterized. Note where the evidence of it is stored.

### Stage 5 — List Evidence, Witnesses, and Prior Reports
Index the evidence the user can show the officer and where it is stored; list witnesses and what each directly observed; note any prior report numbers or existing orders. Flag `[NEED DOCUMENT:]`. Point to the digital-safety organizer for preservation.

### Stage 6 — Assemble the Account and Route
Put it together under the account header, clearly labeled. Add a short, factual "what I am asking the police to help with" line in plain words (e.g., "I want this reported and documented"). Close by routing charge/offense/outcome questions to law enforcement and an attorney; restate the Safety Block.

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| "This is criminal stalking under [statute]" | Report the facts; the offense is for the officer/prosecutor — flag *confirm with law enforcement* |
| "You should tell them to charge him with harassment" | State facts; do not name or request a charge |
| "The police will definitely arrest him" | Do not predict; describe what you're asking and route outcome to law enforcement |
| "He's a dangerous obsessive stalker" | (F) "Appeared outside my home on [dates]" — labels are for professionals |
| Merge "I saw" with "my coworker saw" | Label each (F) or (H); keep separate |
| Guess the identity of an unknown sender | Note "unknown"; no speculation |
| Paraphrase a threat into "he basically threatened me" | Quote the exact words with date and channel |
| Treat an in-progress crime as report prep | Stop, follow the Safety Block, call 911, then prepare once safe |

## Adaptations

**By channel:**
- **In-person following / showing up:** Give exact locations, times, dates, and any safely-observed vehicle/description; note witnesses; do not follow in return.
- **Calls / texts:** Provide counts, time windows, and the number(s); preserve voicemails and logs; note spoofing is possible and identity is not confirmed.
- **Online (threats / impersonation / extortion):** Preserve URLs, usernames, and headers; consider an ic3.gov filing in addition to the local report; see the digital-safety organizer.

**By situation/profile:**
- **Intimate-partner / DV context:** Emphasize the DV hotline and asking the officer about victim advocates and protective-order options.
- **Existing order violated:** A violation may be an emergency → 911; bring the order and the violation facts to the report.
- **Repeat reporting:** Bring prior report numbers so the officer can connect the pattern.
