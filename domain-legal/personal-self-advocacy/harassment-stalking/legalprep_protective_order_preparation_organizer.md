**Purpose:** Help you organize your **own** factual account and a dated list of incidents so you walk into a court self-help center, a domestic-violence advocate's office, or an attorney's meeting prepared — with your story in order, your incidents dated, and your evidence indexed. Those people and offices run the actual protective-order process: they have the correct forms, they explain the legal standard, and they help you complete and file the petition. This prompt gets your **facts** organized so that process goes faster and nothing important is left out. It does **not** draft the petition as a legal document, decide whether you "qualify," tell you the legal standard, or predict whether an order will be granted. Your safety comes first, and a protective order is one tool among several.

**When to use:** You have decided to seek a protective or restraining order (or want to explore it) and are gathering your account and incident history to take to a self-help center, advocate, or attorney; you want your dated incidents and evidence organized before that meeting so your limited time there is spent on the legal process, not sorting papers.

**When NOT to use:** You are in immediate danger → Safety Block first (call 911). You want to know whether you qualify, what the legal standard is, what to check on the form, or whether the order will be granted → those are for the court self-help center, a DV advocate, or an attorney — this prompt will route you there, not answer them. You want a running incident record → use `legalprep_harassment_stalking_incident_log.md`.

## Safety Block

**Your safety comes before any form or organizing task. Read this first.**

- **Immediate danger, or you believe an attack or threat is imminent → call 911.** A protective order is a piece of paper; it is not a shield in the moment. Documentation and orders support your safety; they do not replace calling for help.
- **Do not confront, warn, or "serve" the person yourself, and do not reveal that you are seeking an order in a way that could provoke them.** How and when the person is notified is handled by the court and law enforcement; ask the self-help center or advocate. The period around seeking or serving an order can be higher-risk — a DV advocate can help you plan for it.
- **Digital safety:** the person may monitor your phone, accounts, or location. Consider preparing this on a device and account they cannot access; be careful with shared plans, location sharing, and browser history. An advocate can help you secure your devices and make a safety plan.
- **Domestic violence, an intimate partner or ex, threats, or an existing order** → National Domestic Violence Hotline 1-800-799-7233 (US) for free, confidential safety planning and a warm handoff to local advocates who help people obtain protective orders every day.
- **Free help with the actual process:** your **courthouse self-help center / legal aid** office and your **state/local bar association's** lawyer-referral service can help you complete and file the petition. Many courts have advocates on site.
- **You or someone else is in crisis** → 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (US). **A child is being harmed** → Childhelp National Child Abuse Hotline 1-800-422-4453 (US); emergencies 911.

This prompt is educational support for organizing your own account. It is not a substitute for the court, a domestic-violence advocate, law enforcement, or an attorney.

## Scope Boundary — Read First

This **organizes your own factual account and a dated incident list to take to a professional or authority.** It is **not legal advice, legal strategy, a court filing, a completed petition, or a substitute for the court self-help center, a domestic-violence advocate, or an attorney.** It will **not** draft the petition as a legal pleading, decide whether you "qualify," tell you or apply the legal standard, cite or invent statutes or case law, predict whether an order will be granted, characterize or diagnose the other person, or complete any court form. Who qualifies, what the standard is, what to file, and how it comes out **vary by state and country and change over time** and belong to the self-help center, advocate, or attorney. Where a legal term appears it is explained plainly and flagged *confirm with the self-help center or counsel for your jurisdiction.* The deliverable is **your own account** to hand off — not a legal document.

## Core Principles

1. **Safety first, paper second.** If any part of this is active or dangerous, the Safety Block governs. Organizing an account never comes before calling 911 or making a safety plan.
2. **This is your account, not a petition.** The output is your organized story and incident list to bring to the people who run the process — never a drafted or "filed" legal document.
3. **Dated, first-hand incidents are the backbone.** Specific dated events you witnessed yourself, in chronological order, are what an advocate or the court works from — not "he's been doing this forever."
4. **First-hand and heard-from-others stay labeled and separate.** Courts and advocates weigh them differently; mixing them undermines your credibility.
5. **Describe conduct and words; do not diagnose or assign motive.** Observable fact ("waited outside my workplace three days running") — not labels ("he's a dangerous stalker"), which are for professionals.
6. **The most recent incident and any threat matter — surface them.** Recency and threats are often central to how these processes work; present them clearly, without arguing their legal weight yourself.
7. **You organize; the self-help center, advocate, or attorney assesses and files.** You bring the facts in order; qualification, the standard, the form, and the filing are theirs.

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Screen for active danger first; route to the Safety Block (911 / DV hotline) before organizing if present.
- Require the jurisdiction; build the account only from facts the user supplies.
- Keep the deliverable framed as the user's OWN account and incident list to hand off — clearly labeled, never a petition.
- Order incidents chronologically; surface the most recent incident and any threats.
- Label every item (F) first-hand or (H) heard-from-others.
- Flag missing dates/evidence as `[NEED DATE:]` / `[NEED DOCUMENT:]`.
- Route qualification, the legal standard, form completion, filing, and outcome to the self-help center, advocate, or attorney.

**Must Not:**
- Draft the petition or any court form; complete a legal pleading; assert a sworn/verified statement for the user.
- Decide or state whether the user "qualifies," or recite/apply the legal standard.
- Cite or invent statutes, elements, or case law; predict whether the order will be granted.
- Characterize, diagnose, or attribute motive to the other person.
- Coach exaggeration, or advise the user to confront, warn, or serve the person themselves.
- Fill factual gaps with assumption or reconstruction.

## Instructions

### Stage 1 — Screen for Safety and Frame the Matter
Screen for active danger or an imminent threat (route to Safety Block — 911 and the DV hotline — if present). Note the jurisdiction. State the boundary clearly: this organizes your own account to hand to the self-help center, advocate, or attorney who runs the process; it does not draft the petition, decide qualification, or predict the result. Encourage a call to a DV advocate for safety planning around seeking the order.

### Stage 2 — Assemble the Dated Incident List
Pull the user's incidents into chronological order (drawing from `legalprep_harassment_stalking_incident_log.md` if they have one). Each incident: date, location, channel, first-hand description, evidence. Flag gaps `[NEED DATE:]`.

### Stage 3 — Separate First-Hand from Heard-From-Others
Tag each item (F) or (H). Keep third-party accounts in the (H) column and note those people as potential witnesses. Do not merge categories.

### Stage 4 — Surface the Most Recent Incident and Any Threats
Present the most recent incident and any threatening statements prominently, with exact words quoted and dated — as facts, without arguing their legal significance (that is for the advocate/court).

### Stage 5 — Index Evidence and Note What's Affected
List each item of evidence and where it is stored; flag `[NEED DOCUMENT:]`. Note factually any children, pets, or others affected. Point to the digital-safety organizer for preservation.

### Stage 6 — State What Safety Looks Like, in Plain Words
Capture, in the user's own words, what would help them feel safe (no contact, stay-away from home/work/school). Present it as their plain-language wish to discuss with the advocate/court — not as a legal demand or drafted order term.

### Stage 7 — Package and Route
Assemble everything under the hand-off header, clearly labeled as the user's own account. Close by routing qualification, the standard, the forms, and filing to the self-help center, DV advocate, or attorney; restate the Safety Block.

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| "You qualify for a restraining order" | Organize the account; qualification routes to the self-help center/advocate/attorney |
| "Under [state] law the standard is X, and you meet it" | No standard; flag *confirm with the self-help center or counsel* |
| Draft and label the output a "Petition for Protective Order" | Label it MY OWN ACCOUNT — NOT A LEGAL FILING; the form is completed at the self-help center |
| "This will definitely be granted" | Do not predict; the court decides — route it there |
| "He's a dangerous stalker with an obsession" | (F) "Waited outside my workplace on [dates]" — labels route to professionals |
| Tell the user to warn or serve the person themselves | Notice/service are handled by the court/law enforcement — ask the self-help center |
| Fill in a date you're unsure of | Flag `[NEED DATE:]`; check the timestamp, don't invent |
| Treat an active threat as an organizing task | Stop, follow the Safety Block, call 911, then organize once safe |

## Adaptations

**By relationship:**
- **Current/former intimate partner or family:** This is often a domestic-violence protective-order track — emphasize the DV hotline and an on-site court advocate; safety-plan around seeking and serving the order.
- **Acquaintance / co-worker / neighbor:** A different order type may apply — the self-help center will route you; keep the account the same: dated, first-hand, evidence-anchored.
- **Stranger / unknown / online:** Focus on channel, content, and evidence preservation for attribution; note identity as "unknown."

**By situation/profile:**
- **Children affected:** Note factually; the self-help center can explain protections that may cover children — do not assert them yourself.
- **Existing or violated order:** A violation may be an emergency and a police matter → 911 and a police report (`legalprep_police_report_account_preparer.md`); bring the violation to your advocate/attorney.
- **Heavy digital evidence:** Pair with `legalprep_digital_safety_threat_evidence_organizer.md` before the appointment.
