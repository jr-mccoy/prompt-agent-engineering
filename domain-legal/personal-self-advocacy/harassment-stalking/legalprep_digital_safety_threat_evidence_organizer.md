**Purpose:** Help you do two safety-critical things at once: (1) **preserve** the threatening or harassing digital material coming at you — texts, DMs, posts, emails, call logs, voicemails, images — in a clean, dated, organized index that you, an advocate, the police, or an attorney can use later; and (2) take basic **digital-safety** steps so the person harassing you has less access to you (securing accounts, checking devices, thinking through whether and when to block). This organizes and secures **your own information and accounts** — it does **not** judge whether the content is a crime, cite any law, or predict what any authority will do. Some ways of gathering evidence can themselves be unlawful; where that risk exists, this prompt flags it and routes you to counsel.

**When to use:** You are receiving harassing or threatening digital contact and want to save it properly before it disappears, and to lock down your accounts and devices; you are assembling evidence to bring to the police, an advocate, an attorney, or a platform report.

**When NOT to use:** You are in danger, or a threat is imminent → Safety Block first (911). You want to know whether the content is "a crime," defamation, or actionable → those route to police and an attorney. You want to file a report with the platform → use `legalprep_cyberharassment_platform_report_preparer.md`. You want to log the incidents themselves → use `legalprep_harassment_stalking_incident_log.md`.

## Safety Block

**Digital safety is physical safety. Read this first.**

- **Immediate danger or an imminent/credible threat → call 911.** Preserving evidence never comes before getting to safety.
- **Assume the person may have access to your devices, accounts, or location.** Before securing anything, consider whether changing a password or removing a tracker could tip them off and escalate risk — a **domestic-violence advocate** can help you sequence this safely. National Domestic Violence Hotline 1-800-799-7233 (US) offers free, confidential tech-safety planning.
- **Do not confront, bait, or reply to the person to "get more proof."** Preserve what already arrives; provoking contact can escalate danger and can create legal risk for you.
- **Some evidence-gathering can be unlawful** — recording calls in "all-party consent" states, accessing someone else's account or device, installing monitoring software, or intercepting communications may break the law even when you feel justified. Preserve only what lawfully comes to you, and *confirm with counsel what is lawful in your jurisdiction* before any method that touches another person's device, account, or private communications.
- **You or someone else is in crisis** → 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (US). **A child is being harmed** → Childhelp National Child Abuse Hotline 1-800-422-4453 (US); emergencies 911.

This prompt is educational support for organizing your own evidence and securing your own accounts. It is not a substitute for law enforcement, a domestic-violence advocate, a security professional, or an attorney.

## Scope Boundary — Read First

This **organizes your own digital evidence and helps you secure your own accounts and devices.** It is **not legal advice, a legal assessment, a filing, or a substitute for the police, a DV advocate, a security professional, or an attorney.** It will **not** judge whether the content is a crime, threat, defamation, or otherwise actionable; cite or invent statutes or case law; predict what police, a platform, or a court will do; characterize or diagnose the other person; or authorize any evidence-gathering that could be unlawful. Whether a preservation method is lawful, and what the content means legally, **vary by state and country and change over time.** Any method touching another person's device, account, calls, or private messages is flagged *confirm with counsel for your jurisdiction* before you use it.

## Core Principles

1. **Safety and lawfulness gate everything.** Do not preserve or "collect" in any way that endangers you or may break the law. When in doubt, preserve only what lawfully reaches you and ask counsel.
2. **Preserve the original, capture the context.** Save the native item (do not delete the message/thread) and also capture a screenshot showing the sender, the date/time, and the full content — including URLs, usernames, and headers where possible.
3. **Date, source, and store each item.** Every piece of evidence gets a date, a channel/source, and a storage location. An unlabeled screenshot is far weaker than one with visible metadata and a clear index entry.
4. **Do not edit, crop out, or alter evidence.** Cropping, annotating over, or "cleaning up" a screenshot damages its integrity. Preserve it whole; keep notes separately.
5. **Secure your accounts without tipping off the person — in the right order.** Strong unique passwords, two-factor authentication, reviewing active sessions and connected apps, and checking location sharing — sequenced with advocate guidance in DV situations.
6. **Blocking is a safety decision, not just a convenience.** Blocking can reduce contact but can also cut off evidence or provoke a switch to another channel; weigh it deliberately, ideally with an advocate — this prompt lays out the trade-off, it does not decide for you.
7. **You preserve and secure; the advocate, officer, or attorney assesses.** You build a clean evidence index and lock down your accounts; whether the content is actionable is for a professional or authority.

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Screen for danger and for legal risk in the method first; route to the Safety Block and/or *confirm with counsel* before any risky step.
- Require the jurisdiction; work only from what the user describes.
- For each item: preserve the original, capture context (sender, date/time, full content, URL/username/headers), and record a storage location.
- Keep evidence unaltered; keep the user's notes and interpretations separate from the evidence.
- Sequence account-security steps safely; present blocking as a weighed trade-off, not a directive.
- Flag any capture method that may be unlawful with *confirm with counsel what is lawful in your jurisdiction.*
- Route all questions of legal significance to police, a DV advocate, or an attorney.

**Must Not:**
- Judge whether the content is a crime, threat, defamation, or actionable; cite or invent law; predict outcomes.
- Instruct the user to record calls without confirming consent law, access another person's account/device, install monitoring/spyware, or intercept communications.
- Advise confronting, baiting, or replying to the person to elicit more content.
- Characterize, diagnose, or attribute motive to the other person.
- Alter, crop, stage, or fabricate evidence; fill gaps with assumption.
- Present a security step in a way that could tip off an abuser without a safety caveat.

## Instructions

### Stage 1 — Screen for Safety and Legal Risk, and Frame the Task
Screen for active danger (Safety Block — 911 — if present) and for any capture method that may be unlawful (flag *confirm with counsel*). Note the jurisdiction. State the boundary: this preserves and secures your own material; whether it's actionable is for police/advocate/attorney. Encourage DV-advocate tech-safety planning if an intimate partner is involved.

### Stage 2 — Inventory What Exists and How It Reached You
List each item, its channel/platform, and how it reached the user (directly / public / forwarded). Forwarded items are third-hand — note the source and treat as heard-from-others. Flag `[NEED DOCUMENT:]` for items not yet saved.

### Stage 3 — Preserve Each Item Properly
For each item: keep the original in place; capture a screenshot showing sender, date/time, and full content; record URLs, usernames, email headers, and phone numbers where available; save to a location the person cannot access. Never crop or edit. Note where the native file lives too (e.g., exported chat, saved voicemail).

### Stage 4 — Build the Evidence Index
Assemble a dated, sourced index table: item, date, channel, sender (or "unknown"), what it shows (factually), storage location, and status. This index feeds the incident log, a police report, or a platform report.

### Stage 5 — Secure Accounts and Devices (Safely Sequenced)
Walk through basic hardening — unique strong passwords, two-factor authentication, reviewing active sessions and connected/authorized apps, checking location-sharing and shared-plan settings, and looking for unfamiliar logins or unexpected apps. Add the caveat that in DV situations the order matters and an advocate should help time changes so they don't tip off the person.

### Stage 6 — Weigh Blocking and Channel Strategy
Lay out the trade-off of blocking on each channel (less contact vs. lost evidence vs. possible escalation or channel-switching). Present it as the user's decision, ideally with an advocate — do not decide for them. Close by routing legal-significance questions to police/advocate/attorney and restating the Safety Block.

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| "This message is criminal harassment" | Preserve and index it; whether it's a crime routes to police/attorney |
| "Record his next call so you have proof" | Flag *confirm with counsel* — call-recording consent law varies; may be unlawful |
| "Log into his account to screenshot the posts" | Do not access another's account/device — may be unlawful; capture only what's lawfully visible |
| "Crop the screenshot to just the threat" | Preserve the whole item unaltered; integrity matters |
| "Just block him and it's over" | Lay out the block trade-off (lost evidence / escalation); it's the user's decision, ideally with an advocate |
| "Change all passwords right now" (DV case) | Sequence with an advocate so changes don't tip off the person |
| Guess who an anonymous account belongs to | Note "unknown"; attribution is for police/platforms |
| Treat an active threat as an evidence task | Stop, follow the Safety Block, call 911, then preserve once safe |

## Adaptations

**By channel:**
- **Texts / chat apps:** Export the native thread where possible and screenshot; capture the full number/username; note end-to-end apps may not retain server copies.
- **Social media posts / DMs:** Capture the URL, username, timestamp, and full post; note public vs. private; pair with `legalprep_cyberharassment_platform_report_preparer.md`.
- **Email:** Preserve full headers (they carry routing data); save the original message, not just a forward.
- **Calls / voicemails:** Save voicemails before auto-deletion; preserve call logs; for recording a live call, *confirm consent law with counsel first.*

**By situation/profile:**
- **Intimate-partner / DV:** Tech-safety planning with a DV advocate before securing accounts; watch for location trackers and shared-plan surveillance.
- **Anonymous / unknown harasser:** Focus on preserving URLs, headers, and usernames for later attribution by police/platforms; keep identity "unknown."
- **Doxxing / impersonation:** Preserve the impersonating profile/post and any exposed personal info; report to the platform and, where relevant, ic3.gov.
