**Purpose:** Get a stuck custody mediation moving — or decide it should stop. Diagnose **why** the parties are at impasse (positional lock, missing information, emotional/trust injury, or a structural problem no schedule can fix), then design responses matched to the diagnosis: package proposals that trade across the full custody space, conditional terms (step-up schedules, tripwires, review dates) that convert distrust from a deal-killer into a drafted contingency, caucus strategy, and partial-agreement capture. Equally important is the exit judgment: recognizing futility, coercion, or bad-faith nondisclosure and recommending an end to mediation. Output is a strategy memo for counsel, not advice to the client and not a binding agreement.

**When to use:** A custody mediation session stalled or collapsed; a second/third session is scheduled and the same fight is coming back; the parties agree on fragments but cannot close; counsel suspects mediation is being used in bad faith and needs a stay-or-go analysis.

## Constraints

**Must:**
- **Diagnose before prescribing:** classify the impasse as positional (anchored demands), informational (disputed facts/missing data), emotional/trust (injury, fear of the other's conduct), or structural (geography, work schedules, genuine incompatibility) — mixed types are common; the response differs per type.
- Design **packages, not line-items**: bundle across the trade space so each parent wins what they value most; single-issue haggling re-creates the impasse.
- Convert distrust into **drafted contingencies**: step-up schedules with objective milestones, tripwires (missed exchanges, positive tests, school-attendance drops) with pre-agreed consequences, review dates, and verification mechanisms — "I don't trust them" becomes "then we draft for the failure mode."
- Keep every package **child-centered** and mapped to the best-interests factors `[NEED FACTOR LIST: …]`; a package that trades the child's needs for parental convenience fails review.
- Treat **child support as guideline-governed** — not a custody bargaining chip; flag any deviation for findings `[NEED GUIDELINE: …]`.
- Plan **caucus strategy**: what to raise privately with the mediator, what reality-testing to request, and what the client should hear from the neutral rather than from counsel.
- **Capture partial agreement** before it decays: identify fragments ready for a partial term sheet (hand off to `legal_post_mediation_term_sheet_and_mou_drafter.md`) and confirm capture doesn't waive positions on open issues.
- Run the **continue-or-end analysis**: recommend ending mediation on futility (structural impasse no package resolves), coercion or power imbalance safeguards can't cure, bad-faith nondisclosure, or use of mediation to delay/exhaust — and state what satisfies a mandatory-mediation requirement `[CITE: …]`.
- Measure every recommendation against the **BATNA**; a package worse than the likely litigated outcome plus trial cost is not a deal.
- Respect **mediation confidentiality** in what the memo records and what can be used later `[CITE: …]`.
- Use placeholders `[CITE: …]`, `[NEED: …]` for unsupplied authority or facts; state confidence levels on the impasse diagnosis and BATNA comparison.

**Must Not:**
- Prescribe a generic "split the difference" without diagnosing the impasse type.
- Design packages that buy agreement with the child's interests (e.g., trading school stability for holiday wins).
- Use contingent terms as gotcha traps — tripwires must be objective, provable, and aimed at the child's safety/stability, not surveillance of the other parent.
- Bargain custody against child support or draft custody terms as non-modifiable.
- Keep a coerced or exhausted client at the table to "finish" — coercion ends mediation.
- Paper over bad-faith nondisclosure with a smaller deal.
- Invent best-interests factors, confidentiality rules, or mandatory-mediation requirements.
- Insert generic "consult counsel" disclaimers.

## Instructions

1. **Reconstruct the impasse.** From the mediation history and in-room pattern, classify the impasse type(s) — positional / informational / emotional-trust / structural — with evidence for the diagnosis and a confidence level.
2. **Match response to type.** Positional → re-anchor to BATNA and reframe to interests; informational → identify the disputed fact and the cheapest credible resolution (document exchange, joint expert, school records); emotional/trust → contingent-term design and mediator reality-testing; structural → test whether any package resolves it, or whether the honest answer is adjudication.
3. **Map the full trade space.** List every tradeable element: legal-custody domains (education, medical, religious, activities — divisible), week structure, holidays/summer, transportation burden, ROFR threshold, communication protocol, extracurricular control, review clauses.
4. **Design 2–3 packages.** Each bundle trades across the space so each parent's top interest is served; for each package state: terms, which interest of each parent it serves, which best-interests factors it supports, and how it compares to the BATNA.
5. **Draft the contingency layer.** For each distrust item: an objective milestone or tripwire, the verification mechanism, the pre-agreed consequence (schedule adjustment, supervision, review session), and the sunset/review date. Step-up schedules get explicit stages and criteria.
6. **Caucus plan.** What counsel raises privately: client's real constraints, requests for mediator reality-testing of the other side, safety logistics, and which package to float first and in which room.
7. **Partial-capture plan.** Identify settled fragments; specify what goes into a partial term sheet now, with a no-waiver recital for open issues; route drafting to the term-sheet prompt.
8. **Continue-or-end analysis.** Apply the exit tests — futility, coercion/imbalance, bad-faith nondisclosure, strategic delay — and recommend continue (with changes), adjourn (with homework), or end; state what satisfies any mandatory-mediation rule `[CITE: …]`.
9. **Next-session script.** If continuing: agenda for the next session, opening move, package sequencing, and the decision points that trigger a recess or a call to end.
10. **Memo & confidence.** Assemble the strategy memo with confidence levels on the diagnosis, the package-vs-BATNA comparisons, and the exit recommendation.

## Verification

- [ ] Impasse diagnosed by type with evidence and confidence — not assumed positional.
- [ ] Each response matched to the diagnosed type; structural impasses honestly flagged where no package works.
- [ ] Packages bundle across the trade space and are each compared to the BATNA.
- [ ] Every package mapped to the child's interests and best-interests factors; no child-interest trades for parental wins.
- [ ] Contingent terms objective, verifiable, child-protective — not surveillance traps; review dates included.
- [ ] Child support kept guideline-governed; no custody-for-support trades; custody terms modifiable.
- [ ] Caucus plan and partial-capture plan (with no-waiver recital) included.
- [ ] Continue-or-end analysis run against all four exit tests; mandatory-mediation status addressed `[CITE: …]`.
- [ ] Mediation confidentiality respected in the memo and the plan.
- [ ] No invented factors, rules, or guideline figures — placeholders used; confidence levels stated.

## False-Positive Prevention

| Failure mode | Correction |
|---|---|
| Treating every impasse as positional and just splitting the difference | Diagnose first — informational, trust, and structural impasses need different tools |
| Re-arguing the merits harder at session two | Same inputs produce the same impasse; change the structure (packages, contingencies, caucus) |
| "I don't trust them" treated as unresolvable | Draft for it: objective milestones, tripwires, verification, review dates |
| Tripwires designed as surveillance gotchas | Objective, provable, child-protective triggers only; otherwise they breed litigation |
| Package that wins holidays by trading the child's school stability | Map every package to the child's interests; courts review the result |
| Trading custody time against child support | Support is guideline-governed; the trade is both void-prone and sanctionable |
| Keeping an exhausted or coerced client at the table to close | Coercion ends mediation; safeguards or adjournment, not endurance |
| Papering over suspected nondisclosure with a discounted deal | Bad-faith nondisclosure is an exit test, not a pricing input |
| Capturing partial agreement in a way that waives open positions | Partial term sheet with an express no-waiver recital |
| Calling a structural impasse (true relocation conflict) "high conflict" | Some disputes have no mediated middle; recommend adjudication honestly |
| Accepting any deal to avoid trial regardless of BATNA | Compare every package to the litigated range plus cost/risk — some impasses should hold |
| Treating mandatory mediation as requiring agreement | State what satisfies the requirement [CITE]; good-faith participation ≠ settlement |
