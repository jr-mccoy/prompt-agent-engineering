**Purpose:** Prepare a parent and counsel for custody mediation or settlement — clarifying interests, setting a realistic BATNA against the likely litigated outcome, building a proposal ladder and trade space, and framing a child-centered, de-escalating approach — and produce a draft parenting-plan term sheet. Output is a preparation memo and term sheet, not advice and not a binding agreement.

**When to use:** Before custody mediation (voluntary or court-ordered) or a settlement conference; structuring a parenting-plan offer; preparing a client to negotiate constructively while protecting the child's interests.

---

## Your Input

- **Jurisdiction:** [State; whether mediation is mandatory; the best-interests factors; confidentiality of mediation `[CITE: …]`]
- **Positions:** [What each parent says they want for custody and schedule]
- **Interests:** [The underlying needs behind the positions — time, stability, decision-making, the child's activities, distrust]
- **Likely litigated outcome:** [The realistic range under the best-interests analysis — the BATNA anchor]
- **Trade space:** [Holidays, summers, decision categories, transportation, ROFR, communication terms]
- **Child(ren):** [Ages, needs, schedule realities, activities]
- **Conflict & safety:** [Conflict level; any DV/abuse making joint mediation inappropriate or requiring safeguards]
- **Constraints:** [Work schedules, distance, finances]

---

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Separate **positions from interests** and build the strategy around interests and the **child's needs**.
- Anchor the negotiation to a realistic **BATNA** — the likely litigated best-interests outcome and its cost/risk — so concessions are measured against the real alternative (cross-reference the best-interests analysis).
- Build a **proposal ladder** (opening, target, walk-away) for **legal custody, physical custody, and the schedule**, and identify **trade space** (holidays, summers, decision categories, transportation, ROFR).
- Prepare a **child-centered framing** and a **de-escalation** approach that keeps the discussion off blame and on the child.
- **Screen for safety/DV**: assess whether mediation is appropriate, and if so what safeguards (separate sessions/caucus, shuttle, no direct contact) are needed; some cases should not be mediated jointly.
- Respect **mediation confidentiality** and what can/can't be used later `[CITE: …]`.
- Produce a **draft parenting-plan term sheet** capturing the target proposal.
- Use placeholders `[CITE: ...]`, `[NEED: ...]` for unsupplied authority or facts.

**Must Not:**
- Recommend mediating jointly where DV/safety makes it unsafe without safeguards.
- Frame the strategy around winning against the other parent rather than the child's interests.
- Set a BATNA untethered to the realistic litigated outcome (over-optimism wastes leverage).
- Counsel concealment or bad-faith negotiation.
- Invent the best-interests factors or mediation-confidentiality rules.
- Insert generic "consult counsel" disclaimers.

---

## Instructions

1. **Interests mapping.** Translate each parent's positions into underlying interests and the child's needs.
2. **BATNA.** State the realistic litigated range and its cost/risk as the negotiation anchor.
3. **Proposal ladder.** Define opening/target/walk-away for legal custody, physical custody, and the schedule.
4. **Trade space.** Identify what each side can give (holidays, summers, decision categories, transportation, ROFR) for what they value most.
5. **Framing & de-escalation.** Prepare child-centered language and techniques to keep talks productive.
6. **Safety screen.** Assess mediation-appropriateness and required safeguards; flag if joint mediation is inappropriate.
7. **Agenda & term sheet.** Produce a mediation agenda and a draft parenting-plan term sheet for the target proposal.

## Verification

- [ ] Positions translated into interests and the child's needs.
- [ ] BATNA anchored to the realistic litigated best-interests outcome and its cost/risk.
- [ ] Proposal ladder set for legal custody, physical custody, and schedule.
- [ ] Trade space identified (give/get).
- [ ] Child-centered framing and de-escalation prepared.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

| Failure mode | Correction |
|---|---|
| Mediating jointly despite DV/safety concerns | Screen first; require safeguards or advise against joint mediation |
| Strategy framed around beating the other parent | Anchor to the child's interests and needs |
| Over-optimistic BATNA | Tie the anchor to the realistic litigated range and trial cost/risk |
| Trading without knowing priorities | Map interests so concessions buy what the client values most |
| Vague term sheet | Capture concrete schedule, holidays, decision-making, and communication terms |
| Ignoring mediation confidentiality | Respect what can/can't be used later [CITE] |
| Counseling concealment or bad faith | Negotiate in good faith; disclosure obligations remain |
| Inventing the best-interests factors | Use [CITE]/[NEED] placeholders |
| No de-escalation plan for a high-conflict pair | Prepare framing and techniques; consider caucus-style mediation |
| Opening position with no walk-away | Define opening, target, and walk-away for each issue |
