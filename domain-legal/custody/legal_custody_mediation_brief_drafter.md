**Purpose:** Draft the written statement counsel submits before a custody mediation — orienting the mediator to the child, the current arrangement, what is actually disputed, the client's schedule proposal and the child-development rationale behind it, and what a workable parenting resolution requires. A custody mediation brief is won on **child-centeredness and credibility**: factor-aligned facts presented without attacking the other parent persuade a neutral; grievance catalogs do not. Safety concerns are presented as process-design needs (caucus, shuttle, safeguards), not as advocacy theater. For financial-issues briefs, use `legal_divorce_mediation_brief_drafter.md`.

**When to use:** Mediator requests pre-session statements in a custody or parenting-time mediation (voluntary or court-ordered); counsel wants the mediator anchored on the child's needs and a concrete proposal before the session; a stalled mediation needs a refocused written reset.

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Follow the **mediator's instructions** (length, format, exchange status, deadline); if not supplied, mark `[NEED: mediator instructions]` and default to concise.
- Keep the brief **child-centered**: every position tied to the child's stability, relationships, school, routine, health, or developmental needs — not to parental rights rhetoric or the other parent's faults.
- Map the factual narrative to the **jurisdiction's best-interests factors** using `[NEED FACTOR LIST: …]` / `[CITE: …]` placeholders — never invent the factor list.
- Make the schedule proposal **concrete** (days, exchanges, holidays, transportation) with rationale tied to the child's **age and needs**; an ages-and-stages justification beats a percentage demand.
- Present **co-parenting history factually** — dates, events, kept/broken agreements — without motive attribution or inflammatory characterization; fair presentation is the credibility engine.
- Present **safety concerns as process design**: what safeguards the session needs (caucus, shuttle, separate arrival, support person) and what plan provisions address the risk (supervised exchanges, testing, step-up) — detailed safety material goes in the confidential version.
- Separate **agreed from disputed** items explicitly to shrink the fight.
- Maintain the wall between **exchanged** and **confidential** versions: bottom lines, constraints, candid weaknesses, and safety detail are mediator-only, labeled "CONFIDENTIAL — FOR MEDIATOR ONLY."
- State **what resolution requires** — the child-centered interests a plan must satisfy.
- Respect **mediation confidentiality** `[CITE: …]` and keep custody terms framed as modifiable (best-interests review survives agreement).

**Must Not:**
- Catalog the other parent's faults or attribute motives — it signals unreasonableness to the neutral and poisons the session.
- Invent the state's best-interests factors, custody presumptions, or confidentiality rules.
- Frame the proposal as a parental-rights win ("I deserve 50%") instead of a child-needs plan.
- Put bottom lines or strategy in the exchanged version.
- Minimize or omit a DV/safety dimension to make the case look mediation-friendly — flag it with the needed safeguards instead.
- Misstate the co-parenting history or existing orders.
- Propose bargaining custody against child support or making custody terms non-modifiable.
- Insert generic "consult counsel" disclaimers.

## Instructions

1. **Frame & format.** Apply the mediator's instructions; confirm brief confidentiality status `[CITE: …]`; decide whether a confidential supplement is needed (strategy, safety detail).
2. **Child snapshot.** Open with the child, not the dispute: ages, school, activities, health/developmental needs, current routine — the shared ground both parents care about.
3. **Current arrangement & caretaking history.** Describe the operating schedule, how long it has run, and each parent's actual caretaking role — factually, with dates.
4. **Agreed-items ledger.** List everything already agreed (school, activities, some holidays, communication tools) to shrink the dispute.
5. **Disputed-issues map.** For each disputed item (schedule, legal-custody domains, exchanges, holidays): each parent's position and the actual delta.
6. **Proposal with rationale.** Present the client's concrete proposal — week structure, exchanges, holidays, transportation, ROFR, decision-making — each element justified by the child's age, needs, and the factor it serves `[NEED FACTOR LIST: …]`.
7. **Co-parenting record.** Brief factual record of cooperation and friction points (kept/broken agreements, communication pattern) without characterization — what it shows about what plan structure will work (detail vs. flexibility, communication protocol, parallel-parenting features).
8. **Safety & process design.** If a safety dimension exists: state the process safeguards requested and the plan provisions that address risk; route detail and any protective-order history to the confidential version.
9. **What resolution requires.** The child-centered interests any workable plan must satisfy (school continuity, both-parent relationship, predictability, health-care access).
10. **Confidential supplement & credibility pass.** Draft mediator-only material (bottom lines, constraints, weaknesses, safety detail); then re-read the exchanged version as the mediator — cut anything inflammatory, unsupported, or parent-centered.

## False-Positive Prevention

| Failure mode | Correction |
|---|---|
| Grievance catalog about the other parent | Factual record with dates and plan implications; characterization persuades no neutral |
| "Client deserves 50/50" framing | Child-needs framing: what the schedule does for the child at this age |
| Inventing the state's best-interests factors | [NEED FACTOR LIST] placeholder; map facts to factors once supplied |
| Vague proposal ("substantial time") | Concrete week structure, exchanges, holidays, transportation |
| Minimizing DV to keep the case mediation-friendly | Flag it; request safeguards; some cases need shuttle format or shouldn't mediate jointly |
| Safety history splashed across the exchanged brief | Process request in exchanged version; detail in the confidential supplement |
| Bottom line leaked in the exchanged version | Confidential-only; labeled wall |
| Listing disputed items as agreed to look cooperative | Accurate ledger; the mediator hears both sides |
| Rationale untethered to the child's age | Ages-and-stages: what a 3-year-old needs differs from a 13-year-old |
| Proposing non-modifiable custody terms or custody-for-support trades | Best-interests review survives agreement; support is guideline-governed |
| Ignoring the existing operating schedule | Address the status quo — mediators and courts weight continuity |
| Trial-brief heat aimed at a facilitator | Persuade the neutral: credibility, fairness, settlement orientation |
