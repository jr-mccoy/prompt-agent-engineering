**Purpose:** Produce a defensible workplace-investigation work product in two pieces: (1) a written investigation plan that satisfies the employer's affirmative-defense obligation to take prompt, thorough, neutral action; and (2) a finding-of-fact report with credibility determinations and conclusions tied to policy and law — structured to preserve attorney-client privilege and work-product protection where the investigation is counsel-directed.

**When to use:** A complaint of harassment, discrimination, retaliation, or material policy violation has been received internally or via EEOC/state agency notice, by formal complaint, third-party report, hotline tip, or pattern observed in exit interviews. Use for investigations conducted by HR, internal counsel, or outside counsel.

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Open the investigation **promptly** — document the date of the complaint, the date of the investigation plan, and the date investigation steps begin. Track each interval. EEOC/Title VII case law treats unexplained delay as evidence of a defective response.
- Use a **neutral investigator** with no reporting line to either complainant or respondent, no prior involvement in the allegations, and no personal stake in the outcome. Document the neutrality determination.
- Issue a **litigation hold** to custodians (complainant, respondent, witnesses, supervisors, HR) on receipt of the complaint; document scope of preservation.
- Issue **Upjohn warnings** at the start of every witness interview if the investigation is counsel-directed and privilege is intended: identify counsel as representing the company, not the individual; explain the company controls the privilege and may waive it; explain the individual should keep the conversation confidential; confirm understanding.
- Conduct interviews in a defensible **witness order**:
  1. Complainant — full account, all witnesses, all documents.
  2. Third-party / corroborating witnesses — neutral order, percipient witnesses before character witnesses.
  3. Respondent — last, with allegations specific enough to permit a meaningful response.
- Use a written **interview protocol** for each witness: introduction, role/tenure, scope of knowledge, open questions before closed, walkthrough of each allegation, identification of documents and other witnesses, confidentiality reminder (NLRA-compliant — not a blanket directive), opportunity to add.
- Make **credibility determinations** using recognized factors: inherent plausibility, demeanor (with caution), motive to lie, corroboration, consistency, prior conduct, and specificity. State the basis for each determination — never "I just believe X."
- Make **findings of fact** by a **preponderance of the evidence** standard (default for civil employment investigations); identify the standard in the report.
- Apply law to facts: identify the policy and legal standard (e.g., Title VII hostile-work-environment elements — protected basis, unwelcome conduct, severe or pervasive, subjectively and objectively offensive, basis for employer liability under Faragher/Ellerth or Vance v. Ball State for supervisor status).
- Take **prompt, effective remedial action** reasonably calculated to end the harassment if substantiated; document the remedial action.
- Protect the complainant from **retaliation**: document the anti-retaliation reminder issued to respondent and any supervisors; flag heightened sensitivity in the months following the complaint.
- Mark the report and interview memos with **privilege legends** if counsel-directed: "Attorney-Client Privileged / Attorney Work Product — Prepared at the Direction of Counsel for the Purpose of Providing Legal Advice."
- Preserve verbatim or near-verbatim quotes for material statements; do not paraphrase what may become testimony.

**Must Not:**
- Pre-judge the outcome or use leading questions that signal a preferred answer.
- Impose a blanket "do not discuss this investigation with anyone" directive — under **Apogee Retail / Stericycle**, confidentiality directives must be justified case-by-case and limited in duration. State the specific justification (witness intimidation, evidence destruction risk, privacy of a minor or victim).
- Interview the respondent before having sufficient detail of the allegations to permit meaningful response.
- Allow the respondent's direct supervisor (if also under investigation) to participate in the investigation.
- Disclose the complainant's identity beyond what is necessary, but do not promise absolute anonymity that the investigation cannot honor.
- Use the investigation to build a termination record post hoc — the investigation is for fact-finding, not pretext-construction.
- Cite cases or statutes you have not verified — use `[CITE: ...]` placeholders.
- Include generic "consult counsel" disclaimers in lieu of substantive guardrails.

## Instructions

1. **Intake and triage.** Document the complaint exactly as received; classify (harassment, discrimination, retaliation, policy violation, mixed); identify protected basis; determine whether interim measures (separation of complainant and respondent, paid administrative leave, schedule change) are warranted; assess whether the matter implicates parallel obligations (Title IX, mandatory reporter, regulatory).
2. **Privilege and posture setup.** Confirm investigator identity; confirm privilege intent; issue litigation hold; document the date and scope of preservation.
3. **Build the investigation plan.**
   - Scope statement (what allegations are being investigated).
   - Standard (preponderance of the evidence).
   - Custodians and documents to collect.
   - Witness list, order, and rationale.
   - Interview protocol templates.
   - Timeline and milestones.
   - Interim measures.
   - Confidentiality framework (NLRA-compliant).
   - Reporting structure and recipient.
4. **Collect documents.** Personnel files for complainant, respondent, and key witnesses; relevant emails and chat; prior complaints; policies in effect at the time; calendar entries; access/security logs; performance documentation.
5. **Interview complainant.** Issue Upjohn warning if applicable. Capture the full account: who, what, where, when, witnesses, documents, prior reports, impact, requested resolution. Show relevant documents. Identify additional witnesses.
6. **Interview corroborating witnesses.** Neutral order. Each gets the same protocol. Document what each witness saw, heard, or learned, and from whom and when.
7. **Interview respondent last.** Provide allegations with sufficient specificity to allow meaningful response. Capture admissions, denials, explanations, and identification of contrary witnesses or documents.
8. **Follow-up interviews and document review.** Resolve inconsistencies. Pull additional documents identified during interviews.
9. **Credibility analysis.** For each material disputed fact, assess credibility of competing accounts using the factors above; document the analysis with specific evidence.
10. **Findings of fact.** Issue numbered findings, each supported by record evidence and credibility determinations.
11. **Application of law to facts.** Identify the policy and legal standard; apply to findings; conclude whether policy/law was violated, by whom, and the scope.
12. **Recommendations.** Remedial action proportionate to findings (training, counseling, transfer, demotion, discipline, termination); anti-retaliation reminder; policy updates; pattern monitoring.
13. **Closure.** Communicate outcome to complainant and respondent at an appropriate level of detail (preserving privacy of others); document feedback channels; calendar a follow-up retaliation check at 30/60/90 days.

## Verification

- [ ] Investigation opened promptly; intervals between complaint, plan, interviews, and completion are documented and defensible.
- [ ] Investigator neutrality documented (no reporting line to parties, no prior involvement, no stake).
- [ ] Litigation hold issued and scoped to custodians and sources.
- [ ] Upjohn warnings issued and documented in counsel-directed investigations.
- [ ] Witness order is complainant → corroborating witnesses → respondent.
- [ ] Confidentiality directive is specific, time-limited, and justified — not a blanket directive (Apogee Retail / Stericycle compliant).
- [ ] Standard stated as preponderance of the evidence.
- [ ] Credibility determinations are supported by specific factors and evidence, not bare assertions.
- [ ] Findings of fact are numbered and tied to record evidence.
- [ ] Legal standard identified and applied to facts; `[CITE: ...]` placeholders used for unverified authority.
- [ ] Remedial action is reasonably calculated to end the conduct if substantiated.
- [ ] Anti-retaliation reminder issued and follow-up calendar set.
- [ ] Privilege legends applied where investigation is counsel-directed.

## False-Positive Prevention

| Failure mode | Correction |
|---|---|
| Interviewing the respondent first or before allegations are specific | Re-order; respondent goes last with sufficient detail to respond meaningfully |
| Blanket "do not discuss this investigation" directive | Replace with specific, justified, time-limited confidentiality consistent with Apogee Retail / Stericycle |
| Credibility determination stated as "I find X more credible" without basis | Add specific factors (corroboration, specificity, motive, prior consistent/inconsistent statements) |
| Investigator reports to a witness or party | Replace investigator; document neutrality |
| Treating the investigation as a termination-pretext exercise | Re-scope as fact-finding; let findings drive remedial action |
| No Upjohn warning in a counsel-directed interview | Issue and document the warning; otherwise privilege is at risk |
| Promising the complainant absolute anonymity | Cannot be honored once respondent must respond; explain limits of confidentiality |
| Failing to issue a litigation hold | Issue immediately on receipt of complaint; document custodians and sources |
| Pre-judging by accepting one account without testing it | Apply credibility analysis to both sides of every material disputed fact |
| Ignoring NLRA § 7 implications of confidentiality and non-disparagement directives | Frame all directives consistent with NLRB authority |
| Fabricated case citations or statutory references | Use `[CITE: ...]` placeholders; do not invent authority |
| Stale findings (months elapsed without explanation) | Document each interval; promptness is part of the affirmative defense |
