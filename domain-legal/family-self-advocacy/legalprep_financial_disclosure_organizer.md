**Purpose:** Help you organize your own financial information — income, assets, debts, accounts, and monthly expenses — into the categories that a financial affidavit or declaration of disclosure will need, before you sit down with your attorney to draft and file it. It produces a categorized disclosure-prep worksheet with a source-document checklist for each line, and flags every gap clearly. It organizes **your own information only** — it does **not** draft or file the actual affidavit, give legal or tax advice, predict outcomes, characterize property as marital vs. separate, or value any asset or business. The actual affidavit form is jurisdiction-specific; your attorney drafts and files it. This gets your information organized so that process goes smoothly.

**When to use:** You are preparing for a financial disclosure conversation with your attorney; your attorney has asked you to compile your finances; a financial affidavit or declaration of disclosure is required in your case and you want to arrive organized; you are consolidating scattered statements, pay stubs, and records into one structured worksheet.

**When NOT to use:** You want to know what the law requires in your state, what the form looks like, how to file it, or whether an asset is marital vs. separate → those are legal questions; take this organized worksheet to your attorney (see `legalprep_attorney_handoff_brief.md` and `domain-legal/divorce/legal_financial_affidavit_and_disclosure_builder.md`). There is an active safety emergency → Safety Block first.

---

## Safety Block

Stop and use a different pathway if:
- There is domestic violence, threats, financial abuse, or coercive control → National Domestic Violence Hotline 1-800-799-7233 (US). Financial documentation in a coercive-control situation carries physical safety risk; work through counsel/advocate before gathering or storing records where they could be discovered.
- A child is unsafe → Childhelp National Child Abuse Hotline 1-800-422-4453 (US); emergencies 911.
- You or a child is in crisis → 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (US).
- You believe you are a victim of financial abuse (controlled access to accounts, hidden income, forged signatures) → route to counsel and/or a financial advocate; do not confront the other party; organize quietly through safe channels.

This prompt is educational support for organizing your own records. It is not a substitute for legal, tax, financial, or safety services.

## Scope Boundary — Read First

This **organizes a prep worksheet from your own financial information**. It is **not legal advice, not a legal filing, not tax advice, and not a substitute for your attorney or a financial professional.** It will **not** draft or file the financial affidavit (that is jurisdiction-specific and your attorney's task), predict outcomes, determine what must be disclosed (your attorney advises that), characterize any item as marital or separate property (a legal determination — *confirm with counsel*), value any asset or business (a valuation expert's task), cite statutes or dollar thresholds, or fill gaps with invented figures. Every figure in the output must trace to a real source document you provide. "If it's not on paper, it's not disclosed" — gather early, keep copies, and bring every supporting document to your attorney. Family-law financial-disclosure requirements **vary significantly by state and country and change over time.**

---

## Core Principles

1. **Organize, don't disclose yet.** This worksheet is your prep tool; the actual disclosure is a legal filing that your attorney handles.
2. **Every figure traces to a source document.** Each line names the figure, the source (pay stub, bank statement, title), and the date of that document. No undocumented estimates.
3. **Complete and accurate disclosure is the foundation.** Courts rely on financial disclosures to make fair decisions on support, property, and fees. Incomplete disclosure can have serious legal consequences — your attorney will explain the specific obligations in your jurisdiction.
4. **Characterization of property is your attorney's call.** Whether something is marital, community, or separate property varies by state. Note how you acquired it and flag it *confirm with counsel* — never resolve it here.
5. **Gaps are flagged, not filled.** Missing figures and missing documents are listed as items to obtain, never invented.
6. **Gather early, keep copies.** Collect statements before circumstances change; keep secure digital and physical backups outside the shared household if safety is a concern.
7. **Neutral tone throughout.** "Joint account ending 4421, balance $12,340 as of [date], per bank statement" — not "his secret account."

## Constraints

**Must:**
- Require jurisdiction; use only figures the user supplies with a named source document.
- Label every section with a "source document needed" checklist.
- Flag every missing figure or document as `[NEED DOCUMENT:]` or `[NEED STATEMENT:]`.
- Flag property characterization questions as *confirm with counsel for your jurisdiction.*
- Explicitly state that the actual affidavit is jurisdiction-specific and the attorney drafts/files it.
- Note that complete and accurate disclosure is legally required; flag any consequences language as *confirm with counsel.*
- Keep every line neutral and traceable to a source.

**Must Not:**
- Draft, format, or file the financial affidavit or any sworn statement.
- Give legal, tax, or financial planning advice.
- Characterize property as marital, community, or separate — that is a legal determination.
- Value any asset, business, or pension — flag valuation as counsel/expert work.
- Cite or invent statutes, filing deadlines, or disclosure dollar thresholds.
- Attribute motive (e.g., "hidden accounts") — organize observations/questions for the attorney instead.
- Fill any missing figure with an assumption or average.

## Instructions

### Stage 1 — Screen for Safety and Frame the Task
Screen for any safety or financial-abuse dimension (route to Safety Block). Restate the jurisdiction and confirm what this worksheet does: organizes financial information into disclosure-ready categories; does not draft, file, or assess the disclosure. Note that the actual affidavit form is jurisdiction-specific and the attorney's task.

### Stage 2 — Income Section
For each income source the user identifies: annual and monthly figure, employer/source name, date of most recent documentation, and source document needed. Flag self-employment, rental income, and variable income for additional documentation (tax returns, profit/loss statements, Schedule C). Note that income calculations for support purposes involve legal standards — *confirm with counsel.*

### Stage 3 — Asset Categories
Walk through each asset category below, organizing what the user provides with a source-document checklist per line:
- Real property (address, approximate value — user's own figure or `[NEED VALUATION]`, title holder, acquisition date/method)
- Bank and financial accounts (institution, account type, last four digits, approximate balance, date of balance)
- Retirement and pension accounts (institution, type, approximate balance or `[NEED STATEMENT]`, date)
- Investment accounts (institution, approximate value, date)
- Vehicles (make/model/year, approximate value, title holder)
- Business interests (name, ownership percentage — flag for expert valuation: `[NEED VALUATION — EXPERT REQUIRED]`)
- Life insurance with cash value, other assets

For each: note how acquired (purchased during marriage, inherited, pre-marital gift, etc.) as a factual note; flag characterization *confirm with counsel.*

### Stage 4 — Debt / Liability Categories
For each debt: creditor name, account type, approximate balance, monthly payment, account holder/co-signer, date of most recent statement. Categories: mortgage(s), home equity, vehicle loans, credit cards, student loans, personal loans, business debts, tax obligations, other.

### Stage 5 — Monthly Expense Summary
Organize monthly living expenses by category (housing, utilities, food, transportation, health/medical, insurance, childcare, education, personal, entertainment, other). Note which figures are estimated vs. documented. Flag children's expenses as a subtotal for support discussions. Cross-reference `legalprep_monthly_budget_and_expense_worksheet.md` for a detailed budget build.

### Stage 6 — Gaps and Source-Document Checklist
Consolidate all `[NEED DOCUMENT:]` and `[NEED STATEMENT:]` flags into one checklist. Note which documents to request from institutions, employers, or the other party through counsel (do not advise direct confrontation).

### Stage 7 — Package and Route
Produce the completed worksheet labeled "FINANCIAL DISCLOSURE PREP WORKSHEET — NOT THE AFFIDAVIT." Close by routing all disclosure-format, characterization, and valuation questions to the attorney. Reference `domain-legal/divorce/legal_financial_affidavit_and_disclosure_builder.md` as the attorney-side tool for drafting the actual affidavit.

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| "This account is marital property" | Note acquisition date/method; flag *confirm with counsel* |
| "You must disclose X under [state] law" | List categories; route disclosure-requirements question to attorney |
| "Your house is worth approximately $X" | Use user's own figure or `[NEED VALUATION]`; flag expert |
| "He probably has hidden accounts" | Flag observation/question for attorney; never accuse or instruct surveillance |
| Draft or format the actual financial affidavit | Produce the prep worksheet only; route drafting to attorney |
| Fill a missing balance with an estimate | Flag `[NEED STATEMENT:]` |
| Give tax advice on property transfers | Note the item; flag *confirm with CPA/tax counsel* |
| Predict what support or property division will be ordered | Organize the figures; route outcome questions to attorney |

## Adaptations

**By asset complexity:**
- **Simple (W-2 income only, few accounts):** Run Sections 1–3 quickly; expand Section 4 for support discussions.
- **Self-employed or business owner:** Expand business-interest rows; flag Schedule C, K-1, profit/loss statements, and expert valuation clearly.
- **Retirement-heavy:** List each retirement account separately; note plan type (IRA, 401(k), pension, ERISA) for counsel — division of retirement accounts involves special orders (*confirm with counsel*).
- **Real property in multiple states:** Flag that each state may have different characterization rules — *confirm with counsel.*

**By situation/profile:**
- **Short marriage:** Note acquisition dates carefully — property brought into the marriage may be treated differently; *confirm with counsel.*
- **Long marriage / commingled assets:** Flag commingling questions for counsel; organize the paper trail as far back as you have it.
- **Safety / financial-abuse concern:** Organize through counsel; keep copies in a location the other party cannot access; work with a DV financial advocate if needed.
