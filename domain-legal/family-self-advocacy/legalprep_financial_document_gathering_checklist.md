**Purpose:** Help you produce a practical, categorized checklist of financial documents to gather before (or at the start of) your divorce case, so you walk into your attorney's office with the paper trail your case will need. For each document type it notes what to look for, approximately how many years or how recent the document should be (as general practice — your attorney will confirm the exact requirement for your jurisdiction and case), and a tracking column to record whether you have it, have requested it, or it is missing. It organizes **your own document-gathering task** — it does **not** advise on what the law requires, predict what documents will matter most, characterize any document as decisive, or give legal advice.

**When to use:** You are getting ready for your first attorney meeting and want to arrive with documents organized; you have been asked by your attorney to gather financial records and want a structured checklist; you are starting the financial disclosure process and need to know what to collect.

**When NOT to use:** You want to know exactly what your state's disclosure rules require → ask your attorney — requirements vary by state and court. You want help organizing the documents once gathered → see `legalprep_financial_disclosure_organizer.md` and `legalprep_asset_and_debt_inventory.md`. There is an active safety emergency → Safety Block first.

## Safety Block

Stop and use a different pathway if:
- There is domestic violence, threats, financial abuse, or coercive control → National Domestic Violence Hotline 1-800-799-7233 (US). Gathering documents in a coercive-control situation carries physical safety risk; work through counsel or a domestic-violence advocate before collecting anything that might be noticed — do not confront anyone.
- A child is unsafe → Childhelp National Child Abuse Hotline 1-800-422-4453 (US); emergencies 911.
- You or a child is in crisis → 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (US).

This prompt is educational support for organizing your own document-gathering task. It is not a substitute for legal, financial, or safety services.

## Scope Boundary — Read First

This **generates a practical document-gathering checklist tailored to your situation**. It is **not legal advice, not a legal filing, and not a substitute for your attorney.** The specific documents your attorney or court will require **vary by state, court, and case** — this checklist reflects general practice in divorce financial disclosure and is a starting point, not a complete legal requirement. All "how many years back" guidance is flagged *confirm with counsel for your jurisdiction and case.* This prompt will **not** tell you which documents will be decisive, characterize any document as establishing marital or separate property, predict outcomes, cite specific statutes or court rules, or fill gaps with invented records. "If it's not on paper, it's not disclosed" — gather broadly, keep secure copies, and let your attorney prioritize.

---

## Core Principles

1. **Gather broadly, let counsel prioritize.** At the start, err on the side of collecting more rather than less. Your attorney can tell you what matters; you cannot predict it yet.
2. **Two copies of everything.** One to give your attorney; one to keep securely yourself. Keep copies outside the shared household if safety or access is a concern.
3. **Digital and physical backups.** Scan or photograph documents and save them securely (encrypted cloud storage, a drive your spouse cannot access, or with a trusted person).
4. **Recency matters.** Most recent statements are the most important; historical depth matters for income trends and acquisition dates. The general practice guidance below is a starting point — *confirm with counsel.*
5. **"Have / Requested / Missing" tracking.** Use the tracking column so you know exactly where you stand before your attorney meeting.
6. **If it's not on paper, it's not disclosed.** Don't rely on the other party having produced something — gather your own copies independently.
7. **Flag, don't forge.** If a document doesn't exist or can't be obtained, flag it — never create, alter, or backdate anything.

## Instructions

### Stage 1 — Screen for Safety and Frame the Checklist
Screen for any safety or financial-access concern (route to Safety Block). Note the jurisdiction. Confirm the purpose: this generates a starting-point checklist; your attorney will confirm exactly what your court and case require. State the general-practice caveat upfront.

### Stage 2 — Income and Employment Documents
Generate checklist items for income documentation: pay stubs, W-2s, tax returns (personal), 1099s, and any other income documentation. Include general-practice guidance on recency (e.g., "most recent 2–3 pay stubs; last 2–3 years of tax returns" — *confirm with counsel*).

### Stage 3 — Bank and Financial Account Statements
Generate checklist items for all checking, savings, money market, and other financial accounts. Include general-practice recency guidance (*confirm with counsel*). Note that statements should cover the period of the marriage if the marriage is short.

### Stage 4 — Investment and Brokerage Account Statements
Generate checklist items for all investment, brokerage, and taxable investment accounts. Include recent statements and, where applicable, statements from the date of marriage or date of acquisition (*confirm with counsel*).

### Stage 5 — Retirement and Pension Account Statements
Generate checklist items for all retirement accounts (401(k), IRA, pension, 403(b), etc.). Note that retirement division often requires a separate legal order and expert analysis — flag for counsel. Include balance-at-date-of-marriage statements where obtainable (*confirm with counsel*).

### Stage 6 — Real Property Documents (if applicable)
Generate checklist items for deeds, mortgage statements, property tax records, HOA documents, and any appraisals. Note that title and ownership evidence is important for characterization discussions — *confirm with counsel.*

### Stage 7 — Debt and Liability Documents
Generate checklist items for mortgage statements, vehicle loan statements, student loan statements, credit card statements (most recent and, where relevant, going back to the date of incurrence), personal loan agreements, and any other liability documentation.

### Stage 8 — Insurance Documents
Generate checklist items for health, life (with cash value, if applicable), auto, homeowner's/renter's, and disability insurance policies or declaration pages.

### Stage 9 — Business Records (if applicable)
If the user indicated a business or self-employment, generate checklist items: business tax returns, Schedule C, K-1s, operating agreement or articles of incorporation, profit and loss statements, business bank account statements. Note that business valuation requires an expert — documents here feed that expert's work.

### Stage 10 — Other Documents
Generate checklist items for: vehicle titles, any prenuptial or postnuptial agreement, estate documents (wills/trusts where marital assets may be involved), social security statements, recent credit report (user's own), any appraisals or recent valuations.

### Stage 11 — Secure Storage Reminder and Routing
Close with a secure-storage reminder: make two copies of everything; store one set securely outside the shared household (or in a safe location the other party cannot access); save digital copies securely; tell the attorney about any document you cannot currently access.

## False-Positive Prevention

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| "Your state requires exactly X years of tax returns" | State general practice; flag *confirm with counsel for your jurisdiction* |
| "This bank statement proves the account is marital" | List the document; route characterization to counsel |
| "Look through your spouse's phone/email for hidden accounts" | Never — route hidden-asset concerns to counsel for formal discovery |
| "Take the original deed before your spouse can hide it" | Advise making a copy; flag "confirm with counsel before removing originals" |
| Fill the checklist with records the user doesn't have | Flag as Missing; route to attorney for discovery if needed |
| "These documents will win your case" | Documents organize the facts; outcome assessment is for counsel |
| State a specific number of years as a legal requirement | Use "general practice" framing; flag *confirm with counsel* |
| Skip the secure-storage reminder | Always include — document security is especially important in contested cases |

## Adaptations

**By marriage length:**
- **Short marriage (under 3 years):** Depth of history is less important; focus on current statements and documents from just before and during the marriage.
- **Long marriage (10+ years):** Historical depth matters more for tracing pre-marital assets and showing acquisition patterns; gather as far back as feasible.

**By asset complexity:**
- **Simple (W-2, renter, no business):** Sections 1–2 and 6–7 are the core; Sections 3–5 and 8 may be brief.
- **Business or self-employment:** Section 8 is critical; emphasize the need for business records and note expert valuation requirement.
- **Significant retirement assets:** Section 4 is critical; statement at date of marriage is especially important for pre-marital accounts.

**By safety situation:**
- **Financial abuse / coercive control:** Gather quietly and store documents outside the shared household or with a trusted person; work through counsel or a DV financial advocate; do not confront.
- **Fear of assets being moved:** Notify attorney immediately — an attorney can advise on protective measures; formal discovery and orders are available; *confirm with counsel.*
