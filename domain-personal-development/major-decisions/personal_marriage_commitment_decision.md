# Major Commitment Decision

**Objective:** Structure a decision to make (or defer) a major commitment — marriage, long-term partnership, moving in together — by reading compatibility on the dimensions that actually carry the weight of a shared life, separating dealbreakers from differences you can live with, and naming what you still don't know. The output is one of three: commit, not-yet (with the specific data to gather first), or don't-commit — grounded in observed behavior, not the intensity of how you feel right now.

**When to use:**
- Considering proposing, accepting a proposal, or agreeing to a long-term commitment.
- Deciding whether to move in together as a step toward (or test of) commitment.
- You feel strongly but can't tell whether the strength of feeling is answering the actual question.
- You keep circling "are they the one" and want a structured read instead.

**When NOT to use:**
- There is abuse, coercion, or safety risk — this is not an analysis problem; get to safety and support first (`domain-personal-development/major-decisions/personal_difficult_relationship_audit.md` defers here too).
- The relationship is already difficult and you're deciding whether to stay — use `personal_difficult_relationship_audit.md`.
- You want the legal or financial mechanics of a prenup, cohabitation agreement, or joint finances — route those specifics to a family-law attorney and a fee-only advisor.

**Audience:** An individual weighing their own commitment decision. Not for assessing whether someone else should commit, and not couples therapy. If distress about the relationship is persistent or overwhelming, this is not a substitute for professional support — see `domain-psychology/` and a licensed professional.

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **The commitment on the table.** Marriage, cohabitation, long-term exclusive partnership — and the rough timeline you're deciding against.
2. **Length and stage.** How long together, whether you've lived together, cohabited under stress, or only seen good conditions.
3. **What's pulling you toward commitment.** Specific: what works, what you've observed, what you want to build.
4. **What's giving you pause.** Specific doubts, recurring frictions, or things you've been avoiding thinking about.
5. **Evidence, per dimension.** For each load-bearing dimension below, what you have actually observed — a conversation had, a stressor weathered, a pattern seen — not what you assume or hope.

If your inputs are all feeling and no observed behavior, say so and ask for concrete evidence before scoring. Compatibility cannot be read from intensity.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Score each load-bearing dimension from observed evidence, not from hope, potential, or a single good conversation.
- Distinguish a **dealbreaker** (a stable difference on something you cannot live without, or a demonstrated behavior that makes a safe shared life impossible) from a **workable difference** (real, but survivable with communication and compromise).
- Separate what is **known** from what is **unresolved but knowable** (needs a conversation or a shared experience) and what is **unknowable in advance** (only time reveals it).
- Name the specific conversations or shared experiences that would convert an "unresolved" dimension into a "known" one.
- End with exactly one call: commit / not-yet / don't-commit.

### Must Not
- Reduce the decision to "do you love them" — love is necessary and not sufficient, and it does not resolve incompatibility on children, money, or geography.
- Treat the best version of the person (their potential, who they are on a good day) as their representative state.
- Moralize about the relationship, the person, or the choice. Observe and structure; do not congratulate or warn.
- Manufacture certainty the evidence doesn't support — "not-yet, gather X" is a legitimate and common output.
- Let a single dealbreaker be buried under a long list of green dimensions. One true dealbreaker is decisive on its own.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Score the load-bearing dimensions
For each dimension below, mark **Aligned / Workable difference / Unresolved / Dealbreaker-risk**, and cite the specific evidence (a conversation, an observed behavior, a stressor weathered). No dimension may be "Aligned" on assumption alone.

| # | Dimension | What it covers |
|---|-----------|----------------|
| 1 | Money | Earning drive, spending vs. saving, debt, financial risk appetite |
| 2 | Children | Whether, when, how many, and parenting philosophy |
| 3 | Location & mobility | Where you'll live; willingness to move for the other's life |
| 4 | Sex & intimacy | Desire, expectations, and explicit terms of fidelity |
| 5 | Roles & domestic labor | How earning, housework, and caregiving get divided |
| 6 | Conflict & repair | How disagreements actually go; whether repair happens |
| 7 | Values & worldview | Religion, politics, ethics, how you'd raise a family |
| 8 | Career & ambition | Whose career leads; tolerance for the other's demands |
| 9 | Extended family | In-laws, obligations, and whether boundaries hold |
| 10 | Health & vice | Substance use, mental health, how illness gets handled |
| 11 | Trust & honesty | Track record of the truth and transparency |
| 12 | Trajectory | Growing toward or apart; moving at a compatible life pace |

### Step 2 — Isolate the dealbreakers
Pull out every dimension marked Dealbreaker-risk. For each, test it: is this a **stable, load-bearing difference** (they want children and you do not; they will never leave this city and your life requires that you do) or a **demonstrated behavior** that makes a safe shared life impossible (repeated dishonesty, untreated addiction with denial, contempt in conflict)? A genuine dealbreaker on children, money, geography, or trust is decisive regardless of how strong the rest of the scorecard looks.

### Step 3 — Separate known from knowable from unknowable
Sort every "Unresolved" dimension into:
- **Knowable now** — resolvable with a direct conversation you have been avoiding.
- **Knowable with time/stress** — only a shared experience will show it (living together, a financial shock, a family crisis).
- **Unknowable in advance** — no amount of pre-commitment data resolves it; commitment itself is the experiment.

### Step 4 — Design the data-gathering moves
For each "Knowable now," write the specific conversation to have and the specific question to ask. For each "Knowable with time/stress," name the experience that would reveal it and whether you can arrange it before deciding (e.g., a lease together, a trip under strain, meeting the family). Vague ("we should talk more") does not count — name the topic and the question.

### Step 5 — Run the two counterfactual checks
- **Fresh-observer:** a smart, direct friend with no stake hears the full scorecard. What do they say — and are you flinching from it?
- **Regret-minimization:** picture committing and it going wrong on the weakest dimension, and picture walking away and it having been right. Which regret is larger and which is more reversible?

### Step 6 — The call
One of three:
- **Commit** — no true dealbreaker, load-bearing dimensions aligned or workable with evidence, and the unresolved items are genuinely unknowable-in-advance rather than avoided.
- **Not-yet** — the case is plausible but at least one load-bearing dimension is "knowable now" and unexamined. Name the exact conversations/experiences and the date by which you'll have the answer.
- **Don't-commit** — a genuine dealbreaker exists, or the evidence across load-bearing dimensions does not support the commitment.

Write a calibration anchor future-you can audit against.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **Intensity mistaken for compatibility.** How strongly you feel is not evidence about money, children, or geography. Score those dimensions on their own evidence.
2. **Potential scored as reality.** "They'll change once we're married / once the pressure's off" — score who they are now, under the conditions you've actually seen, not who they might become.
3. **Good-conditions-only sample.** If you've never seen this person under financial strain, illness, or family conflict, several dimensions are "Unresolved," not "Aligned."
4. **Dealbreaker dilution.** A long list of green dimensions does not neutralize one real dealbreaker. Do not average it away.
5. **Avoided-conversation as unknowable.** A difference you haven't discussed is "knowable now," not "unknowable in advance." Don't file avoidance under fate.
6. **Timeline pressure as evidence.** Age, a lease ending, family expectation, or a ring already bought are not inputs to compatibility. Note them as pressures, keep them out of the scorecard.
7. **Sunk-time reasoning.** "We've been together five years, I should just commit" is a sunk-cost argument, not a compatibility one.
