# Estate Wishes Workbook

**Objective:** Produce a decisions workbook — the people you would name for each role and
why, your backups, your wishes for care and for what you leave, the conversations still owed,
and a question list — that lets an estate-planning attorney turn your intentions into documents
in one meeting instead of three.

**When to Use:**
- You have booked (or keep postponing) an estate-planning appointment and do not know what
  you will be asked to decide.
- A life event has changed the answer: a child, a marriage or divorce, a death, a diagnosis,
  a move, a child reaching adulthood.
- You and a partner disagree about who should raise the children or act for you, and want to
  work it out before paying for the meeting.

**Not this prompt if:**
- You want to know what documents and beneficiary designations you already have and whether
  they conflict — `domain-finance/personal-finance-planning/finance_estate_beneficiary_review.md`
  (run it too; the two are designed as a pair).
- You want a will, trust, power of attorney or advance directive written or checked — that is
  an attorney's work. This prompt drafts none of them and does not interpret existing ones.
- You are deciding care for a parent — `personal_caring_for_aging_parent.md`.
- A death has already happened and you are handling the admin — `domain-productivity/home-life/home_after_a_death_admin_checklist.md`.

**Boundary (hard):** No document language, no statements about what the law requires or allows,
no tax figures or thresholds. Role names (executor, agent, guardian, trustee) vary by
jurisdiction; use them as plain-language placeholders and let the attorney map them.

## Inputs / Context

1. **Household:** partner status, children (ages, any with additional needs), dependants,
   pets, where you live and any property elsewhere (flag for the attorney).
2. **People you trust**, and for each: age, health, location, reliability with money and
   paperwork, relationship with your children, and whether they would want the role.
3. **What matters to you** beyond money: care preferences, faith or cultural practices,
   heirlooms, a family business, digital accounts, charities.
4. **What exists already** — from the beneficiary review if run; otherwise "unknown".
5. **Tensions:** blended family, estranged relatives, a family member you would not want involved.

## Method

1. **Map the roles (OC-09).** For each, write in plain language what the person would actually
   do, so you are choosing for the job rather than the honour:

   | Role (plain language) | What they would do | Skills it needs |
   |---|---|---|
   | Settles your estate | Paperwork, accounts, distributions, possibly for a year or more | Organised, even-handed, available |
   | Handles money if you cannot | Pays bills, manages accounts while you are alive but incapacitated | Trustworthy with money, nearby helps |
   | Speaks for your healthcare if you cannot | Talks to doctors, makes treatment decisions in line with your wishes | Calm under pressure, will follow *your* wishes |
   | Raises your children | Daily parenting, schooling, values | Shares your values, willing, stable |
   | Manages money left to children | Holds and spends for them until they are older | Financially careful, can say no |

2. **Score candidates across dimensions (RT-02).** For each role: willingness (asked? not
   assumed), capability, availability and location, relationship with those affected, and
   conflict of interest. Keep "raises the children" and "manages the children's money" as
   separate decisions — the best parent is not always the best money manager.

3. **Name a first choice and a backup for every role**, with one sentence of reasoning each.
   A plan with no backup fails if the first choice dies with you or declines.

4. **Record care wishes in your own words.** What a good day looks like if you are very ill;
   what you would and would not want; who you want with you; spiritual or cultural practices.
   These inform the directive the attorney drafts; they are not the directive.

5. **Record wishes for what you leave.** Broad intentions ("equal among the children";
   "the house stays available to my partner"), specific items and who should have them,
   pets, digital accounts, charities. Mark anything that depends on law or tax as a question.

6. **List the conversations owed.** Everyone named should be asked before the meeting.
   Script the ask briefly; record the answer.

7. **Build the attorney handoff (NE-20).** One package: the roles table, wishes, what already
   exists, special situations (additional-needs child, property in another jurisdiction,
   business, blended family), and numbered questions. The attorney should be able to act on
   it without a second intake call.

## Output Format

```
# Estate wishes workbook — [initials], [date]
Meeting with attorney: [date] · Beneficiary review done? [yes/no]

## Roles
| Role (plain language) | First choice | Why | Backup | Asked? Answer |

## Care wishes (my words)
## What I want to happen to what I leave
## Special situations to flag
## Conversations owed | Person | Topic | By when | Outcome |
## Questions for the attorney (numbered)
## Not decided yet — and what would decide it
## Confidence: which choices feel settled (High) vs. provisional (Low)
```

## Verification

- [ ] Every role has a first choice and a backup, each with a reason.
- [ ] Children's carer and children's money manager are decided separately.
- [ ] Every named person has been asked, or asking them is listed as owed.
- [ ] Care wishes are in the user's own words, not legal phrasing.
- [ ] No document text drafted; no legal rule or tax figure asserted.
- [ ] Special situations and other-jurisdiction property are flagged for the attorney.
- [ ] The question list is numbered and specific.

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **Choosing for the honour.** The eldest sibling or closest friend is not automatically the
   best executor. Choose for the job described in step 1.
2. **Unasked appointees.** Naming someone who would decline, or who learns of it at the
   funeral, is a plan that fails on first contact.
3. **One person for everything.** Concentrating every role in one person removes checks and
   overloads them at the worst time.
4. **Drafting creep.** "Could you just word the guardianship clause?" — no. The workbook
   records intent; the attorney writes.
5. **Asserting law.** "Stepchildren don't inherit unless…" and similar statements vary by
   jurisdiction and change. Turn them into questions.
6. **Ignoring existing designations.** Retirement accounts and life policies often pass by
   beneficiary form regardless of the will; if the beneficiary review has not been run, list
   that as a gap.
7. **Deferring until perfect.** A provisional choice with a backup is better than a blank;
   mark it Low confidence and revisit.

## Example Output

```
# Estate wishes workbook — M.O. and partner, 2026-09-24
Meeting with attorney: 2026-10-20 · Beneficiary review done? yes (2026-09-10; ex-spouse still on pension — flagged)

## Roles
| Raises our children (7, 10) | Sister (Priya) | Shares values; kids stay in same school district | Friend (Dan & Lee) | Asked 09-18: yes |
| Manages children's money | Brother-in-law (Sam) | Accountant, careful; not the carer, so a check on spending | Priya | Not yet asked |
| Settles estate | Partner | Knows accounts | Sam | Partner yes; Sam pending |
| Healthcare voice (for M.O.) | Partner | Will follow wishes | Priya | Yes |
| Money if incapacitated | Partner | — | Sam | Yes / pending |

## Care wishes (M.O.'s words)
"If I can't recognise my family and won't recover, comfort over prolonging. I want the kids to visit if they want to, not be made to."

## What I want to happen to what I leave
Everything to partner; if both die, equally to children, held until an age the attorney advises. Grandmother's ring to daughter.

## Special situations: pension still names ex-spouse (from beneficiary review); cabin in another state

## Questions for the attorney
1. How do we fix the pension beneficiary, and does the will affect it?
2. What does the cabin in another state need so it does not require a second process?
3. At what age and in what structure should money for the children be released?
4. How do we record that Priya raises the children but Sam manages the money?

## Not decided yet: whether Dan & Lee or Priya's partner is the second backup — decide after talking to Priya
## Confidence: guardian High · children's money Medium (Sam not yet asked)
```

## Techniques Used

- **RT-02 Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework** — candidates scored on willingness, capability, availability, relationships, conflict.
- **OC-09 Capability Boundary Specification** — plain-language role jobs, and a hard line at drafting and legal interpretation.
- **NE-20 Third-Party Handoff Package** — a package the attorney can act on without re-intake.
- **CM-02 Constraint Specification** — backup-for-every-role and separate carer/money decisions.
- **QA-12 False Positives Identification** — honour-based choices, unasked appointees, drafting creep.
