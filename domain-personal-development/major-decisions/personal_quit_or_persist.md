# Quit or Persist

**Objective:** Structure the quit-or-persist decision rigorously — whether you're considering quitting a job, shutting down a business, leaving a program, abandoning a project, or walking away from a path you've been on. The hardest part is not seeing the situation clearly; it's knowing whether you're in a genuine no-win situation or in a temporary hard stretch that persistence will clear. This prompt builds the frame: what's the goal, is the current path viable, what are the actual costs in each direction, where is sunk cost biasing the thinking, what would a fresh observer see, and what tripwires should force a reassessment if you persist.

**When to use:**
- Seriously considering quitting something you've been invested in.
- Going in circles on whether to stay or leave without reaching clarity.
- Feeling both the pull to quit and the pull to persist, and not sure which is the signal.
- About to make the call — wanting one final structured pass before acting.

**When NOT to use:**
- You're in acute crisis, not a decision point. Stabilize first.
- The decision is already made — this is a pre-decision tool.
- The stakes are low enough that the cost of a wrong answer is minimal.

**Audience:** Individuals at a genuine decision point about whether to persist in a high-investment path or cut losses and leave.

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **What you're considering quitting.** The job, business, relationship, program, project — with basic context on how long you've been in it and what you've put in.
2. **The goal.** What were you trying to achieve when you started? Has the goal changed?
3. **What's pulling you toward quitting.** Be specific: what's wrong, what's not working, what's the pain.
4. **What's pulling you toward persisting.** What would be lost, what's still possible, what would make it worth staying.
5. **How long you've been feeling this way.** Months? A specific trigger event? A slow accumulation?

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Clarify the goal being pursued before evaluating the path. A path that's failing toward one goal may be succeeding toward a different, unstated goal — or the goal may have changed.
- Distinguish clearly between sunk cost (what's been invested — irrelevant to the forward decision) and forward-looking cost (what you'd invest going forward).
- Compute the cost of one more period (3 months, 6 months, 1 year) explicitly, in specific terms.
- Compute the cost of quitting explicitly: financial, social, identity, opportunity, timing.
- Apply the fresh-observer test: what would someone who has no prior investment in this situation recommend, and why?
- Surface the tripwire: if persistence is chosen, what conditions trigger a mandatory quit?
- End with exactly one of: persist with tripwire / quit / pause — not "it depends."

### Must Not
- Let sunk cost masquerade as a reason to persist. "I've already invested too much to quit" is a sunk-cost fallacy. Surface it and correct for it.
- Let quit-avoidance masquerade as wisdom. "Real winners don't quit" is an equally invalid heuristic. Some quits are correct.
- Let the analysis stay abstract. "It's not going well" — what specifically? "I don't know if I can succeed" — what would success look like and what's the probability?
- Produce a "it depends on your values" conclusion without taking a position. The prompt recommends.
- Conflate "this is hard" with "this is not working." Difficulty is not evidence of failure; it's common to both failing and succeeding situations.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Clarify the goal (present, not original)
State the goal being pursued right now — not the goal you started with, but the goal you're actually trying to reach:
- Has the goal shifted since you started? Is that a legitimate evolution or goal-post moving?
- If you succeeded at the current path, what would you have?
- Is that what you actually want?

Sometimes persistence is wrong not because the path is failing but because the goal has changed and the path no longer leads there.

### Step 2 — Assess viability of the current path
Given what you know now:
- Is the current path viable for reaching the goal? (Not "possible" but "viable given realistic inputs.")
- What would need to be true for it to succeed? Is that plausible?
- What's the probability-weighted outcome if you persist for one more period?
- What evidence would tell you the path is working? Have you seen it?

This is where wishful thinking most commonly enters. Demand specific evidence, not hope.

### Step 3 — Cost of one more period
Define a unit of time: 3 months, 6 months, 1 year — whatever is meaningful for the situation. Then compute the cost of persisting for that period:
- Financial cost (money spent, income foregone, opportunity cost)
- Time cost (hours per week × weeks)
- Emotional and cognitive cost: what does this take out of you?
- Cost to other areas of life: relationships, health, parallel opportunities foreclosed
- What new information you'd have at the end of that period that you don't have now

### Step 4 — Cost of quitting
Compute the cost of quitting now:
- Financial: sunk costs (already spent — do not count toward forward decision), wind-down costs, income transition, contracts, commitments.
- Social: who knows you're doing this, what does quitting signal to them?
- Identity: who have you been while doing this? Who would you be after quitting? Does quitting feel like failure or like relief?
- Opportunity: what does quitting open up that continuing forecloses? (Quitting is not only cost; it's also optionality.)
- Timing: is now a better or worse time to quit than later?

### Step 5 — Sunk cost correction
Explicitly name everything you've invested that you will not recover regardless of the decision:
- Time already spent: [X months / years]
- Money already spent: [$X]
- Identity invested: [...]
- Relationships built around this: [...]

Now draw the line: none of these should influence the forward decision. The forward decision is based on what happens from today onward, not what's behind you. Ask: if you had invested nothing and were evaluating this opportunity fresh today, would you start it?

### Step 6 — Fresh observer test
Describe your situation to a smart, direct person who has no prior knowledge of it and no stake in the outcome. They've heard everything you've said. What do they recommend, and why?

Write their recommendation in their voice, not yours. Push it to be direct — not "it's hard to say" but an actual position.

Common fresh-observer flags:
- "You've been saying you'll quit for X months but haven't — what's actually keeping you?"
- "The goal you're describing isn't the same as the goal you started with."
- "The path you're on requires conditions that aren't present and shows no sign of becoming present."
- "You're persisting to avoid the identity cost of quitting, not because the path is working."

### Step 7 — Tripwire design (if persisting)
If the recommendation is persist, pre-commit to a tripwire — a specific condition that forces the quit regardless of how you feel in the moment:
- Time-based: "If by [specific date] X has not occurred, I quit."
- Event-based: "If [specific event] happens, I quit immediately."
- Metric-based: "If [specific measure] falls below [threshold], I quit."

The tripwire must be observable and non-negotiable. "I'll reassess" is not a tripwire.

### Step 8 — Decision and calibration anchor
One of three:
- **Persist with tripwire:** the path is viable, the next period of investment is justified by expected information gain or outcome, and the tripwire is set.
- **Quit:** the path is not viable for the current goal, the cost of one more period exceeds the expected value of persisting, or the goal has changed and this path doesn't lead there.
- **Pause:** the decision requires information that's not yet available (offer pending, medical result, partner decision), and committing either direction now is premature. Pause has a specific duration and a re-decision date.

Write the calibration anchor: one sentence that captures what you're choosing and the reasoning, that future-you can audit against.

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **Sunk cost persistence.** "I've put in three years, I can't quit now." The three years are gone; they are not a reason to invest more. Restate the forward case without them.
2. **Quit-is-failure framing.** Some quits are correct and some persists are mistakes. The quality of the decision doesn't correlate with which direction it goes.
3. **Difficulty-as-failure signal.** Hard things are hard both when they're working and when they're not. Difficulty is not evidence that the path is wrong; neither is it evidence that it's right.
4. **Vague viability.** "I think it could work if things improve" — this is not a viability assessment. What specifically must improve, by when, and how probable is that?
5. **Identity-as-persistence reason.** "Quitters don't succeed" — this is an identity statement, not analysis. Sunk identity is as irrelevant as sunk money.
6. **Tripwire avoidance.** Setting a tripwire that's vague or self-negotiable defeats its purpose. The whole value of a tripwire is that you can't rationalize your way out of it.
7. **Fresh-observer sanitizing.** Writing the fresh observer's recommendation in a way that aligns with what you want to hear. The fresh observer is useful precisely because they're direct.
8. **Pause-as-avoidance.** Pause is a legitimate choice when information is genuinely pending. It's an avoidance mechanism when the user just isn't ready to decide.
9. **Goal-drift invisibility.** Persisting toward a goal you no longer want because changing goals feels like admitting failure.

# Quit-or-persist — [what you're considering quitting]

## Current goal (present, not original)

- Is that what you actually want? [yes / no / partially]

## Viability of current path
- Evidence the path is working: [specific]
- Evidence it's not: [specific]
- What would need to be true for it to succeed: [...]
- Probability of that: [honest estimate, not hopeful]

## Cost of one more period ([N months])
- Financial: [...]
- Time: [...]
- Emotional / cognitive: [...]
- Cost to other life areas: [...]
- New information I'd have at end of period: [...]

## Cost of quitting now
- Financial (forward only, not sunk): [...]
- Social: [...]
- Identity: [...]
- Opportunity opened by quitting: [...]
- Timing (now vs. later): [...]

## Sunk cost inventory
| What's sunk | Amount / scope | Influences forward decision? |
|-------------|---------------|------------------------------|
| Time        |               | No                           |
| Money       |               | No                           |
| Identity    |               | No                           |

If evaluated fresh today with zero prior investment: [would I start this? yes / no — evidence]

## Fresh observer
"If a smart, direct person with no stake heard all of this, they'd say: [their actual position, not hedged]."

Fresh-observer flag triggered? [yes/no — which one]

## Tripwire (if persisting)
- Type: [time / event / metric-based]
- Condition: if [specific condition] by [specific date], quit immediately.
- Non-negotiable? [confirm this tripwire cannot be rationalized around]

## Decision
- Recommendation: [persist with tripwire / quit / pause until (date)]
- Primary rationale: [goal + viability + cost — not sunk cost]
- If pause: re-decision date and conditions: [...]

## Verification

- [ ] Goal stated in present tense (what you're actually pursuing now, not the original).
- [ ] Viability assessed with specific evidence, not hope.
- [ ] Cost of one more period computed in concrete terms.
- [ ] Cost of quitting computed including opportunities opened.
- [ ] Sunk costs named and explicitly excluded from forward decision.
- [ ] Fresh-start test applied ("if evaluated with zero investment today").
- [ ] Fresh observer wrote a direct recommendation, not a hedge.
- [ ] Tripwire defined (if persisting): specific, observable, non-negotiable.
- [ ] Recommendation is one of three: persist / quit / pause — not "it depends."
- [ ] Calibration anchor written.
