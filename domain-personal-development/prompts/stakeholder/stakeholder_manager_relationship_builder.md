# Diagnose and Repair or Strengthen One Manager Relationship

**Objective:** Determine the real trust state of one manager relationship, pinpoint the specific misfires that shaped it, and produce one calibrated relationship-building move — repair if trust is damaged, deepen if it's merely functional.

**When to use:** A specific manager relationship feels stuck, cool, or strained; you sense you've lost ground and don't know exactly where; or the relationship is fine but shallow and you want it to carry more weight before review season or a stretch ask. Not for a general "how's my career" audit — use `career-transformation/`. Not for drafting the repair conversation itself — use `domain-product-management/` or `domain-negotiation/difficult-conversations/`.

**Audience:** An individual working on their own relationship with their own manager. Not for coaching someone else's relationship, not clinical. If the strain involves harassment, discrimination, or a pattern that's affecting your health, this prompt is out of scope — route to HR, and to `domain-psychology/` and professional support.

---

## Inputs Required

1. **Relationship snapshot.** How long you've reported to this manager, how the relationship started, and how you'd describe it in one honest sentence.
2. **Trust evidence — both directions.** 4–8 concrete moments that tell you something about trust: times they extended you rope (autonomy, a stretch assignment, public backing) and times they pulled it back (checking your work, going around you, cooling off). Each as: *what happened → what it signaled.*
3. **Suspected misfires.** Any specific incidents you think damaged or capped the relationship — a missed deadline, a disagreement handled badly, a surprise you caused them, a promise you didn't keep. Include ones you're not sure about.
4. **What you want the relationship to enable.** Concretely: more autonomy, advocacy in calibration, a bigger project, honest feedback, a reference someday.
5. **What you've observed about how they build trust with others.** Who on the team has their confidence, and what those people seem to do differently.

If input 2 has fewer than 3 concrete trust signals, refuse and ask for more. Trust state can't be diagnosed from a single incident or from generalized unease.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Locate the relationship on a fixed trust ladder

Using input 2, place the relationship on exactly one rung:

| Rung | What it looks like | Typical signal |
|---|---|---|
| **Damaged** | Trust was present and was lost | Rope pulled back after a specific event |
| **Guarded** | Never established; default caution | Consistent low autonomy, no clear rupture |
| **Transactional** | Reliable but shallow | Work flows fine, no advocacy or candor |
| **Solid** | Mutual confidence, some candor | Backs you publicly, gives honest feedback |
| **Sponsoring** | Actively invests in your advancement | Spends their capital on you when you're not in the room |

Cite the specific signals from input 2 that place it. Name the rung above as the realistic target — you build one rung at a time.

### Step 2 — Run root-cause on the misfires

For each suspected misfire in input 3, trace it to which trust dimension it hit:

- **Reliability** — you didn't deliver what/when you said.
- **Judgment** — a call you made worried them.
- **Transparency** — they got surprised; you sat on something.
- **Alignment** — you optimized for something they don't value.
- **Deference-vs-autonomy fit** — you pushed back too hard, or not enough, for their style.

Separate misfires that actually damaged trust (they appear in input 2's "rope pulled back" signals) from ones you're carrying guilt about that left no trace. Drop the guilt-only ones — they're your anxiety, not their ledger.

### Step 3 — Read how this manager grants trust

From input 5, extract the actual trust-earning pattern with this manager. Some grant trust through reliability over time, some through visible competence, some through candor, some through low-drama loyalty. Name which currency this specific manager runs on — the repair or deepening move must be paid in *that* currency, not the one that would work on a generic boss.

### Step 4 — Match the move to the rung

Pick the intervention type that fits the Step 1 rung. Do not apply a repair move to a merely-shallow relationship or a deepening move to a damaged one:

- **Damaged →** a targeted repair: name the rupture, demonstrate the corrected behavior over a bounded stretch, don't over-apologize.
- **Guarded →** a reliability streak: a small, visible, over-delivered commitment that gives them a reason to extend rope.
- **Transactional →** one candor or initiative move that shows there's more to you than task execution.
- **Solid →** a value-add or vulnerability move that invites sponsorship (see `stakeholder_mentor_sponsor_cultivation.md`).

### Step 5 — Produce one calibrated move

Output **one** relationship-building move: rung-appropriate, paid in the manager's trust currency (Step 3), and targeting the highest-signal misfire or gap (Step 2). It must be a specific, observable action with a time bound — a delivered commitment, a proactive flag, a direct conversation, a demonstrated correction. Not "rebuild trust" and not "be more reliable in general."

State one predicted observable: the specific behavior change from the manager that would tell you the move landed.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Place the relationship on exactly one trust rung with cited evidence.
- Trace each real misfire to a specific trust dimension via root-cause.
- Identify the manager's specific trust currency before proposing the move.
- Produce exactly one move, matched to the rung.

### Must Not
- Recommend a grand gesture, over-apology, or trust-fall exercise.
- Prescribe a repair move for a relationship that was never ruptured.
- Blame the manager entirely or the user entirely — trace the actual mechanism.
- Suggest "just have an open conversation" without specifying what's said and why now.
- Moralize about who was right, or cheerlead the user's growth.

### Must Not (boundary)
- Treat a harassment/discrimination/abuse pattern as a trust-repair problem — that routes to HR and professional support, not this prompt.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **Don't diagnose "damaged" from your guilt.** A misfire only damaged trust if it shows up in the manager's *behavior* (input 2). Guilt with no behavioral trace is your anxiety, not a rupture — Step 2 drops it.
2. **Don't diagnose "guarded" as "damaged."** A relationship that was never warm isn't a broken one; the moves are different. Look for a rupture event before calling it damaged.
3. **Don't apply your preferred trust currency.** If you value candor but this manager grants trust through reliability, a bold truth-telling move can backfire. Pay in their currency.
4. **Don't over-read a cool patch.** A busy or stressed manager pulling back temporarily is weather, not a trust verdict — cross-check against their behavior with the whole team.
5. **Don't confuse compliance with trust.** A manager who checks your work isn't necessarily distrustful of you specifically — some check everyone. Look for *differential* treatment.
6. **Don't stack moves.** One rung, one currency, one move. A flurry of relationship-building reads as neediness and erodes the thing you're building.

## Output Format

```
## Trust state
Rung: [Damaged / Guarded / Transactional / Solid / Sponsoring]
Evidence: [specific signals from input 2]
Realistic next rung: [one up]

## Misfire root-cause
| Suspected misfire | Trust dimension hit | Left a behavioral trace? | Keep / Drop |
|---|---|---|---|
| ... | reliability/judgment/transparency/alignment/fit | yes/no | ... |

## This manager's trust currency
[Reliability / competence / candor / low-drama loyalty / other] — evidence: [from input 5]

## The move (calibrated to rung + currency)
[Specific, observable action, by when.]

Predicted check: if it lands, expect [specific manager behavior change] within [timeframe].
```

## Verification

- [ ] Relationship placed on exactly one trust rung with cited evidence.
- [ ] Each suspected misfire traced to a trust dimension; guilt-only ones dropped.
- [ ] The manager's specific trust currency is named from evidence, not assumed.
- [ ] The move type matches the rung (no repair move on an unruptured relationship).
- [ ] Exactly one move, observable and time-bounded, with a predicted manager-behavior check.
- [ ] No grand gestures, over-apology, or generic "open conversation."
- [ ] Harassment/discrimination patterns routed out of scope, not treated as trust repair.
