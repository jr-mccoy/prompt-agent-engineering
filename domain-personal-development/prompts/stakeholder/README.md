# Stakeholder Navigation & Organizational Politics

Prompts for reading and navigating multi-party organizational situations: mapping who holds power, what each person actually wants, where interests align, and what path gets you to your outcome without burning trust. Written for an individual operator working a situation themselves — not for an HR function, not for facilitating someone else's conflict.

## When to use these

Use this subfolder when:

- A decision you need requires buy-in from several people with conflicting interests.
- The "right answer" depends on who you ask, and influence matters as much as logic.
- You're caught between competing teams, agendas, or priorities.
- Something is blocking you and the blocker is political, not technical.
- You want to understand a power dynamic before you act in it.

**Not the right subfolder when:**

- You need bargaining mechanics — BATNA, ZOPA, concession sequencing → see `domain-negotiation/`.
- You need to *write* the message, email, update, or proposal → see `domain-professional-communication/`.
- The situation is a single 1:1 difficult conversation (firing, hard feedback, a breakup) → see `domain-negotiation/difficult-conversations/`.
- You're a manager assigning or coordinating work → see `domain-engineering-workflows/workflows/` or `domain-business-strategy/`.
- The problem is interpersonal-emotional rather than political/structural → see `domain-personal-development/prompts/identity/` or `domain-psychology/`.

## Prompts in this subfolder

**Multi-party situation reads** (map the whole field, decide a path):

| Prompt | One-line description |
|---|---|
| `stakeholder_navigation_guide.md` | Map all stakeholders (power, interests, fears), generate 3 strategic paths, and produce a recommended 72-hour action plan. |
| `stakeholder_politics.md` | Deeper power/incentive read for high-conflict situations — distinguishes stated positions from actual incentives and builds coalition-based options with a 24-hour first move. |

**Specific-relationship builders** (work one relationship for your own effectiveness and advancement):

| Prompt | One-line description |
|---|---|
| `stakeholder_managing_up.md` | Build an evidence-based model of your manager (priorities, pressures, communication style, reward triggers) and produce one change to how you surface work — plus one line you won't cross, so it's calibration, not sycophancy. |
| `stakeholder_manager_relationship_builder.md` | Place a specific manager relationship on a fixed trust ladder, root-cause the misfires that shaped it, and design one rung-appropriate repair-or-deepen move paid in that manager's trust currency. |
| `stakeholder_visibility_and_credit.md` | For good work that's invisible: trace where credit leaks in the work-to-recognition pipeline and design one honest visibility move — real work made legible, not self-promotion theater. |
| `stakeholder_mentor_sponsor_cultivation.md` | Hard-classify your network as mentors (advise) vs. sponsors (spend capital on you), filter against your goal's decision surface, and design one role-appropriate ask with reciprocal value. |
| `stakeholder_cross_team_alliance.md` | Turn a peer on another team into a durable ally: map interests vs. positions, classify the ask on a fixed grid, and give first with one low-cost reciprocal act. |

## How the prompts relate

**The two situation-read prompts are a graduated pair:**

- **Start with `stakeholder_navigation_guide.md`** for most multi-party situations. It produces a broad stakeholder map and a 72-hour plan.
- **Escalate to `stakeholder_politics.md`** when the situation is genuinely political — where power, headcount, turf, or ego is the real subject under the stated issue, and a coalition strategy is needed.
- They can be **chained**: run the navigation guide to map the field, then the politics navigator to pressure-test the power read on the highest-stakes opponent.

**The five relationship-builders work a single named relationship** rather than a whole field:

- `stakeholder_managing_up.md` and `stakeholder_manager_relationship_builder.md` are a pair for the manager relationship — the first calibrates *how you surface work*, the second diagnoses and repairs *trust*. Run managing-up first for a new or working relationship; escalate to the relationship-builder when trust is damaged or capped.
- `stakeholder_visibility_and_credit.md` fixes the leak between doing good work and being seen for it — often the missing piece when managing-up is solid but advancement stalls.
- `stakeholder_mentor_sponsor_cultivation.md` reaches upward (converting advisors into advocates); `stakeholder_cross_team_alliance.md` reaches sideways (peers without authority over each other). Both run on genuine reciprocity, not favors owed.

After you act, use `../agency/agency_feedback_extraction.md` to convert the reactions you get into signal, and `../thinking/thinking_blind_spot_mirror_see_what_im_missing.md` to check your read before committing.

## Shared design principles

- **Stated position ≠ underlying interest.** Every stakeholder gets both, distinctly.
- **No fabrication.** Stakeholders, motives, power levels, and quotes come from the user's input; unknowns are flagged "needs input," not guessed.
- **Look for alignment before zero-sum.** Cross-party shared interest is identified before any win/lose framing.
- **Influence through trust, not manipulation.** No Machiavellian or deceptive tactics are recommended.
- **First move you can make alone.** The recommended next action requires no one else's prior approval.
- **Acknowledge your own bias.** The user's position colors their read; the prompts surface that.
