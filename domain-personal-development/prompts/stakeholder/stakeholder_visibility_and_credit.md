# Audit Where Your Credit Leaks and Design Honest Visibility

**Objective:** Find the specific points where your real contributions fail to reach the people who allocate recognition, then produce one visibility move that makes true work legible — grounded in things you actually did.

**When to use:** Your work is solid but you're passed over, surprised by lukewarm reviews, or watching less-productive peers get noticed; a decision-maker seems unaware of what you actually contributed; or you're about to enter a review/promotion cycle and your impact isn't documented. Not for fabricating impact you didn't have, and not for drafting the brag doc or update itself — that's `domain-product-management/`.

**Audience:** An individual making their own real work visible for their own advancement. Not for assessing whether someone else deserves credit, not clinical. If invisibility at work has tipped into chronic worthlessness or hopelessness, that's beyond a visibility problem — see `domain-psychology/` and a licensed professional.

---

## Inputs Required

1. **Contribution log — last 2 quarters.** 6–12 things you actually did that mattered: shipped work, problems solved, fires prevented, people unblocked, decisions influenced. Each as: *what you did → the actual outcome/impact → who (if anyone) knows.* "Prevented" and "unblocked" count — invisible-by-nature work is exactly the target here.
2. **Who allocates recognition.** The specific people whose awareness determines your reviews, promotions, or opportunities (manager, skip-level, calibration peers, a sponsor).
3. **How recognition currently forms here.** How impact actually gets noticed in your org: self-written reviews, manager observation, peer feedback, visible artifacts, meeting presence, metrics dashboards.
4. **Evidence of the leak.** Specific moments where your work went uncredited or misattributed: someone else got thanked for it, a review missed it, a decision-maker asked "what have you been working on?"
5. **Your discomfort line.** What forms of visibility feel dishonest or gross to you (so the output routes around them rather than through them).

If the contribution log is thin or vague ("worked hard," "helped the team") with fewer than 5 concrete outcomes, refuse and ask for more. You cannot make invisible work visible if you can't yet name what the work was.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Trace the work-to-recognition pipeline and find the leak

Real work reaches recognition through a pipeline. Locate where yours breaks, using a fixed taxonomy of leak points:

| Leak point | Symptom | Root cause |
|---|---|---|
| **Invisible-by-nature** | The work has no artifact (prevention, unblocking, glue) | Impact never took a visible form |
| **Wrong audience** | Your artifact exists but the recognition-allocator never sees it | It lands with peers, not deciders |
| **Attribution gap** | The work is seen but credited to someone else or "the team" | You didn't claim it at the moment it landed |
| **Timing gap** | Recognized in the moment, forgotten by review time | No durable record |
| **Legibility gap** | Seen but not understood as impactful | Framed as activity, not outcome |

Map each contribution (input 1) that went uncredited (input 4) to its leak point. Most people have one dominant leak — name it.

### Step 2 — Separate the real problem from imposter noise

Test the leak against reality: are the uncredited items genuinely high-impact (would a neutral observer agree the outcome mattered), or is some of this a self-worth read rather than a credit read? Keep only contributions where the impact is defensible on the evidence. Drop the ones where the honest answer is "the work was fine but not notable" — inflating those is where visibility becomes theater.

### Step 3 — Match the visibility mechanism to the leak, not to your personality

For the dominant leak point, select the mechanism that actually closes it:

- **Invisible-by-nature →** create a lightweight artifact at the moment of impact (a one-line "heads-up, prevented X" note; a short retro).
- **Wrong audience →** route existing artifacts to the recognition-allocator through the channel input 3 says they actually watch.
- **Attribution gap →** claim in-the-moment, specifically and factually ("I built the caching layer that fixed the latency") — not later, not vaguely.
- **Timing gap →** maintain a durable running record decision-makers can see or that feeds your review.
- **Legibility gap →** reframe from activity to outcome and audience-relevant metric.

### Step 4 — Stay inside honesty and your discomfort line

Check the candidate move against two gates: (a) it references only work you actually did with impact that actually occurred; (b) it does not require the forms of visibility you named in input 5 as dishonest. If the best mechanism crosses your discomfort line, find the least-theatrical version that still closes the leak — quiet legibility beats loud performance.

### Step 5 — Produce one visibility move

Output **one** move: it targets the dominant leak, uses the matched mechanism, routes to the actual recognition-allocator, and stays inside both honesty gates. Specific and observable — a concrete artifact, a specific claim in a specific meeting, a running doc started this week. Not "raise your profile" or "network more."

State the predicted check: what a decision-maker would now be able to say about your work that they couldn't say before.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Map uncredited contributions to specific leak points from the fixed taxonomy.
- Keep only contributions with defensible, evidence-backed impact.
- Match the mechanism to the leak point, not to the user's comfort.
- Produce exactly one visibility move that reaches the recognition-allocator.

### Must Not
- Recommend claiming, implying, or standing near credit for work the user didn't do.
- Recommend manufactured busyness, performative posting, or reply-all theater.
- Prescribe generic "build your personal brand" advice.
- Push the user across their stated discomfort line.
- Moralize about self-promotion being distasteful, or cheerlead the user's underrated genius.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **Don't treat every miss as a credit leak.** Some work genuinely wasn't notable. Step 2 exists to stop inflating ordinary work into stolen glory — that's the fastest way to lose credibility.
2. **Don't diagnose an attribution gap when it's a legibility gap.** People may see your work fine but not grasp why it mattered; claiming louder won't fix framing. Match the leak precisely.
3. **Don't confuse invisibility with under-performance.** Verify the impact is real before designing visibility — the goal is legibility of true work, not a spotlight on thin work.
4. **Don't route to the wrong audience.** Visibility with peers who don't allocate recognition feels productive but doesn't move reviews. Target input 2.
5. **Don't mistake your imposter feeling for evidence of a leak — or your ego for evidence of impact.** Both distort. The evidence in inputs 1 and 4 arbitrates, not the feeling.
6. **Don't prescribe self-promotion that violates the discomfort line.** A move the user won't actually execute is a fantasy; the least-theatrical effective version is the real deliverable.

## Verification

- [ ] Uncredited contributions mapped to specific leak points; one dominant leak named.
- [ ] Only defensible-impact contributions kept; thin work dropped, not inflated.
- [ ] Mechanism matches the leak point, not the user's comfort preference.
- [ ] The move routes to an actual recognition-allocator via a channel they watch.
- [ ] Both honesty gates pass: real work only, inside the discomfort line.
- [ ] Exactly one move, observable and time-bounded, with a predicted decision-maker check.
- [ ] No credit-claiming for others' work, no performative theater, no generic branding advice.
