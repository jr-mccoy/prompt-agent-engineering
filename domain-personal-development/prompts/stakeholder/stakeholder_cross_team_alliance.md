# Build a Durable Cross-Team Alliance to Get Things Done Without Authority

**Objective:** Turn a peer on another team — whose help you need but can't order — into a durable ally by locating the real overlap between their interests and yours, then producing one low-cost reciprocal act that opens the relationship.

**When to use:** You need something from another team (a dependency, a review, prioritization, shared effort) and have no authority to demand it; your requests to a peer team keep stalling; or you want a standing relationship with a counterpart before you need a favor. Not for multi-party concession sequencing or formal bargaining — use `domain-negotiation/`. Not for a whole-org political map — use `stakeholder_navigation_guide.md`.

**Audience:** An individual building their own cross-team working relationships to get things done. Not for a manager coordinating teams from above, not clinical. This is about influence through mutual interest, not manipulation.

---

## Inputs Required

1. **What you need and by when.** The specific cooperation you need from the other team — a task, a decision, capacity, access — and the real deadline or trigger.
2. **The counterpart.** The specific peer (not the whole team) you'd work with: their role, their team's mission, who *they* report to, and what their team is measured on this quarter.
3. **Their current priorities and load.** What that team/person is already committed to, and how your ask lands against it (aligned, orthogonal, or competing for the same capacity).
4. **Relationship history.** Prior interactions with this person or team — smooth, transactional, tense, none — with specifics if any.
5. **What you can offer that costs you little but helps them.** Information, a small task, a connection, cover/air-support, early access, a shared artifact, a warm word to their boss. Be concrete.

If input 2 names only a team and no actual person, or input 3 shows you don't know what the other team is measured on, refuse and ask for more. You can't align interests you haven't identified.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Map both sides' interests, separating them from positions

Using inputs 1–3, lay out your interest (what you actually need and why) and the counterpart's interests (what their team is measured on, what makes their quarter, what their boss watches). Distinguish **position** ("we don't have bandwidth") from **interest** (protecting their roadmap, avoiding blame, hitting their metric). Cross-links `domain-negotiation/preparation/negotiation_interest_mapping.md` for the deeper mechanics — here, keep it to the one relationship.

### Step 2 — Classify the interest relationship on a fixed grid

Place your ask against their priorities:

| Type | What it means | Alliance strategy |
|---|---|---|
| **Aligned** | Your ask advances their metric too | Make the shared win explicit; co-own it |
| **Orthogonal** | Your ask is neutral to them — pure cost | Lower their cost and offer reciprocal value |
| **Competing** | Your ask trades against their priority | Find a smaller version, a trade, or route up (their manager) |
| **Latent-aligned** | Not aligned now, but a future need of theirs matches your strength | Invest early; bank goodwill |

The strategy differs by type — misreading a competing ask as orthogonal is why "quick favor" requests detonate.

### Step 3 — Find the genuine overlap (or admit there isn't one yet)

Name the real point where their interest and yours touch. If the honest answer is "aligned" or "latent-aligned," that's your alliance foundation. If it's "competing" with no overlap, don't manufacture one — the move becomes either a smaller ask, an explicit trade, or escalation through the right manager, and you say so plainly.

### Step 4 — Design one low-cost reciprocal act

Alliances open with *you* giving first, cheaply and visibly, before you collect. From input 5, pick one act that: costs you little, genuinely helps them against *their* measured priority, and is delivered before (or alongside) your ask — not dangled as a bargaining chip. The point is to establish that working with you is net-positive, so the relationship survives past this one need.

### Step 5 — Produce one alliance-opening move

Output **one** move: the reciprocal act plus how and when you make the ask, matched to the Step 2 interest type and routed to the actual counterpart. Specific and observable — a delivered thing, a specific conversation, a shared artifact this week. Not "build a relationship with the platform team."

State the durability check: the future situation in which this ally would now help you *without* a fresh negotiation — the sign it became an alliance, not a transaction.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Separate the counterpart's stated position from their underlying interest.
- Classify the ask against their priorities on the fixed grid.
- Give first: the reciprocal act precedes or accompanies the ask, not held as leverage.
- Produce exactly one alliance-opening move routed to a specific person.

### Must Not
- Recommend guilt, escalation-as-first-move, or going over the counterpart's head before trying interest-alignment.
- Manufacture a fake "shared win" where the ask is genuinely competing — name the real trade instead.
- Treat the ally as a one-time resource; the move must build a standing relationship.
- Offer reciprocal value that costs the user nothing *and* helps the other person nothing (empty gestures).
- Moralize about office politics, or cheerlead collaboration as inherently virtuous.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **Don't read a competing ask as orthogonal.** If your request pulls capacity from something they're measured on, it costs them real points — treating it as a neutral favor is why it stalls. Classify honestly on the grid.
2. **Don't manufacture alignment.** A fabricated "this helps us both!" that the counterpart can see through destroys credibility faster than an honest trade. If it's competing, say so and offer a real exchange or a smaller ask.
3. **Don't lead with escalation.** Going to their manager before trying peer interest-alignment converts a potential ally into an adversary. Escalation is a Step-3 fallback for genuine competing asks, not an opener.
4. **Don't confuse a transaction with an alliance.** Getting the favor once isn't the goal; the durability check in Step 5 distinguishes a relationship you can draw on again from a one-off.
5. **Don't offer reciprocity that isn't reciprocal.** "I'll owe you one" is not value. The offered act must actually help *their* measured priority, or it's noise.
6. **Don't over-invest in a latent-aligned ally under deadline.** Early investment is for banking goodwill, not for an urgent need — if input 1's deadline is tight, a latent-aligned counterpart may be the wrong target this time; say so.

## Output Format

```
## Interests (positions vs. underlying)
- You need: [interest, not just the ask]
- [Counterpart] is measured on: [their interest]; their stated position (if any): [position]

## Interest-relationship type
[Aligned / Orthogonal / Competing / Latent-aligned] — because [evidence from inputs 2–3].

## The genuine overlap (or honest absence)
[Where interests touch] — OR: no overlap; the move is [smaller ask / explicit trade / route through manager].

## Low-cost reciprocal act (given first)
[Specific act from input 5 that helps their measured priority, delivered before/with the ask.]

## One alliance-opening move (this week)
[Reciprocal act + how/when you make the ask, to [specific person], by [when].]

Durability check: next time you need [type of thing], this ally would help without renegotiating because [reason]. That's the sign it's an alliance, not a transaction.
```

## Verification

- [ ] Counterpart's position separated from their underlying interest.
- [ ] Ask classified on the fixed interest grid with cited evidence.
- [ ] The reciprocal act is given first and genuinely helps the ally's measured priority.
- [ ] No manufactured "shared win" where the ask is really competing — honest trade named instead.
- [ ] Escalation appears only as a fallback for genuine competing asks, never as the opener.
- [ ] Exactly one move to a specific person, observable and time-bounded.
- [ ] A durability check distinguishes the alliance from a one-off transaction.
