# Peer Conflict Navigation

**Objective:** Turn a recurring conflict with a colleague at your own level into a
clear read of what is actually happening, a decision on whether it is yours to handle
or a formal matter, and one move — a conversation or a structural change — that
reduces the friction without escalating it needlessly.

**When to Use:**
- A peer presents shared work as theirs, or speaks first about your results.
- You and a colleague keep clashing over approach, priorities, or who owns what.
- A peer's behaviour in meetings (interrupting, dismissing, re-litigating) is
  costing you, and your manager has not noticed.
- You are about to complain to your manager and want to check your read first.
- **Not this prompt if** your work is uncredited but no specific person is taking
  it — that is a pipeline problem: `stakeholder_visibility_and_credit.md`. If you
  need a peer's cooperation and there is no conflict yet, use
  `stakeholder_cross_team_alliance.md`. If you have already decided to have the
  conversation and need the one-page script, use
  `domain-negotiation/difficult-conversations/difficultconvo_pre_brief.md`; this
  prompt decides *whether* and *which* move. For the conflict with your manager, use
  `stakeholder_manager_relationship_builder.md`. If you are the manager or HR
  investigating a complaint between others, this is the wrong side of the table.

**Audience:** An individual dealing with a colleague at their own level. Not for
managers mediating between reports, not an HR investigation, not legal advice. If
the conflict is affecting your sleep or health over weeks, see `domain-psychology/`
and a professional as well.

## Inputs / Context

**Required:**
1. **Incident log** — 3–8 specific incidents with date, what was said or done,
   who was present, and the effect on you. Quote where you can.
2. **Your interpretation** of why the colleague does it.
3. **Shared structure** — who you both report to, what you share (project,
   metrics, budget), how ownership was assigned (in writing or not).
4. **What you have already tried**, and the response.
5. **Relationship value** — will you work with this person for months or years?

**Optional:** How others perceive the two of you; any feedback you have received
about your own part.

## Method

1. **Split facts from interpretations (RT-05).** For each incident, write what a
   camera would record, then the story you added. Interpretations are hypotheses,
   not findings.
2. **Generate two other explanations** for the pattern (unclear ownership,
   pressure from their manager, different norms about "we" vs "I", their own
   insecurity). Note which incidents each explanation fits. Fundamental attribution
   error is the default failure here.
3. **Classify the dominant conflict type (DP-06)** — using the task / process /
   relationship distinction from team-conflict research, plus credit:
   - **Task** — disagreement about the work itself. Often productive.
   - **Process** — who does what, when, how decisions are made.
   - **Credit / status** — attribution and visibility.
   - **Relationship** — personal friction, tone, respect.
   Name one dominant type. Relationship conflict that started as process conflict
   is usually fixed at the process level.
4. **Severity gate (DP-05).** Route out of this prompt to formal channels (HR,
   ethics line, or employment counsel) if any incident involves harassment,
   discrimination, retaliation for a complaint, threats, safety, or integrity
   (falsified data, plagiarism of work product submitted externally). Document
   with dates and stop here.
5. **Choose a conflict mode.** Using the Thomas–Kilmann modes (competing,
   collaborating, compromising, avoiding, accommodating), pick on two axes: how
   much the issue matters to you, and how much the relationship matters. High/high
   → collaborate. Low issue → accommodate or avoid on purpose. State why.
6. **Design one move.** Either *structural* (written ownership, named authors on
   documents, a shared update you send, a RACI line agreed with your manager) or
   *direct* (a private conversation with an opening line that names one incident,
   its effect, and a specific request). Structural moves are preferred for credit
   and process conflicts because they do not depend on the other person changing.
7. **Pre-empt their likely response (NE-23)** — denial, counter-accusation,
   "it's a team effort" — with one calm reply each.
8. **Set the fallback.** If the pattern repeats after the move: how many more
   incidents, over what period, before you raise it with the shared manager — and
   framed as a work problem with a proposed fix, not a character complaint.

## Output Format

```
# Peer conflict read — [colleague role], [date]

## Facts vs. stories
| Date | What happened (camera view) | Story I added |

## Other explanations
| Explanation | Incidents it fits |

## Conflict type: [Task / Process / Credit-status / Relationship] — because [..]

## Severity gate: [clear / ROUTE — reason and channel]

## Mode: [..] — issue importance [H/M/L], relationship importance [H/M/L]

## The move
Type: [structural / direct]   What: [..]   By: [date]
Opening line (if direct): "[..]"

## Likely responses
| They say | You say |

## Fallback
If [n] more incidents by [date] → raise with [manager] as: "[..]"
```

## Verification

- [ ] Every incident has a fact column free of adjectives about motive.
- [ ] At least two alternative explanations tested against the incidents.
- [ ] One dominant conflict type named with reasoning.
- [ ] Severity gate explicitly checked; any formal-channel trigger stops the plan.
- [ ] Mode chosen from the two axes, not from comfort.
- [ ] Exactly one move, dated; fallback has a numeric trigger.

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **Malice inferred from ambiguity.** "They did it on purpose" is rarely
   provable. Act on the effect, not the motive.
2. **Your own share missed.** If you also present joint work as yours, or never
   wrote down ownership, say so; the move must cover your side.
3. **One incident as a pattern.** Fewer than three incidents is a moment — use a
   light touch, not a campaign.
4. **Escalating a formal matter as "friction".** Harassment or retaliation
   treated as a personality clash leaves the person exposed. The gate comes first.
5. **Over-escalating ordinary task conflict.** Disagreement about the work is often
   healthy; going to the manager about it reads as poor judgement.
6. **A speech instead of a request.** A direct conversation ends with one specific,
   doable ask, not a list of grievances.

## Example Output

```
# Peer conflict read — fellow senior analyst (Dana), 2026-10-03

## Facts vs. stories
| 09-04 | Dana presented churn model in staff meeting; slides had no names; I built 80% | "She's stealing my work" |
| 09-11 | Dana replied-all to VP's question about the model before I could | "She wants to be the face of it" |
| 09-19 | VP thanked "Dana and team" in the monthly note | "Now it's official" |
| 09-26 | Dana asked me for the model code "so I can walk the VP through it" | "She's taking it over" |

## Other explanations
| Dana is lead on the client account, so speaks first by habit | 09-04, 09-11, 09-26 |
| No one wrote down who owns the model | all four |
| Dana is up for promotion and under pressure to show impact | 09-11, 09-19 |

## Conflict type: Credit-status, rooted in Process — ownership was never assigned
in writing; the credit problem follows from that.

## Severity gate: clear — no harassment, retaliation, or integrity issue.

## Mode: Collaborating — issue importance High (promotion cycle in Q1),
relationship importance High (same pod for at least a year).

## The move
Type: structural first, then direct.   By: 2026-10-08
1. Add a model card to the repo naming me as author and Dana as account lead;
   send the VP walkthrough invitation myself, inviting Dana.
2. Then a 15-minute 1:1 with Dana.
Opening line: "I want us both to get credit on the churn work. In the 19 Sept note
it went to 'Dana and team', and I'd like the model to carry my name as the builder
from here on. Can we agree I present the model and you present the client impact?"

## Likely responses
| "It's a team effort." | "Agreed — so let's name the roles. I built it; you own the client side." |
| "You never said anything." | "Fair. I'm saying it now and writing it down." |
| "The VP prefers one contact." | "Then let's both be in the room for 15 minutes." |

## Fallback
If 2 more presentations of the model go out without authorship by 2026-11-15 →
raise with our manager as: "We need ownership of the churn model written down
before Q1 reviews; here's my proposal."
```

## Techniques Used

- **RT-05 Evidence-Based Reasoning** — every claim rests on a camera-view incident, not an interpretation.
- **DP-06 Dominant Driver Identification** — one conflict type named so the move targets the root.
- **DP-05 Stakes-Based Gate Policy** — formal-channel triggers stop the self-handled plan.
- **NE-23 Objection Pre-emption** — calm replies prepared for the likeliest pushback.
- **QA-12 False Positives Identification** — guards against inferred malice and over-escalation.
