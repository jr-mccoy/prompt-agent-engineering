# Solo Developer Burnout Prevention

**Objective:** Detect, prevent, and recover from burnout as a solo developer — covering workload assessment, boundary-setting strategies, sustainable pace definition, delegation and elimination opportunities, and recovery protocols — producing a personalized sustainability plan that keeps you productive long-term without sacrificing your health or relationships.

**When to Use:** Use this prompt when you feel constantly exhausted despite sleeping, when you dread opening your laptop, when you can't remember your last day off, when your family/friends are telling you to stop working, when you're making more mistakes than usual, or when you've been running at maximum intensity for more than 2 months. Also use preventively when starting a new project — designing sustainable habits from the start is easier than breaking burnout cycles later.

**Important context:** Burnout is not a badge of honor. It is a business risk. A burned-out solo developer ships worse code, makes poor decisions, responds slowly to users, and eventually quits entirely. Your startup dies when you burn out, because there's no one to take over. Preventing burnout is not self-indulgence — it's the most important thing you can do for the long-term success of your business.

**Disclaimer:** This prompt provides productivity and wellbeing guidance, not medical advice. If you are experiencing symptoms of clinical depression, anxiety, or other mental health conditions, please consult a healthcare professional.

---

## Inputs / Context

Before building a sustainability plan, honestly assess your situation. Answer the questions below; the plan is built from these answers.

### Refusal logic (insufficient input)

- If the user gives **no honest self-assessment** (skips the questions or answers only "I'm fine, just give me a plan"), do not generate a generic wellness plan. Ask them to answer at least the Current State and Symptoms Check questions first — a sustainability plan with no diagnosis is decoration.
- If the answers describe **acute clinical symptoms** (e.g., suicidal ideation, inability to get out of bed, panic attacks), stop the productivity framing and direct the user to a healthcare professional or crisis resource. This prompt is not a substitute for clinical care.
- Do not infer a burnout level the user has not described; ask for the missing assessment input instead of guessing.



1. **Current State (be honest):**
   - "How many hours per week are you working on your app/business?"
   - "When was the last time you took a full day off (no email, no code, no thinking about the app)?"
   - "How would you rate your energy level on a scale of 1-10?"
   - "Are you sleeping 7+ hours most nights?"
   - "Are you exercising or moving your body at least 3 times per week?"

2. **Symptoms Check:**
   - "Do you feel dread or anxiety when thinking about work?"
   - "Are you making more mistakes or taking longer to complete tasks than usual?"
   - "Have you lost interest in things you used to enjoy (including coding)?"
   - "Are you irritable or short-tempered with people around you?"
   - "Do you feel like nothing you do matters or makes progress?"

3. **Work Patterns:**
   - "Do you work evenings and weekends regularly?"
   - "Do you check your phone for app metrics, reviews, or support emails before bed?"
   - "Do you feel guilty when you're not working?"
   - "Is your work interrupted by support requests or monitoring alerts at all hours?"
   - "When was the last time you worked on something just for fun?"

4. **Support System:**
   - "Do you have people in your life who understand what you're going through?"
   - "Do you have other developers or founders you talk to?"
   - "Does your family or partner support your work schedule?"

## Instructions

### CRITICAL: Honest Assessment

**Before creating ANY sustainability plan, you MUST:**

1. **Take the assessment seriously** — Burnout creeps up gradually. Most people don't recognize it until it's severe. The assessment questions above are diagnostic, not decorative.
2. **Distinguish tiredness from burnout** — Being tired after a hard week is normal. Burnout is persistent exhaustion, cynicism, and reduced effectiveness that doesn't go away with rest. They require different responses.
3. **Accept that something must change** — If you're burned out and change nothing, it gets worse. The plan must include things you stop doing, not just things you add.
4. **Be realistic about recovery time** — If you're already burned out, recovery takes weeks to months, not a single weekend.
5. **Prioritize health over product** — A delayed feature is recoverable. A health crisis is not. This is not motivational advice — it's risk management.

### False-Positive Prevention

- ❌ Do NOT diagnose clinical depression, anxiety, or other mental health conditions — recommend professional help if symptoms suggest clinical issues
- ❌ Do NOT recommend "just work harder" or "push through" — that caused the problem
- ❌ Do NOT suggest abandoning the project as the default solution — restructuring usually works
- ❌ Do NOT create a "wellness plan" that adds more tasks to an overwhelmed schedule
- ❌ Do NOT imply that burnout means weakness — it's a structural problem, not a character flaw
- ✅ DO distinguish between immediate recovery needs and long-term prevention
- ✅ DO recommend removing work before adding wellness activities
- ✅ DO address the structural causes (overwork, isolation, no boundaries)
- ✅ DO provide specific, actionable changes rather than generic advice
- ✅ DO normalize the experience — most solo developers go through this

### Phase 1: Burnout Assessment

#### 1.1 The Burnout Spectrum

Assess where you are on this spectrum:

| Level | Name | Symptoms | Recovery Needed |
|-------|------|----------|-----------------|
| **1** | Stretched | Occasional stress, still enjoying work, manageable | Adjust schedule, add breaks |
| **2** | Strained | Persistent tiredness, motivation dipping, some dread | Reduce hours, enforce boundaries |
| **3** | Burning** | Exhaustion, cynicism, frequent mistakes, neglecting health | Significant reduction, time off, restructure |
| **4** | Burned Out | Cannot function effectively, dreading all work, physical symptoms | Extended break, professional help, major restructure |
| **5** | Collapsed | Health crisis, relationship damage, unable to work | Full stop, medical attention, slow rebuilding |

**Be honest about your level.** The interventions are different at each stage.

#### 1.2 Burnout Source Identification

Burnout has specific causes. Identify which apply:

| Source | Signs | Present? |
|--------|-------|----------|
| **Overwork** | Working 60+ hours, no days off | [ ] |
| **Loss of control** | React to everything, plan nothing | [ ] |
| **Insufficient reward** | No revenue, no growth, no recognition | [ ] |
| **Isolation** | No one to talk to about challenges | [ ] |
| **Unfairness** | Comparing yourself to funded teams | [ ] |
| **Values mismatch** | Building what users want, not what you care about | [ ] |
| **Context switching** | CEO/CTO/support/marketing every day | [ ] |
| **Perfectionism** | Nothing is good enough, can't ship | [ ] |
| **Fear of failure** | Anxiety about app failing, money running out | [ ] |
| **No boundaries** | Work bleeds into every moment of life | [ ] |

### Phase 2: Immediate Interventions (This Week)

If you're at Level 3 or above, do these now:

#### 2.1 Emergency Circuit Breakers

```markdown
1. SET A HARD STOP TIME TODAY
   Pick a time (e.g., 6 PM). When it hits, close your laptop.
   Not "after this one thing." Now.

2. TAKE TOMORROW OFF
   If you can't take a full day off, something is structurally wrong.
   Your app will survive 24 hours without you.

3. TURN OFF AFTER-HOURS NOTIFICATIONS
   - Disable Play Console email notifications after 6 PM
   - Mute Slack/Discord work channels
   - Set Crashlytics alerts to email-only (not push)
   - The only alert that justifies 3 AM is "your app is down for ALL users"

4. TELL SOMEONE
   Text a friend, family member, or fellow developer:
   "I'm feeling burned out. I'm going to take some time to recalibrate."
   Saying it out loud makes it real and creates accountability.
```

#### 2.2 The "Stop Doing" List

The most effective intervention is removing tasks, not adding wellness activities.

**Ask yourself for each regular activity:**

| Activity | Can I stop it entirely? | Can I do it less often? | Can someone else do it? |
|----------|------------------------|------------------------|------------------------|
| Daily metrics check | Stop: check weekly instead | | |
| Responding to reviews in real-time | | 2x/week batch | |
| Social media engagement | | 3x/week, not daily | |
| Monitoring dashboards | Automate with alerts | | |
| Feature requests from users | Collect but don't commit | | |
| "Quick fixes" that take hours | Add to backlog, prioritize later | | |
| Reading tech news | Stop for 2 weeks | | |
| Comparing to competitors | Stop. Seriously. | | |

### Phase 3: Structural Prevention (This Month)

#### 3.1 Sustainable Pace Definition

**Define YOUR sustainable pace:**

```markdown
## My Sustainable Pace

Maximum work hours per week: [N] hours
  (Recommendation: 35-40 for full-time, 15-20 for side project)

Work days: [Which days]
Work hours: [Start] to [End]
  (Recommendation: Fixed start and END times. The end time matters more.)

Days off per week: [N]
  (Minimum: 1 full day. Recommended: 1.5-2 days)

Vacation: [N] days per quarter
  (Recommendation: At least 1 week per quarter, even if you don't travel)

Emergency override budget: [N] hours per month
  (For genuine emergencies — not "I just want to finish this feature")
  (If you're using this every month, your "emergencies" are normal work)
```

#### 3.2 Boundary Implementation

**Physical boundaries:**
- Designate a workspace. When you leave it, work is over.
- If you work from your bedroom, at minimum close the laptop and put it away.
- Don't put work communication apps on your personal phone (or set app timers).

**Temporal boundaries:**
- Set calendar blocks for "NOT WORKING" — treat them like meetings.
- Use Screen Time or equivalent to enforce app usage limits.
- No screens 1 hour before bed. Seriously. Sleep quality directly affects code quality.

**Mental boundaries:**
- Write down tomorrow's plan before stopping today. This prevents "I'll just think about it" loops.
- Have a shutdown ritual: review what you did, write tomorrow's top priority, close everything.
- When a work thought intrudes during off-time, write it in a capture note and let it go.

#### 3.3 Connection and Community

Isolation is the most underrated burnout factor for solo developers.

**Low-effort connection options:**

| Option | Effort | Benefit |
|--------|--------|---------|
| Join an Indie Hackers or developer Discord | 30 min/week | Peer support, shared experience |
| Find 1 accountability partner | 30 min/week | Someone who checks in on you |
| Attend 1 local or virtual meetup per month | 2 hours/month | Face-to-face connection |
| Post progress updates (building in public) | 15 min, 3x/week | Community + visibility |
| Talk to a friend/family member about work | 30 min/week | Emotional support |

You don't need a large network. You need 1-3 people who understand what you're doing and can tell you when you're overdoing it.

### Phase 4: Long-Term Sustainability

#### 4.1 Energy Management (Not Just Time Management)

Different tasks drain different types of energy:

| Task Type | Energy Type | When to Schedule |
|-----------|------------|-----------------|
| Deep coding | Creative energy | Your peak hours (usually morning) |
| Bug fixing | Problem-solving energy | Secondary peak hours |
| Support emails | Emotional energy | Batch, not during peak hours |
| Marketing content | Creative energy | After coding, when warmed up |
| Business admin | Administrative energy | Low-energy time (afternoon) |
| Strategic thinking | Reflective energy | After a break (walk, exercise) |

**Key insight:** If you put support emails during your peak creative hours, you've wasted your best energy on your least leveraged activity.

#### 4.2 The Minimum Viable Effort Approach

Not everything needs to be excellent. Define quality levels:

| Area | Minimum Viable | Good Enough | Excellent |
|------|---------------|-------------|-----------|
| **Core product** | | | ← Aim here |
| **Support responses** | | ← Aim here | |
| **Marketing content** | ← Aim here | | |
| **Business admin** | ← Aim here | | |
| **Code formatting** | ← Aim here | | |
| **Internal documentation** | ← Aim here | | |

**Permission to be "good enough":** Your users don't see your code architecture. They see whether the app works and whether you respond to their problems. Allocate perfectionism to things users experience directly.

#### 4.3 Regular Check-Ins

**Weekly (2 minutes — part of your Friday closeout):**
- Energy level this week (1-10): ____
- Hours worked: ____
- Days off taken: ____
- One word to describe this week: ____

**Monthly (15 minutes — part of your monthly review):**
- Am I still on my sustainable pace? [Yes/Drifting/No]
- What's draining me most right now?
- What's energizing me most right now?
- Is there anything I should stop doing?
- Am I looking forward to next month? [Yes/Neutral/No]

**If you answer "No" to the last question two months in a row, something needs to change.**

### Phase 5: Recovery Protocol (If Already Burned Out)

If you're at Level 3-5 on the spectrum:

#### 5.1 Immediate (This Week)

1. **Reduce to 50% of current hours** — not 90%, not 75%. Half.
2. **Cancel all non-essential commitments** — feature deadlines, marketing plans, growth goals. All deferred.
3. **Focus only on keeping the app running** — fix critical bugs, respond to urgent support. Nothing else.
4. **Schedule actual time off** — minimum 3 consecutive days with zero work.
5. **Tell your users** — if needed, post an honest update: "Taking a brief maintenance period to improve quality."

#### 5.2 Short-Term (This Month)

1. **Identify the top 3 burnout sources** from Section 1.2
2. **Address each one specifically:**
   - Overwork → Set hours and enforce them
   - Isolation → Join a community this week
   - No progress → Ship one small improvement (quick win)
   - Perfectionism → Ship something imperfect on purpose
3. **Evaluate what can be cut permanently** — not every feature is worth maintaining
4. **Consider professional support** — a therapist, coach, or mentor

#### 5.3 Long-Term (Next 3 Months)

1. **Rebuild work habits from the ground up** using Phase 3
2. **Start with fewer hours than you think you need** — rebuild up slowly
3. **Measure energy, not output** — track how you feel, not just what you shipped
4. **Celebrate small wins** — you're rebuilding, not starting from zero

## Verification

Before delivering the sustainability plan, confirm each of the following:

- [ ] A burnout level (1–5) is assigned and grounded in the user's actual answers, not assumed.
- [ ] The plan removes work ("stop doing" list) before it adds any wellness activity.
- [ ] At least one structural cause (overwork, isolation, no boundaries, perfectionism, etc.) is named and directly addressed.
- [ ] The sustainable-pace section sets a hard *end* time, not just a start time.
- [ ] If the user is at Level 3+, immediate circuit-breakers (hard stop, day off, notifications off) appear before any long-term advice.
- [ ] No "just push through / work harder" advice appears anywhere.
- [ ] Clinical symptoms, if present, trigger a referral to professional help rather than a productivity fix.
- [ ] The plan is realistic about recovery time (weeks-to-months for actual burnout, not a single weekend).

## Customization Guide

- **For developers with day jobs:** Adjust sustainable pace to prevent total-work burnout (day job + side project). The side project should feel like recreation, not a second job. If it stops being fun, reduce scope.
- **For developers with families:** Add specific family-time protection. Include partner/family in boundary-setting — they're affected by your burnout too. Consider family schedule constraints as non-negotiable.
- **For developers in crisis (Level 4-5):** Skip to Phase 5 immediately. Prevention advice is not helpful when you're in acute burnout. Focus on stabilization first.
- **For developers who feel fine but want prevention:** Focus on Phase 3 (structural prevention) and Phase 4.3 (check-ins). Prevention is about building habits before you need them.
- **For developers who've recovered and want to avoid relapse:** The highest risk of relapse is when things go well — you feel good and slowly increase hours again. Keep the weekly energy check-in permanently.
