# Rebuild Routine, Social Fabric, and Self-Concept After a Relationship Ends

**Objective:** Identify which life structures the ended relationship was quietly holding up and commit to one concrete rebuilding move for the most collapsed one.

**When to use:** A significant relationship has ended — a breakup, a divorce, the end of a long partnership — and the practical scaffolding of your life feels knocked out from under you. Useful in the weeks-to-months after, when the acute shock is passing but the reconstruction hasn't started. Not for deciding whether to end a relationship you're still in — that is `major-decisions/personal_difficult_relationship_audit.md`.

**Audience:** An individual rebuilding their own life after a relationship ends. Not for assessing the ex or the relationship's rights and wrongs, not clinical. If the aftermath is persistent despair, an inability to function for weeks, or any thought of self-harm, this is not a substitute for professional support — see `domain-psychology/`, a licensed professional, or a crisis line.

---

## Inputs Required

1. **Breakup facts.** Length of the relationship, how it ended (mutual / you / them / gradual), how long ago, whether you lived together, and whether there are ongoing ties (children, shared finances, workplace, mutual custody of a pet).
2. **What the relationship held — the inventory.** Across five structures: **daily routine** (meals, mornings, evenings, weekends built around them), **social fabric** (shared friends, their family, couple-friends), **shared identity** (how much of "who I am" ran through the relationship), **physical home** (living situation now disrupted or not), **future story** (plans, timeline, the imagined life).
3. **A real week, now.** How the last 7 days actually go alone — including the times of day that are hardest and any hours that are dangerously empty.
4. **Contact status.** Whether you're in contact with the ex, checking their social media, or no-contact — and whether ongoing ties (input 1) force some contact.
5. **The self-concept hit.** One sentence on what the ending made you believe about yourself or your future (e.g., "I'm unlovable," "I wasted years," "I'll be alone").

If input 2's five structures or input 3 is missing, refuse and ask. You rebuild against the specific scaffolding that fell, measured against the actual week.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Cost each collapsed structure

Rate how badly each of the five structures collapsed *for you now*, from input 3 evidence:

| Structure | Collapse cost | Read from |
|---|---|---|
| Daily routine | High / Med / Low | shapeless days, hard times, empty evenings |
| Social fabric | High / Med / Low | lost mutual friends, isolation |
| Shared identity | High / Med / Low | "who am I without them" |
| Physical home | High / Med / Low | living-situation disruption |
| Future story | High / Med / Low | grief over the imagined life, not the person |

Rate from evidence, not from the loudest feeling.

### Step 2 — Name the dominant collapse

Pick the single highest-cost structure. Tiebreak toward the one whose failure worsens the others (a collapsed daily routine with empty evenings often amplifies rumination and social withdrawal).

### Step 3 — Handle the contact question (input 4)

Contact is a rebuilding precondition, not a side issue. State plainly:

- **No forced ties:** ongoing contact and social-media checking keep the wound open and block reconstruction. Name the specific contact-reduction step (mute, unfollow, a boundary), unless the user is in a phase where a clean goodbye is still needed.
- **Forced ties (co-parenting, shared finances):** full no-contact isn't available; name a *businesslike* contact protocol that protects rebuilding (scheduled, logistics-only, gray-rock) and route custody/co-parenting specifics to the relevant domain resources.

### Step 4 — Separate losing the person from losing the structures

Name which of the pain is about **the person** (missing them specifically) versus **the structures** (missing having-a-routine, having-a-Saturday-plan, having-a-future). Much post-breakup pain is structural and is fixable directly — which is more tractable than the grief and worth reminding the user of.

### Step 5 — Commit one rebuilding move

Design one move for the dominant collapse:

- **Daily routine:** rebuild one anchor at a hard time of day (a claimed morning, a Friday-evening plan) so the empty hour has a shape.
- **Social fabric:** one recurring contact you re-activate or build that is *yours*, not couple-shared (an old friend, a group, a standing plan).
- **Shared identity:** one activity that was yours-before-them or newly yours, reasserting a self that doesn't route through the relationship.
- **Physical home:** one concrete step to make the living space yours again (a rearrangement, a claimed room, a resolved living situation).
- **Future story:** one small, near-term plan that is *yours alone* to look forward to, replacing the imagined shared future with a real near one.

One move, this week, physical and observable.

### Step 6 — The self-concept correction

Address input 5 in one grounded sentence — accurate, not a slogan. The end of a relationship is data about a fit, not a verdict on lovability or worth.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Cost all five structures from input 3 evidence.
- Select exactly one dominant collapse, with a tiebreaker if needed.
- Address the contact question explicitly, adapting to forced vs. non-forced ties.
- Separate person-grief from structure-loss, and commit one rebuilding move.

### Must Not
- Recommend "get back out there / rebound" or dating-app tactics as the fix for structural collapse.
- Adjudicate who was right, or coach the user on winning the breakup / getting them back.
- Encourage social-media monitoring of the ex as "closure."
- Reassure with "the right person is out there" or "you're better off."

---

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **Don't treat all pain as person-grief.** A large share is structural (no Saturday plan, no one to text). Structural loss is directly fixable; conflating it with heartbreak makes the whole thing feel bottomless.
2. **Don't prescribe dating as reconstruction.** Rebounding to refill routine/identity imports a new person into an unrebuilt life; the structures must stand alone first.
3. **Don't force no-contact where ties are real.** Co-parents and financially entangled exes need a workable protocol, not an unrealistic clean break — and custody/legal specifics belong in dedicated resources, not here.
4. **Don't over-diagnose the future-story grief.** Mourning an imagined life is normal and distinct from clinical depression; name it, don't pathologize it — but persistent despair IS clinical.
5. **Don't relitigate the relationship.** This prompt rebuilds forward; it does not assess fault or rehearse the ex's failings, which keeps the user tethered.
6. **Don't miss crisis.** Weeks of non-function, persistent despair, or any self-harm thought routes immediately to `domain-psychology/` and a crisis line — not to a rebuilding move.
