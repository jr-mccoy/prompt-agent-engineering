# Reclaim Purpose, Structure, and Couple Identity After the Children Leave

**Objective:** Map what the active-parenting role actually occupied and commit to one deliberate move that fills the single highest-cost vacated space — purpose, daily structure, or couple identity.

**When to use:** Your youngest child (or an only child) has just left home for college, work, or their own place, and the house and week feel newly hollow. Useful in the first months when the silence is loud. Not for deciding whether or how to support a launching child — this is about your own reorientation.

**Audience:** An individual (or one member of a couple) reorienting their own life after children leave. Not for assessing a spouse or the departed child, not clinical. If the hollowness is persistent grief, loss of the will to do anything, or a sense that life has ended, this is not a substitute for professional support — see `domain-psychology/` and a licensed professional.

---

## Inputs Required

1. **Household facts.** Who left and when, who remains at home (partner, other children, no one), and your paid-work status.
2. **What parenting occupied — the inventory.** For the active-parenting years, list what the role actually filled across five buckets: **time** (hours/week on child logistics), **purpose** (the sense of being needed/useful), **structure** (the calendar spine — school runs, meals, sports), **couple identity** (how much of the relationship ran through the kids), **daily contact** (routine human interaction and touch in the house).
3. **A real week, now.** How the last 7 days actually run without the parenting load — including which hours now feel empty and which time of day is hardest.
4. **The relationship, if partnered.** One honest line: with the kids gone, do you and your partner still have a shared life beyond them, or did the children *become* the relationship?
5. **The specific hollow moment.** One sentence: when does "they're gone" land hardest — a room, a time, a former ritual.

If input 2's five buckets aren't filled or input 3 is missing, refuse and ask. You can't refill a space you haven't measured.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Cost each vacated bucket

For each of the five buckets in input 2, rate how much its loss actually hurts *now*, using input 3 as evidence (empty hours, hard times of day). Use a fixed scale:

| Bucket | Vacancy cost | Read from |
|---|---|---|
| Time | High / Med / Low | empty hours in the real week |
| Purpose | High / Med / Low | the "am I still needed" feeling |
| Structure | High / Med / Low | loss of the calendar spine |
| Couple identity | High / Med / Low | input 4 |
| Daily contact | High / Med / Low | quiet house, no routine interaction |

Rate from evidence, not from what "should" hurt most.

### Step 2 — Name the dominant vacancy

Pick the single highest-cost bucket. If two tie, the tiebreaker is which one, left unfilled, degrades the others (e.g., a collapsed couple identity often drags purpose and contact down with it).

### Step 3 — Read the hollow moment (input 5)

Tie input 5 to the dominant vacancy. The hardest moment usually names the missing bucket directly (empty dinner table → couple identity or daily contact; aimless Sunday → structure or purpose).

### Step 4 — Distinguish refill from replacement

State clearly: the goal is not to *replace* the child (with a pet, over-contact, a rescue project) but to refill the *function* the role served. Name the difference for this specific case — what would be healthy refilling vs. what would be a substitute that keeps you oriented toward the departed child.

### Step 5 — Commit one refilling move

Design one move that fills the dominant vacancy:

- **Purpose:** one specific ongoing commitment where you're genuinely needed (not busywork) — a role, a craft with stakes, a person or cause with a real claim on you.
- **Structure:** one recurring anchor that rebuilds the week's spine (a standing class, a weekly obligation, a rebuilt morning).
- **Couple identity:** one recurring shared activity that is *yours as a couple*, not child-adjacent — plus, if input 4 says the kids became the relationship, one honest conversation about rebuilding a shared life.
- **Daily contact:** one recurring source of routine human interaction (a regular, a group, a return to a workplace or volunteering with people).

One move, recurring, sized to actually start this month.

### Step 6 — The one action this week

Produce the single first step that instantiates the move, with an observable check.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Cost all five buckets from input 3 evidence, not assumption.
- Select exactly one dominant vacancy, with a tiebreaker rule if needed.
- Distinguish refilling the function from replacing the child, for this case.
- Commit one recurring move plus one first action this week.

### Must Not
- Recommend generic "rediscover your hobbies" or "travel more" without tying it to a costed bucket.
- Encourage over-contact with the departed child (daily calls, surprise visits) as the fix.
- Assume the couple relationship is fine, or that it's broken, without input 4.
- Cheerlead about "your time now" or frame the loss as simply liberating.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **Don't assume purpose is the vacancy.** For some, time and structure are fine and the real hole is couple identity or daily contact. Cost from evidence.
2. **Don't route the fix through the child.** Increased contact, tracking, or a manufactured need keeps you parenting a launched adult and stalls their launch and your reorientation.
3. **Don't confuse relief with the absence of loss.** Feeling some relief and some grief simultaneously is normal, not contradiction — don't over-diagnose either.
4. **Don't treat a strained marriage as an empty-nest problem alone.** If input 4 reveals the relationship ran entirely through the kids, that's a real issue to name — but a deteriorating, contemptuous, or unsafe relationship is beyond this prompt; see `relationships_relationship_audit.md` and, if needed, professional support.
5. **Don't pathologize the first weeks.** Acute quiet-house sadness right after departure is expected. Persistent, function-impairing grief or loss of will is not — route to `domain-psychology/`.
6. **Don't over-scope.** One vacancy, one move. Reinventing the whole post-parenting life at once fails.

- [ ] The fix does not route through increased contact with the departed child.
- [ ] One recurring move plus one first action this week, with a check.
- [ ] Couple-identity flag from input 4 handled honestly, not assumed either way.
- [ ] No "travel more," no "your time now" cheerleading.
