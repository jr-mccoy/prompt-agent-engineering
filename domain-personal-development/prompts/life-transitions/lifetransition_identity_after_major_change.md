# Do the 'Who Am I Now' Work When a Defining Role Has Ended or Changed

**Objective:** Separate role-dependent self from durable self, name what genuinely ended versus what merely lost its container, and reclaim one durable thread through a concrete act.

**When to use:** A role that heavily defined you has ended or transformed — athlete after injury, caregiver after the person died or recovered, founder after an exit or shutdown, a professional identity lost, a religious/community role left, a body or ability changed by illness. Useful when the question is genuinely "who am I now," not just "what do I do next." This is the general engine; for specific transitions (retirement, empty nest, job loss, parenthood) prefer the dedicated prompt and use this for what they don't cover.

**Audience:** An individual doing their own identity reorientation. Not for assessing someone else, not clinical. Deep identity loss can shade into grief, depression, or crisis — if you have persistent hopelessness, loss of will, or any thought of self-harm, this is not a substitute for professional support; see `domain-psychology/`, a licensed professional, or a crisis line.

---

## Inputs Required

1. **The role and its end.** The role that defined you, how central it was, how it ended (chosen / forced / gradual / sudden), and how long ago.
2. **The self-inventory under the role.** 6–10 things that felt like "who you are" during the role — traits, capabilities, ways others saw you, ways you saw yourself, the story you told about your life.
3. **Before the role.** Who you were and what you cared about *before* this role existed (if there was a before), or in the domains the role never touched.
4. **Present-tense evidence.** Concrete things from the last few weeks that show which of the input-2 items still show up in your behavior even without the role, and which have vanished.
5. **The core sentence.** The single sentence you find yourself thinking — "I used to be ___ and now I'm ___" or "Without ___ I'm ___."

If input 2 (the self-inventory) or input 4 (present evidence) is missing, refuse and ask. Durable self can only be distinguished from role-dependent self by testing the inventory against present behavior.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Sort the self-inventory by dependence

Classify each item in input 2 using the fixed taxonomy, testing against input 4 (does it still show up without the role?):

| Class | Meaning | Test |
|---|---|---|
| Role-dependent | Existed only because the role supplied the context/audience/tools | Vanished the moment the role ended |
| Role-expressed | A durable trait the role happened to channel | Still shows up, in other forms, in input 4 |
| Role-suppressed | A part of you the role crowded out, now with room to return | Was strong before the role (input 3), re-emerging |

Every item must be tested against evidence, not assigned by feel.

### Step 2 — Name what actually died vs. what lost its container

Use `RT-09` to distinguish two things people conflate:

- **Genuinely ended:** capabilities or relationships that truly require the role and are gone (the specific team, the physical ability, the daily contact with the person cared for). These are real losses to grieve.
- **Lost its container, not itself:** durable traits (Role-expressed items) that feel dead only because their former vehicle is gone. A competitor's drive after sport, a carer's attentiveness after loss — the trait lives; it needs a new container.

State plainly which input-2 items fall in each. This is the pivot of the whole prompt.

### Step 3 — Confront the core sentence (input 5)

Take the "I used to be ___, now I'm ___" sentence and rewrite it accurately from Steps 1–2. The honest version usually reads: "I was ___ [role]. The durable parts — ___ — are still mine and need new form. What genuinely ended is ___, and that is worth grieving." Neither false-continuity ("nothing's really changed") nor total-erasure ("I'm nobody now").

### Step 4 — Identify the durable thread to reclaim first

From the Role-expressed and Role-suppressed items, select the single thread that (a) is most load-bearing for a coherent sense of self and (b) can be given a new container now. Name it and why.

### Step 5 — Give it a container: one concrete act

Design one concrete act that expresses the durable thread outside the ended role — a specific activity, role, craft, or relationship that channels the same trait. It must be physical and startable this month, and it must be a genuine *expression* of the thread, not a hollow substitute for the lost role.

### Step 6 — Name the grief that stays

Explicitly name the genuinely-ended losses from Step 2 that the reclaiming act does *not* fix. Do not paper over them. Acknowledging what won't be replaced is part of an honest reorientation, and forcing an all-positive rebuild is dishonest.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Classify every input-2 item into exactly one of the three classes, tested against input 4.
- Distinguish genuinely-ended loss from lost-container using RT-09.
- Rewrite the core sentence accurately — neither false-continuity nor total-erasure.
- Reclaim exactly one durable thread via one concrete, startable act, and name the residual grief honestly.

### Must Not
- Reassure that "you're still you" without doing the dependence-sorting work.
- Recommend "find a new passion" generically, unmoored from a durable thread.
- Deny the genuine losses or force a purely positive reinvention.
- Diagnose or treat grief/depression — name it and route it.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **Don't over-assign Role-dependent.** In acute loss, everything feels gone; input 4 usually shows durable traits still firing. Test before declaring death.
2. **Don't over-assign Role-expressed either.** Some capabilities really were the role and are genuinely gone (a specific physical ability, an institutional position). Honest grief requires admitting real endings, not relabeling every loss as a "container problem."
3. **Don't confuse identity loss with depression.** They co-occur and overlap. Reorientation work is appropriate for the identity question; persistent hopelessness, anhedonia, or self-harm thoughts are clinical and route to `domain-psychology/` and a professional — this prompt does not treat them.
4. **Don't manufacture a durable thread that isn't there.** If input 3/4 shows the role genuinely was most of the person's expressed self, say so and start smaller — one re-suppressed pre-role interest — rather than inventing continuity.
5. **Don't rush the timeline.** Identity reorientation after a major role-end is a months-to-years process; the prompt produces one starting act, not a resolved new self.
6. **Don't moralize about the old role.** Whether the role "should" have been so central is not the question; observe what it held and reorient.
