# Replace Work-Derived Identity, Structure, and Social Contact After Retirement

**Objective:** Name the four functions the job silently provided, cost the loss of each now, and commit to one recurring anchor that rebuilds the highest-cost function.

**When to use:** You have stopped working — full retirement, phased-out, or an involuntary end late in a career — and the days feel unmoored despite the freedom. Useful in the first months when "every day is Saturday" has curdled from relief into aimlessness. Not for deciding when or whether to retire — that is a decision-stage question.

**Audience:** An individual redesigning their own post-work life. Not for advising a retiring spouse, not clinical, and not financial-planning advice. If the unmooring is persistent depression, loss of will, or a sense of worthlessness rather than ordinary reorientation, this is not a substitute for professional support — see `domain-psychology/` and a licensed professional.

---

## Inputs Required

1. **Retirement facts.** When you stopped, from what work, whether it was chosen or forced, whether phased or abrupt, and who you share your days with (partner, alone, others).
2. **What work provided — the four functions.** For your working life, describe what the job supplied across: **identity** (who you were / what you said at parties), **structure** (the shape of the day and week), **social contact** (colleagues, routine interaction), **purpose** (being useful, contributing, solving problems).
3. **A real week, now.** How the last 7 days actually went — wake time, what fills mornings/afternoons, social contact, and which stretches feel empty or too long.
4. **What you told yourself you'd do.** The retirement plans you had (travel, golf, projects, grandkids) and, honestly, which have materialized and which haven't.
5. **The specific flat moment.** One sentence: when does the aimlessness hit hardest — a time of day, a day of the week, a situation (Monday morning with nowhere to be).

If input 2's four functions or input 3 is missing, refuse and ask. The work-shaped hole can only be refilled once you've named what it held.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Cost each lost function

Rate how much the loss of each function hurts now, from input 3 evidence, on a fixed scale:

| Function | Loss cost | Read from |
|---|---|---|
| Identity | High / Med / Low | discomfort answering "what do you do" / lost status |
| Structure | High / Med / Low | shapeless days, too-long stretches |
| Social contact | High / Med / Low | drop in routine interaction; isolation |
| Purpose | High / Med / Low | "am I contributing anything" feeling |

Rate from the real week, not from what retirees "should" miss.

### Step 2 — Name the dominant loss

Select the single highest-cost function. Common trap: people assume it's Purpose, but for many the acute loss is Structure (the day has no spine) or Social contact (work was 80% of their human interaction). Let evidence decide.

### Step 3 — Check the plan-reality gap (input 4)

Note which retirement plans materialized and which didn't, and why. "I'll finally do X" often fails not from laziness but because X was a solo, unstructured, unaccountable activity — the exact conditions the job used to supply externally. Name whether the unrealized plans are missing structure, accountability, or social scaffolding.

### Step 4 — Read the flat moment (input 5)

Tie input 5 to the dominant loss. Monday-morning flatness points to Structure; the quiet of no colleagues points to Social contact; "I don't matter now" points to Identity/Purpose.

### Step 5 — Design one recurring anchor

Build one anchor that rebuilds the dominant function, borrowing the job's own mechanisms (schedule, other people, external stakes):

- **Structure:** one recurring commitment at a fixed time that other people expect you at — the point is external pull, not a private intention.
- **Social contact:** one recurring venue with the same faces (a club, a regular volunteering shift, a class, a return as a mentor/part-timer).
- **Identity:** one activity substantial enough to answer "what do you do now" honestly — a craft, a role, a cause you can name with pride.
- **Purpose:** one commitment where your contribution is genuinely needed and missed if absent — not busywork, real stakes.

The strongest anchors serve two functions at once (a scheduled volunteering role rebuilds structure + social + purpose). Prefer those. But commit to ONE.

### Step 6 — The one action this week

Produce the single first step — a call, a sign-up, a scheduled visit — that instantiates the anchor, with an observable check.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Cost all four functions from input 3 evidence.
- Select exactly one dominant loss, evidence-led (not defaulting to Purpose).
- Explain the plan-reality gap in terms of missing structure/accountability/scaffolding.
- Commit one recurring anchor (favor dual-function) plus one first action this week.

### Must Not
- Recommend generic "find a hobby," "stay busy," or "volunteer somewhere" without tying to a costed function and building in recurrence + other people.
- Give financial or estate advice — out of scope.
- Frame retirement as pure liberation and dismiss the loss.
- Assume Purpose is the dominant loss without evidence.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **Don't default to Purpose.** The most common acute loss is Structure or Social contact; the "loss of purpose" narrative is culturally louder than it is often true. Cost from evidence.
2. **Don't prescribe solo, unstructured refills.** "Take up painting" fails for the same reason the retirement plans did — no schedule, no people, no stakes. Anchors must import at least one of those.
3. **Don't confuse the honeymoon with the settled state.** The first weeks can feel great (a long-deferred rest); the aimlessness often arrives at month 2–4. Read the current week, and note if it's still early.
4. **Don't pathologize decompression.** Sleeping in and doing little right after a career is legitimate recovery, not decline — distinguish rest from stall.
5. **Don't miss depression.** Persistent worthlessness, loss of will, or withdrawal is not "reorientation" and is elevated-risk in this transition — route to `domain-psychology/` promptly.
6. **Don't over-scope.** One anchor. Rebuilding identity, structure, contact, and purpose simultaneously overwhelms and fails.
