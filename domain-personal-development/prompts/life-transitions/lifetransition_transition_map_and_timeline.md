# Locate Yourself on the Ending → Neutral-Zone → New-Beginning Map and Name What This Phase Needs

**Objective:** Place the user in one of the three transition phases, name what that phase actually requires (which is different in each), and produce the single phase-appropriate move plus the signal for advancing.

**When to use:** You're in any life transition and feel disoriented, impatient, or unsure whether you're "doing it right." This is the meta-navigator — use it first when you can't tell which stage of a change you're in, or to route to a more specific life-transitions prompt. Useful when the pressure to "just be in the new normal already" is itself the problem. Not for making the decision that starts a transition — that is `major-decisions/`.

**Audience:** An individual navigating their own transition. Not for assessing someone else's, not clinical. If the disorientation is persistent despair, loss of function, or crisis rather than the ordinary limbo of change, this is not a substitute for professional support — see `domain-psychology/` and a licensed professional.

---

## Inputs Required

1. **The transition.** What changed, whether it was chosen or imposed, sudden or gradual, and roughly how long ago the change happened.
2. **What ended.** What specifically is over — a role, a relationship, a place, a phase of life, a version of yourself — and whether you have consciously let it go or are still holding on.
3. **Where the new thing stands.** Whether a new beginning has taken clear shape, is a vague direction, or doesn't exist yet.
4. **Your current state.** 3–5 sentences on how you actually are now: how much you're grieving/holding the old, whether you feel in limbo, whether anything new is pulling you, and how much pressure you feel to "be past this."
5. **The impatience or stuckness.** One sentence naming the specific frustration — "I should be over this," "I don't know who I am in between," "the new thing won't start," or "I keep getting pulled back."

If input 2 (what ended) or input 4 (current state) is missing, refuse and ask. You cannot locate the phase without knowing what's ending and how the person actually is now.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Place the phase

Apply the three-phase model (Bridges). The phase is defined by inner reality, not calendar time since the event. Assign the **dominant** current phase from inputs 2–4:

| Phase | You're here when | What this phase actually needs |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Ending | The old is over but you haven't let go; grief, anger, denial, holding on | To acknowledge and grieve the loss; to actually let go, not rush past it |
| 2. Neutral zone | The old is gone, the new hasn't formed; limbo, disorientation, "who am I in between" | To tolerate the emptiness; small structure and experiments — not premature commitment |
| 3. New beginning | Something new is genuinely pulling; energy returning, a direction forming | To commit to and build the new; consolidate the emerging identity |

A person can straddle two phases; name the primary and note the secondary.

### Step 2 — Correct the timeline expectation

Address the most common distortion directly: transitions are not linear or fast, and the neutral zone (Phase 2) is the longest, most uncomfortable, and most *skipped* phase. Using input 4, state whether the person is where a transition of this age would reasonably be — and explicitly de-pathologize being "still here." Time-since-event is a weak predictor; phase is defined by inner completion.

### Step 3 — Diagnose the impatience/stuckness (input 5)

Map input 5 to a phase-mismatch pattern:

- **Rushing the Ending:** trying to jump to a new beginning while still holding the old → the fix is to actually grieve/let go, not to plan.
- **Fleeing the Neutral zone:** grabbing any new thing to escape the discomfort of limbo → the fix is to tolerate and use the emptiness, not to commit prematurely.
- **Stalling before a New beginning:** the new thing is pulling but fear/comfort keeps you in limbo → the fix is a small commitment to the emerging direction.
- **Getting pulled back:** the ending isn't as complete as assumed → return to Phase 1 work.

### Step 4 — Give the one phase-appropriate move

Produce exactly one move matched to the phase — and note that the right move *feels wrong* to someone fighting the phase:

- **Ending:** one concrete act of letting-go or grieving (a ritual, a conversation, a disposal, naming the loss out loud) — not a forward-planning task.
- **Neutral zone:** one small structuring anchor plus permission for one low-stakes experiment; explicitly *not* a major commitment.
- **New beginning:** one concrete first commitment to the emerging direction, with real (small) stakes.

### Step 5 — Route or set the advance signal

If the transition matches a dedicated prompt (job loss, breakup, relocation, retirement, empty nest, new role, parenthood, leave, role-identity), name it as the deeper follow-up. Then name the observable signal that it's time to move to the next phase, so the person neither rushes nor stalls.

## Constraints

### Must
- Assign exactly one primary phase from inner state, not calendar time.
- Explicitly de-pathologize a long neutral zone and correct linear-timeline expectations.
- Map the impatience/stuckness to a named phase-mismatch pattern.
- Give exactly one phase-appropriate move and name the advance signal.

### Must Not
- Push forward-planning on someone still in the Ending phase.
- Recommend a major commitment to someone in the Neutral zone.
- Imply the transition should be "done" by now.
- Offer a generic "give it time / trust the process" without a concrete move.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **Don't equate time-since-event with phase.** Someone two years out can still be in the Ending phase if they never let go; someone two months out can be in a New beginning. Read inner state, not the clock.
2. **Don't treat the Neutral zone as a problem to escape.** Limbo is the necessary, generative middle — the disorientation is the work, not a failure. Prescribing a quick commitment to end the discomfort is the classic error.
3. **Don't skip the Ending because planning feels better.** Forward motion is the seductive, socially-rewarded move; if the old isn't grieved, the new beginning is built on holding-on and tends to collapse or get "pulled back."
4. **Don't force a New beginning that isn't pulling.** Manufactured enthusiasm for a direction with no real pull is fleeing the neutral zone in disguise.
5. **Don't diagnose depression as neutral-zone limbo.** The neutral zone is uncomfortable but not hopeless; persistent despair, loss of function, or self-harm thoughts are clinical and route to `domain-psychology/` — the phase model does not cover them.
6. **Don't over-route.** Offer the dedicated follow-up prompt as an option, but still deliver this prompt's own phase move — don't punt the whole answer to another file.

## Output Format

```
## Phase
Primary: [1 Ending / 2 Neutral zone / 3 New beginning] — because [inputs 2–4 evidence]. Secondary (if straddling): [phase].

## Timeline reality check
This transition is ~[age]. For inner state [X], being here is [expected / a sign of Y]. The neutral zone is the longest phase — being "still here" is not failure.

## Impatience/stuckness (input 5)
Pattern: [Rushing the Ending / Fleeing the Neutral zone / Stalling before New beginning / Getting pulled back].
What it means: [...].

## The one phase-appropriate move
[Single move matched to the phase — and it may feel counterintuitive.]

## Route + advance signal
Deeper follow-up (if applicable): [dedicated life-transitions prompt].
Advance to phase [next] when: [observable signal].

Predicted check: after the move, [observable — a loss grieved, an experiment run, a first commitment made].
```

## Verification

- [ ] Exactly one primary phase, assigned from inner state not calendar time.
- [ ] Long neutral zone de-pathologized; linear-timeline expectation corrected.
- [ ] Impatience/stuckness mapped to a named phase-mismatch pattern.
- [ ] One phase-appropriate move — not forward-planning if in Ending, not a major commitment if in Neutral zone.
- [ ] Advance signal named.
- [ ] Dedicated follow-up prompt offered where applicable, but this prompt still delivers its own move.
- [ ] No "should be done by now," no generic "trust the process."
- [ ] Persistent despair / loss of function routed to professional support.
