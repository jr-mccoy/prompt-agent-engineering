# Protect One Thread of Pre-Parent Self Without Guilt in New Parenthood

**Objective:** Name what parenthood actually changed, separate permanent loss from temporary compression, and protect one specific thread of pre-parent self — with a concrete time and partnership renegotiation to make it real.

**When to use:** You have a new child (newborn through toddler, or a newly blended/adopted family) and feel your former self slipping — hobbies, friendships, work identity, spontaneity. Useful when guilt about wanting any self-time is itself part of the problem. Not for deciding whether to have children — that is `major-decisions/personal_family_planning_tradeoffs.md`.

**Audience:** An individual reconciling their own identity as a new parent. Not for assessing a co-parent, not clinical. Postpartum depression and anxiety are common, real, and treatable — persistent hopelessness, intrusive thoughts, inability to bond, or panic are not "adjustment" and are not addressed here; see `domain-psychology/`, and consult a licensed professional or physician promptly.

---

## Inputs Required

1. **Family facts.** Child's age, how they joined the family (birth, adoption, blending), whether you're partnered or solo, and your paid-work status now (full-time, leave, reduced, not working).
2. **Pre-parent self — the threads.** 5–8 things that made you *you* before: activities, relationships, roles, ways you spent free time, parts of your identity (e.g., "I ran," "I was the friend who hosted," "I read deeply," "I was good at my job").
3. **A real week, now.** How the last 7 days of your 24-hour days actually went, including night wakings, in rough buckets — childcare, paid work, sleep, self, partner, everything else.
4. **The guilt.** One sentence naming the specific thing you feel guilty for wanting (time alone, an evening out, focus at work, missing the baby less than expected).
5. **The partnership split (if partnered).** Rough division of childcare and domestic load between you and a co-parent, and whether it's been explicitly discussed or just defaulted into.

If input 2 (the threads) or input 3 (the real week) is missing, refuse and ask. You cannot protect a thread you haven't named against a week you haven't measured.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Classify each pre-parent thread

For each thread in input 2, assign one status using the fixed taxonomy:

| Status | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Compressed | Temporarily squeezed by the newborn phase; returns as the child grows | Long uninterrupted reading |
| Transformed | Continues in a changed form | Solo travel → local day trips |
| Genuinely paused | Truly on hold for a defined season, by choice | A demanding side venture |
| Actually lost | Structurally incompatible with this life and worth grieving | A spontaneous nightlife identity |

Base each call on input 3 (the real week) and the child's age — the newborn phase is unusually compressed and shouldn't be read as permanent.

### Step 2 — Separate the phase from the permanent

State plainly which losses are **phase-driven** (will ease in months as sleep and autonomy return) versus **structural** (this is simply the shape of the life now). Over-attributing to "forever" is the core distortion of the exhausted early phase.

### Step 3 — Read the guilt (input 4)

Name what the guilt is actually protecting against. New-parent guilt is usually the belief that any self is a subtraction from the child. State in one sentence why the specific wanted thing is compatible with being a good parent — grounded, not a slogan. A depleted parent has less to give; this is a resource argument, not a permission slip.

### Step 4 — Choose the one thread to protect

From the Compressed and Transformed threads, pick the single one whose loss most erodes your sense of self — the identity-load-bearing one, not the easiest to schedule. Name it and why it carries the most weight.

### Step 5 — Make it real in time and partnership

Convert the protected thread into a concrete, recurring time block sized to the newborn reality (even 90 minutes weekly counts). Then:

- **If partnered:** name the specific coverage this requires from the co-parent and script the ask as an explicit trade, referencing input 5's current split. If the split is lopsided, the ask includes rebalancing, not just a one-off favor.
- **If solo:** name the specific external coverage (a swap, paid help, family) that makes the block possible, since it can't come from nowhere.

### Step 6 — One decisive move this week

Produce one action: the scheduled block plus the one conversation or arrangement that protects it, with an observable check.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Classify every thread into exactly one of the four statuses, grounded in the real week and child's age.
- Distinguish phase-driven from structural loss explicitly.
- Protect exactly one thread — the identity-load-bearing one.
- Convert it into a recurring time block plus a specific partnership or coverage arrangement.

### Must Not
- Tell the user to "cherish every moment" or that "it goes so fast" — that is the guilt engine, not help.
- Recommend generic "self-care" (bubble baths, spa days) unmoored from the named thread.
- Assume an equal partnership split or ignore a lopsided one when it's in the input.
- Treat wanting self-time as a flaw to be managed away.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **Don't read newborn compression as permanent loss.** The 0–12 month phase is uniquely constrained; most Compressed threads return. Mislabeling them "Actually lost" manufactures despair.
2. **Don't confuse depletion with lost identity.** Sometimes the self hasn't gone anywhere — the parent is just profoundly sleep-deprived. Check input 3 for sleep before diagnosing identity erosion.
3. **Don't route around a lopsided partnership.** If input 5 shows an unequal load, the answer is renegotiation, not the depleted parent optimizing their remaining scraps.
4. **Don't pathologize normal ambivalence.** Missing your old life, or not feeling instant bliss, is common and not a disorder — but persistent hopelessness, intrusive thoughts, or inability to bond ARE clinical; route them to `domain-psychology/` and a physician, don't normalize them here.
5. **Don't over-schedule.** One protected thread, one block. A parent of a newborn cannot reclaim five identities at once; trying guarantees failure and more guilt.
6. **Don't moralize about the guilt.** Neither indulge it nor lecture it away. State the resource logic and move to the block.

## Verification

- [ ] Every thread classified into exactly one of the four statuses.
- [ ] Phase-driven vs. structural loss stated explicitly, using child's age.
- [ ] Sleep checked before diagnosing identity erosion.
- [ ] Exactly one thread protected, with a stated reason it's load-bearing.
- [ ] Time block plus a specific partnership/coverage arrangement, not a wish.
- [ ] Lopsided partnership triggers a rebalancing ask, not scrap-optimization.
- [ ] No "cherish every moment," no generic self-care, no guilt-lecturing.
- [ ] Clinical postpartum signs routed to `domain-psychology/` and a physician.
