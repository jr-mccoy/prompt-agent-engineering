# Rebuild Routine, Belonging, and Place-Attachment After a Move You Already Made

**Objective:** Identify which layer of post-move settling has stalled and commit to one concrete re-rooting action that most unblocks belonging in the new place.

**When to use:** You have already relocated — the boxes are unpacked or nearly — and something still feels unsettled, temporary, or lonely weeks or months later. Useful when you can't tell whether you're "just adjusting" or genuinely stuck. Not for deciding whether or where to move — that is `major-decisions/personal_relocation_decision.md`.

**Audience:** An individual re-rooting their own life after a move. Not for assessing a relocating partner or child, not clinical. If the unsettledness is persistent low mood, hopelessness, or panic rather than ordinary transition friction, this is not a substitute for professional support — see `domain-psychology/` and a licensed professional.

---

## Inputs Required

1. **Move facts.** From where, to where, how long ago, why (job, relationship, family, cost, chosen vs. forced), and whether you moved alone or with others.
2. **What you lost in the move.** 4–8 specific things the old place gave you that the new one hasn't replaced — a running route, a friend group, a coffee shop, proximity to family, a role in a community, a climate, a commute you liked.
3. **Current weekly rhythm.** How a typical week now actually runs: work, meals, exercise, weekends, social contact. Note which of these are placeholders ("I just work and scroll") vs. real.
4. **Social contact — last 30 days.** Real interactions in the new place beyond transactional ones: names or roles, and how each came about.
5. **The specific unsettled feeling.** One sentence: when does "this isn't home yet" hit hardest — a time of day, a place, a situation.

If input 2 (what was lost) or input 4 (social contact) is missing, refuse and ask. Place-attachment is rebuilt against what was lost, and belonging is measured in actual contact.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Locate the stalled layer

Settling happens in four layers, bottom-up. Diagnose the **lowest** layer that is still incomplete — that's the blocker, because upper layers can't stabilize until lower ones do.

| Layer | Settled looks like | Stalled looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Logistics | Home, admin, transport, essentials work without daily friction | Still living out of boxes / can't find things / unregistered |
| 2. Routine | Repeatable weekly rhythm for sleep, food, movement, work | Every week improvised; placeholder habits (scroll, delivery) |
| 3. Social fabric | ≥2–3 non-transactional recurring contacts | Only work/transactional contact; no one to call |
| 4. Place-attachment | Specific spots feel like "yours"; the map is emotional, not just functional | The place stays a backdrop; nowhere feels claimed |

### Step 2 — Confirm with evidence

Support the layer diagnosis with inputs 3 and 4. If routine is full of placeholders → Layer 2. If contact is all transactional → Layer 3. If routine and contact are fine but it still doesn't feel like home → Layer 4. Don't jump to the emotional layer if a lower one is objectively broken.

### Step 3 — Map losses to replaceable vs. not

Sort input 2 into: **replaceable in the new place** (a running route, a gym, a hobby group), **replaceable differently** (family proximity → scheduled calls + fewer, longer visits), and **not replaceable, to be grieved** (a specific person, a life stage). Naming the third bucket honestly matters — trying to "replace" the unreplaceable keeps people stuck.

### Step 4 — Read the unsettled moment (input 5)

Tie input 5 to the stalled layer. The moment it hits hardest usually points straight at the missing layer (evenings alone → social fabric; not knowing where to go on a free Saturday → place-attachment).

### Step 5 — Commit one re-rooting action

Pick the single action that most advances the stalled layer from Step 1. It must be:

- **Physical and recurring where possible** — a place to go on a schedule, a recurring event, a claimed spot, a person to contact by name.
- Sized to the layer: logistics = a one-time fix; routine = one anchor habit pinned to a day/time; social = one recurring venue where the same faces reappear (repeat exposure, not one-off events); place = deliberately adopting one "third place."

One action. Not a settling-in checklist.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Diagnose the lowest incomplete layer and support it with inputs 3–4.
- Sort every loss in input 2 into replaceable / replaceable-differently / to-be-grieved.
- Choose exactly one re-rooting action, sized to the stalled layer.
- For social-fabric actions, favor recurring same-people venues over one-off events.

### Must Not
- Recommend a generic "get involved in the community" or "give it time" platitude.
- Skip lower broken layers to work on the emotional one.
- Promise the new place will come to feel like home by a date.
- Pathologize ordinary relocation grief; homesickness is expected, not a disorder.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **Don't misread logistics friction as loneliness.** If Layer 1 is broken (can't find the DMV, no doctor yet), daily friction masquerades as sadness. Fix logistics first.
2. **Don't treat one-off events as social fabric.** Attending a meetup once builds nothing. Belonging needs the same faces on repeat; the action must create recurrence.
3. **Don't try to replace the unreplaceable.** A specific lost friend or a parent's proximity may belong in the grieve bucket, not the replace bucket. Forcing replacement prolongs the stall.
4. **Don't mistake a chosen move's regret for stuckness.** If the move was forced, some grief is about loss of agency, not the place — name it, don't try to route-around it with activity.
5. **Don't diagnose depression.** Persistent hopelessness, anhedonia, or not-getting-out-of-bed is not "unsettled" — route to `domain-psychology/`.
6. **Don't over-index on the new place's flaws.** Cataloguing what's worse than the old place is a form of not-arriving; observe it, then act on a layer.

## Output Format

```
## Stalled layer
Lowest incomplete layer: [1 Logistics / 2 Routine / 3 Social / 4 Place].
Evidence: [from inputs 3–4].

## Losses, sorted
- Replaceable here: [items → what replaces them]
- Replaceable differently: [items → the different form]
- To be grieved (not replaced): [items]

## The unsettled moment (input 5)
Hits hardest at [moment] → points to [layer].

## One re-rooting action
[Specific physical/recurring action], starting [when], at [where/with whom].
Sized to: [layer].

Predicted check: within [n] weeks, [observable — a recurring contact, a claimed spot, a stable weekly anchor].
```

## Verification

- [ ] Lowest incomplete layer diagnosed, not the emotional one by default.
- [ ] Diagnosis supported by input 3 (routine) and input 4 (contact).
- [ ] Every loss sorted into one of the three buckets, including honest "to be grieved."
- [ ] Exactly one re-rooting action, sized to the stalled layer.
- [ ] Social actions create recurrence, not one-off attendance.
- [ ] No "give it time," no "get involved," no dated promise of belonging.
- [ ] Persistent low mood/hopelessness routed to professional support.
