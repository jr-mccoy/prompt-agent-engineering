# Run a Staged Recovery Arc After a Layoff or Firing — Separating the Event From Self-Worth

**Objective:** Determine which of four recovery phases the user is actually in and produce the one phase-appropriate move — while explicitly separating the job-loss event from their worth.

**When to use:** You were laid off, fired, made redundant, or non-renewed, and you're deciding what to do now — this week, not eventually. Useful when you feel pressure to "just start applying" but sense you're not ready, or when you've been stuck ruminating for weeks. Not for deciding whether to quit a job you still have — that is `major-decisions/personal_quit_or_persist.md`.

**Audience:** An individual recovering from their own job loss. Not for advising someone else through theirs, not clinical. Job loss is a top-tier life stressor and can trigger real depression or crisis — if you have persistent hopelessness, can't function for weeks, or any thoughts of self-harm, this is not a substitute for professional support; contact `domain-psychology/`, a licensed professional, or a crisis line immediately.

---

## Inputs Required

1. **The event.** What happened (layoff / performance firing / restructure / contract end), when, how much warning, and how it was handled by the employer.
2. **Practical situation.** Runway (months of expenses covered), dependents, health insurance status, and any hard deadline (visa tied to employment, benefits cliff). This gates urgency and must be honest.
3. **Where your head is now.** 3–5 sentences on how you're actually doing: sleeping, eating, the story you're telling yourself, and how much of your day the job loss occupies.
4. **The self-worth hit.** One sentence on what the loss made you believe about yourself (e.g., "I'm not good enough," "I'm behind everyone," "I'll never recover").
5. **What you've done since.** Concrete actions in the days/weeks since — applied, frozen, updated the résumé, told people, avoided telling people, doomscrolled.

If input 2 (runway/deadlines) is missing, refuse and ask. Recovery pacing is impossible to calibrate without knowing real financial pressure — advice differs sharply between 8 months of runway and 3 weeks.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Locate the recovery phase

Assign the **current** phase using the fixed arc. Phases are sequential but overlap; pick the one where the real work now sits, using inputs 3 and 5.

| Phase | You're here when | The work of this phase |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Stabilize | The first days/weeks; shock, disrupted sleep/eating, financial panic | Secure runway basics, protect sleep, tell 1–2 people, stop the freefall |
| 2. Process | Stabilized but grieving, angry, ashamed; replaying it | Feel it, name it, separate event from self-worth — before hunting |
| 3. Reframe | The rawness eases; ready to make meaning | Build an honest, non-defensive narrative of what happened and what's next |
| 4. Re-enter | Narrative is coherent; genuinely ready to search | Structured, sustainable job search with a story you can tell |

### Step 2 — Check for phase-skipping and phase-stalling

Two failure modes, both common:

- **Skipping** (usually Stabilize/Process → Re-enter): frantically applying while still in shock produces desperate applications and interviews that leak grievance. Flag if input 5 shows heavy applying while input 3 shows unprocessed distress. Runway pressure (input 2) can *legitimately* compress phases 2–3 — but even then, some processing is non-negotiable.
- **Stalling** (stuck in Process): weeks of rumination with no movement, past the point where feeling it helps. Flag if input 5 shows near-zero action over a long window and input 3 shows the loss occupying the whole day.

### Step 3 — Separate the event from self-worth (input 4)

Address input 4 directly with `RT-09` root-cause reasoning: attribute the job loss to its actual causes (macro layoffs, a role mismatch, a business decision, a specific fixable behavior, or a genuine performance gap). State plainly which parts were structural/outside your control and which, if any, are yours to own and change. The goal is accurate attribution — neither "it was all them" denial nor "I'm worthless" collapse.

### Step 4 — Produce the one phase-appropriate move

Give exactly one move matched to the Step 1 phase:

- **Stabilize:** one concrete stabilizing action (file for benefits, a 3-line budget, one sleep-protecting change, one person to tell).
- **Process:** one action that lets the grief move (write the unsent account, one honest conversation, a fixed daily worry-window) — explicitly *not* a job-search task.
- **Reframe:** draft the two-sentence honest narrative ("Here's what happened, here's what I'm looking for") that you can say without flinching or blaming.
- **Re-enter:** one structured search action with a sustainable cadence (a target list, a fixed daily application block, one reconnection), not a scattershot 40-application day.

### Step 5 — Set the transition-out check

Name the observable signal that it's time to move to the next phase, so recovery doesn't stall in place.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Gate all pacing on input 2 (runway/deadlines) — honest urgency, not generic optimism.
- Assign exactly one current phase and one phase-appropriate move.
- Attribute the event accurately in Step 3 — structural vs. ownable, using RT-09.
- Name the observable signal for advancing to the next phase.

### Must Not
- Push a job search on someone still in Stabilize/Process (unless runway forces it, and then still protect minimal processing).
- Offer résumé/LinkedIn tactics unless the user is in Re-enter.
- Reassure with "everything happens for a reason" or "you'll land somewhere better."
- Collapse the person's worth into the firing, or absolve them of a genuine, fixable cause.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **Don't rush to Re-enter because it feels productive.** Applying is the visible, socially-approved move, so people flee to it. If distress is unprocessed, the applications and interviews carry the wound. Match the phase, not the optics.
2. **Don't over-process a stabilized person.** If someone is functionally fine and financially pressured, endless "sit with your feelings" is its own stall. Move them.
3. **Don't mis-attribute a structural layoff as personal failure.** A company-wide RIF is not a referendum on the individual; forcing an "own it" narrative is inaccurate and harmful.
4. **Don't whitewash a genuine performance firing.** If input 1/5 shows a real, repeated, ownable pattern, denial-mode reframing sets up a repeat. Name the fixable cause without collapsing worth.
5. **Don't ignore the runway cliff.** Three weeks of runway changes everything; do not prescribe a leisurely processing phase to someone facing an imminent benefits or visa deadline — stabilize the practical emergency first.
6. **Don't miss crisis.** Persistent hopelessness, weeks of non-function, or any self-harm thought is clinical — route to `domain-psychology/` / a crisis line immediately, not to a recovery-phase move.

```
## Practical gate
Runway: [n months]. Dependents/insurance: [...]. Hard deadline: [none / date].
Urgency level: [Low / Medium / Emergency].

## Current phase
Phase [1–4: name] — because [inputs 3 & 5 evidence].
Skipping/stalling flag: [none / skipping into Re-enter / stalled in Process] — [evidence].

## Event vs. self-worth (input 4)
Belief stated: [...].
Accurate attribution: structural/outside control — [...]; ownable & fixable — [... or "none identified"].

## The one move (this week)
[Single phase-appropriate action]. (Not a job-search task if phase ≤ 2, unless runway forces it.)

## Advance signal
Move to phase [next] when [observable signal].

Predicted check: after the move, [observable — a filed claim, a hard conversation had, a two-sentence narrative you can say aloud, a first structured application].
```
