# Limiting Belief Reframer

**Objective:** Take a specific limiting belief and transform it into an empowering, evidence-based alternative — not through "positive thinking" platitudes, but through empirical counter-evidence, contextual analysis, and a concrete micro-experiment you can run within 48 hours to start building the new belief through action.

**When to Use:** Use this prompt when you catch yourself thinking "I can't," "I'm not good enough," "It's too late," "People like me don't," or any other belief that's stopping you from taking action. Works best when you can articulate the belief in a single sentence.

**Important context:** Not every negative thought is a "limiting belief." Some concerns are legitimate risk assessments. This prompt helps you distinguish between beliefs that protect you from real danger and beliefs that protect you from discomfort at the cost of growth. The goal isn't blind optimism — it's accurate thinking.

---

## Inputs / Context

1. **The Belief:**
   - "State your limiting belief in one sentence."
   - "How long have you held this belief?"
   - "Where did it come from? (childhood, a specific failure, cultural messaging, comparison)"

2. **The Impact:**
   - "What does this belief stop you from doing?"
   - "What would you attempt if you didn't hold this belief?"
   - "How does this belief show up in your daily behavior?"

3. **The Evidence:**
   - "What evidence supports this belief? (Be honest — what makes it feel true?)"
   - "What evidence contradicts it? (Times you succeeded despite the belief)"
   - "Do people you respect hold this same belief about themselves?"

**Require a single, stated belief before reframing — and screen it first.** This prompt operates on one belief the user can articulate in a sentence. If they offer only a vague feeling ("I'm just not a confident person," "everything is hard"), ask them to name the specific belief and one situation where it stops them from acting. Do NOT reframe a belief the user hasn't actually stated. Critically: if the "belief" describes a real structural barrier, a protective risk assessment, or a possible clinical concern (persistent hopelessness, self-harm thoughts), do not reframe it — name it for what it is and, for clinical signals, refer to professional support rather than treating it as a thinking error.

## Instructions

### Phase 1: Belief Analysis

1. **Restate the belief** exactly as provided — don't soften it.
2. **Classify the belief type:**

| Type | Description | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Identity** | "I am/am not..." | "I'm not a leader" |
| **Capability** | "I can't/don't know how..." | "I can't learn to code at 40" |
| **Permission** | "I shouldn't/it's not okay to..." | "I shouldn't ask for more money" |
| **Worthiness** | "I don't deserve..." | "I don't deserve a promotion yet" |
| **Timing** | "It's too late/early..." | "It's too late to change careers" |
| **Fixed** | "That's just how things are..." | "The industry doesn't hire self-taught people" |

3. **Assess legitimacy:** Is there a kernel of truth? What part is accurate risk assessment vs. what part is fear-based generalization?

### Phase 2: Counter-Evidence Research

For the limiting belief, provide:

1. **Direct counter-examples** — 2-3 specific, verifiable examples of people who succeeded despite the same belief being "true" for them
2. **Contextual factors** — what conditions make this belief more or less accurate? (It may be true in some contexts but not yours)
3. **The hidden assumption** — what unstated assumption makes this belief feel like a fact? (e.g., "I can't learn to code at 40" assumes learning ability declines sharply, which research contradicts)
4. **Scale of evidence** — how much real evidence supports vs. contradicts this belief?

### Phase 3: Reframe Construction

Build the alternative belief:

1. **The reframed belief** — not a vague positive affirmation, but a specific, evidence-based alternative
   - Bad reframe: "I can do anything I set my mind to!"
   - Good reframe: "People with my background have successfully transitioned to [X] by doing [Y]. I can test whether this path works for me."

2. **Supporting evidence** — 2-3 pieces of evidence that make the reframe credible
3. **The honest caveat** — what's genuinely uncertain or difficult? (Acknowledging difficulty makes the reframe more believable)

### Phase 4: Micro-Experiment Design

Design one concrete experiment to test the new belief:

- **Action:** What specifically will you do? (Must be completable in 48 hours)
- **Success indicator:** What would confirm the reframe has merit?
- **Failure indicator:** What would suggest the original belief has more truth than expected?
- **Either way:** What will you learn regardless of the outcome?

### Phase 5: Integration

- **Daily reminder:** One sentence you can tell yourself when the old belief surfaces
- **Evidence tracker:** A simple way to collect evidence for the new belief over time
- **Escalation plan:** If the micro-experiment succeeds, what's the next slightly bigger experiment?

---

### False-Positive Prevention

- ❌ Do NOT reframe legitimate concerns as "limiting beliefs" — fear of heights when rock climbing is protective
- ❌ Do NOT use toxic positivity — "Just believe in yourself!" is not a reframe
- ❌ Do NOT dismiss the original belief entirely — acknowledge what made it feel true
- ❌ Do NOT promise outcomes — "This reframe will change your life" is dishonest
- ❌ Do NOT ignore structural barriers — "Anyone can be a CEO" ignores real systemic factors
- ✅ DO distinguish between protective beliefs (legitimate risk) and limiting beliefs (unjustified fear)
- ✅ DO use specific, verifiable counter-evidence — not generic inspiration
- ✅ DO acknowledge difficulty while maintaining possibility
- ✅ DO make the micro-experiment low-stakes enough that failure is informative, not devastating
- ✅ DO respect the user's timeline and constraints

- Do this: [Specific action]
- Measure: [What to observe]
- If it works: [Next step]

- When the old belief surfaces, remind yourself: "[One sentence]"
- Track evidence for the new belief: [Simple method]

## Verification

Before delivering the reframe, confirm:

- [ ] The original belief is restated exactly as given, not softened.
- [ ] The belief is classified by type and assessed for a kernel of truth (legitimate concern vs. fear-based generalization).
- [ ] Counter-evidence is specific and verifiable, not generic inspiration or platitudes.
- [ ] The reframe is an evidence-based alternative, not a positive affirmation ("I can do anything").
- [ ] The reframe includes an honest caveat naming what is genuinely hard or uncertain.
- [ ] The micro-experiment is completable in 48 hours and low-stakes enough that failure is informative, not devastating.
- [ ] The experiment defines both a success indicator AND a failure indicator (so the original belief can win).
- [ ] No structural barrier was waved away, and any clinical-distress signal was referred out rather than reframed.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-01** (Clear Objective Statement) — Single belief focus with specific outcome
- **ST-02** (Structured Sequential Instructions) — Analysis, evidence, reframe, experiment
- **RT-02** (Multi-Dimensional Analysis) — Belief type classification and contextual assessment
- **CM-01** (Explicit Context Framing) — Origin, impact, and evidence gathered before reframing
- **RT-04** (Analogical Reasoning) — Uses concrete counter-examples and analogous cases of people who succeeded despite the same belief
- **QA-04** (Uncertainty Acknowledgment) — Honest caveats and falsifiable experiments
