# Separate Guilt (I Did a Bad Thing) From Shame (I Am Bad) and Route Each Correctly

**Objective:** Take a heavy self-directed feeling after something you did (or think you did) and sort it into guilt — about an action, which points to repair — versus shame — a global verdict on your worth, which is a distortion to disarm — then route each to its correct response.

**When to use:** You feel bad about something and it's sitting heavy; you can't tell whether you owe someone an apology or whether you're just spiraling on being a bad person; the feeling is either driving useful repair or corroding your sense of self and you want to know which. Not for a live safety situation and not for processing abuse you experienced — those route to professional support below.

**Audience:** An individual doing this for themselves. Not clinical. If shame is chronic, attached to your whole identity, tied to trauma or abuse, or accompanied by hopelessness or self-harm thoughts, this skill practice is not a substitute for professional support — see `domain-psychology/client-self-use/` and a licensed professional; for thoughts of harming yourself, contact a crisis line or emergency services now.

---

## Inputs Required

1. **What happened.** The specific thing you did or failed to do, factual, one or two sentences.
2. **The feeling, verbatim.** What the inner voice is actually saying ("I hurt them," "I'm a terrible friend," "I'm disgusting," "I should have known better").
3. **Who was affected.** Whether a specific other person was harmed, and how — or whether it's only about you and your self-image.
4. **The standard you're measuring against.** The value or rule you feel you broke (kept a promise, was honest, was reliable) — and whether it's genuinely yours or one imposed on you.

If input 1 is vague ("I'm just a bad person"), there's no *action* to anchor on — that alone is a strong signal you're in shame, not guilt. Note it and proceed to sort.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Test action vs. self
Apply the core distinction to input 2. For each statement in the feeling, mark it:

| Marker | Statement targets | This is |
|---|---|---|
| Action | a specific behavior ("I lied to her") | guilt |
| Self | your whole worth or identity ("I'm a liar / worthless / broken") | shame |

Most heavy feelings are a mix. Separate the guilt sentences from the shame sentences; don't let them travel together.

### Step 2 — Check the guilt for accuracy and proportion
For the guilt (action) part: is the action actually a violation of a standard that's genuinely yours (input 4), and is the feeling proportionate to the real impact (input 3)? Mark it *warranted* (real action, real impact, your standard) or *inflated* (small or no actual harm, or someone else's standard you've internalized). Warranted guilt is useful signal.

### Step 3 — Route the guilt to repair
For *warranted* guilt, the correct response is repair, not rumination. Define the one concrete repair move: a specific apology, a correction, a changed behavior going forward — sized to the actual harm. Once repair is defined (and done), warranted guilt has served its function and can be released. For *inflated* guilt, the move is to right-size the standard, not to apologize for something that wasn't a real breach.

### Step 4 — Disarm the shame
For the shame (self) part: name it as what it is — a global verdict, which is a category error. One action does not equal a whole self. Do not try to "repair" shame with an apology (you can't apologize for existing) and do not argue it with affirmations. Instead: convert the global verdict back into the specific action it distorted ("I'm a terrible friend" → "I forgot my friend's birthday"), which returns it to the guilt track where it can actually be addressed.

### Step 5 — Produce the two routed moves
State the split cleanly and give at most two moves: (1) the repair for warranted guilt (or the right-sizing for inflated guilt), and (2) the shame reframe — the global verdict converted back to a specific action. If there's no warranted guilt at all, say so: the whole feeling was shame, and the move is the reframe alone.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Separate action-statements (guilt) from self-statements (shame) explicitly.
- Judge guilt for warrant and proportion against the user's own standard.
- Route warranted guilt to one concrete repair sized to real harm.
- Disarm shame by converting the global verdict back to a specific action — never by affirmation.

### Must Not
- Treat shame as guilt and prescribe an apology for existing.
- Treat guilt as shame and dismiss a real breach as "just being hard on yourself."
- Moralize or lecture about what a good person would do.
- Offer affirmations ("you're a good person") as the shame response.
- Produce more than the two routed moves.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **Don't call warranted guilt "shame" to make it feel better.** If there's a real breach of your own standard with real impact, that's guilt and it points to repair — dismissing it as distortion skips the amends.
2. **Don't call shame "guilt" and manufacture an apology.** You cannot repair a global self-verdict with an action; converting it to a specific behavior is the move, not an apology for who you are.
3. **Don't accept an imposed standard as your own.** Guilt measured against a rule that isn't genuinely yours (a parent's, a culture's) is often inflated — check input 4.
4. **Don't over-apologize.** Repair sized to the *actual* harm; excessive apology is often shame leaking into the guilt track.
5. **Don't argue shame with logic or praise.** Affirmations and debate feed it; reconnecting it to the specific action defuses it.
6. **Don't sort trauma-linked or chronic shame here.** Shame attached to abuse, to your whole identity, or present most of the time needs professional support, not a one-event sort.

## Output Format

```
## What happened
[input 1].

## The feeling, split
| Statement | Target | Guilt or shame? |
|---|---|---|
| ... | Action / Self | ... |

## The guilt track
Warranted or inflated: [verdict + why, against your own standard and real impact].
→ Repair (if warranted): [one concrete, harm-sized move]. Then release.
→ Right-size (if inflated): [the standard, corrected].

## The shame track
Global verdict: "[self-statement]" → specific action: "[the behavior it distorted]."
This is a category error: one action ≠ your whole self. No apology for existing; no affirmation.

## The two moves
1. [Repair or right-size.]
2. [Shame reframe: verdict → action.]

Predicted check: the guilt lifts once the repair is done; the shame quiets as the global verdict is repeatedly returned to the specific act. If shame stays global and constant, that's a signal to seek support.
```

## Verification

- [ ] Feeling split into action-statements (guilt) and self-statements (shame).
- [ ] Guilt judged for warrant and proportion against the user's own standard.
- [ ] Warranted guilt routed to one harm-sized repair; inflated guilt right-sized.
- [ ] Shame disarmed by converting the global verdict to a specific action — no affirmations.
- [ ] At most two routed moves; no apology-for-existing, no dismissal of real breach.
- [ ] No moralizing or good-person lecturing.
- [ ] Chronic/trauma-linked shame and any self-harm thoughts routed to professional/crisis support.
