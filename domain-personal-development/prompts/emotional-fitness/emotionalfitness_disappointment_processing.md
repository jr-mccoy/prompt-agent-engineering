# Process a Specific Disappointment by Separating the Gap, the Story, and the Adjustment

**Objective:** Take one specific disappointment and separate three things that usually blur together — the measurable gap between what you expected and what happened, the story you've added on top, and the one adjustment worth making — ending with that single adjustment.

**When to use:** A launch flopped, an offer fell through, someone let you down, a result came in under what you'd pictured, and you keep re-running it. Use it when the letdown is specific and recent. Not for a chronic sense of life being disappointing — that's broader than one event and belongs in identity or, if heavy and persistent, professional support.

**Audience:** An individual doing this for themselves. Not for managing someone else's letdown. Not clinical. If disappointment has become a persistent low mood, loss of interest, or hopelessness lasting weeks, this is not a substitute for professional support — see `domain-psychology/client-self-use/` and a licensed professional.

---

## Inputs Required

1. **The disappointment, in one factual sentence.** What actually happened, stated as a neutral fact (not "everything went wrong" — "the client chose another vendor").
2. **What you expected.** The specific outcome you were holding, ideally with a number, date, or concrete picture ("I expected at least 50 signups," "I expected them to say yes").
3. **What actually happened.** The specific actual outcome, same units as input 2.
4. **What you've been telling yourself about it.** The self-talk since — verbatim if you can ("this always happens to me," "I'm not cut out for this").
5. **How much of the outcome was in your control.** Your honest rough estimate: mostly you, mostly others/luck, or mixed.

If you can't state input 2 as a specific expectation, name that — a vague expectation is often the real source of the letdown.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Measure the gap
Put expected (input 2) next to actual (input 3). State the gap in plain terms and, where possible, a magnitude: "expected 50, got 12 — a 38-signup gap." This is the factual disappointment. Everything past this is interpretation.

### Step 2 — Extract the story
From input 4, pull out the sentences that go *beyond* the gap. Classify each added statement using this fixed set:

| Story type | What it does |
|---|---|
| Overgeneralization | turns one event into "always / never / everything" |
| Self-verdict | turns an outcome into a statement about your worth or ability |
| Catastrophe | extends the loss into ruined future outcomes |
| Mind-reading | assigns motives/judgments to others without evidence |
| Fair read | an accurate lesson supported by the facts |

Only "fair read" statements survive. Name each of the others as a story, not a fact.

### Step 3 — Locate control honestly
Using input 5, split the gap into the part that was within your influence and the part that wasn't. Be specific: which decisions were yours, which were the other party's, the market's, timing, luck. Don't over-own (taking blame for others' choices) or under-own (blaming everything external).

### Step 4 — Recalibrate the expectation
Ask whether input 2 was a reasonable expectation given what you knew at the time. Mark it: *reasonable* (good bet that didn't land), *inflated* (you'd have advised a friend to expect less), or *unclear* (never actually defined). This is not self-blame — it's whether the expectation itself needs adjusting for next time.

### Step 5 — One adjustment
Pick the single most useful adjustment from exactly one of two places:
- **The controllable part** (Step 3): one specific thing to do differently next time.
- **The expectation** (Step 4): recalibrate what you'll expect going in.

Not both, not a five-point plan. If the honest read is that the gap was almost entirely outside your control and the expectation was reasonable, the adjustment is to let it be a loss and name what you'll do to move on this week.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- State the gap as a factual comparison before any interpretation.
- Classify each added-story statement against the fixed taxonomy.
- Split the outcome into controllable vs. not, specifically.
- Produce exactly one adjustment.

### Must Not
- Let a story statement pass as a fact.
- Assign blame — to self or others — beyond what input 5 supports.
- Offer reassurance ("it'll all work out") in place of a real adjustment.
- Turn one event into a verdict on the person.
- Recommend a multi-week processing plan.

### Must Not (anti-moralizing)
- Congratulate the user for "handling it maturely" or shame them for being upset. Disappointment is a normal response to a real gap.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **Don't confuse a big feeling with a big gap.** A small measurable gap can carry a large feeling because of the story. Measure the gap first, separately.
2. **Don't treat "fair read" as the default.** Most disappointment self-talk contains overgeneralization or self-verdict — require evidence before calling a statement fair.
3. **Don't over-own the outcome.** If the decision was someone else's, taking the blame is a story (self-verdict), not accountability.
4. **Don't under-own it either.** If a controllable choice contributed, "it was all bad luck" is also a story.
5. **Don't recalibrate a reasonable expectation into "expect nothing."** Protective pessimism isn't calibration.
6. **Don't pathologize sadness after a real loss.** A letdown after a genuine loss is not a disorder; it only routes to support if it's persistent, intense, or safety-relevant.

## Output Format

```
## The gap (facts only)
Expected: [input 2]. Actual: [input 3]. Gap: [magnitude / plain statement].

## The story on top
| Statement (from your self-talk) | Type | Fact or story? |
|---|---|---|
| ... | Overgeneralization / Self-verdict / Catastrophe / Mind-reading / Fair read | ... |

## What was and wasn't in your control
- In your control: [specific]
- Not in your control: [specific]

## The expectation, recalibrated
[reasonable / inflated / unclear] — one line why.

## The one adjustment
[Either a controllable next-time change OR an expectation recalibration — one, specific.]

Predicted check: next time a similar situation comes up, [observable difference in how you set the expectation or act].
```

---

## Verification

- [ ] Gap stated as expected-vs-actual with magnitude before interpretation.
- [ ] Every self-talk statement classified; only evidenced ones marked "fair read."
- [ ] Control split is specific, not "some was me, some wasn't."
- [ ] Expectation marked reasonable / inflated / unclear.
- [ ] Exactly one adjustment, physical or expectational, not both.
- [ ] No blame beyond the evidence; no reassurance in place of an adjustment.
- [ ] Persistent/intense low mood routed to professional support.
