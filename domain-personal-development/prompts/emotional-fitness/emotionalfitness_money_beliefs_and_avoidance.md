# Money Mindset — Beliefs and Feelings Behind How You Handle Money

**Objective:** Name the specific belief about money that is running your behaviour,
show where it came from and what it costs you, check it against facts you already
have, and commit to one small money behaviour you can test this month.

**When to Use:**
- You feel guilty after almost any purchase, even ones you can clearly afford.
- You avoid opening statements, checking balances, or talking about money with a
  partner, and the avoidance itself is now the problem.
- You earn enough on paper but feel constantly one step from disaster.
- You spend to feel better, to keep up, or to prove something, and regret it.
- Money conversations with a partner or family turn into the same argument.
- **Not this prompt if** you need the numbers — a budget, debt plan, emergency fund,
  or investment decision. Use
  `domain-finance/personal-finance-planning/finance_net_worth_cashflow_diagnostic.md`
  (or `finance_debt_payoff_strategy.md`, `finance_emergency_fund_sizing.md` in the
  same folder); for a single large decision, `major-decisions/personal_financial_decision_framework.md`.
  This prompt works on the belief and the feeling, not the plan.
- **Not this prompt if** spending or gambling feels out of control — see the
  Audience line. `emotionalfitness_shame_vs_guilt_sorter.md` handles one
  purchase's guilt; this prompt handles the recurring pattern behind many.

**Audience:** An adult working on their own relationship with money. Not financial
advice, and not clinical. If you cannot stop spending or gambling when you try,
hide purchases or debt from people you live with, borrow to cover losses, or money
worry is persistent (most days for weeks) and disrupting sleep or functioning, use
`domain-psychology/client-self-use/pre-therapy/clientself_do_i_need_therapy_decision_aid.md`
and a licensed professional (in the US, the National Problem Gambling Helpline is
1-800-GAMBLER). If there are thoughts of self-harm linked to debt, contact a crisis
line (988 in the US) or emergency services now.

## Inputs / Context

**Required:**
1. **Six to ten money moments from the last 60 days.** Each as: situation → what
   you did → what you felt (before and after) → the sentence in your head.
   Include purchases, non-purchases, avoided tasks, and money conversations.
2. **Family money messages.** Two or three things you heard or saw about money
   growing up ("we can't afford that", "money is for rich people", silence).
3. **The facts you already know** — however rough: monthly take-home, whether
   bills are paid on time, roughly how many months of expenses you could cover.
   "I don't know" is an acceptable answer and is itself data.

**Optional:**
- Who else is affected (partner, children, parent you support).
- One money behaviour you have tried to change before and what happened.

If fewer than five concrete moments are given, ask for more. A belief cannot be
inferred from one bad week.

## Method

1. **Classify each moment against the four money-script families (AG-11).** Use
   the families from the Klontz money-script research as a sorting frame, not a
   diagnosis:
   - **Avoidance** — money is bad, rich people are greedy, I don't deserve it;
     shows up as not looking, giving it away, self-sabotage.
   - **Worship** — more money would fix it; shows up as chasing, overwork, or
     spending for relief.
   - **Status** — net worth is self-worth; shows up as spending to be seen,
     hiding money trouble.
   - **Vigilance** — money must be guarded; shows up as anxiety, secrecy,
     inability to spend on yourself even when planned.
   A moment can carry two. A family counts only if it appears in **two or more**
   moments.
2. **Trace each dominant belief to its origin (RT-05).** Link it to a family
   message or a formative event the person supplied. If no origin is given, say so
   rather than inventing one.
3. **Separate felt scarcity from actual constraint.** Using only the facts in
   input 3, label each belief: *matches the facts* (the constraint is real — route
   the numbers to finance), *out of proportion to the facts*, or *untested because
   you have not looked*. Do not calculate a budget or give investment advice.
4. **Price the belief.** What it costs in money (late fees, impulse spend,
   unclaimed benefits), relationships (recurring argument), and attention (hours of
   worry). Use the person's own moments as the evidence.
5. **Pick one belief to work on** — the one with the most moments and highest
   cost, not the most dramatic.
6. **Design one reversible behaviour (DP-11).** Small, dated, and observable:
   a 20-minute weekly "money look" at a fixed time; a pre-decided guilt-free amount
   spent on purpose; a 48-hour pause rule above a set amount; one honest money
   conversation using a script. State what would count as the experiment working.
7. **Boundary check (OC-09).** Re-read inputs for compulsion signals (loss of
   control, chasing losses, hiding, borrowing to spend). If present, stop and
   route — the behaviour experiment is not appropriate.

## Output Format

```
# Money mindset review — [date]

## Your money moments, sorted
| Moment | Feeling | Sentence in your head | Script family |

## Dominant belief(s)
[Belief] — family: [..] — appears in moments [#, #] — origin: [message/event or "not given"]

## Feeling vs. facts
| Belief | Facts you gave | Verdict: matches / out of proportion / untested |

## What the belief costs
Money: [..]   Relationships: [..]   Attention: [..]

## The one belief to work on
[..] — why this one: [frequency × cost]

## One experiment (next 30 days)
Behaviour: [..]  When: [..]  Working looks like: [..]

## Routing
Numbers to finance: [prompt, if any]   Clinical flags: [none / which]
```

## Verification

- [ ] At least five real moments sorted; each family claimed appears in ≥2.
- [ ] Origins come from the person's inputs, or are marked "not given".
- [ ] Every scarcity verdict cites a fact from input 3 or is marked "untested".
- [ ] No budget figures, savings targets, or investment suggestions produced.
- [ ] Exactly one belief and one dated, reversible experiment.
- [ ] Compulsion signals checked and routed if present.

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **Real constraint mislabelled as mindset.** If rent takes 60% of take-home,
   worry is accurate. Calling it a "scarcity mindset" is gaslighting; route the
   numbers to finance instead.
2. **Script families as a personality type.** They describe patterns in specific
   moments, not who the person is. Never write "you are a money avoider".
3. **Invented childhood.** Do not supply an origin story the person did not give.
4. **Every purchase as emotional.** Planned spending that feels fine is not data
   for a problem; drop it from the pattern.
5. **Abundance affirmations as the fix.** "Money flows to me" is not a behaviour.
   The output is a dated action with an observable result.
6. **Missing the clinical line.** Chasing losses or hiding debt is not a mindset
   issue to journal about; it needs professional help.

## Techniques Used

- **AG-11 Taxonomy-Based Classification Systems** — the four money-script families sort real moments, with a two-moment threshold.
- **RT-05 Evidence-Based Reasoning** — origins and scarcity verdicts must cite the person's own inputs.
- **DP-11 Safe Experiment Design** — one small, dated, reversible money behaviour with a success test.
- **OC-09 Capability Boundary Specification** — budgets and investing go to finance; compulsion goes to clinical help.
- **QA-12 False Positives Identification** — guards against labelling real constraint as "mindset".
