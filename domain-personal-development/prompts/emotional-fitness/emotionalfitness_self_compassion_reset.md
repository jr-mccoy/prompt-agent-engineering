# After a Mistake, Separate the Fact From the Self-Attack From the Fair Correction

**Objective:** Take a specific mistake you're punishing yourself over and separate three layers — what factually happened, the disproportionate self-attack piled on top, and the fair correction that's actually warranted — ending with the one corrective action and the self-attack dropped.

**When to use:** You messed something up and can't stop replaying it; the internal voice has gone from "that was a mistake" to "I'm an idiot / I always ruin things"; the self-punishment is now bigger than the error and isn't helping you fix anything. Not a substitute for a real apology or repair owed to someone else — this handles your relationship to the mistake, not the amends (for amends, see the guilt route in the shame/guilt sorter).

**Audience:** An individual doing this for themselves. Not clinical, and not a positivity exercise. If harsh self-criticism is constant, generalized to your whole self-worth, or accompanied by hopelessness or self-harm thoughts, this skill practice is not a substitute for professional support — see `domain-psychology/client-self-use/coping-by-concern/clientself_depression_rumination_interrupt_protocol.md` and a licensed professional; if there are thoughts of harming yourself, contact a crisis line or emergency services now.

---

## Inputs Required

1. **The mistake, factual.** What you actually did or failed to do, in one neutral sentence — no adjectives about yourself.
2. **The self-talk.** What you've been saying to yourself about it, verbatim if you can ("I'm so stupid," "I always screw this up," "what's wrong with me").
3. **The actual consequence.** What really resulted — the concrete impact, on whom, how reversible.
4. **What you'd say to a competent friend** who made the exact same mistake. One or two sentences.

If input 1 still contains a self-verdict ("I stupidly..."), strip it to the bare fact before continuing.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — State the fact cleanly
Restate input 1 as a neutral event: a specific action or omission, with no self-characterization attached. "You sent the email to the wrong person." Not "you idiotically sent..." This is the whole of what actually happened.

### Step 2 — Isolate the self-attack
From input 2, pull the statements that go beyond the fact and attack the self rather than the action. Classify each:

| Self-attack type | Marker |
|---|---|
| Global self-verdict | "I'm stupid / worthless / a mess" — rates the person, not the act |
| Always/never | "I always ruin this," "I never get it right" |
| Identity forecast | "I'll never be someone who can do X" |
| Piling-on | dredging up unrelated past mistakes as evidence |

Each of these is punishment, not information. Name them as such — none of them help you fix the mistake.

### Step 3 — Extract the fair correction
Separately, ask what an accurate, proportionate response to the *fact* would be. The fair correction addresses the action, is proportionate to the consequence (input 3), and is forward-looking. It sounds like: "that was careless; add a check before sending." It never rates your worth.

### Step 4 — Apply the friend test
Compare input 4 (what you'd tell a friend) to input 2 (what you told yourself). The gap between them is the exact size of the unfair self-attack. Name the gap. The standard you'd extend to a competent friend is the accurate standard; the harsher one you turn on yourself is the distortion.

### Step 5 — One correction, no punishment
Produce the single corrective action warranted by the fact and consequence — a specific, bounded step (add a checklist item, send the correction, change one setting, have one conversation). Then state explicitly that the self-attack is dropped: it added nothing to the fix and it stops here. Correction proceeds; punishment does not.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Strip the fact to a neutral event before anything else.
- Classify each self-attack statement against the fixed taxonomy.
- State a fair correction that addresses the action, sized to the consequence.
- Use the friend test to size the distortion.
- End with one corrective action and an explicit drop of the self-attack.

### Must Not
- Offer affirmations, "you're amazing," or "be kind to yourself" as content — self-compassion here is *accuracy*, not praise.
- Excuse or erase the mistake ("it's fine, it doesn't matter") — the fair correction stays.
- Rate the person at all, positively or negatively.
- Pile the correction into a multi-item improvement plan.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **Don't confuse self-compassion with letting yourself off.** The fair correction is retained; only the punishment is dropped. This isn't excusing.
2. **Don't confuse the correction with the attack.** "Add a check" is correction; "I'm careless and always will be" is attack. If a statement rates the person, it's attack.
3. **Don't inflate a small consequence.** Size the correction to input 3's actual impact, not to the intensity of the feeling.
4. **Don't accept the friend-standard as too soft either.** If you'd genuinely tell a friend "that was a serious lapse, here's what to fix," use *that* — the friend test calibrates accuracy, not leniency.
5. **Don't treat one mistake as evidence of a pattern** unless input 2's "always" is backed by real repeated instances — and even then, the pattern gets a correction, not a verdict.
6. **Don't run this on constant, global self-criticism.** If the harsh voice isn't tied to one mistake but rates your whole self most of the time, route to professional support rather than resetting a single event.

## Output Format

```
## The fact (neutral)
[What happened, stripped of self-characterization.]

## The self-attack (punishment, not information)
| Your self-talk | Type | Does it help the fix? |
|---|---|---|
| ... | Global verdict / Always-never / Identity forecast / Piling-on | No |

## The fair correction (accurate)
[Proportionate, action-focused, forward-looking — sized to the actual consequence.]

## Friend test
To a friend you'd say: [input 4]. To yourself you said: [input 2]. The gap = the distortion.

## One correction, punishment dropped
Do: [single bounded corrective action, by when].
Drop: the self-attack — it added nothing to the fix and stops here.

Predicted check: the mistake gets its correction; the replaying should quiet. If the harsh voice keeps rating your whole self regardless, that's a signal to seek support.
```

## Verification

- [ ] Fact stated as a neutral event, no self-verdict.
- [ ] Each self-attack statement classified and marked as non-helpful.
- [ ] Fair correction addresses the action and is sized to the real consequence.
- [ ] Friend test used to size the distortion.
- [ ] Exactly one corrective action; self-attack explicitly dropped.
- [ ] No affirmations, no excusing, no person-rating.
- [ ] Global/constant self-criticism (and any self-harm thoughts) routed to professional/crisis support.
