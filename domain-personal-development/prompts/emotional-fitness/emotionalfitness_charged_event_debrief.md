# Debrief a Specific Emotionally Charged Event and Extract One Pattern

**Objective:** After a charged event has cooled (an argument, an outburst, a shutdown, a moment you handled well or badly), walk through what happened / what you felt / what you did, separate what to keep from what to change, and extract the one repeatable pattern worth carrying forward.

**When to use:** A conflict or emotional moment is over and you want to learn from it rather than just replay it; you keep landing in similar moments and want to see the through-line; you handled something well and want to make it repeatable. Do it *after* the charge has dropped, not mid-activation. Not for rehearsing a future conversation (that's a different prep task) and not for processing trauma.

**Audience:** An individual doing this for themselves. Not for building a case against someone else or for grading another person's behavior. Not clinical. If the event was abusive, traumatic, or leaves you with persistent distress, this everyday debrief is not a substitute for professional support — see `domain-psychology/client-self-use/` and a licensed professional.

---

## Inputs Required

1. **The event, factual.** What happened, in sequence, as neutral facts — who, what was said/done, in what order. Strip interpretations for now.
2. **What you felt, across the arc.** The feelings as they moved: at the start, at the peak, after. Named as specifically as you can.
3. **What you did.** Your actual behavior at each stage — words, tone, actions, what you didn't do.
4. **The outcome.** How it ended and where things stand now.
5. **Your honest read of how you handled it.** One line: better than usual, worse than usual, or mixed — and the gut reason.

If you're still activated (heart still going, urge to text an angry message), stop — run a cooldown first (`emotionalfitness_emotional_reset_ritual.md`) and debrief once cool. A hot debrief becomes a grievance rehearsal.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Lay out the timeline
Put inputs 1–3 into a single sequence: at each beat, the fact (what happened), the feeling (what you felt), and the move (what you did). Keep facts and interpretations separate — a debrief goes wrong when the story gets written into the facts.

### Step 2 — Find the hinge moment
Identify the single point where the event turned — where it escalated, where it could have gone differently, or where your best (or worst) move happened. Most charged events have one hinge. Name it and what you felt and did right at that point.

### Step 3 — Sort into keep / change
Go through your moves (input 3) and sort each into a fixed set:

| Verdict | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Keep | a move that served you or the relationship — worth repeating |
| Change | a move that made it worse or cost you — worth adjusting |
| Neutral | didn't matter either way — drop from analysis |

Base each verdict on the outcome (input 4) and the actual impact, not on how the move felt. A move that felt satisfying but worsened the outcome is a Change.

### Step 4 — Separate your part from theirs
Name clearly which parts of the outcome came from your moves and which came from the other person or the situation. Own your part fully; don't take on theirs. The debrief only gives you leverage over your own part — that's the useful part.

### Step 5 — Extract one pattern
Ask what this event has in common with previous similar ones (if any) or what it reveals on its own. State it as one sentence: "When [type of situation], I tend to [move], which leads to [outcome]." One pattern, the most repeatable and highest-cost one — not a full inventory of everything you noticed.

### Step 6 — One carry-forward
Turn the pattern into one concrete thing to do differently (or keep doing) next time a similar moment arises — anchored to the hinge from Step 2. Specific and rehearsable: a move to make at the hinge, a line to say, a thing to stop doing. One.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Keep facts and interpretations separate in the timeline.
- Identify a single hinge moment.
- Sort moves into keep / change / neutral against the outcome, not the feeling.
- Own your part and not the other person's.
- Extract exactly one pattern and one carry-forward.

### Must Not
- Build a case against the other person or assign them blame — the leverage is on your part only.
- Moralize or grade the user's character.
- Rewrite the facts with the story ("they attacked me" as a fact rather than "they said X").
- Produce a list of five lessons — one pattern, one carry-forward.
- Run as a grievance rehearsal or a self-flagellation session.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **Don't debrief while hot.** If still activated, the "facts" will be the grievance. Cool first.
2. **Don't let interpretation into the fact column.** "He was condescending" is interpretation; "he said 'obviously'" is fact. Keep them apart.
3. **Don't grade moves by how they felt.** Snapping back can feel justified and still be a Change if it worsened the outcome. Use impact.
4. **Don't over-own or under-own.** Taking on the other person's choices is a distortion; so is blaming everything on them. Split cleanly.
5. **Don't extract five patterns.** More than one dilutes the carry-forward into nothing. Pick the highest-cost, most repeatable one.
6. **Don't debrief an abusive or traumatic event as an everyday learning moment.** If the event involved abuse or leaves lasting distress, route to professional support rather than extracting a "pattern to fix."

## Output Format

```
## Timeline (fact / feeling / move)
| Beat | Fact | Felt | Did |
|---|---|---|---|
| start | ... | ... | ... |
| ... | ... | ... | ... |

## Hinge moment
[The single turning point — what you felt and did right there.]

## Keep / change
| Your move | Verdict | Why (by outcome) |
|---|---|---|
| ... | Keep / Change / Neutral | ... |

## Your part vs. theirs
Yours: [...]. Theirs/situation: [...]. Leverage is on your part.

## The one pattern
"When [situation], I tend to [move], which leads to [outcome]."

## Carry-forward
[One concrete move at the hinge next time — specific, rehearsable.]

Predicted check: next similar moment, you make the carry-forward move at the hinge at least once. Success is the move happening, not a perfect event.
```

## Verification

- [ ] Timeline separates facts from feelings from moves.
- [ ] A single hinge moment named.
- [ ] Moves sorted keep/change/neutral by outcome, not by feeling.
- [ ] Your part and theirs split cleanly; only your part carried forward.
- [ ] Exactly one pattern and one carry-forward, anchored to the hinge.
- [ ] No case-building against the other person, no character-grading.
- [ ] Debrief done cool; abusive/traumatic events routed to professional support.
