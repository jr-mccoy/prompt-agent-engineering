# Construct a Real Apology That Actually Lands

**Objective:** Produce the exact words of a genuine apology for one specific rupture — one that names the real harm in the other person's terms, contains no "if / but / because," and attaches a concrete repair action — so it repairs rather than re-injures.

**When to use:** You did something that hurt someone you care about, you're ready to own it, and you want the apology to actually land rather than making them feel dismissed. Also useful when a previous apology didn't work and you suspect it was a fake-apology pattern. Not for the fuller work of reconstructing a co-authored fight and sorting mutual responsibility — use `relationships_conflict_repair_guide.md`; not when you were primarily the wronged party — that's a conversation, not an apology.

**Audience:** An individual doing this for themselves. Not for assessing someone else, not clinical. If the rupture involves abuse or a pattern where apologies are used to reset a harmful cycle, an apology is not the move — see the boundary note and route to professional support.

---

## Inputs Required

1. **What you did.** The specific action or words, plainly, without spin. If the first version is already softened ("things got heated"), the prompt will push for the concrete act.
2. **Who was hurt and how.** Your honest best read of the actual impact on them — what it cost them, what it made them feel or believe. In their terms, not yours.
3. **Why it happened (for your eyes only).** The context/reason — captured here specifically so it can be kept OUT of the apology, not smuggled into it.
4. **What you're willing to do differently or to make it right.** A concrete repair action, if one exists. Required for anything beyond a minor slight.
5. **The prior apology, if any.** What you said before that didn't land. Optional.

If input 2 is thin (the user can't articulate the harm from the other person's side), do not draft. State the minimum bar: an apology that doesn't name the real harm is a non-apology, so the first task is to get specific about what it cost them.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Get to the actual harm (RT-09)

Push input 1 from soft to specific: not "I was insensitive" but "I read your texts without asking." Then, from input 2, name the **impact** in the other person's terms — the thing they'd say hurt, not the thing that's easiest to own. The harm the user is most tempted to skip is usually the one that most needs naming.

### Step 2 — Quarantine the "because"

Take input 3 (the reason) and set it aside explicitly. Reasons belong in a later conversation, not in the apology — the moment "I'm sorry" is followed by "because I was stressed," it becomes an explanation and the other person stops feeling heard. The reason is quarantined, not deleted; it can come up *after* the apology has landed, if invited.

### Step 3 — Assemble the four load-bearing parts

A real apology has four parts and no fifth:

| Part | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Name it** | State the specific act, no euphemism | "I dismissed your idea in front of the team." |
| **Own the impact** | Reflect the harm in their terms | "I embarrassed you and made you feel talked over." |
| **No escape hatch** | Zero if / but / because / you-too | (checked, not stated) |
| **Repair** | The concrete thing you'll do | "I'll credit your idea directly in tomorrow's meeting." |

### Step 4 — Run the escape-hatch strip

Scan the draft for every fake-apology pattern and remove it:
- **"If"** — "I'm sorry if you were hurt" (implies maybe they weren't) → "I'm sorry I hurt you."
- **"But"** — "I'm sorry, but you also..." → cut everything after "but."
- **"You feel"** — "sorry you feel that way" (owns nothing) → own the act.
- **"Because"** — reason smuggled in → quarantined (Step 2).
- **"You too"** — counter-charge → not in this apology.
- **Over-apology** — flooding, self-flagellation, "I'm the worst" → makes them comfort you; keep it about them.

### Step 5 — Attach a repair the user controls

The apology is completed by a concrete, controllable repair action (input 4), not by asking for forgiveness. Forgiveness is theirs to give on their timeline; the repair is the user's to offer now. If no repair is possible, the apology stands on naming + owning, and the user says so honestly rather than promising vaguely to "do better."

### Step 6 — Deliver one clean version

Produce the actual words — short, in the user's voice, in the right channel (in person or call for anything serious; text only for minor things). Then set the expectation: a landed apology is one that names the harm and offers repair; it does not obligate the other person to accept it in the moment.

## Constraints

### Must
- Name the specific act and the harm in the other person's terms.
- Keep the reason quarantined out of the apology itself.
- Include a concrete, controllable repair action (or state honestly that none exists).
- Strip every if / but / because / you-too / sorry-you-feel pattern.
- Produce the actual words to say, short and in the user's voice.

### Must Not
- Draft an apology for an abusive dynamic where "sorry" resets harm — refuse and route to support.
- Bundle the apology with a counter-accusation or a self-justifying reason.
- Produce a "sorry you feel that way" non-apology.
- Flood into self-flagellation that makes the wronged person comfort the apologizer.
- Demand or angle for forgiveness, or promise it will be accepted.
- Substitute a vague "I'll do better" for a concrete repair when one is available.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **The buried "because."** Reasons feel like honesty but read as excuses. If any explanation survives in the apology, it hasn't landed. Quarantine it.
2. **Owning the easy harm, skipping the real one.** People apologize for the tone and skip the act, or the act and skip the impact. Check that the named harm is the one the other person actually cares about.
3. **Apology as forgiveness-extraction.** If the real goal is to be let off the hook or end the user's discomfort, it's not repair. Watch for pressure to forgive.
4. **Over-apology inversion.** Drowning in "I'm terrible" flips the roles — now they have to reassure the apologizer. Keep the focus on them.
5. **Wrong channel.** A serious harm apologized-for by text reads as avoidance. Match channel to weight.
6. **Apology instead of change.** A perfect apology that repeats the same harm next month is a manipulation. If this is a pattern, the repair action must be real, and the user may need `relationships_conflict_repair_guide.md` or a boundary, not another apology.
7. **Safety blind spot.** In an abusive dynamic, apologies can be part of the cycle. Re-screen; refuse to script "sorry" that maintains harm.

## Output Format

```
## The real harm (specific)
- What I did: [concrete act, no euphemism]
- What it cost them (their terms): [impact]

## Quarantined (do NOT say in the apology)
- Reason/context: [kept out; may come up later if invited]

## The apology — the four parts
- Name it: [...]
- Own the impact: [...]
- Repair: [concrete controllable action, or honest "no repair, only this"]
- Escape hatches removed: [if / but / because / you-too / sorry-you-feel — confirmed absent]

## The words to say
"[short, first-person, in your voice]"
Channel: [in person / call / text] because [weight].

## Expectation
A landed apology names the harm and offers repair. Whether they accept it now is theirs — your job is the clean apology, not their forgiveness.
```

## Verification

- [ ] The specific act and the harm-in-their-terms are both named, not just the easy one.
- [ ] The reason is quarantined out of the apology text entirely.
- [ ] No if / but / because / you-too / sorry-you-feel remains in the words to say.
- [ ] A concrete controllable repair is attached (or its absence stated honestly).
- [ ] The apology stays focused on the wronged person, not on the apologizer's guilt.
- [ ] Channel matches the weight of the harm.
- [ ] No demand or angling for forgiveness; acceptance is left to the other person.
- [ ] Inputs re-screened; an abusive-cycle apology would have triggered refusal/referral.
