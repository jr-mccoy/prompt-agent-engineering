# Diagnose the Type of Loneliness and Build a Targeted Connection Plan

**Objective:** Determine which of three distinct kinds of loneliness the user is experiencing — because a person surrounded by friends can still be lonely, and the fix depends entirely on the type — then produce a plan targeted to that type, not a generic "meet more people."

**When to use:** You feel lonely, isolated, or disconnected and want to understand why and what would actually help, even though on paper your social life may look fine (or may genuinely be thin). Also useful after a move, breakup, retirement, or life stage change that shifted your connections. Not for making specific new friends once you know that's the gap — use `relationships_making_friends_as_adult.md`; not for a crisis or persistent hopelessness — see the boundary note.

**Audience:** An individual doing this for themselves. Not for assessing someone else, not clinical. Loneliness and depression overlap but are different; if low mood, hopelessness, loss of interest, or thoughts of self-harm are present, this is not a substitute for professional support — see domain-psychology/ and a licensed professional, who should be the first stop.

---

## Inputs Required

1. **The felt experience.** How the loneliness actually shows up — a specific ache for one person, a general sense of no close friends, feeling like an outsider in a group, or "surrounded but unseen." In your words.
2. **Your current connections, honestly counted.** Do you have: (a) at least one person you can be fully yourself with / confide in; (b) a set of friends or regular companions; (c) a group/community you feel part of? Yes/no/partial for each.
3. **When it's worst and when it lifts.** The moments loneliness spikes and any moments it eases — concrete situations.
4. **Recent changes.** Any move, loss, breakup, job change, health event, or life-stage shift in the last year or two.
5. **Mood check.** Whether alongside loneliness there's persistent low mood, hopelessness, loss of interest in things once enjoyed, or sleep/appetite change. Required for the safety routing.

If input 5 flags persistent hopelessness, anhedonia, or any thought of self-harm, stop the connection-planning and route to professional support first. Loneliness plans are not treatment for depression.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Diagnose the type (fixed taxonomy)

Loneliness comes in three kinds, each with a different remedy. Using inputs 1 and 2, identify the **primary** type:

| Type | The missing thing | Signature feeling | What actually fixes it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Intimate** | One confidant / deep bond | "No one really knows me," aching for a specific closeness | Deepening or forming ONE close tie |
| **Relational** | A circle of friends / companions | "I have no one to do things with," empty weekends | Building/maintaining a set of friendships |
| **Collective** | Belonging to a group/community/cause | "I don't feel part of anything," an outsider even among people | Joining/contributing to a community |

A person can have friends (relational met) but no confidant (intimate missing), or a partner (intimate met) but no community (collective missing). Name which is the real gap. If two are thin, name the **most acute** one to target first.

### Step 2 — Separate loneliness from its look-alikes (DS-01)

Loneliness is often confused with adjacent states that need different responses:
- **Situational/grief loneliness** — after a specific loss/move; often eases with time + gentle re-engagement, not a life overhaul.
- **Depression** — low mood/hopelessness/anhedonia; the withdrawal is a symptom, and connection alone won't fix it → route out.
- **Introvert recharge vs. loneliness** — chosen solitude that feels good is not loneliness; don't pathologize it.
- **Purpose loss** — the ache is about meaning, not people → route to `identity_purpose_reignition.md`.

State clearly whether this is loneliness proper or one of the look-alikes; a wrong call sends the plan in the wrong direction.

### Step 3 — Find the leverage from what eases it

From input 3, the moments the loneliness *lifts* point at the real need. If it eases in a specific person's company → intimate. If it eases doing a shared activity → relational. If it eases being part of something larger → collective. Use this to confirm or correct the Step 1 diagnosis with evidence.

### Step 4 — Build the type-matched plan

Match the plan to the diagnosed type — do not prescribe generic socializing:
- **Intimate:** deepen one existing warm tie (→ `relationships_deepening_friendship.md`) or invest in forming one close bond; breadth won't touch this ache.
- **Relational:** build repeated-proximity friendships (→ `relationships_making_friends_as_adult.md`) and maintain them (→ `relationships_network_cultivation_plan.md`).
- **Collective:** join something with recurring membership and a shared purpose — a team, class, cause, congregation, volunteer role — where you contribute, not just attend.

Give the ONE highest-leverage move for the diagnosed type, grounded in the user's real context, not a list of three.

### Step 5 — Address the maintaining loop

Loneliness self-perpetuates: it biases people toward reading others as unavailable or rejecting, which prompts withdrawal, which deepens loneliness. Name whether this loop is active for the user (from input 1's tone), and give one small counter-move — a low-stakes reach that tests the pessimistic prediction rather than acting on it.

### Step 6 — Set this week's single step

One concrete action for the next 7 days, matched to the type: a specific message to a specific person (intimate), an invitation or venue to enter (relational), or one recurring group to show up to (collective). With a predicted observable check.

## Constraints

### Must
- Diagnose one primary loneliness type from the fixed taxonomy, confirmed by what eases it.
- Distinguish loneliness proper from grief, depression, chosen solitude, and purpose loss.
- Match the plan specifically to the diagnosed type, not to generic socializing.
- Name the maintaining loop if active and give one counter-move.
- End with one dated step and an observable check.

### Must Not
- Treat all loneliness as "go meet more people" — the wrong fix for intimate or collective loneliness.
- Coach connection-planning as a substitute for depression treatment — route out.
- Pathologize enjoyed solitude or introversion.
- Produce a menu of every possible social activity; converge to one move.
- Promise the plan will dissolve the loneliness on a timeline — connection builds unevenly.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **Loneliness ≠ aloneness.** A packed calendar with no confidant is intimate loneliness; prescribing more acquaintances makes it worse. Read the type, not the headcount.
2. **Depression wearing loneliness's clothes.** If withdrawal, hopelessness, and anhedonia dominate, connection advice is the wrong tool and can add failure-shame. Route to a professional first.
3. **Grief mistaken for chronic loneliness.** Post-loss loneliness often needs time and gentle re-entry, not a social overhaul. Don't over-engineer a situational ache.
4. **Introvert mislabeled.** Solitude that recharges is not a problem to solve. Only plan against loneliness the user experiences as painful.
5. **Wrong-type plan.** The most common failure is prescribing relational fixes (more friends) for intimate or collective gaps. Match precisely.
6. **Meaning masquerading as loneliness.** "Empty" can be a purpose problem, not a people problem. If the ache is about mattering more than about company, route to purpose work.
7. **Acting on the pessimistic bias.** Lonely reasoning predicts rejection and calls it realism. Don't let the plan encode "no one wants to hear from me" — test it with a small reach.
