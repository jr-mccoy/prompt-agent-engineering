# Design a Deliberate-Practice Loop for One Social Skill

**Objective:** Take one fuzzy social goal ("be better at small talk," "stop being so awkward") and convert it into a single, observable skill with a deliberate-practice loop — a behavioral definition of what "doing it well" looks like, a ladder of low-stakes reps, a short after-action review, and a calibration check that distinguishes genuine improvement from a good day.

## When to Use

- Use when: you want to get measurably better at a specific social behavior and you're tired of vague advice like "just be yourself."
- Use when: you have one named skill in mind (initiating conversations, active listening, gracefully ending interactions, expressing warmth, handling disagreement calmly, tolerating silence, remembering and using names).
- Use when: you learn best by structured reps and review rather than by reading about it.
- Don't use when: the difficulty is acute social anxiety, trauma-rooted avoidance, or a possible clinical condition — that's beyond skill practice and should go to a professional (see boundary note).
- Don't use when: you actually need to prep one specific upcoming conversation — use [relationships_hard_conversation_prep.md](relationships_hard_conversation_prep.md).
- Don't use when: the goal is breaking a broad goal into a multi-skill curriculum — use [goals_skill_breakdown_blueprint.md](../goals/goals_skill_breakdown_blueprint.md).

## Inputs / Context

Gather these. If 1–3 are missing, ask.

1. **The skill, as specifically as possible.** If the user gives a fuzzy version, the prompt will narrow it.
2. **Where they'll get reps.** Their actual social environments (work hallway, gym, group chats, family dinners, neighbors, hobby groups).
3. **Current baseline.** What happens now when they try? Where exactly does it break down (freezing at the start, trailing off mid-conversation, not knowing how to exit)?
4. **Realistic practice capacity.** How often can they genuinely practice? Optional.

**Refusal logic:** If the inputs describe panic, dread, physical symptoms, trauma history, or avoidance severe enough to impair daily life, do not treat it as a practice problem. Note that this looks beyond social-skill coaching and suggest a licensed mental-health professional, who can address the anxiety/trauma directly (and after which skill practice may help). Do not push exposure on someone in distress.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Narrow to one observable skill

Convert the user's goal into a single skill stated as an observable behavior (DD-02). "Be more likable" → "ask one follow-up question that builds on what the other person just said." If the user named several, pick the one with the highest leverage for them and park the rest.

### Step 2 — Define "done well" behaviorally

Write 2–4 concrete markers of the skill performed well — things an observer could notice. This becomes the practice target and the review rubric. Avoid internal states ("feel confident") as the primary marker; use behaviors ("maintained eye contact through one full exchange," "let a 3-second pause sit without filling it").

### Step 3 — Build a graduated rep ladder

Design 3–5 reps from very low stakes to moderate, so the user builds before stretching (DS-06):
- **Rung 1:** trivially safe (e.g., one warm comment to a barista).
- **Rung 2–3:** slightly more exposure (a colleague, a group setting).
- **Rung 4–5:** the real target context.
Each rung names the context, the exact micro-action, and roughly how many reps before moving up. Reps should fit the user's actual environments from the inputs.

### Step 4 — Specify the after-action review

Give a 60-second post-rep review the user runs after attempts:
- What did I actually do (vs. avoid)?
- Which "done well" marker did I hit / miss?
- One thing to adjust next rep.
Keep it about the behavior, not self-judgment ("I did X" not "I'm hopeless at this").

### Step 5 — Add a calibration check (QA-20-style)

Distinguish signal from noise: one good interaction is a data point, not mastery, and one flop isn't failure. Define what a real improvement trend looks like (e.g., "I initiate in 3 of 5 opportunities, two weeks running") versus a lucky/unlucky day. This prevents both false discouragement and premature "I've got it."

### Step 6 — Set the first rep and a reasonable timeframe

Name the very first rep (where, when, the exact micro-action) and a check-in point to reassess the loop. Tie the first rep to something already in the user's week (see [agency_foundation_session.md](../agency/agency_foundation_session.md) if they need help starting).

## Constraints

### Must
- Reduce to one observable skill with behavioral "done well" markers.
- Build a graduated ladder of reps grounded in the user's real environments.
- Provide a short, behavior-focused after-action review.
- Include a calibration check that separates trend from single events.
- Set a concrete first rep with a when and where.

### Must Not
- Treat clinical anxiety/trauma/avoidance as a practice problem — refuse and refer.
- Prescribe a personality change ("become an extrovert"); practice behaviors, not identity.
- Pile on more than one skill at a time.
- Use internal feelings as the primary success metric.
- Push high-exposure reps before low-stakes ones, or push reps on a distressed user.

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **Skill too broad.** "Better conversationalist" isn't practicable. If it can't be observed in a single behavior, narrow it further before building the loop.
2. **Anxiety mislabeled as skill gap.** Freezing from dread is different from not knowing the move. If distress dominates, route to a professional, not a rep ladder.
3. **Feeling-as-metric.** "I felt less nervous" is welcome but unreliable as the target — center observable behavior so progress is real even on anxious days.
4. **Ladder skipping.** Jumping to the hard context invites a flop that the user reads as proof they "can't." Insist on low-stakes rungs first.
5. **Single-event overweighting.** One smooth chat or one awkward one shouldn't swing the assessment; hold the calibration trend.
6. **Authenticity erosion.** Reps should help the user be more themselves with others, not perform a scripted persona. If the practice feels fake, adjust the markers.

## Expected Output

A practice loop with: the narrowed skill, behavioral markers, a rep ladder, the after-action review, the calibration check, and a first rep.

### Output Format

```
## The skill (observable)
[one behavior]

## Done well — markers
- [observable marker 1]
- [observable marker 2]

## Rep ladder
| Rung | Context | Exact micro-action | Reps before moving up |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [low stakes] | [...] | [...] |
| 2 | [...] | [...] | [...] |
| ... | | | |

## 60-second after-action review
1. What did I do vs. avoid?
2. Marker hit/missed?
3. One adjustment for next rep.

## Calibration check
- Real improvement looks like: [trend over time]
- Not a verdict: [one good or bad rep]

## First rep
- When/where: [...]
- Exact action: [...]
- Reassess on: [date]
```

## Example Output

```
## The skill (observable)
Initiate a short conversation with someone I don't know well, by asking one specific question and one follow-up.

## Done well — markers
- I spoke first (didn't wait to be approached).
- I asked a follow-up that built on their answer, not a new unrelated question.
- I let the conversation end naturally without over-apologizing or fleeing.

## Rep ladder
| Rung | Context | Exact micro-action | Reps before moving up |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Coffee shop | "Busy today?" + one follow-up to the barista | 3–4 |
| 2 | Gym | Comment + question to someone on the next machine | 3 |
| 3 | Work kitchen | Start a non-work exchange with a colleague I don't know | 4 |
| 4 | Hobby group | Initiate with a new member, two follow-ups | ongoing |

## 60-second after-action review
1. Did I speak first, or wait?
2. Did my follow-up build on what they said?
3. Next time: [e.g., slow down before exiting].

## Calibration check
- Real improvement: I initiate in most opportunities two weeks running, and follow-ups come more easily.
- Not a verdict: one chat that fizzled, or one that flowed by luck.

## First rep
- When/where: Tomorrow morning, my usual coffee shop.
- Exact action: "Busy today?" to the barista, then one follow-up about their answer.
- Reassess on: in two weeks.
```

## Verification

- [ ] The skill is one observable behavior, not a personality trait or broad goal.
- [ ] "Done well" markers are observable, not primarily internal feelings.
- [ ] The rep ladder is graduated low→high and uses the user's real environments.
- [ ] An after-action review is provided and stays behavior-focused, not self-judging.
- [ ] A calibration check separates a trend from single good/bad reps.
- [ ] A concrete first rep with when/where/action is set.
- [ ] Inputs re-screened; clinical anxiety/trauma would have triggered a refusal/referral.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-01 (Clear Objective Statement):** Frames the work as a single-skill deliberate-practice loop, not vague self-improvement.
- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** Narrow → define → ladder → review → calibrate → first rep, in order.
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework):** Considers context, action, and progress signal across the rep ladder.
- **DD-02 (Vague-to-Concrete Translation):** Turns "be likable" into an observable, repeatable behavior.
- **DS-06 (Prioritization and Severity Guidance):** Orders reps low-to-high stakes and picks one skill when several are offered.
- **QA-20 (Dual-Failure Quality Test):** Calibration guards both premature "mastered it" and false "I'll never get this."
