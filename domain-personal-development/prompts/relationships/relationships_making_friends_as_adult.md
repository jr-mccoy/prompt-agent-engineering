# Build a Concrete Plan to Make New Friends as an Adult

**Objective:** Convert a vague wish for more friends into a specific plan — the venues that will supply repeated contact with the same people, the two or three acquaintances worth converting first, and the exact first invitations to send this week.

**When to use:** You moved, changed jobs, had kids, left a friend group, or simply realized your social circle has thinned and you want *new* friends (not just to revive old ones). Also useful when you have plenty of acquaintances but nothing has crossed into friendship. Not for maintaining relationships you already have — use `relationships_network_cultivation_plan.md`; not for turning one existing shallow friendship deep — use `relationships_deepening_friendship.md`.

**Audience:** An individual doing this for themselves. Not for assessing someone else, not clinical. If the barrier is dread, panic, or avoidance severe enough to impair daily life rather than a missing plan, this is not a substitute for professional support — see domain-psychology/ and a licensed professional.

---

## Inputs Required

1. **Current social reality.** Who, if anyone, you currently see regularly outside work/family, and how often. One or two lines.
2. **Available venues.** The recurring places/activities already in your life or realistically addable: workplace, gym, hobby group, class, neighborhood, faith community, kids' school, running club, volunteer shift, regular bar/cafe. List real options, not hypotheticals.
3. **Acquaintance inventory.** 3–10 people you already have weak ties to (know their name, exchange pleasantries) but have never invited anywhere. Role/context each — "guy at climbing gym," "coworker two desks over."
4. **Honest capacity.** How many hours per week you can actually give to new social effort, and which days/times are real.
5. **What you want in a friend.** Rough shape — someone to do an activity with, someone to talk deeply with, a group to belong to. Optional but sharpens targeting.

If inputs 2 and 3 are both thin (no recurring venues named AND fewer than 3 acquaintances), do not produce a plan built on cold approaches to strangers. State the minimum bar: adult friendship is built on repeated exposure to the same people, so the first task is to name or add at least one recurring venue.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Score venues for friendship potential

Adult friendship needs three ingredients (the sociology short-hand: **repeated unplanned contact + shared context + a setting that lowers the guard**). Score each venue in input 2:

| Venue | Repeated same-people contact? | Shared activity/interest? | Low-pressure to talk? |
|---|---|---|---|

Rank venues by how many of the three they supply. A once-off event scores low (no repetition); a weekly class with the same roster scores high. Pick the **top 1–2** venues to invest in — not all of them.

### Step 2 — Locate the acquaintances on the friendship ladder

Adult friendships climb a fixed ladder. Place each person from input 3 on their current rung:

1. **Familiar stranger** — recognize, don't speak.
2. **Nodding acquaintance** — greet, small talk.
3. **Contextual friend** — talk easily *within* the shared setting only.
4. **First-invite** — you've done something together once, outside the setting.
5. **Friend** — repeated one-on-one or small-group time, some personal disclosure.

The conversion job is moving a specific person **up one rung**, not skipping to the top.

### Step 3 — Pick two conversion targets

Using DS-06, select the **2–3** highest-potential people to focus on: usually those on rungs 2–3 in a high-scoring venue, whom you find genuinely likable (not just available). Name why each is a target in one line. Park the rest — spreading effort across ten weak ties produces zero friends.

### Step 4 — Design the specific first invitation

For each target, write the actual invitation that moves them up one rung. It must be:
- **Anchored to the shared context** ("A few of us grab coffee after the Tuesday class — want to come?" beats "want to hang out?").
- **Low-stakes and time-bounded** — a defined activity with a natural end, not open-ended.
- **Easy to decline** — no pressure, so a no costs nothing.

Give one warmer and one more casual variant so the user can match their own voice.

### Step 5 — Plan the repetition, not the single ask

One invitation is a data point; friendship needs several. Specify: after a first yes, what the second and third touch look like (repeat the activity, add one slightly more personal thing). After a first no or a flake, the rule is one graceful re-offer later, then move on — pursuing a non-reciprocator is the fast route to feeling rejected.

### Step 6 — Set this week's single move

Produce ONE concrete action for the next 7 days: which person, which venue, which invitation, sent by when. Not a menu — the single highest-leverage first step.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Ground the plan in at least one recurring, same-people venue.
- Place each named acquaintance on the fixed ladder and target moving one rung.
- Focus effort on 2–3 people, not the whole inventory.
- Write context-anchored, low-stakes, easy-to-decline invitations in the user's voice.
- End with one dated action for this week.

### Must Not
- Advise cold-approaching strangers with no shared context as the primary tactic.
- Recommend friendship apps, "putting yourself out there more," or generic "say yes to everything" advice without a specific venue and person.
- Produce a menu of ten things to try; converge to one first move.
- Promise a particular person will become a friend — the user controls the invitation, not the response.
- Frame friendship as a numbers/networking funnel to be optimized.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **Event ≠ venue.** A one-time meetup, conference, or party supplies no repetition and rarely produces friends. Don't score it as a real venue; the plan needs recurring same-people contact.
2. **Availability mistaken for fit.** The most available acquaintance isn't automatically a target. Convert people the user actually likes, or the resulting "friendship" is a chore.
3. **Rung-skipping.** Jumping from nodding acquaintance to "want to be close friends?" reads as intense and backfires. Insist on one rung at a time.
4. **Effort-spreading.** Ten shallow invitations to ten people is worse than three real ones to two. Watch for a plan that dilutes across the whole inventory.
5. **Reading a flake as rejection.** Adults are busy; one no or cancel is not a verdict. But endless one-sided pursuit is — cap it at one graceful re-offer.
6. **Loneliness that isn't a plan problem.** If the real barrier is dread, hopelessness, or belief that one is fundamentally unlikable, that may be closer to depression or social anxiety than a missing venue — route to `relationships_loneliness_diagnostic_plan.md` or a professional rather than pushing exposure.

## Output Format

```
## Venues, scored
| Venue | Repeated contact | Shared activity | Low-pressure | Invest? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ... | ✓/✗ | ✓/✗ | ✓/✗ | top pick / no |

## Acquaintances on the friendship ladder
| Person (context) | Current rung | Target rung | Why a target |
|---|---|---|---|

## Conversion targets (2–3)
1. [Person] — [one-line why]
2. ...

## First invitations
- [Person] — warmer: "[context-anchored, low-stakes ask]"
- [Person] — casual: "[variant]"

## The repetition plan
- After a yes: [second and third touch]
- After a no/flake: one graceful re-offer, then move on.

## This week's single move
[Person + venue + invitation + by when]

Predicted check: within [X weeks], you'll have moved at least one person up one rung (a first activity together outside the shared setting).
```

---

## Verification

- [ ] At least one recurring, same-people venue anchors the plan.
- [ ] Every named acquaintance is placed on the fixed ladder with a one-rung target.
- [ ] Effort is concentrated on 2–3 targets, not the full inventory.
- [ ] Invitations are context-anchored, time-bounded, and easy to decline, in two tones.
- [ ] A repetition plan exists, with a cap on one-sided pursuit.
- [ ] Exactly one dated first move is set for this week.
- [ ] No cold-stranger or app-funnel advice; no promise of a specific outcome.
- [ ] Distress/hopelessness beyond a planning gap is routed to the loneliness prompt or a professional.
