# Build a Plan to Cultivate and Maintain Personal Relationships

**Objective:** Turn the diffuse sense of "I'm letting friendships drift" into a concrete, sustainable maintenance system — a tiered inventory of the people who actually matter, a realistic contact cadence for each tier, and a short list of specific, scheduled gestures for the next few weeks, designed to survive a busy life without guilt-tripping the user.

## When to Use

- Use when: you've noticed close friendships or family ties going quiet and want a deliberate way to keep them warm.
- Use when: you moved, changed jobs, had kids, or went through a life shift and your social world contracted.
- Use when: you keep meaning to reach out to specific people but it never makes it onto a real plan.
- Use when: you want depth in a few relationships rather than breadth across many.
- Don't use when: you're trying to build professional contacts, expand a career network, or find clients/collaborators — use [solo_dev_network_building.md](../solo-dev/solo_dev_network_building.md) (or a professional-networking prompt). This prompt is for personal, non-work relationships.
- Don't use when: a specific relationship is in conflict or rupture — use [relationships_conflict_repair_guide.md](relationships_conflict_repair_guide.md).

## Inputs / Context

Gather these. If 1–3 are missing, ask first.

1. **People who matter.** A rough list — close friends, family you want to stay close to, old friends you miss, newer connections worth growing. Roles, not full names, are fine.
2. **Your honest capacity.** How much real social bandwidth do you have weekly? (Be realistic — minutes and energy, not aspiration.)
3. **What's drifting and why.** Which relationships have gone quiet, and what got in the way (distance, busyness, an unspoken awkwardness, just inertia)?
4. **What "good" looks like to you.** Deeper few, or warmer many? Optional but shapes the plan.

**Refusal logic:** If a named relationship involves abuse, a person who is dangerous to the user, or active addiction/crisis dynamics the user is trying to manage alone, do not fold it into a "cultivation" plan. Note that those situations need their own support (counselor or appropriate professional) and keep them out of the cadence system. Don't coach the user to maintain contact with someone unsafe.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Inventory and tier the relationships

Sort the named people into a small set of tiers by closeness and the user's intended investment. Suggested tiers (adapt to the user):

| Tier | Who | Intended cadence (default — user adjusts) |
|---|---|---|
| **Inner circle** | The handful you'd call in a crisis | Regular real contact (weekly–biweekly) |
| **Close** | Good friends/family you want to stay genuinely close to | Monthly meaningful contact |
| **Warm/dormant** | People you value but have drifted from | Periodic revive (quarterly), or one re-open |
| **Light** | Pleasant ties worth a low-effort touch | Occasional / seasonal |

Keep tiers small. A plan that lists 40 people in "close" is a plan that will fail.

### Step 2 — Rate current health per relationship

For each person in the top two tiers, give a quick rating: **Warm / Cooling / Cold / Strained**. This determines whether the next move is *maintain*, *revive*, or *repair* (route the strained ones out to the audit or repair prompts rather than scheduling a chirpy text).

### Step 3 — Match cadence to real capacity

Take the user's stated weekly bandwidth and check it against the cadence the tiers imply. If the math doesn't work (more people than capacity), help the user either move people down a tier or accept a lighter cadence — explicitly, rather than silently overcommitting and feeling guilty later. Prioritize: protect the inner circle's cadence first (DS-06).

### Step 4 — Convert intentions into specific gestures

For the next 2–4 weeks, produce a short list of concrete actions, each with a person, a gesture, and a when. Gestures should be varied and proportionate: a real call, a voice memo, a "saw this and thought of you," a coffee invite, a card, showing up to something that matters to them. Favor a few genuine touches over many hollow "hey stranger!" texts.

### Step 5 — Design a low-friction recurring loop

Give the user one lightweight recurring habit that keeps the system alive — e.g., a 10-minute weekly "who haven't I been in touch with?" check tied to an existing review (see [agency_weekly_review.md](../agency/agency_weekly_review.md)). The loop should fit the user's actual life, not add a new burden.

### Step 6 — Name the guilt trap and defuse it

Maintenance plans collapse under guilt. State plainly: relationships ebb and flow, missing a cadence is not betrayal, and reviving a dormant friendship rarely requires apologizing for the gap. Give the user one reusable, low-pressure re-open line for dormant ties.

## Constraints

### Must
- Tier the relationships and keep tiers small and realistic.
- Rate current health and route strained relationships to repair/audit, not to a cheery cadence.
- Reconcile the cadence with the user's actual capacity, out loud.
- Produce specific, dated, varied gestures for the near term.
- Provide one sustainable recurring loop.
- Defuse the guilt frame explicitly.

### Must Not
- Treat people as a CRM pipeline or optimize relationships for utility/ROI.
- Build a plan that exceeds the user's stated capacity.
- Schedule contact with someone unsafe; route those situations to support instead.
- Recommend generic mass-blast "hey stranger" messages as the core tactic.
- Promise the plan will rekindle a specific friendship — the user controls the gesture, not the response.

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **Networking-in-disguise.** If the goal is really career advancement, this is the wrong prompt — redirect to professional networking. Keep this one about relationships valued for their own sake.
2. **Capacity overshoot.** The most common failure: a cadence the user can't sustain, leading to guilt and abandonment. Always check the math against stated bandwidth.
3. **Scheduling over a rupture.** A "cooling" tie may just need a text; a "strained" one needs repair first. Don't paper over conflict with cheerful cadence.
4. **Quantity over genuineness.** Lots of low-effort pings can feel worse to recipients than a few real ones. Bias the plan toward depth.
5. **Guilt as fuel.** A plan run on "I'm a bad friend" guilt won't last. Reframe to choice and ebb-and-flow.
6. **Unsafe-person inclusion.** Re-screen the inventory; remove anyone the user is trying to manage who is dangerous or in active crisis, and route to support.

## Expected Output

A maintenance plan with: tiered inventory, health ratings, a capacity reconciliation, near-term dated gestures, a recurring loop, and a re-open line.

### Output Format

```
## Your people, tiered
| Tier | Person (role) | Current health | Next move (maintain/revive/repair) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inner circle | [...] | Warm | Maintain |
| Close | [...] | Cooling | Revive |
| Warm/dormant | [...] | Cold | Revive |

## Capacity check
- Stated bandwidth: [...]
- What the tiers require: [...]
- Reconciliation: [what got adjusted so this is sustainable]

## Next 2–4 weeks (specific gestures)
| When | Person | Gesture |
|---|---|---|
| [date] | [...] | [call / voice memo / invite / card] |

## Your recurring loop
[one lightweight habit, tied to something you already do]

## Re-open line for dormant ties (reuse freely)
"[low-pressure, no-apology message]"

## Routed elsewhere
- [Strained relationship] → repair/audit (not cadence)
- [Unsafe relationship, if any] → professional support
```

## Example Output

```
## Your people, tiered
| Tier | Person (role) | Current health | Next move |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inner circle | College best friend | Warm | Maintain |
| Inner circle | Sister | Cooling | Revive |
| Close | Two old work friends | Cold | Revive |
| Close | New neighbor couple | Warm | Maintain/grow |
| Warm/dormant | Friend from old city | Cold | One re-open |

## Capacity check
- Stated bandwidth: ~2 hours/week, low energy on weekdays.
- What the tiers require: inner circle weekly-ish, close monthly — roughly fits.
- Reconciliation: Moved three "close" acquaintances down to "light" so the real close tier is 4 people, not 9. Weekday contact = voice memos, not calls.

## Next 2–4 weeks (specific gestures)
| When | Person | Gesture |
|---|---|---|
| Sat | Sister | Actual phone call, no agenda |
| Tue commute | College best friend | Voice memo replying to her last one |
| This week | Old city friend | Re-open text |
| Next weekend | Neighbor couple | Invite them over for coffee |

## Your recurring loop
During your existing Sunday weekly review, spend 5 minutes scanning the tier list: "Who's gone quiet that I'd miss?" Pick one person to touch this week.

## Re-open line for dormant ties (reuse freely)
"Hey — you crossed my mind today and I realized it's been ages. No agenda, just wanted to say I miss you. How are you, really?"

## Routed elsewhere
- None strained right now.
- None unsafe.
```

## Verification

- [ ] Relationships are tiered, with tiers kept small and realistic.
- [ ] Each top-tier relationship has a current-health rating and a maintain/revive/repair routing.
- [ ] Cadence was reconciled against stated capacity, with adjustments made explicit.
- [ ] Near-term gestures are specific, dated, varied, and biased toward genuine depth.
- [ ] A single low-friction recurring loop is provided.
- [ ] The guilt frame is defused; a no-apology re-open line is included.
- [ ] No relationship is treated as a utility pipeline; no unsafe person is scheduled for contact.

## Techniques Used

- **ST-01 (Clear Objective Statement):** Frames the goal as a sustainable maintenance system, not a one-off reach-out.
- **ST-02 (Structured Sequential Instructions):** Inventory → health → capacity → gestures → loop → guilt-defusal in order.
- **RT-02 (Multi-Dimensional Analysis Framework):** Sorts relationships across closeness, health, and intended investment.
- **DS-06 (Prioritization and Severity Guidance):** Protects the inner circle's cadence first when capacity is tight.
- **CM-02 (Constraint Specification):** Keeps the plan within real capacity and bans CRM-style instrumentalizing.
- **QA-20 (Dual-Failure Quality Test):** Guards both the hollow-mass-blast failure and the overcommit-then-guilt failure.
