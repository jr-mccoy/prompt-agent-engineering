# First Dates and Early Dating — Safety and Values Plan

**Objective:** Go on first and early dates with a plan that keeps you safe, makes the
conversation easier, checks whether this person fits your values — and spots the patterns of
scams and coercion early, when walking away is still easy.

**When to Use:**
- You have a first date coming up with someone from an app, a site or a loose introduction.
- You are new to (or returning to) dating and unsure what is normal caution and what is paranoia.
- Something about a match feels off — they won't video call, they're moving fast, a sad story
  is appearing — and you want a second view.
- You have had a few dates and want to check how it is going against what matters to you.
- **Not this prompt if** you are deciding whether you are ready to date at all after divorce or
  a long relationship — `relationships_dating_after_divorce.md`. A teenager's dating and consent —
  `domain-parenting/caregiver-facing/ages-13-18/parenting_teen_dating_consent_conversation.md`.
  An established relationship that feels controlling — `domain-psy-ops/personal-defense/psyops_coercive_control_pattern_recognition.md`.
  Money already sent to a scammer — `domain-legal/personal-self-advocacy/consumer-scams/legalprep_scam_fraud_report_preparer.md`.
  An older relative in an online "romance" — `domain-psy-ops/personal-defense/psyops_elder_targeted_fraud_recognition.md`.
  Couples therapy is clinical and not covered here.

**Audience:** Adults dating for themselves. Non-clinical; it will not coach continued contact
with someone who has been threatening, coercive or violent.

## Inputs / Context

1. **The match:** how you met; how long you have been messaging; what they have told you about
   job, location and life; whether you have video-called.
2. **The plan so far:** where, when, how you will get there and back.
3. **Anything that feels off**, in your words — even if it seems minor.
4. **Your values and dealbreakers** (or the ranked list from `relationships_dating_after_divorce.md`).
5. **Your nerves:** what you worry will go wrong.
6. **Who you can tell:** a friend who can be your check-in person.

## Method

1. **Verify before meeting (DS-26).** Defaults: a short video call before the first meeting;
   a reverse image search of their profile photos; details that stay consistent (job, city,
   family); a social footprint that fits their story. Keep messaging on the app until you meet —
   moving quickly to another platform is a common scam step.
2. **Set the safety plan.** Public place you know; your own transport there and back; meet
   in daylight or a busy evening spot for date one; a friend knows who, where, the profile, and a
   check-in time, plus a code word that means "call me with an excuse"; phone charged; buy your
   own drinks, keep them in sight, and leave any you lost sight of; drink less than you normally
   would; your home address, workplace and children's school stay private until trust is built.
3. **Plan an easy conversation that still checks values.** Three or four open questions that
   are natural and revealing: "What does a good weekend look like for you?", "What are you
   looking forward to this year?", "How did you end up doing what you do?" Watch, without
   interrogating: how they talk about exes (contempt is a signal), how they treat staff, whether
   they ask about you, whether they respect a small no (a dessert, a second drink). Prepare a
   graceful exit line in advance ("I've enjoyed this — I need to head off now").
4. **Rate warning signs (DP-28).**
   - **Romance-scam pattern (red):** won't video call or the camera is always "broken"; declares
     strong feelings within days; works abroad (military posting, oil rig, ship, overseas doctor
     or contractor); a crisis arrives (medical bill, customs fee, frozen account, travel money);
     any request for money, gift cards, cryptocurrency, or to "invest" on a platform they recommend.
   - **Coercion pattern (red):** pushes past a no; anger at small refusals; jealousy or checking
     your phone or location; pressure to send intimate photos; pressure to cut off friends;
     pressure for sex or to go somewhere private early.
   - **Amber:** vague or shifting details; only messages late at night; love-bombing (constant
     flattery, gifts, "I've never felt this way"); talks only about themselves; heavy drinking.
   - **Green:** consistent story; video call easy; respects pace and a no; curious about you.
5. **Set stop signals (DP-13).** Any red → stop. Money requested in any form → stop, never send,
   and report the profile to the platform; if money has gone, report it
   (`domain-legal/personal-self-advocacy/consumer-scams/legalprep_scam_fraud_report_preparer.md`; in the US, the FTC and the FBI's IC3 accept
   romance-scam reports `[VERIFY: your country's reporting body]`). Threats to share intimate
   images (sextortion) → do not pay, stop contact, keep evidence, report to the platform and police.
   Two ambers that persist across dates → slow down and talk to a friend.
6. **Hold the boundaries (CM-02).** Your no is complete without a reason; consent is ongoing
   and can be withdrawn; you can leave any date at any point; you owe no one a second date.
7. **Debrief and check for false readings (QA-12).** After each date: Did I feel safe and
   respected? Did their words match their actions? What did I learn about my values list?
   Watch both errors: nerves mistaken for a red flag, and charm mistaken for safety.
8. **Emergency routes.** In danger → emergency services. Sexual assault → emergency care and a
   sexual-assault service (US: RAINN, 1-800-656-4673). Abuse or stalking → a domestic-violence
   service (US: 1-800-799-7233). Elsewhere `[VERIFY: local services]`.

## Output Format

```
# Date plan — [you] meeting [first name], [date/time], [place]
## Verification: video call [y/n] · photo search [result] · story consistent [y/n]
## Safety plan: transport · check-in person + time · code word · drinks · private details held back
## Conversation: 3–4 open questions · what I'm watching for · exit line
## Warning-sign check | Sign | Green / amber / red | Evidence |
## Stop signals → actions
## After-date debrief (3 questions) · values check against my list
## If something goes wrong: [emergency / scam report / sextortion / assault / abuse routes]
```

## Verification

- [ ] A video call or other verification happens before the first in-person meeting.
- [ ] Safety plan names transport, a check-in person and time, and a code word.
- [ ] Conversation questions are open and natural; values are observed, not interrogated.
- [ ] Scam and coercion signs are rated with evidence; any red leads to a stop.
- [ ] Money requests in any form are a stop signal with a reporting route.
- [ ] Emergency, assault, sextortion and abuse routes are present; non-US marked [VERIFY].

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **Nerves as red flags.** Feeling anxious before a date is normal; red flags are about *their*
   behaviour, not your adrenaline.
2. **Charm as safety.** Scammers and controlling partners are often unusually attentive at first.
3. **"They haven't asked for money yet."** Romance scams can run for months before the ask.
4. **Explaining away a no that wasn't respected.** Small boundary tests early predict bigger ones.
5. **Interview-style dates.** Firing values questions kills the conversation; observe instead.
6. **Shame after a scam.** Being targeted is not foolishness; report it — it helps stop the next one.

## Techniques Used

- **DS-26 Safe Defaults Pattern** — verification and safety-plan defaults before any meeting.
- **DP-28 Traffic-Light Verdict System** — green/amber/red with named scam and coercion criteria.
- **DP-13 Kill Signal Definition** — specific signals that end contact and trigger reporting.
- **CM-02 Constraint Specification** — boundaries and consent stated as non-negotiable.
- **QA-12 False Positives Identification** — nerves vs. red flags; charm vs. safety.
