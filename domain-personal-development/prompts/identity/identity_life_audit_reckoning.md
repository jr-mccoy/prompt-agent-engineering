# Structured Audit at a Major Life Inflection

**Objective:** Produce a structured, multi-dimensional life audit at a moment of inflection (midlife, post-launch, post-loss, post-promotion, after the closure of a long arc, or a chosen check-in birthday). Output: an honest read across six dimensions, the contradictions between dimensions, and a small set of decisions or directions to test in the next 90 days. Not a prescription for the next chapter; a clear-eyed read of where the user actually is.

**When to use:** A major life inflection point, planned (a milestone birthday, a known transition) or unplanned (post-loss, post-completion, sudden free time, sudden constraint). Run when the user has space to think — not under acute distress.

**Audience:** An individual at an inflection. **Not therapy.** Active grief, depression, suicidal ideation, or acute crisis are out of scope; refer to professional support before this kind of structured reckoning.

---

## Inputs Required

For each of the six dimensions below, the user provides 3–6 honest, specific data points. Generic answers ("relationships are fine") get rejected — the audit depends on specificity.

1. **Identity.** Who the user thinks they are now (their primary self-stories). Who they used to think they were 10 years ago. What recent feedback or experience has updated this self-view.
2. **Relationships.** Who they spend most time with. Who they would call at 2am. Who they've drifted from. Who they wish they were closer to. Who they've outgrown or been outgrown by. Marriage / partnership status, plainly.
3. **Work.** What they actually do day-to-day. What they think they're doing. What they're paid for. What they're known for. What feels alive vs. dead.
4. **Health.** Physical: sleep, movement, nutrition, weight relative to baseline, ongoing conditions. Mental: stress baseline, mood baseline, anxiety baseline. Energy curve. Last full physical exam.
5. **Time and money.** Where the last 12 months of discretionary time went (rough hours by category). Where the last 12 months of money went. Net worth direction (up / flat / down). Financial slack vs. constraint.
6. **Time-left awareness.** This is the one most users skip. Honest answers to: what's the user's current age vs. realistic life expectancy. How many years of high-energy/high-mobility life are likely. What things that are currently deferred would need to happen in the next 5–10 years to be possible at all (kids, certain physical pursuits, certain relationships, certain risks).

7. **The triggering inflection.** What specifically prompted this audit. One sentence.
8. **What the user is afraid this audit will reveal.** Honest answer. Often the most informative input.

If input 8 is "nothing — I'm just curious," ask again. The audit is most useful when there is something the user is half-expecting to see.

If the user is in active acute crisis (suicidal ideation, current addiction, partner violence, recent severe loss within < 6 weeks), refuse this prompt and refer to professional support. The structured reckoning is for users with enough stability to look honestly.

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Acknowledge once

One paragraph (3–5 sentences max): a life audit is a heavy thing to run; the prompt is going to be honest, not gentle; honesty is the gift. State explicitly that the prompt will not prescribe a "next chapter," and will not tell the user what to do — its job is to produce a clear read.

### Step 2 — Audit each dimension separately

For each of the six dimensions, output a 3-part read:

**(a) Where the user is, by their own evidence.** No interpretation; mirror the inputs back as a coherent paragraph.

**(b) The honest assessment.** One sentence per dimension naming the current state plainly. Examples:

- Identity: "The story you tell about who you are matches what you're spending time on, but it's behind by ~5 years — your work is actually X now, not Y."
- Relationships: "Two friendships from a former chapter are doing most of the lifting; the chapter that produced them ended 8 years ago, and no new equivalents have formed."
- Work: "The role is closer to what you wanted at 30 than what you want at 45; you're operating it at 80% of capacity."
- Health: "Stable but trending; sleep is the binding constraint, not weight."
- Time and money: "Discretionary time is being used the way someone with no financial constraint would use it; you do have constraints. Money is going to convenience and entertainment more than to what you say matters."
- Time-left: "On a realistic curve, ~ 15–20 years of high-mobility life remain. Three deferred items have a closing window (kids, athletic pursuit, geographic move with aging parents)."

**(c) The contradiction with another dimension, if present.** Look for cases where a state in one dimension is incompatible with a state in another. Examples:

- Work says "career is the priority" but Time-and-money shows entertainment is consuming most discretionary time.
- Identity says "I'm an X person" but Relationships shows the user has drifted from everyone in the X world.
- Health is fine in stated terms but Time-left shows the deferred items require an energy budget that current health won't supply at 60.

Cross-dimension contradictions are the audit's most valuable signal.

### Step 3 — Compile the contradictions

Output a list of contradictions found in Step 2(c). Each contradiction is a short paragraph: dimension A's state, dimension B's state, why they're incompatible.

If there are zero contradictions and the user listed input 8 as "I'm afraid I'll find …", check: is the input 8 fear addressed in any dimension? If not, force the audit to look at it directly. Often the absent contradiction is the one the user is avoiding seeing.

### Step 4 — Address input 8 directly

Take input 8 (what the user is afraid the audit will reveal) and answer it specifically:

- Confirmed by the audit: state that, with specifics from Steps 2–3.
- Not confirmed by the audit: state that, with specifics.
- Partially confirmed: state which part.

Do not soften this step. The prompt's value depends on it.

### Step 5 — Surface the time-left dimension explicitly

Most life audits avoid time-left because it's uncomfortable. The prompt does not. State plainly:

- Realistic high-energy years remaining, given current health and family longevity (rough).
- Deferred items with closing windows (named, with rough deadline windows).
- Items that *don't* have closing windows (and therefore can wait).

The point is to surface which of the user's "someday" items have actual deadlines.

### Step 6 — Refuse the "next chapter" temptation

Do not output a 5-year plan, a vision statement, or a "what to do with your life" prescription. Refuse explicitly.

The output is the diagnosis, not the next chapter. The user, with this honest read, decides.

### Step 7 — Propose 2–3 specific 90-day tests, not a life plan

The audit identifies *directions worth testing* over the next 90 days, not a new identity. Tests should be:

- **Reversible.** A 90-day experiment, not a life decision.
- **Targeted at the most-actionable contradiction.** Not all of them.
- **Concrete.** "Have a hard conversation with X" beats "improve relationships."

Examples:

- "Take 4 weeks of unscheduled time to test whether the work feels alive or dead with no calendar pressure. End condition: at week 4, did energy return when work resumed?"
- "Reach out to 5 people from the [dormant network] over 30 days; observe whether any reconnection produces actual reciprocity or whether the chapter is closed."
- "Run a 60-day experiment where money goes to [deferred priority] for the period and see whether the deferred priority earns ongoing budget."

If the audit revealed Pattern 1 from `identity_purpose_reignition.md` (depletion) or risk of Stage 3 burnout, refuse the 90-day test layer and route to recovery first.

### Step 8 — Set follow-on prompts as required

Based on what the audit surfaced, route to:

- Values mismatch in any dimension → `identity_values_clarification.md`.
- Purpose loss on a specific arc → `identity_purpose_reignition.md`.
- Comparison/envy loud in input 7 → `identity_comparison_envy_diagnostic.md`.
- Career-shaped contradiction → `career_residual_skills_inventory.md` and the rest of the career-transformation set.
- Health is the binding constraint → name it; recommend a real physician visit, not this prompt's output.
- Relationships are the binding constraint and involve partner — refer to relationship counseling, not this prompt.

### Step 9 — Set a re-audit date

12 months out is the typical cadence. Mark the date.

## Constraints

### Must
- Output a 3-part read for each of the six dimensions.
- Surface cross-dimension contradictions explicitly.
- Address input 8 (the user's fear) directly, without softening.
- Surface the time-left dimension plainly, including deferred items with closing windows.
- Output only 2–3 reversible 90-day tests, not a life plan.
- Refer to follow-on prompts and to professional support where appropriate.
- Set a re-audit date.

### Must Not
- Output a multi-year plan, vision statement, or "next chapter."
- Tell the user what to do with their life.
- Soften the address of input 8.
- Refuse to look at the time-left dimension.
- Issue advice that requires clinical assessment (mental health, medical conditions).
- Pretend a major life decision can be derived from a single audit.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **Don't accept generic dimension answers.** If the user says "relationships are fine," push for specific names and behaviors before continuing.
2. **Don't avoid the time-left dimension.** Most users will skip it; the prompt must not. The deferred-item-with-closing-window analysis is the dimension's primary value.
3. **Don't manufacture contradictions where none exist.** If the dimensions are coherent, that's a finding — many audits are reassuring, and the user deserves to hear it cleanly.
4. **Don't tell the user the audit means they need to leave their job / partner / city.** The audit shows contradictions; decisions belong to the user.
5. **Don't run this for someone in acute crisis.** Refer.
6. **Don't conflate this with `identity_values_clarification.md`.** That one extracts revealed values from decisions; this one cross-references multiple life dimensions at an inflection.
7. **Don't promise that a 90-day test will resolve a life-stage question.** Tests reveal information; the user decides.
