# Re-Enter Life After a Low Period That Is Already Lifting

**Objective:** Take a low stretch that is *beginning to lift* and re-enter deliberately — screen that the dip is genuinely lifting and non-clinical, restore the physical baseline, then sequence one gentle re-contact at a time with what the user withdrew from — without catch-up heroics.

**When to use:** The user came through a rough patch (overwhelm, a hard season, low energy) during which they pulled back from people, routines, and work, and things are now starting to feel workable again but life hasn't restarted. **Not for this:** a single stalled project — use `resilience_momentum_rebuild.md`; or a dip that has *not* lifted (see Audience and Step 1) — that needs a different route.

**Audience:** An individual re-entering their own life after a low period. Not clinical, and not a burnout-staging tool — for that, use `agency_burnout_recovery.md`. This prompt is only for a dip that is genuinely on the way up. If the low period has not lifted, is worsening, or involves persistent hopelessness, anhedonia lasting weeks, or any thought of self-harm, this is not the right tool and not a substitute for professional support — see `agency_burnout_recovery.md`, `domain-psychology/`, and a licensed professional. In the US, call or text 988.

---

## Inputs Required

1. **The low period** — what it was, roughly how long, and what the user pulled back from.
2. **Is it lifting?** Honestly — energy returning, some days better, interest coming back? Or still flat/worsening?
3. **What triggered it**, and whether that trigger is now resolved or fading (situational vs. ongoing).
4. **What the user withdrew from** — sorted into people, routines, work/creative, and health/body.
5. **Current baseline** — rough sleep, eating, and movement right now.
6. **One thing the user misses** from before the dip.

**Refusal logic:** If input 2 is "still flat," "getting worse," or describes weeks of hopelessness/anhedonia, do **not** run the re-entry sequence — issue the boundary referral (Audience) and stop. This prompt is for after the trough, not inside it. If input 3 shows the trigger is ongoing and unresolved, flag that re-entry may not hold and name it.

---

## Instructions

### Step 1 — Screen: is the dip lifting and situational?

Check inputs 2 and 3:
- Lifting + trigger resolved/fading → proceed.
- Still flat / worsening → boundary referral; stop.
- Lifting but trigger ongoing → proceed with a caution that re-entry may need the trigger addressed too; note it.

State the screen result explicitly before any re-entry step. Do not skip it.

### Step 2 — Restore the physical baseline first

Re-entry from a broken baseline collapses. Before any social or work re-contact, prescribe restoring the physical floor from input 5 — one sleep-window anchor, one regular meal rhythm, and daylight/movement. This is not the re-entry; it is the ground the re-entry stands on. Keep it to one or two anchors, not a wellness overhaul.

### Step 3 — Map what was withdrawn and sequence it

Take input 4 and sequence re-contact using this fixed taxonomy — one domain moves at a time, lowest-stakes first, **not all at once**:

| Withdrawn domain | First re-contact size |
|---|---|
| **People** | One low-stakes message or contact to one person — not re-entering the whole network. |
| **Routine** | One anchor routine restored (a walk, a morning ritual). |
| **Work / creative** | One small, real, completable unit — not the whole backlog. |
| **Health / body** | One anchor (a short walk, one cooked meal) — not a fitness program. |

Rank the domains by DS-06: which single re-contact would reopen the most-missed door (tie to input 6) at the lowest stakes?

### Step 4 — Pick one re-contact this week

Choose **exactly one** re-contact — the top-ranked, lowest-stakes one — to do this week. One reconnection, not a relaunch of everything. Make it specific and small enough to complete on a middling day.

### Step 5 — Set the no-catch-up rule

State plainly: the user does not owe the world make-up work for the low period. Re-entry is re-entry, not repayment. Forbid catch-up heroics (clearing the whole backlog, over-apologizing to everyone, a sudden all-domain relaunch) — they reliably trigger the next dip.

### Step 6 — Verify by prediction and re-screen

State the check: within a week the baseline anchors are holding and the one re-contact is done, without an all-at-once relaunch. Add a re-screen: if re-contact attempts consistently bounce off or the flatness returns, the dip may not have been lifting after all — route to `agency_burnout_recovery.md` / `domain-psychology/`.

---

## Constraints

### Must
- Screen that the dip is lifting and non-clinical before any re-entry step.
- Restore one or two physical baseline anchors before social/work re-contact.
- Sequence re-contact one domain at a time, lowest-stakes first.
- Output exactly one re-contact for this week.
- State the no-catch-up rule and a re-screen tripwire.

### Must Not
- Run the re-entry sequence on a dip that hasn't lifted — refer instead.
- Prescribe an all-domain relaunch or a wellness overhaul.
- Frame re-entry as repayment or make-up work.
- Cheerlead ("you've got this") or moralize about the withdrawal.
- Diagnose depression, burnout stage, or any condition.

---

## False-Positive Prevention

1. **Don't run this on an unlifted dip.** Re-entry tactics applied to active burnout or depression compound self-blame. If input 2 says flat/worsening, refer and stop — the screen is load-bearing.
2. **Don't skip baseline restoration.** Re-contacting people and work from a wrecked sleep/eating floor collapses fast; the floor comes first.
3. **Don't relaunch everything at once.** A single good day tempts an all-domain surge; that surge is the classic re-dip trigger. One domain, one contact.
4. **Don't allow catch-up heroics.** Clearing the backlog or over-apologizing to make up for lost time is repayment framing — forbid it.
5. **Don't confuse a lifting dip with full recovery.** Early lift is fragile; keep re-contact small and hold the re-screen tripwire.
6. **Don't pathologize an ordinary rough stretch that is genuinely lifting.** Not every low period is clinical; when the screen passes, help with re-entry rather than over-referring.
